Temperature-dependent reflectivity can give several equilibrium temperatures for the same radiative input.
This is an educational zero-dimensional model for equilibrium stability and history dependence, not a calibrated predictor of Earth's climate.
Explicitly parameterizing equilibrium by temperature allows independent analytical checks of root finding and time integration.

First find folds and endpoint equilibria through `Brent` in `scioxide-rootfind`, then integrate temperature under a round-trip forcing sweep through `DormandPrince5` in `scioxide-ode`.
Learn why temperature jumps at folds, why jumps lag static folds, how large the delay is, and how to check numerical results.

## Model assumptions and units

The only state is globally averaged surface temperature $T$; omit meridional heat transport, seasons, and changes in clouds or water vapor.
A layer of areal heat capacity $C$ warms by absorbed shortwave minus emitted longwave radiation, giving

$$
C\frac{dT}{dt}=F(T,Q),\qquad F(T,Q)=Q\bigl(1-\alpha(T)\bigr)-(A+BT)
$$


$Q$ is globally averaged incoming solar radiation at the atmosphere's top (one quarter of the solar constant), $\alpha(T)$ planetary albedo, and $A+BT$ a linear temperature approximation to outgoing longwave radiation.
Linear longwave approximations originate in Budyko (1969) and Sellers (1969).
Values $A=203.3$ W/m² and $B=2.09$ W/(m²·K) are used in North, Cahalan and Coakley's (1981) review and widely in teaching.

Represent ice cover smoothly through albedo

$$
\alpha(T)=0.46-0.16\tanh\frac{T+10}{5}
$$


Far below −10 °C, $T$ gives $\alpha\to0.62$ (ice-covered); far above it, albedo approaches $\alpha\to0.30$, with transition width about 5 °C.
This form and its coefficients are chosen for teaching, not calibrated to observations.

Temperature $T$ is Celsius [°C], time $t$ years, $Q$ and $A$ W/m², and $B$ W/(m²·K).
Set heat capacity $C=10$ W·year/(m²·K).
One year is approximately $3.156\times10^7$ seconds, so $C$ corresponds to about $3.2\times10^8$ J/(m²·K), or a roughly 75 m ocean mixed layer for water heat capacity $4.18\times10^6$ J/(m³·K).

The $A+BT$ formula uses Celsius; do not substitute numerical Kelvin temperatures directly.
In absolute temperature $T_K$, it becomes $A+BT=(A-273.15B)+BT_K$, changing the constant to about −367.6 W/m².

The constant-albedo linear model has relaxation time $\tau_0=C/B\approx4.78$ years.
This basic timescale later measures sweep speed.
Earth's present $Q$ is about 340 W/m², giving a warm model equilibrium of 16.6 °C.
It is close to observed global mean temperature without calibrating the albedo coefficients.

## Equilibrium curves and fold conditions

Equilibrium $F(T,Q)=0$ is linear in $Q$, so parameterizing by temperature gives

$$
Q^*(T)=\frac{A+BT}{1-\alpha(T)}
$$

the explicit solution above.
Plotting $(Q^*(T),T)$ allows folds in the $Q$ direction to be traversed simply by varying parameter $T$, unlike solving for temperature at fixed $Q$.
General implicit curves need pseudo-arclength continuation or augmented-system Newton methods; this model does not.

Differentiating $Q^*$ gives

$$
\frac{dQ^*}{dT}=\frac{B\bigl(1-\alpha\bigr)+(A+BT)\alpha'(T)}{\bigl(1-\alpha\bigr)^2},\qquad
\alpha'(T)=-\frac{0.16}{5}\operatorname{sech}^2\frac{T+10}{5}
$$


Folds (saddle-node bifurcations) have tangents parallel to the $T$ axis, where $dQ^*/dT=0$.
The denominator is positive, reducing the fold condition to numerator zeros,

$$
g(T)=B\bigl(1-\alpha(T)\bigr)+\bigl(A+BT\bigr)\alpha'(T)=0
$$


Since $\alpha'<0$, the second term is positive feedback: falling albedo increases absorption as temperature rises.
Where this feedback exceeds longwave negative feedback $B(1-\alpha)$, $dQ^*/dT<0$ and the curve folds.

Stability depends on the temperature derivative of $F$,

$$
F_T(T,Q)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}=-Q\,\alpha'(T)-B
$$


Substituting $Q=Q^*(T)$ on the equilibrium curve gives

$$
F_T\bigl(T,Q^*(T)\bigr)=-\frac{g(T)}{1-\alpha(T)}=-\bigl(1-\alpha(T)\bigr)\frac{dQ^*}{dT}
$$


Branches with $Q^*$ increasing with $T$ have $F_T<0$; decreasing branches have $F_T>0$.
At folds, exactly $F=0$ and $F_T=0$.
Equilibrium response $dT/dQ=-F_Q/F_T$ ($F_Q=1-\alpha>0$) to small $Q$ changes diverges at a fold, giving an infinite equilibrium condition number.

$g$ has maximum $\alpha'$ magnitude at $T=-10$ °C and becomes negative, $g(-10)\approx-4.71$.
At sufficiently low or high temperatures, $\alpha'\to0$, giving $g\to B(1-\alpha)>0$; for example $g(-40)\approx0.794$ and $g(20)\approx1.46$.
Thus $g$ changes sign in both $[-40,-10]$ and $[-10,20]$, giving two folds.
The numerical table below locates the cold-branch endpoint at $T\approx-17.93$ °C, $Q\approx422.08$ W/m², and the warm endpoint at $T\approx-3.126$ °C, $Q\approx289.04$ W/m².
Within $289.04<Q<422.08$ there are two stable equilibria, cold and warm, and one unstable equilibrium between them (bistability).
For example, $Q=340$ W/m² gives equilibria −35.45 °C, −10.14 °C, and 16.60 °C.

## Linear stability and relaxation times

Near equilibrium $T^*$, write $T=T^*+\delta$ and expand $F$ to first order; since $C\,\dot\delta=F_T\,\delta$,

$$
\delta(t)=\delta(0)\,e^{F_T t/C}
$$


Equilibria with $F_T<0$ are linearly stable, with perturbations decaying on timescale $\tau=C/\lvert F_T\rvert$.
Those with $F_T>0$ are unstable; their perturbations grow on the same timescale.
At the two stable equilibria for $Q=340$ W/m², albedo is nearly constant, giving $F_T\approx-B$ and $\tau\approx4.79$ years.
The intermediate unstable equilibrium has $F_T\approx8.78$ W/(m²·K), amplifying perturbations by $e$ in about 1.14 years.

Near a fold, $F_T\to0$ and hence $\tau\to\infty$, slowing perturbation decay.
This is critical slowing down.
At finite sweep speed of $Q$, slow decay prevents temperature tracking equilibrium, delaying jumps beyond the static fold.

### Quasistatic lag

For $Q$ changing at constant rate $\dot Q$, decompose temperature as $T=T^*(Q(t))+\delta$.
Assuming slowly varying $\delta$ and neglecting $C\dot\delta$ gives $C\,dT^*/dt\approx F_T\,\delta$; with $dT^*/dQ=-F_Q/F_T$,

$$
\delta\approx\frac{C}{F_T}\frac{dT^*}{dQ}\dot Q=-\frac{C\bigl(1-\alpha(T^*)\bigr)\dot Q}{F_T^2}
$$


For $\dot Q>0$ temperature lies below equilibrium, and for $\dot Q<0$ above, lagging forcing changes in both cases.
This approximation requires $Q$ to change little over $\tau$ relative to equilibrium structure; division by $F_T^2$ makes it fail near folds.

### Delay through a fold

Near fold $(T_c,Q_c)$, $F_T=0$, so expanding $F$ through second order gives

$$
C\frac{dx}{dt}\approx F_Q\,\dot Q\,t+\frac12F_{TT}\,x^2,\qquad x=T-T_c
$$


Measure $t$ from when $Q=Q_c$, with $F_Q=1-\alpha(T_c)$ and $F_{TT}=-Q_c\alpha''(T_c)$.
Changing variables through $x=Lu$ and $t=\Theta s$ balances the three terms, giving

$$
\Theta=\left(\frac{2C^2}{F_Q\,\lvert\dot Q\rvert\,\lvert F_{TT}\rvert}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
L=\frac{F_Q\,\lvert\dot Q\rvert\,\Theta^2}{C}
$$


Orient $u$ according to the sign of $F_{TT}$ to obtain coefficient-free Riccati equation $du/ds=s+u^2$.
Setting $u=-w'/w$ gives $w''+sw=0$; the solution following stable branch $u\approx-\sqrt{-s}$ for $s\to-\infty$ is $w=\operatorname{Ai}(-s)$.
It diverges at $s\approx2.338$ corresponding to first $\operatorname{Ai}$ zero $-2.338$, delaying the jump by $\Delta Q\approx2.338\,\lvert\dot Q\rvert\,\Theta$ beyond the fold (Haberman 1979).
Since $\Theta\propto\lvert\dot Q\rvert^{-1/3}$, delay scales as $\lvert\dot Q\rvert^{2/3}$; halving sweep speed multiplies it by $2^{-2/3}\approx0.63$.
At default sweep $\dot Q=0.2$ W/m²/year, the cold endpoint has $F_Q\approx0.393$, $F_{TT}\approx0.769$ W/(m²·K²), and $\Theta\approx14.9$ years, predicting $\Delta Q\approx7.0$ W/m².

Even a very slow sweep does not normally follow the intermediate unstable equilibrium.
Rounding- or discretization-sized deviations grow exponentially there (factor $e$ per roughly 1.14 years at $Q=340$ W/m²), so trajectories only cross that branch during jumps.

### Checking the constant-albedo limit

Replacing albedo by constant $\alpha_0$ makes $F$ linear in $T$, leaving the single equilibrium

$$
T^*=\frac{Q(1-\alpha_0)-A}{B}
$$


At constant $Q$, solution $T(t)=T^*+\bigl(T(0)-T^*\bigr)e^{-t/\tau_0}$ has timescale $\tau_0=C/B$.
For constant-rate $Q=Q_0+\dot Q\,t$, the exact solution is

$$
T(t)=T^*\bigl(Q(t)\bigr)-\frac{C(1-\alpha_0)\dot Q}{B^2}\bigl(1-e^{-t/\tau_0}\bigr)+\bigl(T(0)-T^*(Q_0)\bigr)e^{-t/\tau_0}
$$


At long times its lag approaches $C(1-\alpha_0)\dot Q/B^2$, matching the quasistatic expression with $F_T=-B$.
Away from folds, stable branches have nearly constant albedo, approximately satisfying this limit.
For example, at $Q=340$ W/m², $\alpha_0=0.30$ gives $T^*\approx16.603$ °C and $\alpha_0=0.62$ gives $T^*\approx-35.455$ °C, within 0.002 °C of warm 16.602 °C and cold −35.453 °C equilibria.
Wrong temperature units or time units in $C$ break this agreement and timescale $\tau_0\approx4.78$ years, making it a first check after modifying the model.

## Choosing numerical methods

### Brent folds and endpoint equilibria

Folds are simple zeros of $g(T)=0$; endpoint equilibria are zeros of $F(T,Q)=0$.
Earlier evaluations provide sign-changing brackets for both.
Bracketing methods suit known brackets; use Brent's method (Brent–Dekker).

While maintaining bracket $[a,b]$, Brent selects points through inverse quadratic interpolation of the last three points or a two-point secant.
It switches to bisection when interpolation leaves the bracket or a step fails to shrink below half the step two iterations earlier.
One evaluation per iteration gives local superlinear simple-root convergence, with bisection ensuring convergence for continuous functions.
Newton needs $g'$ and may reach the other root depending on initialization; separate Brent brackets distinguish roots.

Termination tests bracket width.
Defaults succeed when width is at most $\tau_a+\tau_r\max(\lvert a\rvert,\lvert b\rvert)$ ($\tau_a=\tau_r=4\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$), with at most 100 iterations.
At $\lvert T\rvert\le80$ the stopping width is roughly at most $10^{-13}$ °C.
Because fold $Q_c=Q^*(T_c)$ is evaluated where $dQ^*/dT=0$, temperature error $\Delta T$ in $T_c$ affects $Q_c$ only by $\frac12\lvert Q^{*\prime\prime}\rvert\Delta T^2$.
Its $Q_c$ is therefore accurate to rounding level.
[Brent–Dekker interpolation and safeguards](oxref:rootfind-brent) gives algorithm and stopping details.

### Sweeping with DormandPrince5

Sweep dynamics are a scalar nonlinear ODE, with stable-branch timescale $\tau_0\approx4.8$ years and jumps lasting a few years.
One component and inexpensive right-hand-side evaluation make explicit embedded `DormandPrince5` suitable.

`DormandPrince5` advances with a seven-stage fifth-order formula and estimates local error against a fourth-order formula using the same stages (an embedded 5(4) pair).
The last stage evaluates $f$ at the new solution and becomes the next first stage (FSAL), requiring six evaluations per step after the first.
Estimated local error $e$ is accepted under $\lvert e\rvert/(\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\max(\lvert y_{\mathrm{old}}\rvert,\lvert y_{\mathrm{new}}\rvert))\le1$; the ratio determines the next step.
The default I-controller retains step size for ratios between 0.5 and 0.75.
Shampine's fourth-order continuous extension gives within-step values without additional evaluations.

Stable branches have linearized eigenvalue $\lambda=F_T/C\approx-B/C$; the real-axis stability interval $[-3.306,0]$ of `DormandPrince5` restricts steps to $h\lesssim3.306\,C/B\approx15.8$ years.
Branch solutions vary slowly on sweep timescale $D$, allowing larger steps if only accuracy mattered.
At the default $D=1000$ years, accepted steps reach about 20 years; stepping beyond the stability limit increases estimated error and reduces the next step.
Oscillating near a stability-imposed step limit with rejections is typical weak stiffness.
For a 200-year one-way sweep, faster variation restricts steps to about eight years for accuracy.
A few hundred steps make explicit methods adequate, but slower sweeps or looser tolerances increase stability-limited stepping.
See [Embedded-pair local error estimates](oxref:ode-embedded-pairs), [Step size controllers](oxref:ode-step-controllers), and [Continuous extensions](oxref:ode-dense-output).

### Sweep speed and error estimates

Increase $Q$ from 250 to 450 W/m² at a constant rate over $D$ years, then return at the same rate.
This range crosses both folds, 289.04 and 422.08 W/m².
Rate is $\dot Q=200/D$ W/m²/year; at default $D=1000$ years, $Q$ changes about 0.96 W/m² over $\tau_0$.
This is small compared with the 133 W/m² bistable width, supporting quasistatic approximations on the branches.
The input range from $D=200$ to 2000 years corresponds to approximately 0.024–0.0024 in $\tau_0/D$.

Three errors contribute.
First, Brent bracket widths barely affect fold $Q_c$.
Second, ODE local errors decay on stable branches over timescale $\tau$ through $F_T<0$, limiting accumulation.
They are amplified while jumps cross the unstable branch, but only for a few years.
Even tightening default-input tolerance to $10^{-11}$ changes jump $Q$ by less than $10^{-6}$ W/m² and endpoint temperature by less than $10^{-7}$ °C.
Third, locating jumps from output samples gives the largest error.
With 601 one-way outputs, $Q$ spacing is about 0.33 W/m²; linear interpolation across rapid temperature changes leaves about 0.1 W/m² error in jump $Q$.
ODE right-hand-side evaluations dominate work: a few hundred steps and a few thousand evaluations.
Four Brent solves (two folds, two endpoint equilibria) require only a few dozen evaluations altogether.

## Round-trip sweep cell

Find folds and endpoint equilibria with Brent, then use `DormandPrince5` to integrate $Q$ from 250 to 450 W/m² and back to 250.
Input is one-way sweep time $D$ [years], varying rate $\dot Q=200/D$.
Outputs are one chart and two tables.
The chart overlays outgoing and returning trajectories $(Q(t),T(t))$ on dashed analytical equilibria, marking fold $Q$ values vertically.
Table `folds` gives fold positions and their $\lvert F\rvert$ and $\lvert F_T\rvert$.
Table `legs` gives outgoing/returning jump $Q$, delay beyond each fold, endpoint equilibrium differences, and quasistatic predictions.

`root` collects root finding in one closure, passing `ScalarFn::new`-wrapped functions to `Brent.solve(&f, bracket, &options)`.
`Brent` is a stateless unit struct; `solve` of trait `ScalarRootSolver` takes `Interval` starting data.
`Interval::new(lo, hi)` returns `NonFinite` for NaN or infinite endpoints and `InvalidInput` for `lo > hi`.
`solve` returns `InvalidInput` for equal endpoint signs and `NonFinite` for non-finite function results.
Iteration limits return an unsuccessful `RootResult`, not an error; check `is_success()` and convert false to an error carrying `termination()`.
`point()` selects the final endpoint with smaller $\lvert f\rvert$.

For integration, `OdeFn::new(1, rhs)` constructs a one-dimensional system (zero dimensions produce `InvalidInput`).
The right-hand-side closure takes time `t`, state `y`, and output `out`, writing $F(T,Q(t))/C$ to `out[0]`.
`OdeProblem::new(&model, end)` borrows the system and holds terminal time; `OdeState` holds starting time and initial state.
Give `OdeOptions` tolerances `ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-8, 1e-8, NormSelection::Infinity)` (absolute, relative, norm) and 601 output times through `OdeOutputSchedule::times`.
Output times must be monotone within the integration interval; continuous-output interpolation supplies them without shortening steps to land on them.

`advance` updates `state` in place and sends output to `OdeTrajectory`.
The returning `advance` starts from the outgoing terminal state, avoiding a separate copy.
Each call nevertheless restarts the method without inheriting step sizes or history and estimates its initial step again.
$Q(t)$ has a discontinuous time derivative at sweep reversal.
A step crossing it violates local error estimation's smoothness assumptions and shrinks.
Splitting calls at reversal puts the discontinuity at a step endpoint.
Workspace `OdeWorkspace::new()` starts empty; the first `advance` grows it as required, and the return reuses it unchanged.

Returned `OdeSolveReport` from `advance` contains termination `termination()`, success `is_success()` (`ReachedEnd` or a terminal event), and accepted/rejected step counts.
Step/evaluation limits or minimum-step exhaustion return unsuccessful `Ok`, with `state` holding the last accepted point.
After plotting each interval, check success; on failure, stop without adding its table row and emit outcome `partial`.
Non-finite starting/terminal times or initial states produce `NonFinite` errors; non-finite right-hand-side values at accepted states also produce `NonFinite` errors.

Locate jumps at the first crossing of $T=-10$ °C (the albedo transition center), linearly interpolating adjacent output points.
Find endpoint equilibria $T^*$ through Brent: warm $Q=450$ W/m² in bracket $[30,80]$ and cold $Q=250$ W/m² in $[-80,-30]$.
The latter also initializes the sweep.
Evaluate albedo's $\tanh$ through `RealScalar::tanh`.

```rust
//| id: app-climate-hysteresis
//| ref: app-climate-hysteresis
//| title: "Radiative forcing sweeps and folds"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   duration: {"type":"range","label":"One-way sweep time [years]","min":200,"max":2000,"step":200,"value":1000}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fast-sweep","label":"200 years one way","description":"Jumps lag folds substantially; endpoint temperatures have not fully returned to equilibrium.","inputs":{"duration":200}}
//|   - {"id":"slow-sweep","label":"2000 years one way","description":"Delay shrinks proportionally to sweep speed to the 2/3 power.","inputs":{"duration":2000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"folds","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"folds","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"legs","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":5.5,"max":6.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"legs","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":-0.3205055,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"legs","path":"/rows/1/4","approx":{"value":0.1739890,"atol":1e-5}}
//|   - case: fast-sweep
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"legs","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":16,"max":18}}
//|       - {"artifact":"legs","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":-5}}
//|   - case: slow-sweep
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"legs","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":3.4,"max":4.3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"legs","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":2.8,"max":3.6}}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, Interval, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_ode::{
    DormandPrince5, OdeFn, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule, OdeProblem, OdeState,
    OdeTrajectory, OdeWorkspace,
};
use scioxide_rootfind::{Brent, ScalarFn, ScalarRootOptions, ScalarRootSolver};
// Teaching constants: A [W/m²], B [W/(m² K)], C [W·year/(m² K)].
let (a, b, c) = (203.3, 2.09, 10.0);
let alpha = |t: f64| 0.46 - 0.16 * RealScalar::tanh((t + 10.0) / 5.0);
// α′(T) = −(0.16/5) sech²((T + 10)/5)
let derivative = |t: f64| -0.032 * (1.0 - RealScalar::tanh((t + 10.0) / 5.0).powi(2));
// Net heating F(T, Q), F_T(T, Q), and equilibrium Q(T).
let net = |q: f64, t: f64| q * (1.0 - alpha(t)) - a - b * t;
let slope = |q: f64, t: f64| -q * derivative(t) - b;
let equilibrium = |t: f64| (a + b * t) / (1.0 - alpha(t));
// Find a zero of f with Brent in sign-changing bracket [lo, hi].
let root = |f: &dyn Fn(f64) -> f64, lo: f64, hi: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let bracket = Interval::new(lo, hi).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let result = Brent
        .solve(
            &ScalarFn::new(|t: f64| Ok(f(t))),
            bracket,
            &ScalarRootOptions::default(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if !result.is_success() {
        return Err(format!(
            "Root finding on [{lo}, {hi}] did not converge: {:?}",
            result.termination()
        ));
    }
    Ok(result.point())
};
// Folds: zeros of Q′(T) numerator B(1 − α) + (A + BT)α′.
let mut folds = Vec::new();
for (lo, hi) in [(-40.0, -10.0), (-10.0, 20.0)] {
    let t = root(
        &|t| b * (1.0 - alpha(t)) + (a + b * t) * derivative(t),
        lo,
        hi,
    )?;
    let q = equilibrium(t);
    folds.push((t, q, net(q, t).abs(), slope(q, t).abs()));
}
// Endpoint equilibria: warm branch at Q = 450, cold branch at Q = 250.
let cold = root(&|t| net(250.0, t), -80.0, -30.0)?;
let ends = [
    (450.0, root(&|t| net(450.0, t), 30.0, 80.0)?),
    (250.0, cold),
];
let method = DormandPrince5::new();
let tolerance =
    ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-8, 1e-8, NormSelection::Infinity).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut ws = OdeWorkspace::new();
let mut state = OdeState::from_slice(0.0, &[cold]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut curves, mut legs, mut steps) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), (0, 0));
let mut termination = None;
// Q(t) is nonsmooth at reversal; integrate intervals with separate advance calls.
for (leg, (name, q0, delta_q)) in [("Increasing", 250.0, 200.0), ("Decreasing", 450.0, -200.0)]
    .into_iter()
    .enumerate()
{
    let start_time = leg as f64 * duration;
    let q = move |time: f64| q0 + delta_q * (time - start_time) / duration;
    let model = OdeFn::new(1, |t: f64, y: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
        out[0] = net(q(t), y[0]) / c;
        Ok(())
    })
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let times = (0..=600)
        .map(|i| start_time + duration * i as f64 / 600.0)
        .collect();
    let options = OdeOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(tolerance.clone())
        .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::times(times));
    let problem = OdeProblem::new(&model, start_time + duration);
    let mut path = OdeTrajectory::new();
    let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
    let report = method
        .advance(&problem, &options, &mut state, &mut path, &mut ws, cx)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    steps = (
        steps.0 + report.accepted_steps(),
        steps.1 + report.rejected_steps(),
    );
    termination = report.termination();
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = path
        .times()
        .iter()
        .zip(path.states())
        .map(|(&t, y)| [q(t), y.as_slice()[0]])
        .collect();
    // Jump: interpolate Q at the first T = −10 °C crossing (albedo midpoint).
    let jump = points
        .windows(2)
        .find(|w| (w[0][1] + 10.0) * (w[1][1] + 10.0) <= 0.0)
        .map(|w| w[0][0] + (w[1][0] - w[0][0]) * (-10.0 - w[0][1]) / (w[1][1] - w[0][1]));
    let fold_q = if leg == 0 { folds[0].1 } else { folds[1].1 };
    curves.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points }));
    if !report.is_success() {
        break;
    }
    // Endpoint lag T − T* and quasistatic approximation −C(1 − α)Q̇/F_T².
    let (q_end, t_eq) = ends[leg];
    let t_end = state.values()[0];
    let predicted =
        -c * (1.0 - alpha(t_eq)) * (delta_q / duration) / slope(q_end, t_eq).powi(2);
    legs.push((
        name,
        jump,
        jump.map(|j| (j - fold_q).abs()),
        t_end,
        t_end - t_eq,
        predicted,
    ));
}
// Independent check: plot explicit equilibria parameterized by temperature.
let branch: Vec<_> = (0..=720)
    .map(|i| {
        let t = -60.0 + i as f64 / 6.0;
        [equilibrium(t), t]
    })
    .collect();
curves.push(serde_json::json!({
    "name": "Equilibrium curve (analytical)",
    "points": branch,
    "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
}));
let marks: Vec<_> = folds
    .iter()
    .map(|f| {
        serde_json::json!({
            "kind": "line",
            "axis": "x",
            "value": f.1,
            "label": "Fold",
            "style": { "lineType": "dotted" }
        })
    })
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &curves,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Q [W/m²]",
        "yLabel": "T [°C]",
        "xMin": 250,
        "xMax": 450,
        "annotations": marks
    })
```

## Reading results

At default $D=1000$ years, outgoing temperature follows the cold branch and jumps warm after passing fold line 422.08 W/m².
The return follows the warm branch downward and jumps cold after 289.04 W/m².
Different branches through the bistable range form a hysteresis loop.
The middle dashed equilibrium segment, $dQ^*/dT<0$, is unstable and crossed only during jumps.

The two rows of `folds` are the cold endpoint ($T\approx-17.93$ °C, $Q\approx422.08$ W/m²) and warm endpoint ($T\approx-3.126$ °C, $Q\approx289.04$ W/m²).
$\lvert F\rvert$ and $\lvert F_T\rvert$ are of order $10^{-14}$ W/m² and $10^{-15}$ W/(m²·K); computing $Q$ from $Q^*(T)$ keeps them at rounding level.
These columns check consistency of the $g$, $F$, and $F_T$ formulas, not an independent calculation.

In `legs`, the outgoing jump at $Q\approx428.1$ W/m² lags its fold by about 6.0 W/m².
Returning delay is about 5.1 W/m².
Leading-order estimates are 7.0 outgoing and 5.9 returning W/m²; measurements are about 0.86 times these.
Ratios below 1 are plausibly due to defining estimates by solution blow-up versus tabulated $T=-10$ °C crossings, and omitted higher-order terms.
Endpoint $T-T^*$ is about −0.3205 °C outgoing and +0.1740 °C returning, agreeing with quasistatic predictions within $10^{-7}$ °C.
ODE solutions and Brent equilibria combined with linearized predictions are independent computations, so agreement checks both.

At 2000 years one way, delays shrink to about 3.8 and 3.2 W/m²; the 1000-year delays are about 1.57 times these, near $2^{2/3}\approx1.59$.
Endpoint differences become about −0.160 °C and +0.0870 °C, halving proportionally to $\dot Q$.
At 200 years, delays grow to about 17.0 and 14.3 W/m².
The outgoing jump then occurs at $Q\approx439$ W/m², leaving only about eleven years (about 2.3 times $\tau_0$) until the endpoint; its lag is roughly −7.9 °C, far from quasistatic −1.6 °C.
Quasistatic approximations apply only several $\tau_0$ or more after a jump.

An outcome of `partial` means the interval missed its endpoint and has no table row.
A missing jump means temperature did not cross $-10$ °C before that interval ended.
This does not occur in the input range, but faster sweeps can delay jumps beyond the remaining interval.
If enough time has passed since a jump yet endpoint lags disagree, check tolerance, heat-capacity time units, and temperature units.
Long tracking of the dashed unstable branch indicates a model or right-hand-side sign error.

Jump $Q$ uses linear output interpolation and has error of order 0.1 W/m².
Tenfold output density (6001 one-way points) gives default delays about 6.05 and 5.08 W/m², differing by at most 0.07 W/m² from 600-interval results.
For more precise delays, increase output density or locate $T+10=0$ through `scioxide-ode` event detection ([Event detection](oxref:ode-event-detection)).

## Summary and further reading

Equilibria have explicit form $Q^*(T)=(A+BT)/(1-\alpha(T))$; folds are simple $g(T)=B(1-\alpha)+(A+BT)\alpha'=0$ roots found by Brent in sign-changing brackets.
Stability follows the sign of $F_T=-(1-\alpha)\,dQ^*/dT$; divergent relaxation $C/\lvert F_T\rvert$ at folds causes jump delay.
Measure sweep speed by $Q$ changes over $\tau_0=C/B\approx4.8$ years; `legs` verifies branch lags of $-C(1-\alpha)\dot Q/F_T^2$ and fold delays proportional to $\lvert\dot Q\rvert^{2/3}$.
Check results against analytical equilibria, constant-albedo limits, and quasistatic predictions; do not infer jumps more precisely than output spacing allows.
