The planar circular restricted three-body problem has two primaries orbiting their barycenter circularly and a negligible-mass third body moving in their gravitational field.
It approximates probes near Earth and Moon when solar gravity and lunar eccentricity are neglected.
Integrate rotating-frame equations with `scioxide-ode`, detect lunar closest approaches, find collinear equilibrium L1 through Brent in `scioxide-rootfind`, and assess accessible regions with the Jacobi integral.
Examine how integration errors affect event times through invariant drift and tolerance refinement.
[Long-term Kepler integration](../../scioxide-ode/examples/kepler-long-time/) covers energy/angular-momentum errors over hundreds of two-body orbits.
This page focuses on errors when combining integration, event detection, and root finding.

## Circular primary motion and nondimensionalization

Relative position $\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{r}_1$ of primaries with masses $m_1 \ge m_2$ obeys the two-body equation

$$
\ddot{\mathbf{d}} = -\frac{G(m_1+m_2)}{\lvert\mathbf{d}\rvert^3}\,\mathbf{d}
$$

below.
with gravitational constant $G$.
A circular orbit of radius $a$ rotates uniformly at angular speed $n = \sqrt{G(m_1+m_2)/a^3}$.
Choosing length $a$, mass $m_1+m_2$, and time $1/n$ as units gives $G = 1$, $a = 1$, and $n = 1$; the primary period is $2\pi$.
For mass ratio $\mu = m_2/(m_1+m_2)$, primaries orbit at barycentric distances $\mu$ and $1-\mu$.
In their rotating frame, they remain at $(-\mu,0)$ and $(1-\mu,0)$.

Default $\mu = 0.01215$ is widely used for Earth–Moon mass ratio.
With separation 384400 km and sidereal month 27.32 days, time and speed units are about 4.35 days and 1.02 km/s.
Dimensionless time 10 then means about 43.5 days; radius 0.01 about 3840 km, roughly 2.2 lunar radii.
Neglecting lunar eccentricity (about 0.055), solar gravity, and body sizes makes these educational approximations unsuitable for accurate trajectory design.
Initial state $(0.8, 0, 0, 0.25)$ is also educational, starting about 0.188 (72,000 km) from the Moon at rotating-frame speed about 0.26 km/s.

## Equations of motion and the Jacobi integral

Inertial position $\mathbf{R}$ and rotating position $\mathbf{r} = (x, y)$ satisfy $\mathbf{R} = \mathsf{Q}(t)\,\mathbf{r}$ through rotation matrix $\mathsf{Q}(t)$ of angle $t$.
For angular velocity vector $\boldsymbol{\omega} = (0, 0, 1)$, differentiating twice gives

$$
\ddot{\mathbf{R}} = \mathsf{Q}(t)\bigl(\ddot{\mathbf{r}} + 2\,\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\dot{\mathbf{r}} + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r})\bigr)
$$


The second term is Coriolis acceleration and the third centrifugal acceleration.
In the plane, $\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\dot{\mathbf{r}} = (-\dot y, \dot x)$ and $\boldsymbol{\omega}\times(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r}) = -(x, y)$.
With primary distances $r_1 = \sqrt{(x+\mu)^2+y^2}$ and $r_2 = \sqrt{(x-1+\mu)^2+y^2}$, define effective potential

$$
\Omega(x,y)=\frac{x^2+y^2}{2}+\frac{1-\mu}{r_1}+\frac{\mu}{r_2}
$$


$\Omega$ reverses potential sign; its first term is centrifugal and its other two gravitational.
Transforming inertial equation $\ddot{\mathbf{R}} = -\nabla\bigl(-(1-\mu)/r_1 - \mu/r_2\bigr)$ into the rotating frame gives

$$
\ddot x = 2\dot y + \Omega_x, \qquad \ddot y = -2\dot x + \Omega_y
$$


Subscripts denote partial derivatives, with $\Omega_x = x - (1-\mu)(x+\mu)/r_1^3 - \mu(x-1+\mu)/r_2^3$ and $\Omega_y = y - (1-\mu)y/r_1^3 - \mu y/r_2^3$.
State order is $(x, y, v_x, v_y)$; $v_x = \dot x$ and $v_y = \dot y$ are rotating-frame velocities.

Multiplying the first equation by $v_x$ and the second by $v_y$ and adding cancels Coriolis terms as $2v_y v_x - 2v_x v_y = 0$, leaving

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\,\frac{v_x^2+v_y^2}{2} = \Omega_x v_x + \Omega_y v_y = \frac{d\Omega}{dt}
$$


Thus Jacobi integral $C = 2\Omega - v_x^2 - v_y^2$ is constant along motion.
Moving primaries mean inertial specific energy $E = \lvert\dot{\mathbf{R}}\rvert^2/2 - (1-\mu)/r_1 - \mu/r_2$ and angular momentum $h = (\mathbf{R}\times\dot{\mathbf{R}})_z$ are not separately constant.
Substituting inertial velocity $\dot{\mathbf{R}} = \mathsf{Q}(t)(\mathbf{v} + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times\mathbf{r})$ gives $E = \lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert^2/2 + (x v_y - y v_x) + (x^2+y^2)/2 - (1-\mu)/r_1 - \mu/r_2$ and $h = (x v_y - y v_x) + x^2 + y^2$, hence

$$
E - h = \frac{\lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert^2}{2} - \Omega = -\frac{C}{2}
$$


The conserved quantity is combination $E - n h$ (here $n = 1$).

Explicit Runge–Kutta methods such as `DormandPrince853` are nonsymplectic and do not preserve $C$ exactly.
Each local truncation error $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ changes $C$ by $\nabla C\cdot\boldsymbol{\delta}$ without a mechanism cancelling stepwise changes, so $C$ drift often grows with integration time.
The rotating Hamiltonian contains position–momentum product $y p_x - x p_y$ and is not separable as $T(p) + V(q)$.
Methods `StormerVerlet` requiring separable Hamiltonians cannot be applied directly.
Drift in $C$ independently indicates integration error but, as one scalar, misses errors along level surface $C = \text{constant}$, including orbital phase errors.
Small $C$ drift does not guarantee phase accuracy.

For relative position $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{rel}} = (x-1+\mu, y)$ to the second body (Moon), closest approach uses half the squared-distance derivative,

$$
g = \frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lvert\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{rel}}\rvert^2 = (x-1+\mu)\,v_x + y\,v_y
$$

selecting zeros of $g$ that cross upward from negative to positive.
Distance changes from decreasing to increasing, so upward crossings locate minima.
Downward crossings of circle-entry event $r_2 - 0.01 = 0$ at radius 0.01 terminate integration as collisions.
This radius is not the lunar radius; it prevents approaching model singularity $r_2 \to 0$.
Near a singularity, acceleration grows as $1/r_2^2$ and steps shrink toward `StepSizeLimit` stopping, or the right-hand side becomes non-finite and produces `NonFinite`.

The definitions block contains the right-hand side, Jacobi integral, event functions, and one-integration function `trajectory`.
`Orbit(mu)` implements `OdeSystem`, with `dimension` equal to 4 and `rhs` writing the equations into `out`.
`acceleration` returns rotating acceleration $(\ddot x, \ddot y)$, shared by the right-hand side and event time derivatives.
`Encounter(mu, collision)` is an `EventFunction` describing closest approach $g$ (`Rising`, nonterminal) if `collision` is false, or collision $r_2 - 0.01$ (`Falling`, terminal) if true.
`total_derivative` returns total derivative $dg/dt$: $dg/dt = \lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert^2 + \mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{rel}}\cdot\ddot{\mathbf{r}}$ for closest approach or radial velocity $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{rel}}\cdot\mathbf{v}/r_2$ for collisions.
`Section(x0)` describes crossing line $x = x_0$ from positive to negative.

```rust
//| id: app-cr3bp-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Rotating equations and events"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, NormSelection, SolveContext};
use scioxide_ode::{
    DormandPrince853, EventDirection, EventFunction, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule,
    OdeProblem, OdeSolveReport, OdeState, OdeSystem, OdeTrajectory,
};
pub(crate) struct Orbit(pub(crate) f64);
impl Orbit {
    pub(crate) fn omega(&self, y: &[f64]) -> f64 {
        let mu = self.0;
        0.5 * (y[0] * y[0] + y[1] * y[1])
            + (1.0 - mu) / (y[0] + mu).hypot(y[1])
            + mu / (y[0] - 1.0 + mu).hypot(y[1])
    }
    pub(crate) fn jacobi(&self, y: &[f64]) -> f64 {
        2.0 * self.omega(y) - y[2] * y[2] - y[3] * y[3]
    }
    // Rotating acceleration (x'', y'') = (2 v_y + Ω_x, −2 v_x + Ω_y).
    pub(crate) fn acceleration(&self, y: &[f64]) -> [f64; 2] {
        let mu = self.0;
        let dx = y[0] + mu;
        let ex = dx - 1.0;
        let r = dx.hypot(y[1]).powi(3);
        let s = ex.hypot(y[1]).powi(3);
        [
            2.0 * y[3] + y[0] - (1.0 - mu) * dx / r - mu * ex / s,
            -2.0 * y[2] + y[1] - (1.0 - mu) * y[1] / r - mu * y[1] / s,
        ]
    }
}
impl OdeSystem<f64> for Orbit {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        4
    }
    fn rhs(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let [ax, ay] = self.acceleration(y);
        out.copy_from_slice(&[y[2], y[3], ax, ay]);
        Ok(())
    }
}
// Encounter(μ, false) is closest approach g = r_rel·v; Encounter(μ, true) is collision r_2 − 0.01.
pub(crate) struct Encounter(pub(crate) f64, pub(crate) bool);
impl EventFunction<f64> for Encounter {
    fn eval(&self, _: f64, y: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(if self.1 {
            (y[0] - 1.0 + self.0).hypot(y[1]) - 0.01
        } else {
            (y[0] - 1.0 + self.0) * y[2] + y[1] * y[3]
        })
    }
    fn has_total_derivative(&self) -> bool {
        true
    }
    // slope is the state derivative (v_x, v_y, x'', y'').
    fn total_derivative(&self, _: f64, y: &[f64], slope: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        let (ex, ey) = (y[0] - 1.0 + self.0, y[1]);
        Ok(if self.1 {
            (ex * slope[0] + ey * slope[1]) / ex.hypot(ey)
        } else {
            slope[0] * y[2] + slope[1] * y[3] + ex * slope[2] + ey * slope[3]
        })
    }
    fn direction(&self) -> EventDirection {
        if self.1 {
            EventDirection::Falling
        } else {
            EventDirection::Rising
        }
    }
    fn is_terminal(&self) -> bool {
        self.1
    }
}
pub(crate) struct Section(pub(crate) f64);
impl EventFunction<f64> for Section {
    fn eval(&self, _: f64, y: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(y[0] - self.0)
    }
    fn direction(&self) -> EventDirection {
        EventDirection::Falling
    }
    fn is_terminal(&self) -> bool {
        false
    }
}
pub(crate) fn trajectory(
    mu: f64,
    initial: &[f64],
    end: f64,
    tol: f64,
    events: &[&dyn EventFunction<f64>],
) -> Result<(OdeTrajectory<f64>, OdeSolveReport<f64>), SciOxideError> {
    let model = Orbit(mu);
    let problem = OdeProblem::new(&model, end).with_events(events);
    let options = OdeOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(ErrorTolerance::scalar(tol, tol, NormSelection::Infinity)?)
        .with_maximum_step(0.05)
        .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::times(
            (0..=400).map(|i| end * i as f64 / 400.0).collect(),
        ));
    let method = DormandPrince853::new();
    let mut ws = method.workspace(&problem, &options)?;
    let mut state = OdeState::from_slice(0.0, initial)?;
    let mut path = OdeTrajectory::new();
    let report = method.advance(
        &problem,
        &options,
        &mut state,
        &mut path,
        &mut ws,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )?;
    Ok((path, report))
}
```

Inside `trajectory`, `OdeProblem::new(&model, end).with_events(events)` borrows the right-hand side and event array; `OdeState::from_slice(0.0, initial)` supplies starting time, and `initial` is cloned.
`ErrorTolerance::scalar(tol, tol, NormSelection::Infinity)` sets absolute and relative tolerances to `tol`, accepting steps by the maximum norm of component local error estimates $e_i$ divided by $\mathrm{tol} + \mathrm{tol}\max(\lvert y_{n,i}\rvert, \lvert y_{n+1,i}\rvert)$ ([Error normalization and acceptance](oxref:ode-error-normalization)).
Negative or non-finite `tol` produces `InvalidInput` or `NonFinite` here.
`with_maximum_step(0.05)` bounds step size, for reasons discussed next.
At 401 equally spaced times supplied to `OdeOutputSchedule::times`, emit dense-output values without influencing step selection.
`method.workspace(&problem, &options)` allocates workspace for dimension/event count; `advance` updates `state` to terminal time or a terminal event, sends output/events to `OdeTrajectory`, and returns `OdeSolveReport`.
Workspace is recreated per call, so repeated integrations allocate each time.
`advance` returns `ConvergenceFailure` for localization iteration limits and `NonFinite` for non-finite right-hand-side or event results; minimum-step stopping returns `termination()` in an `Ok` report.

## Integration and event algorithms

`DormandPrince853` is a twelve-stage explicit Runge–Kutta method advancing an eighth-order solution, combining fifth- and third-order embedded estimates for local error.
The first step uses thirteen right-hand-side calls, later steps twelve; steps needing dense output for samples/events add three for a seventh-order continuous extension ([Extension order by method](oxref:ode-dense-output-orders)).
Nonstiff smooth dynamics and tight tolerances favor a high-order explicit method with fewer evaluations.
Acceptance controls local error only; global error sums step errors propagated through the flow.
Nearby trajectories can separate rapidly near bodies, increasing global error over time even at fixed tolerance.

Events inspect accepted-step endpoint signs; a sign change triggers TOMS 748 bracketing of $\psi(t) = g(t, \tilde{\mathbf{y}}(t))$ on continuous extension $\tilde{\mathbf{y}}(t)$ ([Root finding on continuous extensions](oxref:ode-event-localization)).
Default search `EventSearchPolicy::Endpoints` checks endpoints only; positive–negative–positive $g$ within a step hides an approaching/receding pair ([Search policies and missed zeros](oxref:ode-event-search-policy)).
`with_maximum_step(0.05)` keeps each step much shorter than separation between closest and farthest approaches.
Since `Encounter` provides total derivatives, adding `with_event_options(OdeEventOptions::new().with_search(EventSearchPolicy::DerivativeAssisted(n)))` to `options` (`n` is a `Budget` subdivision count) also searches within-step crossing pairs using signs at the points dividing the step into $n$ equal parts and changes in the sign of $dg/dt$.
Collision events count only `Falling`, excluding trajectories that touch circle $r_2 = 0.01$ and turn away.
Only events with direction `Either` record tangencies.

Three sources affect event-time $t^*$ error.
First is the final localization bracket width, narrowed by default to order $4\varepsilon + 4\varepsilon\lvert t\rvert$ (machine epsilon $\varepsilon \approx 2.2\times10^{-16}$).
Second is continuous-extension error, of order $O(h^{8})$ in step size $h$ for an exact starting state.
Third is global state error $\delta\mathbf{y}$ already present at the step's start.
A state perturbation $\delta\mathbf{y}$ changes $\psi$ by $\nabla_{\mathbf{y}} g\cdot\delta\mathbf{y}$, shifting its zero by

$$
\delta t^* \approx -\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{y}} g\cdot\delta\mathbf{y}}{dg/dt\,(t^*)}
$$


$1/\lvert dg/dt\rvert$ acts as timing condition number.
At closest approach, $dg/dt = \lvert\mathbf{v}\rvert^2 + \mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{rel}}\cdot\ddot{\mathbf{r}}$ becomes small for low relative speed and shallow minima, making timing sensitive to state error.
For collisions, $dg/dt$ is radial velocity; near-tangent entry makes it small, and slight state errors can even change whether entry occurs.
Localization width demonstrates root-finding convergence, not ODE accuracy.

Closest distance $\lvert\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{rel}}(t^*)\rvert$ is insensitive to timing error at first order.
At a minimum, $d\lvert\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{rel}}\rvert/dt = 0$, so a timing shift $\delta t$ changes distance proportionally to $\delta t^2$.
Distance errors mainly come from global position errors.

L1 solves $\Omega_x(x, 0) = 0$ on $y = 0$; between primaries, $-\mu < x < 1-\mu$,

$$
f(x) = x - \frac{1-\mu}{(x+\mu)^2} + \frac{\mu}{(1-\mu-x)^2}
$$


$f'(x) = 1 + 2(1-\mu)/(x+\mu)^3 + 2\mu/(1-\mu-x)^3 > 0$.
Since $f$ is negative near the left endpoint and positive near the right, the root is unique and simple.
`Brent` is Brent–Dekker, combining inverse quadratic/secant steps with bisection when bracket shrinkage is insufficient ([Brent–Dekker interpolation and safeguards](oxref:rootfind-brent)).
It converges superlinearly near simple roots while preserving the bracket at every iteration.
`ScalarRootOptions::default()` succeeds with `BracketTolerance` when bracket width is at most $4\varepsilon + 4\varepsilon\max(\lvert a\rvert, \lvert b\rvert)$, with an iteration limit of 100.
L1 root finding is independent of time integration, so its error is bounded by the final bracket width (order $10^{-15}$); time integration dominates runtime.

## Closest approaches and accessible regions

For Jacobi integral $C$, only $2\Omega \ge C$ inside zero-velocity curve $C = 2\Omega$ at $\mathbf{v} = 0$ is accessible (the Hill region).
Writing the L1 value as $C_{L1} = 2\Omega(x_{L1}, 0)$, condition $C > C_{L1}$ separates Earth/Moon regions, while $C < C_{L1}$ opens an L1 passage.
An open passage permits crossing but does not require a particular trajectory to use it.
At $\mu = 0.01215$, $x_{L1} \approx 0.836918$ and $C_{L1} \approx 3.18834$; initial $C \approx 3.13954$ is smaller.
It is also below lunar-exterior L2 value $C_{L2} \approx 3.17216$, opening that passage too.

Integrate initial $(0.8, 0, 0, 0.25)$ to dimensionless time 10 with inputs mass ratio $\mu$ and tolerance exponent $k$ (tolerance $10^{k}$), tabulating close approaches and collisions.
Also find L1 with Brent, tabulating $C$, $C_{L1}$, and maximum output-time drift in $C$.
`path.events()` returns events in time order; `index()` indexes the `events` array (0 closest approach, 1 collision).
Read localization width through `Crossing` in `evidence()` and `bracket` in `Contact`; exact-zero sampled `SampledZero` has undefined width.
The final column gives $dg/dt$ from `total_derivative` using the event state and slopes from `acceleration`, corresponding to the reciprocal of the timing condition number.
Check Brent success through `is_success()` and turn non-convergence into an error.
Integration termination `report.termination()` indicates terminal time for `ReachedEnd` or a collision stop for `TerminalEvent`.

```rust
//| id: app-cr3bp-jacobi
//| ref: app-cr3bp-jacobi
//| uses: [app-cr3bp-model]
//| title: "Closest-approach events and the Jacobi integral"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode, scioxide-rootfind]
//| inputs:
//|   mu: {"type":"range","label":"Mass ratio μ [-]","min":0.005,"max":0.03,"step":0.00005,"value":0.01215}
//|   exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Tolerance exponent","min":-12,"max":-6,"step":1,"value":-10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"collision","label":"μ = 0.03","description":"Increasing lunar mass ratio causes entry into radius 0.01 on the first encounter, stopping at a collision event.","inputs":{"mu":0.03}}
//|   - {"id":"loose","label":"Tolerance 1e-6","description":"Looser tolerance increases Jacobi drift.","inputs":{"exponent":-6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"invariant","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"events","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":5.16510,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"events","path":"/rows/2/4","approx":{"value":0.103,"atol":0.005}}
//|   - case: collision
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"events","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Collision"}
//|       - {"artifact":"events","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.53170,"atol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: loose
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"invariant","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":1e-9,"max":1e-7}}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, Interval};
use scioxide_ode::{EventEvidence, EventFunction, OdeTermination};
use scioxide_rootfind::{Brent, ScalarFn, ScalarRootOptions, ScalarRootSolver};
let events: [&dyn EventFunction<f64>; 2] = [&Encounter(mu, false), &Encounter(mu, true)];
let initial = [0.8, 0.0, 0.0, 0.25];
let model = Orbit(mu);
let (path, report) = trajectory(mu, &initial, 10.0, 10.0_f64.powf(exponent), &events)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// L1 is the simple Ω_x(x, 0) = 0 root between primaries.
let f =
    ScalarFn::new(|x: f64| Ok(x - (1.0 - mu) / (x + mu).powi(2) + mu / (1.0 - mu - x).powi(2)));
let l1 = Brent
    .solve(
        &f,
        Interval::new(-mu + 1e-4, 1.0 - mu - 1e-4).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
        &ScalarRootOptions::default(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
if !l1.is_success() {
    return Err("L1 root finding did not converge".into());
}
let c = model.jacobi(&initial);
let cl1 = model.jacobi(&[l1.point(), 0.0, 0.0, 0.0]);
let drift = path
    .states()
    .iter()
    .map(|y| (model.jacobi(y.as_slice()) - c).abs())
    .fold(0.0, f64::max);
let rows = path
    .events()
    .iter()
    .map(|event| {
        let y = event.state();
        let width = match event.evidence() {
            EventEvidence::Crossing { bracket, .. }
            | EventEvidence::Contact { bracket, .. } => bracket.width().ok(),
            EventEvidence::SampledZero => None,
        };
        // dg/dt(t*); timing errors amplify as δt* ≈ δg / |dg/dt|.
        let [ax, ay] = model.acceleration(y);
        let slope = [y[2], y[3], ax, ay];
        let rate = events[event.index()].total_derivative(event.time(), y, &slope)?;
        let name = if event.index() == 0 {
            "Closest approach"
        } else {
            "Collision"
        };
        let distance = (y[0] - 1.0 + mu).hypot(y[1]);
        Ok((name, event.time(), distance, width, rate))
    })
    .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, SciOxideError>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Events", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Time [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Distance to second body [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Localization bracket width [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "dg/dt [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 4 } }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "events" })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "L1 x [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Initial C [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "C_L1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum |ΔC| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(l1.point(), c, cl1, drift)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "invariant" })
);
let points: Vec<_> = path
    .states()
    .iter()
    .map(|y| [y.as_slice()[0], y.as_slice()[1]])
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({"name":"Rotating-frame orbit","points":points})],
    options = &serde_json::json!({"xLabel":"x [-]","yLabel":"y [-]"})
);
let outcome = match report.termination() {
    Some(OdeTermination::ReachedEnd) => Outcome::satisfied("Integrated to terminal time"),
    Some(OdeTermination::TerminalEvent) => Outcome::satisfied("Stopped integration at a collision event"),
    _ => Outcome::partial("Integration stopped early"),
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{:?}", report.termination()))
        .diagnostic("Maximum |ΔC|", drift, None)
);
```

Defaults have no collisions and four closest approaches at 5.165, 6.700, 8.545, and 9.928, with distances about 0.677, 1.117, 1.048, and 0.663.
In the rotating plot, the trajectory starts 0.188 from the Moon ($x \approx 0.988$), recedes, and orbits Earth ($x \approx -0.012$) roughly twice.
Initially $v_x = 0$ and $y = 0$ give $g = 0$, but a zero at the integration start is not counted as an event.
Bracket widths of order $10^{-15}$ demonstrate localization to tolerance $4\varepsilon$, not timing accuracy.
$dg/dt$ is about 6.1 and 5.1 at the first/fourth events, but about 0.27 and 0.10 at the second/third.
The latter are distant, low-speed, shallow minima whose timing is 20–60 times more sensitive to the same state error.
Maximum $C$ drift is about $3\times10^{-10}$ at tolerance $10^{-10}$.
Measured at dense-output values, this includes continuous-extension error.

At tolerance exponent $-6$, $C$ drift rises to about $10^{-8}$ and closest times change in the seventh decimal place.
Mass ratio 0.005 gives only one closest approach; at 0.03 the orbit enters radius 0.01 around time 0.53 and stops at collision.
Collision $dg/dt$ is negative radial velocity (about $-2.3$ at $\mu = 0.03$).
Smaller magnitude means more tangent entry and greater sensitivity of collision occurrence to initial state or tolerance.
If event counts change with tolerance or events have near-zero $dg/dt$, vary both maximum step and tolerance before trusting results.

## Tolerance refinement

A single-tolerance event table does not reveal timing error itself.
Integrate the same $\mu = 0.01215$ initial state from tolerances $10^{-5}$ through $10^{-12}$, comparing closest times/distances and terminal positions with a $10^{-13}$ reference solve.
This is a refinement check; differences include reference error of roughly $10^{-12}$ or less.
Different event counts prevent correspondence, leaving time/distance differences undefined.
Read right-hand-side calls through `report.counts().provider(EvaluationRole::RightHandSide)` and accepted steps through `report.accepted_steps()`.

```rust
//| id: app-cr3bp-refinement
//| ref: app-cr3bp-refinement
//| uses: [app-cr3bp-model]
//| title: "Closest times and Jacobi integrals under tolerance changes"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":199,"max":206}}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/7/2","range":{"max":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::EvaluationRole;
use scioxide_ode::{EventFunction, OdeTrajectory};
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let (mu, end) = (0.01215, 10.0);
let initial = [0.8, 0.0, 0.0, 0.25];
let model = Orbit(mu);
let c0 = model.jacobi(&initial);
let approach = Encounter(mu, false);
let events: [&dyn EventFunction<f64>; 1] = [&approach];
// Reference uses tolerance 1e-13 (a refinement check).
let (reference, _) =
    trajectory(mu, &initial, end, 1e-13, &events).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let distance = |y: &[f64]| (y[0] - 1.0 + mu).hypot(y[1]);
let last = |p: &OdeTrajectory<f64>| p.states().last().map(|y| y.as_slice().to_vec());
let reference_end = last(&reference).ok_or("Reference output is missing")?;
let (mut rows, mut series) = (Vec::new(), vec![Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new()]);
for k in 5..=12 {
    let tol = 10.0_f64.powi(-k);
    let (path, report) =
        trajectory(mu, &initial, end, tol, &events).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let matched = path.events().len() == reference.events().len();
    let pairs = || path.events().iter().zip(reference.events());
    let time_error = matched.then(|| {
        pairs()
            .map(|(a, b)| (a.time() - b.time()).abs())
            .fold(0.0, f64::max)
    });
    let distance_error = matched.then(|| {
        pairs()
            .map(|(a, b)| (distance(a.state()) - distance(b.state())).abs())
            .fold(0.0, f64::max)
    });
    let end_state = last(&path).ok_or("Output is missing")?;
    let end_error = (end_state[0] - reference_end[0]).hypot(end_state[1] - reference_end[1]);
    let drift = path
        .states()
        .iter()
        .map(|y| (model.jacobi(y.as_slice()) - c0).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max);
    let evaluations = report.counts().provider(EvaluationRole::RightHandSide);
    // Omit nonpositive values from logarithmic axes.
    let values = [time_error, Some(end_error), Some(drift)];
    for (s, v) in series.iter_mut().zip(values) {
        if let Some(v) = v.filter(|&v| v > 0.0) {
            s.push([tol, v]);
        }
    }
    let steps = report.accepted_steps();
    rows.push((
        tol,
        path.events().len(),
        time_error,
        distance_error,
        end_error,
        drift,
        steps,
        evaluations,
    ));
}
let names = ["Maximum |Δt*|", "Terminal position difference", "Maximum |ΔC|"];
let mut chart: Vec<_> = names
    .iter()
    .zip(series)
    .map(|(name, points)| {
        let style = serde_json::json!({ "showSymbol": true });
        serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points, "style": style })
    })
    .collect();
let diagonal: Vec<_> = rows.iter().map(|r| [r.0, r.0]).collect();
let dashed = serde_json::json!({ "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false });
chart.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": "Tolerances", "points": diagonal, "style": dashed }));
emit_line_chart!(
    &chart,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Tolerance [-]",
        "yLabel": "Reference difference [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Tolerance [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Closest-approach count", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum |Δt*| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum |Δr*| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Terminal position difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Maximum |ΔC| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Accepted steps [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Right-hand-side evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "refinement" })
);
```

Horizontal axes show tolerance, vertical axes reference differences; the dashed line is tolerance itself.
From tolerance $10^{-5}$ through $10^{-7}$, accepted counts remain about 200 and differences do not change.
Time 10 at maximum step 0.05 requires 200 steps, so the maximum determines spacing in this range.
At tolerances $10^{-8}$ or smaller, step counts increase and timing differences fall to about $4\times10^{-9}$ at $10^{-10}$ and $2\times10^{-11}$ at $10^{-12}$.
At $10^{-9}$ or tighter, timing differences exceed tolerance and track terminal-position differences, showing dominance of global integration error amplified by $1/\lvert dg/dt\rvert$, not localization width.
Closest-distance differences are about one fifth to one eighth of timing differences.
Minimum distances have no first-order timing sensitivity, so these differences come from global position error.
Right-hand-side calls are 15–17 per step (twelve stages, three continuous-extension stages, and rejected steps), only about 4800 even at tolerance $10^{-12}$.
If tighter tolerances no longer reduce differences, maximum step or reference accuracy limits them.

## Linear L4 oscillations

Equilibrium L4 is the equilateral triangle vertex $(x_4, y_4) = (1/2-\mu, \sqrt3/2)$, with $r_1 = r_2 = 1$.
Linearizing deviations $(\xi, \eta)$ gives

$$
\ddot\xi - 2\dot\eta = \Omega_{xx}\,\xi + \Omega_{xy}\,\eta, \qquad \ddot\eta + 2\dot\xi = \Omega_{xy}\,\xi + \Omega_{yy}\,\eta
$$


At L4 the second derivatives are $\Omega_{xx} = 3/4$, $\Omega_{yy} = 9/4$, and $\Omega_{xy} = b = 3\sqrt3(1-2\mu)/4$.
Substituting $(\xi, \eta) \propto e^{\lambda t}$ and setting the determinant to zero gives characteristic equation $\lambda^4 + \lambda^2 + 27\mu(1-\mu)/4 = 0$.
Let $\lambda = i\omega$, giving $\omega^4 - \omega^2 + 27\mu(1-\mu)/4 = 0$ and

$$
\omega^2 = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{D}}{2}, \qquad D = 1 - 27\mu(1-\mu)
$$


For $D > 0$ both $\omega^2$ are positive, giving superposed fast and slow oscillations.
The smaller mass-ratio boundary where $D > 0$ is $\mu_R = (1-\sqrt{23/27})/2 \approx 0.03852$, Routh's value.
At $\mu = 0.01215$, slow period is about 21.07 and fast period 6.58, making Earth–Moon L4 linearly stable.
This is linear stability, not proof of permanent finite-amplitude confinement.

Excite only the slow mode using the eigenvector's real part as initial perturbation.
Substituting $e^{i\omega t}$ into the first equation gives $(-\omega^2 - 3/4)\,\xi = (2i\omega + b)\,\eta$, so with $c = -\omega^2 - 3/4$, $\eta = c\,(b - 2i\omega)\,\xi/(b^2 + 4\omega^2)$.
At $t = 0$, setting $\xi = A$ and $\dot\xi = 0$ gives $\eta(0) = A c b/(b^2+4\omega^2)$ and $\dot\eta(0) = 2A c\,\omega^2/(b^2+4\omega^2)$.
Here $A = 10^{-5}$ (`epsilon` in code) is perturbation amplitude.

For input mass ratio $\mu$, integrate this state to time 60 at tolerance $10^{-12}$, comparing mean same-direction crossing intervals of line $x = x_4$ with linear period $2\pi/\omega$.
Detect the section through nonterminal `Section(x4)` events and use successive `path.events()` times for periods.
Plot distance from L4 over time.
For inputs $D \le 0$, leave linear period undefined and observe perturbation growth.

```rust
//| id: app-cr3bp-l4
//| ref: app-cr3bp-l4
//| uses: [app-cr3bp-model]
//| title: "L4 perturbations and linear periods"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   mu: {"type":"range","label":"Mass ratio μ [-]","min":0.01,"max":0.05,"step":0.005,"value":0.02}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"unstable","label":"μ = 0.04","description":"Above Routh's threshold, linear period is undefined and distance from L4 grows.","inputs":{"mu":0.04}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"period","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":15.861084572979225,"atol":0.001}}
//|   - case: unstable
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"period","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"period","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":8.7,"max":9.0}}
let x = 0.5 - mu;
let y = 3.0_f64.sqrt() / 2.0;
let disc = 1.0 - 27.0 * mu * (1.0 - mu);
let frequency = if disc > 0.0 {
    Some(((1.0 - disc.sqrt()) / 2.0).sqrt())
} else {
    None
};
let epsilon = 1e-5;
let mut initial = [x + epsilon, y, 0.0, 0.0];
if let Some(w) = frequency {
    let b = 3.0 * 3.0_f64.sqrt() * (1.0 - 2.0 * mu) / 4.0;
    let c = -w * w - 0.75;
    let den = b * b + 4.0 * w * w;
    initial[1] += epsilon * c * b / den;
    initial[3] = epsilon * c * 2.0 * w * w / den;
}
let section = Section(x);
let events: [&dyn scioxide_ode::EventFunction<f64>; 1] = [&section];
let (path, report) =
    trajectory(mu, &initial, 60.0, 1e-12, &events).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let periods: Vec<_> = path
    .events()
    .windows(2)
    .map(|w| w[1].time() - w[0].time())
    .collect();
let measured = if periods.is_empty() {
    None
} else {
    Some(periods.iter().sum::<f64>() / periods.len() as f64)
};
let reference = frequency.map(|w| 2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI / w);
let points: Vec<_> = path
    .times()
    .iter()
    .zip(path.states())
    .map(|(&t, v)| [t, (v.as_slice()[0] - x).hypot(v.as_slice()[1] - y)])
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({"name":"Distance from L4","points":points})],
    options = &serde_json::json!({"xLabel":"Time [-]","yLabel":"Distance [-]"})
);
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Linear period [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Mean section-crossing interval [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Comparable periods", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(reference, measured, periods.len())],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "period" })
);
let outcome = if report.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Finite-time integration completed")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Integration stopped early")
};
emit_outcome!(outcome.status(format!("{:?}", report.termination())));
```

At default $\mu = 0.02$, linear period is about 15.8611; three-cycle mean intervals agree through the fifth decimal place.
Residual difference is about $8\times10^{-6}$; multiplying $A$ by ten roughly multiplies it by ten, suggesting nonlinear effects.
Distance oscillates on scale $A$ without amplitude growth over time.
Setting $\mu$ to 0.01 lengthens the period to about 23.4; 0.035 shortens it to 10.6.
$\mu = 0.04$ and 0.05 exceed $\mu_R$, giving complex characteristic roots $\alpha \pm i\beta$ with real parts $\alpha > 0$.
Distance grows as $e^{\alpha t}$; at $\mu = 0.04$, $\alpha \approx 0.068$ gives roughly 57-fold growth by time 60.
Its mean interval, about 8.9, is close to $2\pi/\beta \approx 8.85$ for the growing oscillation.
At $\mu = 0.05$, $\alpha \approx 0.18$ and the amplitude becomes large enough that linearization breaks down.

## Summary and further reading

Closest approaches are upward crossings of squared-distance derivative $g = \mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{rel}}\cdot\mathbf{v}$; timing accuracy is governed more by global error amplified by $1/\lvert dg/dt\rvert$ than localization width.
For small tabulated $dg/dt$, such as shallow minima or near-tangent entries, vary tolerance and maximum step to verify timing and event-count stability.
Jacobi drift independently indicates error but misses phase; combine it with refined timing comparisons.
When tighter tolerances leave step counts unchanged, maximum step limits accuracy; select missed-event safeguards separately from accuracy requirements.
Comparison with L1 value $C_{L1}$ determines open passages; check L4 periods against characteristic roots and section-crossing intervals.
