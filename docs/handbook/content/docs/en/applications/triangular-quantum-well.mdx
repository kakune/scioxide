A half-line potential proportional to position has quantum states expressible through Airy functions.
Solve the same eigenproblem two ways.
One computes eigenvalues of a symmetric tridiagonal finite difference matrix; the other computes Airy zeros.
Comparisons separate eigensolver error, differential-equation discretization error, and replacing an infinite interval with a finite one.
Use `scioxide-linalg` for tridiagonal eigenvalues, `scioxide-rootfind` for special-function zeros, and `scioxide-special` for Airy values.

## Model and nondimensionalization

A triangular well confines a particle between a high barrier and a restoring uniform electric or gravitational field.
A representative example is electrons near a GaAs/AlGaAs heterojunction.
Conduction-band bending confines them between the interface barrier and the electric-field slope, quantizing motion perpendicular to the interface.
Approximating this bending with a constant field gives the triangular well (Ando, Fowler and Stern, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 54, 1982, 437).
Another example is ultracold neutrons bouncing gravitationally above a horizontal mirror, the quantum bouncer.
Quantized height levels have been observed experimentally (Nesvizhevsky et al., *Nature* 415, 2002, 297).

Make the following assumptions.
Treat one particle of effective mass $m>0$, absorbing band-structure effects solely into that mass (effective-mass approximation).
The barrier is infinite and the wavefunction vanishes at interface $x=0$.
Actual GaAs/AlGaAs barriers are finite, a few hundred meV, permitting slight penetration.
Field $F$ is spatially constant; neglect the self-consistent Hartree potential from electron interactions.
Inputs are educational values, not fitted measurements of a particular device.

Place the particle in $x>0$ with potential $V(x)=qFx$.
Here $q>0$ is charge magnitude and $F>0$ field magnitude oriented toward increasing potential.
For neutrons, replace $qF$ with $mg$ (gravitational acceleration $g$).
The stationary Schrödinger equation and boundary conditions are

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''(x)+qFx\,\psi(x)=E\psi(x),
\qquad \psi(0)=0,\qquad \psi(x)\to0\quad(x\to\infty)
$$


Here $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant and $E$ is the energy eigenvalue.

Only two physical quantities appear: $\hbar^2/(2m)$ and $qF$.
Their dimensions are energy × length² and energy / length, allowing one length and one energy unit.
Set $x=\ell\xi$ and $E=E_0\lambda$ and substitute to obtain

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m\ell^2}\psi''(\xi)+qF\ell\,\xi\psi(\xi)=E_0\lambda\psi(\xi)
$$


Equating kinetic and potential coefficients through $\hbar^2/(2m\ell^2)=qF\ell$ gives units

$$
\ell=\left(\frac{\hbar^2}{2mqF}\right)^{1/3},\qquad E_0=qF\ell=\left(\frac{(qF\hbar)^2}{2m}\right)^{1/3}
$$


Dividing by $E_0$ gives the dimensionless eigenproblem

$$
-\psi''(\xi)+\xi\psi(\xi)=\lambda\psi(\xi),\qquad \psi(0)=0,\qquad \psi(\xi)\to0\quad(\xi\to\infty)
$$


Field and mass disappear, so compute dimensionless $\lambda_n$ once and convert any field/mass through $E_n=E_0\lambda_n$.
Since $E_0\propto F^{2/3}m^{-1/3}$, eigenenergies scale with field to power $2/3$ and effective mass to power $-1/3$.

Using common GaAs conduction-band mass $m=0.067m_e$ (electron mass $m_e$) and $F=10\ \mathrm{kV/cm}$ gives $\ell\approx8.28\ \mathrm{nm}$ and $E_0\approx8.28\ \mathrm{meV}$.
$10\ \mathrm{kV/cm}$ is slope $1\ \mathrm{meV}$ per $1\ \mathrm{nm}$, so at this field $\ell$ in nm and $E_0$ in meV share their numerical value.
For neutrons ($m\approx1.675\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$, $g=9.80665\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$), $\ell\approx5.87\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, ground energy is about $1.41\ \mathrm{peV}$, and classical turning height $\lambda_1\ell$ is about $13.7\ \mu\mathrm{m}$.

## Exact Airy solutions

Setting $s=\xi-\lambda$ turns the dimensionless equation into $\psi''=s\psi$.
This is Airy's equation $w''(s)=sw(s)$ ([DLMF 9.2.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.2)), with independent solutions $\operatorname{Ai}(s)$ and $\operatorname{Bi}(s)$.
As $s\to+\infty$, $\operatorname{Ai}(s)$ decays like $e^{-(2/3)s^{3/2}}$ while $\operatorname{Bi}(s)$ grows like $e^{(2/3)s^{3/2}}$ ([DLMF §9.7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.7)).
Thus only a constant multiple of $\psi(\xi)=\operatorname{Ai}(\xi-\lambda)$ vanishes as $\xi\to\infty$.
The remaining condition $\psi(0)=0$ becomes $\operatorname{Ai}(-\lambda)=0$.

All $\operatorname{Ai}$ zeros are simple and lie on the negative real axis ([DLMF §9.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.9)).
Writing them nearest the origin first as $a_1>a_2>\cdots$ (all negative), eigenvalues are

$$
\lambda_n=-a_n,\qquad \psi_n(\xi)=\operatorname{Ai}(\xi+a_n)\qquad(n=1,2,\ldots)
$$


The first three are $\lambda_1=2.338107410459767$, $\lambda_2=4.087949444130970$, and $\lambda_3=5.520559828095551$.
$\psi_n$ has $n-1$ nodes in $(0,\infty)$; $\xi=\lambda_n$ is its classical turning point.

Asymptotic expansions estimate zero locations.
DLMF 9.9.6 and 9.9.18 give

$$
a_n=-T\!\left(\tfrac{3\pi}{8}(4n-1)\right),\qquad
T(t)\sim t^{2/3}\left(1+\frac{5}{48}t^{-2}-\frac{5}{36}t^{-4}+\cdots\right)\qquad(t\to\infty)
$$


Its leading $\lambda_n\approx\left(\tfrac{3\pi}{2}(n-\tfrac14)\right)^{2/3}$ matches WKB quantization $\int_0^{\lambda}\sqrt{\lambda-\xi}\,d\xi=\tfrac23\lambda^{3/2}=\pi(n-\tfrac14)$.
The $\tfrac14$ in $n-\tfrac14$ combines hard-wall phase $\pi$ and smooth-turning-point phase $\pi/2$ corrections.
Leading-only absolute error is about $1.8\times10^{-2}$ at $n=1$, reduced to $1.5\times10^{-3}$ through $5/48$; at $n=3$ these fall to about $3.4\times10^{-3}$ and $2.6\times10^{-5}$.
Higher-index zeros have better asymptotics; for low levels in double precision, use the expansion only as a starting approximation and refine the original equation.

Exact solutions also give expectations used later in error estimates.
For Airy solution $w$, the antiderivative identity ([DLMF §9.11(iv)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.11))

$$
\int w^2\,ds=sw^2-w'^2,\qquad
\int s^2w^2\,ds=\tfrac15\left(s^3w^2-s^2w'^2+2sww'-w^2\right)
$$

This identity can be verified by differentiating its right-hand side and using $w''=sw$.
Integrating $w=\operatorname{Ai}$ from $s=a_n$ to $\infty$ and using $\operatorname{Ai}(a_n)=0$ and decay at infinity gives

$$
\int_{a_n}^{\infty}\operatorname{Ai}^2ds=\operatorname{Ai}'(a_n)^2,\qquad
\int_{a_n}^{\infty}s^2\operatorname{Ai}^2ds=\tfrac{a_n^2}{5}\operatorname{Ai}'(a_n)^2
$$


For normalized $\psi_n$, therefore, $\langle(\xi-\lambda_n)^2\rangle=\lambda_n^2/5$.
A similar computation gives $\langle\xi\rangle=2\lambda_n/3$.
For the GaAs ground state, mean electron position is $\tfrac23\lambda_1\ell\approx12.9\ \mathrm{nm}$ from the interface.

## Finite differences and two error sources

Truncate the infinite domain to $[0,L]$ with zero wavefunction at both ends.
Divide into $m$ intervals with $h=L/m$ and $\xi_j=jh$, using $\psi_j\approx\psi(\xi_j)$ at $N=m-1$ interior points as unknowns.
Replace the second derivative by central difference $\psi''(\xi_j)\approx(\psi_{j-1}-2\psi_j+\psi_{j+1})/h^2$ and substitute $\psi_0=\psi_m=0$, obtaining matrix eigenproblem $T\boldsymbol\psi=\lambda\boldsymbol\psi$.
$T$ is an $N\times N$ real symmetric tridiagonal matrix with diagonal $2/h^2+\xi_j$ and adjacent entries $-1/h^2$.
Its eigenvalues are real; denote the lowest ones in ascending order by $\lambda_1^{(h,L)}<\lambda_2^{(h,L)}<\cdots$.

Finite difference error has two causes.
One is discretization error at grid spacing $h$; the other is truncation error from a wall at $L$.

Taylor expansions estimate discretization error.
For smooth $\psi$, central differences satisfy

$$
\frac{\psi(\xi+h)-2\psi(\xi)+\psi(\xi-h)}{h^2}=\psi''(\xi)+\frac{h^2}{12}\psi''''(\xi)+O(h^4)
$$


The discrete operator approximates $-\psi''+\xi\psi-\tfrac{h^2}{12}\psi''''$; first-order perturbation by its extra term gives

$$
\lambda_n^{(h)}-\lambda_n\approx-\frac{h^2}{12}\,\frac{\langle\psi_n,\psi_n''''\rangle}{\langle\psi_n,\psi_n\rangle}
$$


Differentiating $\psi_n''=(\xi-\lambda_n)\psi_n$ twice gives $\psi_n''''=2\psi_n'+(\xi-\lambda_n)^2\psi_n$, with $\int\psi_n\psi_n'\,d\xi=\tfrac12[\psi_n^2]$ zero at both ends.
Using expectation $\langle(\xi-\lambda_n)^2\rangle=\lambda_n^2/5$ from the preceding section gives

$$
\lambda_n^{(h)}-\lambda_n\approx-\frac{\lambda_n^2}{60}\,h^2
$$


Discrete eigenvalues are too small, with errors proportional to $h^2$ and $\lambda_n^2$.
The ground-state coefficient is $\lambda_1^2/60\approx0.0911$.
Halving spacing reduces errors to approximately $1/4$ of their original size, marking second-order accuracy.
At the same $h$, higher levels have larger errors because their eigenfunctions oscillate faster and $\psi''''$ is larger.

Truncation error is independent of $h$ and remains as $h\to0$.
Solutions vanishing at both ends of $[0,L]$ have form $c_1\operatorname{Ai}(\xi-\lambda)+c_2\operatorname{Bi}(\xi-\lambda)$, with eigenvalues satisfying

$$
\operatorname{Ai}(-\lambda)\operatorname{Bi}(L-\lambda)-\operatorname{Bi}(-\lambda)\operatorname{Ai}(L-\lambda)=0
$$


Writing $\lambda=\lambda_n+\delta$ and linearizing as $\operatorname{Ai}(-\lambda)\approx-\delta\operatorname{Ai}'(a_n)$, use $\operatorname{Ai}'(a_n)=-1/(\pi\operatorname{Bi}(a_n))$ from Wronskian $\operatorname{Ai}\operatorname{Bi}'-\operatorname{Ai}'\operatorname{Bi}=1/\pi$ ([DLMF 9.2.7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.2)) to obtain

$$
\delta_n(L)\approx\pi\operatorname{Bi}(a_n)^2\,\frac{\operatorname{Ai}(L+a_n)}{\operatorname{Bi}(L+a_n)}
\approx\frac{\pi}{2}\operatorname{Bi}(a_n)^2\exp\!\left(-\tfrac43(L-\lambda_n)^{3/2}\right)
$$


the estimates above.
The second uses asymptotic $\operatorname{Ai}$ and $\operatorname{Bi}$ at $L-\lambda_n\gg1$ ([DLMF §9.7](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.7)).
Moving the wall inward strengthens confinement, so $\delta_n$ is positive.
Truncation error decreases exponentially in turning-point-to-wall distance $L-\lambda_n$ to power $3/2$.
At fixed $L$, higher levels have turning points closer to the wall and greater truncation effects.

The two errors have opposite signs and may cancel, appearing small.
Do not infer accuracy from a small difference alone; vary $L$ and $h$ independently.
First enlarge $L$ until low eigenvalues barely change, then vary $h$ to examine convergence order.

Eigenvalues at spacings $h$ and $h/2$ also cancel the $h^2$ term through Richardson extrapolation,

$$
\lambda_n^{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{4\lambda_n^{(h/2)}-\lambda_n^{(h)}}{3}=\lambda_n+O(h^4)
$$


It assumes errors of form $Ch^2+O(h^4)$, the same principle as [Romberg integration](../../scioxide-integrate/algorithms/romberg-extrapolation/).
Truncation error $\delta_n(L)$ persists after extrapolation because it does not depend on $h$.

## Eigenvalue and zero algorithms

Compute $T$ eigenvalues with the tridiagonal eigensolver in `scioxide-linalg`.
`HermitianEigenDecomposition::tridiagonal(&diagonal, &off_diagonal)` takes only diagonal entries (length $N$) and subdiagonal entries (length $N-1$), without forming a dense matrix.
Both inputs are borrowed slices; results own eigenvalues and eigenvectors.
The default full-spectrum path uses Wilkinson-shift implicit QL through order 32, then divide and conquer.
Storing every eigenvector requires $O(N^2)$ memory.

Only the lowest three eigenpairs are needed here.
Select ascending indices 0, 1, 2 through `HermitianEigenOptions::default().with_index_range(EigenIndexRange::new(0, 3)?)` and pass to `tridiagonal_with_options` for Sturm bisection eigenvalues and inverse-iteration eigenvectors.
Sturm recurrences count eigenvalues below a test value in $O(N)$ work, guiding repeated interval halving.
Memory is $O(Nk)$ for $k$ selected vectors, returned as an $N\times k$ matrix.
For eigenvalues alone, `HermitianEigenvaluesResult::tridiagonal_with_options` uses the same bisection with $O(N)$ memory.

Bisection resolves eigenvalues to working precision.
Backward-stable tridiagonal solvers have absolute eigenvalue errors of order $\varepsilon\lVert T\rVert$ (machine epsilon in double precision $\varepsilon\approx2.2\times10^{-16}$).
On the default fine grid, $\lVert T\rVert\approx4/h^2\approx4\times10^2$, giving error about $10^{-13}$, far below discretization error of order $10^{-3}$.
Tabulated differences therefore reflect discretization and truncation, not eigensolver error.
However, $\lVert T\rVert$ grows as $h^{-2}$ while extrapolation error shrinks as $h^4$, narrowing their separation under refinement.
At minimum input $h=0.025$ below, the $\varepsilon\lVert T\rVert$ estimate gives fine-grid rounding error about $10^{-11}$ versus ground-state extrapolation error about $1.9\times10^{-10}$, only a little over one order apart.

Eigenvector columns have 2-norm 1, with their largest-magnitude component positive.
Out-of-range selected indices produce `InvalidInput`, length mismatches `DimensionMismatch`, and non-finite entries `NonFinite`.
Iteration limits or inverse iteration missing its residual target produce `ConvergenceFailure`; loss of direction produces `NumericalInstability`.

Reference $a_n$ comes from `special_zeros(SpecialZeroFamily::<f64>::AiryAi, 3)` in `scioxide-rootfind`.
It returns negative $\operatorname{Ai}$ zeros indexed from 1 nearest the origin, returning $a_n$ itself (negative).
Initial approximations use tabulated values through zero 8 and the asymptotic expansion afterward.
Newton refinement stays within brackets separated by midpoints of adjacent initial approximations.
Out-of-bracket updates or failure to verify opposite signs around convergence trigger bisection-safeguarded refinement.
Function and derivative evaluations use Airy functions from `scioxide-special`.
Finally evaluate the original equation at the returned point, checking estimated error at or below the default absolute/relative tolerance $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ and rounding displacement below 0.45 times adjacent initial spacing.
Tolerance is an acceptance criterion; Newton's quadratic convergence actually refines near the rounding limit of double precision.
The first three values match DLMF tables within double-precision rounding error.
If any zero fails acceptance, convenience `special_zeros` returns an error without partial results.
Use `special_zeros_with_options` when partial results and termination states are needed.

Eigenfunction comparisons evaluate $\operatorname{Ai}(x)$ through `scioxide_special::airy_ai(x)`.
For real $\lvert x\rvert\le16$, use Taylor polynomials around integers; larger $\lvert x\rvert$ uses DLMF §9.7 asymptotics, with tested errors within 64 ulp.
Near zeros, tests assess absolute error rather than ulp relative error.
These references are finite-precision computations, not guaranteed enclosing intervals.

Both cells use the same finite difference matrix; share assembly and selection settings in a definitions block.
`airy_operator(L, m)` returns diagonal and subdiagonal arrays for $m$ subdivisions of $[0,L]$; `lowest_three()` returns settings for selecting the lowest three eigenpairs.

```rust
//| id: app-triangular-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Discrete Airy operator and selection of three low levels"
//| crates: [scioxide-linalg]
use scioxide_linalg::{EigenIndexRange, HermitianEigenOptions};

/// Divide [0, L] into m intervals; return the three diagonals of −D² + ξ at interior ξⱼ = jh (j = 1, …, m − 1).
pub(crate) fn airy_operator(extent: f64, intervals: usize) -> (Vec<f64>, Vec<f64>) {
    let h = extent / intervals as f64;
    // Diagonal 2/h² + ξⱼ, subdiagonal −1/h²; boundary values ψ₀ = ψₘ = 0 are excluded.
    let diagonal = (1..intervals)
        .map(|j| 2.0 / (h * h) + j as f64 * h)
        .collect();
    let off_diagonal = vec![-1.0 / (h * h); intervals - 2];
    (diagonal, off_diagonal)
}

/// Return Sturm-bisection settings selecting ascending eigenpair indices 0, 1, 2.
pub(crate) fn lowest_three() -> Result<HermitianEigenOptions<f64>, String> {
    let range = EigenIndexRange::new(0, 3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(HermitianEigenOptions::default().with_index_range(range))
}
```

## Convergence with grid spacing

Inputs are right endpoint $L$ and coarse interior point count $N$.
The coarse grid has $N+1$ intervals and $h=L/(N+1)$; the fine grid uses $2N+1$ interior points ($2N+2$ intervals), making spacing exactly $h/2$.
Compute the three lowest eigenvalues on both grids and compare them with reference values $-a_n$ from Airy zeros.
Columns show level $n$, reference, coarse/fine values, fine error, coarse/fine error ratio, and Richardson error.
All errors are absolute.
Ratios near 4 indicate dominant second-order discretization error.

Overlay the fine ground-state eigenvector with sampled $\operatorname{Ai}(\xi_j+a_1)$.
The vector's 2-norm is 1; multiply by $1/\sqrt{h}$ to give discrete $L^2$ norm $\bigl(h\sum_j\psi_j^2\bigr)^{1/2}$ equal to 1, normalizing Airy values the same way.
Choose signs for a positive inner product.
Export the difference's discrete $L^2$ norm and fine ground energy to the final unit-conversion cell.

```rust
//| id: app-triangular-well
//| ref: app-triangular-well
//| uses: [app-triangular-model]
//| title: "Comparing Airy zeros and finite difference eigenvalues"
//| run: button
//| cache: inputs
//| exports: [ground_energy, shape_error]
//| crates: [scioxide-linalg, scioxide-rootfind, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   extent: {"type":"range","label":"Right endpoint L [-]","min":8,"max":16,"step":1,"value":12}
//|   nodes: {"type":"range","label":"Interior point count N","min":30,"max":120,"step":10,"value":60}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"energies","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.338107410459767,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energies","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":3.8,"max":4.2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energies","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-5}}
use scioxide_linalg::HermitianEigenDecomposition;
use scioxide_rootfind::{SpecialZeroFamily, special_zeros};

let n = nodes as usize;
let options = lowest_three()?;
// Return three low eigenvalues and the ground-state eigenvector.
let solve = |intervals: usize| -> Result<(Vec<f64>, Vec<f64>), String> {
    let (main, off) = airy_operator(extent, intervals);
    let eigen = HermitianEigenDecomposition::tridiagonal_with_options(&main, &off, &options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Eigenvectors form a row-major (interior points × 3) matrix; column 0 is the ground state.
    let vectors = eigen.eigenvectors().as_slice();
    let ground = vectors.iter().step_by(3).copied().collect();
    Ok((eigen.eigenvalues().to_vec(), ground))
};
let zeros = special_zeros(SpecialZeroFamily::<f64>::AiryAi, 3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// N points give N + 1 intervals; 2N + 1 points give 2N + 2, exactly halving spacing.
let (coarse, _) = solve(n + 1)?;
let (fine, mut wave) = solve(2 * n + 2)?;
let rows: Vec<_> = (0..3)
    .map(|i| {
        let exact = -zeros[i];
        let error = (fine[i] - exact).abs();
        let ratio = (error > 0.0).then(|| (coarse[i] - exact).abs() / error);
        // Richardson (4λ(h/2) − λ(h))/3 cancels the h² term.
        let richardson = ((4.0 * fine[i] - coarse[i]) / 3.0 - exact).abs();
        (i + 1, exact, coarse[i], fine[i], error, ratio, richardson)
    })
    .collect();
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({"significantDigits":6});
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({"notation":"scientific","significantDigits":3});
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
    {"key":"0","label":"Level","type":"number","format":sig6,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"1","label":"Airy reference [-]","type":"number","format":sig6,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"2","label":"Coarse grid [-]","type":"number","format":sig6,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"3","label":"Fine grid [-]","type":"number","format":sig6,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"4","label":"Fine-grid error [-]","type":"number","format":sci3,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"5","label":"Error ratio [-]","type":"number","format":sig6,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"6","label":"Richardson error [-]","type":"number","format":sci3,"nullText":"Undefined"}
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({"id":"energies"})
);
let count = wave.len();
let h = extent / (count + 1) as f64;
// Independent check: normalize sampled Ai(ξⱼ + a₁) in the same discrete norm.
let mut reference: Vec<f64> = (1..=count)
    .map(|j| scioxide_special::airy_ai(j as f64 * h + zeros[0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string()))
    .collect::<Result<_, _>>()?;
let norm = (h * reference.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>()).sqrt();
for v in &mut reference {
    *v /= norm;
}
let sign = if wave.iter().zip(&reference).map(|(a, b)| a * b).sum::<f64>() < 0.0 {
    -1.0
} else {
    1.0
};
for v in &mut wave {
    *v *= sign / h.sqrt();
}
let shape_error = (h * wave
    .iter()
    .zip(&reference)
    .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).powi(2))
    .sum::<f64>())
.sqrt();
let points = |v: &[f64]| {
    v.iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(i, &y)| [(i + 1) as f64 * h, y])
        .collect::<Vec<_>>()
};
let dashed = serde_json::json!({"lineType":"dashed"});
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({"name":"Finite difference eigenfunction","points":points(&wave)}),
        serde_json::json!({"name":"Airy functions","points":points(&reference),"style":dashed})
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({"xLabel":"x / ℓ [-]","yLabel":"Normalized wavefunction [-]"})
);
export!(ground_energy, fine[0]);
export!(shape_error, shape_error);
emit_outcome!(
    Outcome::satisfied("Eigenvalue and Airy-zero computations completed")
        .explanation("Examine discretization and finite-domain errors through table differences and changes in L.")
);
```

Ratios about 4.00, 4.02, and 4.03 show dominant second-order discretization.
Coarse ground error about $3.53\times10^{-3}$ agrees with estimate $\lambda_1^2h^2/60\approx3.53\times10^{-3}$ to three digits.
All discrete eigenvalues are too small, matching the predicted sign.
Richardson errors are about $6.8\times10^{-7}$, $1.6\times10^{-5}$, and $4.3\times10^{-5}$, two to three orders smaller than fine-grid errors.
Ratios slightly above 4, increasingly so for higher levels, reflect omitted $O(h^4)$ contributions.
The two curves visually overlap, with difference discrete $L^2$ norm about $8.5\times10^{-4}$.

Increasing $N$ to 120 approximately halves $h$, reducing fine error to $1/4$ and ground extrapolation error to $4.7\times10^{-8}$ (about $1/14$).
Extrapolation error ratios approach theoretical 16 only once $h$ is sufficiently small.
Reducing $N$ to 30 moves ratios away from 4, to about 4.1 for level 3.

Reducing $L$ to 8 raises level 3's ratio to about 7 and its extrapolation error to $1.1\times10^{-3}$.
Its turning point $\xi=\lambda_3\approx5.52$ is near the wall, making truncation comparable to discretization.
Conversely, increasing $L$ to 16 at fixed $N$ coarsens spacing and increases discretization error.
Comparisons across $L$ need matched spacings.
The next cell fixes spacing and varies only $L$.

## Domain truncation and Richardson extrapolation

Input coarse spacing $h$; for each $L=4,5,\ldots,14$ compute $h$ and $h/2$ eigenvalues and tabulate signed Richardson error $\lambda_n^{\mathrm{R}}-\lambda_n$.
Set interval count $m$ to the nearest integer to $L/h$.
Extrapolation removes $h^2$ but retains spacing-independent truncation error $\delta_n(L)$, independent of $h$.
Prediction columns use first-order estimate $\pi\operatorname{Bi}(a_n)^2\operatorname{Ai}(L+a_n)/\operatorname{Bi}(L+a_n)$ from Airy values independently of finite differences.
No vectors are needed; compute only eigenvalues through `HermitianEigenvaluesResult`.

Plot errors and predictions logarithmically.
Omit nonpositive values and report their count in text.

```rust
//| id: app-triangular-truncation
//| ref: app-triangular-truncation
//| uses: [app-triangular-model]
//| title: "Domain truncation error and Richardson extrapolation"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-linalg, scioxide-rootfind, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   spacing: {"type":"range","label":"Coarse spacing h [-]","min":0.025,"max":0.1,"step":0.025,"value":0.05}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"truncation","path":"/rows/4/5","approx":{"value":1.104e-3,"rtol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"truncation","path":"/rows/4/6","approx":{"value":1.100e-3,"rtol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"truncation","path":"/rows/10/1","range":{"min":0.0,"max":1e-8}}
use scioxide_linalg::HermitianEigenvaluesResult;
use scioxide_rootfind::{SpecialZeroFamily, special_zeros};
use scioxide_special::{airy_ai, airy_bi};

let zeros = special_zeros(SpecialZeroFamily::<f64>::AiryAi, 3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = lowest_three()?;
// Sturm bisection for eigenvalues only, without forming eigenvectors.
let lowest = |extent: f64, intervals: usize| -> Result<Vec<f64>, String> {
    let (main, off) = airy_operator(extent, intervals);
    let eigen = HermitianEigenvaluesResult::tridiagonal_with_options(&main, &off, &options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(eigen.eigenvalues().to_vec())
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut observed = vec![Vec::new(); 3];
let mut predicted = vec![Vec::new(); 3];
let mut skipped = 0;
for extent in (4..=14).map(f64::from) {
    let intervals = (extent / spacing).round() as usize;
    let coarse = lowest(extent, intervals)?;
    let fine = lowest(extent, 2 * intervals)?;
    let mut row = vec![extent];
    for i in 0..3 {
        let a = zeros[i];
        let error = (4.0 * fine[i] - coarse[i]) / 3.0 + a;
        // Independent check: first-order wall-at-L perturbation πBi(aₙ)²Ai(L + aₙ)/Bi(L + aₙ).
        let bi = airy_bi(a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let tail = airy_ai(extent + a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            / airy_bi(extent + a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let shift = core::f64::consts::PI * bi * bi * tail;
        for (series, value) in [(&mut observed[i], error), (&mut predicted[i], shift)] {
            if value > 0.0 {
                series.push([extent, value]);
            } else {
                skipped += 1;
            }
        }
        row.extend([error, shift]);
    }
    rows.push(row);
}
let integer = serde_json::json!({"fractionDigits":0});
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({"notation":"scientific","significantDigits":3});
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
    {"key":"0","label":"Right endpoint L [-]","type":"number","format":integer,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"1","label":"Level 1 extrapolation error [-]","type":"number","format":sci3,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"2","label":"Level 1 prediction [-]","type":"number","format":sci3,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"3","label":"Level 2 extrapolation error [-]","type":"number","format":sci3,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"4","label":"Level 2 prediction [-]","type":"number","format":sci3,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"5","label":"Level 3 extrapolation error [-]","type":"number","format":sci3,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"6","label":"Level 3 prediction [-]","type":"number","format":sci3,"nullText":"Undefined"}
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({"id":"truncation"})
);
let symbols = serde_json::json!({"lineType":"dotted","showSymbol":true});
let series: Vec<_> = (0..3)
    .flat_map(|i| {
        [
            serde_json::json!({
                "name":format!("Level {} extrapolation error", i + 1),
                "points":observed[i],
                "style":symbols
            }),
            serde_json::json!({
                "name":format!("Level {} prediction", i + 1),
                "points":predicted[i],
                "style":{"lineType":"dashed"}
            }),
        ]
    })
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel":"Right endpoint L [-]",
        "yLabel":"Eigenvalue error [-]",
        "yType":"log",
        "yMin":1e-26,
        "yMax":10.0
    })
);
emit_text!(format!("Nonpositive points omitted from chart: {skipped}"));
```

At default $h=0.05$, errors and predictions overlap for small $L$ and fall rapidly with $L$.
For example, level 1 at $L=6$ gives about $2.7\times10^{-5}$ for both, and level 3 at $L=8$ about $1.1\times10^{-3}$.
As $L$ grows further, dashed predictions keep falling while extrapolation errors level off.
Floors for levels 1, 2, 3 are about $3.1\times10^{-9}$, $6.5\times10^{-8}$, and $1.8\times10^{-7}$: the residual $O(h^4)$ discretization error.
At $h$ of 0.1, floors rise to about $4.9\times10^{-8}$, $1.0\times10^{-6}$, and $2.8\times10^{-6}$, with ratios about 16 matching $h^4$ scaling.
At $h=0.075$, $L/h$ is noninteger, so actual spacing changes slightly with $L$ and the floors fluctuate.
Comparing bends in prediction and extrapolation curves shows how far to extend $L$ before grid error hides truncation.
At default $h$, even level 3 has truncation below the floor for $L\approx11$ or larger.

At $L=4$, level 3's turning point $\lambda_3\approx5.52$ lies outside the domain, invalidating the first-order estimate.
The prediction is negative, so this one point is omitted.
Actual extrapolation error is about 2.1; this state resembles a box-confined state more than an Airy level.

Fine-grid values without extrapolation can disguise truncation effects.
Negative discretization and positive truncation cancel there.
For level 1 at $L=6$ and $h=0.05$, fine error about $-2.9\times10^{-5}$ appears roughly half the predicted discretization $-5.7\times10^{-5}$.
Varying $L$ and $h$ independently separates these contributions.

## Conversion to physical units

The following cell receives fine-grid dimensionless ground energy and eigenfunction-difference $L^2$ norm from the convergence cell.
Field and mass do not change dimensionless eigenvalues; convert only $\ell$ and $E_0$ without resolving the eigenproblem.
Input fields in kV/cm and convert to SI V/m ($1\ \mathrm{kV/cm}=10^5\ \mathrm{V/m}$).
Using elementary charge for $q$ makes $E_0=qF\ell$ divided by $q$ directly an energy in eV.
Elementary charge and reduced Planck constant use SI defining values; electron mass uses [NIST CODATA](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?me).
Physical constant uncertainty is negligible at displayed precision.

```rust
//| id: app-triangular-physical
//| ref: app-triangular-physical
//| title: "Unit conversion from field and effective mass"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| dependsOn:
//|   eigen: app-triangular-well
//| inputs:
//|   field: {"type":"range","label":"Field [kV/cm]","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   mass_ratio: {"type":"range","label":"Effective mass / electron mass","min":0.03,"max":1,"step":0.001,"value":0.067}
let ground_energy = eigen["ground_energy"].as_f64().ok_or("Eigenvalue is missing")?;
let shape_error = eigen["shape_error"].as_f64().ok_or("Eigenfunction difference is missing")?;
// SI constants: kV/cm converts to 10⁵ V/m; J/e converts to eV.
let charge = 1.602176634e-19_f64;
let hbar = 1.054571817e-34_f64;
let mass = mass_ratio * 9.1093837139e-31;
let electric = field * 1e5;
let length = (hbar * hbar / (2.0 * mass * charge * electric)).cbrt();
let energy_mev = 1000.0 * electric * length;
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({"significantDigits":6});
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
    {"key":"0","label":"ℓ [nm]","type":"number","format":sig6,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"1","label":"Ground energy [meV]","type":"number","format":sig6,"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"2","label":"Eigenfunction L₂ difference [-]","type":"number","format":sig6,"nullText":"Undefined"}
    ]),
    vec![(length * 1e9, ground_energy * energy_mev, shape_error)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({"id":"physical"})
);
```

At defaults (GaAs, $10\ \mathrm{kV/cm}$), $\ell\approx8.28\ \mathrm{nm}$ and ground energy about $19.36\ \mathrm{meV}$.
The difference from exact $\lambda_1E_0\approx19.37\ \mathrm{meV}$ is about $0.007\ \mathrm{meV}$, the fine discretization error $8.8\times10^{-4}$ times $E_0$.
Physical accuracy follows dimensionless eigenvalue accuracy; conversion loses no digits.
Tenfold field multiplies $\ell$ by $10^{-1/3}\approx0.46$ and energy by $10^{2/3}\approx4.6$.
Increasing effective mass toward electron mass reduces both $\ell$ and $E_0$.
Eigenfunction $L^2$ differences are dimensionless and unchanged by physical inputs.

## Summary and further reading

Triangular-well eigenvalues are Airy zeros $-a_n$: compute dimensionless values once for all fields and masses, restoring physical units through $E_0\propto F^{2/3}m^{-1/3}$.
Central-difference eigenvalues are too small by about $\lambda_n^2h^2/60$; ratios near 4 under spacing halving verify second-order accuracy.
Finite-wall errors are positive and decrease as $\exp(-\tfrac43(L-\lambda_n)^{3/2})$, so choose $L$ by distance from turning point $\lambda_n$ to the wall.
Opposite-sign errors can cancel; vary $L$ and $h$ independently, reading truncation where Richardson errors decrease with $h^4$.
For only a few low levels, select indices through `with_index_range` and use bisection/inverse iteration with $O(Nk)$ memory.
