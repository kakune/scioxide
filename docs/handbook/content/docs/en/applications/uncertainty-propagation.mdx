Supplying parameters as distributions rather than point estimates also produces variability in the output.
This page assigns independent uniform distributions to transmission rate, recovery rate, and initial infectious fraction, separating their contributions to the SIR final size.
These distributions are assumptions for this example, rather than posterior distributions estimated from observations.
A closed form for final size using the Lambert W function lets us check ODE integration and sampling errors separately.

## Problem setup and input distributions

Assume a closed, homogeneous population without births, deaths, migration, or reinfection.
Write the susceptible, infectious, and recovered fractions (the removed compartment on the SEIR page) as $(s,i,r)$, measure time in days, and use

$$
s'=-\beta si,\qquad i'=\beta si-\gamma i,\qquad r'=\gamma i
$$


The notation matches [SEIR forward sensitivities](../epidemiology-seir/), with the latent compartment omitted.
The right-hand sides sum to zero, preserving $s+i+r=1$.
Initially the recovered fraction is zero, the infectious fraction $i_0>0$, and the susceptible fraction $s_0=1-i_0$.
Nondimensionalizing time as $\tau=\gamma t$ gives $ds/d\tau=-R_0si$ and $di/d\tau=(R_0s-1)i$, where $R_0=\beta/\gamma$ is the basic reproduction number.
Thus final size depends only on $R_0$ and $i_0$, and $\beta$ and $\gamma$ interact through their ratio.

Assume mutually independent inputs $X=(\beta,\gamma,i_0)$ uniformly distributed over the following intervals.

| Input | Interval | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $\beta$ [day⁻¹] | $[0.3,0.6]$ | Contact transmission rate |
| $\gamma$ [day⁻¹] | $[0.1,0.2]$ | Recovery rate (infectious period 5–10 days) |
| $i_0$ | $[0.001,0.01]$ | Initial infectious fraction |

Since $R_0$ ranges from 1.5 to 6, every sample produces an epidemic.
The output is the final recovered fraction $z=r_\infty$, the fraction ever infected by the end of the epidemic.

## Independent final-size verification

While $i>0$, $r'>0$, allowing $r$ to serve as the independent variable.
Dividing $s'$ by $r'$ gives $ds/dr=-R_0s$, and $r(0)=0$ gives $s=s_0\exp(-R_0r)$.
Since $i\to0$ at $t\to\infty$, we have $s_\infty+r_\infty=1$, and $z$ satisfies

$$
1-z=s_0\exp(-R_0z)
$$


The function $F(z)=1-z-s_0e^{-R_0z}$ is concave with $F(0)=i_0>0$ and $F(1)<0$, so it has exactly one root in $(0,1)$.
Setting $u=-R_0(1-z)$ gives $ue^u=-R_0s_0e^{-R_0}$, so the final recovered fraction is

$$
z=r_\infty=1+\frac{W_0(-R_0s_0e^{-R_0})}{R_0}
$$


Here $W_0$ is the principal branch of Lambert W, with $W_0\ge-1$ (DLMF §4.13).
Since $s_\infty=-W/R_0$, the principal branch corresponds to the root whose final effective reproduction number $R_0s_\infty$ is at most 1.
The other real branch $W_{-1}$ gives a negative root of $F$, which has no meaning as a fraction.
Do not omit $s_0$ when varying the initial infectious fraction.
Also distinguish the infection-free solution $z=0$ with exactly $i_0=0$ (the $W_{-1}$ side) from the limit $1-z=e^{-R_0z}$ introducing a small infection (the $W_0$ side).

The function `scioxide_special::lambert_w0` computes $W_0$ using Halley iteration, with error within 64 ulp over the tested range `f64` ([Evaluating the real branches](oxref:special-lambert-real-branches)).
The relative condition number of $W_0$ is $1/(1+W_0)$, growing in the limits $R_0\to1$ and $i_0\to0$ as the argument approaches the branch point $-1/e$.
Over this input range, the worst case is $R_0=1.5$, $i_0=0.001$, with condition number only about 2.7.

A conserved quantity also gives the peak height.
Integrating $di/ds=-1+1/(R_0s)$ shows that $i+s-\log s/R_0$ is conserved; at $s=1/R_0$ where $i'=0$, this gives

$$
i_{\max}=1-\frac{1+\log(R_0s_0)}{R_0}
$$


## ODE integration and the peak event

To follow the same procedure as for models without a closed form, the main sensitivity estimation uses the ODE terminal value $r(400)$ as output.
The method `DormandPrince5` is a seven-stage explicit Runge–Kutta embedded 5(4) pair, using the difference between its two solutions to estimate local truncation error ([Local error estimation with embedded pairs](oxref:ode-embedded-pairs)).
FSAL reduces right-hand-side evaluations to six per step from the second step onward.

The arguments of `ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-9, 1e-9, NormSelection::Infinity)` are absolute tolerance, relative tolerance, then norm.
A step is accepted if $\lvert e_k\rvert\le10^{-9}+10^{-9}\max(\lvert y_{n,k}\rvert,\lvert y_{n+1,k}\rvert)$ for every component ([Error normalization and acceptance](oxref:ode-error-normalization)).
This controls local error and does not guarantee global error.

After the peak, $i$ decays approximately exponentially at rate $\gamma(1-R_0s_\infty)$.
This rate is smallest at the $\beta=0.3$, $\gamma=0.2$ endpoint, about 0.075 day⁻¹, so $i$ becomes sufficiently small by day 400.
As $R_0$ approaches 1, this rate approaches zero, and the difference between $r(400)$ and $r_\infty$ exceeds integration error.

The peak is found from a positive-to-negative crossing (`EventDirection::Falling`) of the event function $g=\beta s-\gamma$.
Since $i'=i\,g$, the sign change in $g$ corresponds to a maximum of $i$.
The detector checks signs on accepted-step dense output and finds a root with TOMS 748 in an interval with a sign change ([Root finding on a continuous extension](oxref:ode-event-localization)).
The default search checks only step endpoints and can miss two roots inside one step; this model has only one root because $R_0s$ decreases monotonically.

Terminal-value error is of order $10^{-9}$, far below the output standard deviation 0.073.
By comparison, designs with a few dozen rows have sampling errors of order 0.1.
Under this page's settings, sampling error determines the sensitivity-index error.

## Sobol indices

For independent inputs, output has a unique decomposition into mutually orthogonal components $z=f_0+\sum_jf_j(X_j)+\sum_{j<k}f_{jk}(X_j,X_k)+f_{123}$ (Sobol or ANOVA decomposition).
Orthogonality also decomposes the variance as $V=\sum_jV_j+\sum_{j<k}V_{jk}+V_{123}$.
The first-order index $S_j=\mathrm{Var}(\mathbb E[z\mid X_j])/\mathrm{Var}(z)=V_j/V$ represents the contribution of input $X_j$ alone.
The total-effect index $S_{Tj}$ also includes interactions involving that input and is written as

$$
S_{Tj}=\frac{\mathbb E[\mathrm{Var}(z\mid X_{\sim j})]}{V}=1-\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\mathbb E[z\mid X_{\sim j}])}{V}
$$


Here $X_{\sim j}$ denotes inputs other than $X_j$; replacing an input with small $S_{Tj}$ by a constant barely changes output variance.
This decomposition assumes independent inputs.
Finite-sample estimates can fall outside 0 to 1 and are not clipped for display.

Integrating the closed form with a product Gauss–Legendre rule of 20 points per axis gives the following accurate values (32 points leaves the displayed digits unchanged).

| Input | $S_j$ | $S_{Tj}$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\beta$ | 0.498 | 0.605 |
| $\gamma$ | 0.395 | 0.502 |
| $i_0$ | $2.0\times10^{-5}$ | $4.6\times10^{-5}$ |

The mean is approximately 0.91823 and the variance approximately 0.0052778.
The $S_{Tj}-S_j$ values for $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are both about 0.107, measuring interaction through the ratio $\beta/\gamma$.
Since $i_0$ changes $s_0$ by only about 1%, it contributes almost nothing.

## Pick-freeze estimators and evaluation counts

Prepare independent $N\times d$ sample matrices $A$ and $B$; let $A_B^{(j)}$ be $A$ with column $j$ replaced by column $j$ of $B$ ([Pick-freeze estimation](oxref:stats-ishigami-pick-freeze)).
The pair $f(A_B^{(j)})$ and $f(B)$ shares only $X_j$, so the expectation of their product minus $f_0^2$ equals $V_j$.
The pair $f(A_B^{(j)})$ and $f(A)$ shares all inputs except $X_j$, so half the expected squared difference equals $\mathbb E[\mathrm{Var}(z\mid X_{\sim j})]$.
Writing the mean of the $2N$ outputs from $f(A)$ and $f(B)$ as $m$ and their variance as $\hat V$, scioxide-stats uses

$$
\hat S_j=\frac1{N\hat V}\sum_{k=1}^N\bigl(f(B)_k-m\bigr)\bigl(f(A_B^{(j)})_k-f(A)_k\bigr),\qquad
\hat S_{Tj}=\frac1{2N\hat V}\sum_{k=1}^N\bigl(f(A)_k-f(A_B^{(j)})_k\bigr)^2
$$


The first-order estimator is Saltelli et al.'s (2010), and the total-effect estimator Jansen's (1999), with bias $O(1/N)$.
The evaluation count is $N(d+2)$, or $5N$ for this problem.
scioxide-stats provides first-order and total-effect indices but not second-order indices $S_{jk}$.

For independent rows, neglecting fluctuations in $\hat V$ gives the approximation $\mathrm{SE}(\hat S_j)\approx\sigma_j/\sqrt N$.
Here $\sigma_j$ is the standard deviation of a summand divided by $V$.
An independent computation with $10^5$ rows estimates $\sigma_\beta\approx1.3$ and $\sigma_\gamma\approx1.1$, giving first-order-index SE of 0.13–0.16 for $N=64$.
For $i_0$, the total-effect summand has standard deviation about $1.9\times10^{-4}$, 50 times smaller than the first-order summand's approximately $9.7\times10^{-3}$.
Total-effect indices are better suited to identifying inputs with small contributions.

## Monte Carlo and randomized quasi-Monte Carlo error

Monte Carlo error with independent random numbers decreases as $O(N^{-1/2})$.
Quasi-Monte Carlo uses low-discrepancy sequences; the Koksma–Hlawka inequality bounds error by $V_{\mathrm{HK}}(f)\,D_N^*$ ([Discrepancy and the Koksma–Hlawka inequality](oxref:random-qmc-koksma-hlawka)).
Here $V_{\mathrm{HK}}$ is Hardy–Krause variation and $D_N^*$ is star discrepancy; for a $D$-dimensional Sobol sequence, the discrepancy is $O((\log N)^D/N)$.
The pick-freeze design forms rows of $A$ and $B$ from the first and second halves of one point, so $D=2d=6$; at $N=64$, $(\log N)^6/N\approx81$, making the bound uninformative.

The type `QmcReplicatePlan` therefore applies independent Matoušek linear scrambles to the Sobol sequence $R$ times, measuring error from variability across replicates.
Each randomized point is uniform on $[0,1)^6$; writing the estimate from independent replicate $r$ as $\hat S^{(r)}$, it returns

$$
\bar S=\frac1R\sum_{r=1}^R\hat S^{(r)},\qquad
\mathrm{SE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{R(R-1)}\sum_{r=1}^R\bigl(\hat S^{(r)}-\bar S\bigr)^2}
$$

these quantities ([Preparing and estimating with QmcReplicatePlan](oxref:random-sobol-replicate-standard-error)).
SE is an empirical estimate rather than an upper bound.
At $R=8$ there are seven degrees of freedom, so a 95% interval uses the $t$-distribution quantile 2.36.
For smooth integrands, scrambled-net variance decreases as $O(N^{-3}(\log N)^{D-1})$ (Owen 1997; Owen 2003 shows that linear scrambling has the same variance).
This describes the large-$N$ limit; by default each replicate has only eight points, so the difference from MC is small.
Net equidistribution applies to the first $2^m$ points, so `QmcReplicatePlan::new` returns `InvalidInput` if a Sobol sequence is given a point count that is not a power of two.
This is why the cell's $N$ choices are 32, 64, and 128.

The mean and variance table uses a single three-dimensional Sobol sequence shifted only by `QmcScramble::DigitalShift(seed)`.
Without replicates there is no empirical SE; since all coordinate shifts come from one seed, their mutual independence is not guaranteed either.

## API flow

The function `closed(p)` returns the closed-form $z$ from `p = [β, γ, i₀]`, conveying `DomainError` from `lambert_w0` through `?`.
The function `solve(p)` creates the right-hand side with component order $(s,i,r)$ using `OdeFn::new(3, …)`, and creates a problem borrowing the right-hand side and events with `OdeProblem::new(&model, 400.0).with_events(&events)`.
The function `advance` advances `OdeState` in place, and the output sink `OdeTrajectory` records the terminal state and events under the default `FinalOnly` schedule.
It returns `Ok` even at a limit or minimum step, so we check `report.is_success()` (`ReachedEnd` or `TerminalEvent`), treating other statuses and an undetected peak as errors.

The method `sobol_indices_monte_carlo(3, n, &root, sampler, model)` creates a row-$k$ generator with `root.split(k)`, alternately calling `sampler(j, rng)` for $A$ and $B$ for each coordinate.
The object `root` itself does not advance, so `reference` evaluating the closed form with the same `root` uses the same points as ODE estimation, making their difference reflect only integration error.
The returned `evaluations()` holds all outputs; for MC with independent rows, it can be passed to `sobol_bootstrap` to construct percentile intervals.

The constructor `QmcReplicatePlan::new(&QmcPlan::sobol(6)?, &root, options)` randomizes a six-dimensional Sobol sequence (Joe–Kuo direction numbers) eight times.
The `points` of `sobol_indices_qmc(&plan, 3, n / 8, transform, model)` must equal the point count per replicate, and sequence dimension must be $2d$; otherwise it gives `InvalidInput`.
The first three coordinates form a row of $A$ and the last three a row of $B$; `transform(j, u)` maps unit-interval values to inputs.
Sobol sequences have better projections in earlier coordinates, so we place $\beta$, expected to contribute strongly, first.
The method `randomized_standard_error()` returns `Some` only for RQMC estimates.
For either estimator, NaN or infinite model output gives `NonFinite`, while zero output variance gives `NumericalInstability`.

MC and RQMC both use $5N$ evaluations.
The default $N=64$ requires 707 ODE solves (320 MC, 320 RQMC, 64 for the mean table's Sobol sequence, and three representative points), accounting for most of the computation time.
The mean table's MC column reaggregates the design's $f(A)$ with `OnlineMoments` and needs no additional solves.

## Reading the tables

In the first table, “MC” uses ODE terminal values, while “Closed-form MC” estimates from the closed form at the same points.
With the default inputs ($N=64$, seed 7), first-order indices for $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are about 0.45 and 0.21 with MC, and about $0.62\pm0.10$ and $0.39\pm0.10$ with RQMC.
Differences from the accurate values remain within 1.4 estimated SE for MC and about 1.3 SE for RQMC.
The RQMC total effect for $\gamma$ is about $0.84\pm0.21$, 1.6 SE from the accurate value, which lies inside its $\pm2.36\,\mathrm{SE}$ interval.
The first-order index for $i_0$ is of order $\pm0.004$ with an uncertain sign, but its total effect is below $10^{-4}$, indicating a small contribution.
The difference between “MC” and “Closed-form MC” is of order $10^{-9}$; if it becomes comparable to SE, reconsider the integration tolerances or interval.

In the second table, final sizes at $R_0=1.5$, 3, and 6 are about 0.584, 0.941, and 0.998, with peaks about 0.064, 0.302, and 0.536.
Terminal values differ from the closed form by order $3\times10^{-10}$, and peak differences are at most $10^{-9}$, consistent with the local error tolerance.

The mean and variance table shows results as sample size increases through $N/4$, $N/2$, and $N$.
At $N=64$, the MC mean is about 0.903 (SE about 0.010), 1.5 SE from the accurate value 0.91823, while the Sobol mean error is below $10^{-4}$.
Even the 1024-point closed-form value differs from the accurate value by order $4\times10^{-5}$ and cannot be treated as an exact expectation.
The MC mean SE uses an independent-sample formula; do not reuse it for a Sobol sequence.

Increasing the design row count need not decrease error monotonically for a particular seed.
At $N=128$ with seed 7, the Sobol mean error exceeds that at $N=64$.
At $N=32$, each RQMC replicate has four points and larger SE; the MC total effect for $\gamma$ is about 1.09, exceeding 1.
A small first-order index combined with large standard error is not evidence of no contribution.
Samples are insufficient if changing the seed reverses index rankings or a total effect is clearly smaller than its first-order index.

## Summary and further reading

For this SIR final size, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ explain almost all variance, with interaction about 0.11 and the contribution of $i_0$ below $10^{-4}$.
Evaluating the closed form $z=1+W_0(-R_0s_0e^{-R_0})/R_0$ at the same points separates ODE and sampling errors.
Pick-freeze estimation uses $N(d+2)$ evaluations; at $N=64$ the first-order-index SE remains of order 0.1, so change seeds and check SE before ranking inputs.
Choose a power-of-two point count per RQMC replicate and use a $t$-distribution quantile with $R-1$ degrees of freedom for SE-based intervals.
