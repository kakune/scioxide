Computations such as interpolating before integration or solving an ODE before parameter estimation combine multiple crates.
Tightly converging a later computation does not remove input or discretization errors from an earlier stage.
Choose tolerances and computation count limits by considering how errors at each stage affect the final quantity.

When connecting model computations, inverse problems, and independent checks, inspect units, domains, and diagnostics at each boundary.


---

## Error propagation through first-order sensitivity analysis

Let the outer quantity of interest or observable be $Q = G(u, p)$, where $p$ denotes design parameters and $u = S(p)$ is the state vector obtained by an inner solver (such as an ODE solver or a solver for a system of equations).
If numerical error $\delta u$ enters the inner computation, the resulting error $\delta Q$ in the final observable $Q$ is expressed by the first-order terms of a Taylor expansion as follows.

$$
\delta Q \approx G_u \delta u + (G_p + G_u S_p) \delta p.
$$

Here, $G_u = \partial G/\partial u$, $G_p = \partial G/\partial p$, and $S_p = \partial S/\partial p$ are the sensitivities to the state, to the parameters, and of the inner solution, respectively.
$\delta u$ represents the error of the inner computation at fixed parameters, distinct from the state change $S_p\delta p$ caused by a parameter perturbation $\delta p$.
The inner computation's error is amplified or attenuated according to the observation sensitivity $G_u$.

- **Observing a difference**: The absolute sensitivity of $G(u)=u_1-u_2$ is constant, but its relative error grows when the difference is small.
  Do not confuse absolute and relative sensitivity.
- **Observing a mean**: Positive and negative local errors may cancel.
  This does not necessarily reduce a bias acting in the same direction.

## Appropriate tolerance allocation and fixed error floors

Designing a combined computational pipeline requires identifying the bottleneck that dominates total error.
With an outer observation and modeling error floor $e_{\mathrm{model}}$, spatial and temporal discretization error $e_h$, and observation sensitivity $A$, a model for the final error bound is expressed as follows.

$$
e_{\mathrm{total}} \le e_{\mathrm{model}} + e_h + A e_{\mathrm{in}}.
$$

Here $e_{\mathrm{in}}$ is the state error of the inner computation, and $A$ is a coefficient assessing error amplification into the observable.
To guarantee the inequality, each term must bound its corresponding error, and $A$ must bound sensitivity over the range in question.
A sum with estimated terms is an error estimate, not necessarily a rigorous upper bound.
Converting an iteration's residual tolerance to state error also requires information such as a condition number.

The following cell models the sum of nondimensionalized error contributions.
Its four inputs are sensitivity, discretization error, modeling and observation error, and inner state error.
These are not solver measurements.
The chart shows the combined upper bound as inner error decreases; the table shows contributions and their proportions at the selected point.
Once the inner contribution is smaller than the others, improvements in the curve slow down.

```rust
//| id: concept-composed-error-budget
//| title: "Varying outer sensitivity, discretization error, and observation floors to identify the dominant error in a combined pipeline"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   amplification_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Outer sensitivity exponent a (A=10ᵃ)","description":"Amplification sensitivity transmitting inner error into the final quantity.","min":0,"max":6,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   discretization_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Discretization error exponent h (eₕ=10ʰ)","description":"Discretization error caused by spatial grids or time steps.","min":-10,"max":-2,"step":1,"value":-5}
//|   floor_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Modeling and observation floor exponent f (e_model=10ᶠ)","description":"The error floor of the measuring instrument or theoretical model itself.","min":-10,"max":-2,"step":1,"value":-4}
//|   inner_error_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Inner state error exponent i (eᵢₙ=10ⁱ)","description":"An upper bound or estimate of inner state error, distinct from a residual tolerance.","min":-14,"max":-2,"step":1,"value":-8}
// A first-order upper-bound model adding each error contribution, not measured solver error.
let amplification = 10.0_f64.powf(amplification_exponent);
let discretization = 10.0_f64.powf(discretization_exponent);
let observation = 10.0_f64.powf(floor_exponent);
let inner = 10.0_f64.powf(inner_error_exponent);
let floor = observation + discretization;
let propagated = amplification * inner;
let total = floor + propagated;
let mut total_curve = Vec::new();
let mut inner_curve = Vec::new();
for i in 0..=144 {
    let inner_error = 10.0_f64.powf(-14.0 + i as f64 / 12.0);
    let contribution = amplification * inner_error;
    total_curve.push([inner_error, floor + contribution]);
    inner_curve.push([inner_error, contribution]);
}
let series = vec![
    serde_json::json!({"name":"Error bound model", "points":total_curve}),
    serde_json::json!({"name":"Inner error contribution", "points":inner_curve, "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}}),

"annotations": [{"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": floor, "label": "Combined modeling and discretization error"},
{"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": floor / amplification, "label": "Balance floor/A"},
{"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": inner, "label": "Selected eᵢₙ", "style": {"lineType": "dotted"}}]})
);

    ("Modeling and observation", observation),
    ("Discretization", discretization),
    ("Inner error × sensitivity", propagated),
]
.into_iter()

    let comparison = if value == largest {

    } else {

    };

.collect();

emit_table_with_columns!(
    [
        serde_json::json!({"key":"name", "label":"Contribution", "type":"string"}),
        serde_json::json!({"key":"value", "label":"Value [-]", "type":"number", "format":sci3}),
        serde_json::json!({"key":"share", "label":"Proportion [%]", "type":"number"}),
        serde_json::json!({"key":"comparison", "label":"Comparison", "type":"string"})
    ],
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({"id":"contributions"})
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
    {"key":"0","label":"Combined upper-bound model [-]","type":"number","format":{"significantDigits":6},"nullText":"Undefined"},
    {"key":"1","label":"eᵢₙ at equal inner and other contributions [-]","type":"number","format":{"significantDigits":6},"nullText":"Undefined"}

```

While the inner contribution is large, reducing that error also reduces the combined upper bound.
Once it is sufficiently smaller than the others, improving only the inner computation barely changes the upper bound.
This curve is an error upper-bound model; it does not describe cancellation from error signs or an actual lower bound.

## Managing nested computation count limits (`SolveScope`) and allocating tolerances

For nested solves, such as solving an ODE inside optimization, `SolveScope` can aggregate work counts.
A child solve constructed through `SolveScope::from(&mut context)` inherits its parent's deadline, cancellation, and executor, and also has local limits.
Merely calling ordinary convenience functions does not automatically combine every computation into one set of counts.

- **Isolating local iteration allowances**: Limits on iterations and proposals (`BudgetKind::Iteration` / `Proposal`) are local to each level.
  Even if the inner solver performs 50 iterations, it does not consume the parent's own iteration (`own()`) allowance.
  Child work appears as nested counts (`nested()`) in the parent's accounting, with totals available through `total()`.
- Shared limits, such as evaluation or sample counts, also count child work toward outer solves, including the parent.
  Admission checks prevent partially consuming allowances when any applicable limit would be exceeded.
- **Independent transactions**: Acceptance of child solver progress does not advance the parent solver's state on its own; each level independently maintains its transaction.

See the [`scioxide-core` index of solve identities and states](oxref:core-api-solve-state) for related types.

## Typical crate connection patterns and boundary contracts

The following summarizes boundary contracts to observe in representative combined pipelines.

### 1. Interpolation $\to$ numerical quadrature $\to$ root finding (inverse estimation of material properties)

Construct a continuous spline curve from a discrete material-property table with `scioxide-interp`, integrate it with `scioxide-integrate` to obtain cumulative heat, and use `scioxide-rootfind` to solve for the temperature at which the target heat is reached.

- **Boundary contract**: Strictly reject extrapolation beyond the domain $[T_{\min}, T_{\max}]$ guaranteed by spline interpolation.
  Where the derivative $E'(T) = c(T)$ of cumulative heat $E(T) = \int_{T_0}^T c(T')\,dT'$ (the specific heat integrand itself) is small, small quadrature errors cause large errors in the solution temperature $T$, so include inverse-sensitivity weighting in the root-finding stopping criterion.

### 2. Ordinary differential equations $\to$ fast Fourier transforms (oscillation spectrum analysis)

Solve nonlinear oscillations with an adaptive time-step solver in `scioxide-ode` and analyze the resulting displacement signal in frequency using `scioxide-fft`.

- **Boundary contract**: Internal step points of adaptive ODE solvers are not equally spaced.
  Do not pass such a sequence directly to an FFT.
  For example, reconstruct equally spaced samples using dense output (continuous interpolated output), choose a sampling interval $\Delta t$ accounting for the Nyquist frequency, and apply a window (Hann / Blackman) as needed before the FFT.

### 3. Automatic differentiation $\to$ mathematical optimization (parameter identification and inverse problems)

Compute objective derivatives with `scioxide-ad` and pass them to the quasi-Newton method (BFGS) in `scioxide-optimize`.
Automatic differentiation has no truncation error from finite difference steps, but model discretization error and rounding error remain.

- **Boundary contract**: Verify that variable component ordering in forward-mode (dual number) and backward-mode (reverse-mode) automatic differentiation matches the indices of the optimization parameter vector.
  If local nonsmooth points occur (absolute values or conditional branches), state the subgradient policy explicitly.

## Summary and further reading

When connecting crates, carry units, domains, and error meanings through the pipeline along with values.
Distinguish residual tolerances from state errors, and use sensitivity to estimate their contributions to the final quantity.
Compare error contributions and work at each stage to decide whether to improve the inner or outer computation.
Choose specific tools from the [crate directory](../../crates/), consult [Solve lifecycle and computation count management](../../scioxide-core/algorithms/solve-lifecycle-and-accounting/) for accounting details, and see [Nested solves, accounting, and diagnostics](../../scioxide-core/algorithms/nested-solves-and-diagnostics/) for rules counting inner solves toward parent limits.
