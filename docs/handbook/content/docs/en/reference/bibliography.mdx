This page introduces sources for the book's formulas and algorithms by field.
Consult them for detailed assumptions concerning regularity, convexity, stability regions, domains, and other conditions.

Each description identifies the theory covered and the corresponding crates.

## Floating-point numbers, error analysis, and numerical stability

- **IEEE Computer Society**, [*IEEE Standard for Floating-Point Arithmetic (IEEE Std 754-2019)*](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/754/6210/): The international standard defining binary and decimal floating-point formats, rounding modes (nearest, downward, upward, toward zero), subnormal numbers, and exceptional values (`NaN`, $\pm\infty$).
  It is the foundational reference for computer arithmetic.
- **Nicholas J. Higham**, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2002: Develops a unified theory of forward error, backward error, and condition numbers.
  It proves backward stability results for summation algorithms (including Kahan summation), polynomial evaluation, linear systems, least squares, and eigenvalue computation, and provides design criteria for `scioxide-core` and `scioxide-linalg`.
- **David Goldberg**, [*What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic*](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E19957-01/806-3568/ncg_goldberg.html), ACM Computing Surveys, Vol. 23, No. 1, pp. 5–48, 1991: A classic exposition of floating-point rounding error, guard digits, and cancellation mechanisms from a computer scientist's perspective.

## Numerical linear algebra, matrix factorizations, and sparse Krylov iteration

- **Gene H. Golub and Charles F. Van Loan**, *Matrix Computations*, 4th ed., Johns Hopkins University Press, 2013: A standard numerical linear algebra textbook covering LU factorization with partial pivoting, Householder QR, singular value decomposition (SVD), and eigenvalue computations for real symmetric and general matrices through orthogonal transformations and backward stability.
  It is a direct source for the factorization algorithms in `scioxide-linalg`.
- **Lloyd N. Trefethen and David Bau III**, *Numerical Linear Algebra*, SIAM, 1997: A textbook centered on geometric intuition and singular value decomposition, with lectures on projectors, orthogonalization, QR factorization, condition numbers, and GMRES.
- **Yousef Saad**, *Iterative Methods for Sparse Linear Systems*, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2003: A standard sparse solver reference covering conjugate gradients (CG), minimum residual (MINRES), generalized minimum residual (GMRES), and preconditioning (incomplete Cholesky, ILU(0), polynomial preconditioning).
  It provides the theoretical foundations of the Krylov implementations in `scioxide-sparse` and `scioxide-linalg`.

## Special functions, polynomial approximation, and function transforms

- **NIST**, [*Digital Library of Mathematical Functions (DLMF)*](https://dlmf.nist.gov/): A standard reference compiling definitions, asymptotic expansions, continued fractions, and complex analytic continuations for gamma and incomplete gamma functions, Bessel and Airy functions, complete elliptic integrals, hypergeometric functions, and others.
  It underlies accuracy assessment and branch selection in `scioxide-special`.
- **Lloyd N. Trefethen**, *Approximation Theory and Approximation Practice*, SIAM, 2013: A textbook combining theory and numerical experiments on avoiding the Runge phenomenon, Chebyshev expansions, the second barycentric interpolation formula, and best polynomial approximation.
  It corresponds to barycentric interpolation implementations in `scioxide-poly` and `scioxide-interp`.
- **Carl de Boor**, *A Practical Guide to Splines*, rev.
  ed., Springer, 2001: A standard text systematizing spline theory, including piecewise polynomial splines, local B-spline bases, de Boor's algorithm, and the theory and algorithms of knot placement.
- **James W. Cooley and John W. Tukey**, *An Algorithm for the Machine Calculation of Complex Fourier Series*, Mathematics of Computation, Vol. 19, No. 90, pp. 297–301, 1965: The original paper on the divide-and-conquer fast Fourier transform (FFT), the foundational algorithm of `scioxide-fft`.

## Interpolation, rational approximation, and smoothing

- Yuji Nakatsukasa, Olivier Sète, Lloyd N. Trefethen, [The AAA Algorithm for Rational Approximation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00337).
  The source for the barycentric representation, greedy support point selection, SVD, and doublets in [rational approximation](../../scioxide-interp/algorithms/rational-approximation/).
- Michael S. Floater, Kai Hormann, [Barycentric rational interpolation with no poles and high rates of approximation](https://www.inf.usi.ch/hormann/papers/Floater.2007.BRI.pdf), 2007.
  Covers blending local polynomials without poles on the real axis and conditions on approximation order.
- Paul H. C. Eilers, Brian D. Marx, [Flexible Smoothing with B-splines and Penalties](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/courses/stat527/s13/readings/EilersMarx_StatSci_1996.pdf), 1996.
  Provides the background for [penalties based on coefficient differences](../../scioxide-interp/algorithms/smoothing-and-parametric-splines/).
- F. N. Fritsch, R. E. Carlson, [Monotone Piecewise Cubic Interpolation](https://doi.org/10.1137/0717021), 1980.
  Covers monotonicity conditions for [piecewise cubic interpolation](../../scioxide-interp/algorithms/pchip-and-splines/).
- Richard Franke, [Scattered Data Interpolation: Tests of Some Methods](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1982-0637296-4), 1982.
  The source of the test function used in [RBF cross-validation](../../scioxide-interp/examples/station-cross-validation/).
- W. G. Spitzer, D. Kleinman, D. Walsh, [Infrared Properties of Hexagonal Silicon Carbide](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.113.127), Phys. Rev. 113, 127, 1959.
  Provides constants of the 6H-SiC Lorentz model used in [resonance poles](../../scioxide-interp/examples/aaa-resonance-poles/), for the electric field component perpendicular to the c axis, fitted to reflection spectra.

## Pseudorandom numbers, Monte Carlo methods, and descriptive statistics

- **Pierre L'Ecuyer**, *Uniform Random Number Generation*, Annals of Operations Research, Vol. 53, pp. 77–142, 1994: Discusses the theoretical framework for multidimensional lattice structures of pseudorandom generators, spectral tests, and generation of independent streams.
- **Ilya M. Sobol'**, *On the distribution of points in a cube and the approximate evaluation of integrals*, USSR Computational Mathematics and Mathematical Physics, Vol. 7, No. 4, pp. 86–112, 1967: The original paper on low-discrepancy quasi-random sequences (Sobol sequences), providing the theoretical basis of $O(1/N)$ convergence in quasi-Monte Carlo integration.
- **B. P. Welford**, *Note on a Method for Calculating Corrected Sums of Squares and Products*, Technometrics, Vol. 4, No. 3, pp. 419–420, 1962: The original paper on Welford's algorithm, which incrementally updates sample variance and covariance in one pass without cancellation.
  It is the foundation of `scioxide_stats::OnlineMoments`.
- **Rob J. Hyndman and Yanan Fan**, *Sample Quantiles in Statistical Packages*, The American Statistician, Vol. 50, No. 4, pp. 361–365, 1996: Systematically classifies and organizes nine different conventions for empirical quantile estimators.
  It underlies `scioxide_stats::QuantileMethod` (variants corresponding to R types 1–9).

## Automatic differentiation, nonlinear root finding, numerical quadrature, and mathematical optimization

- **Andreas Griewank and Andrea Walther**, *Evaluating Derivatives: Principles and Techniques of Algorithmic Differentiation*, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2008: A standard automatic differentiation reference discussing the algebraic and graph-theoretic foundations of forward mode (dual numbers) and reverse mode (adjoint calculations and recorded operations), and the computational complexity of higher derivatives.
  It underlies the design of `scioxide-ad`.
- **Andreas Griewank and Andrea Walther**, *Algorithm 799: revolve: An Implementation of Checkpointing for the Reverse or Adjoint Mode of Computational Differentiation*, ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software, Vol. 26, No. 1, pp. 19–45, 2000: The original work on binomial checkpointing that minimizes forward steps when traversing a sequence of equal-cost steps backward with a fixed number of storage slots.
  It is the source for `scioxide_ad::CheckpointSchedule::binomial`.
- **Andrea Walther**, *Computing Sparse Hessians with Automatic Differentiation*, ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software, Vol. 34, No. 1, Article 3, 2008: A method for obtaining Hessian structural patterns from regions of nonlinear interaction.
  It is the source for `scioxide_ad::Linearization::hessian_pattern`.
- **Assefaw H. Gebremedhin, Fredrik Manne and Alex Pothen**, *What Color Is Your Jacobian?
  Graph Coloring for Computing Derivatives*, SIAM Review, Vol. 47, No. 4, pp. 629–705, 2005: A survey of compressing and recovering sparse Jacobians and Hessians through coloring.
  It provides background for `scioxide_sparse::derivative_coloring` and sparse derivative recovery.
- **Mike Giles and Paul Glasserman**, *Smoking Adjoints: Fast Monte Carlo Greeks*, Risk, Vol. 19, No. 1, pp. 88–92, 2006: A method using adjoints to compute pathwise Monte Carlo derivatives and estimate price sensitivities.
  It is a typical reverse-mode application of `scioxide_ad::BatchPlan`.
- **Pierre Blanchard, Desmond J. Higham and Nicholas J. Higham**, *Accurately Computing the Log-Sum-Exp and Softmax Functions*, IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis, Vol. 41, No. 4, pp. 2311–2330, 2021: Error analysis of log-sum-exp and softmax shifted by their maximum.
  It underlies the accuracy of `scioxide_ad::logsumexp` and `softmax_into`.
- **Richard P. Brent**, *Algorithms for Minimization without Derivatives*, Prentice-Hall, 1973: The original source of Brent's root-finding method and one-variable optimization method, combining the reliability of bisection with the superlinear convergence of inverse quadratic interpolation.
  It is the source for `scioxide_rootfind::Brent`.
- **Hidetosi Takahasi and Masatake Mori**, *Double Exponential Formulas for Numerical Integration*, Publications of the Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences, Vol. 9, No. 3, pp. 721–741, 1974: The original paper establishing rapidly convergent quadrature through a double-exponential change of variables (DE / tanh-sinh formulas) for integrals with endpoint singularities or infinite intervals.
  It underlies `scioxide_integrate::DoubleExponentialIntegrator`.
- **Jorge Nocedal and Stephen J. Wright**, *Numerical Optimization*, 2nd ed., Springer, 2006: A standard mathematical optimization textbook systematizing line searches (Wolfe conditions), trust-region methods, quasi-Newton methods (BFGS / L-BFGS), sequential quadratic programming (SQP), and interior-point methods, including stopping conditions and proofs of global convergence.
  It is the source for the solvers in `scioxide-optimize`.

## Optimization experiments and reference data

- Stephen Boyd et al., [Distributed Optimization and Statistical Learning via the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/admm_distr_stats.html), 2011.
  Consult it for ADMM primal and dual residuals and LASSO variable splitting.
- Laszlo Hars, [Numerical Solutions of the Thomson Problems](https://www.hars.us/).
  Provides reference energies for small point counts and computations for reproducing them.
- David J. Wales et al., the Cambridge Cluster Database [Thomson problems](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/~wales/CCD/Thomson/table.html) and [Lennard-Jones clusters](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/~wales/CCD/jon/structures/LJ/tables.html).
  Compare reference values for structure searches separately from analytical proofs.
- QAPLIB, [problem and solution listings](https://coral.ise.lehigh.edu/data-sets/qaplib/qaplib-problem-instances-and-solutions/).
  Consult it for the correspondence between quadratic assignment matrices, optima, and permutations.

## Ordinary and partial differential equations

- **Ernst Hairer, Syvert P. Nørsett, and Gerhard Wanner**, *Solving Ordinary Differential Equations I: Nonstiff Problems*, 2nd ed., Springer, 1993: A standard reference on nonstiff ODEs, detailing explicit Runge–Kutta methods (DOPRI5, DOP853), adaptive step controllers (PI controllers), dense output interpolation, and the algebraic theory of order conditions.
  It directly informs the design of explicit methods in `scioxide-ode`.
- **Ernst Hairer and Gerhard Wanner**, *Solving Ordinary Differential Equations II: Stiff and Differential-Algebraic Problems*, 2nd ed., Springer, 1996: A classic on stiff problems covering implicit Runge–Kutta methods (Radau IIA, SDIRK), backward differentiation formulas (BDF), A-stability and L-stability, and differential-algebraic equation (DAE) solvers.
- **Randall J. LeVeque**, *Finite Difference Methods for Ordinary and Partial Differential Equations: Steady-State and Time-Dependent Problems*, SIAM, 2007: A standard PDE textbook explaining finite difference discretization of elliptic boundary value problems, the implicit Crank–Nicolson method for heat equations, von Neumann stability analysis, and the Lax equivalence theorem (consistency + stability $\iff$ convergence).
  It informs finite difference scheme design in `scioxide-pde`.
- **Alan M. Turing**, *The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis*, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London.
  Series B, Biological Sciences, Vol. 237, No. 641, pp. 37–72, 1952: The historic original paper on morphogenesis proposing diffusion-driven instability (the Turing mechanism) in reaction–diffusion systems.
  It is the source for [Application: Turing instability in reaction–diffusion systems](../../applications/reaction-diffusion/).

## Polynomial conventions and calibration formulas

- [NIST DLMF §18.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.3) and [§18.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.9): Weights, normalizations, three-term recurrences, and differentiation formulas for classical orthogonal polynomials in the poly chapter.
- [NIST Monograph 175](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.MONO.175), Tables 9.3.1 and 9.3.3: Coefficients of the Type T thermocouple reference function in the poly chapter and electromotive forces used for independent checks.

## Numerical differentiation, computational geometry, and interval arithmetic

- B. Fornberg, “Generation of finite difference formulas on arbitrarily spaced grids”, *Mathematics of Computation* 51(184), 1988, 699–706.
  The source of finite difference weights for nonequidistant nodes in `scioxide-differentiate`.
- C. J. F. Ridders, “Accurate computation of F′(x) and F′(x)F″(x)”, *Advances in Engineering Software* 4(2), 1982, 75–76.
  A method that extrapolates while reducing the step size to estimate derivative errors.
- W. Squire and G. Trapp, “Using complex variables to estimate derivatives of real functions”, *SIAM Review* 40(1), 1998, 110–112.
  The definition and applicability conditions of complex-step differentiation.
- J. R. Shewchuk, “Adaptive precision floating-point arithmetic and fast robust geometric predicates”, *Discrete & Computational Geometry* 18, 1997.
  Adaptive-precision geometric predicates used for orientation and inside/outside tests in `scioxide-spatial`.
- W. Kabsch, “A solution for the best rotation to relate two sets of vectors”, *Acta Crystallographica* A32, 1976.
  A method for computing the least-squares rotation when aligning point sets.
- R. E. Moore, R. B. Kearfott and M. J. Cloud, *Introduction to Interval Analysis*, SIAM, 2009.
  Background for interval arithmetic, inclusion, and existence and uniqueness of zeros in `scioxide-validated`.
- S. M. Rump, “Verification methods: Rigorous results using floating-point arithmetic”, *Acta Numerica* 19, 2010, 287–449.
  Differences between ordinary floating-point and verified computation, and enclosure methods for linear systems and other problems.

## Statistical estimation and application models

- B. Efron and R. J. Tibshirani, *An Introduction to the Bootstrap*, Chapman & Hall, 1993.
  Background for resampling and confidence intervals in `scioxide-stats`.
- P. McCullagh and J. A. Nelder, *Generalized Linear Models*, 2nd ed., Chapman & Hall, 1989.
  Regression link functions, likelihoods, and uncertainty in estimation.
- A. Saltelli et al., “Variance based sensitivity analysis of model output”, *Computer Physics Communications* 181(2), 2010, 259–270.
  Estimation of first-order indices used in [global sensitivity of SIR](../../applications/uncertainty-propagation/).
- [NASA/JPL, Three-Body Periodic Orbits](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/tools/periodic_orbits.html).
  An introduction to coordinate systems, the Jacobi constant, and periodic orbits in the [restricted three-body problem](../../applications/orbital-mechanics/).
- [NIST DLMF §9.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.2) and [§9.9](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.9).
  The Airy equation and its zeros for the [triangular well](../../applications/triangular-quantum-well/).
- [NIST Fundamental Physical Constants](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/).
  Charge, Planck's constant, and electron mass for converting the triangular well to physical units.

## Summary and further reading

When reading sources, check the smoothness, precision, and arithmetic environment assumed by the theory.
Each algorithm page and API guide in this book points to the specific sources for the method and the implementation's applicability conditions.
Use the [master public API index](../master-api-index/) to navigate from an identifier to its explanation.
