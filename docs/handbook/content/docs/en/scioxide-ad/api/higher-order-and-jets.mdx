This table covers derivatives of order two and above and operators applying derivatives without forming matrices.
Hessian–vector products use dual-number arithmetic for reverse-mode gradients (forward over reverse, recorded on `Tape<Dual<f64>>`), costing a small constant multiple of a gradient.
Sparse Hessian and Jacobian recovery uses methods of `Tape` and `Linearization` (`hessian_pattern`, `sparse_hessian_into`, `sparse_jacobian_into`); see the `Tape` and `Linearization` table rows and [Structural patterns and sparse derivative recovery](../../algorithms/sparse-derivatives/).
Item-name links point to the version 0.1.0 API reference on docs.rs.

## Hessians and their action

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [`hvp`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-ad/0.1.0/scioxide_ad/fn.hvp.html) | Function | One-shot Hessian–vector product $H v$ | Records on `Tape<Dual<f64>>` with seed `Dual(x_i, v_i)`; one reverse sweep gives the gradient in the value part and $Hv$ in the derivative part. Creates a new tape and allocates the result, executing the function twice (one statement-counting pass and one recording). Values match `hessian` bit for bit; the pattern is valid at points taking the same branches. NaN or infinity in stored derivatives gives `NonFinite` regardless of `NonFinitePolicy`. ## Taylor coefficients

| Item | Kind | Role | Key specifications | Discussion | Runnable example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [`Jet`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-ad/0.1.0/scioxide_ad/struct.Jet.html) | Struct | Normalized Taylor coefficient tuple $c_k = \frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^k x}{dt^k}$ | `new`, `variable`, `coefficients`, `coefficient` (`None` for `k ≥ N`). Checks `N ≥ 1` at compile time. Nonlinear operations cost $O(N^2)$ arithmetic operations, linear ones $O(N)$. $c_0$ matches `V` arithmetic bit for bit. Accuracy at restricted points is within $64\varepsilon(k+1)g$ plus the smallest subnormal number (through order 8). `atan2` and `combine` (including `implicit_solution_into`) are exact through first order; `exprel`, `sinc`, `sincpi`, and `map_scalar` through second order; higher coefficients are NaN. Allocates inputs. ## Summary

Obtain Hessian action $Hv$ with `hvp` or `Tape<Dual<f64>>::hvp_into` at a small constant multiple of gradient cost.
Dense Hessian `hessian` records once per direction; for large dimensions, use actions or sparse-structure coloring.
Pass `HessianOperator` and `JacobianOperator` to iterative solvers to avoid allocation per application.
Use `Jet` and `jet` for higher coefficients along one line, remembering that coefficients above total order two become NaN through operations such as `map_scalar`.
