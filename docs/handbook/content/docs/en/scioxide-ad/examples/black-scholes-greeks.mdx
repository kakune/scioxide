Partial derivatives describing how an option price responds to market variables are called Greeks.
The first derivative Δ and second derivative Γ with respect to the underlying price describe the amount of the underlying needed for hedging and how readily that amount changes.
Derivatives ν, Θ, and ρ with respect to volatility, time, and interest rate describe the price sensitivity to each variable.

This example writes the Black–Scholes price of a European call once as a generic `R: Real` function, computes first-order Greeks with forward-mode `MultiDual` and second-order Greeks with `hessian`, and checks them against closed forms and put–call parity.
The standard normal cumulative distribution function (CDF) $\Phi$ in the price is incorporated with scioxide-special values and user-supplied derivative rules.
[Monte Carlo Greeks by pathwise differentiation](../black-scholes-monte-carlo/) estimates derivatives of the same price along Monte Carlo paths, using this page's closed forms for verification.

## The Black–Scholes formula

Assume that the underlying price follows geometric Brownian motion with constant volatility $\sigma$, the risk-free rate $r$ is constant, there are no dividends, and exercise is allowed only at expiration (a European option).
Call and put prices with strike $K$ and time to expiration $T$ are given by the following formulas.

$$
C = S\,\Phi(d_1) - K e^{-rT}\Phi(d_2),\quad
P = K e^{-rT}\Phi(-d_2) - S\,\Phi(-d_1),\quad
d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r + \sigma^2/2)\,T}{\sigma\sqrt T},\quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt T
$$

The units are currency for $S, K, C, P$, years for $T$, a continuously compounded annual rate (1/year) for $r$, and annual volatility (1/√year, with dimensionless $\sigma^2 T$) for $\sigma$.

Put–call parity $C - P = S - K e^{-rT}$ follows from an arbitrage argument independently of the model; in these formulas, it follows from $\Phi(x) + \Phi(-x) = 1$.

## Greeks and the sign of Θ

The Greeks as partial derivatives with respect to inputs $z = [S, \sigma, T, r]$ and their call closed forms are listed below, where $\varphi$ is the standard normal density.

| Greek | Definition | Call closed form |
|---|---|---|
| Δ | $\partial C/\partial S$ | $\Phi(d_1)$ |
| Γ | $\partial^2 C/\partial S^2$ | $\varphi(d_1)/(S\sigma\sqrt T)$ |
| ν | $\partial C/\partial\sigma$ | $S\varphi(d_1)\sqrt T$ |
| Θ | $-\partial C/\partial T$ | $-S\varphi(d_1)\sigma/(2\sqrt T) - rKe^{-rT}\Phi(d_2)$ |
| ρ | $\partial C/\partial r$ | $KTe^{-rT}\Phi(d_2)$ |
| vanna | $\partial^2 C/\partial S\,\partial\sigma$ | $-\varphi(d_1)\,d_2/\sigma$ |
| volga | $\partial^2 C/\partial\sigma^2$ | $S\varphi(d_1)\sqrt T\,d_1 d_2/\sigma$ |

The first-order closed forms simplify because $S\varphi(d_1) = K e^{-rT}\varphi(d_2)$ cancels the terms containing derivatives of $d_1, d_2$.
For example, $\partial C/\partial S = \Phi(d_1) + S\varphi(d_1)\,\partial_S d_1 - K e^{-rT}\varphi(d_2)\,\partial_S d_2$, and since $\partial_S d_1 = \partial_S d_2$, the final two terms cancel.

The sign of Θ follows a convention.
Θ is the rate of change $\partial V/\partial t$ with calendar time $t$; with fixed expiration date $T_{\mathrm{exp}}$, the remaining time is $T = T_{\mathrm{exp}} - t$, giving $\Theta = -\partial V/\partial T$.
Since the AD input is remaining time $T$, negate the third gradient component $\partial C/\partial T$ to obtain Θ.
Θ here is per year; divide by 365 (or the number of trading days per year) for a daily value.
Similarly, ν and ρ are rates of change for a unit change (100 percentage points) in $\sigma$ and $r$; divide by 100 for values per percentage point.

## Incorporating Φ with `try_map_scalar`

The elementary functions in `Real` include exponentials, logarithms, trigonometric and hyperbolic functions, softplus, and sigmoid, but no error function or normal CDF.
Use `Real::try_map_scalar` to supply a scalar function as a triple of its value and first and second derivatives $[f(x), f'(x), f''(x)]$.
For $\Phi$, return $[\Phi(x), \varphi(x), -x\varphi(x)]$.
`Dual`, `MultiDual`, `Var`, `Var<Dual>`, and `Dual<Dual>` correctly assemble derivatives through total order two from this triple.
Deeper components (such as third- and higher-order coefficients in `Jet`) become NaN, so this function cannot compute third derivatives.
The rule is called once per lane (one SIMD element of `Lanes`) and must be deterministic.
`try_map_scalar` returns the first error from the rule unchanged ([Derivative rules for scalar functions](../../algorithms/custom-primitives/)).

scioxide-special's `normal_cdf` supplies the value.
This function computes each tail of $\Phi(x) = \tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(-x/\sqrt2)$ directly and has an accuracy guarantee of at most 8 ulp error for `f64` on $-38 \le x \le 38$.
Nonfinite arguments return `NonFinite`, which propagates from the pricing function to its caller.
Compute $\varphi$ with `RealScalar::exp` from `scioxide_core`.

AD combines this rule with ln, √, exp, and products through the chain rule, assembling the Greeks without the closed-form cancellations.
The closed-form check uses the same `normal_cdf` to compute $\Phi(d_1)$ and $\Phi(d_2)$ for the table's formulas, so both share the values of $\Phi$.
The comparison therefore checks chain-rule assembly and derivative rules, rather than the accuracy of $\Phi$ itself.

## Forward-mode `MultiDual` and Hessians

Compute first-order Greeks with forward-mode `MultiDual<f64, 4>`.
`MultiDual<V, N>` stores a value $v$ and $N$ directional derivatives $(\dot v_0, \dots, \dot v_{N-1})$.
Passing the input array $z = [S, \sigma, T, r]$ to `MultiDual::<f64, 4>::variables(z)` makes entry $k$ an independent variable with value $z_k$ and only tangent $k$ equal to 1; after one function evaluation, `gradient()` returns the four partial derivatives.
`variables` cannot fail because the array length is fixed at $N$; `MultiDual::variable(z_k, k)`, used to seed only selected directions, returns `OutOfBounds` if the direction index is at least $N$.
Each component matches four separate directional evaluations with `Dual` bit for bit, while the value is computed only once.
Forward mode is cheaper for a few inputs; beyond roughly four inputs, reverse mode becomes cheaper ([Forward mode and dual numbers](../../algorithms/forward-mode/)).

Compute second-order Greeks with `hessian`.
`hessian` records the function on `Var<Dual<f64>>` once per unit direction and returns a row-major $4\times 4$ matrix ([Second derivatives and Hessian operators](../../algorithms/second-order-derivatives/)).
A new tape executes once more for the first direction to count statements, giving five function executions.
Γ, vanna, and volga are its $(S,S)$, $(S,\sigma)$, and $(\sigma,\sigma)$ entries.
The pricing function returns `Result`, so pass the closure directly as `|x| price(x, strike, 1.0)`.

The following block defines the model shared by the two cells.
`normal_pdf` corresponds to $\varphi$, `normal_cdf` to $\Phi$ with its derivative rule, and `price` to the price $V = s\,[S\Phi(s d_1) - Ke^{-rT}\Phi(s d_2)]$ with sign $s$ ($s = 1$ for a call, $s = -1$ for a put).

```rust
//| id: ad-bs-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Black–Scholes prices and the normal CDF"
//| crates: [scioxide-ad, scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_ad::Real;
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};

/// Standard normal density φ(x) = exp(−x²/2)/√(2π).
pub(crate) fn normal_pdf(x: f64) -> f64 {
    RealScalar::exp(-0.5 * x * x) / RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI)
}

/// Φ(x). Values from scioxide-special's normal_cdf; derivative rules Φ′ = φ and Φ″ = −xφ.
pub(crate) fn normal_cdf<R: Real>(x: R) -> Result<R, SciOxideError> {
    x.try_map_scalar(|v| {
        let density = normal_pdf(v);
        Ok([scioxide_special::normal_cdf(v)?, density, -v * density])
    })
}

/// V = s·[S Φ(s d₁) − K e^{−rT} Φ(s d₂)] (s = 1 for a call, s = −1 for a put). z = [S, σ, T, r].
pub(crate) fn price<R: Real>(z: &[R], strike: f64, sign: f64) -> Result<R, SciOxideError> {
    let (spot, vol, maturity, rate) = (z[0], z[1], z[2], z[3]);
    let vol_sqrt_t = vol * maturity.sqrt();
    let d1 = ((spot / strike).ln() + (rate + vol * vol * 0.5) * maturity) / vol_sqrt_t;
    let d2 = d1 - vol_sqrt_t;
    let discounted_strike = (-(rate * maturity)).exp() * strike;
    let value = spot * normal_cdf(d1 * sign)? - discounted_strike * normal_cdf(d2 * sign)?;
    Ok(value * sign)
}
```

## Comparing Greeks

The following cell takes $S$ and $K$ (50–150 currency units), $\sigma$ (0.05–0.8 /√year), $T$ (0.05–3 years), and $r$ (0–0.1 /year), then compares the call price and seven Greeks from AD with their closed forms.
The relative difference is $|\mathrm{AD} - \text{closed form}|/|\text{closed form}|$; if the closed form is zero, the relative difference is displayed as “Undefined”.
The closed-form ln, exp, and √ are reference values computed with `f64` standard functions.
This cell passes the inputs and AD call values (price and five Greeks) to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: ad-bs-greeks
//| ref: ad-bs-greeks
//| title: "Differentiate the call price with MultiDual and hessian and compare Greeks with closed forms"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [ad-bs-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-ad, scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
//| exports: [market, call]
//| inputs:
//|   spot: {"type":"range","label":"Underlying price S [currency]","min":50,"max":150,"step":1,"value":100}
//|   strike: {"type":"range","label":"Strike K [currency]","min":50,"max":150,"step":1,"value":100}
//|   vol: {"type":"range","label":"Volatility σ [1/√year]","description":"Standard deviation of annual logarithmic returns.","min":0.05,"max":0.8,"step":0.01,"value":0.2}
//|   maturity: {"type":"range","label":"Time to expiration T [years]","min":0.05,"max":3,"step":0.05,"value":1}
//|   rate: {"type":"range","label":"Interest rate r [1/year]","description":"Continuously compounded risk-free rate.","min":0,"max":0.1,"step":0.005,"value":0.05}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"deep-in","label":"S = 130, K = 90, σ = 0.35, T = 0.25, r = 0.03","description":"Deep in the money, Δ approaches 1.","inputs":{"spot":130,"strike":90,"vol":0.35,"maturity":0.25,"rate":0.03}}
//|   - {"id":"forward-like","label":"S = 150, K = 50, σ = 0.05, T = 0.05","description":"d₁ is large, and Γ and ν round to 0.","inputs":{"spot":150,"strike":50,"vol":0.05,"maturity":0.05}}
//|   - {"id":"long-volatile","label":"σ = 0.8, T = 3, r = 0","description":"d₁ and d₂ have opposite signs, making volga negative.","inputs":{"vol":0.8,"maturity":3,"rate":0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":10.450583572185565,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.6368306511756191,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/4/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/5/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/7/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: deep-in
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.9871806700826756,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: forward-like
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":-2.49375780599365,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/5/2","approx":{"value":2.49375780599365,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|   - case: long-volatile
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/6/2","approx":{"value":0.2717753238096447,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"greeks","path":"/rows/7/2","approx":{"value":-32.61303885715736,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_ad::{MultiDual, hessian};

let z = [spot, vol, maturity, rate];
// Forward mode: give the four inputs unit tangents and obtain the gradient in one evaluation.
let point = MultiDual::<f64, 4>::variables(z);
let call = price(&point, strike, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let [delta, vega, dc_dt, rho] = call.gradient();
// Second order: row-major 4×4 Hessian (one recording per unit direction).
let h = hessian(|x| price(x, strike, 1.0), &z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let h = h.as_slice();

// Independent check: closed-form Greeks (Φ from scioxide-special; ln, exp, and √ are f64 reference values).
let n = |x: f64| scioxide_special::normal_cdf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string());
let st = maturity.sqrt();
let d1 = ((spot / strike).ln() + (rate + 0.5 * vol * vol) * maturity) / (vol * st);
let d2 = d1 - vol * st;
let disc = (-rate * maturity).exp();
let pdf = normal_pdf(d1);
let closed = [
    spot * n(d1)? - strike * disc * n(d2)?,
    n(d1)?,
    pdf / (spot * vol * st),
    spot * pdf * st,
    -spot * pdf * vol / (2.0 * st) - rate * strike * disc * n(d2)?,
    strike * maturity * disc * n(d2)?,
    -pdf * d2 / vol,
    spot * pdf * st * d1 * d2 / vol,
];
// Θ = −∂C/∂T (negate the derivative with respect to remaining time T).
let ad = [call.value(), delta, h[0], vega, -dc_dt, rho, h[1], h[5]];
let names = [
    ("Price C", "Currency"),
    ("Δ = ∂C/∂S", "1"),
    ("Γ = ∂²C/∂S²", "1/currency"),
    ("ν = ∂C/∂σ", "Currency·√year"),
    ("Θ = −∂C/∂T", "Currency/year"),
    ("ρ = ∂C/∂r", "Currency·year"),
    ("vanna = ∂²C/∂S∂σ", "√year"),
    ("volga = ∂²C/∂σ²", "Currency·year"),
];
let rows: Vec<(&str, &str, f64, f64, Option<f64>)> = names
    .iter()
    .zip(ad.iter().zip(&closed))
    .map(|(&(name, unit), (&a, &c))| {
        let relative = (c != 0.0).then(|| (a - c).abs() / c.abs());
        (name, unit, a, c, relative)
    })
    .collect();
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Greek" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Unit" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "AD", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Closed form", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative difference", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "Undefined" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "greeks" })
);
export!(market, &[spot, strike, vol, maturity, rate]);
export!(call, &ad[..6].to_vec());
```

At the default inputs ($S = K = 100$, $\sigma = 0.2$, $T = 1$, $r = 0.05$), the results are $C = 10.4506$, $\Delta = 0.63683$, $\Gamma = 0.018762$, $\nu = 37.524$, $\Theta = -6.4140$ /year, and $\rho = 53.232$; all relative differences are at most $5.4\times 10^{-16}$.
Dividing Θ by 365 gives $-0.0176$ currency units per day, expressing the call's loss of value with time even if the other variables do not change.

Deep in the money with $S = 130$, $K = 90$, $\sigma = 0.35$, $T = 0.25$, and $r = 0.03$, $\Delta = 0.98718$ and the relative difference in Γ increases to $7.2\times 10^{-15}$.
AD separately computes and adds the canceling terms without using the cancellation from $S\varphi(d_1) = Ke^{-rT}\varphi(d_2)$.
When those terms are roughly an order of magnitude larger than the result, the relative rounding error increases accordingly.
The difference still remains at the rounding-error level, without the step-dependent truncation error of finite differences.

With $S = 150$, $K = 50$, $\sigma = 0.05$, and $T = 0.05$, $d_1$ is about 98, rounding to $\Phi(d_1) = 1$ and $\varphi(d_1) = 0$ and giving $\Delta = 1$ and $\Gamma = \nu = 0$; the relative differences for Γ, ν, vanna, and volga display as “Undefined”.
The remaining $\Theta = -rKe^{-rT} = -2.4938$ /year and $\rho = KTe^{-rT} = 2.4938$ show that the call behaves like a forward contract with delivery price $K$.
With $\sigma = 0.8$, $T = 3$, and $r = 0$, the signs differ as $d_1 = 0.693$ and $d_2 = -0.693$, so volga $= -32.61$ is negative and vanna $= 0.2718$ is positive.

## Differentiating put–call parity

Differentiating parity $C - P = S - Ke^{-rT}$ with respect to each component of $z$ gives $\Delta_C - \Delta_P = 1$, $\Gamma_C = \Gamma_P$, $\nu_C = \nu_P$, $\Theta_C - \Theta_P = -rKe^{-rT}$, and $\rho_C - \rho_P = KTe^{-rT}$.
The right-hand side contains no $\Phi$, so comparing it with differences between separately AD-differentiated call and put results provides a check without closed-form Greeks.

The following cell receives the inputs and forward-mode call values from the previous cell and differentiates the put in reverse mode.
Set the pricing function's sign to $s = -1$, compute the price and first-order Greeks with `value_and_grad`, and obtain Γ with `hessian`.
`value_and_grad` records the function on a new tape and returns the value and gradient after one reverse sweep with cotangent 1 ([Reverse mode and tapes](../../algorithms/reverse-mode-and-replay/)).
Since the tape is new, the function is called twice: once to count statements and once to record.
Differentiating calls and puts with different modes also checks agreement between forward and reverse mode.

```rust
//| id: ad-bs-parity
//| ref: ad-bs-parity
//| title: "Check differentiated put–call parity with a forward-mode call and reverse-mode put"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [ad-bs-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-ad, scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
//| dependsOn:
//|   greeks: ad-bs-greeks
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"parity","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":-0.3631693488243809,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"parity","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"parity","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"min":-1e-14,"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"parity","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"parity","path":"/rows/3/5","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"parity","path":"/rows/4/5","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"parity","path":"/rows/5/5","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_ad::{hessian, value_and_grad};
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

let market: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(greeks["market"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let call: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(greeks["call"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (spot, strike, vol, maturity, rate) =
    (market[0], market[1], market[2], market[3], market[4]);
let z = [spot, vol, maturity, rate];
// Put in reverse mode: value_and_grad for price and gradient, hessian for Γ.
let (value, g) = value_and_grad(|x| price(x, strike, -1.0), &z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let h = hessian(|x| price(x, strike, -1.0), &z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let g = g.as_slice();
let put = [value, g[0], h.as_slice()[0], g[1], -g[2], g[3]];
// Right-hand side: derivatives of C − P = S − K e^{−rT} with respect to each z component (theoretical values).
let disc = RealScalar::exp(-rate * maturity);
let rhs = [
    spot - strike * disc,
    1.0,
    0.0,
    0.0,
    -rate * strike * disc,
    strike * maturity * disc,
];
let names = [
    "C − P = S − K e^{−rT}",
    "Δ_C − Δ_P = 1",
    "Γ_C − Γ_P = 0",
    "ν_C − ν_P = 0",
    "Θ_C − Θ_P = −rK e^{−rT}",
    "ρ_C − ρ_P = K T e^{−rT}",
];
let rows: Vec<(&str, f64, f64, f64, f64, f64)> = (0..6)
    .map(|i| {
        let lhs = call[i] - put[i];
        (names[i], call[i], put[i], lhs, rhs[i], lhs - rhs[i])
    })
    .collect();
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Relation" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Call (forward mode)", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Put (reverse mode)", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Left: call − put", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Right (theory)", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Difference (left − right)", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "parity" })
);
```

At the default inputs, the put gives $P = 5.5735$, $\Delta_P = -0.36317$, $\Theta_P = -1.6579$ /year, and $\rho_P = -41.890$; the price, Δ, Γ, and Θ relations have zero difference.
The ν and ρ relation differences are $7.1\times 10^{-15}$ and $-2.8\times 10^{-14}$, respectively 1–2 ulp of their magnitudes (37.5 and 95.1).
The put recording passes through $\Phi(-d_1)$ and $\Phi(-d_2)$, so the check also tests the pricing function's sign handling and derivative rules at negative arguments.

Setting the previous cell's inputs to $S = 130$, $K = 90$, $\sigma = 0.35$, $T = 0.25$, and $r = 0.03$ gives a Θ relation difference of $-1.3\times 10^{-15}$ and a ν relation difference of $8.9\times 10^{-16}$, with all other differences 0.
Deep out of the money with $S = 60$, $K = 140$, $\sigma = 0.05$, and $T = 0.05$, the call price and every Greek round to 0, while the parity relations retain zero differences through the put values (such as $\Delta_P = -1$).

## Summary

Functions absent from `Real` can be differentiated within the pricing function like other operations by supplying their value and first- and second-derivative triple through `try_map_scalar`; derivatives through order two are assembled correctly.
For a price with four inputs, one `MultiDual<f64, 4>` evaluation gives Δ, ν, Θ, and ρ, and `hessian` gives Γ, vanna, and volga; relative differences from closed forms remain at the rounding-error level (at most $1.1\times 10^{-14}$ for the tested inputs).
Since the AD input is remaining time $T$, obtain calendar-time Θ by negating the third gradient component.
The closed-form check shares values of $\Phi$, so interpret it as checking chain-rule assembly rather than $\Phi$ accuracy, and supplement it with relations without $\Phi$, such as put–call parity.
