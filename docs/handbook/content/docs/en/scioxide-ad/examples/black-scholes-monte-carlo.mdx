For options without a closed-form price, estimate the price by Monte Carlo and the Greeks from the same paths.
This example treats the same European call as [Black–Scholes Greeks](../black-scholes-greeks/) by Monte Carlo, examining estimator behavior where comparison with closed forms is possible.
Write the discounted payoff of one path as a `BatchFunction`, differentiate each path with `BatchPlan` to estimate price, Δ, ν, Θ, and ρ, and measure differences from closed forms in standard errors.
Then verify that Δ's standard error decreases inversely with the square root of the path count and that pathwise Γ is zero and biased.
Notation, units, and the sign convention for Θ ($\Theta = -\partial C/\partial T$) follow that page.

## Monte Carlo price estimation

Under the risk-neutral measure, the terminal underlying price can be written using a standard normal random variable $Z$ as

$$
S_T = S\exp\bigl((r - \sigma^2/2)\,T + \sigma\sqrt T\,Z\bigr),\qquad
C = \mathbb{E}\bigl[e^{-rT}\max(S_T - K, 0)\bigr]
$$


Monte Carlo estimates $C$ with the sample mean $\bar Y$ of discounted payoffs $Y_i = e^{-rT}\max(S_{T,i} - K, 0)$ over $n$ paths $Z_1, \dots, Z_n$.
Its standard error (SE) is $s/\sqrt n$ using sample standard deviation $s$, so quadrupling the path count approximately halves the estimation error.

## Pathwise differentiation

The pathwise method differentiates inside the expectation (Giles–Glasserman).
For a parameter $\theta$, use $\partial C/\partial\theta = \mathbb{E}[\partial Y/\partial\theta]$ and estimate sensitivity by averaging the derivatives of each path's payoff.
Interchanging differentiation and expectation is valid when the payoff $Y$ is differentiable in $\theta$ for almost every path and Lipschitz continuous in $\theta$ with a constant having finite expectation.
The call payoff is Lipschitz continuous in $S_T$ with slope at most 1, and $S_T$ is smooth in $S, \sigma, T, r$, so this condition holds.
For example, the pathwise Δ is $e^{-rT}\,\mathbf 1\{S_T > K\}\,S_T/S$, and the estimates of Δ, ν, Θ, and ρ are unbiased.

This interchange fails for Γ.
$\partial Y/\partial S$ is a step function jumping at $S_T = K$, so its derivative with respect to $S$ contains a delta function concentrated at $S_T = K$, a term omitted by pathwise differentiation.
Since $S_T$ is proportional to $S$, the second pathwise derivative is 0 on every path, and its mean does not estimate the true $\Gamma > 0$.
The omitted term's expectation is $e^{-rT}K\varphi(d_2)/(S^2\sigma\sqrt T)$, equal to Γ itself by $S\varphi(d_1) = Ke^{-rT}\varphi(d_2)$, so the bias is the entire Γ.
Γ needs a different estimator, such as a likelihood-ratio method or payoff smoothing.
scioxide-ad does not supply those estimators; to use one, implement its formula yourself in `item` of a `BatchFunction`.

## Pathwise differentiation with `BatchPlan`

Compute pathwise derivatives by AD with `BatchPlan`.
Use paths as items, $[S, \sigma, T, r]$ as parameters, and each path's single $Z$ as data, averaging with `BatchReduction::Mean`.
The `prepare` method of `BatchFunction` constructs path-independent quantities (drift $(r - \sigma^2/2)T$, $\sigma\sqrt T$, and discount $e^{-rT}$) once per block, and `item` returns one path's payoff.
With four parameters, `BatchPlan` selects forward mode `MultiDual<Lanes<f64, 8>, 4>` for gradients, packing eight paths into eight lanes (one SIMD element each) and evaluating them together ([Batch evaluation of many items](../../algorithms/batches/)).
Value-only, forward, and reverse evaluation all produce the same price bits.
Block results are added pairwise by block index, so the estimate, gradient, and SE have identical bits regardless of execution policy and worker count.
The cells on this page execute sequentially (wasm cannot use threads).
The kink $S_T = K$ in `max(S_T − K, 0)` uses the midpoint of one-sided derivatives, but has probability 0 ([Handling kinks and nonfinite values](../../algorithms/nonsmooth-and-nonfinite-policies/)).

## Gradient standard errors from batch means

With `BatchReduction::Mean` and at least two items, `BatchEstimate::standard_error` returns the price SE $s/\sqrt n$.
`BatchOutputs::with_parameter_gradient_error` computes gradient SEs from block averages (batch means).
For $B$ blocks, let $\bar g_b$ be the mean gradient for parameter $k$ in a block of $n_b$ items and $\bar g$ the overall mean; then

$$
\mathrm{SE}_k = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{b=1}^{B} n_b\,(\bar g_b - \bar g)^2}{(B - 1)\,n}}
$$


Blocks contain 256 items by default, giving $B = 64$ when $n = 2^{14}$.
With fewer than two blocks, gradient SEs are undefined and `evaluate_into` returns `InvalidInput`.

The SE of Δ can be compared with a closed form.
The expected square of pathwise Δ is $e^{-2rT}\,\mathbb E[\mathbf 1\{S_T > K\}\,S_T^2]/S^2 = e^{\sigma^2 T}\,\Phi(d_1 + \sigma\sqrt T)$, so its standard deviation is

$$
\sigma_\Delta = \sqrt{e^{\sigma^2 T}\,\Phi(d_1 + \sigma\sqrt T) - \Phi(d_1)^2}
$$

and if the SE decreases as $1/\sqrt n$, then $\mathrm{SE}\cdot\sqrt n$ approaches $\sigma_\Delta$.

The following block defines the model shared by the first and Γ cells.
`EuropeanCall` and `Market` represent one path's payoff, `standard_normals` generates standard normal random values from a seed, `pathwise_mean` returns the path mean, its gradient, and SEs, and `mode_name` names the batch evaluation's numeric type.
`closed_form` supplies the closed forms $[C, \Delta, \Gamma, \nu, \Theta, \rho]$ and $\sigma_\Delta$ for checking; $\Phi$ uses scioxide-special's `normal_cdf`, while ln, exp, and √ are reference values computed with `f64` standard functions.

```rust
//| id: ad-bs-mc-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "One-path payoff, standard normal random values, path mean, and closed forms"
//| crates: [scioxide-ad, scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_ad::{
    BatchEstimate, BatchFunction, BatchMode, BatchOptions, BatchOutputs, BatchPlan, BatchReduction,
    Real,
};
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, SciOxideError};
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, Xoshiro256StarStar};

/// Discounted payoff e^{−rT} max(S_T − K, 0) for one path. S_T = S exp((r − σ²/2)T + σ√T Z).
pub(crate) struct EuropeanCall {
    pub(crate) strike: f64,
}

/// Path-independent quantities. parameters = [S, σ, T, r].
pub(crate) struct Market<R> {
    spot: R,
    drift: R,
    vol_sqrt_t: R,
    discount: R,
}

impl BatchFunction for EuropeanCall {
    type Shared<R: Real> = Market<R>;
    fn prepare<R: Real>(&self, p: &[R]) -> Result<Market<R>, SciOxideError> {
        let (spot, vol, maturity, rate) = (p[0], p[1], p[2], p[3]);
        Ok(Market {
            spot,
            drift: (rate - vol * vol * 0.5) * maturity,
            vol_sqrt_t: vol * maturity.sqrt(),
            discount: (-(rate * maturity)).exp(),
        })
    }
    fn item<R: Real>(&self, m: &Market<R>, _: &[R], z: &[R::Base]) -> Result<R, SciOxideError> {
        let terminal = m.spot * (m.drift + m.vol_sqrt_t * R::passive(z[0])).exp();
        Ok(m.discount * (terminal - self.strike).max(R::zero()))
    }
}

/// n standard normal random values from Xoshiro256** with seed seed (scioxide-random's Normal).
pub(crate) fn standard_normals(seed: u64, n: usize) -> Result<Vec<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let law = Normal::new(0.0, 1.0)?;
    let mut rng = Xoshiro256StarStar::from_seed(seed);
    law.sample_n(&mut rng, n)
}

/// Mean over paths normals: estimate (price and s/√n), gradient with respect to [S, σ, T, r], and its SEs (batch means).
pub(crate) fn pathwise_mean(
    option: &EuropeanCall,
    parameters: &[f64],
    normals: &[f64],
) -> Result<(BatchEstimate<f64>, [f64; 4], [f64; 4]), SciOxideError> {
    let n = normals.len();
    let plan = BatchPlan::<8>::new(n, 4, 0, 1, BatchReduction::Mean, BatchOptions::new())?;
    let mut workspace = plan.workspace(1)?;
    let (mut gradient, mut error) = ([0.0; 4], [0.0; 4]);
    let outputs = BatchOutputs::new()
        .with_parameter_gradient(&mut gradient)
        .with_parameter_gradient_error(&mut error);
    let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
    let estimate = plan.evaluate_into(
        option,
        parameters,
        &[],
        normals,
        outputs,
        &mut workspace,
        cx,
    )?;
    Ok((estimate, gradient, error))
}

/// Name of the numeric type selected by batch evaluation and number of forward-mode tangents.
pub(crate) fn mode_name(mode: BatchMode) -> (&'static str, Option<usize>) {
    match mode {
        BatchMode::Value => ("Value", None),
        BatchMode::Forward { tangents } => ("Forward mode", Some(tangents)),
        BatchMode::Reverse => ("Reverse mode", None),
    }
}

/// Closed-form [C, Δ, Γ, ν, Θ, ρ] and standard deviation √(e^{σ²T} Φ(d₁ + σ√T) − Φ(d₁)²) of pathwise Δ.
/// market = [S, K, σ, T, r]. Φ from scioxide-special; ln, exp, and √ are f64 reference values.
pub(crate) fn closed_form(market: &[f64; 5]) -> Result<([f64; 6], f64), SciOxideError> {
    let [spot, strike, vol, maturity, rate] = *market;
    let st = maturity.sqrt();
    let d1 = ((spot / strike).ln() + (rate + 0.5 * vol * vol) * maturity) / (vol * st);
    let d2 = d1 - vol * st;
    let disc = (-rate * maturity).exp();
    let pdf = (-0.5 * d1 * d1).exp() / (2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI).sqrt();
    let (n1, n2) = (
        scioxide_special::normal_cdf(d1)?,
        scioxide_special::normal_cdf(d2)?,
    );
    let greeks = [
        spot * n1 - strike * disc * n2,
        n1,
        pdf / (spot * vol * st),
        spot * pdf * st,
        -spot * pdf * vol / (2.0 * st) - rate * strike * disc * n2,
        strike * maturity * disc * n2,
    ];
    let second_moment = (vol * vol * maturity).exp() * scioxide_special::normal_cdf(d1 + vol * st)?;
    // Set to 0 deep out of the money when rounding makes the difference negative.
    Ok((greeks, (second_moment - n1 * n1).max(0.0).sqrt()))
}
```

At the default inputs ($S = K = 100$, $\sigma = 0.2$, $T = 1$, $r = 0.05$, $n = 2^{14}$, seed 2024), the estimates are price $10.399 \pm 0.114$, Δ $0.6414 \pm 0.0045$, ν $36.98 \pm 0.52$, Θ $-6.385 \pm 0.063$, and ρ $53.74 \pm 0.38$, differing from closed forms by $-0.45$, $1.02$, $-1.04$, $0.46$, and $1.36$ SE, respectively.
All are within 2 SE, so pathwise differentiation agrees with the closed forms within statistical error.
The five estimates share the same paths and their errors are correlated; they cannot be interpreted as five independent tests.

Setting $n = 2^{18}$ gives a price SE of $0.0287$; multiplying the path count by 16 reduces the SE to about one quarter.
Differences from closed forms are $0.47$, $1.23$, $0.14$, $-0.50$, and $1.35$ SE.
With seed 7, the differences are $1.70$, $1.77$, $1.41$, $-1.60$, and $1.68$ SE, with the price and Greeks shifted together (Θ has the opposite sign because $-\partial C/\partial T$).
This correlation also comes from sharing paths.

## Convergence of Δ

The following cell plots the Δ estimates and SEs passed by the previous cell against path count $n$.
The graph shows Δ estimates using the first $2^{11}, 2^{12}, \dots, n$ paths of the same sequence and intervals of $\pm 2\,\mathrm{SE}$, together with the closed-form Δ line.
The table shows the SE at each $n$, $\mathrm{SE}\cdot\sqrt n$, and its ratio to the closed-form $\sigma_\Delta$; if the SE decreases as $1/\sqrt n$ and batch means estimate it correctly, the ratio remains near 1.

At the default inputs, $\mathrm{SE}\cdot\sqrt n$ for $2^{11}$ through $2^{14}$ paths is $0.601$, $0.609$, $0.583$, and $0.576$, remaining at $1.00$–$1.06$ times the closed-form $\sigma_\Delta = 0.5764$.
The SE decreases by about a factor of $1/\sqrt 2$ whenever paths double, and intervals shrink while continuing to contain the closed-form line.
If block means are approximately normal, the relative variation of the estimated SE is about $1/\sqrt{2(B - 1)}$: 27% for $B = 8$ and 9% for $B = 64$; interpret departures of the ratio from 1 on this scale.
Setting $n = 2^{18}$ in the first cell gives 1024 blocks, and the ratio at $2^{18}$ paths becomes $1.016$.

## Pathwise Γ

Compute pathwise Γ with `BatchPlan::hvp_into`.
For parameter direction $u$, `hvp_into` returns a directional derivative of the averaged parameter gradient (a Hessian–vector product); when `evaluate_into` selects forward mode for the plan, this uses `Dual<MultiDual<Lanes<f64, 8>, 4>>` (forward-over-forward).
Setting $u = e_S$ makes its first component the pathwise Γ estimate.

## Behavior when parameters change

With $\sigma = 0.8$, $T = 3$, and $r = 0$, $\sigma\sqrt T = 1.39$ makes the distribution of $S_T$ heavy-tailed, and the distribution of pathwise ν becomes heavy-tailed too.
With $n = 2^{14}$ and seed 2024, the ν difference is $-3.0$ SE.
At the same $n$, changing the seed among 2024, 7, and 11 gives ν SEs of 2.9, 5.7, and 4.9; the SE estimate itself is unstable.
With $n = 2^{18}$, the difference falls to $-0.80$ SE.
At $r = 0$, $S_T$ depends on $\sigma$ and $T$ only through $\sigma\sqrt T$, so pathwise $\partial Y/\partial T$ equals $(\sigma/(2T))\,\partial Y/\partial\sigma$ and the standardized Θ difference differs from ν's only in sign.
For heavy-tailed quantities, draw conclusions from SE comparisons only after increasing the path count and checking that standardized differences stabilize.

Deep out of the money ($S = 60$, $K = 140$, $\sigma = 0.05$, $T = 0.05$), every path has payoff 0 and SE is also 0, so standardized differences display as “None”.
The estimate and closed form are both 0; Monte Carlo estimation here is a trivial zero-variance estimate.

## Summary

For Monte Carlo sensitivities, write a generic one-path payoff as a `BatchFunction` and pass it to `BatchPlan` for pathwise differentiation; forward mode is selected when parameters and inputs total at most four.
`with_parameter_gradient_error` obtains gradient SEs from batch means of at least two blocks; for Δ, check that $\mathrm{SE}\cdot\sqrt n$ is near the closed-form standard deviation $\sigma_\Delta$.
Read differences between estimates and closed forms in SE units; estimates from the same paths have correlated errors and are not a collection of independent tests.
Pathwise differentiation is unbiased for Greeks with Lipschitz-continuous payoffs, but for Γ it omits the term from the payoff derivative's jump and returns 0; implement another estimator, such as a likelihood-ratio estimator, in `item`.
For heavy-tailed distributions, SE estimates also fluctuate; increase the path count and check that standardized differences stabilize.
