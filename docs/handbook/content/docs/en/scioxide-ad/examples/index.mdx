These examples use concrete subjects from physics, chemistry, engineering, finance, and machine learning to demonstrate the full process from obtaining derivatives to checking them against closed forms or other methods.
Each example writes a function once for `R: Real` and selects numeric types and transformations.
“Independent check” in the table identifies quantities checked by means other than automatic differentiation.

| Example | Subject | Main APIs | Independent check |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Gradient and curvature of the Rosenbrock function](rosenbrock-sensitivity/) | Valley of an optimization test function | `value_and_grad`, `hessian`, `Dual`, `Tape::gradient_into` | Closed-form gradient and Hessian, curvature along the valley |
| [Duality of JVPs and VJPs](jvp-vjp-duality/) | Jacobian of a vector-valued map | `jvp`, `Tape::linearize`, products on `Linearization`, `JacobianMode` | Residual of $\langle Jv, w\rangle = \langle v, J^\mathsf{T}w\rangle$, sweep counts |
| [Hessians and Hessian–vector products](hessian-vector-product/) | One Newton–CG step | `hvp`, `Tape<Dual<f64>>::hvp_into`, `HessianOperator` | Dense Hessian, finite differences |
| [Sparse Hessian recovery by coloring](sparse-hessian-chain/) | Energy of chain interactions | `hessian_pattern`, `derivative_coloring`, `sparse_hessian_into` | Agreement with dense Hessian, color count and dimension |
| [Kinks and differentiation conventions in L1 regularization](nonsmooth-l1-policy/) | LASSO objective | Midpoint convention of `abs`, `KinkPolicy`, `TapeReport::kinks` | Subdifferential intervals, one-sided derivatives |
| [Constructing equations of motion from a double-pendulum Lagrangian](double-pendulum-lagrangian/) | Analytical mechanics | `grad`, `hessian`, `hvp`, `Tape::gradient_into` (values of `MultiDual`) | Closed-form mass matrix and generalized forces, energy conservation and observed integration order |
| [Backpropagation and gradient checking for a one-layer neural network](neural-network-backprop/) | Regression training | `value_and_grad`, `BatchPlan`, `FiniteDifference` from scioxide-differentiate | Central-difference relative error (U shape versus step), tape/batch agreement |
| [Greeks of the Black–Scholes formula](black-scholes-greeks/) | Option prices and sensitivities | `try_map_scalar`, `MultiDual`, `hessian`, `value_and_grad` | Closed-form Greeks, put–call parity |
| [Monte Carlo Greeks with pathwise differentiation](black-scholes-monte-carlo/) | Mean of geometric Brownian paths | `evaluate_into` and `hvp_into` on `BatchPlan`, normal random numbers from scioxide-random | Closed-form Greek differences measured in standard errors, bias of Γ |
| [Forces and normal modes of Lennard-Jones clusters](lennard-jones-normal-modes/) | Molecular vibrations | `grad`, `hvp`, `value_and_grad`, `hessian`, Newton's method from scioxide-rootfind, symmetric eigendecomposition from scioxide-linalg | Dimer equilibrium distance and frequency, zero-mode count, closed-form eigenvalues |
| [Implicit sensitivities of the van der Waals equation of state](van-der-waals-implicit/) | Volume sensitivities of a real gas | `implicit_solution_into`, `Residual`, `value_and_grad`, `hessian`, polynomial roots from scioxide-rootfind | Closed forms from the implicit function theorem, isothermal compressibility, near-critical asymptotics |
| [ODE power-series solutions and convergence radii with Taylor jets](taylor-series-ode/) | Series solutions of ordinary differential equations | Recursion on `Jet` coefficients | Exact coefficients, convergence radius from coefficient ratios |

Before reading the examples, examine [Choosing an automatic differentiation method](../choosing/) to understand numeric-type and transformation choices and why each example uses its APIs.
Algorithm derivations appear on the pages of [Automatic differentiation algorithms](../algorithms/).

## Summary

The examples cover first derivatives (Rosenbrock, JVPs and VJPs), second derivatives (Hessian actions, sparse Hessians, double pendulum, Lennard-Jones), kinks (L1 regularization), implicit solutions (van der Waals), higher-order coefficients (ODE series solutions), and sums of many items (neural networks, Monte Carlo Greeks).
Every example checks results using closed forms, conserved quantities, or another method.
Start with a subject close to your problem and adapt its API use and independent-check construction.
