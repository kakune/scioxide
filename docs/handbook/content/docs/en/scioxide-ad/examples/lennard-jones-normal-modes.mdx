Expanding a molecule's or cluster's potential to second order around a stable structure separates small atomic vibrations into independent harmonic oscillators.
These vibrations are called normal modes; their frequencies give infrared and Raman spectral positions and vibrational free energies in the harmonic approximation.
The calculation needs the potential gradient (negated to give force) and Hessian (the force-constant matrix); automatic differentiation returns both without finite-difference truncation error (rounding error remains).

This example considers a dimer and regular tetrahedral tetramer bound by Lennard-Jones (LJ) potentials.
Write the energy once as a generic `R: Real` function, use `grad` and `hvp` to find the dimer's equilibrium distance by Newton's method, and use `hessian` with scioxide-linalg's symmetric eigendecomposition to find normal and zero modes, checking all against closed forms.
[Lennard-Jones cluster structure search in scioxide-optimize](../../../scioxide-optimize/examples/lennard-jones-clusters/) addresses minimum-energy structures of larger clusters; this page uses the same potential and units ($\varepsilon = \sigma = 1$).

## Model and reduced units

For two atoms at separation $r$, the LJ potential and its first and second derivatives are

$$
V(r) = 4\varepsilon\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right], \qquad
V'(r) = \frac{24\varepsilon}{r}\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6} - 2\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12}\right], \qquad
V''(r) = \frac{24\varepsilon}{r^2}\left[26\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - 7\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right]
$$


Here $\varepsilon$ is the well depth and $\sigma$ the distance at which $V(\sigma) = 0$.
From $V'(r_{\mathrm{eq}}) = 0$, the equilibrium distance is $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 2^{1/6}\sigma \approx 1.122462\,\sigma$, the potential there is $V(r_{\mathrm{eq}}) = -\varepsilon$, and the force constant is $k = V''(r_{\mathrm{eq}}) = 72\varepsilon/(2^{1/3}\sigma^2) \approx 57.1464\,\varepsilon/\sigma^2$.
$V''$ changes sign at $r = (26/7)^{1/6}\sigma \approx 1.2445\,\sigma$, with negative curvature beyond that distance.

Arrange the coordinates of $N$ atoms as $\boldsymbol{x} = (\boldsymbol{x}_1, \dots, \boldsymbol{x}_N) \in \mathbb{R}^{3N}$, with total energy $U(\boldsymbol{x}) = \sum_{i<j} V(\lvert \boldsymbol{x}_i - \boldsymbol{x}_j \rvert)$ and force $\boldsymbol{F}_i = -\nabla_i U$ on atom $i$.

Use $\sigma$ for length, $\varepsilon$ for energy, and the common atomic mass $m$ for mass (reduced units).
The time unit is $\tau = \sigma\sqrt{m/\varepsilon}$, Hessian eigenvalues have units $\varepsilon/\sigma^2$, and angular frequencies have units $1/\tau$; cells set $\varepsilon = \sigma = m = 1$.
Typical argon values $\sigma = 3.405$ Å, $\varepsilon/k_B = 119.8$ K, and $m = 39.95$ u give $\tau \approx 2.16$ ps, with angular frequency $1/\tau$ corresponding to wavenumber $1/(2\pi c\tau) \approx 2.46$ cm⁻¹.
This conversion gives values within the LJ model and makes no claim to reproduce real argon spectroscopy.

## Hessian structure and zero modes

For an atom pair $(i, j)$, set $r = \lvert \boldsymbol{x}_i - \boldsymbol{x}_j \rvert$ and $\boldsymbol{n} = (\boldsymbol{x}_i - \boldsymbol{x}_j)/r$; differentiating $V(r)$ twice with respect to $\boldsymbol{x}_i$ gives the $3 \times 3$ matrix

$$
B_{ij} = k_\parallel\, \boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n}^{\mathsf T} + k_\perp \left(I - \boldsymbol{n}\boldsymbol{n}^{\mathsf T}\right), \qquad k_\parallel = V''(r), \quad k_\perp = \frac{V'(r)}{r}
$$


The diagonal blocks of the Hessian $H = \nabla^2 U$ are $\sum_{j \ne i} B_{ij}$, and the off-diagonal blocks are $-B_{ij}$.
$k_\parallel$ is the stiffness along the bond and $k_\perp$ perpendicular to it; a pair at equilibrium distance has $k_\perp = 0$.

An isolated cluster's energy is invariant under translation, so the three translational directions are eigenvectors of $H$ with eigenvalue 0 at every configuration.
Rotational invariance also holds; differentiating the identity $\nabla U(R\boldsymbol{x}) = R\,\nabla U(\boldsymbol{x})$ in the rotation angle gives $H(\Omega\boldsymbol{x}) = \Omega\,\nabla U(\boldsymbol{x})$ for an antisymmetric matrix $\Omega$ acting identically on every atom.
Thus rotations are zero modes at a stationary point with $\nabla U = \boldsymbol{0}$, but generally not at a nonstationary configuration.
At a stationary point, nonlinear configurations have three rotational zero modes and linear configurations two (rotation about the molecular axis does not move atoms), giving six and five zero modes in total.

For a dimer and regular tetrahedron with all pair distances equal, uniform scaling by $s$ about the center of mass at the origin leaves the gradient radial, $\nabla_i U = g\,\boldsymbol{x}_i$, so $H(\Omega\boldsymbol{x}) = g\,\Omega\boldsymbol{x}$ and rotational vectors are eigenvectors with eigenvalue $g$.
For the dimer, $g = 2k_\perp$; for the regular tetrahedron, $g = 4k_\perp$.

This gives the dimer eigenvalues at separation $r$: three translational 0s, two rotational $2k_\perp$, and one stretching $2k_\parallel$.
At equilibrium, only stretching remains; using reduced mass $\mu = m/2$ gives $\omega = \sqrt{k/\mu} = 12 \cdot 2^{-1/6}\sqrt{\varepsilon/(m\sigma^2)} \approx 10.6908/\tau$.

A regular tetrahedron of edge length $a$ has $T_d$ symmetry; its 12-dimensional displacement space decomposes into irreducible representations $A_1 + E + T_1 + 2T_2$, with rotation corresponding to $T_1$ and translation to one $T_2$.

| Mode | Multiplicity | Eigenvalue | Value at equilibrium ($a = r_{\mathrm{eq}}$) [$\varepsilon/\sigma^2$] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Translation $T_2$ | 3 | $0$ | $0$ |
| Rotation $T_1$ | 3 | $4k_\perp$ | $0$ |
| $E$ | 2 | $k_\parallel + 3k_\perp$ | $k \approx 57.146$ |
| $T_2$ | 3 | $2k_\parallel + 2k_\perp$ | $2k \approx 114.293$ |
| $A_1$ (uniform stretching) | 1 | $4k_\parallel$ | $4k \approx 228.586$ |

The $A_1$ mode moves all four atoms equally outward from the center of mass; unit displacement of each atom extends each of the six edges by $\sqrt{8/3}$, so the Rayleigh quotient is $6 \cdot (8/3)\,k_\parallel / 4 = 4k_\parallel$.
The other values follow similarly in symmetry-adapted coordinates, and the eigenvalue sum $12k_\parallel + 24k_\perp$ equals $\operatorname{tr} H$ (six times the pair contribution $2(k_\parallel + 2k_\perp)$).
When all six pairs are at $r_{\mathrm{eq}}$, each pair is at a local potential minimum, so the regular tetrahedron with edge $r_{\mathrm{eq}}$ is a local minimum of the tetramer ($U = -6\varepsilon$).

## Generic energy function and differentiation

Write the energy once as a generic `R: Real` function, selecting the numeric type to choose the differentiation mode.
`R = f64` gives ordinary evaluation, `Var<'t, f64>` passed by `grad` and `value_and_grad` gives reverse mode, and `Var<'t, Dual<f64>>` passed by `hvp` and `hessian` gives forward-over-reverse second derivatives.
Every type computes values with IEEE arithmetic in the same operation order, independently of differentiation mode ([Numeric types, lanes, and generic code](../../algorithms/number-types-and-lanes/)).

`grad` records the function once on a new tape and returns the gradient after one reverse sweep.
A new tape executes once more before recording to count statements, giving two function executions.
A tape is a sequence recording each operation as a statement; the reverse sweep traverses it from the end, accumulating adjoints ([Reverse mode and tapes](../../algorithms/reverse-mode-and-replay/)).
`hvp(f, x, v)` records dual-number inputs `Dual::new(xᵢ, vᵢ)` and performs one reverse sweep, obtaining $\nabla U$ in the value part and $H\boldsymbol{v}$ in the derivative part, and returns $H\boldsymbol{v}$.
`hessian` repeats this for each unit direction, recording the function $n = 3N$ times and writing $H\boldsymbol{e}_j$ in column $j$ (with the first direction's statement-counting execution, total function executions are $n + 1$).
Each recording's length is proportional to the pair count $N(N-1)/2$, so dense Hessian computation costs $O(N^3)$.

These one-shot functions create new tapes per call, execute with `ExecutionContext::sequential_native()`, and allocate the resulting `Vector` and `Matrix`.
For many evaluation points, reuse the same tape with `Tape::gradient_into` and `Tape::<Dual<f64>>::hvp_into` to eliminate allocation and statement-counting execution after the first call ([Tape lifecycle and reuse](../../algorithms/tape-lifecycle/)).
The closure must be generic over the tape lifetime `'t`, so write it directly in the call's argument position, as in `grad(|x| Ok(lj_energy(x)), &point)`.

AD gradients and Hessians are exact derivatives of the evaluated expression, without finite-difference truncation error; only rounding error remains.
Column $j$ of the matrix returned by `hessian` is HVP $j$, and its specification does not address bitwise matrix symmetry.
Pass $(H + H^{\mathsf T})/2$ to eigendecomposition and inspect the change caused by symmetrization.

The following block defines the model shared by the two cells.
`lj_energy` implements $U(\boldsymbol{x})$, computing $(\sigma/r)^6$ as $(1/r^2)^3$ without a square root.
Its `recip`, `powi`, and arithmetic operations are all `Real` methods, differentiated using the chosen numeric type's rules.
`pair_derivatives` supplies closed-form $(V, V', V'')$ checks computed with `f64` standard functions.

```rust
//| id: ad-lj-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Lennard-Jones cluster energy and pair-potential derivatives"
//| crates: [scioxide-ad]
use scioxide_ad::Real;

/// Total energy U(x) = Σ_{i<j} 4(r_ij⁻¹² − r_ij⁻⁶) in reduced units (ε = σ = 1).
/// x holds 3N entries of per-atom (x, y, z) coordinates, evaluated in the same operation order for every numeric type.
pub(crate) fn lj_energy<R: Real>(x: &[R]) -> R {
    let atoms = x.len() / 3;
    let mut total = R::zero();
    for i in 0..atoms {
        for j in i + 1..atoms {
            let dx = x[3 * i] - x[3 * j];
            let dy = x[3 * i + 1] - x[3 * j + 1];
            let dz = x[3 * i + 2] - x[3 * j + 2];
            // (σ/r)⁶ = (1/r²)³
            let s6 = (dx * dx + dy * dy + dz * dz).recip().powi(3);
            total += (s6 * s6 - s6) * 4.0;
        }
    }
    total
}

/// Pair potential (V(r), V′(r), V″(r)). Closed-form reference values computed with f64 standard functions.
pub(crate) fn pair_derivatives(r: f64) -> (f64, f64, f64) {
    let s6 = r.powi(-6);
    let value = 4.0 * (s6 * s6 - s6);
    let slope = 24.0 / r * (s6 - 2.0 * s6 * s6);
    let curvature = 24.0 / (r * r) * (26.0 * s6 * s6 - 7.0 * s6);
    (value, slope, curvature)
}
```

## Dimer equilibrium distance

The following cell finds the dimer's equilibrium distance with Newton's method using AD derivatives.
Let $\boldsymbol{x}(r)$ place the two atoms at $\mp r/2$ on the z axis, and let $\boldsymbol{e} = d\boldsymbol{x}/dr$ be a displacement increasing their separation by 1.
Compute $f(r) = dU/dr = \nabla U \cdot \boldsymbol{e}$ with `grad` and $f'(r) = d^2U/dr^2 = \boldsymbol{e}^{\mathsf T} H \boldsymbol{e}$ with `hvp`.
`hvp` returns $H\boldsymbol{e}$ with one recording and one reverse sweep without forming $H$, costing less than `hessian` for a single direction.

Solve $f(r) = 0$ with scioxide-rootfind's `Newton`.
To rootfind, $f'$ is a caller-supplied derivative (`DerivativeStrategy::Analytic`), whose value AD computes.
`Newton` is an unsafeguarded Newton method returning termination status in `RootTermination` ([Newton, secant, and Muller methods](../../../scioxide-rootfind/algorithms/open-scalar-methods/)).

Near a simple root, Newton error $e_k = \lvert r_k - r_{\mathrm{eq}} \rvert$ satisfies $e_{k+1} \approx C e_k^2$ if $f'$ is exact.
The constant is $C = \lvert f''(r_{\mathrm{eq}}) \rvert / (2 f'(r_{\mathrm{eq}})) = \lvert V'''(r_{\mathrm{eq}}) \rvert / (2k) \approx 9.354$.
The ratio $e_{k+1}/e_k^2$ approaching this constant independently checks that the curvature returned by `hvp` is the correct derivative of $f$.

The input is initial distance $r_0$ (in units $\sigma$).
Outputs are an evaluation table (first 12 evaluations), a table comparing equilibrium distance, curvature, and angular frequency with closed forms, and an outcome with `RootTermination` as its status.
If convergence fails, comparison values display as “Not converged”.

```rust
//| id: ad-lj-dimer-equilibrium
//| ref: ad-lj-dimer-equilibrium
//| title: "Find dimer equilibrium distance by Newton's method with AD forces and curvature"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [ad-lj-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-ad, scioxide-core, scioxide-differentiate, scioxide-rootfind]
//| inputs:
//|   r_start: {"type":"range","label":"Initial distance r₀ [σ]","description":"Newton starting point. Beyond 1.2445σ, V″ < 0.","min":0.95,"max":1.4,"step":0.01,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"overshoot","label":"r₀ = 1.20σ (small curvature)","description":"The first update crosses equilibrium to 0.968σ, then converges to the same equilibrium distance.","inputs":{"r_start":1.2}}
//|   - {"id":"beyond-inflection","label":"r₀ = 1.30σ (beyond the inflection point)","description":"With V″ < 0, updates move outward and stop with IterationLimit.","inputs":{"r_start":1.3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/5/4","approx":{"value":9.354,"atol":0.005}}
//|       - {"artifact":"equilibrium","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.122462048309373,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"equilibrium","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":57.14643787085512,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"equilibrium","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/status","equals":"StepTolerance"}
//|   - case: overshoot
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.9679178488,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"equilibrium","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: beyond-inflection
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"equilibrium","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":null}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/status","equals":"IterationLimit"}
use std::cell::RefCell;

use scioxide_ad::{grad, hvp};
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_differentiate::DerivativeStrategy;
use scioxide_rootfind::{Newton, ScalarFn, ScalarRootOptions, ScalarRootSolver};

// Place two atoms on the z axis at ∓r/2. e = dx/dr increases their separation by 1.
let dimer = |r: f64| [0.0, 0.0, -0.5 * r, 0.0, 0.0, 0.5 * r];
let e = [0.0, 0.0, -0.5, 0.0, 0.0, 0.5];
let along = |w: &[f64]| w.iter().zip(&e).map(|(a, b)| a * b).sum::<f64>();
let trace = RefCell::new(Vec::new());
// f(r) = dU/dr = ∇U·e uses one reverse-mode sweep; f′(r) = eᵀ(H e) uses one forward-over-reverse HVP.
let function = ScalarFn::new(|r: f64| -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    let slope = along(grad(|x| Ok(lj_energy(x)), &dimer(r))?.as_slice());
    trace.borrow_mut().push((r, slope));
    Ok(slope)
})
.with_derivative(|r: f64| -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    Ok(along(hvp(|x| Ok(lj_energy(x)), &dimer(r), &e)?.as_slice()))
});
// To rootfind, f′ is a caller-supplied derivative (Analytic) computed by AD.
let result = Newton::new(DerivativeStrategy::Analytic)
    .solve(&function, r_start, &ScalarRootOptions::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent checks: closed-form r_eq = 2^(1/6)σ and Newton quadratic-convergence constant |V‴|/(2V″) ≈ 9.354.
let r_eq = RealScalar::powf(2.0, 1.0 / 6.0);
let trace = trace.into_inner();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (k, &(r, slope)) in trace.iter().enumerate().take(12) {
    let error = (r - r_eq).abs();
    let next = trace.get(k + 1).map(|&(r, _)| (r - r_eq).abs());
    // Omit ratios once error reaches the rounding scale, where they cease to be meaningful.
    let ratio = next
        .filter(|&e| e > 1e3 * f64::EPSILON)
        .map(|e| e / (error * error));
    rows.push((k, r, slope, error, ratio));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 16 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Evaluation k", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Distance r_k [σ]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "2", "label": "dU/dr [ε/σ]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "|r_k − 2^(1/6)σ| [σ]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "e_(k+1)/e_k² [1/σ]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "iterations" })
);

// Compare equilibrium distance, curvature V″ there, and angular frequency √(2V″/m) (reduced mass m/2) with closed forms.
let success = result.is_success();
let r_star = result.point();
let curvature = along(
    hvp(|x| Ok(lj_energy(x)), &dimer(r_star), &e)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .as_slice(),
);
let (_, _, k_exact) = pair_derivatives(r_eq);
let omega = RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 * curvature);
let checks = vec![
    ("Equilibrium distance r_eq [σ]", success.then_some(r_star), r_eq),
    (
        "Curvature V″(r_eq) [ε/σ²]",
        success.then_some(curvature),
        k_exact,
    ),
    ("Angular frequency ω [1/τ]", success.then_some(omega), 12.0 / r_eq),
];
let rows: Vec<_> = checks
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, value, exact)| (name, value, exact, value.map(|v| (v - exact).abs())))
    .collect();
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Newton and AD", "type": "number", "format": digits, "nullText": "Not converged" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "equilibrium" })
);

let termination = result.termination();
let outcome = if success {
    Outcome::satisfied("Found equilibrium distance as a root of dU/dr = 0")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Newton stopped before reaching a root of dU/dr = 0")
        .explanation("The last point is not the equilibrium distance. Use a bracketed solver.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(termination.to_string())
        .diagnostic("Iterations", result.iterations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("|dU/dr|", result.residual().abs(), Some("ε/σ"))
        .diagnostic("Last point r", r_star, Some("σ"))
);
```

With default $r_0 = 1.00\,\sigma$, eight iterations reach `StepTolerance`, and the equilibrium distance agrees with $r_{\mathrm{eq}} = 2^{1/6}\sigma$ to the last digit of `f64`.
Errors decrease through $0.122$, $0.0698$, $0.0290$, $6.3 \times 10^{-3}$, $3.6 \times 10^{-4}$, $1.2 \times 10^{-6}$, and $1.3 \times 10^{-11}$, while ratio $e_{k+1}/e_k^2$ approaches from $4.66$ to $9.354$.
Equilibrium curvature agrees with $k = 72 \cdot 2^{-1/3}$ to within $6 \times 10^{-14}$, and angular frequency $\sqrt{2k/m} \approx 10.6908$ agrees with closed form $12 \cdot 2^{-1/6}$ to within $5 \times 10^{-15}$.

With $r_0 = 1.20\,\sigma$, small curvature makes the first update cross equilibrium to $0.968\,\sigma$ before converging to the same value (10 iterations).
At $r_0 = 1.30\,\sigma$, beyond the inflection point $1.2445\,\sigma$, $f > 0$ and $f' < 0$, so updates move outward.
Far away, $f(r) \approx 24 r^{-7}$ merely approaches 0 without a root; after 100 iterations the status is `IterationLimit` and the outcome is `partial`.
The returned point has the smallest $\lvert f \rvert$ among evaluated points, lying as far away as $r \approx 9.9 \times 10^{5}\,\sigma$.
The residual $\lvert dU/dr \rvert$ is small at $2.6 \times 10^{-41}$, but this point is not the equilibrium distance.
Even $r_0 = 1.24\,\sigma$ inside the inflection point has small $f' > 0$, so the first update jumps to distance $-2.42\,\sigma$ (a configuration exchanging the atoms), again failing to converge.
A small residual does not imply reaching a minimum; check both termination status and curvature sign.
If a root bracket is known, use rootfind's `SafeguardedNewton`.
Reliably finding multi-atom minima needs a method globalized by line search or trust regions, covered in [Lennard-Jones cluster structure search in scioxide-optimize](../../../scioxide-optimize/examples/lennard-jones-clusters/).

## Identifying zero eigenvalues

Computed eigenvalues are not exactly 0.
Hessian entries are rounded sums of terms of order $\lVert H \rVert$, and symmetric eigensolvers are backward stable; Weyl's inequality ([Eigenvalue problems and Schur decompositions](../../../scioxide-linalg/algorithms/eigenproblems/)) therefore bounds eigenvalue errors by a small multiple of machine epsilon $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ times $\lVert H \rVert_2$.

Set $n = 3N$ and use threshold $\eta = 100 \cdot n \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \cdot \lVert H \rVert_F$, counting eigenvalues with $\lvert \lambda \rvert \le \eta$ as zero modes.
Here $\lVert H \rVert_F \ge \lVert H \rVert_2$; $n$ accounts for rounding growing with sum length, and the factor 100 allows for constants in the solver's error bound.

For the equilibrium tetrahedron, $\lVert H \rVert_F = \sqrt{30}\,k \approx 313$, giving $\eta \approx 8.3 \times 10^{-11}$.
Computed zero modes are of order $10^{-13}$ and the smallest vibrational eigenvalue is $k \approx 57$, so replacing 100 by any value from 1 to $10^{11}$ leaves classification unchanged.
Use the same $\eta$ to group degenerate eigenvalues.

## Hessian eigenvalues and zero modes

The following cell centers the selected cluster's mass at the origin and sets every pair distance to $s$ times $r_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
Compute energy and forces with `value_and_grad`, the Hessian with `hessian`, and eigenvalues and eigenvectors with scioxide-linalg's `HermitianEigenDecomposition::decompose`.

`decompose` divides the matrix by its largest absolute entry, reduces it to tridiagonal form with Householder transformations, and for order at most 32 solves it by implicit QL (cost $O(n^3)$).
Eigenvalues are ascending; eigenvectors are orthonormal columns, oriented so each column's largest absolute entry is positive.
The default `EigenvalueAccuracy::Absolute` stops iteration relative to the overall spectrum's magnitude, fitting zero detection with a threshold proportional to $\lVert H \rVert$.
The decomposition returns no termination status and reports an `ConvergenceFailure` error at the iteration limit, so the cell emits no outcome.

Input interpretation `HermitianInput::Exact` rejects matrices lacking exact symmetry with `InvalidInput`.
`HermitianInput::Projected(HermitianProjectionPolicy::Always)` decomposes $(H + H^{\mathsf T})/2$ and returns the relative change $\lVert H - (H + H^{\mathsf T})/2 \rVert_F / \max(\lVert H \rVert_F, 1)$ through `projection_report()`.

Group consecutive eigenvalues differing by at most $\eta$ and compare them positionally with ascending closed-form values.
If every member of a group has the same type, the AD multiplicity agrees with the closed form (zero-mode groups combine translation and rotation).
Since every mass is $m$, the mass-weighted Hessian is $H/m$ and angular frequency for a positive eigenvalue is $\omega = \sqrt{\lambda/m}$.

Inputs are the cluster and scaling factor $s$ (the configuration at $s = 1$ is a local minimum; other values give nonstationary configurations).
Outputs are an eigenvalue-group table, a zero-mode-count and threshold table, and a table comparing energy, maximum force, and $\operatorname{tr} H$ with closed forms.
This cell passes the configuration, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, closed-form type names, and threshold to the next cell.

## Normal-mode shapes

Eigenvectors of different eigenvalues are orthogonal, so vibrational modes at a stationary point are orthogonal to translational and rotational zero modes.
For equal atomic masses and center of mass at the origin, this is equivalent to both the displacement sum $\sum_i \boldsymbol{v}_i$ and $\sum_i \boldsymbol{x}_i \times \boldsymbol{v}_i$, proportional to displacement-induced angular momentum, being 0 (the Eckart conditions), providing an independent eigenvector check.
The uniform stretching $A_1$ eigenvector is proportional to the configuration vector $\boldsymbol{x}$, and other vibrational modes are orthogonal to $\boldsymbol{x}$.

Within a degenerate group (triple $T_2$ or double $E$), eigenvectors may be any orthonormal basis of that subspace; rounding in the decomposition determines which basis is returned.
The Eckart conditions and orthogonality to $\boldsymbol{x}$ hold for every basis.

## Nonstationary configurations and larger clusters

Moving the scale factor away from 1 retains three translational zero modes at every configuration, while only rotational eigenvalues leave 0 in proportion to the gradient.
Perform normal-mode analysis after optimizing the structure to a stationary point.
Projecting out translations and rotations is common for structures slightly away from stationarity, but scioxide-ad provides no such projection; callers must project $H$ before eigendecomposition if needed.

As the atom count grows, the Hessian becomes $3N \times 3N$ and is dense for all-pair LJ interactions; both $3N$ recordings by `hessian` and symmetric eigendecomposition therefore grow in cost as $O(N^3)$.
For long chains with an interaction-distance cutoff, Hessian sparsity coloring recovers it with fewer recordings ([Recovering sparse Hessians by coloring](../sparse-hessian-chain/)).
If only a few low frequencies are needed, `HessianOperator` implements `LinearOperator` and can be passed as an operator to `solve` of scioxide-linalg's `PartialSpectrumSolver`, using only products without forming a dense $H$ ([Second derivatives and Hessian operators](../../algorithms/second-order-derivatives/), [Applying matrix functions to vectors, partial spectra, and low-rank approximation](../../../scioxide-linalg/algorithms/operator-actions-and-partial-spectra/)).

For unequal atomic masses, obtain $\omega^2$ from eigenvalues of $M^{-1/2} H M^{-1/2}$; isotope substitution does not change the zero-mode count.

## Summary

Write LJ cluster energy once as a generic `R: Real` function to obtain forces with `grad`, directional curvature with `hvp`, and the force-constant matrix with `hessian`, all without finite-difference truncation error.
Newton's method with AD derivatives converges quadratically to dimer equilibrium distance $2^{1/6}\sigma$; ratio $e_{k+1}/e_k^2$ approaching theoretical value $9.354$ checks the curvature.
Count zero modes with $\eta = 100 \cdot 3N \cdot \epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \cdot \lVert H \rVert_F$: six for the equilibrium tetrahedron and five for the dimer; tetrahedral vibrations form groups $E$, $T_2$, and $A_1$ of multiplicities 2, 3, and 1, matching $k$, $2k$, and $4k$.
Rotational eigenvalues leave 0 in proportion to the gradient at nonstationary configurations, so check stationarity by the zero-mode count before interpreting frequencies.
`hessian` does not specify symmetry of its result; symmetrize with `HermitianInput::Projected` and inspect the change with `projection_report()`.
