An equation of state relates pressure $p$, temperature $T$, and molar volume $V$; the van der Waals equation is cubic in $V$.
Finding volume from pressure and temperature requires selecting a cubic root; [van der Waals gases and Maxwell's equal-area rule in scioxide-rootfind](../../../scioxide-rootfind/examples/van-der-waals-maxwell/) primarily covers root finding and determining liquid–vapor coexistence pressure with Maxwell's equal-area rule.

This example examines sensitivities $\partial V/\partial p$, $\partial V/\partial T$, and second-order $\partial^2 V/\partial p^2$ of the computed volume.
The implicit function theorem determines sensitivities solely from residual derivatives at the root, without differentiating root-finding iterations.
In scioxide-ad, write the residual once as a `Residual`; `implicit_solution_into` attaches implicit-function-theorem derivatives to the root supplied by the caller.
[Derivatives and estimated errors of the van der Waals equation of state](../../../scioxide-differentiate/examples/van-der-waals-adaptive/) examines steps and errors when finite-differencing $\partial p/\partial V$ for the same equation; this page computes the reciprocal sensitivity without finite-difference error.

## Reduced equation of state

Using reduced variables $T_r = T/T_c$, $p_r = p/p_c$, and $V_r = V/V_c$ divided by critical values $T_c$, $p_c$, and $V_c$ gives the substance-independent van der Waals form

$$
p_r = \frac{8T_r}{3V_r - 1} - \frac{3}{V_r^2}, \qquad V_r > \frac{1}{3}
$$


Below, omit subscript $r$; $p$, $T$, and $V$ all denote reduced quantities.

Match the rootfind and differentiate examples' CO2 critical values $T_c = 304.13$ K and $p_c = 7.3773$ MPa, using the van der Waals relation $V_c = 3RT_c/(8p_c) \approx 0.12854$ L/mol ($R = 8.314462618$ J/(mol·K)) for $V_c$.
This exceeds the measured critical volume (about 0.094 L/mol); van der Waals is a qualitative model.

Define the residual as

$$
F(V; p, T) = p - \frac{8T}{3V - 1} + \frac{3}{V^2}
$$

and multiply by $V^2(3V - 1)$ to obtain the cubic $3pV^3 - (p + 8T)V^2 + 9V - 3 = 0$.
For $T \ge 1$, the isotherm decreases monotonically with $V$, giving only one real root for every $p > 0$ (a triple root $V = 1$ at $T = p = 1$).
For $T < 1$, there are three real roots between the isotherm's minimum and maximum pressures (the spinodals); for example, $T = 0.9$ gives $0.420 < p < 0.724$.

The smallest root is the liquid branch and the largest the vapor branch; the middle root is mechanically unstable with $\partial p/\partial V > 0$.
Whether liquid or vapor is stable or metastable depends on pressure relative to the saturation pressure ($p_{\mathrm{sat}} \approx 0.647$ at $T = 0.9$), whose computation is left to the rootfind example.

## Sensitivities from the implicit function theorem

At a root with $F_V \ne 0$, differentiating $F(V(p, T); p, T) = 0$ with respect to $p$ and $T$ gives

$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial p} = -\frac{F_p}{F_V} = -\frac{1}{F_V}, \qquad
\frac{\partial V}{\partial T} = -\frac{F_T}{F_V} = \frac{8}{(3V - 1)F_V}, \qquad
F_V = \frac{24T}{(3V - 1)^2} - \frac{6}{V^3} = -\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial V}\right)_T
$$


Since $F$ is linear in $p$ ($F_p = 1$, $F_{Vp} = 0$), differentiating $\partial V/\partial p$ again with respect to $p$ gives

$$
\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial p^2} = \frac{F_{VV}}{F_V^2}\,\frac{\partial V}{\partial p} = -\frac{F_{VV}}{F_V^3}, \qquad
F_{VV} = \frac{18}{V^4} - \frac{144T}{(3V - 1)^3}
$$


Isothermal compressibility and thermal expansion are $\kappa_T = -(1/V)(\partial V/\partial p)_T$ and $\alpha_p = (1/V)(\partial V/\partial T)_p$; from reduced variables they are $\kappa_T = -(\partial V_r/\partial p_r)/(p_c V_r)$ and $\alpha_p = (\partial V_r/\partial T_r)/(T_c V_r)$.
For an ideal gas, $\kappa_T = 1/p$ and $\alpha_p = 1/T$, and the volume ratio $V/V_{\mathrm{ideal}} = pV/(RT)$ is the compressibility factor $Z$.

At the critical point $(V, T, p) = (1, 1, 1)$, both $F_V = 0$ and $F_{VV} = 0$ hold, and sensitivities diverge.
Expansion in $t = T - 1$ and $v = V - 1$ gives $p - 1 \approx 4t - 6tv - \tfrac{3}{2}v^3$.

On the critical isotherm ($t = 0$), $v \approx -(2(p - 1)/3)^{1/3}$ and $\partial V/\partial p \approx -\tfrac{2}{9}v^{-2}$, giving $\lvert \partial V/\partial p \rvert \approx \tfrac{2}{9}(3/(2\delta))^{2/3} \approx 0.2912\,\delta^{-2/3}$ in terms of $\delta = \lvert p - 1 \rvert$.
On the critical isochore ($V = 1$, $p = 1 + 4t$, $t > 0$), $F_V = 6t$, so $\partial V/\partial p = -1/(6\delta)$ holds exactly for $\delta = t$.
The divergence exponents $2/3$ and $1$ depend on the approach to the critical point; both are mean-field exponents, differing from critical exponents measured in real fluids.

## Implicit solutions and `Residual`

`Residual` is a trait for writing a residual $F(y, \theta)$ with $n$ unknowns $y$ and $q$ parameters $\theta$ once for every numeric type `R: Real<S, Mask = bool>` with a scalar mask.
Here $n = 1$ and $q = 2$, with $y = (V)$ and $\theta = (p, T)$.

`implicit_solution_into(residual, solution, parameters, tolerance, out)` receives a solution `solution` (`&[S]`) satisfying $F = 0$ in the innermost values (base), excluding parameter derivatives, and writes $y(\theta)$ with derivatives to `out`.
It checks the supplied solution rather than solving $F = 0$.
At the innermost level, it evaluates $F$ at `solution` and checks $\lVert F \rVert_2 \le \mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}\,\lVert y \rVert_2$ with `tolerance` (`ResidualTolerance`); failure returns `InvalidInput` (argument `solution`, `OutOfRange`).

At each higher differentiation level, the same function obtains the solution one level below and forms columns of $F_y$ and $F_\theta$ from $n + q$ residual evaluations with unit `Dual` tangents.
It factorizes the base values of $F_y$ once by partially pivoted LU (`solve_into`), then uses `Real::combine` to form pairs of values and partial derivatives using $\partial y/\partial \theta = -F_y^{-1}F_\theta$.
On a tape, each output is recorded as one `combine` statement.
Repeating at each level makes second-order sensitivities correct through the nesting order for `Var<'t, Dual<f64>>` passed by `hessian`.

Per-level cost is $n + q$ residual evaluations, one $n \times n$ factorization, and $O(n(n + q))$ allocated workspace; this allocation remains even with tape reuse.
A length mismatch between `out` and `solution` returns `DimensionMismatch`; a zero or nonfinite LU pivot of $F_y$ returns `Singular`; residual errors propagate unchanged.
`Singular` occurs only for exactly zero or nonfinite pivots; a merely near-zero $F_y$ yields large finite sensitivities ([Differentiating implicit functions](../../algorithms/implicit-functions/)).

The solution itself is supplied as a nondifferentiated value, so root-finding computations (here companion-matrix eigenvalues refined by Aberth–Ehrlich iteration) are not recorded.
Sensitivity correctness depends only on whether the supplied root solves $F = 0$ and on the accuracy of $F_V$.

The following block defines the model shared by the two cells.
`VanDerWaals` implements residual $F$; all `R` operations are `Real` methods, evaluating the same expression with `f64`, `Dual`, or `Var`.
`volume_roots` obtains all cubic roots with scioxide-rootfind's `polynomial_roots_with_options` (default settings, method `Auto`) and returns real roots in ascending order.
Coefficients use the same ascending-power order $[-3,\,9,\,-(p + 8T),\,3p]$ as the rootfind example.
`residual_partials` supplies closed-form $F_V$ and $F_{VV}$ checks using `f64` arithmetic.

```rust
//| id: ad-vdw-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Reduced van der Waals residual and volume roots"
//| crates: [scioxide-ad, scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind]
use scioxide_ad::{Real, Residual};
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_rootfind::{PolynomialRootOptions, PolynomialRootReport, polynomial_roots_with_options};

/// Residual F(V; p, T) = p − 8T/(3V − 1) + 3/V². Unknown [V], parameters [p, T] (reduced quantities).
pub(crate) struct VanDerWaals;

impl Residual for VanDerWaals {
    fn residual<R: Real<f64, Mask = bool>>(
        &self,
        solution: &[R],
        parameters: &[R],
        out: &mut [R],
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let v = solution[0];
        let (p, t) = (parameters[0], parameters[1]);
        out[0] = p - t * 8.0 / (v * 3.0 - 1.0) + (v * v).recip() * 3.0;
        Ok(())
    }
}

/// Ascending real roots of 3pV³ − (p + 8T)V² + 9V − 3 = 0 and the all-roots report.
pub(crate) fn volume_roots(
    p: f64,
    t: f64,
) -> Result<(Vec<f64>, PolynomialRootReport<f64>), SciOxideError> {
    let coefficients = [-3.0, 9.0, -(p + 8.0 * t), 3.0 * p];
    let roots = polynomial_roots_with_options(&coefficients, &PolynomialRootOptions::default())?;
    // For real-coefficient polynomials, all-roots computation returns near-real roots with exactly zero imaginary part when its checks pass.
    let mut real: Vec<f64> = roots
        .roots()
        .iter()
        .filter(|z| z.im == 0.0)
        .map(|z| z.re)
        .collect();
    real.sort_by(f64::total_cmp);
    Ok((real, roots.report()))
}

/// Closed-form checks F_V = 24T/(3V − 1)² − 6/V³ and F_VV = 18/V⁴ − 144T/(3V − 1)³ (f64 arithmetic).
pub(crate) fn residual_partials(v: f64, t: f64) -> (f64, f64) {
    let d = 3.0 * v - 1.0;
    let f_v = 24.0 * t / (d * d) - 6.0 / (v * v * v);
    let f_vv = 18.0 / (v * v * v * v) - 144.0 * t / (d * d * d);
    (f_v, f_vv)
}
```

## Sensitivities of a selected state

The following cell obtains real cubic roots for $(T, p)$ with `volume_roots` and passes the selected root to `implicit_solution_into`.
`value_and_grad` returns $V$ and $(\partial V/\partial p, \partial V/\partial T)$ with one reverse sweep; `hessian` returns the $2 \times 2$ Hessian through directional forward-over-reverse recordings.
Set tolerances $\mathrm{atol} = 10^{-12}$ and $\mathrm{rtol} = 0$ in residual units (reduced pressure).
The supplied root has a residual at the rounding scale; this check rejects a mistaken root or unconverged value.

Inputs are reduced temperature, reduced pressure, and root selection (if there is only one real root, every selection uses it).
Outputs are a table of three sensitivities and their relative differences from closed forms, a table comparing molar volume, $\kappa_T$, and $\alpha_p$ with an ideal gas, and a list of real roots.
Finally, show the all-roots computation's `PolynomialRootReport` termination status as an outcome.
`Complete` means every root passed backward-error checks; forward root error is approximately the condition estimate times the backward error.

The all-roots status is `Complete`, with maximum backward error $4.5 \times 10^{-17}$ and maximum condition estimate $17.5$.

Compressibility factor $Z = 0.721$ shows that intermolecular attraction reduces volume below the ideal-gas value.
Isothermal compressibility $0.389$ MPa⁻¹ is 1.58 times the ideal-gas $1/p = 0.246$ MPa⁻¹; the same attraction makes the gas easier to compress.
Thermal expansion $8.95 \times 10^{-3}$ K⁻¹ is also 2.45 times the ideal-gas $1/T$.

Switching to the liquid root gives $V \approx 0.0803$ L/mol and $\kappa_T = 0.0592$ MPa⁻¹, about one seventh of the vapor value.
At the middle root, $\partial V_r/\partial p_r = 1.378 > 0$, giving negative $\kappa_T = -0.202$ MPa⁻¹; together with $(\partial p_r/\partial V_r)_T = 0.725 > 0$ in the real-root table, this indicates mechanical instability.

With $T = 1.00$ and $p = 1.01$, there is one real root ($V_r = 0.8458$); $\kappa_T = 0.674$ MPa⁻¹ is 5.0 times the ideal-gas value, and $\partial^2 V/\partial p^2 = 331.7$ exceeds the default by more than an order of magnitude.

At the exact critical point $T = 1.00$ and $p = 1.00$, the cubic has triple root $V = 1$.
All-roots computation returns three real roots $0.999995$, $0.9999996$, and $1.0000015$; backward-error checks pass, leaving termination status `Complete`.

## Divergence approaching the critical point

The following cell records $\ln V$ after the implicit solution and uses `grad` to obtain $\partial \ln V/\partial p$ in one reverse sweep.
Implicit solutions compose with ordinary operations, yielding $\kappa_T = -(\partial \ln V_r/\partial p_r)/p_c$ directly.
Take 21 logarithmically spaced reduced distances $\delta$ from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-1}$ and supply an all-roots result at each point.

The input selects an approach: critical isotherm ($T = 1$, $p = 1 \pm \delta$) or critical isochore ($V = 1$, $T = 1 + \delta$, $p = 1 + 4\delta$).
The log–log graph shows $\kappa_T$ and dashed asymptotes; the table compares the slope between the smallest two points with the mean-field exponent.
The last table classifies the `implicit_solution_into` error when exact root $V = 1$ is supplied at critical point $(1, 1)$.

//|   path: {"type":"select","label":"Approach","options":["Critical isotherm T = 1","Critical isochore V = 1"],"value":"Critical isotherm T = 1"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"isochore","label":"Critical isochore V = 1","description":"Points follow the dashed 1/(6δp_c) line throughout, with slope −1.","inputs":{"path":"Critical isochore V = 1"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"slopes","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":390.163,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"slopes","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":-0.6709,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"slopes","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":-0.6623,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"critical","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Singular"}
//|       - {"artifact":"critical","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|   - case: isochore

//|       - {"artifact":"slopes","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":-1,"atol":1e-6}}

//|       - {"artifact":"slopes","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":22591.82447,"rtol":1e-6}}
use scioxide_ad::{Real, grad, implicit_solution_into};
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideErrorKind, SciOxideErrorView, RealScalar, ResidualTolerance};

let pc = 7.3773; // CO2 critical pressure [MPa]
let tolerance = ResidualTolerance::new(1e-12, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let isotherm = path == "Critical isotherm T = 1";
// κ_T = −(∂ln V_r/∂p_r)/p_c. Record ln V after the implicit solution and differentiate with one reverse sweep.
let compressibility = |p: f64, t: f64, v: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let g = grad(
        |x| {
            let mut y = [x[0]];
            implicit_solution_into(&VanDerWaals, &[v], x, tolerance, &mut y)?;
            Ok(y[0].ln())
        },
        &[p, t],
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(-g.as_slice()[0] / pc)
};
let deltas: Vec<f64> = (0..=20)
    .map(|i| RealScalar::powf(10.0, -6.0 + 0.25 * f64::from(i)))
    .collect();
let sides: &[f64] = if isotherm { &[1.0, -1.0] } else { &[1.0] };
let mut series = Vec::new();
let mut slopes = Vec::new();
for &side in sides {
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for &delta in &deltas {
        let (p, t) = if isotherm {
            (1.0 + side * delta, 1.0)
        } else {
            (1.0 + 4.0 * delta, 1.0 + delta)
        };
        // For T ≥ 1, there is one real root. Stop if all-roots checks fail.
        let (volumes, report) = volume_roots(p, t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if !report.is_success() || volumes.len() != 1 {
            return Err(format!(
                "Volume root undetermined at δ = {delta:e}: {}",
                report.termination()
            ));
        }
        points.push([delta, compressibility(p, t, volumes[0])?]);
    }
    // Compare the smallest-δ value with the asymptote and measure the log–log slope between the smallest two points.
    let [d0, k0] = points[0];
    let [d1, k1] = points[1];
    let slope = RealScalar::ln(k1 / k0) / RealScalar::ln(d1 / d0);
    let label = match (isotherm, side > 0.0) {
        (false, _) => "T > 1",
        (true, true) => "p > 1",
        (true, false) => "p < 1",
    };
    let (asymptote, exponent) = if isotherm {
        (
            2.0 / 9.0 * RealScalar::powf(1.5 / d0, 2.0 / 3.0) / pc,
            -2.0 / 3.0,
        )
    } else {
        (1.0 / (6.0 * d0 * pc), -1.0)
    };
    slopes.push((label, k0, asymptote, slope, exponent));
    series.push(serde_json::json!({
        "name": format!("AD（{label}）"),
        "points": points,
        "style": { "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true }
    }));
}
// Independent checks: asymptotes. Isotherm (2/9)(3/(2δ))^(2/3)/p_c (V ≈ 1); isochore 1/(6δp_c) (exact at V = 1).
let asymptote: Vec<[f64; 2]> = deltas
    .iter()
    .map(|&d| {
        let value = if isotherm {
            2.0 / 9.0 * RealScalar::powf(1.5 / d, 2.0 / 3.0)
        } else {
            1.0 / (6.0 * d)
        };
        [d, value / pc]
    })
    .collect();
series.push(serde_json::json!({
    "name": "Asymptote",
    "points": asymptote,
    "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
}));
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Reduced distance from critical point δ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Isothermal compressibility κ_T [1/MPa]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 1e-6,
        "xMax": 0.1,
        "yMin": 0.01,
        "yMax": 1e5
    })
);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let digits5 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 5 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Branch" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "κ_T at δ = 10⁻⁶ [1/MPa]", "type": "number", "format": digits5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Asymptote [1/MPa]", "type": "number", "format": digits5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Slope d ln κ_T / d ln δ between smallest two points [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Mean-field exponent [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    slopes,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "slopes" })
);

// At critical point (V, T, p) = (1, 1, 1), F_V = 0 and the LU pivot of F_y is exactly 0.
let critical = grad(
    |x| {
        let mut y = [x[0]];
        implicit_solution_into(&VanDerWaals, &[1.0], x, tolerance, &mut y)?;
        Ok(y[0])
    },
    &[1.0, 1.0],
);
let row = match critical {
    Ok(_) => ("No error", None, String::new()),
    Err(e) => {
        let kind = match e.kind() {
            SciOxideErrorKind::Singular => "Singular",
            _ => "Other",
        };
        let pivot = match e.view() {
            SciOxideErrorView::Singular { pivot } => pivot,
            _ => None,
        };
        (kind, pivot, e.to_string())
    }
};
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Classification at critical point" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Pivot index", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },

    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "critical" })

On the critical isotherm, $\kappa_T$ at $\delta = 10^{-6}$ is 390.2 MPa⁻¹ for $p > 1$ and 399.4 MPa⁻¹ for $p < 1$, following the dashed line of slope $-2/3$.
The difference from asymptote 394.7 MPa⁻¹ is about 1.2%, the size of the first-order correction in $v \approx \mp 0.0087$.
Slopes between the smallest two points are $-0.6709$ for $p > 1$ and $-0.6623$ for $p < 1$; departures from $-2/3$ are first-order corrections in $v$ from the next expansion term ($p - 1 = -\tfrac{3}{2}v^3(1 - \tfrac{7}{2}v + \cdots)$) and factor $1/V$ in $\kappa_T$.
As $\delta$ increases, $V$ moves away from 1 and points depart further from the asymptote.

Switching to the critical isochore makes $V = 1$ an exact root; points follow dashed $1/(6\delta p_c)$ throughout with slope $-1.0000$ and value about $2.26 \times 10^{4}$ MPa⁻¹ at $\delta = 10^{-6}$.
Even at the same critical point, divergence rates differ by approach; use matching paths when comparing near-critical sensitivities.

Supplying exact root $V = 1$ at the critical point makes $F_V = 24/4 - 6 = 0$ exactly 0, and `implicit_solution_into` returns `Singular` (pivot 0).
As the previous cell showed, rounding the root prevents an error even at this point; absence of `Singular` does not imply distance from the critical point.

## Solution accuracy and sensitivity accuracy

Implicit-function-theorem sensitivities depend on $F_V$ and $F_\theta$ at the supplied root $V$.
Root error $\Delta V$ changes $F_V$ by $F_{VV}\,\Delta V$, giving sensitivity relative error approximately $\lvert F_{VV}\,\Delta V / F_V \rvert$.
Near the critical point, small $F_V$ and large root condition numbers both degrade sensitivity accuracy.

For an accepted root residual $\rho$, root error is $\Delta V \approx \rho/\lvert F_V \rvert$ and the formula above gives sensitivity relative error of about $\lvert F_{VV} \rvert \rho / F_V^2$.
Looser tolerance increases this error bound; choose tolerances matching the root-finding method's accuracy.

For $T < 1$, moving $p$ toward a spinodal pressure merges two roots with $F_V \to 0$, and sensitivities on that branch also diverge.
Beyond the spinodal pressure, those roots become complex conjugates, reducing real roots from three to one; the remaining root is used regardless of the selection input.

## Summary

Write residual $F$ once as a `Residual`, without differentiating root-finding iterations: `implicit_solution_into` attaches implicit-function-theorem derivatives to the supplied root, yielding first-order sensitivities with `value_and_grad` and second-order ones with `hessian`.
Sensitivities agree with closed forms $-1/F_V$, $8/((3V - 1)F_V)$, and $-F_{VV}/F_V^3$ within rounding error; obtain $\kappa_T$ and $\alpha_p$ from reduced values and critical constants.
Implicit solutions compose with later operations such as $\ln V$; divergence exponents approaching the critical point are $-2/3$ or $-1$ depending on the approach.
`implicit_solution_into` checks root residuals against tolerance but checks neither forward root error nor small $F_V$; inspect all-roots condition estimates and $\lvert F_V \rvert$ before using sensitivities.
`Singular` occurs only for exactly zero pivots of $F_y$ and cannot detect proximity to the critical point.
