Mathematically equivalent formulas can produce different floating-point results.
Even when the final result fits, overflowing intermediate products or sums produce `inf` or `NaN`.
Adding terms with very different magnitudes can round away the smaller term (loss of small contributions).
Range-safe arithmetic in `scioxide-core` avoids these problems by retaining exact intermediates and rounding once at the end, or separating significands and exponents to keep intermediates in range.
[Floating-point numbers and condition numbers](../../../concepts/floating-point-and-conditioning/) covers general concepts such as unit roundoff $u$ and cancellation.

## Loss of small contributions and fixed-size integer accumulators

Summing $a = 10^{16}$, $b = 1$, and $c = -10^{16}$ left-to-right gives zero: spacing in double precision near $10^{16}$ is 2, so $10^{16} + 1$ rounds to $10^{16}$ (the real sum is 1).

`RangeAccumulator` stores finite floating-point values as integer digits, eliminating this error.

- It holds positive and negative digit arrays (limbs), each containing 100 64-bit words.
  The least significant bit has weight $2^{-3222}$, covering products as small as $(2^{-1074})^3$ for three double-precision values.
- Each significand is added without rounding to words selected by its exponent.
  Products `add_product` and triple products `add_triple_product` also accumulate exactly as integer significand products.
- `finish()` subtracts negative digits from positive digits and converts to the target type with one round-to-nearest, ties-to-even operation.
  Exact cancellation returns $+0$.
- Storage is fixed-size on the stack, without heap allocation.
  Each update and `merge` of two states takes constant time independently of input length.
- Since `merge` is exact, partitioned partial sums merge to the same result in any order.

Non-finite inputs are rejected as `NonFinite` (`NonFiniteOrigin::Input`) before modifying state.
If the final result exceeds the type's finite range, `finish()` returns `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
`finish()` only borrows state, preserving the accumulated value.
Updates and `merge` return the same error on limb-capacity overflow, but carry headroom prevents this for sums of inputs whose lengths fit memory.
Failed updates and `merge` preserve state.

`finish_scaled()` retrieves out-of-range sums as extended-range `Scaled`; `finish_quotient(d)` divides by $d$ and rounds the quotient once.

`checked_sum` returns the exact sum rounded to nearest, ties to even, in time proportional to input length.
Up to two terms use one IEEE addition.
Three terms use TwoSum (an error-free sum/residual transformation); four or more use Ogita–Rump–Oishi compensated Sum2, returning its candidate only when the error bound identifies one rounding target uniquely.
Otherwise exact accumulation is used, giving identical results on every path.
`checked_sum_of_products` similarly uses error-free products and rounding checks for up to four pairs, falling back to exact accumulation when checks are inconclusive.

Where spacing equals 2 (points on the second chart's horizontal line), $L + 1$ is a midpoint and ties-to-even rounding gives $L$ or $L + 2$, producing a fraction of 0 or 2.
At $L > 2^{54} \approx 1.8 \times 10^{16}$, where spacing is at least 4, $s$ is below half a spacing and the fraction is zero.
The table's left-to-right sum is 1 at $L = 10^{8}$ and zero at $L = 10^{16}$ and $10^{18}$; `checked_sum` and `merge` columns equal 1 for every $L$.

At $s = 0.01$, the left-to-right sum for $L = 10^{8}$ is $0.0100000054$; rounding at spacing $1.5 \times 10^{-8}$ changes significant digits of $s$ from the eighth onward.
At $s = 10^{5}$, the left-to-right sum for $L = 10^{18}$ is 99968, a multiple of spacing 128.
Left-to-right sums can take only integer multiples of spacing, so the surviving fraction forms steps as spacing approaches $s$ and becomes zero when spacing / $s$ exceeds 2.
For every $s$, the `checked_sum` and `merge` columns match $s$.

## Compensated sums and exact accumulation

`CompensatedSum` uses Neumaier compensation, retaining only two scalars: a main sum and rounding residual.
Start from `ZERO`, repeatedly apply `add`, and read with `total`.
`parts` and `from_parts` save and restore both finite components.
Each operation has constant work without allocation; failed additions preserve both components.

Use `compensated_reduce` to sum multiple quantities in one pass.
It builds `N` `CompensatedSum` values from an iterator of length-`N` arrays.
Each column uses four TwoSum lanes, merged in fixed order every 256 rows, giving consistent results between calls (rounding may differ from sequential `add` updates).

Compensation retains first-order rounding errors but works at working precision, guaranteeing neither exact sign nor correct rounding.
Choose `RangeAccumulator` for an exact sum with one rounding.

`PromotingCompensatedSum` uses `CompensatedSum` in ordinary range, promoting once to `ScaledAccumulator` by exactly transferring both components on a subnormal product or overflow.
When adding terms individually through `add_value` or `add_product`, terms preceding promotion need not be re-added.
Iterator constructors `from_values` and `from_products` rescan cloned iterators when range is exceeded.
This supports wide exponents but remains working-precision compensation, not exact accumulation.

`PromotingProduct` likewise moves products to `Scaled` when intermediates become subnormal or overflow.
It rounds per factor, unlike an exact product rounded only at the end.
Both are fixed-size values, reporting non-finite input as `NonFinite` and exponents beyond `i64` as `NumericalInstability` (`ExponentOutOfRange`).

`fast_mul_add` uses FMA when `HARDWARE_FMA` is true for the target; otherwise it computes product and sum separately.
The latter rounds twice and cannot replace `checked_mul_add` or an error-free product residual.
Use it in approximate algorithms permitting two roundings.

## Accuracy contracts for checked arithmetic

`checked_*` functions accept finite inputs only and report unrepresentable final results as `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
Non-finite input produces `NonFinite`; division by zero produces `DomainError`.
Functions marked “one rounding” return the mathematical value rounded to nearest, ties to even (0 ULP from the correctly rounded result, at most 0.5 ULP from the mathematical value).
Sums, products, dot products, and quotients return exact zero as $+0$; negative nonzero values rounded to zero become $-0$.

| Function | Computed value | Accuracy contract |
|---|---|---|
| `checked_sum` | $\sum_i x_i$ | One rounding |
| `checked_sum_of_products` | $\sum_i a_i b_i$ | One rounding. Length mismatches produce `DimensionMismatch` |
| `checked_difference` | $a - b$ | One rounding |
| `checked_difference_of_products` | $ab - cd$ | One rounding |
| `checked_mul_add` | $ab + c$ | One rounding (IEEE fused multiply-add for `std`, accumulator for `no_std`, with identical results) |
| `checked_float_product` | $ab$ | One rounding |
| `checked_float_product_factors` | $\prod_{i=1}^{n} x_i$ | $\gamma_{n-1} \lvert P \rvert + \eta/2$. Empty products equal 1 |
| `checked_float_quotient` | $a / b$ | One rounding |
| `checked_product_quotient` | $ab / c$ | Within 4 ULP ($\gamma_2 \lvert Q \rvert + \eta/2$) |
| `checked_midpoint` | $(a + b) / 2$ | One rounding without overflow |
| `checked_affine` | $(1-t)a + tb$, $t \in [0, 1]$ | One rounding. $t = 0, 1$ returns endpoints themselves; out-of-range $t$ produces `InvalidInput` |
| `checked_affine_ratio` | $\bigl((d-n)a + nb\bigr)/d$, integer $0 \le n \le d$ | One rounding. $n = 0, d$ returns endpoints themselves; $d = 0$ or $n > d$ produces `InvalidInput` |
| `checked_scalbn` | $x \cdot 2^n$ | One rounding (rounding occurs only for subnormal or zero results) |
| `checked_next_after` | Adjacent representable number | Exact |
| `checked_scaled_norm` | $\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert_2$ | [LASSQ error bounds](#lassq-type-scaled-norms) |
| `checked_quadratic_roots` | Roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ | [Residual bounds](oxref:core-quadratic-roots) |

Here $P$ and $Q$ are exact product and quotient, $u = \varepsilon / 2$ unit roundoff, $\gamma_k = k u / (1 - k u)$ ($ku < 1$), and $\eta$ the smallest positive subnormal.
The $\eta/2$ term appears only for subnormal results.

`checked_float_product_factors` decomposes factors into significands and exponents, multiplying significands in input order, summing exponents separately, and restoring scale once at the end.
Intermediate products therefore neither overflow nor underflow.
Significands round after every multiplication: $n$ factors cause $n - 1$ roundings, bounding relative error by $(1 + u)^{n-1} - 1 \le \gamma_{n-1}$.
Error grows proportionally to factor count.
`checked_product_quotient` likewise computes with normalized significands without forming products $ab$.

`ProductSum<S>` retains exact sums of real or complex two- and three-factor products, rounding each component once through `finish()`.
Expanding complex products into real and imaginary parts allows complex inner products with one rounding per component.

## Endpoint-preserving affine combinations

Midpoints, interior division points, and equally spaced grid points in $[a, b]$ recur in interpolation, bisection, and quadrature nodes.
$(a + b)/2$ can overflow in $a + b$.
$a + t(b - a)$ can overflow in $b - a$ and may fail to recover $b$ at $t = 1$.
Repeatedly adding step $h = (b - a)/d$ accumulates the rounding error in $h$ proportionally to the point index.

`checked_midpoint`, `checked_affine`, and `checked_affine_ratio` all round exact values once.
Single rounding is monotone, keeping results between the endpoints inclusively and preventing overflow.

`checked_affine_ratio(left, right, n, d)` computes $\bigl((d - n)\,\mathrm{left} + n\,\mathrm{right}\bigr)/d$ with integer weights.
Weights $d - n$ and $n$ remain exact `usize` integers, even with more bits than the significand.
Not converting ratio $n/d$ to floating point first ensures one rounding of the mathematical division point even for unrepresentable ratios such as $1/3$.
$n = 0$ and $n = d$ return `left` and `right` bit for bit.
Construct equally spaced point $i$ through `checked_affine_ratio(left, right, i, d)`.

The following cell constructs three grids dividing interval $[0, 1]$ into $d$ equal parts.
Input is integer subdivision count $d$ (3–1000).
Here point $i$ equals $i/d$, so one double-precision division `i / d` is a correctly rounded reference.
For each method, the table shows the count of differing points, maximum reference difference in local ulp spacing, and endpoint minus 1.

```rust
//| id: core-affine-ratio-grid
//| ref: core-affine-ratio-grid
//| title: "Constructing equally spaced grid points three ways"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   divisions: {"type":"range","label":"Subdivision count d [-]","description":"Divide [0, 1] into d equal parts, giving d + 1 points.","min":3,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fine-grid","label":"d = 1000","description":"Repeated-step errors accumulate with point count.","inputs":{"divisions":1000}}
//|   - {"id":"missed-endpoint","label":"d = 49","description":"Even left + i·h fails to recover endpoint 1.","inputs":{"divisions":49}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/2/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":-1.11e-16,"atol":1e-18}}
//|   - case: fine-grid
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":1}}
//|   - case: missed-endpoint
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/2/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"grid","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":-1.11e-16,"atol":1e-18}}
use scioxide_core::{RealScalar, checked_affine_ratio};

let d = divisions as usize;
let (left, right) = (0.0_f64, 1.0_f64);
let step = (right - left) / d as f64;
// Rows are repeated stepping, left + i·h, and checked_affine_ratio.
let mut differing = [0_u32; 3];
let mut worst = [0.0_f64; 3];
let mut last = [left; 3];
let mut running = left;
for i in 0..=d {
    // Reference: points on [0, 1] equal i/d; one IEEE division gives correct rounding.
    let reference = i as f64 / d as f64;
    let ulp = RealScalar::next_after(reference, f64::INFINITY) - reference;
    let ratio = checked_affine_ratio(left, right, i, d).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    last = [running, left + i as f64 * step, ratio];
    for (method, value) in last.into_iter().enumerate() {
        let error = (value - reference).abs() / ulp;
        if error > 0.0 {
            differing[method] += 1;
        }
        worst[method] = RealScalar::max(worst[method], error);
    }
    running += step;
}
let names = ["Repeatedly add step h", "left + i·h", "checked_affine_ratio"];
let rows: Vec<(&str, u32, f64, f64)> = (0..3)
    .map(|method| {
        (
            names[method],
            differing[method],
            worst[method],
            last[method] - right,
        )
    })
    .collect();
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Points differing from reference [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum difference [ulp]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Endpoint − 1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "grid" })
);
```

At the default $d = 10$, repeating step $h = 0.1$ makes four of eleven points differ by 1 ulp; the endpoint is $1 - 1.1 \times 10^{-16}$ rather than 1.
`left + i·h` differs by 1 ulp at three points.
`checked_affine_ratio` matches every reference point and gives endpoint exactly 1.

At $d = 1000$, repeated stepping differs at 992 of 1001 points, accumulating up to 7 ulp.
`left + i·h` stays within 1 ulp but differs at 144 points.
At $d = 49$, even `left + i·h` gives endpoint $1 - 1.1 \times 10^{-16}$.
This 1 ulp discrepancy matters when subsequent processing assumes endpoint `right`, such as boundary evaluation or connecting adjacent intervals.
`checked_affine_ratio` matches references at every point for every $d$.

## LASSQ-type scaled norms

Direct $\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert_2 = \sqrt{\sum_i x_i^2}$ overflows $x_i^2$ beyond the largest double (about $1.8 \times 10^{308}$) for $x_i = 10^{200}$, and makes $x_i^2$ zero for $x_i = 10^{-200}$.
`ScaledNorm` and `checked_scaled_norm` retain $\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert_2 = s \sqrt{q}$ through scale $s$ and normalized sum of squares $q$.
$s$ is the largest absolute component added so far, with $q \ge 1$ ($s = q = 0$ if all components are zero).
For a new component $x \ne 0$, update as follows.

- If $|x| \le s$, use $q \leftarrow q + (|x| / s)^2$.
- If $|x| > s$, use $q \leftarrow 1 + q\,(s / |x|)^2$ and $s \leftarrow |x|$.

These are the update rules.
Squared ratios never exceed 1, preventing square overflow.
Underflowing squared ratios are negligible relative to $q \ge 1$ and included in the error bound below.
`finish()` returns $s \sqrt{q}$, producing `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`) only when the norm exceeds finite range.
`checked_scaled_norm` updates `ScaledNorm` in time proportional to component count.

Two states combine through `merge`; merging in partition-index order gives identical results independently of executor.
Failed updates and merges preserve state.

Error depends on summation order and grows proportionally to component count.
Let $m$ count nonzero component updates and nontrivial merges; with $k = 8m + 8$, when $ku < 1$,

$$
\bigl|\, \widehat{\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert} - \lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert_2 \,\bigr| \le \gamma_k \lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert_2 + \frac{\eta}{2}
$$


This bound includes rounding of ratios, squares, addition, square roots, and the final product.
In double precision, $m = 10^6$ gives $\gamma_k \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-10}$.
For $ku \ge 1$, no useful relative error bound applies.

## Quadratic roots and residual contracts

The quadratic formula for $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ ($a \ne 0$),

$$
x_\pm = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}, \qquad D = b^2 - 4ac
$$

subtracts nearby quantities in one numerator when $b^2 \gg 4|ac|$.
Rounding error in $\sqrt{D} \approx |b|$ is about $u|b|$, while the subtraction result is only $2|a|$ times the small root $x_{\rm s} \approx -c/b$, of order $2|ac|/|b|$.
First-order estimates therefore amplify the small root's relative error to order $u\, b^2 / (2|ac|)$, leaving no significant digits at $b^2 \gtrsim 2|ac|/u$.

Avoid cancellation by computing only the root where $-b$ and $\pm\sqrt{D}$ share a sign, then obtain the other through Vieta's relation $x_1 x_2 = c/a$.

$$
q = -\frac{1}{2}\left( b + \operatorname{sign}(b)\sqrt{D} \right), \qquad x_1 = \frac{q}{a}, \qquad x_2 = \frac{c}{q}
$$

Both terms in $q$ have the same sign, giving $|q| \ge |b|/2$ and avoiding cancellation in $q$.
$x_2 = c/q$ also contains no subtraction, preserving small-root accuracy even when root magnitudes are widely separated.
For $b = 0$, roots are $\pm\sqrt{D}/(2a)$ without cancellation to begin with.

`checked_quadratic_roots(a, b, c)` computes roots with this $q$ formula when $D > 0$.
For $D < 0$, returns conjugate roots with real part $-b/(2a)$ and imaginary parts $\pm\sqrt{-D}/(2|a|)$.
Cancellation remains in computing $D = b^2 - 4ac$ itself: nearby $b^2$ and $4ac$ can even corrupt the sign of $D$, misclassifying real versus complex roots.
Check the sign and error of $D$ in two stages.

1. For `std` with every coefficient magnitude in $[2^{-200}, 2^{200}]$ ($[2^{-24}, 2^{24}]$ for `f32`), compute $D$ in double precision from FMA product residuals and TwoSum.
   Use this $D$ only when its computed error bound is sufficiently small relative to $|D|$.
2. Otherwise, accumulate $b^2$ and $-4ac$ exactly in `RangeAccumulator` to decide the sign, then take an exponent-scaled square root.
   If exactly $D = 0$, return repeated root $-b/(2a)$.

Computation uses constant time and fixed stack storage without heap allocation.
$a = 0$ produces `InvalidInput`, non-finite coefficients produce `NonFinite`, and roots outside the finite range produce `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).

Success returns both roots in array `[Complex<S>; 2]`.
Real roots also use `Complex<S>`, with exactly zero components set to $+0$ (real-root imaginary parts are $+0$).
Nonzero components underflowing to zero retain their mathematical signs.
Real roots ascend, conjugates place positive imaginary part first, and repeated roots appear twice.
Both roots are finite.

Near repeated roots, conditioning is very large, so forward accuracy cannot be guaranteed in ULP.
Instead, every returned root $z$ has a residual guarantee for $p(z) = az^2 + bz + c$.
With $x = |\operatorname{Re} z| + |\operatorname{Im} z|$ and $T = |a| x^2 + |b| x + |c|$, the sum of absolute real and imaginary parts of $p(z)$ is at most

$$
32\, \varepsilon\, T + (2|a|x + |b|)\, \eta + |a|\, \eta^2
$$


$\varepsilon$ is machine epsilon; the $\eta$ term represents rounding root components into subnormals or zero.
For a simple root $z$, first-order forward error is approximately this residual divided by $|p'(z)| = |2az + b|$, growing as $p'(z)$ approaches zero near a repeated root.

```rust
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   b_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Coefficient exponent p [-]","description":"b = −10^p. Larger p separates the two roots further.","min":2,"max":16,"step":1,"value":8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"lost-root","label":"p = 16","description":"The quadratic formula's small root becomes zero.","inputs":{"b_exponent":16}}
//|   - {"id":"close-roots","label":"p = 2","description":"The root ratio is 10⁴, leaving significant digits even in the quadratic formula.","inputs":{"b_exponent":2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1e-8,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":7.45e-9,"rtol":1e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1.5e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0,"max":142}}
//|   - case: lost-root
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1e-16,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1.5e-14}}
//|   - case: close-roots
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.010001000200050014,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1.5e-14}}
use scioxide_core::{RangeAccumulator, RealScalar, checked_quadratic_roots};

let (a, c) = (1.0_f64, 1.0_f64);
let b = -RealScalar::powi(10.0_f64, b_exponent as i32);
// Real roots ascend; both imaginary parts are +0.
let [small, large] = checked_quadratic_roots(a, b, c).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Comparison: use the quadratic formula directly with intrinsic f64 methods, causing cancellation in the small root.
let root_disc = (b * b - 4.0 * a * c).sqrt();
let naive_small = (-b - root_disc) / (2.0 * a);
let naive_large = (-b + root_disc) / (2.0 * a);

// Check: accumulate a z² + b z + c exactly and round once (real roots have zero imaginary part).
let residual = |z: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let mut sum = RangeAccumulator::new();
    sum.add_triple_product(a, z, z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    sum.add_product(b, z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    sum.add(c).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(sum.finish().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.abs())
};
// Residual bound 32 ε T + (2|a|x + |b|) η + |a| η².
let bound = |z: f64| {
    let x = z.abs();
    let t = a.abs() * x * x + b.abs() * x + c.abs();
    let eta = f64::from_bits(1);
    32.0 * f64::EPSILON * t + (2.0 * a.abs() * x + b.abs()) * eta + a.abs() * eta * eta
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, naive, root) in [
    ("Small root", naive_small, small.re),
    ("Large root", naive_large, large.re),
] {
    rows.push((
        name,
        naive,
        root,
        residual(naive)?,
        residual(root)?,
        bound(root),
    ));
}
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 7 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Root" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Quadratic formula [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "2", "label": "checked_quadratic_roots [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Quadratic formula residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Checked residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Residual bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "roots" })
);
```

At the default $p = 8$, the direct formula's small root is $7.45 \times 10^{-9}$, about 25% from the true root (approximately $10^{-8}$), giving residual about 0.25.
This discrepancy fits the estimate $u\,b^2/(2|ac|) \approx 0.55$ above.
The small root from `checked_quadratic_roots` is $1.000000 \times 10^{-8}$, with residual $7.9 \times 10^{-17}$ below bound $1.4 \times 10^{-14}$.
Both methods give large root $10^{8}$, with residual 1 within bound 142.
The residual bound scales with $T$, so residual 1 for the large root at $T \approx 2 \times 10^{16}$ corresponds relatively to $5 \times 10^{-17}$.

At $p \ge 9$, $b^2 - 4$ rounds to $b^2$, making the direct small root zero and its residual exactly $c = 1$.
Even at $p = 16$, the small root from `checked_quadratic_roots` is $10^{-16}$ with residual $2.1 \times 10^{-17}$.
At $p = 2$, the direct small root agrees to about 12 digits, but residual $1.2 \times 10^{-13}$ exceeds bound $1.4 \times 10^{-14}$ for `checked_quadratic_roots` roots.

## Summary

Accumulate differently scaled sums and cancellation-prone dot products exactly through `checked_sum`, `checked_sum_of_products`, or `RangeAccumulator`, rounding once at the end.
Merge partitioned partial sums through `merge` on `RangeAccumulator` states for partition-independent results.
Use `checked_float_product_factors` or `checked_product_quotient` for product chains or expressions such as $ab/c$ with out-of-range intermediates, reading errors in ULP or $\gamma_k$ bounds.
Construct midpoints, division points, and equally spaced grids through `checked_midpoint`, `checked_affine`, or `checked_affine_ratio` to preserve endpoints and keep results between them.
`checked_quadratic_roots` uses cancellation-free quadratic formulas, with residual bounds for both returned roots.
Assess forward error separately near repeated roots.
