Partition functions underlie gas heat capacities, free energies, and chemical equilibrium constants.
Diatomic rotation sums products of degeneracy $2J + 1$ and Boltzmann factor $e^{-E_J / k_B T}$.
Referencing energies to dissociation in chemical equilibrium lowers every level by dissociation energy $D_0$, making exponents too large for direct evaluation.
This example uses weighted logarithmic sums `weighted_logsumexp` and normalization `softmax_into` to compute partition functions and populations reliably despite that shift.

## Rigid-rotor model

Treating heteronuclear diatomics such as CO, HCl, and HF as rigid rotors gives rotational energies for quantum number $J = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$,

$$
E_J = h c B\, J(J+1) = k_B \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}\, J(J+1), \qquad \theta_{\mathrm{rot}} = \frac{h c B}{k_B}
$$


$B$ is the rotational constant [cm⁻¹] (equilibrium value $B_e$ below), $\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$ the characteristic rotational temperature [K], and $hc/k_B \approx 1.4388$ cm·K. Magnetic quantum numbers $M_J = -J, \ldots, J$ give degeneracy $g_J = 2J + 1$ per level.
Homonuclear molecules need additional nuclear-spin statistical weights and are excluded here.

The rotational partition function, occupation probability of level $J$, and rotational internal energy are

$$
q(T) = \sum_{J=0}^{\infty} (2J+1)\, e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rot}} J(J+1)/T}, \qquad
p_J = \frac{(2J+1)\, e^{-\theta_{\mathrm{rot}} J(J+1)/T}}{q(T)}, \qquad
\frac{U}{k_B T} = \sum_J p_J\, \frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rot}} J(J+1)}{T}
$$


| Molecule | $B$ [cm⁻¹] | $\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$ [K] | $D_0$ [eV] |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO | 1.931 | 2.78 | 11.09 |
| HCl | 10.59 | 15.2 | 4.43 |
| HF | 20.96 | 30.2 | 5.87 |

## Overflow with a dissociation reference

Referencing energy to separated atoms changes level $J = 0$ to $-D_0$ and gives exponents

$$
v_J = -\frac{E_J - D_0}{k_B T} = \frac{D_0}{k_B T} - \frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rot}} J(J+1)}{T}
$$


For CO with $D_0 = 11.09$ eV and $k_B = 8.617333 \times 10^{-5}$ eV/K, temperature $T = 100$ K gives $D_0 / k_B T \approx 1287$.
The largest double is $e^{709.78}$, so $e^{1287}$ is unrepresentable and direct summation becomes `inf`.

In logarithmic sums, shift $C = D_0 / k_B T$ is simply added to the result as $\ln q' = C + \ln q$, leaving probabilities $p_J$ unchanged.
`weighted_logsumexp(v, g)` returns $\ln q'$ as `LogValue` from weights $g_J = 2J + 1$ and exponents $v_J$; `softmax_into` normalizes $v_J + \ln g_J$ to obtain $p_J$.

## Independent checks

For $T \gg \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$, use Mulholland's expansion from Euler–Maclaurin summation,

$$
q(T) = \frac{T}{\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}} \left( 1 + \frac{x}{3} + \frac{x^2}{15} + \frac{4 x^3}{315} + O(x^4) \right), \qquad x = \frac{\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}}{T}
$$


Computing $U / k_B T = d \ln q / d \ln T$ from this expansion gives

$$
\frac{U}{k_B T} = 1 - \frac{x}{3} - \frac{x^2}{45} + O(x^3)
$$

This approaches the classical equipartition value 1 (two rotational degrees of freedom) as $x \to 0$.
For $T \lesssim \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$, the first three terms $q \approx 1 + 3 e^{-2x} + 5 e^{-6x}$ give a good approximation.
Also, treating $J$ continuously and maximizing $p_J$ gives $(2J + 1)^2 = 2T/\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$, placing the most populated level near $J \approx \sqrt{T / 2\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}} - 1/2$.

Truncate the sum at $\theta_{\mathrm{rot}} J(J+1)/T > 60$ (Boltzmann factor below $e^{-60} \approx 10^{-26}$) or at $J = 400$.
Throughout temperature range $T \le 3162$ K, the first condition always determines truncation.

Both cells share the following definitions.
`molecule_constants` returns tabulated $\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$ and $D_0$; `reduced_energies` lists reduced energies $\epsilon_J = \theta_{\mathrm{rot}} J(J+1)/T$ through truncation; `degeneracies` returns degeneracies $g_J = 2J + 1$.

```rust
//| id: core-rotational-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Rigid-rotor constants and reduced energies"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
/// Characteristic rotational temperature θ_rot [K] and dissociation energy D₀ [eV].
pub(crate) fn molecule_constants(molecule: &str) -> (f64, f64) {
    match molecule {
        "HCl" => (15.2, 4.43),
        "HF" => (30.2, 5.87),
        _ => (2.78, 11.09),
    }
}

/// Reduced energy ε_J = θ_rot J(J+1)/T [-]. List through ε_J > 60 or J = 400.
pub(crate) fn reduced_energies(theta: f64, temperature: f64) -> Vec<f64> {
    let energy = |j: u32| theta * f64::from(j * (j + 1)) / temperature;
    let last = (0..=400).find(|&j| energy(j) > 60.0).unwrap_or(400);
    (0..=last).map(energy).collect()
}

/// Degeneracy g_J = 2J + 1 (J = 0, …, levels − 1).
pub(crate) fn degeneracies(levels: usize) -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..levels).map(|j| 2.0 * j as f64 + 1.0).collect()
}
```

## Logarithm of the partition function

Compute $\ln q'$ with `weighted_logsumexp` and compare with `logsumexp` of $v_J + \ln g_J$, combining exponents with logarithmic degeneracies.
Inputs are molecule, the base-10 logarithm of temperature $T$ [K], and whether to use the dissociation reference.
Selecting the dissociation reference adds $D_0 / k_B T$ to all exponents.

The first table shows both $\ln q'$ methods and the logarithm of directly summed exponentials.
The second compares shift-subtracted $\ln q = \ln q' - D_0/k_B T$ against an independent reference.
The reference uses Mulholland's expansion for $T > \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$ and a three-term sum at or below that threshold, evaluated with intrinsic `f64` methods.
Both are approximate: near transition $T \approx \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$, differences of order $3 \times 10^{-3}$ remain on the expansion side and $10^{-5}$ on the three-term side.

```rust
//| id: core-rotational-partition-function
//| ref: core-rotational-partition-function
//| title: "Logarithm of the rotational partition function"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [core-rotational-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   molecule: {"type":"select","label":"Molecule","options":["CO","HCl","HF"],"value":"CO"}
//|   log_t: {"type":"range","label":"Temperature log₁₀(T / K)","description":"0 means 1 K, 2 means 100 K, and 3.5 about 3162 K.","min":0,"max":3.5,"step":0.1,"value":2}
//|   include_d0: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Use the dissociation reference","description":"Add D₀ / k_B T to every level exponent.","value":true}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"hf-cold","label":"HF, 10 K","description":"T < θ_rot switches the reference to a three-term sum.","inputs":{"molecule":"HF","log_t":1}}
//|   - {"id":"ground-reference","label":"CO, 100 K, ground-level reference","description":"Without a shift, directly summed exponentials remain finite.","inputs":{"include_d0":false}}
//|   - {"id":"hcl-hot","label":"HCl, 1000 K","description":"Many levels contribute at high temperature, agreeing with Mulholland's expansion.","inputs":{"molecule":"HCl","log_t":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sums","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1290.5330934028543,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sums","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":3.59199457,"atol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-8,"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: hf-cold
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Three-term sum"}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-11,"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sums","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":null}
//|   - case: ground-reference
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sums","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"sums","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":3.5919945733209,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-8,"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: hcl-hot
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Mulholland expansion"}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-8,"max":1e-8}}
use scioxide_core::{RealScalar, logsumexp, weighted_logsumexp};

let (theta, d0_ev) = molecule_constants(&molecule);
let t = RealScalar::powf(10.0_f64, log_t);
// A dissociation reference adds D₀ / k_B T to all exponents (k_B = 8.617333e-5 eV/K).
let shift = if include_d0 {
    d0_ev / (8.617333e-5 * t)
} else {
    0.0
};
let energy = reduced_energies(theta, t);
let degeneracy = degeneracies(energy.len());
// Exponent v_J = D₀ / k_B T − θ J(J+1)/T.
let logits: Vec<f64> = energy.iter().map(|e| shift - e).collect();

// Pass weights g_J separately from exponents v_J.
let ln_q = weighted_logsumexp(&logits, &degeneracy)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .log_magnitude()
    .ok_or("The sum became zero")?;
// Take logarithms of weights and combine them with exponents.
let combined: Vec<f64> = logits
    .iter()
    .zip(&degeneracy)
    .map(|(&v, &g)| v + RealScalar::ln(g))
    .collect();
let ln_q_combined = logsumexp(&combined).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Comparison: sum exponentials directly with intrinsic f64 methods; non-finite values become None.
let direct: f64 = logits
    .iter()
    .zip(&degeneracy)
    .map(|(v, g)| g * v.exp())
    .sum::<f64>()
    .ln();
let direct = RealScalar::is_finite(direct).then_some(direct);

let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let lost = "Non-finite";
let sum_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Temperature T [K]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Truncation level J [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Shift D₀ / k_B T [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "ln q'（weighted_logsumexp）[-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "4", "label": "ln q' (logsumexp of v + ln g) [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Direct-sum ln q' [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits, "nullText": lost }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    sum_columns,
    vec![(t, energy.len() - 1, shift, ln_q, ln_q_combined, direct)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sums" })
);

// Independent references with intrinsic f64 methods; x = θ_rot / T.
let x = theta / t;
let (name, reference) = if t > theta {
    let series = 1.0 + x / 3.0 + x * x / 15.0 + 4.0 * x.powi(3) / 315.0;
    ("Mulholland expansion", (series / x).ln())
} else {
    let terms = 1.0 + 3.0 * (-2.0 * x).exp() + 5.0 * (-6.0 * x).exp();
    ("Three-term sum", terms.ln())
};
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Reference kind" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "ln q = ln q' − D₀ / k_B T [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Reference [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![(name, ln_q - shift, reference, (ln_q - shift) - reference)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "check" })
);
```

At the defaults (CO, $T = 100$ K, dissociation reference), $\ln q' \approx 1290.533$ and direct exponential sums become non-finite.
`weighted_logsumexp` and `logsumexp` agree to displayed digits.
Shift-subtracted $\ln q \approx 3.59199$ differs from Mulholland's expansion by $1.9 \times 10^{-9}$, matching its next term $x^4/315 \approx 1.9 \times 10^{-9}$ ($x = 0.0278$).
Using the ground-level reference makes the shift zero, and direct sums give the same $\ln q$.

For HF at $T = 10$ K ($\log_{10} T = 1$), $T < \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$ and the reference switches to three terms.
The $J \ge 3$ contribution is only of order $10^{-15}$, making three terms nearly exact.
With a dissociation reference, difference $4 \times 10^{-13}$ fits adjacent spacing in double precision near $\ln q' \approx 6812$ (about $9 \times 10^{-13}$).
It is rounding in $\ln q'$ retained in small $\ln q \approx 0.0071$ after subtracting the shift.
At $T = 1000$ K for HCl, the shift is only about 51, so direct sums remain finite and agree with log-sum-exp.

## Populations and internal energy

Normalize $v_J + \ln g_J$ through `softmax_into` to obtain $p_J$, compute $\sum_J p_J$ with `checked_sum`, and $U / k_B T = \sum_J p_J\, \theta_{\mathrm{rot}} J(J+1)/T$ with `checked_sum_of_products`.
Inputs are molecule and the base-10 logarithm of temperature $T$ [K].
Dissociation shift $D_0 / k_B T$ leaves $p_J$ unchanged and is omitted here.

The first table shows probability sums and reference differences for $U / k_B T$.
The reference uses expansion $1 - x/3 - x^2/45$ at $T > \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$, and a sum over only levels $J \le 2$ at or below that threshold.
The second compares the most populated level with continuous prediction $\max(0, \sqrt{T/2\theta_{\mathrm{rot}}} - 1/2)$.
Plot $p_J$ from $J = 0$ through 40, marking the continuous most-populated level vertically.

```rust
//| id: core-rotational-populations
//| ref: core-rotational-populations
//| title: "Rotational level probabilities and internal energy"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [core-rotational-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   molecule: {"type":"select","label":"Molecule","options":["CO","HCl","HF"],"value":"CO"}
//|   log_t: {"type":"range","label":"Temperature log₁₀(T / K)","description":"0 means 1 K, 2 means 100 K, and 3.5 about 3162 K.","min":0,"max":3.5,"step":0.1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"hf-cold","label":"HF, 10 K","description":"Almost all molecules occupy J = 0; rotational excitation is frozen out.","inputs":{"molecule":"HF","log_t":1}}
//|   - {"id":"co-hot","label":"CO, 1000 K","description":"The most populated level rises to J = 13 and U / k_B T approaches 1.","inputs":{"log_t":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1,"atol":5e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.99072,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-1e-6,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"mode","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":4}
//|       - {"artifact":"mode","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":3.741,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: hf-cold
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1,"atol":5e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":"Three terms with J ≤ 2"}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-1e-11,"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"mode","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":0}
//|   - case: co-hot
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1,"atol":5e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-1e-8,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"mode","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":13}
use scioxide_core::{RealScalar, checked_sum, checked_sum_of_products, softmax_into};

let (theta, _) = molecule_constants(&molecule);
let t = RealScalar::powf(10.0_f64, log_t);
let energy = reduced_energies(theta, t);
// Exponent ln g_J − θ J(J+1)/T.
let logits: Vec<f64> = degeneracies(energy.len())
    .iter()
    .zip(&energy)
    .map(|(&g, e)| RealScalar::ln(g) - e)
    .collect();

let mut population = vec![0.0; logits.len()];
let mut scratch = vec![0.0; logits.len()];
softmax_into(&logits, &mut population, &mut scratch).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Use exact-sum, single-rounding functions for sums and expectations.
let total = checked_sum(&population).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let internal = checked_sum_of_products(&population, &energy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Intrinsic f64 references: high-temperature expansion or low-temperature three-level sum.
let x = theta / t;
let (u_name, u_reference) = if t > theta {
    ("1 − x/3 − x²/45", 1.0 - x / 3.0 - x * x / 45.0)
} else {
    let (w1, w2) = (3.0 * (-2.0 * x).exp(), 5.0 * (-6.0 * x).exp());
    (
        "Three terms with J ≤ 2",
        (2.0 * x * w1 + 6.0 * x * w2) / (1.0 + w1 + w2),
    )
};
let digits17 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 17 });
let digits9 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 9 });
let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let energy_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Σ p_J（checked_sum）[-]", "type": "number", "format": digits17 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "U / k_B T [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits9 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Reference formula" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Reference [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits9 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "x = θ_rot / T [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    energy_columns,
    vec![(
        total,
        internal,
        u_name,
        u_reference,
        internal - u_reference,
        x,
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "energy" })
);

// The most populated level and continuous approximation J* = max(0, √(T / 2θ) − 1/2).
let (mode, p_mode) = population
    .iter()
    .copied()
    .enumerate()
    .fold(
        (0, 0.0),
        |best, (j, p)| if p > best.1 { (j, p) } else { best },
    );
let j_star = (RealScalar::sqrt(t / (2.0 * theta)) - 0.5).max(0.0);
let mode_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Most populated level J [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Its probability p_J [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Continuous most-populated J* [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    mode_columns,
    vec![(mode, p_mode, j_star)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "mode" })
);
let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = population
    .iter()
    .take(41)
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, &p)| [j as f64, p])
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": format!("{molecule}, T = {t:.1} K"), "points": points })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Rotational quantum number J [-]",
        "yLabel": "Population p_J [-]",
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": j_star, "label": "Continuous most-populated J*" }]
    })
);
```

At the defaults (CO, $T = 100$ K), $x \approx 0.028$; $U / k_B T \approx 0.99072$ agrees with expansion $1 - x/3 - x^2/45$ within $2 \times 10^{-7}$.
The probability sum equals 1 in `f64`.
The most populated level is $J = 4$ ($p_4 \approx 0.142$), the nearest integer to continuous $3.74$.

For CO at $T = 1000$ K, it rises to $J = 13$ (continuous $12.91$) and $U / k_B T \approx 0.99907$ approaches equipartition value 1.
For HF at $T = 10$ K, $99.3\,\%$ of molecules occupy $J = 0$, with $U / k_B T \approx 0.043$ (rotational excitation frozen out).
Near transition $T \approx \theta_{\mathrm{rot}}$, reference errors are larger, with differences of order $2 \times 10^{-2}$ on the expansion side and $10^{-4}$ on the three-term side.

## Summary

Shifting energy references greatly changes exponents, but log-sums and normalization only add the shift to $\ln q$, leaving occupation probabilities unchanged.
Use `weighted_logsumexp` for weighted sums, `softmax_into` for probabilities, and single-rounding `checked_sum` and `checked_sum_of_products` for probability sums and expectations.
Check partition functions and internal energy against high-temperature Mulholland expansions or low-temperature few-term sums, and population maxima against continuous approximations.
