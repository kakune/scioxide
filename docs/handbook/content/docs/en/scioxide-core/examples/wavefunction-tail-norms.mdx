In quantum mechanics, wavefunction tails penetrating beyond a potential determine tunneling.
Even when tail values fit double precision, squaring them for a norm can round to zero.
Conversely, growing solutions encountered in shooting methods overflow when squared.
This example computes sampled wavefunction norms with `checked_scaled_norm`, checking merged `ScaledNorm` states from two halves and independent log-sum-exp references.

## Harmonic oscillator tails and growing solutions

For a harmonic oscillator of mass $m$ and angular frequency $\omega$, use dimensionless coordinate $\xi = x/x_0$ with length unit $x_0 = \sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$; the ground state is

$$
\psi_0(\xi) = \pi^{-1/4}\, e^{-\xi^2/2}
$$


Beyond classical turning point $\xi = 1$, it decays as a Gaussian.
For large $\xi$, Schrödinger solutions are approximately

$$
\psi(\xi) \approx c_1\, e^{-\xi^2/2} + c_2\, e^{+\xi^2/2}
$$

Intermediate shooting solutions while searching for eigenvalues contain growing components $e^{+\xi^2/2}$.
Below, sample $\psi = e^{\mp \xi^2/2}$ without its constant multiplier at $N$ points in $[a, a+2]$, obtaining vector $\boldsymbol{\psi}$ and norm $\lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2 = \sqrt{\sum_k \psi_k^2}$.
Both $\xi$ and $\psi$ are dimensionless.

## Failures of direct computation

The largest normal `f64` is about $1.8 \times 10^{308} \approx e^{709.8}$; its smallest subnormal is $4.9 \times 10^{-324} \approx e^{-744.4}$.
Direct sum of squares $\sum_k \psi_k^2$ fails in three situations.

For decay, $\psi = e^{-392} \approx 6 \times 10^{-171}$ is still normal at $\xi = 28$, but $\psi^2 = e^{-784}$ is below the least subnormal and becomes zero.
A window containing only samples with $\xi > 27.3$ therefore makes both $\sum_k \psi_k^2$ and the norm vanish.

For growth, $\psi = e^{364.5} \approx 2 \times 10^{158}$ is representable at $\xi = 27$, while $\psi^2 \approx 4 \times 10^{316}$ exceeds the maximum, reaching `inf`.

At $\xi > 37.7$, components $e^{\mp\xi^2/2}$ themselves leave normal double range.
Growth becomes `inf`; decay becomes subnormal, loses significant digits, and vanishes at $\xi > 38.6$.
Beyond this, logarithmic storage is needed, so restrict windows to $a \le 35$ ($\xi \le 37$).

## Scaled norms and references

`checked_scaled_norm` holds scale $s$ (largest magnitude so far) and sum of squared component ratios $q$ after division by $s$, representing $\lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2 = s\sqrt{q}$.
Ratios are at most 1, preventing square overflow.
Squared ratios of small components may underflow but are negligible relative to $q \ge 1$.
For nonzero component count $m$, $k = 8m + 8$, and $u = \varepsilon/2$, under $ku < 1$ the absolute error is at most $\gamma_k \lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2 + \eta/2$ ($\gamma_k = ku/(1 - ku)$, smallest positive subnormal $\eta$).
For normal norms this means relative error at most $\gamma_k$, giving $\gamma_{520} \approx 5.8 \times 10^{-14}$ at $m = 64$ ([Exact accumulation and range-safe arithmetic](../../algorithms/range-safe-arithmetic/)).

`ScaledNorm` builds the same state sequentially, merging states $(s_1, q_1)$ and $(s_2, q_2)$ through `merge`.
Merging has the same meaning as computing $q = q_1 (s_1/s)^2 + q_2 (s_2/s)^2$ with $s = \max(s_1, s_2)$.
Count each merge as one in the bound's $m$.

When components fit but the norm exceeds double range, `finish()` returns `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
Then extract `scale()` and `sum_squares()`, form extended-range `Scaled` product $s \cdot \sqrt{q}$, and obtain $\ln \lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2$ through `ln_abs()`.
Norms in this example's windows remain in range.

For an independent reference, known component logarithms $e_k = \mp \xi_k^2/2$ give

$$
\ln \lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2 = \frac{1}{2} \ln \sum_k e^{2 e_k} = \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{LSE}(2e_1, \ldots, 2e_N)
$$

Evaluate this expression through `logsumexp`.
No exponentials are formed, so computation avoids double-range restrictions and stays within 16 ULP of the LSE value.
Differences in $\ln \lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2$ approximately equal relative norm differences.

Both cells share the following definitions.
`tail_exponents` returns component logarithms $e_k = \mp \xi_k^2/2$ of the chosen solution at equally spaced points in $[a, a+2]$; `naive_norm` computes the direct square-root sum of squares; `gamma` returns bound $\gamma_{8m+8}$.

```rust
//| id: core-wavefunction-tail-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Tail samples and error bounds"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
/// Component logarithms e_k = ∓ξ_k²/2, with count equally spaced ξ_k in [start, start + 2] (count ≥ 2).
pub(crate) fn tail_exponents(mode: &str, start: f64, count: usize) -> Vec<f64> {
    let sign = if mode.starts_with("Decay") {
        -1.0
    } else {
        1.0
    };
    (0..count)
        .map(|k| {
            let xi = start + 2.0 * k as f64 / (count - 1) as f64;
            sign * 0.5 * xi * xi
        })
        .collect()
}

/// Comparison: direct square root of sum of squares with f64 arithmetic and intrinsics.
pub(crate) fn naive_norm(psi: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    psi.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>().sqrt()
}

/// Error bound γ_k = ku/(1 − ku), k = 8m + 8, u = ε/2 (m counts nonzero components and merges).
pub(crate) fn gamma(m: usize) -> f64 {
    let ku = (8 * m + 8) as f64 * f64::EPSILON / 2.0;
    ku / (1.0 - ku)
}
```

## Norms in one window

For the selected window, compute $\ln \lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2$ directly, with `checked_scaled_norm`, and with `ScaledNorm` accumulated in halves then combined through `merge`, comparing each result with the reference.
Inputs are start position $a$, solution kind, and sample count $N$.
Compute components $\psi_k$ and logarithms with `RealScalar::exp` and `RealScalar::ln`; only the direct comparison uses `f64` arithmetic and intrinsics.
The table shows each norm, its logarithm, difference from reference $\operatorname{LSE}(2e)/2$, and relative bound $\gamma_k$.
Non-finite values, such as logarithms of zero or infinity, display as “Non-finite.”

```rust
//| id: core-wavefunction-tail-norms
//| ref: core-wavefunction-tail-norms
//| title: "Norms of vectors sampling wavefunction tails"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [core-wavefunction-tail-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   window_start: {"type":"range","label":"Window start a (ξ) [-]","description":"Sample [a, a + 2].","min":0,"max":35,"step":1,"value":28}
//|   mode: {"type":"select","label":"Solution","options":["Decaying solution exp(-ξ²/2)","Growing solution exp(+ξ²/2)"],"value":"Decaying solution exp(-ξ²/2)"}
//|   sample_count: {"type":"range","label":"Sample count N","description":"Equally spaced sample points within the interval.","min":16,"max":256,"step":16,"value":64}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"growing","label":"Growing solution, a = 27","description":"Squares overflow, making direct computation non-finite.","inputs":{"window_start":27,"mode":"Growing solution exp(+ξ²/2)"}}
//|   - {"id":"subnormal","label":"Decaying solution, a = 27","description":"Squares become subnormal and direct computation loses significant digits.","inputs":{"window_start":27}}
//|   - {"id":"in-range","label":"Decaying solution, a = 10, N = 256","description":"Squares fit the range and all three methods agree.","inputs":{"window_start":10,"sample_count":256}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":-391.9075731605,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":-1.2e-13,"max":1.2e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":-1.2e-13,"max":1.2e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":5.77e-14,"rtol":1e-2}}
//|   - case: growing
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":420.5864845297,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":-1.2e-13,"max":1.2e-13}}
//|   - case: subnormal
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-1e-6,"max":-1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":-1.2e-13,"max":1.2e-13}}
//|   - case: in-range
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_core::{RealScalar, ScaledNorm, checked_scaled_norm, logsumexp};

let n = sample_count as usize;
let exponents = tail_exponents(&mode, window_start, n);
let psi: Vec<f64> = exponents.iter().map(|&e| RealScalar::exp(e)).collect();

// Reference: ln‖ψ‖ = LSE(2 e_k) / 2, without forming exponentials.
let doubled: Vec<f64> = exponents.iter().map(|e| 2.0 * e).collect();
let reference = 0.5 * logsumexp(&doubled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let naive = naive_norm(&psi);
let checked = checked_scaled_norm(&psi).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Accumulate both halves separately, then merge.
let (mut first, mut second) = (ScaledNorm::new(), ScaledNorm::new());
for &v in &psi[..n / 2] {
    first.add(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
for &v in &psi[n / 2..] {
    second.add(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
first.merge(second).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let merged = first.finish().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// m counts nonzero components; a merge adds 1 to m.
let m = psi.iter().filter(|&&v| v != 0.0).count();
let finite = |v: f64| RealScalar::is_finite(v).then_some(v);
let row = |name: &'static str, norm: f64, bound: Option<f64>| {
    let log = finite(RealScalar::ln(norm));
    let difference = log.map(|value| value - reference);
    (name, finite(norm), log, reference, difference, bound)
};
let sci6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 6 });
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 13 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let lost = "Non-finite";
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "‖ψ‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6, "nullText": lost },
    { "key": "2", "label": "ln ‖ψ‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits, "nullText": lost },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Reference LSE(2e)/2 [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Reference difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": lost },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative error bound γ_k [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![
        row("Direct", naive, None),
        row("checked_scaled_norm", checked, Some(gamma(m))),
        row("Merged ScaledNorm", merged, Some(gamma(m + 1))),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "norms" })
);
```

At the defaults (decay, $a = 28$, $N = 64$), direct computation gives zero (logarithm $-\infty$).
`checked_scaled_norm` and the merged `merge` result give $\ln \lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2 = -391.9075731605$, matching the reference to displayed digits.
For growth at $a = 27$, direct computation is infinite, while both scaled results match reference $420.5864845297$ to displayed digits.
For decay at $a = 27$, direct computation is nonzero but subnormal squares lose digits, reducing the logarithm by $4.5 \times 10^{-8}$.
Where squares fit, as for decay at $a = 10$ and $N = 256$, direct and scaled results agree within rounding.

One ULP of logarithm $\ln \lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2 \approx -392$ represented in `f64` is $5.7 \times 10^{-14}$, so a log difference within 1 ULP means a relative norm difference approximately no larger than this.
This matches relative bound $\gamma_{520} \approx 5.8 \times 10^{-14}$ in scale; the table differences cannot resolve smaller errors.

## Variation with window position

For 36 windows $a = 0, 1, \ldots, 35$, compute 32-sample $\lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2$ directly and with `checked_scaled_norm`, plotting on a logarithmic axis.
Input is solution kind.
Omit windows whose direct norm is zero or infinite from logarithmic series, reporting their counts.
Also tabulate the largest relative difference between each window's `checked_scaled_norm` and reference $\exp(\operatorname{LSE}(2e)/2)$, and the 32-point bound $\gamma_{264}$.

```rust
//| id: core-wavefunction-tail-sweep
//| ref: core-wavefunction-tail-sweep
//| title: "Window locations and norms"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [core-wavefunction-tail-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   mode: {"type":"select","label":"Solution","options":["Decaying solution exp(-ξ²/2)","Growing solution exp(+ξ²/2)"],"value":"Growing solution exp(+ξ²/2)"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"decaying","label":"Decaying solution","description":"Direct computation ends at a = 27, then becomes zero; eight windows are omitted.","inputs":{"mode":"Decaying solution exp(-ξ²/2)"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":11}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":36}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1.5e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":2.93e-14,"rtol":1e-2}}
//|   - case: decaying
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":8}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1.5e-13}}
use scioxide_core::{RealScalar, checked_scaled_norm, logsumexp};

let (mut naive_curve, mut scaled_curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut naive_lost, mut worst) = (0_u32, 0.0_f64);
for a in 0..=35 {
    let start = f64::from(a);
    let exponents = tail_exponents(&mode, start, 32);
    let psi: Vec<f64> = exponents.iter().map(|&e| RealScalar::exp(e)).collect();
    let naive = naive_norm(&psi);
    if naive > 0.0 && RealScalar::is_finite(naive) {
        naive_curve.push([start, naive]);
    } else {
        naive_lost += 1;
    }
    let scaled = checked_scaled_norm(&psi).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    scaled_curve.push([start, scaled]);
    // Reference exp(LSE(2e)/2). Norms fit f64 throughout this range.
    let doubled: Vec<f64> = exponents.iter().map(|e| 2.0 * e).collect();
    let reference = RealScalar::exp(0.5 * logsumexp(&doubled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    worst = worst.max(((scaled - reference) / reference).abs());
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Direct computation", "points": naive_curve }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "checked_scaled_norm", "points": scaled_curve }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Window start a (ξ) [-]",
        "yLabel": "Norm ‖ψ‖₂ [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 35.0
    })
);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Windows with zero or infinite direct norms [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Window count [windows]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum relative reference difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Bound γ₂₆₄ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(naive_lost, 36_u32, worst, gamma(32))],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
```

Norms range from about 17 to $1.9 \times 10^{297}$ for growth and 3.8 to $9.9 \times 10^{-267}$ for decay.
Logarithms roughly follow parabolas, $\ln \lVert \boldsymbol{\psi} \rVert_2 \approx (a + 2)^2/2$ for growth and $\approx -a^2/2$ for decay (each the component logarithm at the window endpoint of maximum magnitude).
For growth, the direct series ends at $a = 24$, omitting 11 of 36 windows.
For decay it ends at $a = 27$, omitting eight windows.
Both `checked_scaled_norm` series continue through $a = 35$.

Maximum relative reference differences are $5.1 \times 10^{-14}$ for growth and $5.3 \times 10^{-14}$ for decay.
These exceed the 32-point bound $\gamma_{264} \approx 2.9 \times 10^{-14}$, but include reference error.
For reference $\exp(\operatorname{LSE}/2)$, absolute error in argument $\operatorname{LSE}/2$ becomes relative error directly.
In the $|\operatorname{LSE}/2| \approx 600$ window, merely rounding the argument to `f64` introduces relative error up to $5.7 \times 10^{-14}$, so differences of order $5 \times 10^{-14}$ cannot be attributed uniquely.

## Summary

Representable double components can still have out-of-range squares, making direct norms zero or `inf`.
`checked_scaled_norm` and `ScaledNorm` use scales and squared ratios, avoiding square-range issues whenever the norm fits; separately accumulated states merge through `merge`.
For normal norms, relative error is at most $\gamma_{8m+8}$.
Single-window log differences lie within this bound; moving-window relative differences (about $5 \times 10^{-14}$) are indistinguishable from reference rounding error.
If the norm itself is out of range, receive `NumericalInstability` and construct `Scaled` from `scale()` and `sum_squares()` to obtain its logarithm.
When components themselves round to zero, a logarithmic representation (`LogValue` or arrays of logarithms) is needed.
