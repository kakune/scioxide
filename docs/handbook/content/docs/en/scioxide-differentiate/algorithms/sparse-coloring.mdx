Jacobians of PDE discretizations and reaction networks are sparse, with mostly zero entries, because each equation depends on few variables.
Dense differences use evaluations proportional to variable count $n$; exploiting sparsity reduces them to a count proportional to colors.
This page explains Curtis, Powell, and Reid's (1974) principle and `scioxide-differentiate` coloring Plan counts, output order, and cautions.
Coloring algorithms and color counts are covered in scioxide-sparse [Orderings and coloring](../../../scioxide-sparse/algorithms/orderings-and-coloring/#derivative-column-coloring); here we recover differences from supplied coloring.

## Structurally orthogonal columns

Let sparse structure (pattern) $\mathcal{S}$ be the positions of entries in Jacobian $\mathbf{J} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ that may be zero.
$(i, j) \notin \mathcal{S}$ means $F_i$ does not depend on $x_j$.
Columns $j$ and $k$ are structurally orthogonal if no row places both in $\mathcal{S}$.

Group mutually orthogonal columns $C$ into one color, perturbing them simultaneously by their own steps $h_j$.

$$
\mathbf{x}_C^{\pm} = \mathbf{x} \pm \sum_{j \in C} h_j \mathbf{e}_j .
$$

For row $i$, at most one column $j$ of $C$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}$, giving $F_i(\mathbf{x}_C^{\pm}) = F_i(\mathbf{x} \pm h_j \mathbf{e}_j)$.
Each row $i$ of the colored central difference therefore equals a difference perturbing only column $j$.

$$
\frac{F_i(\mathbf{x}_C^{+}) - F_i(\mathbf{x}_C^{-})}{2 h_j} = \frac{F_i(\mathbf{x} + h_j \mathbf{e}_j) - F_i(\mathbf{x} - h_j \mathbf{e}_j)}{2 h_j} = J_{ij} + \frac{h_j^2}{6} \frac{\partial^3 F_i}{\partial x_j^3} + O(h_j^4) \qquad ((i, j) \in \mathcal{S},\ j \in C).
$$

Truncation and rounding match dense column $j$, while counts scale with colors $c$ rather than columns $n$.
This assumes $F_i$ truly ignores variables outside $\mathcal{S}$.

Columns in a row's $\mathcal{S}$ must all differ in color, so color count $c$ is at least maximum row entries.

$$
c \ge \max_i \bigl\lvert \{ j : (i, j) \in \mathcal{S} \} \bigr\rvert .
$$

For lower bandwidth $p_l$ and upper $p_u$ (only $-p_l \le j - i \le p_u$ nonzero), each row has at most $p_l + p_u + 1$ entries; coloring by column index modulo $p_l + p_u + 1$ uses exactly that many, independently of $n$.
Finding minimum colors is generally NP-hard; practical methods use greedy coloring.
A greedy method visits columns in index order and assigns the smallest color unused by preceding columns sharing rows; its count is at most one plus the largest count of other columns sharing rows with a column.
See [Derivative-column coloring](../../../scioxide-sparse/algorithms/orderings-and-coloring/#derivative-column-coloring) for bounds and banded measurements.

## Specifying and coloring structures

Supply nonzero positions through scioxide-core [`StructuralPattern`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-core/0.1.0/scioxide_core/struct.StructuralPattern.html)`::new(rows, cols, row_ptr, col_indices)` in CSR format, with strictly increasing column indices per row.
`row_ptr` gives starts of $m + 1$ rows and `col_indices` stored column indices; malformed structures give `InvalidInput` (`Malformed`).
The pattern must include every position of an entry that may be zero.
Accidentally zero entries may be included, but omitting actual nonzeros silently mixes their values into same-color columns and returns wrong Jacobians ([Omitted nonzeros](#omitted-nonzeros) below).

scioxide-sparse [`derivative_coloring`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-sparse/0.1.0/scioxide_sparse/fn.derivative_coloring.html)`(pattern.view())` constructs coloring with that greedy method.
The result is scioxide-core [`ColoringSchedule`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-core/0.1.0/scioxide_core/struct.ColoringSchedule.html) of kind `ColoringKind::DerivativeColumns`, owning a pattern copy; `color_count()`, `colors()`, and `groups()` expose color counts, per-column colors, and ascending columns per color.
Pass custom colors to `ColoringSchedule::new(pattern, ColoringKind::DerivativeColumns, colors)` to check that shared-row columns differ; collisions give `InvalidInput` (`Malformed`, argument `colors`).
Coloring guarantees conflict freedom only on the declared pattern, not agreement with actual function dependencies.
scioxide-differentiate has no scioxide-sparse dependency; callers use it to construct coloring.

## ColoredJacobianPlan

[`ColoredJacobianPlan`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-differentiate/0.1.0/scioxide_differentiate/struct.ColoredJacobianPlan.html)`::new(&schedule, method)` borrows coloring and constructs a sparse-Jacobian Plan using adaptive settings `AdaptiveDifference`.
Construction precomputes each column's entry rows, output positions, and absolute tolerances, taking $O(n + \mathrm{nnz})$ time and storage for $n$ columns and $\mathrm{nnz}$ entries.
Coloring kind other than `DerivativeColumns` gives `InvalidInput` (argument `schedule`, `UnsupportedConfiguration`); levels outside 3–64 or a nonmaximum norm give `InvalidInput` (argument `method`, `OutOfRange`); nondefault `ZeroDirectionPolicy` gives `InvalidInput` (argument `method`, `Inconsistent`).
Evaluate with owned `evaluate(f, x, bounds)` or borrowed `evaluate_into` accepting output, estimates, workspace, and context.
Functions write every one of $m$ rows, with real or complex values.

### Per-column extrapolation

Each column is a univariate adaptive problem outputting only its stored rows, with its own step, table, and termination ([Richardson extrapolation and Ridders' method](../richardson-and-ridders/)).
Initial steps are $h_j = 0.5 \max(1, \lvert x_j \rvert)$, or supplied `with_step`, divided by the reduction ratio per level.
Same-color columns share perturbed points per level; at sample $s$ each uses its own displacement number $s$.
Converged columns stay at the base, leaving only unfinished columns perturbed.
Stored entry $k$ tests $\tau_{\mathrm{abs},k} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} \lvert J_k \rvert$ (absolute $\tau_{\mathrm{abs},k}$, relative $\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}$, entry $J_k$ at storage index $k$); vector absolute tolerances follow stored-entry order and count.

Values and estimates follow pattern storage order (CSR, by row), with length `output_len()`.
Without external or limit stops, report termination is the first nonconverged column's reason in index order.
If every column has estimates, values and estimated errors are written to the output even without `Converged` ([Report and publication rules](oxref:differentiate-report-rules)).

### Evaluation counts

Without boundaries, each level samples two points $\mathbf{x}_C^{\pm}$.
With bounds, each column chooses symmetric $\{-h, 0, h\}$ or inward one-sided $\{0, h, 2h\}$ using adaptive rules at its step ([Boundaries for adaptive, directional, and node differences](oxref:differentiate-bounded-other-methods)).
Both have two nonbase points; the first sample combines first displacements $-h$ or $h$, and the second combines second displacements $h$ or $2h$.
Same-color columns share no rows, so mixing node shapes leaves two samples per level.
Levels $L_C$ for color $C$ equal its slowest column's levels; calls add one base evaluation to their sum.

$$
N_{\mathrm{eval}} = 1 + 2 \sum_{C} L_C .
$$

Sum over every color $C$; equal levels $L$ give $1 + 2cL$.
Counts are at most $1 + 2cL_{\max}$ with or without bounds, where $L_{\max}$ is the level limit.
Dense adaptive Jacobians take $1 + 2\sum_j L_j$ calls, giving approximate ratio $c / n$.
Reported iterations `iterations()` sum per-column levels $\sum_j L_j$.
Each evaluation is one full function call; each color level also requires $O(n + m)$ work to assemble perturbed points, plus extrapolation-table updates for every stored entry.
Ridders updates every table column per level, giving quadratic work in levels per entry; Richardson work is linear in levels.

### One-shot functions and workspaces

For default values alone, one-shot [`sparse_jacobian`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-differentiate/0.1.0/scioxide_differentiate/fn.sparse_jacobian.html)`(f, x, &schedule)` performs the same computation with `AdaptiveDifference::default()` and no bounds.
Default tolerance is $\sqrt{\varepsilon}(1 + \lvert J_{ij} \rvert)$ per stored entry, at most 16 levels, and no more than $1 + 32c$ calls.
Termination other than `Converged` returns no values, applying `into_values()` error rules (`ConvergenceFailure` for `RoundoffLimit`, `RefinementLimit`, or `StepSizeLimit`).

Workspace is [`ColoredJacobianWorkspace`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-differentiate/0.1.0/scioxide_differentiate/struct.ColoredJacobianWorkspace.html), with Plan-borrowing requirements [`ColoredJacobianRequirements`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-differentiate/0.1.0/scioxide_differentiate/struct.ColoredJacobianRequirements.html) ([Reusing Plans, requirements, and workspaces](../plans-workspaces-and-reports/)).
For $m$ rows and $L$ levels, size is $O(n + m + \mathrm{nnz} \cdot L)$ entries; constructing with Plan `workspace(())` lets `evaluate_into` avoid allocation.
Only two sets of points and function values coexist, independently of color count.

## Measuring banded evaluation counts

The next cell compares dense adaptive `AdaptiveDifference::jacobian` and coloring on Broyden's banded function from the Moré–Garbow–Hillstrom test set.

$$
F_i(\mathbf{x}) = x_i (2 + 5 x_i^2) + 1 - \sum_{j \in J_i} x_j (1 + x_j), \qquad J_i = \{ j \ne i : i - p_l \le j \le i + 1,\ 0 \le j < n \}.
$$

The original has lower bandwidth $p_l = 5$ and upper 1; the cell varies $p_l$ from 1 to 5.
Rows have at most $p_l + 2$ entries, giving color-count lower bound $p_l + 2$.
The analytic Jacobian is $\partial F_i / \partial x_i = 2 + 15 x_i^2$ and $\partial F_i / \partial x_j = -(1 + 2 x_j)$ ($j \in J_i$).
At point $x_i = -1 + 0.5\, i / n$, vary $n$ from 8 to 64 and record counts and maximum errors.

The following block supplies helpers shared by two cells.
`csr_pattern` constructs CSR `StructuralPattern` from rowwise columns; `stored_max_error` returns maximum absolute difference between stored-order values and reference $J_{ij}$.

```rust
//| id: differentiate-coloring-helpers
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "CSR structures from rowwise columns and stored-entry errors"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, StructuralPattern};

/// Construct n × n CSR from row i's strictly increasing columns(i).
pub(crate) fn csr_pattern<I: IntoIterator<Item = usize>>(
    n: usize,
    columns: impl Fn(usize) -> I,
) -> Result<StructuralPattern, SciOxideError> {
    let (mut row_ptr, mut col_indices) = (vec![0], Vec::new());
    for i in 0..n {
        col_indices.extend(columns(i));
        row_ptr.push(col_indices.len());
    }
    StructuralPattern::new(n, n, row_ptr, col_indices)
}

/// Maximum absolute difference between CSR-order values and reference(i, j).
pub(crate) fn stored_max_error(
    pattern: &StructuralPattern,
    values: &[f64],
    reference: impl Fn(usize, usize) -> f64,
) -> f64 {
    let view = pattern.view();
    let mut largest = 0.0_f64;
    for (i, span) in view.row_ptr().windows(2).enumerate() {
        for k in span[0]..span[1] {
            largest = largest.max((values[k] - reference(i, view.col_indices()[k])).abs());
        }
    }
    largest
}
```

Table $\bar{L}$ is per-column levels from colored iterations divided by $n$; prediction is $1 + 2c\bar{L}$.
Colored maximum error compares stored entries with analytic values; dense–colored difference is maximum difference on stored entries; termination is the colored report's.
The chart plots both evaluation counts logarithmically.

At $p_l = 5$, colors equal lower bound 7 for every $n$; colored calls stay at $1 + 2 \cdot 7 \cdot 4 = 57$ independently of $n$.
Dense calls $1 + 8n$ scale with $n$; at $n = 64$, 513 calls are 9 times the colored count.
At $n = 8$, 7 colors are near $n$, making 65 versus 57 calls similar.

Every column converges in 4 levels.
$F_i$ is cubic per variable, so central errors have only $h^2$ and extrapolation column 1 removes truncation.
Still, convergence does not occur at level 2.
Ridders estimates use the larger difference from two parent entries; level-2 parents are unextrapolated, retaining $h^2$ in the estimate.
Only level 3 has both parents extrapolated, lowering estimates to rounding; two consecutive successes give level-4 convergence ([Step and stopping rules](oxref:differentiate-stopping-rules)).

Colored maximum error is at most $1.3 \times 10^{-14}$; stored dense–colored differences are exactly zero.
$F_i$ ignores other same-color variables, and dense adaptation uses identical initial steps and ratios.
Colored perturbations therefore produce bitwise-identical row values to single-column perturbations, followed by identical extrapolation.

At $p_l = 1$ (case narrow-band), colors drop to 3 and colored calls to $1 + 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 25$.
Richardson (case richardson) also converges in 4 levels for the same reason, with unchanged counts and exactly zero error at this point.

## Omitted nonzeros

Omitting actual nonzero $(i, k)$ can give column $k$ the same color as column $j$ with an entry in row $i$.
Colored perturbation $\mathbf{x}_C^{\pm}$ then moves both $x_j$ and $x_k$; row $i$ differences include column $k$'s change.

$$
\frac{F_i(\mathbf{x}_C^{+}) - F_i(\mathbf{x}_C^{-})}{2 h_j} = J_{ij} + \frac{h_k}{h_j} J_{ik} + O(h^2).
$$

Column steps shrink by identical ratios, keeping $h_k / h_j$ constant across levels, so extrapolation converges to this wrong value.
Estimates measure interlevel changes, missing contamination and still giving `Converged`.
`ColoredJacobianPlan` does not compare functions with patterns and reports no error.

Detection requires an independent computation.
For example, compute one dense `FiniteDifference::jacobian` Jacobian and check zero entries outside the pattern.
If $F_i$ ignores $x_k$, both difference points give bitwise-identical $F_i$, making outside-pattern differences exactly zero.
For mechanically generated patterns, derive them from scioxide-ad recordings with `jacobian_pattern()` ([Structural patterns from recordings](../../../scioxide-ad/algorithms/sparse-derivatives/#structural-patterns-from-a-recording)).

The next cell computes a four-variable Jacobian with the correct pattern and one omitting $(0, 2)$.

$$
\mathbf{F} = \bigl( x_0^2 + \gamma x_2, \; x_0 + x_1^2, \; x_1 + x_2^2, \; x_2 + x_3^2 \bigr), \qquad \mathbf{x} = (1, 0.5, x_2, 2).
$$

Correct columns 0 and 2 conflict in row 0 and differ in color; omitting $(0, 2)$ merges their colors.
Row-0 differences become $2 x_0 + \gamma h_2 / h_0$ instead of $J_{00} = 2 x_0 = 2$, predictable from initial steps $h_j = 0.5 \max(1, \lvert x_j \rvert)$.
Setting coupling $\gamma$ to 0 makes $(0, 2)$ truly zero, so omission is harmless.
“Maximum outside pattern” independently checks maximum absolute outside-pattern entries from a dense fixed-step Jacobian.

```rust
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let digits10 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let gamma: f64 = coupling.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot read γ")?;
let x2: f64 = x2.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot read x₂")?;
let x = [1.0_f64, 0.5, x2, 2.0];
let f = |p: &[f64], y: &mut [f64]| {
    y[0] = p[0] * p[0] + gamma * p[2];
    y[1] = p[0] + p[1] * p[1];
    y[2] = p[1] + p[2] * p[2];
    y[3] = p[2] + p[3] * p[3];
    Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
};
// Reference: analytic J(i, j).
let exact = |i: usize, j: usize| match (i, j) {
    (0, 2) => gamma,
    (i, j) if j == i => 2.0 * x[i],
    (i, j) if j + 1 == i => 1.0,
    _ => 0.0,
};
// Independent check: dense fixed-step Jacobian, row-major; outside-pattern entries should be zero.
let dense = FiniteDifference::default()
    .jacobian(f, &x, 4, None, None)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .into_values()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Initial column step h_j = 0.5 max(1, |x_j|); ratio h₂/h₀ is level-independent.
let step = |v: f64| 0.5 * v.abs().max(1.0);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, row0) in [
    ("Correct pattern", vec![0, 2]),
    ("Pattern omitting (0, 2)", vec![0]),
] {
    let dropped = row0.len() == 1;
    // Row 0 uses row0; rows i ≥ 1 use columns i − 1 and i.
    let pattern = csr_pattern(4, |i| if i == 0 { row0.clone() } else { vec![i - 1, i] })
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let schedule = derivative_coloring(pattern.view()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let plan = ColoredJacobianPlan::new(&schedule, AdaptiveDifference::default())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let result = plan.evaluate(f, &x, None).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let values = result.values().ok_or("Jacobian not published")?;
    let estimates = result.errors().ok_or("Error estimate not published")?;
    let mixed = if dropped {
        gamma * step(x[2]) / step(x[0])
    } else {
        0.0
    };
    let view = pattern.view();
    let in_pattern = |i: usize, j: usize| {
        view.col_indices()[view.row_ptr()[i]..view.row_ptr()[i + 1]].contains(&j)
    };
    let largest_estimate = estimates.iter().copied().fold(0.0, f64::max);
    let outside = (0..16)
        .filter(|&k| !in_pattern(k / 4, k % 4))
        .map(|k| dense[k].abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max);
    let colors: Vec<String> = schedule.colors().iter().map(|c| c.to_string()).collect();
    rows.push((
        name,
        schedule.color_count(),
        colors.join(", "),
        values[0],
        2.0 * x[0] + mixed,
        stored_max_error(&pattern, values, exact),
        largest_estimate,
        outside,
        result.report().termination().to_string(),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Pattern" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Colors", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Colors of columns 0–3" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "J₀₀ (true 2) [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Prediction 2x₀ + γh₂/h₀ [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits10 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Maximum stored-entry error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Maximum estimated error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Maximum outside pattern [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "patterns" })
);
```

At defaults $\gamma = 0.5$ and $x_2 = -0.5$, correct coloring uses 3 colors and every stored entry matches analytic values.
Omitting $(0, 2)$ gives 2 colors (0, 1, 0, 1) and $J_{00}$ equals predicted $2 + 0.5 \cdot h_2 / h_0 = 2.5$ instead of true 2.
Estimates remain $2.4 \times 10^{-14}$ and termination `Converged`, without errors.
Outside maxima are exactly 0 for correct patterns and $\lvert J_{02} \rvert = 0.5$ for omitted ones; only this independent check detects the omission.

At $\gamma = 0.01$, $J_{00} = 2.01$ reduces contamination without changing estimates.
At $\gamma = 0$, the omitted entry is truly zero; two colors give correct values and zero outside maxima.
At $x_2 = -4$, $h_2 / h_0 = 2 / 0.5 = 4$, and $J_{00}$ becomes $2 + 0.5 \cdot 4 = 4$ with fourfold contamination.
Contamination depends on step ratios as well as $J_{02}$, so mismatched variable scales can worsen errors.

## Summary

Sparse Jacobians group structurally orthogonal columns into colors for simultaneous perturbation, making calls proportional to colors rather than columns.
Colors are at least maximum row entries; banded counts depend only on bandwidth, and `derivative_coloring` constructs greedy coloring in column order.
`ColoredJacobianPlan` stores adaptive tables per column, shares same-color points, and returns values and estimates in CSR order.
Calls are $1 + 2\sum_C L_C$ with or without bounds, determined by each color's slowest column; if functions ignore other same-color variables, results match dense adaptive columns.
If `StructuralPattern` omits actual nonzeros, values contaminate same-color columns and silently return incorrect `Converged` without larger estimates; verify patterns once with dense differences or automatic differentiation.
