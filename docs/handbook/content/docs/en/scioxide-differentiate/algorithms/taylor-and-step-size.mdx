When function $f$ is evaluable but no derivative formula is available, finite differences evaluate $f$ near point $x$ and approximate derivatives with linear combinations.
Evaluation points are nodes, their differences from $x$ are displacements, and typical displacement magnitude is step size $h$ (notation in [Shared contracts and structure of numerical-differentiation algorithms](oxref:differentiate-notation)).
Approximation error has two parts.
Truncating Taylor expansions after finitely many terms gives truncation error, which decreases as $h$ shrinks.
Function-value and subtraction rounding errors increase as $h$ shrinks.
This page derives and measures truncation orders and explains how fixed-step `FiniteDifference` chooses steps, places nodes, and constructs weights.
[Rounding, noise, and optimal step size](../roundoff-and-noisy-functions/) covers rounding, noise, and optimal balancing of both errors.

## Forward and central differences

For sufficiently smooth $f$ near $x$, values at $x \pm h$ expand as follows.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x + h) &= f(x) + h f'(x) + \frac{h^2}{2} f''(x) + \frac{h^3}{6} f'''(x) + \frac{h^4}{24} f^{(4)}(x) + O(h^5), \\
f(x - h) &= f(x) - h f'(x) + \frac{h^2}{2} f''(x) - \frac{h^3}{6} f'''(x) + \frac{h^4}{24} f^{(4)}(x) + O(h^5).
\end{aligned}
$$

Subtracting $f(x)$ from the first expression and dividing by $h$ gives forward differences.
For $f$ of class $C^2$, Taylor's Lagrange remainder gives the following for some $\xi \in (x, x + h)$.

$$
D_h^{+} f(x) = \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h} = f'(x) + \frac{h}{2} f''(\xi).
$$

A formula has accuracy order $p$ when error decreases proportionally to $h^p$.
Forward errors scale with $h$, so forward differences are first order.

Subtracting the second expansion from the first cancels $f(x)$ and even powers.
For $f$ of class $C^3$, the intermediate value theorem combines the mean of remainders $f'''(\xi_\pm)$ into $f'''(\xi)$, giving this for some $\xi \in (x - h, x + h)$.

$$
D_h^{0} f(x) = \frac{f(x + h) - f(x - h)}{2h} = f'(x) + \frac{h^2}{6} f'''(\xi).
$$

Central differences are second order and more accurate than forward differences for small $h$.
Nodes $x - h, x, x + h$ have weights $(-\tfrac{1}{2}, 0, \tfrac{1}{2})/h$; $f(x)$ has zero weight.
These orders assume smooth $f$; if $f'''$ does not exist throughout the interval spanned by the nodes, the order decreases ([Reaction-rate sensitivity under nonnegative concentration constraints](oxref:differentiate-boundary-order-loss) gives one-sided examples).

## Second differences

Adding both expansions cancels odd terms and gives three-point central second differences.
For $f$ of class $C^4$, the following holds for some $\xi \in (x - h, x + h)$.

$$
D_h^{2,0} f(x) = \frac{f(x + h) - 2 f(x) + f(x - h)}{h^2} = f''(x) + \frac{h^2}{12} f^{(4)}(\xi).
$$

For forward second differences using only three one-sided points $x, x + h, x + 2h$, expand $f(x + 2h)$ too and match $h^2$ coefficients.

$$
D_h^{2,+} f(x) = \frac{f(x + 2h) - 2 f(x + h) + f(x)}{h^2} = f''(x) + h f'''(x) + \frac{7 h^2}{12} f^{(4)}(x) + O(h^3).
$$

Forward second differences have only first-order accuracy.
Both use three nodes; the nonzero $f(x)$ weight is a constant multiple of $1/h^2$.
Denominator $h^2$ makes rounding grow faster than for first differences, as the [next page](oxref:differentiate-roundoff-rule) shows.

| Formula | Nodes | Leading truncation | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| First, forward $D_h^{+}$ | $x, x + h$ | $\tfrac{1}{2} h f''$ | 1 |
| First, central $D_h^{0}$ | $x - h, x, x + h$ | $\tfrac{1}{6} h^2 f'''$ | 2 |
| Second, central $D_h^{2,0}$ | $x - h, x, x + h$ | $\tfrac{1}{12} h^2 f^{(4)}$ | 2 |
| Second, forward $D_h^{2,+}$ | $x, x + h, x + 2h$ | $h f'''$ | 1 |

## Measuring truncation orders

The next cell computes first and second derivatives of $f(x) = e^{a x}$ with forward and central differences over steps $h = 10^{-1}$–$10^{-3}$.
Here rounding is much smaller than truncation, so errors should follow leading $C h^p$.
From the table, coefficient $C$ is $a^2 e^{a x_0}/2$ first forward, $a^3 e^{a x_0}/6$ first central, $a^4 e^{a x_0}/12$ second central, and $a^3 e^{a x_0}$ second forward.
Main computations use `derivative` and `second_derivative` of `FiniteDifference`, with steps supplied by `DifferenceStep::absolute(h)` and function values evaluated through `RealScalar::exp`.
Exact and leading values are references using f64 inherent methods.
Observed order is $\ln(e_1 / e_2) / \ln(h_1 / h_2)$ from endpoint steps $h_1 = 10^{-1}$, $h_2 = 10^{-3}$ and errors $e_1$, $e_2$.
The last column divides smallest-step error by its leading term; near 1 means the formula also predicts magnitude.

```rust

//| ref: differentiate-truncation-order
//| title: "Truncation-error orders of forward and central differences"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-differentiate]
//| inputs:
//|   a: {"type":"range","label":"Exponential coefficient a [inverse x units]","description":"Change derivative magnitude a^k e^{ax}.","min":0.5,"max":3.0,"step":0.5,"value":1.0}
//|   x0: {"type":"range","label":"Evaluation point x0 [x units]","min":-1.0,"max":1.0,"step":0.5,"value":0.5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"steep","label":"a = 3","description":"Larger derivatives increase errors without changing logarithmic slopes (orders); higher terms matter at large h.","inputs":{"a":3.0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.002}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.002}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.005}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.002}}
//|   - case: steep
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.06}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.03}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.03}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.06}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.005}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.01}}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_differentiate::{
    DerivativeOperation as Op, DifferenceScheme as Scheme, DifferenceStep, FiniteDifference,
};

let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
// References: leading coefficients and exact derivatives (f64 inherent methods).
let g = (a * x0).exp();
let cases = [
    (Op::First, Scheme::Forward, "First, forward"),
    (Op::First, Scheme::Central, "First, central"),
    (Op::Second, Scheme::Central, "Second, central"),
    (Op::Second, Scheme::Forward, "Second, forward"),
];
// Nine points h = 10^{-1}, 10^{-1.25}, …, 10^{-3}.
let steps: Vec<f64> = (0..=8)
    .map(|k| 10.0_f64.powf(-1.0 - 0.25 * k as f64))
    .collect();
let mut series = Vec::new();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (operation, scheme, name) in cases {
    // Theoretical p and coefficient C of C h^p, inserting f⁽ᵏ⁾ = aᵏ e^{ax} into the table.
    let (order, leading) = match (operation, scheme) {
        (Op::First, Scheme::Forward) => (1, a.powi(2) * g / 2.0),
        (Op::First, _) => (2, a.powi(3) * g / 6.0),
        (_, Scheme::Central) => (2, a.powi(4) * g / 12.0),
        _ => (1, a.powi(3) * g),
    };
    let exact = match operation {
        Op::First => a * g,
        _ => a * a * g,
    };
    let f = |t: f64| Ok::<f64, SciOxideError>(RealScalar::exp(a * t));
    let mut observed = Vec::new();
    let mut theory = Vec::new();
    for &h in &steps {
        let method = FiniteDifference::default()
            .with_scheme(scheme)
            .with_step(DifferenceStep::absolute(h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
        let result = match operation {
            Op::First => method.derivative(f, x0),
            _ => method.second_derivative(f, x0),
        }
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let value = result.values().ok_or("Derivative not published")?[0];
        observed.push([h, (value - exact).abs()]);
        theory.push([h, leading * h.powi(order)]);
    }
    // Postprocessing: endpoint observed order and smallest-step ratio to leading error.
    let (first, last) = (observed[0], observed[observed.len() - 1]);
    let slope = (first[1] / last[1]).ln() / (first[0] / last[0]).ln();
    let predicted = theory[theory.len() - 1][1];
    rows.push((name, order, slope, last[1], predicted, last[1] / predicted));
    series.push(serde_json::json!({
        "name": name,
        "points": observed,
        "style": { "showSymbol": true }
    }));
    series.push(serde_json::json!({
        "name": format!("{name} leading term"),
        "points": theory,
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
    }));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Formula" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Theoretical order p [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Observed order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Error at h = 10⁻³ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Leading C h^p [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Error ÷ leading term [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Step h [x units]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute derivative error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
```

At defaults $a = 1$ and $x_0 = 0.5$, forward observed orders are 1.007 and 1.013, central 2.000.
Smallest-step ratios are 0.998–1.001, matching leading-error magnitudes.
Marked observations approach dashed leading terms as $h$ shrinks; logarithmic slopes give orders.
At large $h$, higher term $O(h^{p+1})$ causes deviations.
At $a = 3$, larger derivatives increase every formula's error without changing slopes.

## SciOxide default steps

Smaller steps reduce truncation but increase rounding proportionally to $1/h$ ($1/h^2$ for second differences).
Balancing the two makes the optimal step a power of machine epsilon $\varepsilon$ (derived in [Rounding, noise, and optimal step size](oxref:differentiate-optimal-step)).
Here $\varepsilon$ is the gap from 1 to its next floating-point value, $2^{-52} \approx 2.22 \times 10^{-16}$ in double precision.
Without explicit steps, [`FiniteDifference`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-differentiate/0.1.0/scioxide_differentiate/struct.FiniteDifference.html) uses the following per coordinate $x_i$.

$$
h_i = c \cdot \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert).
$$

| Formula | Derivative order | Factor $c$ | Double-precision $c$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forward `Forward` | First (`derivative`, `gradient`, `jacobian`, `jvp`) | $\varepsilon^{1/2}$ | $1.49 \times 10^{-8}$ |
| Central `Central` | First | $\varepsilon^{1/3}$ | $6.06 \times 10^{-6}$ |
| Forward `Forward` | Second (`second_derivative`, `hessian`) | $\varepsilon^{1/3}$ | $6.06 \times 10^{-6}$ |
| Central `Central` | Second | $\varepsilon^{1/4}$ | $1.22 \times 10^{-4}$ |

For $\lvert x_i \rvert \ge 1$, steps are relative and proportional to $\lvert x_i \rvert$; for $\lvert x_i \rvert < 1$, absolute step $c$ is measured in $x_i$ units.
This keeps steps from vanishing near $x_i = 0$.
The rule assumes $f$ and its derivatives vary over widths about $\max(1, \lvert x \rvert)$ (Nocedal and Wright, *Numerical Optimization*, second edition, §8.1).
For natural scales far from 1, such as molar volume $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3/mol}$ or large physical quantities, nondimensionalize or specify steps ([Derivatives and error estimates for the van der Waals equation](../../examples/van-der-waals-adaptive/)).

The mixed Hessian term $\partial^2 f / \partial x_i \partial x_j$ uses a tensor product of first-difference nodes, but divides by the product of two steps, so it uses the step rule for second derivatives.
The directional derivative `jvp` uses one difference along $\mathbf{v}$; the step $\alpha$ in the point $\mathbf{x} + \alpha \mathbf{v}$ uses the first-derivative factor $c$ as $\alpha = c / \max_i (\lvert v_i \rvert / \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert))$.
This $\alpha$ keeps every displacement $\alpha \lvert v_i \rvert$ within that coordinate's default step.
For explicit coordinate steps $h_i$ of $v_i \ne 0$, use $\alpha = \min_i h_i / \lvert v_i \rvert$.
Adaptive `AdaptiveDifference` instead starts from large $0.5 \cdot \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert)$ and shrinks ([Richardson extrapolation and Ridders' method](../richardson-and-ridders/)).

## Specifying steps with `DifferenceStep`

Specify steps through [`DifferenceStep`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-differentiate/0.1.0/scioxide_differentiate/struct.DifferenceStep.html) passed to `FiniteDifference::with_step` (adaptive `AdaptiveDifference::with_step` uses them as initial steps).
Four mutually exclusive forms are available.

| Constructor | Step $h_i$ |
|---|---|
| `DifferenceStep::absolute(h)` | Same $h$ for all coordinates ($x$ units) |
| `DifferenceStep::relative(r)` | $r \cdot \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert)$ |
| `DifferenceStep::absolute_coordinates(&[h_1, h_2, …])` | Absolute per-coordinate steps, for differing coordinate units |
| `DifferenceStep::relative_coordinates(&[r_1, r_2, …])` | Relative per-coordinate steps $r_i \cdot \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert)$ |

Every constructor rejects nonfinite or nonpositive values with `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Per-coordinate forms clone values; failures give `SizeOverflow` or `AllocationFailure`.
Arrays mismatching input dimension give `DimensionMismatch` at evaluation.
Steps specify magnitudes only; formulas and boundary adaptation determine directions ([Domain boundaries and one-sided differences](../bounds-and-one-sided/)).
Explicit steps replace default rules for both first and second derivatives.

One-shot [`derivative`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-differentiate/0.1.0/scioxide_differentiate/fn.derivative.html)`(f, x)` computes scalar first derivatives with default central steps, returning values only.
It evaluates $f$ 3 times including $f(x)$, without allocation.
To select steps or formulas, change `FiniteDifference` settings and invoke methods such as `derivative`.

## Rounded displacements

In floating point, $x + h$ rounds to representable numbers.
Actual points $x_+ = \mathrm{fl}(x + h)$ and $x_- = \mathrm{fl}(x - h)$ have displacement magnitudes $\delta_+ = x_+ - x$ and $\delta_- = x - x_-$ differing from nominal $h$.
Here $\mathrm{ulp}(x)$ is adjacent floating-point spacing near $x$.
Nearest rounding makes the difference between $\delta_\pm$ and $h$ at most half $\mathrm{ulp}(x)$.
If $h$ greatly exceeds $\mathrm{ulp}(x)$ this is negligible, but for large $\lvert x \rvert$ and small $h$, dividing by nominal $h$ turns it directly into relative derivative error.
Ignoring truncation and function rounding, central differences divided by nominal $h$ give the following.

$$
\frac{f(x_+) - f(x_-)}{2h} \approx \frac{\delta_+ + \delta_-}{2h} \, f'(x).
$$

`FiniteDifference` recomputes actual displacements $\delta_\pm$ after addition and constructs Fornberg weights for them ([Difference-stencil weights and arbitrary nodes](../stencil-weights/)).
When $\delta_+ \ne \delta_-$, this gives asymmetric three-point weights and nonzero weight at $f(x)$.
Nominal $h$ only determines node placement.
Even without bounds, room extends to the largest floating-point value, and nodes avoid sides with room below $h$.
Central differences then use roomier-side triples $x$, $x \pm h$, $x \pm 2h$ for first derivatives or quadruples $x$, $x \pm h$, $x \pm 2h$, $x \pm 3h$ for second derivatives, preserving second order ([Domain boundaries and one-sided differences](oxref:differentiate-bounded-fixed-nodes)).
If room exists but rounding erases one direction's displacement, forward differences reverse direction; central differences give `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`) without a one-sided fallback.
No distinct nodes in either direction gives the same error.

The next cell checks this effect.
Differentiate $f(t) = \sin(t - X)$ centrally at $x = X + \xi$ near $X = 10^8$.
When $t$ and $X$ are close ($X/2 \le t \le 2X$), $t - X$ is exact by Sterbenz's lemma, leaving only $\sin$ evaluation rounding.
At $2^{26} \le x < 2^{27}$, $\mathrm{ulp}(x) = 2^{-26} \approx 1.49 \times 10^{-8}$; steps are noninteger ulp multiples from 1.3 to 100.7.
Also compare manually computed central differences divided by nominal $h$ and relative-error prediction $\lvert (\delta_+ + \delta_-)/(2h) - 1 \rvert$.
The last column estimates rounding $\varepsilon \lvert f(x) \rvert / (\bar\delta \lvert f'(x) \rvert)$ assuming function errors within 1 ulp (relative at most $\varepsilon$), with $\bar\delta = (\delta_+ + \delta_-)/2$ (derived on the [next page](oxref:differentiate-roundoff-rule)).
Exact value $\cos \xi$ is an f64 reference.

use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_differentiate::{DifferenceScheme, DifferenceStep, FiniteDifference};

let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });

    let (plus, minus) = ((x + h) - x, x - (x - h));

    rows.push((
        multiple,
        plus / ulp,
        minus / ulp,
        ((nominal - exact) / exact).abs(),
        predicted,
        ((value - exact) / exact).abs(),

$\delta_\pm / \mathrm{ulp}$ is an integer, differing from nominal $h / \mathrm{ulp}$.
Manual nominal-$h$ errors match prediction $\lvert (\delta_+ + \delta_-)/(2h) - 1 \rvert$ to at least 4 digits, ranging from $3.85 \times 10^{-2}$ to $0.231$ for $h$ of 1.3–10.4 ulp.
Even at $h$ of 100 ulp, error $3 \times 10^{-3}$ remains.
`FiniteDifference` relative errors at default $\xi = 0.3$ range from $6.1 \times 10^{-12}$ to $6.7 \times 10^{-10}$, all below rounding estimates.
At $\xi = 0.9$, larger $\lvert f \rvert / \lvert f' \rvert = \tan \xi$ raises estimates about fourfold without changing nominal-$h$ division errors.
This example uses extremely small steps to isolate displacement handling; practical defaults greatly exceed ulp.

## Subtracting base values and known base `base`

Derivative weights $w_j$ differentiate constants to zero and satisfy $\sum_j w_j = 0$.
Subtracting base $f(x)$ from every sample therefore preserves the result.

$$
\sum_j w_j f(x_j) = \sum_j w_j \bigl( f(x_j) - f(x) \bigr) + f(x) \sum_j w_j .
$$

Rounded asymmetric weights, however, do not sum to exactly zero.
The final term $f(x) \sum_j w_j$ remains and grows with $\lvert f(x) \rvert$.
`FiniteDifference` weights differences $f(x_j) - f(x)$ to remove this term.
Removed errors are comparable to function rounding, so improvements are only constant factors and matter when $\lvert f(x) \rvert$ exceeds derivative scales.
If subtraction overflows, fall back to weighting raw samples.
Symmetric central normalized weights $(-\tfrac{1}{2}, 0, \tfrac{1}{2})$ are exact and sum exactly to zero.

Fixed differences use $f(x)$ for base subtraction and asymmetric weights, evaluating $f(x)$ even centrally.
Callers already knowing $f(x)$ can pass it as `base` to `gradient`, `hessian`, `jacobian`, `jvp` and their `_into` forms, or `derivative_into`, of `FiniteDifference` to save one evaluation.
One-shot functions and `derivative` and `second_derivative` of `FiniteDifference` have no `base` (use one-variable `hessian` for second derivatives with `base`).
Adaptive, node, and complex-step differences have no equivalent argument.
`base` is scalar for gradients and Hessians, or an output-count array for Jacobians and directions.
Nonfinite values give `NonFinite` and length mismatches `DimensionMismatch`, but equality with $f(x)$ is not checked.
`base` serves as both sample at $x$ and subtraction base.

For $n$ variables, gradient and Hessian evaluation counts follow; supplying `base` saves one evaluation in each case.
Hessians evaluate bitwise-identical points only once.

| Operation | Forward | Central |
|---|---|---|
| `derivative`, `gradient`, `jacobian` | $n + 1$ | $2n + 1$ |
| `second_derivative`, `hessian` | $1 + 2n + n(n - 1)/2$ | $1 + 2n^2$ |
| `jvp` | 2 | 3 |

Univariate ($n = 1$) second derivatives take 3 calls forward (nodes $x, x + h, x + 2h$) or centrally.

A $\delta_b$ offset in `base` shifts derivatives by $w_x \delta_b$, the offset multiplied by $f(x)$ weight $w_x$.
$w_x$ equals $-1/h$ for first forward, 0 first central, $1/h^2$ second forward, and $-2/h^2$ second central.
Only first central differences are nearly unaffected; even central second derivatives suffer error $2 \delta_b / h^2$.

The next cell computes gradients and Hessians of $f(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_i e^{x_i}$ with $n$ variables without `base` and with `base` deliberately offset by $\delta_b$.
At point $x_i = 0.1(i + 1)$, $\lvert x_i \rvert < 1$, making defaults equal factor $c$ itself.
Compare counts with table predictions and maximum errors against exact values (gradient and diagonal $e^{x_i}$, mixed 0) with offset estimate $\lvert w_x \rvert \delta_b$.
Exact-value references use f64 inherent methods.

use scioxide_differentiate::DifferenceScheme::{Central, Forward};
use scioxide_differentiate::FiniteDifference;

let n = n as usize;
let delta_b: f64 = shift.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot read offset")?;
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..n).map(|i| 0.1 * (i as f64 + 1.0)).collect();
let f = |p: &[f64], y: &mut [f64]| {
    y[0] = p.iter().map(|&v| RealScalar::exp(v)).sum();
    Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
};
// Reference: ∂f/∂xᵢ = ∂²f/∂xᵢ² = e^{xᵢ}, mixed 0, f(x) = Σ e^{xᵢ} (f64 inherent methods).
let exact: Vec<f64> = x.iter().map(|v| v.exp()).collect();
let fx: f64 = exact.iter().sum();

    let reference = |k: usize| match (hessian, k / n == k % n) {
        (false, _) => exact[k],
        (true, true) => exact[k % n],
        (true, false) => 0.0,
    };
    let errors = values
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(k, v)| (v - reference(k)).abs());
    errors.fold(0.0, f64::max)
};

for (operation, hessian) in [("Gradient", false), ("Hessian", true)] {
    for (name, scheme) in [("Forward", Forward), ("Central", Central)] {
        // Theoretical calls without base and |w_x|, the f(x) weight magnitude; |xᵢ| < 1 gives h = c.
        let (calls, weight) = match (hessian, scheme) {
            (false, Forward) => (n + 1, 1.0 / eps.sqrt()),
            (false, Central) => (2 * n + 1, 0.0),
            (true, Forward) => (1 + 2 * n + n * (n - 1) / 2, eps.powf(-2.0 / 3.0)),
            (true, Central) => (1 + 2 * n * n, 2.0 / eps.sqrt()),

        let method = FiniteDifference::default().with_scheme(scheme);
        for base in [None, Some(fx + delta_b)] {
            let result = if hessian {
                method.hessian(f, &x, None, base)
            } else {
                method.gradient(f, &x, None, base)
            }
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            let values = result.values().ok_or("Derivative not published")?;

                None => ("None", calls, None),
                Some(_) => ("f(x) + δ_b", calls - 1, Some(weight * delta_b)),
            };
            rows.push((
                operation,
                name,
                label,
                expected,
                result.report().evaluations(),
                max_error(values, hessian),
                estimate,
            ));
        }
    }
}

let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Operation" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Formula" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "base" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Predicted evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Maximum error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Offset contribution |w_x| δ_b [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" }
]);

At defaults $n = 3$ and $\delta_b = 10^{-12}$, every count matches theory and `base` saves one call (forward gradient 4 versus 3; central Hessian 19 versus 18).
Incorrect-`base` maximum errors are $6.71 \times 10^{-5}$ for forward gradients, $2.73 \times 10^{-2}$ forward Hessians, and $1.34 \times 10^{-4}$ central Hessians, each within 0.1% of estimate $\lvert w_x \rvert \delta_b$.
Central-gradient error remains $1.30 \times 10^{-11}$, as without `base`, consistent with zero weight on $f(x)$.
At $\delta_b = 10^{-9}$, forward-gradient error is $6.71 \times 10^{-2}$ and forward-Hessian error 27.3, unusable as derivatives.
At $\delta_b = 0$, rows with `base` have the same errors as those without `base`.
At $n = 1$, second derivatives take 3 calls forward or central, 2 with `base`.
`base` saves one evaluation but makes callers responsible for the supplied value's correctness.

## Summary

First forward differences are first order, first central second order, central second differences second order, and forward second differences first order; logarithmic slopes measure these orders.
Without explicit steps, `FiniteDifference` uses $c \cdot \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert)$ with factor $c$ equal to $\varepsilon^{1/2}$, $\varepsilon^{1/3}$, or $\varepsilon^{1/4}$ according to the formula and derivative order.
For scales far from 1, specify absolute or relative steps through `DifferenceStep`.
Weights use actual rounded displacements, excluding nominal-versus-represented step discrepancies from results.
Known $f(x)$ saves one evaluation through `base`, but callers ensure its correctness; even central second differences receive an error of $2\delta_b/h^2$ from an offset.
