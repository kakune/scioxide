This chapter's examples use `scioxide-differentiate` to differentiate concrete models from thermodynamics, chemical reactions, molecular modeling, reaction–diffusion, radiation, equations of state, and celestial mechanics.
Every example includes an independent check using analytic derivatives, conservation laws or identities, or another method.
Changing controls from their defaults reveals effects of step or node selection, variable scale, distance from boundaries, and noise.

## Subjects shared with other chapters

Two examples examine models shared with other chapters from different perspectives, using matching parameters.
The van der Waals example uses CO2 critical values $T_c = 304.13\,\mathrm{K}$ and $p_c = 7.3773\,\mathrm{MPa}$.
Liquid–gas coexistence is covered primarily by [van der Waals gas and Maxwell's equal-area rule](../../scioxide-rootfind/examples/van-der-waals-maxwell/) in the root-finding chapter, and implicit root sensitivities by [Implicit sensitivity of the van der Waals equation](../../scioxide-ad/examples/van-der-waals-implicit/) in automatic differentiation.
The orbital example uses an ellipse with semimajor axis $a = 1$, a focus at the origin, and default eccentricity $e = 0.6$, matching [Kepler's equation and eccentricity](../../scioxide-rootfind/examples/kepler-equation/), which solves Kepler's equation with six root-finding methods.

## Summary

The examples each use one model to explore tabular differentiation, complex steps, multivariate gradients and Hessians, sparse Jacobians, boundaries, source selection, adaptive differences, or workspace reuse.
Checks use quantities known independently of numerical differentiation, such as analytic formulas or conservation laws; do not accept results solely from adaptive error estimates or small residuals.
Several examples show that default steps or tolerances mismatched to variable scales can return incorrect results with `Converged` or `RoundoffLimit`.
Choose steps and tolerances to suit quantity scales and coordinate origins.
