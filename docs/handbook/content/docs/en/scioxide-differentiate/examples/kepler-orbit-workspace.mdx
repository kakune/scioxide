Time evolution computes same-shaped derivatives thousands of times across stages.
Preparing `scioxide-differentiate` methods and workspace once and repeating borrowed `gradient_into` avoids reallocating workspace per stage.
This example integrates the planar Kepler problem with velocity Verlet and computes forces as difference gradients of potential energy.
Check energy and angular momentum, analytic forces, and exact Kepler-equation trajectories to identify effects of difference formulas.
Later, differentiate eccentric anomaly $E$ with respect to mean anomaly $M$, comparing with implicitly derived $\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}M$.

Orbital elements match the [root-finding example](../../../scioxide-rootfind/examples/kepler-equation/) using six Kepler solvers.
Semimajor axis is $a = 1$, focus at the origin, periapsis along $x$, and default eccentricity $e = 0.6$.

## Model and integration

In dimensionless units with gravitational parameter $\mu = 1$, a unit mass at planar position $\mathbf{x}$ has potential $U(\mathbf{x}) = -\mu / \lvert \mathbf{x} \rvert$ and force $\mathbf{F} = -\nabla U = -\mu \mathbf{x} / \lvert \mathbf{x} \rvert^3$.
Energy $E_{\mathrm{tot}} = \lvert \mathbf{v} \rvert^2 / 2 + U$ and angular momentum $L = x_1 v_2 - x_2 v_1$ are conserved.
An ellipse with semimajor axis $a = 1$ and eccentricity $e$ starting at periapsis $\mathbf{x}_0 = (1 - e, 0)$ with velocity $\mathbf{v}_0 = (0, \sqrt{(1 + e)/(1 - e)})$ has period $2\pi$, energy $E_{\mathrm{tot}} = -1/2$, and angular momentum $L = \sqrt{1 - e^2}$.

Kepler's equation gives the exact trajectory.
Mean motion $n = \sqrt{\mu / a^3} = 1$ makes mean anomaly $M = t$ at time $t$ after periapsis.
Solve for eccentric anomaly $E$ from

$$
M = E - e \sin E
$$

as the root.
The position is then

$$
x_1 = \cos E - e, \qquad x_2 = \sqrt{1 - e^2} \sin E
$$

(Derivation and root uniqueness appear in the root-finding example.)
Use this exact trajectory to measure integration errors.

Velocity Verlet advances positions and velocities at step $\Delta t$ as follows.

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n + \Delta t \, \mathbf{v}_n + \frac{\Delta t^2}{2} \mathbf{F}_n,
\qquad
\mathbf{v}_{n+1} = \mathbf{v}_n + \frac{\Delta t}{2} (\mathbf{F}_n + \mathbf{F}_{n+1}).
$$

Here $\mathbf{F}_n = \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}_n)$, requiring one force evaluation per stage.
The method is symplectic: for conservative forces, energy errors oscillate within width $O(\Delta t^2)$ even over long times instead of growing continually.
Central forces conserve angular momentum except for rounding.
Orbital phase error, however, grows linearly with time, increasing distance from the exact trajectory each orbit.

The following model definitions serve three cells.
`potential` represents $U(\mathbf{x})$; `exact_force` analytic force $\mathbf{F} = -\mathbf{x} / \lvert \mathbf{x} \rvert^3$; `verlet` velocity Verlet from periapsis; `eccentric_anomaly` Kepler root $E(M)$; `kepler_position` exact position at $t$.
`verlet` accepts a force function, passing position $\mathbf{x}_{n+1}$ and $U(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})$ already computed for energy monitoring.
To compute $E(M)$, map $M$ to $[-\pi, \pi]$, then use $\lvert M \rvert$ as the mean anomaly in Newton's method starting from $E_0 = \pi$.
$f(E) = E - e \sin E - \lvert M \rvert$ is convex on $[0, \pi]$ because $f'' = e \sin E \ge 0$, and $f(\pi) \ge 0$, so iterates from this initial value decrease monotonically to the root without crossing it (the root-finding example's initial-value rule).

```rust
//| id: differentiate-kepler-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Potential, velocity Verlet, and Kepler solutions"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

/// Potential U(x) = −1/|x|, μ = 1.
pub(crate) fn potential(p: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    -1.0 / RealScalar::sqrt(p[0] * p[0] + p[1] * p[1])
}

/// Analytic force F = −x/|x|³.
pub(crate) fn exact_force(p: &[f64; 2]) -> [f64; 2] {
    let r = RealScalar::sqrt(p[0] * p[0] + p[1] * p[1]);
    [-p[0] / (r * r * r), -p[1] / (r * r * r)]
}

/// Advance total velocity-Verlet stages from periapsis (1 − e, 0), returning max |ΔE/E|, max |ΔL/L|, and stage positions.
/// force accepts x and U(x) and returns force.
pub(crate) fn verlet(
    e: f64,
    dt: f64,
    total: usize,
    mut force: impl FnMut(&[f64; 2], f64) -> Result<[f64; 2], String>,
) -> Result<(f64, f64, Vec<[f64; 2]>), String> {
    let (mut x, mut v) = (
        [1.0 - e, 0.0],
        [0.0, RealScalar::sqrt((1.0 + e) / (1.0 - e))],
    );
    let mut u = potential(&x);
    let mut f = force(&x, u)?;
    let (e0, l0) = (
        0.5 * (v[0] * v[0] + v[1] * v[1]) + u,
        x[0] * v[1] - x[1] * v[0],
    );
    let (mut energy, mut momentum) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    let mut path = Vec::with_capacity(total);
    for _ in 0..total {
        x = [
            x[0] + dt * (v[0] + 0.5 * dt * f[0]),
            x[1] + dt * (v[1] + 0.5 * dt * f[1]),
        ];
        u = potential(&x);
        let next = force(&x, u)?;
        v = [
            v[0] + 0.5 * dt * (f[0] + next[0]),
            v[1] + 0.5 * dt * (f[1] + next[1]),
        ];
        f = next;
        energy = energy.max(((0.5 * (v[0] * v[0] + v[1] * v[1]) + u - e0) / e0).abs());
        momentum = momentum.max(((x[0] * v[1] - x[1] * v[0] - l0) / l0).abs());
        path.push(x);
    }
    Ok((energy, momentum, path))
}

/// Kepler root E(M) for M = E − e sin E; map to |M| ≤ π and solve by Newton from E₀ = π.
pub(crate) fn eccentric_anomaly(e: f64, mean: f64) -> f64 {
    let tau = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI;
    let turns = (mean / tau).round();
    let reduced = mean - turns * tau;
    let target = reduced.abs();
    let mut anomaly = std::f64::consts::PI;
    for _ in 0..64 {
        let residual = anomaly - e * RealScalar::sin(anomaly) - target;
        let step = residual / (1.0 - e * RealScalar::cos(anomaly));
        anomaly -= step;
        if step.abs() <= 4.0 * f64::EPSILON {
            break;
        }
    }
    turns * tau + anomaly.copysign(reduced)
}

/// Exact position (cos E − e, √(1 − e²) sin E) at time t = mean anomaly M.
pub(crate) fn kepler_position(e: f64, time: f64) -> [f64; 2] {
    let anomaly = eccentric_anomaly(e, time);
    [
        RealScalar::cos(anomaly) - e,
        RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 - e * e) * RealScalar::sin(anomaly),
    ]
}
```

## Bias in difference forces

Difference forces depart from true forces through truncation.
At default steps $h = c \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert)$, central $c = \varepsilon^{1/3}$ relative bias is about $\varepsilon^{2/3} \approx 4 \times 10^{-11}$ and forward $c = \varepsilon^{1/2}$ about $\varepsilon^{1/2} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-8}$ ([Taylor expansion and step-size selection](../../algorithms/taylor-and-step-size/)).
At periapsis, small $\lvert \mathbf{x} \rvert = 1 - e$ and rapid stepwise $U$ variation increase bias above this guideline.

Bias is far below time-step error ($\Delta t^2$, about $4 \times 10^{-5}$ for 1000 stages per orbit), barely affecting energy errors.
It gradually displaces the orbit, appearing in differences from analytic-force integration at the same step and growing with time.
Numerical gradients are generally neither exactly conservative nor central, so angular momentum conservation also breaks at the bias scale.

## Reusing workspace and known potential

Each stage computes $U(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})$ for energy monitoring; pass it to `base` of `gradient_into` ([Taylor expansion and step-size selection](oxref:differentiate-base-argument)).
For a two-variable gradient, function evaluations fall from $2 \cdot 2 + 1 = 5$ to 4 with central differences and from $2 + 1 = 3$ to 2 with forward differences.
Construct `FiniteDifference` once per formula and allocate workspace once with `FiniteDifferenceWorkspace::workspace(DerivativeRequest::new(&method, DerivativeShape::gradient(2)))`, reusing it for every stage ([Reusing Plans, requirements, and workspaces](oxref:differentiate-capacity-checks)).
Stop computation if an evaluation report does not publish output.

## Eccentric-anomaly derivatives

Many orbital quantities involve derivatives with respect to mean anomaly $M$.
For example, $\dot E = n \, \mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}M$ determines velocities through time derivatives $\dot x_1 = -\sin E \, \dot E$ and $\dot x_2 = \sqrt{1 - e^2} \cos E \, \dot E$.
$E(M)$ is implicit as a Kepler root; differentiating both sides of $M = E - e \sin E$ with respect to $M$ gives $1 = (1 - e \cos E) \, \mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}M$ and hence

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}M} = \frac{1}{1 - e \cos E}
$$

this exact identity.
The denominator is Kepler derivative $f'(E)$ in $f(E) = E - e \sin E - M$; $\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}M = 1/f'(E)$ is root sensitivity (absolute condition number) to changes in $M$.
Its maximum is $1/(1 - e)$ at periapsis $E = 0$ and minimum $1/(1 + e)$ at apoapsis $E = \pi$.

Since $E(M)$ comes from root-finding, pass $E(M)$ as known base `base`.
Use workspace-free `FiniteDifference::derivative_into` for this scalar first derivative, relying only on stack storage.
Calls per point are 2 central or 1 forward.

```rust
//|       - {"artifact":"anomaly","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":100,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"anomaly","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":1e-5,"max":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, ExtendedInterval, SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_differentiate::{DifferenceScheme, DifferenceStep, FiniteDifference};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let e = eccentricity;
let grid: Vec<f64> = (0..=360)
    .map(|k| 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * k as f64 / 360.0)
    .collect();
// Exact implicit derivative dE/dM = 1/(1 − e cos E).
let exact: Vec<f64> = grid
    .iter()
    .map(|&m| 1.0 / (1.0 - e * RealScalar::cos(eccentric_anomaly(e, m))))
    .collect();
// Match absolute steps to default factors ε^(1/3) and ε^(1/2) for |M| ≤ 1.
let methods = [
    ("Central difference", DifferenceScheme::Central, f64::EPSILON.cbrt()),
    ("Forward difference", DifferenceScheme::Forward, f64::EPSILON.sqrt()),
];
let (mut rows, mut errors, mut curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for (name, scheme, c) in methods {
    let mut method = FiniteDifference::default().with_scheme(scheme);
    if step == "Absolute" {
        method = method.with_step(DifferenceStep::absolute(c).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    }
    let (mut evaluations, mut worst, mut at, mut zeros) = (0, 0.0_f64, None, 0);
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for (&m, &d) in grid.iter().zip(&exact) {
        let mut slope = 0.0;
        let f = |mean: f64| Ok::<f64, SciOxideError>(eccentric_anomaly(e, mean));
        // Pass solved E(M) as base, omitting the M evaluation.
        let base = Some(eccentric_anomaly(e, m));
        let scope = ExecutionContext::sequential();
        let report = method
            .derivative_into(f, m, ExtendedInterval::entire(), base, &mut slope, scope)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if !report.output_available() {
            return Err(format!(
                "Derivative not published: {}",
                report.termination()
            ));
        }
        evaluations = evaluations.max(report.evaluations());
        let relative = (slope - d).abs() / d;
        if relative > worst {
            (worst, at) = (relative, Some(m));
        }
        if relative > 0.0 {
            points.push([m, relative]);
        } else {
            zeros += 1;
        }
        if scheme == DifferenceScheme::Central {
            curve.push([m, slope]);
        }
    }
    rows.push((name, evaluations, worst, at, exact[0], zeros));
    errors.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points }));
}
let exact_curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = grid.iter().zip(&exact).map(|(&m, &d)| [m, d]).collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "line",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "link": { "group": "kepler-mean-anomaly" },
    "xLabel": "Mean anomaly M [rad]",
    "yLabel": "dE/dM [-]",
    "series": [
        {
            "name": "Exact 1/(1 − e cos E)",
            "points": exact_curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        },
        {
            "name": "Central difference",
            "points": curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dotted", "showSymbol": true }
        }
    ]
}));
emit_line_chart!(
    &errors,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "kepler-mean-anomaly" },
        "xLabel": "Mean anomaly M [rad]",
        "yLabel": "dE/dM relative error [-]",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Difference formula" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Calls per point [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximizing M [rad]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Periapsis dE/dM = 1/(1 − e) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Zero-error points [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "anomaly" })
);
```

At default $e = 0.6$, $\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}M$ varies fourfold from periapsis $2.5$ to apoapsis $0.625$; central points overlap the dashed exact curve.
Maximum relative errors are $2.3 \times 10^{-9}$ centrally and $6.1 \times 10^{-8}$ forward.
Central relative error is $5.7 \times 10^{-11}$ at periapsis $M = 0$ but about 40 times larger, $2.3 \times 10^{-9}$, at equivalent periapsis $M = 2\pi$.
Default steps $h = \varepsilon^{1/3} \max(1, \lvert M \rvert)$ grow by $2\pi$ at $M = 2\pi$; truncation proportional to $h^2$ grows by $(2\pi)^2 \approx 39$.
Forward relative errors at periapsis and apoapsis ($M = 0$, $\pi$, $2\pi$) are over an order below the maximum.
Around $M_0 = 0, \pi, 2\pi$, odd relation $E(M_0 + \delta) - E(M_0) = -\{E(M_0 - \delta) - E(M_0)\}$ gives $E''(M_0) = 0$, eliminating leading forward truncation $h E''/2$.

Absolute steps (central $\varepsilon^{1/3}$, forward $\varepsilon^{1/2}$) reduce central errors at $M = 2\pi$ to $5.9 \times 10^{-11}$, comparable with $M = 0$, with maximum $1.1 \times 10^{-10}$.
For coordinates like angles with arbitrary origins, relative factor $\max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert)$ makes errors depend on coordinate values; choose absolute `DifferenceStep::absolute`.
At $e = 0.99$, periapsis $\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}M$ reaches 100 and changes over $M$ width about $10^{-3}$, making relative-step central errors reach $2.4 \times 10^{-4}$ at $M = 2\pi$.
Every error compares with the exact implicit-derivative identity.

## Summary

Prepare methods and workspace once and repeat `gradient_into` to compute stage gradients without reallocating.
Passing monitored potential to `base` saves one call per stage.
Verlet energy errors depend on time-step $O(\Delta t^2)$ and exceed default force bias, so conservation cannot distinguish formulas here.
Force bias appears in analytic-force trajectory differences and angular momentum; forward displacement is about three orders above central.
Workspace-free `derivative_into` suffices for repeated scalar derivatives, including implicit $E(M)$ checked by implicit differentiation.
