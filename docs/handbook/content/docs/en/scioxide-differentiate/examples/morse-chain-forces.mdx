In molecular models, the negative gradient of potential energy $U(\mathbf{x})$ gives atomic forces, and its Hessian is the vibrational stiffness matrix.
Models with cumbersome analytic derivatives or external energy-only computations require differences.
This example uses a one-dimensional Morse-bonded chain, obtaining forces from gradients and curvature from Hessians and directions.
Besides analytic forces and stiffness, check zero total force in isolation (action–reaction) and energy invariance under uniform translation.

## Morse-potential atomic chain

Place $N$ atoms on a line at $x_1 < x_2 < \dots < x_N$ (Å), bonding neighbors by Morse potentials.

$$
U(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} V(x_{i+1} - x_i), \qquad V(r) = D_e \bigl[ 1 - e^{-a (r - r_e)} \bigr]^2 .
$$

$D_e$ is well depth in eV, $a$ inverse width in Å⁻¹, and $r_e$ equilibrium bond length in Å.
For carbon–carbon single bonds, use $D_e = 3.6\,\mathrm{eV}$, $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{\text{Å}^{-1}}$, and $r_e = 1.54\,\mathrm{\text{Å}}$.
$D_e$ matches mean C–C bond enthalpy about 348 kJ/mol, 3.6 eV per bond; $r_e$ matches ethane or diamond C–C lengths.
For $a = 2.0\,\mathrm{\text{Å}^{-1}}$, the equilibrium force constant is $V''(r_e) = 2 D_e a^2 = 28.8\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}^2} \approx 461\,\mathrm{N/m}$.
Setting $\rho = r - r_e$ and $u = e^{-a\rho}$ gives these bond derivatives.

$$
V'(r) = 2 D_e a \, u (1 - u), \qquad V''(r) = 2 D_e a^2 \, u (2 u - 1), \qquad V'''(r) = -2 D_e a^3 \, u (4 u - 1).
$$

$V''$ changes sign at $u = 1/2$, or $\rho = \ln 2 / a = 0.347\,\mathrm{\text{Å}}$; more stretched bonds have negative curvature.

At uniform extension $\Delta r$ in configuration $x_i = (i - 1)(r_e + \Delta r)$, left and right bond forces cancel on internal atoms.
Only endpoint forces remain: with $r = r_e + \Delta r$, they are $F_1 = V'(r)$ and $F_N = -V'(r)$.
Both are internal bond forces, so their sum is zero.
The Hessian is tridiagonal, with $H_{ii} = V''(r_{i-1,i}) + V''(r_{i,i+1})$ (one contribution at endpoints) and $H_{i,i+1} = H_{i+1,i} = -V''(r_{i,i+1})$.
Translation invariance $U(\mathbf{x} + c\mathbf{1}) = U(\mathbf{x})$ makes each row sum zero, hence $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$.

The following block defines shared code for the two cells.
`chain` gives uniformly stretched configurations; `energy` represents $U(\mathbf{x})$; `gradient` gives analytic $\nabla U$; `bond_force`, `bond_curvature`, and `bond_third` give $V'$, $V''$, and $V'''$.
Exponentials use SciOxide `RealScalar::exp`.

```rust
//| id: differentiate-morse-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Morse-chain energy and analytic derivatives"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

/// Well depth De [eV].
pub(crate) const DEPTH: f64 = 3.6;
/// Inverse width a [Å⁻¹].
pub(crate) const WIDTH: f64 = 2.0;
/// Equilibrium length re [Å].
pub(crate) const BOND_LENGTH: f64 = 1.54;

/// Uniform bond extension Δr: xᵢ = (i − 1)(re + Δr) [Å].
pub(crate) fn chain(atoms: usize, stretch: f64) -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..atoms)
        .map(|i| i as f64 * (BOND_LENGTH + stretch))
        .collect()
}

/// u = e^{−a(r − re)}.
fn decay(r: f64) -> f64 {
    RealScalar::exp(-WIDTH * (r - BOND_LENGTH))
}

/// V′(r) = 2 De a u(1 − u) [eV/Å].
pub(crate) fn bond_force(r: f64) -> f64 {
    let u = decay(r);
    2.0 * DEPTH * WIDTH * u * (1.0 - u)
}

/// V″(r) = 2 De a² u(2u − 1) [eV/Å²].
pub(crate) fn bond_curvature(r: f64) -> f64 {
    let u = decay(r);
    2.0 * DEPTH * WIDTH * WIDTH * u * (2.0 * u - 1.0)
}

/// V‴(r) = −2 De a³ u(4u − 1) [eV/Å³].
pub(crate) fn bond_third(r: f64) -> f64 {
    let u = decay(r);
    -2.0 * DEPTH * WIDTH.powi(3) * u * (4.0 * u - 1.0)
}

/// U(x) = Σ De (1 − u)² [eV].
pub(crate) fn energy(x: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    x.windows(2)
        .map(|w| DEPTH * (1.0 - decay(w[1] - w[0])).powi(2))
        .sum()
}

/// Analytic ∇U [eV/Å]; bond i contributes −V′ to atom i and +V′ to i + 1.
pub(crate) fn gradient(x: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]) {
    out.fill(0.0);
    for i in 0..x.len() - 1 {
        let force = bond_force(x[i + 1] - x[i]);
        out[i] -= force;
        out[i + 1] += force;
    }
}
```

## Forces and their sum

`gradient` of `FiniteDifference::default()` takes coordinatewise central differences, returning $\nabla U$ in $2N + 1$ energy calls including $x$.
Force is $\mathbf{F} = -\nabla U$.
Default atomic steps $h_i = \varepsilon^{1/3} \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert)$ grow with coordinates ([SciOxide default steps](oxref:differentiate-default-steps)).

Central truncation is $h_i^2 \, \partial^3 U / \partial x_i^3 / 6$.
At atom 1, $U$ depends on $x_1$ only through $V(x_2 - x_1)$, giving $\partial^3 U / \partial x_1^3 = -V'''(r)$; atom $N$ gives $\partial^3 U / \partial x_N^3 = V'''(r)$.
Internal contributions cancel for uniform extensions.
Difference forces are therefore $F_1 \approx V' + h_1^2 V'''/6$ and $F_N \approx -V' - h_N^2 V'''/6$, predicting their sum as follows.

$$
\sum_i F_i \approx \frac{(h_1^2 - h_N^2) \, V'''(r)}{6} .
$$

Defaults have $h_1 \ne h_N$, preventing endpoint truncation cancellation and leaving a nonzero total.
Equal absolute steps give $h_1 = h_N$ and zero predicted total.

Absolute step $6 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{\text{Å}}$ nearly equals atom 1's default $\varepsilon^{1/3} = 6.06 \times 10^{-6}$.

```rust
//| id: differentiate-morse-forces
//| ref: differentiate-morse-forces
//| title: "Finite-difference forces and total force in a Morse chain"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [differentiate-morse-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-differentiate]
//| inputs:
//|   atoms: {"type":"range","label":"Atoms N","min":3,"max":10,"step":1,"value":5}
//|   stretch: {"type":"range","label":"Bond extension Δr [Å]","description":"Negative values compress bonds.","min":-0.2,"max":0.4,"step":0.05,"value":0.1}
//|   step_rule: {"type":"select","label":"Step selection","options":["Default, proportional to coordinates","Absolute 6e-6 Å"],"value":"Default, proportional to coordinates"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"absolute-step","label":"Absolute 6e-6 Å","description":"Equal endpoint steps cancel truncation, leaving rounding-scale total force.","inputs":{"step_rule":"Absolute 6e-6 Å"}}
//|   - {"id":"compressed","label":"Δr = −0.2 Å","description":"Compressed bonds reverse endpoint forces, pushing the chain outward.","inputs":{"stretch":-0.2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"forces","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":2.1371141814,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"forces","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"forces","path":"/rows/4/5","approx":{"value":2.82e-8,"rtol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"total","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":2.76e-8,"rtol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"total","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.76e-8,"rtol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"total","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":11}
//|   - case: absolute-step
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"total","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":-1e-10,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"total","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|   - case: compressed
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"forces","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":-1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"forces","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-8}}
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_differentiate::{DifferenceStep, FiniteDifference};

let n = atoms as usize;
let x = chain(n, stretch);
let absolute = step_rule.starts_with("Absolute");
let mut method = FiniteDifference::default();
if absolute {
    method = method.with_step(DifferenceStep::absolute(6e-6).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}
let objective = |p: &[f64], y: &mut [f64]| {
    y[0] = energy(p);
    Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
};
let result = method
    .gradient(objective, &x, None, None)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let derivative = result.values().ok_or("Gradient not published")?;
// Requested steps: defaults ε^(1/3) max(1, |xᵢ|).
let steps: Vec<f64> = x
    .iter()
    .map(|xi| {
        if absolute {
            6e-6
        } else {
            f64::EPSILON.cbrt() * xi.abs().max(1.0)
        }
    })
    .collect();
// Reference: uniform extension gives only endpoint ±V′(r).
let r = BOND_LENGTH + stretch;
let rows: Vec<(usize, f64, f64, f64, f64, f64)> = (0..n)
    .map(|i| {
        let exact = match i {
            0 => bond_force(r),
            i if i == n - 1 => -bond_force(r),
            _ => 0.0,
        };
        (
            i + 1,
            x[i],
            steps[i],
            -derivative[i],
            exact,
            (-derivative[i] - exact).abs(),
        )
    })
    .collect();
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed10 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Atom i", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Position x_i [Å]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 2 } },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Requested h_i [Å]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference force [eV/Å]", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Analytic force [eV/Å]", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Error [eV/Å]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "forces" })
);
let total: f64 = derivative.iter().map(|g| -g).sum();
// Leading prediction (h₁² − h_N²) V‴(r)/6; + 0.0 normalizes −0 to 0.
let predicted = (steps[0].powi(2) - steps[n - 1].powi(2)) * bond_third(r) / 6.0 + 0.0;
let total_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Total Σ F_i [eV/Å]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Leading prediction [eV/Å]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "2N + 1 [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    total_columns,
    vec![(total, predicted, result.report().evaluations(), 2 * n + 1)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "total" })
);
```

At defaults $N = 5$ and $\Delta r = 0.1\,\mathrm{\text{Å}}$, endpoints agree with analytic $\pm V'(r) = \pm 2.1371141814\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}}$ and internal forces are rounding-sized.
Atom 1 ($x_1 = 0$) uses $\varepsilon^{1/3}$; atom 5 ($x_5 = 6.56\,\mathrm{\text{Å}}$) uses 6.56 times that, giving errors $6.8 \times 10^{-10}$ and $2.8 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}}$ with ratio near squared-step ratio $6.56^2 = 43$.
Remaining total $2.8 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}}$ nearly matches leading-truncation prediction.
Absolute steps equalize and cancel endpoint truncation, reducing total to rounding-scale $4 \times 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}}$.
But atom $N$'s own error only falls to atom 1's level, about $6.5 \times 10^{-10}$, not zero.
Near-zero total diagnoses some difference errors but cannot reveal cancelling individual errors or prove individual accuracy.
Negative $\Delta r$ compresses bonds and reverses endpoint forces.
Every input uses $2N + 1$ evaluations.

## Hessians and directional derivatives

Vibrations require stiffness $\mathbf{H}$, but iterative eigenmethods may need only products $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}$.
`hessian` of `FiniteDifference::default()` computes diagonal central second differences and off-diagonal four-point combinations of first differences in $i$ and $j$, evaluating only the upper triangle.
Two calls for each of $N$ diagonals, four for each of $N(N-1)/2$ off-diagonals, plus one $x$ total $1 + 2N^2$ evaluations.
Default steps for second differences are $\varepsilon^{1/4} \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert)$, giving errors of order $O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})$; both steps and errors grow with atomic coordinates.

If gradient $\nabla U$ is available, compute $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}$ as the gradient's directional derivative $\mathrm{d}\nabla U(\mathbf{x} + \alpha \mathbf{v})/\mathrm{d}\alpha$ through `FiniteDifference::jvp` ([Directional derivatives](oxref:differentiate-jvp-formula)).
Central directional derivatives need 3 gradient evaluations at $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{x} \pm \alpha \mathbf{v}$, with step $\alpha = \varepsilon^{1/3} / \max_i (\lvert v_i \rvert / \max(1, \lvert x_i \rvert))$.
This cell uses defined `gradient`, analytic $\nabla U$, as its gradient.
With energy alone, directional differences of numerical gradients need $2N + 1$ energy calls per gradient, totaling $3(2N + 1)$.

The next cell compares the finite-difference Hessian with the analytic tridiagonal stiffness matrix, then compares products with the translation vector $\mathbf{1}$ or alternating vector $(1, -1, 1, \dots)$ computed from the Hessian and by directional differentiation.

The second shows analytic bond curvature $V''(r)$ and $\lVert \mathbf{H}\mathbf{v} \rVert$.

```rust
//| id: differentiate-morse-curvature
//| ref: differentiate-morse-curvature
//| title: "Morse-chain stiffness and Hessian–vector products"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [differentiate-morse-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-differentiate]
//| inputs:
//|   atoms: {"type":"range","label":"Atoms N","min":3,"max":10,"step":1,"value":6}
//|   stretch: {"type":"range","label":"Bond extension Δr [Å]","description":"Beyond 0.35 Å, bond curvature V″ becomes negative.","min":-0.2,"max":0.4,"step":0.05,"value":0.1}
//|   vector: {"type":"select","label":"Vector v","options":["Translation (1, …, 1)","Alternating (1, −1, …)"],"value":"Translation (1, …, 1)"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"alternating","label":"Alternating vector","description":"H v becomes nonzero; check both computations against analytic values.","inputs":{"vector":"Alternating (1, −1, …)"}}
//|   - {"id":"beyond-inflection","label":"Δr = 0.4 Å","description":"Beyond the inflection point, negative V″ reverses stiffness signs.","inputs":{"stretch":0.4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":73}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":15.030989,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|   - case: alternating
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|   - case: beyond-inflection
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"max":1e-8}}
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_differentiate::FiniteDifference;

let n = atoms as usize;
let x = chain(n, stretch);
let v: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|i| {
        if vector.starts_with("Translation") || i % 2 == 0 {
            1.0
        } else {
            -1.0
        }
    })
    .collect();
let objective = |p: &[f64], y: &mut [f64]| {
    y[0] = energy(p);
    Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
};
let method = FiniteDifference::default();
let result = method
    .hessian(objective, &x, None, None)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let hessian = result.values().ok_or("Hessian not published")?;
// Differentiate analytic gradient ∇U with N outputs directionally.
let analytic_gradient = |p: &[f64], y: &mut [f64]| {
    gradient(p, y);
    Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
};
let directional = method
    .jvp(analytic_gradient, &x, &v, n, None, None)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let jv = directional.values().ok_or("Directional derivative not published")?;
// Reference: uniform-extension V″ and tridiagonal stiffness.
let curvature = bond_curvature(BOND_LENGTH + stretch);
let exact = |i: usize, j: usize| match i.abs_diff(j) {
    0 if i == 0 || i == n - 1 => curvature,
    0 => 2.0 * curvature,
    1 => -curvature,
    _ => 0.0,
};
let h_error = (0..n * n)
    .map(|k| (hessian[k] - exact(k / n, k % n)).abs())
    .fold(0.0, f64::max);
let product = |entry: &dyn Fn(usize, usize) -> f64| -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..n)
        .map(|i| (0..n).map(|j| entry(i, j) * v[j]).sum())
        .collect()
};
let hv = product(&|i, j| hessian[i * n + j]);
let exact_hv = product(&exact);
let max_error = |values: &[f64]| {
    values
        .iter()
        .zip(&exact_hv)
        .map(|(u, w)| (u - w).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max)
};
let rows: Vec<(&str, usize, usize, Option<f64>, f64)> = vec![
    (
        "Hessian, energy differences",
        result.report().evaluations(),
        1 + 2 * n * n,
        Some(h_error),
        max_error(&hv),
    ),
    (
        "Gradient direction",
        directional.report().evaluations(),
        3,
        None,
        max_error(jv),
    ),
];
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Computation" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Predicted evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum stiffness error [eV/Å²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum H v error [eV/Å²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })
);
let norm = exact_hv.iter().map(|w| w * w).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
let digits6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let reference_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Bond curvature V″(r) [eV/Å²]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Analytic ‖H v‖ [eV/Å²]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    reference_columns,
    vec![(curvature, norm)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "reference" })
);
```

At defaults $N = 6$ and $\Delta r = 0.1\,\mathrm{\text{Å}}$, bond curvature is $V''(r) = 15.03\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}^2}$.
The Hessian uses $1 + 2 \times 36 = 73$ calls and agrees with stiffness within $1.4 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}^2}$ (relative about $10^{-5}$ against $V''$).
Products with the translation vector are analytically zero; Hessian multiplication leaves a residual of size $1.8 \times 10^{-4}$, while directional differentiation gives a result of size $5 \times 10^{-10}$, close to zero.
Directions require only 3 gradient calls and first-order differences, with smaller error than Hessian multiplication.
The alternating vector gives $\lVert \mathbf{H}\mathbf{v} \rVert = 128\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}^2}$, with maximum errors $2.9 \times 10^{-4}$ for the matrix product and $5.3 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}^2}$ for directional differentiation.
At $\Delta r = 0.4\,\mathrm{\text{Å}}$, $\rho$ exceeds inflection $0.347\,\mathrm{\text{Å}}$, giving $V'' < 0$ and negative stiffness diagonals.
Stiffness equals $V''$ times a positive-semidefinite tridiagonal matrix (the chain graph Laplacian), so curvature is negative in every nontranslation direction.

## Summary

Forces computed from finite-difference energy gradients require $2N + 1$ energy evaluations with `FiniteDifference::gradient`, matching analytic forces with errors $7 \times 10^{-10}$–$3 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{eV/\text{Å}}$.
An isolated system's exact total is zero, but coordinate-scaled defaults leave unequal endpoint truncation and residual of leading size $(h_1^2 - h_N^2) V'''/6$.
Equal absolute steps reduce totals to rounding, but small totals do not establish individual force accuracy.
Hessians need $1 + 2N^2$ calls, with second-difference errors of order $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
With gradients available, compute $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{v}$ through directional `FiniteDifference::jvp` in 3 gradient calls and with smaller errors.
