A DFT of length $N$ evaluates a finite sequence's $z$-transform $X(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_jz^{-j}$ at $N$ equally spaced points on the unit circle.
The chirp Z-transform (CZT) places $M$ evaluation points on an arc or logarithmic spiral in the $z$-plane, allowing $M$ to be chosen independently of $N$.
Evaluating only a narrow frequency band on the unit circle at fine spacing is called a zoom FFT.
`scioxide-fft` provides single-evaluation functions `czt` and `zoom_fft`, a contour representation `CztContour`, and a reusable plan `CztPlan`.
This page explains the definition, contour construction, plan usage, and zoom FFT frequency-band specification, then investigates damped-oscillation poles through the contour radius.
[CZT algorithm selection and error](../czt-algorithms-and-accuracy/) covers the two CZT algorithms and their error bounds.

## Contours in the z-plane and the CZT definition

Given a starting point $A$ and adjacent-point ratio $W$, set the evaluation points to $z_k=AW^{-k}$ ($k=0,\ldots,M-1$).
The CZT computes

$$
X_k = s\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_j z_k^{-j} = s\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_jA^{-j}W^{jk}
$$

as above.
$s$ is the factor determined by `Normalization`: relative to the input length $N$, it is $1/N$ for `Forward`, $1/\sqrt N$ for `Orthonormal`, and 1 for `Inverse` and `Unscaled`.
Because the CZT always uses the forward sum, selecting `Inverse` does not apply $1/N$.
The number $M$ of output points does not enter the factor.

`CztContour` stores the contour logarithmically.

$$
z_k = \exp\bigl(\rho_0 + k\rho + 2\pi i\,(t_0 + k t)\bigr)
$$

$\rho_0=\ln\lvert A\rvert$ is the starting logarithmic radius, and $\rho=-\ln\lvert W\rvert$ the logarithmic-radius increment per point.
$t_0=\arg A/2\pi$ is the starting phase and $t=-\arg W/2\pi$ the phase increment, measured in turns (1 turn is $2\pi$ rad).
$\rho=0$ gives an arc of radius $e^{\rho_0}$, while $\rho\ne0$ gives a logarithmic spiral.
Logarithmic storage supports contours whose $e^{\rho_0}$ is too large or too small to represent in the scalar type.
On the contour `CztContour::dft(n)`, setting $M=N=n$ makes the CZT identical to the forward DFT.

## Constructing a contour

There are 4 ways to construct a contour.

- `CztContour::new(log_radius, log_radius_step, start_turns, step_turns)` takes $\rho_0,\rho,t_0,t$ directly.
  Any NaN or infinite value gives `NonFinite`.
- `CztContour::from_wa(w, a)` and the function `czt(input, m, w, a, normalization)` take complex arguments in the order $W$, $A$.
  Computed $\ln\lvert W\rvert$ or $\ln\lvert A\rvert$ values with absolute value at most $2\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon$ is the scalar type's machine epsilon) are rounded to 0, so a rounded $e^{-2\pi i/N}$ also produces a contour on the unit circle.
  Use `new` when intentionally specifying a radial change this small.
  Zero $W$ or $A$ gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`); NaN or infinite components give `NonFinite`.
- `CztContour::dft(n)` is the DFT contour $z_k=e^{2\pi ik/n}$ for length $n$ and reduces the phase $k/n$ using integer arithmetic.
  $n=0$ gives `InvalidInput` (`Empty`).
- `CztContour::zoom(start, end, m, fs, endpoint)` is a frequency band on the unit circle, described in the [zoom FFT](#zoom-fft) section.

`CztContour::point(k)` returns $z_k$.
The plan computes in logarithmic form without constructing $z_k$, so the contour may contain points unrepresentable in the scalar type.
In contrast, `point` returns `NumericalInstability` if $z_k$ is unrepresentable in the scalar type.

## Plans, workspaces, and resource limits

`CztPlanSpec::new(n, m, contour, normalization)` specifies the transform.
The defaults are sequential execution, automatic FFT implementation selection, and no limits; change them with `with_execution_policy`, `with_backend_strategy`, and `with_limits`.
Read the specification back with methods such as `input_len()`, `output_len()`, `contour()`, `normalization()`, and `limits()`.
If $N$ or $M$ is 0, preparation returns `InvalidInput` (`Empty`).

`CztPlan::new(spec)` prepares the plan; `CztPlan::build(spec, context)` uses the FFT implementation and execution context from `FftPlanningContext` ([Injecting an external FFT implementation](oxref:fft-providers)).
Preparation selects Bluestein's method or direct summation from the contour and sizes; `algorithm()` returns `CztAlgorithm::Bluestein` or `CztAlgorithm::ScaledDirect`.
The selection rules are in [Algorithm selection](oxref:fft-czt-algorithm).
`plan_bytes()` returns the plan's storage size, including FFT tables.

Allocate a workspace with `plan.workspace(())` and transform one sequence with `execute_into(input, output, &mut workspace, cx)`.
Input may be a real or complex sequence (including strided or conjugated views); output must be a complex sequence of length $M$.
Incompatible lengths give `DimensionMismatch`; NaN or infinite input gives `NonFinite`.
The result is constructed in the workspace and copied to output only on success, so a failed call leaves output unchanged.
`execute_axis_into` transforms all sequences along one axis of a multidimensional array, writing every sequence or, on failure, none.

`CztLimits` specifies byte limits with 2 fields.

| Field | Quantity limited |
|---|---|
| `preparation_bytes` | Storage simultaneously held by the CZT plan itself during preparation. Excludes internal FFT plans and allocation overhead |
| `workspace_bytes` | Entire execution workspace, including temporary storage for axis transforms |

Both default to `usize::MAX`; exceeding either gives `ResourceLimit`.
There is no dedicated operation-count limit for direct summation.

Functions `czt` and `zoom_fft` prepare and discard a plan on every call, returning results in a new `Vec` of length $M$.
Bluestein preparation computes one FFT of the chirp kernel, so a function call requires 3 FFTs of length $L$, while reusing a plan requires only 2.
Create and reuse a plan and workspace when transforming many sequences on the same contour.

Allocate the `execute_axis_into` workspace with `plan.workspace(AxisWorkspaceRequest::Axis { shape, axis })`, passing the array shape `shape` and transform axis `axis`.
`()` is identical to `AxisWorkspaceRequest::Plain` and is for `execute_into` on one sequence.
The axis workspace holds all sequence outputs together and is therefore larger than the workspace for one sequence.
`workspace_requirements` returns the same size without allocating; read its byte count with `bytes()`.

The following cell transforms the 3 columns of a row-major real array of shape $[N, 3]$ at $M=24$ points on the contour $W=\lvert W\rvert e^{-2\pi i/24}$, $A=1$.
Inputs are sequence length $N$ and whether to use an outward spiral with $\lvert W\rvert=0.99$ (false selects the unit circle with $\lvert W\rvert=1$).
It checks 3 things.
For the first sequence, compare the function `czt` with the plan's `execute_into`.
Compare `execute_axis_into` along axis 0 with `execute_into` applied to each sequence separately.
Prepare plans with `workspace_bytes` in `CztLimits` set to exactly the byte count returned by `workspace_requirements` and to one byte less, then allocate workspaces for one sequence and axis 0.
Differences are divided by the maximum magnitude of the plan output.

```rust
//| id: fft-czt-function-axis-limits
//| ref: fft-czt-function-axis-limits
//| title: "The czt function, plans, axis execution, and byte limits"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   samples: {"type":"integer","label":"Sequence length N","description":"Samples per sequence. The array shape is [N, 3].","min":4,"max":256,"value":16}
//|   spiral: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Use an outward spiral with |W| = 0.99 (uncheck for the unit circle)","value":true}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"unit-circle","label":"Unit circle","description":"ρ = 0 selects Bluestein's method regardless of N.","inputs":{"spiral":false}}
//|   - {"id":"long-spiral","label":"Spiral with N = 256","description":"NM = 6144 > 4096, but χ exceeds 0.5, selecting direct summation without stored coefficients.","inputs":{"samples":256}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"ScaledDirect"}
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":"Success"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":"ResourceLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":"ResourceLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":"Success"}
//|   - case: unit-circle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Bluestein"}
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":"ResourceLimit"}
//|   - case: long-spiral
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"ScaledDirect"}
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":"ResourceLimit"}
use scioxide_core::{Complex, ExecutionContext, NdView, NdViewMut};
use scioxide_fft::{
    AxisWorkspaceRequest, CztContour, CztLimits, CztPlan, CztPlanSpec, Normalization, czt,
};

let (n, m, lanes) = (samples as usize, 24_usize, 3_usize);
let tau = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI;
// W = |W| e^{−2πi/M}, A = 1 (f64 from_polar and trigonometric functions are used only to generate input)
let w = Complex::from_polar(if spiral { 0.99 } else { 1.0 }, -tau / m as f64);
let a = Complex::new(1.0, 0.0);
// Row-major array of shape [N, 3]. Column c is one sequence along axis 0
let data: Vec<f64> = (0..n * lanes)
    .map(|i| {
        let (j, c) = ((i / lanes) as f64, (i % lanes) as f64);
        (0.3 * j + c).cos() + 0.5 * (1.1 * j * (c + 1.0)).sin()
    })
    .collect();
let column = |c: usize| -> Vec<f64> { (0..n).map(|j| data[j * lanes + c]).collect() };

let contour = CztContour::from_wa(w, a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let spec = CztPlanSpec::new(n, m, contour, Normalization::Unscaled);
let plan = CztPlan::new(spec).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
let mut workspace = plan.workspace(()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut lanes_out = vec![vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); m]; lanes];
for (c, out) in lanes_out.iter_mut().enumerate() {
    plan.execute_into(&column(c)[..], &mut out[..], &mut workspace, cx)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
let scale = lanes_out
    .iter()
    .flatten()
    .map(|v| v.norm())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);

// Check 1: the czt function creates and discards a plan with the same W and A on each call
let once = czt(&column(0)[..], m, w, a, Normalization::Unscaled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let function_gap = once
    .iter()
    .zip(&lanes_out[0])
    .map(|(p, q)| (*p - *q).norm())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);

// Check 2: transform 3 sequences along axis 0 at once, with output shape [M, 3]
let shape = [n, lanes];
let out_shape = [m, lanes];
let request = AxisWorkspaceRequest::Axis {
    shape: &shape,
    axis: 0,
};
let mut axis_workspace = plan.workspace(request).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut stacked = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); m * lanes];
let x = NdView::row_major(&data, &shape).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let y = NdViewMut::row_major(&mut stacked, &out_shape).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
plan.execute_axis_into(x, 0, y, &mut axis_workspace, cx)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut axis_gap = 0.0_f64;
for (c, out) in lanes_out.iter().enumerate() {
    for (k, value) in out.iter().enumerate() {
        axis_gap = axis_gap.max((stacked[k * lanes + c] - *value).norm());
    }
}

// Prepare with a workspace_bytes limit and allocate the requested workspace
let plain_bytes = plan
    .workspace_requirements(())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .bytes();
let axis_bytes = plan
    .workspace_requirements(request)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .bytes();
let attempt = |limits: CztLimits, request: AxisWorkspaceRequest| {
    CztPlan::new(spec.with_limits(limits)).and_then(|p| p.workspace(request).map(|_| ()))
};
let label = |outcome: Result<(), scioxide_core::SciOxideError>| match outcome {
    Ok(()) => "Success".to_string(),
    Err(e) => format!("{:?}", e.kind()),
};
let workspace_limit = |bytes: usize| CztLimits {
    workspace_bytes: bytes,
    ..CztLimits::default()
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (limit, request, name) in [
    (plain_bytes, AxisWorkspaceRequest::Plain, "One sequence"),
    (plain_bytes - 1, AxisWorkspaceRequest::Plain, "One sequence"),
    (plain_bytes, request, "Axis 0"),
    (axis_bytes, request, "Axis 0"),
] {
    let outcome = label(attempt(workspace_limit(limit), request));
    rows.push((name, limit, outcome));
}
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let agreement_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Algorithm" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "plan_bytes() [B]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "One-sequence workspace [B]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Axis-0 workspace [B]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "czt–plan difference / maximum output [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Axis–per-sequence difference / maximum output [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let agreement = (
    format!("{:?}", plan.algorithm()),
    plan.plan_bytes(),
    plain_bytes,
    axis_bytes,
    function_gap / scale,
    axis_gap / scale,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    agreement_columns,
    vec![agreement],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "agreement" })
);
let limit_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Workspace request" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "workspace_bytes limit [B]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Result" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    limit_columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "limits" })
);
```

For the default spiral with $N=16$, $NM=384\le4096$ selects direct summation; the one-sequence workspace is 640 B and the axis-0 workspace is 2048 B.
The unit circle uses Bluestein's method, with a one-sequence workspace of 1664 B and an axis-0 workspace of 3072 B.
In every case, the difference between function `czt` and plan execution, and between axis execution and per-sequence execution, is exactly 0.
This is because function `czt` creates the same plan from the same $W$ and $A$, and axis execution transforms each sequence using the same procedure.

Setting `workspace_bytes` to exactly the required byte count allows preparation and allocation to succeed; one byte less gives `ResourceLimit`.
Preparation compares the one-sequence workspace against the limit, and `workspace` compares each requested size against it.
Thus, a limit exactly equal to the one-sequence size allows preparation but gives `ResourceLimit` when allocating the axis-0 workspace.
For the $N=256$ spiral, $NM=6144>4096$ and $\chi=\lvert\rho\rvert\max(N,M)^2\approx659>0.5$ keep the algorithm at direct summation.
At this size coefficients are not stored, so the one-sequence workspace is 8576 B for $M+2N$ complex values rather than $M+N$.

## zoom FFT

`CztContour::zoom(start, end, m, fs, endpoint)` and `zoom_fft(input, start, end, m, fs, endpoint, normalization)` evaluate at the unit-circle frequencies

$$
f_k = \mathrm{start} + k\Delta, \qquad z_k = e^{2\pi i f_k/f_s}, \qquad k=0,\ldots,m-1
$$

shown above.
When `endpoint` is true, $\Delta=(\mathrm{end}-\mathrm{start})/(m-1)$ includes the endpoint; when false, $\Delta=(\mathrm{end}-\mathrm{start})/m$ excludes it.
$m=1$ evaluates only start.
Negative frequencies and descending bands with start > end are also supported.
If the bandwidth divided by $f_s$ is exactly one turn (end − start = $f_s$), the phase is reduced with integer arithmetic as in `dft`.
$m=0$ gives `InvalidInput` (`Empty`), $f_s\le0$ gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), and non-finite start, end, or $f_s$ gives `NonFinite`.
If start$/f_s$ or the increment $\Delta/f_s$ exceeds the range of `f64`, the result is `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).

Since $z_k^{-j}=e^{-2\pi i jf_k/f_s}$, zoom FFT evaluates the finite sequence's discrete-time Fourier transform (DTFT) $X(f)=\sum_jx_je^{-2\pi ijf/f_s}$ at $f_k$.
These values are the same as selecting part of a long zero-padded FFT.
Finer evaluation points do not change the frequency resolution, which remains approximately $1/T$ as determined by the observation time $T=N/f_s$.
The example [Fine narrowband spectra with zoom FFT](../../examples/zoom-fft-tone-resolution/) compares observation time with evaluation-point count.

## Damped-oscillation poles and contour radius

Consider the damped oscillation $x_j=\rho_s^j\cos(\omega_0 j)$, $\rho_s=e^{-\sigma\Delta t}$, $\omega_0=2\pi f_0\Delta t$ with sampling interval $\Delta t=1/f_s$.
$\sigma$ [1/s] is the decay rate and $f_0$ [Hz] the oscillation frequency.
For an infinite sequence, the $z$-transform has poles at $z=\rho_s e^{\pm i\omega_0}$.
On the arc $z=re^{i\omega}$ of radius $r$, $\lvert X\rvert$ has a Lorentzian peak near $\omega=\omega_0$, with power full width at half maximum approximately $\lvert\ln(r/\rho_s)\rvert f_s/\pi$ in frequency.
The peak is sharpest at $r=\rho_s$, where the contour passes through the poles; the peak width reveals the decay rate.

For a finite sequence ending after $N$ samples, the $z$-transform is a polynomial and has no poles.
Setting $q_\pm=\rho_se^{\pm i\omega_0}/z$, the geometric-series sum gives

$$
X(z) = \frac12\left(\frac{1-q_+^N}{1-q_+}+\frac{1-q_-^N}{1-q_-}\right)
$$

and even at $q_+=1$ the value is finite, namely $N/2$.
Thus the peak width at $r=\rho_s$ remains approximately $0.886/T$, determined by the rectangular window of observation time $T=N\Delta t$.
At $r<\rho_s$ the weights $r^{-j}$ amplify the later samples, broadening the peak again.

The following cell evaluates a damped oscillation with $f_s=64$ Hz, $N=256$ ($T=4$ s), and $f_0=10$ Hz on an arc of radius $r=e^{-\sigma_c\Delta t}$ over 8–12 Hz (201 points at 0.02 Hz spacing).
Construct the contour by passing $\rho_0=-\sigma_c\Delta t$, $\rho=0$, $t_0=8/64$, and $t=0.02/64$ to `CztContour::new`.
Inputs are the signal decay rate $\sigma$ and contour decay rate $\sigma_c$ [1/s]; when $\sigma_c=\sigma$ the contour passes through the pole radius.
As an independent check, compute the geometric-series closed form above with the intrinsic methods of `f64`.
Report the maximum difference divided by the radially weighted input magnitude $B_k=\sum_j\lvert x_j\rvert r^{-j}$.
The plot shows amplitude divided by peak height; the table reports the distance between intersections with the half-power line as the full width at half maximum.

```rust
//| id: fft-czt-damped-mode
//| ref: fft-czt-damped-mode
//| title: "Measure damped-oscillation peak widths by changing the contour radius"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   damping: {"type":"range","label":"Signal decay rate σ [1/s]","description":"Decay rate of x_j = exp(−σ j Δt) cos(2π f0 j Δt).","min":0.5,"max":4,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//|   contour_damping: {"type":"range","label":"Contour decay rate σ_c [1/s]","description":"Contour radius r = exp(−σ_c Δt). At σ_c = σ it passes through the pole radius.","min":-1,"max":6,"step":0.25,"value":0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"through-pole","label":"σ_c = σ = 2","description":"The contour passes through the pole radius, narrowing the peak to the observation-window width.","inputs":{"contour_damping":2}}
//|   - {"id":"inside-pole","label":"σ_c = 4","description":"At r < ρ_s, later samples are amplified and the peak broadens again.","inputs":{"contour_damping":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peak","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":10,"atol":0.011}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peak","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.638,"atol":0.005}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peak","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: through-pole
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peak","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":128,"rtol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peak","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.221,"atol":0.003}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peak","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: inside-pole
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peak","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.638,"atol":0.005}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peak","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_core::{Complex, ExecutionContext};
use scioxide_fft::{CztContour, CztPlan, CztPlanSpec, Normalization};

let (fs, n, f0) = (64.0_f64, 256_usize, 10.0_f64);
let (f_start, f_step, m) = (8.0_f64, 0.02_f64, 201_usize);
let tau = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI;
// Damped oscillation x_j (f64 exp and cos are used only to generate input)
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| (-damping * j as f64 / fs).exp() * (tau * f0 * j as f64 / fs).cos())
    .collect();

// Arc of radius r = exp(−σ_c Δt): ρ0 = −σ_c Δt, ρ = 0, with phase specified in turns
let log_radius = -contour_damping / fs;
let contour =
    CztContour::new(log_radius, 0.0, f_start / fs, f_step / fs).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let spec = CztPlanSpec::new(n, m, contour, Normalization::Unscaled);
let plan = CztPlan::new(spec).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = plan.workspace(()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut output = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); m];
plan.execute_into(
    &x[..],
    &mut output[..],
    &mut workspace,
    ExecutionContext::sequential(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check: compute e^u − 1 without cancellation and form the geometric-series closed form (e^{Nu} − 1)/(e^u − 1)
let expm1 = |re: f64, im: f64| {
    let half = (0.5 * im).sin();
    Complex::new(
        re.exp_m1() * im.cos() - 2.0 * half * half,
        re.exp() * im.sin(),
    )
};
let geometric = |re: f64, im: f64| {
    if re == 0.0 && im == 0.0 {
        Complex::new(n as f64, 0.0)
    } else {
        expm1(n as f64 * re, n as f64 * im) / expm1(re, im)
    }
};
// On an arc, the weighted input magnitude B_k = Σ |x_j| r^{−j} is independent of k
let mass: f64 = x
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, v)| v.abs() * (-(j as f64) * log_radius).exp())
    .sum();
// u = ln(ρ_s / r) + i(±ω0 − ω_k)
let decay = (contour_damping - damping) / fs;
let omega0 = tau * f0 / fs;
let mut relative_error = 0.0_f64;
let mut magnitudes = Vec::with_capacity(m);
for (k, value) in output.iter().enumerate() {
    let omega = tau * (f_start + k as f64 * f_step) / fs;
    let exact = (geometric(decay, omega0 - omega) + geometric(decay, -omega0 - omega)) * 0.5;
    relative_error = relative_error.max((*value - exact).norm() / mass);
    magnitudes.push(value.norm());
}

// Peak location and full width at half maximum, with half-power locations linearly interpolated
let peak_index = (0..m)
    .max_by(|&i, &j| magnitudes[i].total_cmp(&magnitudes[j]))
    .unwrap_or(0);
let peak = magnitudes[peak_index];
let level = peak / 2.0_f64.sqrt();
let frequency = |k: usize| f_start + k as f64 * f_step;
let crossing = |inner: usize, outer: usize| {
    let t = (magnitudes[inner] - level) / (magnitudes[inner] - magnitudes[outer]);
    frequency(inner) + t * (frequency(outer) - frequency(inner))
};
let left = (1..=peak_index)
    .rev()
    .find(|&k| magnitudes[k - 1] < level)
    .map(|k| crossing(k, k - 1));
let right = (peak_index..m - 1)
    .find(|&k| magnitudes[k + 1] < level)
    .map(|k| crossing(k, k + 1));
let width = left.zip(right).map(|(a, b)| b - a);

let curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = magnitudes
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, v)| [frequency(k), v / peak])
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "|X(r e^{iω})| / peak height", "points": curve })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Frequency f [Hz]",
        "yLabel": "Amplitude / peak height [-]",
        "xMin": 8.0,
        "xMax": 12.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.05,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.7071067811865476, "label": "Half-power level" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": f0, "label": "f0 = 10 Hz" }
        ]
    })
);
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Contour radius r [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Pole radius ρ_s [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Peak location [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Peak height [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Full width at half maximum [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "Outside the band" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "|σ − σ_c|/π [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Difference from closed form / B_k [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Algorithm" }
]);
let row = (
    log_radius.exp(),
    (-damping / fs).exp(),
    frequency(peak_index),
    peak,
    width,
    (damping - contour_damping).abs() / std::f64::consts::PI,
    relative_error,
    format!("{:?}", plan.algorithm()),
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "peak" })
);
```

The defaults $\sigma=2$, $\sigma_c=0$ evaluate on the unit circle, giving the DTFT.
The peak's full width at half maximum is 0.638 Hz, close to the infinite sequence's Lorentzian width of $\sigma/\pi\approx0.637$ Hz.
Setting $\sigma_c=\sigma=2$ makes the contour pass through the pole radius $\rho_s=e^{-2/64}\approx0.9692$.
The peak height becomes 128.0, equal to $N/2$, and its width narrows to 0.221 Hz.
This is the $0.886/T\approx0.22$ Hz width of a rectangular window lasting $T=4$ s; the finite-sequence peak cannot become narrower.
At $\sigma_c=4$, $r<\rho_s$ and the width broadens again to 0.638 Hz.
Searching for the $\sigma_c$ that minimizes width estimates the decay rate, but differences finer than the window width cannot be resolved.

Every case has $NM=51456>4096$ and $\rho=0$, selecting Bluestein's method.
The difference from the closed form is $4.6\times10^{-15}$ times $B_k$ at the default, and $1.7\times10^{-14}$ and $3.1\times10^{-14}$ times it at $\sigma_c=2$ and $4$.
At $\sigma_c=6$, $r^{-j}$ produces weights up to $e^{6\cdot255/64}\approx2.4\times10^{10}$, increasing the peak height to $6.7\times10^7$, yet the difference divided by $B_k$ remains $3.5\times10^{-14}$.
This is because the starting radius is handled as an input weight with a separate binary exponent and does not enter the spiral amplification $e^{2\chi}$ in the [error bound](oxref:fft-czt-error).

## Summary

The CZT evaluates a finite sequence's $z$-transform on $z_k=AW^{-k}$, a contour with arithmetic progressions of logarithmic radius and turns; the factor $s$ depends only on input length $N$.
Construct contours with `CztContour` methods `new` (logarithms and turns), `from_wa` ($W$, $A$), `dft`, and `zoom`; plans handle even unrepresentable radii in logarithmic form.
Reuse `CztPlan` and its workspace for many sequences on one contour; use functions `czt` and `zoom_fft` for single evaluations.
For transforms along a multidimensional array axis, allocate a workspace holding all sequence outputs with `AxisWorkspaceRequest::Axis`; preparation or allocation exceeding the byte limits in `CztLimits` gives `ResourceLimit`.
Zoom FFT evaluates the DTFT at equally spaced unit-circle frequencies, with `endpoint` selecting endpoint inclusion, but observation time determines the resolution.
Matching the contour radius to the pole radius narrows a damped-oscillation peak to the window width; the location of this match reveals the decay rate.
