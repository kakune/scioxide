The discrete Fourier transform (DFT) represents a sequence of length $N$ as a superposition of $N$ complex sinusoids.
This page examines the DFT definition and sign convention in `scioxide-fft`, deriving the direction in which `Normalization` places the factor $1/N$ and the corresponding Parseval identity.
It then gives the factors converting bin values to amplitudes of the original sinusoids.
[Sampling, aliasing, and the frequency axis](../sampling-and-frequency-axis/) covers sampling continuous signals, aliasing, and frequency axes.

## DFT definition and inverse transform

For a complex sequence $x_0,\dots,x_{N-1}$ of length $N$, the forward and inverse transforms in `scioxide-fft` use the following sums before normalization.

$$
X_k = \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} x_j\, e^{-2\pi i jk/N}, \qquad
x_j = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} X_k\, e^{+2\pi i jk/N}
\qquad (j, k = 0,\dots,N-1)
$$

The forward transform (`FftDirection::Forward`) has exponent sign $-$; the inverse transform (`FftDirection::Inverse`) has sign $+$.
Setting $\omega = e^{-2\pi i/N}$ and forming the matrix $F_{kj} = \omega^{jk}$ gives the forward transform $X = Fx$ and unscaled inverse transform $x \mapsto F^{\mathsf H}x$.
Column inner products are $\sum_k \omega^{-jk}\omega^{j'k} = N\delta_{jj'}$, so $F^{\mathsf H}F = NI$ holds.
Therefore, applying an unscaled forward transform followed by an inverse transform multiplies the sequence by $N$.
Normalization in the next section determines where to divide by this $N$.

$X_k$ is periodic in $k$ with period $N$; bin $k \ge N/2$ represents negative frequency $(k-N)/N$ [cycles/sample].
A real sequence's spectrum satisfies $X_{N-k} = \overline{X_k}$ (Hermitian symmetry), so `rfft` returns only bins $0,\dots,\lfloor N/2\rfloor$.
[Frequency axis and bin order](oxref:fft-frequency-axis) covers the mapping from bins to physical frequencies, and [Real FFTs and Hermitian symmetry](../real-and-hermitian-transforms/) covers packed spectra.

## Normalization and Parseval's identity

`Normalization` selects whether the round-trip factor $N$ is divided out on the forward or inverse side.
Letting $s$ be the forward scaling, the normalized forward transform is $X = sFx$; from $F^{\mathsf H}F = NI$,

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \lvert X_k\rvert^2 = s^2\, x^{\mathsf H}F^{\mathsf H}F\,x = s^2 N \sum_{j=0}^{N-1} \lvert x_j\rvert^2
$$

the identity above follows (Parseval's identity).
The scalings and energy ratios of the 4 variants are as follows.

| `Normalization` | Forward scaling $s$ | Inverse scaling | Round-trip scaling | $\sum_k\lvert X_k\rvert^2/\sum_j\lvert x_j\rvert^2$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Inverse` | $1$ | $1/N$ | $1$ | $N$ |
| `Forward` | $1/N$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1/N$ |
| `Orthonormal` | $1/\sqrt N$ | $1/\sqrt N$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| `Unscaled` | $1$ | $1$ | $N$ | $N$ |

To restore the input on a round trip, pass the same value in both directions (except `Unscaled`).
Under `Orthonormal`, $F/\sqrt N$ is unitary, so the transform preserves the 2-norm.
For multidimensional transforms, $N$ is the product of the selected axis lengths.
The scaling is unchanged for packed real layouts such as `rfft`.
When summing packed bins, however, count every bin twice except DC and even-length Nyquist.
[Normalization](oxref:fft-normalization) collects the counting rules for $N$ in DCT, DST, chirp Z-transforms, and nonuniform Fourier transforms.

The following cell transforms a deterministic complex sequence of length $N$ under all 4 normalizations, measuring the table's scalings and Parseval ratios.
Forward scaling is $X_0/\sum_j x_j$, energy ratio is $\sum_k\lvert X_k\rvert^2/\sum_j\lvert x_j\rvert^2$, and round-trip scaling is $\lVert\mathrm{ifft}(\mathrm{fft}(x))\rVert_2/\lVert x\rVert_2$.
Columns marked “(table)” or “(formula)” copy values from the table above and place them beside the measurements for comparison.
Input generation uses f64 `cos` and `sin`.

```rust
//| id: fft-normalization-parseval
//| ref: fft-normalization-parseval
//| title: "Measure the scalings and Parseval ratios of all 4 normalizations"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   length: {"type":"range","label":"Transform length N","description":"The difference between scalings 1/N and 1/√N grows with N.","min":1,"max":256,"step":1,"value":8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"single-sample","label":"N = 1","description":"Every normalization has scaling and energy ratio 1.","inputs":{"length":1}}
//|   - {"id":"prime-length","label":"N = 97","description":"Prime lengths give the same scalings, with round-trip error remaining on the scale of rounding.","inputs":{"length":97}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.125,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":1,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/3/3","approx":{"value":8,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":8,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/2/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: single-sample
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":1,"rtol":1e-15}}
//|   - case: prime-length
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.010309278350515464,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":97,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_fft::{Normalization, fft, ifft};

let n = length.round() as usize;
// Deterministic input x_j = 1 + cos(0.7 j) + i sin(1.3 j²/N) (generated with f64 functions)
let x: Vec<Complex<f64>> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| {
        let t = j as f64;
        Complex::new(1.0 + (0.7 * t).cos(), (1.3 * t * t / n as f64).sin())
    })
    .collect();
let sum = x.iter().fold(Complex::new(0.0, 0.0), |acc, v| acc + *v);
let energy: f64 = x.iter().map(|v| v.norm_sqr()).sum();
let nf = n as f64;
let variants = [
    ("Inverse", Normalization::Inverse, 1.0, nf, 1.0),
    ("Forward", Normalization::Forward, 1.0 / nf, 1.0 / nf, 1.0),
    (
        "Orthonormal",
        Normalization::Orthonormal,
        1.0 / nf.sqrt(),
        1.0,
        1.0,
    ),
    ("Unscaled", Normalization::Unscaled, 1.0, nf, nf),
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, norm, forward_scale, energy_ratio, round_trip) in variants {
    let spectrum = fft(&x, norm).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let back = ifft(&spectrum, norm).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let spectral_energy: f64 = spectrum.iter().map(|v| v.norm_sqr()).sum();
    // Round-trip error is the relative L2 difference between the sequence divided by the tabulated scaling and the original sequence
    let error: f64 = back
        .iter()
        .zip(&x)
        .map(|(b, a)| (*b / round_trip - *a).norm_sqr())
        .sum();
    let back_energy: f64 = back.iter().map(|v| v.norm_sqr()).sum();
    rows.push((
        name,
        (spectrum[0] / sum).re,
        forward_scale,
        spectral_energy / energy,
        energy_ratio,
        (back_energy / energy).sqrt(),
        (error / energy).sqrt(),
    ));
}
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Normalization" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "X₀/Σx [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Forward scaling (table) [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Σ|X|²/Σ|x|² [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Parseval ratio (formula) [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Round-trip scaling [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Relative round-trip error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "normalization" })
);
```

At the default $N = 8$, $X_0/\sum x$ for `Forward` is $0.125$ and `Orthonormal` is $1/\sqrt 8 \approx 0.354$; only `Orthonormal` has energy ratio 1.
The round-trip scaling for `Unscaled` is $N = 8$; the other 3 give 1.
Relative round-trip error is of order $2\times10^{-16}$ ($6$–$8\times10^{-16}$ at $N = 97$), showing agreement within rounding error after dividing out the scaling.
Prime lengths such as $N = 97$ do not change the scaling ([FFT algorithms and arbitrary lengths](../planner-and-arbitrary-lengths/) covers prime-length algorithms).

## Conversion to physical amplitude

A real sinusoid of amplitude $A$ centered on a bin other than DC or Nyquist splits equally between positive and negative frequencies, each with magnitude $AN/2$ (a forward transform with `Unscaled` or `Inverse`).
To recover one-sided amplitudes, multiply bin magnitudes by $2/N$ except at DC and even-length Nyquist, where the factor is $1/N$.

$$
A_k = \begin{cases}
\lvert X_0\rvert/N & (k = 0) \\
2\lvert X_k\rvert/N & (0 < k < N/2) \\
\lvert X_{N/2}\rvert/N & (k = N/2,\ N \text{ is even})
\end{cases}
$$

With `Forward`, division by $N$ has already been applied, so the factors are $2$ and $1$.
With `Orthonormal`, they are $2/\sqrt N$ and $1/\sqrt N$.
This conversion gives the correct amplitude only for bin-centered sinusoids; frequencies between bins appear smaller because of leakage.
[Reading the frequencies and amplitudes of two sinusoids with rfft](oxref:fft-first-spectrum) and [Observing aliasing and leakage](oxref:fft-aliasing-experiment) provide amplitude-reading experiments.

## Summary

The forward transform in `scioxide-fft` has exponent sign $-$ and the inverse sign $+$; an unscaled round trip multiplies the sequence by $N$.
`Normalization` selects division by this $N$ in the inverse transform (`Inverse`) or forward transform (`Forward`), division by $\sqrt N$ in each direction (`Orthonormal`), or no division (`Unscaled`).
Forward scaling $s$ determines the Parseval ratio $s^2N$; only `Orthonormal` preserves the sum of squares.
Under `Inverse` and `Unscaled`, read one-sided amplitudes as $2/N$ times the bin magnitude ($1/N$ at DC and Nyquist), and record the `Normalization` used alongside the result.
