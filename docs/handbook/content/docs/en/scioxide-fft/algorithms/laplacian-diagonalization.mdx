The table in [DCT, DST, and boundary conditions](oxref:fft-dct-boundary-symmetry) classifies the 8 kernels as sequences extended evenly or oddly at both endpoints.
This page derives how these extensions are exactly the second-difference boundary conditions and make each kernel an eigenvector of the corresponding difference matrix.
Once eigenvalues are known, the discrete Poisson equation can be solved in $O(n\log n)$ by transforming, dividing by diagonal entries, and inverse-transforming.
The first cell numerically checks the closures for all 8 types; the second includes grid spacing $h$ and compares their eigenvalues with those of the continuous Laplace operator.

## Second differences closed by boundary conditions

For a sequence $u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}$ of length $n$, applying the second difference

$$
(\mathbf D\mathbf u)_j=u_{j-1}-2u_j+u_{j+1},\qquad j=0,\ldots,n-1
$$

requires specifying the outside values $u_{-1}$ and $u_n$.
Choosing the symmetry center and parity determines outside values from inside values as follows.

| Left symmetry | $u_{-1}$ | Right symmetry | $u_n$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Even at grid point 0 | $u_1$ | Even at grid point $n-1$ | $u_{n-2}$ |
| Even at half-grid point $-\tfrac12$ | $u_0$ | Even at half-grid point $n-\tfrac12$ | $u_{n-1}$ |
| Odd at grid point $-1$ | $0$ | Odd at grid point $n$ | $0$ |
| Odd at half-grid point $-\tfrac12$ | $-u_0$ | Odd at half-grid point $n-\tfrac12$ | $-u_{n-1}$ |

An odd extension about a grid point makes its central value 0, giving a grid-point Dirichlet condition.
An even extension about a half-grid point gives $u_{-1}=u_0$, making the difference $u_0-u_{-1}$ across the cell boundary 0.
This is a zero-flux Neumann condition on a cell-centered grid.

For a sequence of the form $c_j=\cos(\omega j+\varphi)$, the addition formula $\cos(a-b)+\cos(a+b)=2\cos a\cos b$ gives

$$
(\mathbf D\mathbf c)_j=(2\cos\omega-2)\,c_j=-4\sin^2\frac{\omega}{2}\,c_j
$$

at interior indices $j$.
It also holds at the endpoints when the outside-value rules agree with the extended values $c_{-1}$, $c_n$ of $c_j$.
Each kernel has the form $\cos(\omega_k j+\varphi)$ in $j$, with extension symmetry as in the [table](oxref:fft-dct-boundary-symmetry); it is therefore an eigenvector of $\mathbf D$ closed by those boundary conditions (G. Strang, "The discrete cosine transform", *SIAM Review* 41(1), 1999, 135–147).
The eigenvalue is $\lambda_k=-4\sin^2(\omega_k/2)$, and the kernel's angular frequency $\omega_k$ in $j$ takes the following values by type.

| Type | $\omega_k$ | Type | $\omega_k$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| DCT-I | $\pi k/(n-1)$ | DST-I | $\pi(k+1)/(n+1)$ |
| DCT-II | $\pi k/n$ | DST-II | $\pi(k+1)/n$ |
| DCT-III | $\pi(k+\tfrac12)/n$ | DST-III | $\pi(k+\tfrac12)/n$ |
| DCT-IV | $\pi(k+\tfrac12)/n$ | DST-IV | $\pi(k+\tfrac12)/n$ |

At grid-point even closures (both ends for DCT-I, the left for DCT-III, the right for DST-III), a coefficient 2 appears in the endpoint row of $\mathbf D$ ($(-2,\,2,\,0,\ldots)$ on the left), so $\mathbf D$ is not symmetric.
These eigenvectors are not orthogonal in the usual inner product, but are orthogonal with endpoint weights $\tfrac12$.
This explains the endpoint weights $\sqrt2$ used by `Orthonormal`.

### Checking the 8 closures

For each type, the following cell constructs kernel sequences $c_k[j]$ ($j=0,\ldots,n-1$) with `scioxide-fft`, applies $\mathbf D$ closed by the table's outside values, and measures whether they are eigenvectors.
To extract the kernel of type $T$ as a function of $j$, apply the transpose of the $T$ matrix to unit vector $\mathbf e_k$.
Apart from endpoint weights, DCT and DST transposes are their dual types (I and IV are self-dual, II and III dual to each other); an inverse plan takes forward type $T$ and computes its dual.
Thus define $c_k$ as the output of passing $\mathbf e_k$ to an inverse `Unscaled` plan of type $T$ (endpoint weights only give a constant multiple of $c_k$).
The second difference $\mathbf D c_k$ is an independent check computed directly from the outside-value table; the output table reports the maximum difference between Rayleigh quotient $\langle\mathbf Dc_k,c_k\rangle/\langle c_k,c_k\rangle$ and eigenvalue formula $\lambda_k$ and the maximum residual $\max_j\lvert(\mathbf Dc_k)_j-\lambda_kc_{k,j}\rvert/\max_j\lvert c_{k,j}\rvert$ over $k$.
Switching outside values to “0 at both ends” closes every type with grid-point Dirichlet conditions, showing that the kernels cease to be eigenvectors unless their tabulated closures are used.

```rust
//| id: fft-dct-dst-difference-eigenvectors
//| ref: fft-dct-dst-difference-eigenvectors
//| title: "Measure whether the 8 kernels are eigenvectors of boundary-closed second differences"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   length: {"type":"range","label":"Length n","description":"Length of the sequence acted on by the difference. At least 2 to accommodate DCT-I.","min":2,"max":24,"step":1,"value":8}
//|   closure: {"type":"select","label":"Outside values","description":"Rules for u₋₁ and uₙ that close the difference.","options":["Tabulated closure","0 at both ends"],"value":"Tabulated closure"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"odd","label":"n = 7","description":"For odd lengths too, all 8 residuals remain on the scale of rounding error.","inputs":{"length":7}}
//|   - {"id":"zero-ghost","label":"0 at both ends","description":"Only DST-I remains an eigenvector; residuals for other types become O(1).","inputs":{"closure":"0 at both ends"}}
//|   - {"id":"shortest","label":"n = 2","description":"The closures also hold at the minimum length accepted by DCT-I.","inputs":{"length":2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/6/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/7/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: odd
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/6/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: zero-ghost
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/4/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/6/4","range":{"min":0.5}}
//|   - case: shortest
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"closures","path":"/rows/6/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_fft::RealTransformKind::{self, Dct, Dst};
use scioxide_fft::{DctType, DstType, FftDirection, Normalization};
use scioxide_fft::{RealTransformPlan, RealTransformPlanSpec};

// Outside-value rules. Node is even about a grid point, Half even about a half-grid point,
// HalfOdd odd about a half-grid point, and Zero odd about a grid point.
#[derive(Clone, Copy)]
enum Ghost {
    Node,
    Half,
    HalfOdd,
    Zero,
}
let n = length.round() as usize;
let nf = n as f64;
let use_table = closure == "Tabulated closure";
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let kinds: [RealTransformKind; 8] = [
    Dct(DctType::I),
    Dct(DctType::II),
    Dct(DctType::III),
    Dct(DctType::IV),
    Dst(DstType::I),
    Dst(DstType::II),
    Dst(DstType::III),
    Dst(DstType::IV),
];
// (Name, left end, right end, a, b) by type. Kernel angular frequency is ω_k = π(k + a)/(n + b)
let model = |kind: RealTransformKind| match kind {
    Dct(DctType::I) => ("DCT-I", Ghost::Node, Ghost::Node, 0.0, -1.0),
    Dct(DctType::II) => ("DCT-II", Ghost::Half, Ghost::Half, 0.0, 0.0),
    Dct(DctType::III) => ("DCT-III", Ghost::Node, Ghost::Zero, 0.5, 0.0),
    Dct(DctType::IV) => ("DCT-IV", Ghost::Half, Ghost::HalfOdd, 0.5, 0.0),
    Dst(DstType::I) => ("DST-I", Ghost::Zero, Ghost::Zero, 1.0, 1.0),
    Dst(DstType::II) => ("DST-II", Ghost::HalfOdd, Ghost::HalfOdd, 1.0, 0.0),
    Dst(DstType::III) => ("DST-III", Ghost::Zero, Ghost::Node, 0.5, 0.0),
    Dst(DstType::IV) => ("DST-IV", Ghost::HalfOdd, Ghost::Half, 0.5, 0.0),
};
// Outside values u₋₁ (left) and uₙ (right). For Node, the inside point is one point inward from the endpoint.
let outside = |ghost: Ghost, u: &[f64], left: bool| {
    let (edge, inner) = if left {
        (u[0], u[1])
    } else {
        (u[n - 1], u[n - 2])
    };
    match ghost {
        Ghost::Node => inner,
        Ghost::Half => edge,
        Ghost::HalfOdd => -edge,
        Ghost::Zero => 0.0,
    }
};
let label = |ghost: Ghost, left: bool| match (ghost, left) {
    (Ghost::Node, true) => "u₋₁ = u₁",
    (Ghost::Half, true) => "u₋₁ = u₀",
    (Ghost::HalfOdd, true) => "u₋₁ = −u₀",
    (Ghost::Zero, true) => "u₋₁ = 0",
    (Ghost::Node, false) => "uₙ = uₙ₋₂",
    (Ghost::Half, false) => "uₙ = uₙ₋₁",
    (Ghost::HalfOdd, false) => "uₙ = −uₙ₋₁",
    (Ghost::Zero, false) => "uₙ = 0",
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for kind in kinds {
    let (name, table_left, table_right, a, b) = model(kind);
    let (left, right) = if use_table {
        (table_left, table_right)
    } else {
        (Ghost::Zero, Ghost::Zero)
    };
    // An inverse plan computes the dual (transpose), so passing e_k gives the kernel sequence c_k[j]
    let spec =
        RealTransformPlanSpec::new(n, kind, FftDirection::Inverse, Normalization::Unscaled);
    let plan = RealTransformPlan::<f64>::new(spec).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut workspace = plan.workspace(()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (mut eigen_gap, mut residual) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for k in 0..n {
        let mut unit = vec![0.0; n];
        unit[k] = 1.0;
        let mut c = vec![0.0; n];
        plan.execute_into(
            &unit,
            &mut c,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        // Independent check: outside-value-closed second difference D c and eigenvalue formula −4 sin²(ω_k/2)
        let ghost_left = outside(left, &c, true);
        let ghost_right = outside(right, &c, false);
        let dc: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
            .map(|j| {
                let prev = if j == 0 { ghost_left } else { c[j - 1] };
                let next = if j + 1 == n { ghost_right } else { c[j + 1] };
                prev - 2.0 * c[j] + next
            })
            .collect();
        let omega = pi * (k as f64 + a) / (nf + b);
        let lambda = -4.0 * (omega / 2.0).sin().powi(2);
        let dot = |p: &[f64], q: &[f64]| p.iter().zip(q).map(|(x, y)| x * y).sum::<f64>();
        let rayleigh = dot(&dc, &c) / dot(&c, &c);
        let scale = c.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, v| m.max(v.abs()));
        let worst = dc
            .iter()
            .zip(&c)
            .fold(0.0_f64, |m, (d, v)| m.max((d - lambda * v).abs()));
        eigen_gap = eigen_gap.max((rayleigh - lambda).abs());
        residual = residual.max(worst / scale);
    }
    rows.push((
        name,
        label(left, true),
        label(right, false),
        eigen_gap,
        residual,
    ));
}

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Type" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Left outside value" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Right outside value" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum Rayleigh-quotient difference from λₖ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum residual ‖Dc − λc‖∞/‖c‖∞ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "closures" })
);
```

At the default $n=8$, every type has residual at most $1.4\times10^{-15}$ and Rayleigh-quotient difference from the formula's $\lambda_k$ at most $1.4\times10^{-15}$.
With the tabulated outside-value closures, every kernel is a second-difference eigenvector within rounding error, confirming the boundary-condition table and eigenvalue formulas for all 8 types.
Residuals also remain at most $9\times10^{-16}$ at $n=7$ and $n=2$.
Setting both outside values to 0 leaves the DST-I residual at $9.0\times10^{-16}$ but raises the other 7 residuals to $0.98$–$1.0$; their kernels are no longer eigenvectors.

## Grid spacing and eigenvalues

Divide $[0,1]$ into $N$ equal intervals and set $h=1/N$.
With Dirichlet conditions $u(0)=u(1)=0$, the unknowns are the $N-1$ interior grid values at $x_j=jh$ ($j=1,\ldots,N-1$), and the centered second difference gives

$$
(\mathbf L\mathbf u)_j=\frac{u_{j-1}-2u_j+u_{j+1}}{h^2},\qquad u_0=u_N=0
$$

the $(N-1)\times(N-1)$ symmetric tridiagonal matrix above.
This is length-$n=N-1$ $\mathbf D$ divided by $h^2$, with grid-point Dirichlet outside values (the DST-I closure).
From the DST-I row above, $\mathbf v_k=[\sin(jk\pi/N)]_{j=1}^{N-1}$ satisfies

$$
\mathbf L\mathbf v_k=\lambda_k\mathbf v_k,\qquad
\lambda_k=-\frac{4}{h^2}\sin^2\frac{k\pi}{2N},\qquad k=1,\ldots,N-1
$$

the relation above.

Discretizing Neumann conditions $u'(0)=u'(1)=0$ on the cell-centered grid $x_j=(j+\tfrac12)h$ ($j=0,\ldots,N-1$) gives reflected outside values $u_{-1}=u_0$, $u_N=u_{N-1}$.
This is the DCT-II closure, with eigenvectors $\cos(\pi(j+\tfrac12)k/N)$ and eigenvalues $\lambda_k$ given by the same formula ($k=0,\ldots,N-1$).
The constant vector at $k=0$ has eigenvalue 0, reflecting that a Neumann solution is determined only up to an additive constant.

The ratio to the continuous eigenvalue $-(k\pi)^2$ (Dirichlet eigenfunction $\sin k\pi x$, Neumann eigenfunction $\cos k\pi x$) is

$$
\frac{\lambda_k}{-(k\pi)^2}=\left(\frac{\sin(k\pi h/2)}{k\pi h/2}\right)^2=1-\frac{(k\pi h)^2}{12}+O(h^4)
$$

so modes with smaller $kh$ are closer to the continuous value, and their relative difference decreases in proportion to $h^2$.
For modes with $k$ near $N$, $\lvert\lambda_k\rvert$ saturates at $4/h^2$ and deviates substantially from the continuous value.

### Measuring eigenvalues

For each mode $k$, the following cell independently constructs $\mathbf v_k$ and $\mathbf L\mathbf v_k$ from the difference formula, then transforms with DST-I for Dirichlet or DCT-II for Neumann (both `Orthonormal`).
The ratio of the coefficient of $\mathbf L\mathbf v_k$ corresponding to mode $k$ to the same coefficient of $\mathbf v_k$ is the measured eigenvalue.
The largest other coefficient divided by coefficient $k$ indicates whether the transform diagonalizes the matrix.
The basis vectors are reference values constructed with `sin` and `cos` from `f64`.
The plot shows $\lvert\lambda\rvert$ on a logarithmic axis, omitting Neumann $k=0$ (eigenvalue 0).

```rust
//| id: fft-boundary-bases
//| ref: fft-boundary-bases
//| title: "Measure discrete-Laplacian eigenvalues with DST-I and DCT-II"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   grid_intervals: {"type":"range","label":"Interval subdivisions N","description":"The unknowns are N−1 interior points for Dirichlet and N cell centers for Neumann.","min":24,"max":128,"step":8,"value":32}
//|   mode: {"type":"range","label":"Tabulated mode k","description":"Mode index whose eigenvalues are compared in the table.","min":1,"max":20,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   boundary: {"type":"select","label":"Boundary condition","description":"Changes the transform type and grid.","options":["Dirichlet (grid points, DST-I)","Neumann (cell centers, DCT-II)"],"value":"Dirichlet (grid points, DST-I)"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"neumann","label":"Neumann","description":"DCT-II diagonalizes the matrix, giving the same eigenvalue as Dirichlet for the same k.","inputs":{"boundary":"Neumann (cell centers, DCT-II)"}}
//|   - {"id":"fine-grid","label":"N = 128","description":"h becomes 1/4 as large, reducing the relative difference from the continuous value to approximately 1/16.","inputs":{"grid_intervals":128}}
//|   - {"id":"high-mode","label":"N = 24, k = 20","description":"k is close to N, and the difference eigenvalue deviates substantially from the continuous value.","inputs":{"grid_intervals":24,"mode":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":0.0072,"rtol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: neumann
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":0.0072,"rtol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: fine-grid
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":4.5e-4,"rtol":0.02}}
//|   - case: high-mode
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":20}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigen","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0.4}}
use scioxide_fft::{DctType, DstType, Normalization, dct, dst};

let intervals = grid_intervals.round() as usize;
let selected = mode.round() as usize;
let dirichlet = boundary.starts_with("Dirichlet");
let h = 1.0 / intervals as f64;
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
// Dirichlet: N−1 interior grid points; mode k = 1..N−1 is coefficient k−1.
// Neumann: N cell centers; mode k = 0..N−1 is coefficient k.
let (size, first) = if dirichlet {
    (intervals - 1, 1)
} else {
    (intervals, 0)
};
let transform = |values: &[f64]| {
    if dirichlet {
        dst(values, DstType::I, Normalization::Orthonormal)
    } else {
        dct(values, DctType::II, Normalization::Orthonormal)
    }
};
let (mut measured, mut discrete, mut continuous) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut row = None;
for k in first..first + size {
    let kf = k as f64;
    // Basis vectors (reference values constructed with f64 sin and cos).
    let v: Vec<f64> = (0..size)
        .map(|j| {
            if dirichlet {
                ((j + 1) as f64 * kf * pi * h).sin()
            } else {
                ((j as f64 + 0.5) * kf * pi * h).cos()
            }
        })
        .collect();
    // Construct L v independently from the difference formula. Outside values are 0 for Dirichlet and reflected for Neumann.
    let outside = |inner: f64| if dirichlet { 0.0 } else { inner };
    let lv: Vec<f64> = (0..size)
        .map(|i| {
            let left = if i > 0 { v[i - 1] } else { outside(v[0]) };
            let right = if i + 1 < size {
                v[i + 1]
            } else {
                outside(v[size - 1])
            };
            (left - 2.0 * v[i] + right) / (h * h)
        })
        .collect();
    let coefficients_v = transform(&v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let coefficients_lv = transform(&lv).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let index = k - first;
    let eigenvalue = coefficients_lv[index] / coefficients_v[index];
    let formula = -4.0 / (h * h) * (kf * pi * h / 2.0).sin().powi(2);
    let exact = -(kf * pi).powi(2);
    if k > 0 {
        measured.push([kf, -eigenvalue]);
        discrete.push([kf, -formula]);
        continuous.push([kf, -exact]);
    }
    if k == selected {
        // Maximum coefficient other than k divided by coefficient k (indicator of diagonalization).
        let off = coefficients_lv
            .iter()
            .enumerate()
            .filter(|(i, _)| *i != index)
            .fold(0.0_f64, |m, (_, c)| m.max(c.abs()))
            / coefficients_lv[index].abs();
        row = Some((
            k,
            eigenvalue,
            formula,
            ((eigenvalue - formula) / formula).abs(),
            exact,
            ((formula - exact) / exact).abs(),
            off,
        ));
    }
}

    serde_json::json!({
        "name": "|λₖ| measured by the transform",
        "points": measured,
        "style": { "showSymbol": true, "lineType": "dotted" }
    }),
    serde_json::json!({
        "name": "Difference formula (4/h²) sin²(kπh/2)",
        "points": discrete,
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }),
    serde_json::json!({
        "name": "Continuous (kπ)²",
        "points": continuous,
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }),
];
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Mode index k [-]",
        "yLabel": "Eigenvalue magnitude |λ| [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": selected as f64, "label": "Tabulated mode" }
        ]
    })
);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let digits10 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "k", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Measured λₖ [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits10 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference-formula λₖ [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative difference from formula [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Continuous −(kπ)² [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits10 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative discrete–continuous difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Maximum other coefficient / coefficient k [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    row.into_iter().collect::<Vec<_>>(),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "eigen" })
);
```

At the defaults Dirichlet, $N=32$, $k=3$, measured eigenvalue $-88.18619242043626$ differs from difference formula $-88.18619242043624$ by relative $1.6\times10^{-16}$, and other coefficients remain at most $4.2\times10^{-15}$ times coefficient $k$.
The relative discrete–continuous difference is $0.0072$, agreeing with $(k\pi h)^2/12=0.00723$.
Switching to Neumann gives the same eigenvalue for the same $k$; what changes is the eigenvector and the range of $k$ (starting at 0).
At $N=128$ the relative discrete–continuous difference falls to $4.5\times10^{-4}$, consistent with $h^2$ convergence: reducing $h$ to 1/4 reduces error to 1/16.
At $N=24$, $k=20$, the relative difference reaches $0.46$, with discrete eigenvalues saturating toward $4/h^2$ as seen at the plot's right edge.

## Fast Poisson solvers

Since $\mathbf L$ is diagonalized by DST-I, the Dirichlet difference equation $\mathbf L\mathbf u=\mathbf f$ can be solved in 3 stages.

1. Compute $\hat f_k$ with `dst(f, DstType::I, Normalization::Orthonormal)`.
2. Set $\hat u_k=\hat f_k/\lambda_k$ for each $k$.
3. Recover $\mathbf u$ with `idst(…, DstType::I, Normalization::Orthonormal)`.

DST-I with `Orthonormal` is symmetric and orthogonal, so stage 3 uses the same transform as stage 1.
The cost is $O(N\log N)$ for 2 FFTs and $N-1$ divisions.
In one dimension, tridiagonal LU solves in $O(N)$, so this solver's advantage appears in two or more dimensions.
For the five-point difference on an $N\times N$ grid, applying DST-I to both axes with `dstn` gives a diagonal matrix with eigenvalues $\lambda_k+\lambda_l$, enabling a solve in $O(N^2\log N)$.
For Neumann conditions, use DCT-II; after checking that the right side has zero mean (the compatibility condition), choose an arbitrary constant for the $k=0$ component with eigenvalue 0.
[Solving the periodic Poisson equation with a spectral method](../../examples/spectral-poisson/) covers the same solver with periodic boundaries using `rfftn`.

## Summary

DCT and DST kernels are eigenvectors of the second difference closed by the table's outside values, with eigenvalues $-4\sin^2(\omega_k/2)$.
Choose the type from the boundary conditions: an incompatible outside-value closure destroys the eigenvector property.
Grid-point Dirichlet conditions correspond to DST-I and cell-centered Neumann conditions to DCT-II; low-mode discrete eigenvalues approach continuous $-(k\pi)^2$ at rate $h^2$.
Solve the discrete Poisson equation in $O(N\log N)$ by applying the diagonalizing transform, dividing by eigenvalues, and inverse-transforming.
