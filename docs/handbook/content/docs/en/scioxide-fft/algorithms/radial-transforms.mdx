The Fourier transform of a circularly or spherically symmetric function reduces to a one-dimensional radial integral.
This integral is called a Hankel transform.
`scioxide-fft` computes Hankel transforms by 2 methods.
This page explains the discrete Hankel transform (`DiscreteHankelPlan`, `dht`, `idht`), with nodes at Bessel-function zeros on $[0, R]$.
[FFTLog and logarithmic grids](../fftlog/) covers logarithmic-grid FFTLog for radii and wavenumbers spanning many decades.

## Fourier transforms of axisymmetric functions

Suppose a $d$-dimensional function $f(\mathbf{x})$ depends only on radius $r = \lvert\mathbf{x}\rvert$.
Its Fourier transform $\hat f(\mathbf{k}) = \int f(\mathbf{x})\, e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\, d\mathbf{x}$ also depends only on wavenumber magnitude $k = \lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert$; integrating over angles gives

$$
\hat f(k) = (2\pi)^{d/2}\, k^{1-d/2} \int_0^\infty f(r)\, r^{d/2-1}\, J_{d/2-1}(kr)\, r\,dr
$$

the expression above ($J_\nu$ is the Bessel function of the first kind).
Define the Hankel transform of order $\nu$ by

$$
F(k) = \int_0^\infty g(r)\, J_\nu(kr)\, r\,dr
$$

the expression above.
The $d$-dimensional Fourier transform is the Hankel transform with $\nu = d/2 - 1$ and $g(r) = r^{d/2-1} f(r)$ multiplied by factor $(2\pi)^{d/2} k^{1-d/2}$.
In two dimensions, $\nu = 0$ and $g = f$ give $\hat f(k) = 2\pi F(k)$.
In three dimensions, $\nu = 1/2$ and $g(r) = r^{1/2} f(r)$ give $\hat f(k) = (2\pi)^{3/2} k^{-1/2} F(k)$.
Circular apertures in optics, axisymmetric diffusion, and cosmological power spectra and correlation functions use transforms of this form.

The Hankel transform in `scioxide-fft` accepts $\nu > -1/2$.
In one dimension ($\nu = -1/2$), the Hankel transform becomes a cosine transform; use [DCT, DST, and boundary conditions](../dct-dst-and-boundaries/) for discrete computation.

## Fourier–Bessel series and zero grids

Let $j_1 < j_2 < \cdots$ be the positive zeros of $J_\nu$.
If $g$ is 0 at $r \ge R$, the Fourier–Bessel series of $[0, R]$ is

$$
g(r) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} c_m\, J_\nu\!\left(\frac{j_m r}{R}\right), \qquad
c_m = \frac{2}{R^2 J_{\nu+1}(j_m)^2} \int_0^R g(r)\, J_\nu\!\left(\frac{j_m r}{R}\right) r\,dr
$$

as above (Bessel-function orthogonality, DLMF 10.22.37).
The coefficient integral is exactly the Hankel transform at wavenumber $\kappa_m = j_m / R$, giving $c_m = 2F(\kappa_m) / (R^2 J_{\nu+1}(j_m)^2)$.

Also assume $F$ is negligible at $\kappa \ge j_{n+1}/R$, and truncate the series at $n$ terms.
Evaluating the truncated series at the radial nodes

$$
r_k = R\,\frac{j_k}{j_{n+1}} \qquad (k = 1, \dots, n)
$$

gives a formula for obtaining $g(r_k)$ from $F(\kappa_m)$.
Interchanging roles and expanding $F$ in a Fourier–Bessel series of $[0, j_{n+1}/R]$ gives the converse formula for obtaining $F(\kappa_m)$ from $g(r_k)$.
The two formulas are as follows.

$$
F(\kappa_m) \approx \left(\frac{R}{j_{n+1}}\right)^2 \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{2\, J_\nu(j_m j_k / j_{n+1})}{J_{\nu+1}(j_k)^2}\, g(r_k),
\qquad
g(r_k) \approx \frac{1}{R^2} \sum_{m=1}^{n} \frac{2\, J_\nu(j_k j_m / j_{n+1})}{J_{\nu+1}(j_m)^2}\, F(\kappa_m)
$$

The left formula is forward quadrature, and the right inverse quadrature.
This is Johnson's (1987) discrete Hankel transform.

There are 2 assumptions.
$g$ must be sufficiently small at $r > R$, and $F$ sufficiently small at $\kappa > j_{n+1}/R$.
A nonzero function cannot be exactly zero outside both ranges, so the discrete Hankel transform is approximate.
Error mainly comes from the tails of $g$ and $F$ remaining outside the ranges.
McMahon's asymptotic expression $j_m \approx (m + \nu/2 - 1/4)\pi$ gives frequency cutoff $j_{n+1}/R \approx n\pi/R$.
For required wavenumber range $\kappa_{\max}$, $n \gtrsim R\,\kappa_{\max}/\pi$ is a guide to sample count.

The forward and inverse matrices share symmetric kernel $J_\nu(j_k j_m / j_{n+1})$ and weights $2/J_{\nu+1}(j_k)^2$, differing only by their factors.
The two quadratures are not exact inverses; their matrix product is generally not the identity.
At $\nu = 1/2$, however, zeros are $j_k = k\pi$, the kernel is proportional to $\sin(\pi km/(n+1))/\sqrt{km}$, and weights to $\pi^2 k$.
Then DST-I orthogonality $\sum_{m=1}^{n} \sin(\pi km/(n+1))\sin(\pi ml/(n+1)) = \frac{n+1}{2}\delta_{kl}$ makes the matrix product the identity apart from rounding.
In either case, returning to the input on a round trip does not establish closeness to the continuous transform.

## Validating zeros

The caller supplies the zeros.
For $\nu = 1/2$, $J_{1/2}(x) = \sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\,\sin x$, so the zeros are $k\pi$.
[Special-function zeros in scioxide-rootfind](../../../scioxide-rootfind/algorithms/special-function-zeros/) computes zeros of other orders.

Preparation applies the following 3 checks to the $n+1$ supplied zeros $z_k$, returning `InvalidInput` if they fail.

1. The Newton correction $c_k = \lvert J_\nu(z_k) / J_\nu'(z_k)\rvert$ is at most $\max(a, r z_k)$.
   $a$ and $r$ are the absolute and relative tolerances of `Tolerance`, defaulting to `Tolerance::from_sqrt_epsilon()` ($a = r = \sqrt{\varepsilon}$).
2. The sign of $J_\nu'(z_k)$ matches the sign $(-1)^k$ at positive zero $k$.
3. $z_k$ lies within $\max(a, r z_k)$ of the interval for zero $k$ independently found during preparation.
   This also rejects sequences skipping an even number of intermediate zeros.

Accepted zeros are used unchanged, without further rounding.
Default tolerances accept zeros rounded to working precision (correction approximately $\varepsilon z_k/2$).
Tighten tolerances with `DiscreteHankelPlanSpec::with_tolerance` when full-precision zeros are required.
Bessel evaluations used to find zero intervals also count toward preparation work.

## Cost and rounding error

Preparation evaluates $n(n+1)/2$ distinct symmetric-kernel values and $n$ weights with Bessel functions and retains an $n \times n$ matrix.
Preparation evaluation count and storage are both $O(n^2)$.
Execution is one matrix-vector product with $O(n^2)$ multiply-adds.
It uses no FFT (`resolved_backends()` returns an empty sequence).

Each matrix row and the input store significands and binary exponents separately.
Thus computation is possible when factors $(R/j_{n+1})^2$ or $1/R^2$ exceed the scalar range, provided the result remains in range.
Multiply-adds use compensated summation in fixed order, yielding identical bits regardless of execution policy (thread count).

The rustdoc states a rounding-error range as an accuracy contract.
Each output component differs from a compensated extended-exponent dot product by at most

$$
8\varepsilon\,(n+2) \sum_k \lvert m_{ik}\rvert\,\lvert x_k\rvert
$$

the expression above plus twice the smallest subnormal number.
$m_{ik}$ is a matrix entry for the plan's direction, and $x_k$ is an input real or imaginary component.
This bound covers rounding alone, excluding the difference from the continuous integral (quadrature error).
Quadrature error depends on the tails of $g$ and $F$; check against an analytic solution or a wider-range computation.

## Plans and convenience functions

- Specification `DiscreteHankelPlanSpec::new(order, extent, direction)` takes order $\nu$, range $R$, and direction.
  `FftDirection::Forward` is forward quadrature from $g(r_k)$ to $F(\kappa_m)$; `Inverse` is inverse quadrature.
  `with_tolerance` sets zero tolerances, `with_limits` limits, `with_iteration_budget` the per-execution iteration limit ($n$ iterations per sequence transform), and `with_execution_policy` matrix-vector execution policy.
- Limits [`HankelLimits`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-fft/0.1.0/scioxide_fft/struct.HankelLimits.html) contain preparation storage `preparation_bytes`, workspace storage `workspace_bytes`, and preparation work `preparation_work` (the total number of function calls and Bessel-function iterations).
  All default to unlimited.
  Exceeding storage returns `ResourceLimit`; exhausting work returns `ConvergenceFailure`.
- Plan `DiscreteHankelPlan::new(zeros, spec)` borrows and validates the first $n+1$ positive zeros and owns its nodes, frequencies, and matrix.
  The zero sequence is not retained in the plan.
  `build(zeros, spec, scope)` charges preparation work to the parent solve's accounting.
- `nodes()` returns nodes $r_k$ in increasing order; `frequencies()` returns frequencies $\kappa_m$ in increasing order.
  $r_k$ has the same length unit as $R$; $\kappa_m$ uses its reciprocal unit.
- Execution `execute_into(input, output, workspace, scope)` takes a complex sequence of length $n$.
  The matrix is real, so real and imaginary parts are transformed separately.
  Allocate workspace once with `workspace(())` and reuse it for execution of the same plan.
  `execute_axis_into` transforms each sequence along one axis of a multidimensional array in one call.
- Obtain diagnostics [`DiscreteHankelDiagnostics`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-fft/0.1.0/scioxide_fft/struct.DiscreteHankelDiagnostics.html) with `diagnostics()`.
  `maximum_zero_residual` is the maximum $\lvert J_\nu(z_k)\rvert$ at zeros, `maximum_zero_correction` the maximum Newton correction, and `preparation_work` the preparation work used.
  These diagnose zeros and preparation, not quadrature-error estimates.
- The plan implements `LinearOperator` and `AdjointOperator`.
  The adjoint applies the transpose of the matrix for the plan's direction.
- Convenience functions `dht(input, zeros, order, extent)` and `idht` apply forward or inverse quadrature once to real samples and return a real sequence.
  Zero tolerances are fixed at defaults.
  A length of `input` other than `zeros.len() − 1` returns `DimensionMismatch`.

Failure classifications are as follows.
Fewer than 2 zeros, nonpositive or nonincreasing zeros, failed tolerances, nonconsecutive zeros, $\nu \le -1/2$, or $R \le 0$ give `InvalidInput`.
NaN or infinite zeros, order, range, or samples give `NonFinite`.
Unrepresentable nodes or frequencies, or weights rounding to 0, give `NumericalInstability`.

The following cell transforms $n$ samples of $g(r)=r^{1/2}e^{-r}$ on a grid with $\nu=1/2$ and $R=6$, checking convenience functions, plans, and limits.
The input is sample count $n$; supply zeros $k\pi$ ($k=1,\ldots,n+1$).
It checks 2 things.
First, compare `dht` with the forward plan's `execute_into` result, then apply `idht` to that result's real part and compare with the inverse-plan result.
Differences are divided by the maximum magnitude of the plan output.
Second, examine `preparation_work` and `workspace_bytes` in `HankelLimits`.
Use preparation work reported by the forward plan's `DiscreteHankelDiagnostics` and workspace bytes returned by `workspace_requirements(())` as reference values.
For each, try preparation and workspace allocation at exactly that limit and at one less.

```rust
//| id: fft-hankel-function-limits
//| ref: fft-hankel-function-limits
//| title: "Agreement of dht/idht with plans and the fields of HankelLimits"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   samples: {"type":"integer","label":"Sample count n","description":"Use n+1 zeros. Preparation work and storage grow as n².","min":4,"max":48,"value":16}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"many-samples","label":"n = 48","description":"The matrix becomes 9 times larger, increasing preparation work and plan_bytes().","inputs":{"samples":48}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":16}
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":"Success"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":"ConvergenceFailure"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":512,"atol":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":"ResourceLimit"}
//|   - case: many-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"agreement","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":"ConvergenceFailure"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1536,"atol":0.5}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, Complex, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError};
use scioxide_fft::{
    DiscreteHankelDiagnostics, DiscreteHankelPlan, DiscreteHankelPlanSpec, FftDirection,
    HankelLimits, dht, idht,
};

let n = samples as usize;
let (order, extent) = (0.5_f64, 6.0_f64);
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
// Positive zeros kπ of J_{1/2} (k = 1, …, n+1)
let zeros: Vec<f64> = (1..=n + 1).map(|k| k as f64 * pi).collect();
let spec = |direction| DiscreteHankelPlanSpec::new(order, extent, direction);
let forward =
    DiscreteHankelPlan::new(&zeros, spec(FftDirection::Forward)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let inverse =
    DiscreteHankelPlan::new(&zeros, spec(FftDirection::Inverse)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Samples g(r_k) = r_k^{1/2} e^{−r_k} (f64 functions are used only to generate input)
let g: Vec<f64> = forward
    .nodes()
    .iter()
    .map(|r| r.sqrt() * (-r).exp())
    .collect();
let input: Vec<Complex<f64>> = g.iter().map(|&v| Complex::new(v, 0.0)).collect();
let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
let mut workspace = forward.workspace(()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut transform = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); n];
forward
    .execute_into(&input, &mut transform, &mut workspace, cx)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut back = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); n];
inverse
    .execute_into(&transform, &mut back, &mut workspace, cx)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Convenience functions take real sequences, create a plan with default zero tolerances, and discard it
let once = dht(&g, &zeros, order, extent).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let real_transform: Vec<f64> = transform.iter().map(|z| z.re).collect();
let once_back = idht(&real_transform, &zeros, order, extent).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let gap = |p: &[f64], q: &[Complex<f64>]| {
    let scale = q.iter().map(|z| z.norm()).fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
    p.iter()
        .zip(q)
        .map(|(a, b)| (*a - b.re).abs().max(b.im.abs()))
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max)
        / scale
};

// Try forward preparation and workspace allocation at exactly the limit and one below it
let d: DiscreteHankelDiagnostics = forward.diagnostics();
let plain_bytes = forward
    .workspace_requirements(())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .bytes();
let attempt = |limits: HankelLimits| {
    DiscreteHankelPlan::new(&zeros, spec(FftDirection::Forward).with_limits(limits))
        .and_then(|plan| plan.workspace(()).map(|_| ()))
};
let label = |outcome: Result<(), SciOxideError>| match outcome {
    Ok(()) => "Success".to_string(),
    Err(e) => format!("{:?}", e.kind()),
};
let work_limit = |work: usize| -> Result<HankelLimits, String> {
    let budget = Budget::try_from(work).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(HankelLimits {
        preparation_work: budget,
        ..HankelLimits::default()
    })
};
let bytes_limit = |bytes: usize| HankelLimits {
    workspace_bytes: bytes,
    ..HankelLimits::default()
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for work in [d.preparation_work, d.preparation_work - 1] {
    rows.push(("preparation_work", work, label(attempt(work_limit(work)?))));
}
for bytes in [plain_bytes, plain_bytes - 1] {
    rows.push(("workspace_bytes", bytes, label(attempt(bytes_limit(bytes)))));
}

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let agreement_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Sample count n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "dht–plan difference / maximum output [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "idht–plan difference / maximum output [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum |J_ν| at zeros [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "plan_bytes() [B]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let agreement = (
    n,
    gap(&once, &transform),
    gap(&once_back, &back),
    d.maximum_zero_residual,
    forward.plan_bytes(),
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    agreement_columns,
    vec![agreement],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "agreement" })
);
let limit_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "HankelLimits field" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Limit [count or B]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Result" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    limit_columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "limits" })
);
```

At both the default $n=16$ and $n=48$, `dht` and `idht` exactly match plan results.
The convenience functions create the same plan from the same zeros, order, and range with default zero tolerances and use it once.
At $n=16$, preparation work is 7051; setting `preparation_work` to 7051 permits preparation, while 7050 gives `ConvergenceFailure`.
The workspace holds $2n$ complex values, or 512 B; setting `workspace_bytes` to 511 gives `ResourceLimit`.
The work limit is an iteration limit that stops immediately on exhaustion, so its failure classification differs from storage limits.
At $n=48$, work increases to 31502, workspace to 1536 B, and `plan_bytes()` from 2664 B to 19944 B.

## Checking a three-dimensional Gaussian

Transform the three-dimensional Gaussian $f(r) = e^{-r^2/2}$.
Use dimensionless quantities with length unit 1.
With $\nu = 1/2$ and $g(r) = r^{1/2} e^{-r^2/2}$, the analytic Hankel transform is $F(\kappa) = \kappa^{1/2} e^{-\kappa^2/2}$ (Weber's integral, DLMF 10.22.51, with $\nu = 1/2$).
Converting to a Fourier transform gives $\hat f(k) = (2\pi)^{3/2} e^{-k^2/2}$.

The following cell varies sample count $n$ and range $R$, computes $F(\kappa_m)$ with a forward plan, and compares with the analytic solution.
It also measures the difference after returning to $g(r_k)$ with an inverse plan.
Tighten zero tolerances to $10^{-13}$ with `with_tolerance`.
The first plot shows $F$ and the analytic solution; the second plots absolute differences at each frequency on a logarithmic axis.
The two plots have linked wavenumber axes.
The table reports tails $g(R)$ and $F(\kappa_n)$, maximum difference, maximum round-trip difference, zero residuals, and preparation work.

//| ref: fft-hankel-gaussian
//| title: "Compute a three-dimensional Gaussian radial transform on a Bessel-zero grid"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   samples: {"type":"range","label":"Sample count n","description":"Use n+1 zeros. The frequency cutoff κₙ ≈ nπ/R increases.","min":4,"max":64,"step":4,"value":24}

//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"few-samples","label":"n = 8","description":"Frequency cutoff is 3.1, leaving an F tail outside the grid.","inputs":{"samples":8}}
//|   - {"id":"short-extent","label":"R = 4","description":"Truncating g at r = 4 leaves a g-tail error even with more samples.","inputs":{"extent":4}}

//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}

//|   - case: few-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":3.141592653589793,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":9.93e-4,"rtol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: short-extent
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":6.709252558050237e-4,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":7.25e-5,"rtol":0.01}}
//|   - case: long-extent
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":6.7059525e-9,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":2.36e-10,"rtol":0.02}}
use scioxide_core::{Complex, ExecutionContext, Tolerance};
use scioxide_fft::{DiscreteHankelPlan, DiscreteHankelPlanSpec, FftDirection};

let n = samples.round() as usize;
let order = 0.5_f64; // Order ν = 1/2 (three-dimensional radial transform)
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
// Positive zeros of J_{1/2}(x) = √(2/(πx)) sin x are kπ (k = 1, …, n+1)
let zeros: Vec<f64> = (1..=n + 1).map(|k| k as f64 * pi).collect();
let tolerance = Tolerance::new(1e-13, 1e-13).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let spec =
    |direction| DiscreteHankelPlanSpec::new(order, extent, direction).with_tolerance(tolerance);
let forward =
    DiscreteHankelPlan::new(&zeros, spec(FftDirection::Forward)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let inverse =
    DiscreteHankelPlan::new(&zeros, spec(FftDirection::Inverse)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// g(r) = r^{1/2} e^{−r²/2}. Analytic solution F(κ) = κ^{1/2} e^{−κ²/2} has the same form (both evaluated with f64 functions)
let profile = |x: f64| x.sqrt() * (-0.5 * x * x).exp();
let input: Vec<Complex<f64>> = forward
    .nodes()
    .iter()
    .map(|&r| Complex::new(profile(r), 0.0))
    .collect();
let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
let mut output = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); n];
let mut workspace = forward.workspace(()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
forward
    .execute_into(&input, &mut output, &mut workspace, cx)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Return to radial nodes with inverse quadrature (allocate workspace per plan)
let mut recovered = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); n];
let mut back_workspace = inverse.workspace(()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
inverse
    .execute_into(&output, &mut recovered, &mut back_workspace, cx)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check: compute differences from the analytic solution at frequency nodes κ_m = j_m / R
let (mut computed, mut exact, mut errors) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut largest = 0.0_f64;
for (value, &kappa) in output.iter().zip(forward.frequencies()) {
    let reference = profile(kappa);
    let error = (*value - Complex::new(reference, 0.0)).norm();
    largest = largest.max(error);
    computed.push([kappa, value.re]);
    exact.push([kappa, reference]);
    // Exclude exactly zero differences, which cannot be plotted on a log axis
    if error > 0.0 {
        errors.push([kappa, error]);
    }
}
let roundtrip = input
    .iter()
    .zip(&recovered)
    .map(|(a, b)| (*a - *b).norm())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let kappa_top = forward.frequencies()[n - 1];
let d = forward.diagnostics();

emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Discrete Hankel transform",
            "points": computed,
            "style": { "showSymbol": true, "lineType": "dotted" }

At the defaults $n = 24$, $R = 8$, the frequency cutoff is $\kappa_{24} = 9.42$ and the two tails are $g(8) = 3.6\times 10^{-14}$ and $F(9.42) = 1.6\times 10^{-19}$.
The maximum difference from the analytic solution is $1.1\times 10^{-15}$, on the scale of rounding error.
Setting $n = 8$ lowers the cutoff to $\kappa_8 = 3.14$, leaving the $F(3.14) = 1.3\times 10^{-2}$ tail outside the grid and giving maximum difference $9.9\times 10^{-4}$.
At $R = 4$, the cutoff rises to 18.8 but truncating the $g(4) = 6.7\times 10^{-4}$ tail gives maximum difference $7.3\times 10^{-5}$.
At $R = 12$ the $g$ tail falls to $10^{-31}$, but the cutoff falls to 6.28, and the $F(6.28) = 6.7\times 10^{-9}$ tail gives maximum difference $2.4\times 10^{-10}$.
In every case the maximum difference is no larger than the larger tail; increasing $R$ reduces the $g$ tail, while decreasing it reduces the $F$ tail.

Maximum round-trip differences are of order $10^{-16}$–$10^{-15}$ in every case.
At $\nu = 1/2$ the round trip would be the identity without rounding, so this measures rounding error alone.
Even at $n = 8$ with analytic-solution difference $10^{-3}$, the round-trip difference is $3.3\times 10^{-16}$; agreement on a round trip does not establish closeness to the analytic solution.
The maximum $\lvert J_\nu\rvert$ at zeros is $9.4\times 10^{-16}$, and f64-rounded zeros $k\pi$ pass tolerance $10^{-13}$.
Preparation work (total function calls and iterations) is 11951 at $n = 24$ and 47870 at $n = 64$.
Kernel evaluation count $n(n+1)/2$ increases 6.9-fold from 300 to 2080, while work increases only 4.0-fold.
Work also counts iterations within each Bessel evaluation; their count depends on the argument, so work is not proportional to evaluation count.

## Summary

The $d$-dimensional Fourier transform of an axisymmetric function is a Hankel transform of order $\nu = d/2 - 1$ multiplied by a factor.
`DiscreteHankelPlan` applies quadrature to the integral over $[0, R]$ using the caller's first $n+1$ Bessel zeros, checking zero accuracy and order during preparation.
Preparation and execution both cost $O(n^2)$; rounding error has a bound, but quadrature error depends on the tail of $g$ at $r = R$ and of $F$ at $\kappa \approx n\pi/R$.
Choose $R$ where $g$ is sufficiently small and $n$ at least $R\,\kappa_{\max}/\pi$, checking against an analytic solution or a wider-range computation.
Agreement between forward and inverse round trips does not establish closeness to the continuous transform.
`dht` and `idht` are single-use functions giving plan-identical results; preparation exceeding `HankelLimits` stops with `ResourceLimit` for storage or `ConvergenceFailure` for work.
