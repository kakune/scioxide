In a real sequence's discrete Fourier transform (DFT), negative-frequency bins are conjugates of positive-frequency bins.
`rfft` uses this property to return only nonnegative-frequency bins; `irfft` reconstructs the original real sequence from them.
For the converse problem, with a conjugate-symmetric time sequence and real frequency sequence, use `hfft` and `ihfft`.
This page derives the symmetry and explains algorithms and costs, packed-storage types, and the symmetry checks that inverse transforms impose on input.
[DFT definition and normalization](../dft-sampling-and-normalization/) gives DFT sign and normalization conventions.

## Real-sequence spectra and Hermitian symmetry

Let $X_k=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_j\,e^{-2\pi i jk/N}$ be the DFT of sequence $x_j$ of length $N$.
When all $x_j$ are real, conjugation reverses only the exponent sign, so

$$
\overline{X_k}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_j\,e^{2\pi i jk/N}=X_{(N-k)\bmod N}
$$

the relation above holds.
This property is called Hermitian symmetry (conjugate symmetry).
Since $X_{N-k}$ is determined by $X_k$, storing the $\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$ bins at $k=0,\ldots,\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ determines the entire spectrum.

A bin satisfying $k=N-k$ is its own conjugate and hence real.
This always holds for DC $X_0=\sum_j x_j$ and also for Nyquist $X_{N/2}=\sum_j(-1)^jx_j$ at even lengths.
An odd length has no bin at exactly $1/2$ cycles per sample; the last stored bin $X_{(N-1)/2}$ is generally complex.
Thus the final bin's imaginary part must not be set to 0 for odd lengths.

Packed bin $k$ has frequency $k/(Nd)$ ($d$ is the sampling interval); `rfftfreq(n, d)` returns these $\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$ values.

## Algorithms for rfft and irfft

For even $N=2m$, combine even- and odd-indexed samples into one complex value $z_j=x_{2j}+i\,x_{2j+1}$ ($j=0,\ldots,m-1$) and compute one length-$m$ complex FFT $Z_k$.
The DFTs of the even and odd sequences can be extracted as

$$
E_k=\tfrac12\bigl(Z_k+\overline{Z_{m-k}}\bigr),\qquad
O_k=-\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(Z_k-\overline{Z_{m-k}}\bigr)
$$

above (indices are $\bmod m$), and $X_k=E_k+e^{-2\pi i k/N}O_k$ gives the $k=0,\ldots,m$ bins.
The cost is one complex FFT of length $m$ plus $O(N)$ postprocessing, approximately half a complex FFT of length $N$.
`irfft` reverses the same procedure.

For an odd length that is a proper multiple of 3, 5, or 7 ($N=pm$, $p\in\{3,5,7\}$, $N>p$), split samples into $p$ decimated sequences $x_{pt+r}$ ($r=0,\ldots,p-1$).
Pair sequences $r=1,\ldots,p-1$ into complex sequences and apply length-$m$ complex FFTs; recursively transform sequence $r=0$ with a length-$m$ real FFT.
Finally combine spectra with $p$-point butterflies, again costing approximately half a complex FFT.
Other odd lengths (3, 5, and 7 themselves, primes such as 11 and 13, and $11^2$) treat the real sequence as complex with zero imaginary part and use a length-$N$ complex FFT, so cost is not halved.
Every path costs $O(N\log N)$.

Stored bin count is approximately halved, but reductions in runtime and workspace depend on factorization and execution path.
The forward-transform error limit, as for complex FFTs, is relative $L^2$ error $2\varepsilon\max(1,\log_2N)$ ($\varepsilon$ is the scalar type's machine epsilon).
This is a normwise limit verified for native kernels by tests against high-precision DFTs.
Bins much smaller than the largest bin can have large relative errors.

## HermitianSpectrum and logical length

Real sequences of lengths 6 and 7 both pack into 4 bins.
Because bin count does not determine original length, `HermitianSpectrum<S>` owns both bin `Vec` and logical length $N$.

- `logical_len()` returns logical length $N$; `len()` returns bin count $\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$.
- `bins()` returns bins through a shared borrow, `bins_mut()` through a mutable borrow; `into_vec()` discards the logical length and extracts bin `Vec`.
- `from_vec(logical_len, bins)` returns `InvalidInput` (`Empty`) for logical length 0 and `DimensionMismatch` for a bin count other than $\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$.
- `zeros(logical_len)` allocates a zero-valued spectrum.

Construction checks only lengths, not finite values or symmetry.
After bins are edited with `bins_mut()`, symmetry is checked when the inverse transform executes ([Hermitian-symmetry validation](#hermitian-symmetry-validation)).

## Storage layouts in plans

For repeated transforms of the same length, prepare an `FftPlan` from `FftPlanSpec::new(n, direction, normalization, layout)`.
`FftLayout` selects input/output storage layout.

| `FftLayout` | Forward input → output | Inverse input → output | Convenience functions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Complex` | Complex length $N$ → complex length $N$ | Same | `fft`, `ifft` |
| `Real` | Real samples → packed bins | Packed bins → real samples | `rfft`, `irfft` |
| `Hermitian` | Packed conjugate-symmetric samples → real spectrum | Real spectrum → packed samples | `hfft`, `ihfft` |

`execute_into` receives input as `FftInput` and output as `FftOutput`.
Real slices and arrays convert to the `Real` variant, complex slices to `Complex`, and `&HermitianSpectrum` and `&mut HermitianSpectrum` to `Hermitian` carrying logical length.
Pass noncontiguous storage with `VectorView`; attach logical length with `FftInput::hermitian(logical_len, &bins)` or variant `FftInput::Hermitian { logical_len, bins }` when borrowing packed bins.
A variant incompatible with the plan's layout and direction gives `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`); a logical length or bin count differing from the plan gives `DimensionMismatch`.
Plans fix direction and normalization, so prepare another, inverse-direction plan for a round trip.
[Plan preparation and ownership](oxref:fft-plan-preparation) covers plan and workspace reuse.

## Hermitian-symmetry validation

`irfft`, `hfft`, `irfftn`, `hfftn`, and plans taking packed input check that the input is Hermitian symmetric within rounding error.
For each independent spectrum, let $M$ be the maximum absolute real or imaginary packed-bin component, $\varepsilon$ the scalar type's machine epsilon, and $\eta$ the smallest positive subnormal number; set tolerance to

$$
\tau=\max(8\varepsilon M,\ \eta)
$$

the expression above.
In one dimension, DC and, for even lengths, Nyquist bins must satisfy $|\operatorname{Im}X|\le\tau$.
In multidimensional transforms, both conjugate partners are stored on the packed axis's DC and Nyquist planes, so for pair $(X,Y)$ check

$$
|\operatorname{Re}X-\operatorname{Re}Y|\le2\tau,\qquad
|\operatorname{Im}X+\operatorname{Im}Y|\le2\tau
$$

the relations above ([HermitianSpectrumNd and inverse transforms](oxref:fft-real-nd-spectrum)).

Exceeding tolerance returns `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), without silent projection.
Non-finite components give `NonFinite`.
Deviations within tolerance are projected to exact Hermitian symmetry in workspace, without changing the caller's input.
Self-conjugate bins have their imaginary part set to 0; pairs are replaced by their average.
This projection changes the unnormalized transform result by at most $\sqrt2\,N(\tau+\varepsilon M+\eta)$.

$8\varepsilon$ is 16 times unit roundoff $u=\varepsilon/2$, and $\tau$ is an allowed input perturbation.
It does not guarantee the accuracy of the whole transform.
Separately, transforms returning real values from packed input compare imaginary parts of supposedly real intermediate results with a rounding-error bound for odd lengths lacking a real-data decomposition and on multidimensional DC/Nyquist planes.
Exceeding that bound returns `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).
Every error leaves output unchanged.

### Experiment on round-trip error and tolerance

The following cell takes $x_j=\cos(2\pi\cdot3j/N)+0.5$ through a round trip with `rfft` and `irfft` and computes maximum absolute error.
It then adds $\delta$, with input-selected ratio $\delta/\tau$, to the DC bin's imaginary part and passes it to `irfft`, examining acceptance and the output change when accepted.
The cell independently computes $\tau$ from its formula.
Input cosines are reference values constructed with `cos` from `f64`.

```rust
//| id: fft-real-hermitian-invariants
//| ref: fft-real-hermitian-invariants
//| title: "Real FFT round-trip error and Hermitian-symmetry tolerance"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   length: {"type":"range","label":"Sample length N","description":"For even lengths the Nyquist bin is real; for odd lengths the final bin is complex.","min":8,"max":64,"step":1,"value":16}
//|   perturbation_ratio: {"type":"range","label":"DC imaginary perturbation δ/τ","description":"At most 1 is accepted and projected; above 1 is rejected.","min":0,"max":2.5,"step":0.1,"value":0.8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"reject","label":"δ/τ = 1.5","description":"Tolerance is exceeded, and irfft returns InvalidInput.","inputs":{"perturbation_ratio":1.5}}
//|   - {"id":"odd-prime","label":"N = 13","description":"An odd length without real-data decomposition. There are 7 bins, the final one complex.","inputs":{"length":13}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roundtrip","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":16}
//|       - {"artifact":"roundtrip","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"roundtrip","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roundtrip","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":8,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roundtrip","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.4210854715202004e-14,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":0}}
//|   - case: reject
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":"Rejected (InvalidInput)"}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":null}
//|   - case: odd-prime
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roundtrip","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":7}
//|       - {"artifact":"roundtrip","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roundtrip","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":6.5,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":"Accepted"}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_fft::{HermitianSpectrum, Normalization, irfft, rfft};

let n = length.round() as usize;
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
// Input x_j = cos(2π·3j/N) + 0.5 (reference values constructed with f64 cos).
let input: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| (2.0 * pi * 3.0 * j as f64 / n as f64).cos() + 0.5)
    .collect();
// Inverse leaves the forward transform unscaled and applies 1/N on the inverse.
let spectrum = rfft(&input, Normalization::Inverse).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let roundtrip = irfft(&spectrum, Normalization::Inverse).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let max_gap = |a: &[f64], b: &[f64]| {
    a.iter()
        .zip(b)
        .map(|(p, q)| (p - q).abs())
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max)
};
let roundtrip_error = max_gap(&input, &roundtrip);
// Independent check: τ = max(8εM, η). M is the maximum absolute real or imaginary packed-bin component.
let maximum = spectrum
    .bins()
    .iter()
    .map(|z| z.re.abs().max(z.im.abs()))
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let tau = (8.0 * f64::EPSILON * maximum).max(f64::from_bits(1));
// Add δ to the DC bin's imaginary part. from_vec checks only length.
let delta = perturbation_ratio * tau;
let mut bins = spectrum.clone().into_vec();
bins[0] = Complex::new(bins[0].re, delta);
let perturbed = HermitianSpectrum::from_vec(n, bins).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (verdict, change) = match irfft(&perturbed, Normalization::Inverse) {
    // Projection only sets the DC imaginary part to 0, so compare with an unperturbed round trip.
    Ok(values) => ("Accepted".to_string(), Some(max_gap(&values, &roundtrip))),
    Err(e) => (format!("Rejected ({:?})", e.kind()), None),
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let digits6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let roundtrip_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Logical length N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Bin count ⌊N/2⌋+1", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum absolute round-trip error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum component M [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Tolerance τ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    roundtrip_columns,
    vec![(n, spectrum.len(), roundtrip_error, maximum, tau)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "roundtrip" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "δ/τ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Perturbation δ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "irfft decision" },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "Maximum difference from unperturbed output [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": sci3,
        "nullText": "No output"
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![(perturbation_ratio, delta, verdict, change)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "check" })
);
```

At the default $N=16$, DC and bin $k=3$ are both approximately 8, giving $M\approx8$ and $\tau=8\varepsilon M\approx1.42\times10^{-14}$.
Maximum absolute round-trip error is $3.3\times10^{-16}$ ($1.5\varepsilon$).
At $\delta/\tau\le1$, validation passes and output is bit-for-bit identical to the unperturbed round trip.
Projection restores the DC imaginary part to 0, leaving the same values entering the transform as in the unperturbed case.
Setting $\delta/\tau$ to at least 1.1 rejects input with `InvalidInput` and produces no output.
The decision uses stored floating-point values, so near exactly $\delta/\tau$ = 1, rounding in $\delta$ can change the result.

At $N=13$, $M\approx6.5$ and there are 7 bins.
Since 13 is prime, this real FFT uses a length-13 complex FFT.
Round-trip error is $4.4\times10^{-16}$, comparable to even lengths, and validation behavior is unchanged.

Passing validation does not establish that a spectrum is the intended one.
Edits preserving symmetry still change results: changing the DC real part, for example, changes the signal mean.
Check amplitudes and units separately.

## hfft and ihfft

If a time sequence $h_j$ satisfies $h_{N-j}=\overline{h_j}$, its DFT is real.
`hfft` takes the first $h_0,\ldots,h_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$ values of this conjugate-symmetric sequence as `HermitianSpectrum` and returns its forward DFT

$$
y_k=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}h_j\,e^{-2\pi i jk/N}
$$

as a real sequence of length $N$.
`ihfft` computes the inverse DFT $h_j=\sum_k y_k e^{2\pi i jk/N}$ of real sequence $y$, returning the first $\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$ conjugate-symmetric values together with the logical length.
`hfft` is the inverse of `ihfft`; both follow the `Normalization` convention.
For plans, select `FftLayout::Hermitian`.

`rfft` and `hfft` use the same type but have different orientations.
The `HermitianSpectrum` output of `rfft` contains frequency bins; the input to `hfft` contains time samples.
`hfft` also checks imaginary parts of input $h_0$ and even-length $h_{N/2}$ with the tolerance above.

The following cell checks that packed representations agree with the beginning of full-length transforms.
For the asymmetric real sequence $x_j=1/(1+j)+0.25\,(j\bmod3)$, compare `rfft` bins with the beginning of complex `fft` and the `ihfft` result with the beginning of complex `ifft`.
It also measures whether the `fft` result itself satisfies $X_{N-k}=\overline{X_k}$ and whether `hfft` restores `ihfft`.

```rust
//| id: fft-hermitian-duality
//| ref: fft-hermitian-duality
//| title: "Agreement of packed transforms with full-length complex transforms"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   length: {"type":"range","label":"Length N","description":"Check bin counting ⌊N/2⌋+1 for odd and even lengths.","min":1,"max":40,"step":1,"value":16}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"odd","label":"N = 15","description":"Odd length. There are 8 bins and no Nyquist bin.","inputs":{"length":15}}
//|   - {"id":"single","label":"N = 1","description":"There is only one bin, DC.","inputs":{"length":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"gaps","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gaps","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gaps","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gaps","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: odd
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":8}
//|       - {"artifact":"gaps","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gaps","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: single
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"gaps","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"max":0}}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_fft::{Normalization, fft, hfft, ifft, ihfft, rfft};

let n = length.round() as usize;
let norm = Normalization::Inverse;
// Asymmetric real sequence (constructed with arithmetic alone).
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| 1.0 / (1.0 + j as f64) + 0.25 * (j % 3) as f64)
    .collect();
let as_complex: Vec<Complex<f64>> = x.iter().map(|&v| Complex::new(v, 0.0)).collect();
// zip stops at the shorter sequence, allowing comparison of the packed sequence with the beginning of the full-length sequence.
let max_gap = |a: &[Complex<f64>], b: &[Complex<f64>]| {
    a.iter()
        .zip(b)
        .map(|(p, q)| (*p - *q).norm())
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max)
};
let packed = rfft(&x, norm).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let full = fft(&as_complex, norm).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rfft_gap = max_gap(packed.bins(), &full);
// Does the full-length result satisfy X[N−k] = conj(X[k])?
let symmetry_gap = (1..n)
    .map(|k| (full[n - k] - full[k].conj()).norm())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let half = ihfft(&x, norm).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let inverse = ifft(&as_complex, norm).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let ihfft_gap = max_gap(half.bins(), &inverse);
let back = hfft(&half, norm).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let hfft_gap = x
    .iter()
    .zip(&back)
    .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let size_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Logical length N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "rfft bin count", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "ihfft packed length", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    size_columns,
    vec![(packed.logical_len(), packed.len(), half.len())],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sizes" })
);
let gap_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Comparison" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum absolute difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    gap_columns,
    vec![
        ("rfft versus first ⌊N/2⌋+1 fft bins", rfft_gap),
        ("ihfft versus first ⌊N/2⌋+1 ifft values", ihfft_gap),
        ("fft result X[N−k] versus conj(X[k])", symmetry_gap),
        ("hfft(ihfft(x)) versus x", hfft_gap),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "gaps" })
);
```

At the default $N=16$, every row differs by at most $2.2\times10^{-16}$; packed transforms return the beginning of full-length transforms.
At $N=15$, the difference between `rfft` and `fft` grows to $1.3\times10^{-15}$.
Because 15 is divisible by 3, `rfft` takes a decimation path with a different operation order from the full-length complex FFT.
The third row checks without using `rfft` and shows that the complex FFT result is Hermitian symmetric within its rounding error.
At $N=1$ the sole bin is DC, and the round trip through `hfft` and `ihfft` returns input unchanged.

## Summary

A real-sequence spectrum satisfies $X_{N-k}=\overline{X_k}$, so `rfft` returns only $\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1$ bins, and `HermitianSpectrum` retains logical length $N$ with them.
Even lengths and odd proper multiples of 3, 5, or 7 cost approximately half a length-$N$ complex FFT; other odd lengths use a length-$N$ complex FFT.
The last bin at an odd length is complex and must not be constrained to real values.
The inverse compares DC and Nyquist imaginary parts against $\tau=\max(8\varepsilon M,\eta)$, projecting in workspace within tolerance and returning `InvalidInput` beyond it.
Use `hfft` and `ihfft` when the time sequence is conjugate symmetric and the frequency sequence real.
