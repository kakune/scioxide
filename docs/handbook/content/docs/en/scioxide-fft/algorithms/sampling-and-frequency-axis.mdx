Uniformly sampling a continuous-time signal and applying a DFT produces aliasing and spectral leakage in the result.
This page derives both phenomena and introduces functions mapping bin indices to physical frequencies.
[DFT definition and normalization](../dft-sampling-and-normalization/) covers DFT definition, signs, normalization, and conversion factors from bin values to amplitudes.
Here $N$ denotes length and $\Delta t$ the sampling interval.

## Sampling theorem and aliasing

Sampling a continuous signal $x(t)$ at interval $\Delta t$ (sampling frequency $f_s = 1/\Delta t$) can be written as its product $x_p(t) = x(t)p(t)$ with the Dirac comb $p(t) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t - n\Delta t)$.
By Poisson summation, the comb's Fourier transform is a comb in frequency space.

$$
\mathcal{F}\{p\}(f) = \frac{1}{\Delta t}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(f - m f_s)
$$

The convolution theorem therefore makes the sampled spectrum a superposition of copies of the original spectrum $X(f)$ spaced by $f_s$.

$$
X_p(f) = \frac{1}{\Delta t}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty} X(f - m f_s)
$$

If $x(t)$ contains components at or above the Nyquist frequency $f_{\mathrm{Nyq}} = f_s/2$, neighboring copies enter and overlap the interval $[-f_s/2, f_s/2]$.
Conversely, if all components of $x(t)$ lie in $\lvert f\rvert < f_s/2$, the copies do not overlap and the original signal can be reconstructed from samples (the sampling theorem).
At grid points,

$$
e^{2\pi i (f + m f_s) n\Delta t} = e^{2\pi i f n\Delta t}\, e^{2\pi i mn} = e^{2\pi i f n\Delta t} \qquad (m, n \in \mathbb{Z})
$$

so sinusoids at frequencies $f$ and $f + m f_s$ have identical sample values and cannot be distinguished from samples alone.
For real signals, a component of frequency $f_0$ appears at $\lvert f_0 - m f_s\rvert$ ($m$ is the integer nearest $f_0/f_s$).

## Finite observation windows and spectral leakage

Extracting an interval of length $N$ is equivalent to multiplying by rectangular window $w_n = 1$ ($0 \le n < N$).
For complex sinusoid $x_n = e^{2\pi i\alpha n/N}$ with $\alpha$ periods in the observation window, geometric summation gives the DFT

$$
X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} e^{2\pi i(\alpha - k)n/N}
= e^{i\pi(\alpha - k)(N-1)/N}\,\frac{\sin(\pi(\alpha - k))}{\sin(\pi(\alpha - k)/N)}
$$

as above.
This kernel is called the Dirichlet kernel (discrete sinc function).

1. For integer $\alpha$, only bin $k=\alpha\bmod N$ has value $N$; the numerator $\sin(\pi\cdot\text{integer})$ vanishes at other integer bins.
2. For noninteger $\alpha$, the numerator is nonzero at every integer bin, spreading energy into surrounding bins.
   This is called spectral leakage.
   Halfway between bins ($\alpha - k = 1/2$), the nearest bin magnitude falls to $\lvert 1/\sin(\pi/(2N))\rvert \approx 2N/\pi$.
   [Noninteger periods and spectral leakage](../../examples/spectral-leakage/) demonstrates suppressing leakage with window functions.

## Frequency axis and bin order

$X_k$ is periodic in $k$ with period $N$, so bin $k \ge N/2$ is the same as negative-frequency bin $k - N$.
For sampling interval $d = \Delta t$, bin $k$ has physical frequency $k/(Nd)$ when $k < N/2$ and $(k-N)/(Nd)$ when $k \ge N/2$; bin spacing is $\Delta f = 1/(Nd) = f_s/N$.
Without zero-padding, observation time $T = Nd$ determines this spacing.
Zero-padding $N$ observed points to length $L>N$ refines the evaluation grid to $f_s/L$ without changing observation time or the window's main-lobe width.

Construct frequency axes with the following 4 functions.
Frequency units are reciprocal to the units of $d$; giving $d$ in seconds gives Hz.

| Function | Returned sequence | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `fftfreq(n, d)` | $[0, 1, \dots, \lceil n/2\rceil - 1, -\lfloor n/2\rfloor, \dots, -1]/(nd)$ | $n$ |
| `rfftfreq(n, d)` | $[0, 1, \dots, \lfloor n/2\rfloor]/(nd)$ | $\lfloor n/2\rfloor + 1$ |
| `fftshift(x)` | `x` rotated left by $\lceil n/2\rceil$ elements. Bin 0 moves to index $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ | $n$ |
| `ifftshift(x)` | `x` rotated left by $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ elements. Inverse of `fftshift` | $n$ |

For even $n$, `fftfreq` returns Nyquist bin $n/2$ as negative frequency $-1/(2d)$, while `rfftfreq` returns positive frequency $+1/(2d)$.
For odd $n$, the rotations differ, so always undo `fftshift` with `ifftshift`.
Supplying $d < 0$ reverses every sign.
Each value is computed directly from integer $k$ as $(k/n)/d$, avoiding accumulated rounding error in the grid.

$n = 0$ and $d = 0$ give `InvalidInput`; NaN or infinite $d$ gives `NonFinite`.
If $1/(nd)$ or a frequency falls outside the finite range or rounds to 0, the result is `NumericalInstability`.
If $n$ or an index is not exactly representable in the scalar type, or adjacent frequencies cannot be distinguished in that type, the result is `ScalarConversion`.
`fftshift` and `ifftshift` accept sequences of arbitrary `Clone` elements and return an empty sequence for empty input.
[fftshift and ifftshift](oxref:fft-frequency-shift) covers axiswise shifts of multidimensional arrays (`fftshift_nd`, `ifftshift_nd`).

The following cell lists outputs of the 4 functions for length $n$ and sampling interval $d$.
The final column is an independent check manually implementing the formula in the table above.
Switch $n$ between even and odd values to examine the Nyquist-bin sign and different rotation amounts.

```rust
//| id: fft-frequency-order
//| ref: fft-frequency-order
//| title: "Compare fftfreq, rfftfreq, fftshift, and ifftshift order for even and odd lengths"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   length: {"type":"range","label":"Length n","description":"Even lengths have a Nyquist bin; odd lengths have different fftshift and ifftshift rotations.","min":1,"max":12,"step":1,"value":6}
//|   spacing: {"type":"range","label":"Sampling interval d [s]","description":"All frequencies are integer multiples of 1/(n d) [Hz].","min":0.25,"max":2,"step":0.25,"value":0.5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"odd-length","label":"n = 5","description":"No Nyquist bin exists; fftshift rotates by 3 elements and ifftshift by 2.","inputs":{"length":5}}
//|   - {"id":"single-sample","label":"n = 1","description":"Every function returns only 0.","inputs":{"length":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"frequency-order","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":-1,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"frequency-order","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-1,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"frequency-order","path":"/rows/3/4","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"frequency-order","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":0.3333333333333333,"atol":1e-15}}
//|   - case: odd-length
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"frequency-order","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-0.8,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"frequency-order","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"frequency-order","path":"/rows/3/4","equals":null}
//|   - case: single-sample
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"frequency-order","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":0}}
use scioxide_fft::{fftfreq, fftshift, ifftshift, rfftfreq};

let n = length.round() as usize;
let d = spacing;
let full = fftfreq(n, d).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let packed = rfftfreq(n, d).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let centered = fftshift(&full).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let restored = ifftshift(&centered).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: k/(n d) for k < ⌈n/2⌉, otherwise (k − n)/(n d)
let by_hand = |k: usize| {
    let signed = if k < n.div_ceil(2) {
        k as f64
    } else {
        k as f64 - n as f64
    };
    signed / (n as f64 * d)
};
let rows: Vec<(usize, f64, f64, f64, Option<f64>, f64)> = (0..n)
    .map(|k| {
        (
            k,
            full[k],
            centered[k],
            restored[k],
            packed.get(k).copied(),
            by_hand(k),
        )
    })
    .collect();
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Index k", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "fftfreq [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "After fftshift [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Restored by ifftshift [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "rfftfreq [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Check (k or k − n)/(n d) [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "frequency-order" })
);
```

At the defaults $n = 6$, $d = 0.5$ s, index 3 of `fftfreq` is $-1$ Hz (the Nyquist bin), while index 3 of `rfftfreq` is $+1$ Hz.
After `fftshift` the sequence increases from $-1$ Hz; `ifftshift` restores the original ordering.
At $n = 5$ there is no Nyquist bin; the first value after `fftshift` is $-2/(5 \cdot 0.5) = -0.8$ Hz and bin 0 lies at index $\lfloor 5/2\rfloor = 2$.

## Observing aliasing and leakage

The following cell transforms a sinusoid sampled at 64 Hz with 64 points (observation time 1 s, bin spacing 1 Hz) using `rfft`, plotting one-sided amplitude on the `rfftfreq` frequency axis.
One-sided amplitude is the unscaled bin magnitude times $2/N$ ($1/N$ at DC and Nyquist; [Conversion to physical amplitude](oxref:fft-physical-amplitude)).
Moving signal frequency to an integer above the 32 Hz Nyquist frequency places the maximum bin at predicted aliased frequency $\lvert f_0 - m f_s\rvert$.
Noninteger frequencies show leakage on both sides of the peak.
Input generation uses f64 `sin`; the predicted aliased frequency is an independent hand-calculated check.

```rust
//| id: fft-sampling-alias
//| ref: fft-sampling-alias
//| title: "Move signal frequency across the Nyquist frequency"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   signal_frequency_hz: {"type":"range","label":"Signal frequency [Hz]","description":"Change the sinusoid frequency at sampling frequency 64 Hz.","min":1,"max":55,"step":0.5,"value":12}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"alias-40hz","label":"40 Hz","description":"Above Nyquist, it appears in the 24 Hz bin with amplitude 1.","inputs":{"signal_frequency_hz":40}}
//|   - {"id":"half-bin","label":"12.5 Hz","description":"Halfway between bins, the maximum bin amplitude falls near 2/π.","inputs":{"signal_frequency_hz":12.5}}
//|   - {"id":"nyquist","label":"32 Hz","description":"All samples are sin(πn) = 0, making the signal unobservable.","inputs":{"signal_frequency_hz":32}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"alias","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":12,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"alias","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":12,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"alias","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: alias-40hz
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"alias","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":24,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"alias","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":24,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"alias","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: half-bin
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"alias","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.6425,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: nyquist
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"alias","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_fft::{Normalization, rfft, rfftfreq};

// Sampling frequency 64 Hz, 64 points (observation time 1 s, bin spacing 1 Hz)
let sample_rate = 64.0_f64;
let count = 64_usize;
// Use f64 sin to generate input
let samples: Vec<f64> = (0..count)
    .map(|n| (2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * signal_frequency_hz * n as f64 / sample_rate).sin())
    .collect();

// Transform unscaled; multiply by 2/N for one-sided amplitude (1/N at DC and Nyquist)
let spectrum = rfft(&samples, Normalization::Unscaled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Frequency of bin k: k/(N Δt) [Hz]
let frequencies = rfftfreq(count, 1.0 / sample_rate).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = spectrum
    .bins()
    .iter()
    .zip(&frequencies)
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, (value, &f))| {
        let scale = if k == 0 || 2 * k == count { 1.0 } else { 2.0 };
        [f, scale * value.norm() / count as f64]
    })
    .collect();

// Bin of maximum one-sided amplitude (retain the higher frequency on ties)
let peak = curve
    .iter()
    .fold(curve[0], |best, &p| if p[1] >= best[1] { p } else { best });
// Independent check: predicted alias |f0 − m fs| (m is the integer nearest f0/fs)
let expected_alias =
    (signal_frequency_hz - (signal_frequency_hz / sample_rate).round() * sample_rate).abs();

emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "One-sided amplitude", "points": curve })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Frequency [Hz]",
        "yLabel": "One-sided amplitude [-]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 32.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.05,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": sample_rate / 2.0, "label": "Nyquist frequency" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": expected_alias, "label": "Predicted alias" }
        ]
    })
);
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Signal frequency [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Predicted alias [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum bin [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Its one-sided amplitude [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Bin spacing [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        signal_frequency_hz,
        expected_alias,
        peak[0],
        peak[1],
        frequencies[1] - frequencies[0],
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "alias" })
);
```

At the default 12 Hz, only the 12 Hz bin has amplitude 1.
Supplying 40 Hz gives amplitude 1 at bin $\lvert 40 - 64\rvert = 24$ Hz.
This sample sequence matches a sign-reversed 24 Hz sinusoid, so one-sided amplitude alone cannot distinguish them.
At half-integer frequencies such as 12.5 Hz, the maximum bin (12 Hz) falls to amplitude 0.6425 and leakage spreads into surrounding bins.
This is close to $2/\pi \approx 0.637$ estimated from the Dirichlet kernel of one complex sinusoid; the remaining difference is leakage from the negative-frequency component.
At exactly 32 Hz, $\sin(\pi n)$ is near 0 at every sample, making the signal itself unobservable.
The largest bin then has only rounding-scale magnitude (of order $4\times10^{-15}$).

## Summary

Sampling replicates spectra every $f_s$, and components above Nyquist alias to $\lvert f_0 - m f_s\rvert$; limit the band below $f_s/2$ before sampling.
A sinusoid with a noninteger number of observed periods leaks into surrounding bins according to the Dirichlet kernel; halfway between bins, maximum-bin amplitude falls to approximately $2/\pi$ times its original value.
Bin spacing $f_s/N$ depends on observation time; zero-padding only refines the evaluation grid and does not improve resolution.
Construct frequency axes with `fftfreq` and `rfftfreq`, checking the even-length Nyquist sign and the differing odd-length rotations of `fftshift` and `ifftshift`.
