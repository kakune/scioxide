Multiplying integers in positional notation reduces to multiplying polynomials whose coefficients are the digits.
Computing coefficient convolution with an FFT gives product coefficients in $O(L\log L)$ operations using a transform of length $L$.
Floating-point FFT results have rounding error, however, which must be sufficiently small to recover integers.

This page computes linear coefficient convolution with `rfft` and `irfft`, then forms product digits by nearest-integer rounding and carrying.
An integer convolution using no floating point provides the check, measuring true coefficient error and counting incorrect digits.
Vary base $B$ and digit count $d$ to investigate where error approaches $1/2$ and integer recovery fails.
This example studies FFT integer-recovery conditions; it is not an arbitrary-precision integer library.

## Base and polynomial products

Write two $d$-digit integers in base $B\ge2$, least significant digit first, as

$$
u=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}u_j B^j,\qquad
v=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}v_j B^j,\qquad 0\le u_j,v_j<B
$$

shown above.
The coefficients of the product of polynomials $U(z)=\sum_j u_jz^j$ and $V(z)=\sum_jv_jz^j$ are

$$
w_\ell=\sum_j u_jv_{\ell-j},\qquad 0\le\ell<2d-1
$$

the linear convolution above, taking out-of-range digits as 0.
A length-$L$ DFT product gives circular convolution; zero-padding to $L\ge2d-1$ prevents wraparound and makes it equal to linear convolution.
`next_fast_len` returns a length at least this lower bound with only prime factors 2, 3, 5, and 7.
The caller supplies zero-padding.

## Convolution with real FFTs

Coefficients are real, so `rfft` computes only nonnegative-frequency bins $X_0,\ldots,X_{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}$.
The returned `HermitianSpectrum` owns the bins and logical length $L$.
Wrap the elementwise product of the two spectra's bins with `HermitianSpectrum::from_vec(L, bins)` and pass it to `irfft` to obtain a real sequence of length $L$.
Using `Normalization::Unscaled` for the forward transform and `Normalization::Inverse` for the inverse applies only the inverse factor $1/L$, giving the convolution coefficients themselves.
The product of real-sequence spectra preserves Hermitian symmetry, so the packed-bin product can be inverse-transformed directly.

## Conditions for integer recovery

Let $\widetilde w_\ell$ be the FFT-computed coefficient.
If, relative to the true integer coefficient,

$$
|\widetilde w_\ell-w_\ell|<\frac12
$$

the condition above holds, nearest-integer rounding recovers $w_\ell$.
In contrast, distance $|\widetilde w_\ell-\operatorname{round}(\widetilde w_\ell)|$ to the nearest integer equals true error only when that error is below $1/2$.
Beyond error $1/2$, a computed value lies near a different integer, so a small distance does not imply correctness.

Coefficient magnitudes are bounded by $d(B-1)^2$.
`f64` represents every integer of absolute value at most $2^{53}\approx9.0\times10^{15}$ exactly, but only some larger integers.
If the bound exceeds $2^{53}$, some inputs have true coefficients not even representable in `f64`.
Even a bound below $2^{53}$ does not ensure intermediate FFT rounding error below $1/2$.

Theorem 5.1 of Percival ("Rapid multiplication modulo the sum and difference of highly composite numbers", *Math. Comp.* 72 (2003), 387–395) gives the maximum-error bound for circular convolution computed with a length-$L=2^n$ radix-2 FFT,

$$
\max_\ell\lvert\widetilde w_\ell-w_\ell\rvert<\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2\left[(1+\varepsilon)^{3n}(1+\sqrt5\,\varepsilon)^{3n+1}(1+\beta)^{3n}-1\right]
$$

as above.
Here $\varepsilon$ bounds relative addition/subtraction and multiplication errors ($2^{-53}$ in double precision); $\beta$ bounds absolute error in computed twiddle factors $e^{2\pi ik/L}$.
Keeping only first-order terms in $\varepsilon$ and $\beta$ gives a factor $3(1+\sqrt5)\varepsilon n+3\beta n$; for correctly rounded twiddles ($\beta=\varepsilon/\sqrt2$), the error bound is approximately $12\,\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2\log_2L$.
This bounds the worst input; the same paper notes that random-input errors may cancel and become much smaller.
Because `scioxide-fft` also uses mixed radices, Bluestein, and real-FFT packing, and does not publish twiddle errors, this theorem cannot be applied as a guarantee.
Below, plot $\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2\log_2L$ without the constants as an indicative error scale, not as an upper bound.

## Carrying

Rounded coefficients $w_\ell$ may be at least $B$.
Starting at the least significant digit, add carry $q_\ell$ from the preceding digit and set

$$
s_\ell=w_\ell+q_\ell,\qquad
a_\ell=s_\ell\bmod B,\qquad
q_{\ell+1}=\left\lfloor s_\ell/B\right\rfloor,\qquad q_0=0
$$

as above to obtain the digit sequence of $0\le a_\ell<B$.
Append digits for any remaining final carry.
Carrying uses integer arithmetic without introducing error, but incorrectly rounded coefficients still produce incorrect digits.

## Experiment on base, digit count, and error

The following cell takes base $B=10^k$ and maximum digit count $2^m$, multiplying two random-digit integers for each digit count $d=1,2,4,\ldots,2^m$.
Select digits uniformly from $[0,B)$ with splitmix64 pseudorandom numbers.
As an independent check, compute integer coefficients $w_\ell$ through a direct double sum ($O(d^2)$), measuring true coefficient error.
Also form product digits by carrying both FFT coefficients and integer coefficients, then count mismatching digits.
Within the input range, $d(B-1)^2\le2^{12}\cdot10^{14}<2^{63}$, so `i64` computes coefficients and carries exactly.

The table reports coefficient bounds, true maximum coefficient error, maximum nearest-integer distance, incorrectly rounded coefficients, and incorrect product digits by digit count.
The log-log plot shows true maximum error with markers, nearest-integer distance dotted, guide $\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2\log_2L$ dashed, and $1/2$ as a horizontal line.
Exclude zero-error points from logarithmic axes and report their count below the table.

```rust
//| id: fft-integer-multiplication
//| ref: fft-integer-multiplication
//| title: "FFT-multiplication coefficient error versus base and digit count"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   radix_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Base exponent k (B = 10^k)","description":"Coefficient bound d(B−1)² and error grow in proportion to B².","min":1,"max":7,"step":1,"value":5}
//|   digits_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Maximum digit-count exponent m (d ≤ 2^m)","description":"Examine d = 1, 2, 4, …, 2^m.","min":2,"max":12,"step":1,"value":12}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","description":"Change the pseudorandom digit sequence.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"decimal","label":"B = 10","description":"Small coefficients give errors over 10 orders of magnitude below 1/2.","inputs":{"radix_exponent":1}}
//|   - {"id":"radix-1e7","label":"B = 10^7","description":"For large d, error exceeds 1/2 and product digits become incorrect.","inputs":{"radix_exponent":7}}
//|   - {"id":"radix-1e6","label":"B = 10^6","description":"At d = 4096, error grows close to 1/2.","inputs":{"radix_exponent":6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/0","equals":4096}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/1","equals":8192}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/3","approx":{"value":0.0039,"rtol":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/6","equals":0}
//|   - case: decimal
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/3","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/6","equals":0}
//|   - case: radix-1e7
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/3","range":{"min":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/5","range":{"min":1}}
//|   - case: radix-1e6
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/3","approx":{"value":0.375,"atol":0.2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/6","equals":0}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_fft::{HermitianSpectrum, Normalization, irfft, next_fast_len, rfft};

let base = 10_i64.pow(radix_exponent as u32);
let max_digits = 1_usize << (digits_exponent as u32);
// Input generation: uniform digits in [0, B) using splitmix64 (independent of the FFT).
let mut state = seed as u64;
let mut next_digit = || {
    state = state.wrapping_add(0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15);
    let mut z = state;
    z = (z ^ (z >> 30)).wrapping_mul(0xBF58_476D_1CE4_E5B9);
    z = (z ^ (z >> 27)).wrapping_mul(0x94D0_49BB_1331_11EB);
    ((z ^ (z >> 31)) % base as u64) as i64
};
// Carrying: s = w + q, a = s mod B, q ← ⌊s/B⌋. Retain negative carries as errors.
let carry_digits = |coefficients: &[i64]| {
    let (mut digits, mut carry) = (Vec::with_capacity(coefficients.len() + 4), 0_i64);
    for &w in coefficients {
        let total = w + carry;
        digits.push(total.rem_euclid(base));
        carry = total.div_euclid(base);
    }
    while carry > 0 {
        digits.push(carry % base);
        carry /= base;
    }
    if carry < 0 {
        digits.push(carry);
    }
    digits
};
let (mut rows, mut zero_points) = (Vec::new(), 0_usize);
let (mut true_curve, mut nearest_curve, mut scale_curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut d = 1_usize;
while d <= max_digits {
    let u: Vec<i64> = (0..d).map(|_| next_digit()).collect();
    let v: Vec<i64> = (0..d).map(|_| next_digit()).collect();
    // Zero-pad to L ≥ 2d − 1 so circular convolution equals linear convolution.
    let needed = 2 * d - 1;
    let length = next_fast_len(needed).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (mut a, mut b) = (vec![0.0; length], vec![0.0; length]);
    for j in 0..d {
        a[j] = u[j] as f64;
        b[j] = v[j] as f64;
    }
    let fa = rfft(&a, Normalization::Unscaled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let fb = rfft(&b, Normalization::Unscaled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let bins: Vec<Complex<f64>> = fa
        .bins()
        .iter()
        .zip(fb.bins())
        .map(|(&x, &y)| x * y)
        .collect();
    let spectrum = HermitianSpectrum::from_vec(length, bins).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let conv = irfft(&spectrum, Normalization::Inverse).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Independent check: integer-only coefficient double sum.
    let mut exact = vec![0_i64; needed];
    for (i, &x) in u.iter().enumerate() {
        for (j, &y) in v.iter().enumerate() {
            exact[i + j] += x * y;
        }
    }
    // Split w into a leading f64 part and integer remainder to measure errors correctly even beyond 2^53.
    let error = |(&x, &w): (&f64, &i64)| {
        let high = w as f64;
        ((x - high) - (w - high as i64) as f64).abs()
    };
    let true_error = conv.iter().zip(&exact).map(error).fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
    let nearest = conv[..needed]
        .iter()
        .map(|&x| (x - x.round()).abs())
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
    let rounded: Vec<i64> = conv[..needed].iter().map(|&x| x.round() as i64).collect();
    let wrong_coefficients = rounded.iter().zip(&exact).filter(|(r, w)| r != w).count();
    let (fft_digits, exact_digits) = (carry_digits(&rounded), carry_digits(&exact));
    let digit_count = fft_digits.len().max(exact_digits.len());
    let digit_at = |digits: &[i64], i: usize| digits.get(i).copied().unwrap_or(0);
    let wrong_digits = (0..digit_count)
        .filter(|&i| digit_at(&fft_digits, i) != digit_at(&exact_digits, i))
        .count();
    let norm = |x: &[i64]| x.iter().map(|&c| (c as f64).powi(2)).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
    let scale = f64::EPSILON / 2.0 * norm(&u) * norm(&v) * (length as f64).log2().max(1.0);
    let bound = d as f64 * ((base - 1) as f64).powi(2);
    rows.push((
        d,
        length,
        bound,
        true_error,
        nearest,
        wrong_coefficients,
        wrong_digits,
    ));
    if true_error > 0.0 {
        true_curve.push([d as f64, true_error]);
    } else {
        zero_points += 1;
    }
    if nearest > 0.0 {
        nearest_curve.push([d as f64, nearest]);
    }
    if scale > 0.0 {
        scale_curve.push([d as f64, scale]);
    }
    d *= 2;
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Digit count d", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "FFT length L", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Coefficient bound d(B−1)² [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "True maximum coefficient error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum nearest-integer distance [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Incorrectly rounded coefficients [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Incorrect product digits [digits]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
emit_text!(format!(
    "Digit counts excluded from the plot for zero true error: {zero_points}"
));
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "True maximum coefficient error",
            "points": true_curve,
            "style": { "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Maximum nearest-integer distance",
            "points": nearest_curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dotted" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Guide ε‖u‖₂‖v‖₂log₂L",
            "points": scale_curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Digit count d [digits]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute coefficient error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.5, "label": "Recovery condition 1/2" }]
    })
);
```

At the default $B=10^5$, true maximum coefficient error is only $3.9\times10^{-3}$ even at $d=4096$ ($L=8192$), with no incorrect digits.
At $B=10$ it is $2.9\times10^{-11}$ for the same digit count.
Error grows approximately 100-fold when the base grows tenfold and approximately twofold when digit count doubles.
$\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2$ is approximately $dB^2/3$, comparable to the largest coefficient (approximately $dB^2/4$), so error is roughly proportional to coefficient magnitude.
The dashed guide $\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2\log_2L$ lies at 1.4–10 times the measured errors at every point for $B=10$, $10^5$, $10^6$, and $10^7$.
Measured error is a few ulps of the largest coefficient (the spacing of adjacent `f64` values), explaining why table entries are small integer multiples of $2^{-k}$.

At $B=10^6$, error reaches 0.375 for $d=4096$, just below $1/2$.
The largest coefficient then is approximately $1.0\times10^{15}\approx2^{50}$, only $1/8$ of $2^{53}$.
At $B=10^7$, error is 0.375 for $d=32$ and exactly 0.5 for $d=64$; at $d=128$, 51 coefficients round incorrectly, giving 51 incorrect product digits.
At $d=128$ the largest coefficient is approximately $3.2\times10^{15}$, below $2^{52}$, so true coefficients are exactly representable in `f64`.
The digit errors arise from intermediate FFT rounding rather than storage error; guide $\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2\log_2L$ reaches 3.6 here.
Integer recovery for every input requires $d(B-1)^2\le2^{53}$ but also choosing base and digit count so the guide is well below $1/2$.

Even in rows whose error exceeds $1/2$, nearest-integer distance stays at most 0.5 and becomes 0 at $d\ge1024$.
Every `f64` value at least $2^{53}$ is an integer, so large FFT coefficients may themselves be integers without being the correct integers.
In large computations where true error cannot be measured, a small nearest-integer distance does not establish correctness.

The table applies to the displayed random digits; changing the seed changes maximum error within a similar range.
FFT work is $O(L\log L)$, but this cell's checking double sum costs $O(d^2)$ and is much more expensive than the FFT for $d=4096$.

## Summary

For FFT integer multiplication, zero-pad digit sequences to $L\ge2d-1$ and compute linear-convolution coefficients with an unscaled forward transform and inverse $1/L$.
A sufficient condition for correct nearest-integer rounding is true coefficient error below $1/2$; nearest-integer distance cannot replace it.
Measured errors are 1/10–1/1.4 of guide $\varepsilon\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2\log_2L$, growing approximately 100-fold for a tenfold base increase and twofold for doubled digit count.
With `f64` and $B=10^5$, even 4096 digits give error $3.9\times10^{-3}$; at $B=10^7$, FFT rounding corrupts digits even for 128-digit inputs whose coefficients are below $2^{52}$.
Agreement here checks the chosen inputs; a guarantee additionally requires an algorithm meeting an error bound such as Percival's theorem or checks using residues.
