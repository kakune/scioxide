The two-sided spectrum of an infinite sinusoid $x(t) = A\sin(2\pi f_0 t)$ has lines only at $\pm f_0$.
A computer can handle only samples extracted over finite time $[0, T)$.
Extraction multiplies by a window, corresponding to convolution with the window spectrum in frequency space.
When an integer number of periods does not fit the observation interval, energy spreads into surrounding frequency bins.
This is called spectral leakage.

This page uses a one-second vibrometer record (sampling frequency 128 Hz, $N = 128$ samples, bin spacing 1 Hz) to measure rectangular- and Hann-window leakage and amplitude correction.
Let acceleration amplitude be $A = 1\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

## Leakage and the Dirichlet kernel

Consider sinusoid samples $x_n = \sin(2\pi\alpha n/N)$ ($n = 0, \dots, N-1$) with $\alpha = f_0 T$ periods during observation time $T = N\Delta t$.
Combining the [DFT of one complex sinusoid](oxref:fft-spectral-leakage) for positive and negative frequencies gives DFT $X_k = \sum_n x_n e^{-2\pi i kn/N}$ as

$$
X_k = \frac{1}{2i}\left[D_N(\alpha - k) - D_N(-\alpha - k)\right], \qquad
D_N(u) = e^{i\pi u (N-1)/N}\,\frac{\sin(\pi u)}{\sin(\pi u/N)}
$$

the expression above.
$D_N$ is the Dirichlet kernel, with $D_N(0) = N$.
This formula also holds for noninteger $k$ and expresses the sequence's discrete-time Fourier transform at frequency $k$ measured in bins.
DFT bins sample this continuous curve at integer $k$.

1. With integer periods ($\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}$, $0 < \alpha < N/2$), only bin $k = \alpha$ and negative-frequency bin $k = N - \alpha$ remain; numerator $\sin(\pi(\pm\alpha - k))$ vanishes at other integer bins.
   Within the $0 \le k \le N/2$ range returned by `rfft`, only bin $k = \alpha$ remains.
2. With noninteger periods ($\alpha \notin \mathbb{Z}$), the numerator is nonzero at every integer bin.
   For half-integer $\alpha = k_0 + 1/2$, the numerator becomes $\pm 1$, while bin magnitudes decay only as $1/\lvert\alpha - k\rvert$ even far from the peak.
   The rectangular window's largest sidelobe is only about 13.3 dB below the main lobe (amplitude factor 0.22).

## Hann windows and amplitude correction

Applying a window that smoothly approaches 0 at both endpoints reduces leakage into distant bins.
DFT analysis uses the periodic Hann window

$$
w_n = \frac{1}{2}\left[1 - \cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{N}\right)\right], \qquad 0 \le n < N
$$

shown above.
Multiplication by $\cos(2\pi n/N)$ shifts frequency by $\pm 1$ bin, so the windowed spectrum is the 3-term sum of rectangular-window spectra

$$
X_k^{\mathrm{Hann}} = -\frac{1}{4}X_{k-1} + \frac{1}{2}X_k - \frac{1}{4}X_{k+1}
$$

shown above.
Far away, the 3 terms cancel, making sidelobes decay as $\lvert k - \alpha\rvert^{-3}$ (18 dB per octave), with the largest sidelobe about 31.5 dB down.
In exchange, the main lobe doubles in width, reducing the ability to separate nearby frequencies.
The symmetric Hann window with denominator $N-1$ has both endpoint values 0 and suits filter design; the 3-term formula above is then approximate.

Windowing reduces signal magnitude and therefore underestimates peak amplitude without correction.
The window mean

$$
\mathrm{CG} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} w_n
$$

is called coherent gain.
Adjusting the one-sided amplitude in [Conversion to physical amplitude](oxref:fft-physical-amplitude) to $2\lvert X_k\rvert/(N\,\mathrm{CG})$ recovers bin-centered sinusoid amplitude $A$ (neglecting the negative-frequency term's contribution).
The periodic Hann window has $\mathrm{CG} = 1/2$.
For sinusoids between bins, main-lobe shape causes additional peak-amplitude reduction.
This is called scalloping loss; at a half-integer offset, amplitude is multiplied by $2/\pi \approx 0.64$ (3.9 dB) for a rectangular window or approximately 0.85 (1.4 dB) for Hann.

## Measuring leakage

The following cell windows a sinusoid of $\alpha$ periods (frequency $f_0 = \alpha$ Hz) and computes one-sided bins with `rfft`.
`rfft` returns $N/2 + 1$ bins in an `HermitianSpectrum`, accessed through `bins()`.
Compute the frequency axis with `rfftfreq(N, Δt)`.
Double one-sided bin amplitudes except at DC and Nyquist, then divide by coherent gain.
Distant leakage is the fraction of power in bins $\lvert k - \alpha\rvert > 3$; power also includes one-sided weights.

As an independent check, compute bins from the Dirichlet-kernel formula above (a 3-term sum for Hann) and compare with `rfft`.
Overlay the same closed form evaluated every 1/16 bin as a dashed continuous curve, showing bins as samples of it.
Window coefficients, signal, and closed-form trigonometric functions use intrinsic f64 methods.

```rust
//| id: fft-leakage-windows
//| ref: fft-leakage-windows
//| title: "Measure spectral leakage by varying periods and windows"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   cycles: {"type":"range","label":"Periods in the observation interval α (= f₀ [Hz])","description":"Integer values have no leakage; half-integers maximize leakage and peak reduction.","min":4,"max":8,"step":0.25,"value":5.5}
//|   window: {"type":"select","label":"Window function","options":["Rectangular window","Hann window (periodic)"],"value":"Rectangular window"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"hann","label":"Hann window","description":"Distant leakage drops by a factor of 400, while the main lobe doubles in width.","inputs":{"window":"Hann window (periodic)"}}
//|   - {"id":"integer-cycles","label":"α = 6","description":"For integer periods, even a rectangular window has no leakage and returns amplitude 1.","inputs":{"cycles":6}}
//|   - {"id":"quarter-hann","label":"α = 5.25, Hann window","description":"A quarter-bin offset has less scalloping loss than a half-bin offset.","inputs":{"cycles":5.25,"window":"Hann window (periodic)"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":5,"atol":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.6662289344268328,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.0514735844984633,"rtol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: hann
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.8490371647027412,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.2819648927252093e-4,"rtol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: integer-cycles
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":6,"atol":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-26}}
//|   - case: quarter-hann
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.9603374284333767,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"leakage","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_fft::{Normalization, rfft, rfftfreq};

// 128 samples, sampling frequency 128 Hz (observation time 1 s, bin spacing 1 Hz), amplitude A = 1 m/s²
let n = 128_usize;
let size = n as f64;
let dt = 1.0 / 128.0;
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let hann = window != "Rectangular window";
// Periodic Hann window w_n = (1 − cos(2πn/N)) / 2 (coefficients generated with f64 cos)
let weights: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| {
        if hann {
            0.5 * (1.0 - (2.0 * pi * j as f64 / size).cos())
        } else {
            1.0
        }
    })
    .collect();
let signal: Vec<f64> = weights
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, w)| w * (2.0 * pi * cycles * j as f64 / size).sin())
    .collect();
let coherent_gain = weights.iter().sum::<f64>() / size;

// Main computation: unscaled rfft and frequency axis
let spectrum = rfft(&signal, Normalization::Unscaled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let frequencies = rfftfreq(n, dt).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// One-sided weights: 1 at DC and Nyquist, 2 elsewhere
let side = |k: usize| if k == 0 || 2 * k == n { 1.0 } else { 2.0 };

// Independent check: closed form using Dirichlet kernel D_N(u) (3-term sum for Hann). ν is frequency in bins
let kernel = |u: f64| {
    let ratio = if u == 0.0 {
        size
    } else {
        (pi * u).sin() / (pi * u / size).sin()
    };
    let phase = pi * u * (size - 1.0) / size;
    Complex::new(ratio * phase.cos(), ratio * phase.sin())
};
let rectangle = |nu: f64| {
    let d = kernel(cycles - nu) - kernel(-cycles - nu);
    Complex::new(d.im / 2.0, -d.re / 2.0) // d / (2i)
};
let closed = |nu: f64| {
    if hann {
        rectangle(nu) * 0.5 - (rectangle(nu - 1.0) + rectangle(nu + 1.0)) * 0.25
    } else {
        rectangle(nu)
    }
};

let amplitude = |k: usize, z: Complex<f64>| side(k) * z.norm() / (size * coherent_gain);
let mut bins = Vec::new();
let (mut peak, mut total, mut far, mut mismatch) = ((0.0_f64, 0.0_f64), 0.0, 0.0, 0.0_f64);
for (k, &z) in spectrum.bins().iter().enumerate() {
    let value = amplitude(k, z);
    if value > peak.1 {
        peak = (frequencies[k], value);
    }
    total += side(k) * z.norm_sqr();
    if (k as f64 - cycles).abs() > 3.0 {
        far += side(k) * z.norm_sqr();
    }
    mismatch = mismatch.max((z - closed(k as f64)).norm() / size);
    bins.push([frequencies[k], value]);
}
// Exclude and count exactly zero-amplitude bins, which cannot be plotted on a log axis
let plotted: Vec<[f64; 2]> = bins.iter().copied().filter(|b| b[1] > 0.0).collect();
let dropped = bins.len() - plotted.len();
// Continuous closed-form curve (1/16-bin spacing; also double DC and Nyquist for a smooth curve)
let curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=64 * 16)
    .map(|i| i as f64 / 16.0)
    .map(|nu| [nu, 2.0 * closed(nu).norm() / (size * coherent_gain)])
    .filter(|p| p[1] > 0.0)
    .collect();

emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "rfft bins",
            "points": plotted,
            "style": { "showSymbol": true, "lineType": "dotted" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Closed form (continuous)",
            "points": curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Frequency [Hz]",
        "yLabel": "One-sided amplitude [m/s²]",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 64.0,
        "yMin": 1e-17,
        "yMax": 2.0,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": cycles, "label": "Signal frequency f₀" }]
    })
);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed0 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 0 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Window" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Coherent gain [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum bin [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed0 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Peak amplitude [m/s²]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Distant-leakage fraction [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Maximum closed-form difference / N [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Omitted zero-amplitude bins [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let row = (
    window.clone(),
    coherent_gain,
    peak.0,
    peak.1,
    far / total,
    mismatch,
    dropped,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "leakage" })
);
```

At default $\alpha = 5.5$ (half-integer) with a rectangular window, the peak occurs in the 5 Hz bin and amplitude falls to 0.666 m/s².
It is slightly above $2/\pi = 0.637$ because the negative-frequency term also contributes to this bin.
Bins more than 3 bins from $\alpha$ contain leakage of $5.1\times 10^{-2}$ of total power.
At the same $\alpha$ with Hann, coherent gain is 0.5, peak amplitude 0.849 m/s², and distant leakage $1.3\times 10^{-4}$, about 400 times smaller.
The plot shows Hann sidelobes falling faster than rectangular-window sidelobes and the main lobe spanning 2 bins on each side of the peak.
For Hann at $\alpha = 5.25$, the quarter-bin offset gives peak amplitude 0.960 m/s².

At integers such as $\alpha = 6$, even a rectangular window gives peak amplitude 1 m/s² and distant leakage only of order $10^{-30}$ (squared rounding error).
Other plotted bins are of order $10^{-16}$, lying at zeros of the dashed continuous curve.
In every case the maximum `rfft`–closed-form difference divided by $N$ is at most $10^{-15}$, checking normalization and one-sided weighting against the formulas.

## Summary

Sinusoids with noninteger observed periods leak into surrounding bins with Dirichlet-kernel shape; rectangular-window leakage decays only inversely with frequency separation.
The periodic Hann spectrum is a 3-term sum of rectangular-window spectra, greatly reducing distant leakage while doubling main-lobe width.
Read amplitudes after dividing by coherent gain, accounting for remaining scalloping loss between bins (down to about 0.64 of amplitude for rectangular windows and 0.85 for Hann).
Obtain `rfft` bins through `HermitianSpectrum::bins()` and frequencies through `rfftfreq`; check normalization and one-sided weighting with integer-period signals or Dirichlet-kernel closed forms.
