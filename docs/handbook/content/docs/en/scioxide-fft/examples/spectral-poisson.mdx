Expanding a periodic Poisson equation in a Fourier basis reduces it to division at each wavenumber.
It can be solved on an $N\times N$ grid in $O(N^2\log N)$ operations without assembling a matrix, but the caller must decide the right-side mean and constant part of the solution.

This example solves $-\Delta u=f$ on $[0,2\pi)^2$ with `rfftn` and `irfftn`.
Use manufactured solutions, choosing the solution first and computing its right side, to measure differences between numerical and known solutions.
Compare a finitely representable Fourier solution with one containing infinitely many modes to distinguish exponential grid-refinement convergence from the rounding floor.
Coordinates $x,y$, solution $u$, and right side $f$ are all dimensionless.

[Reordering and fill in two-dimensional Poisson matrices](../../../scioxide-sparse/examples/poisson-reordering-fill/) in scioxide-sparse treats the same two-dimensional equation with direct methods on a difference matrix.
[Following a Poisson problem from conservation laws to grid convergence](../../../scioxide-pde/examples/poisson-grid/) in scioxide-pde studies difference, finite-element, and finite-volume convergence orders for a one-dimensional Poisson equation.
This page focuses on periodic boundaries and spectral methods.

## Diagonalization in a Fourier basis

For the period-$2\pi$ basis $e^{i(k_xx+k_yy)}$ (integer $k_x,k_y$), the eigenvalue of $-\Delta$ is $k_x^2+k_y^2$.
Define Fourier-series coefficients by

$$
\widehat f_{k_x,k_y}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(x,y)e^{-i(k_xx+k_yy)}\,dx\,dy
$$

the expression above; for nonzero wavenumbers,

$$
\widehat u_{k_x,k_y}=\frac{\widehat f_{k_x,k_y}}{k_x^2+k_y^2}
$$

the relation above follows.
The DFT of samples at grid points $x_i=2\pi i/N$, $y_j=2\pi j/N$ equals $N^2$ times the Fourier coefficient for wavenumbers below $N/2$ plus aliased high-wavenumber coefficients.
Applying the same $1/(k_x^2+k_y^2)$ to DFT bins and inverse-transforming gives the same division over grid-representable wavenumbers.
With forward `Normalization::Unscaled` and inverse `Normalization::Inverse`, the inverse removes round-trip scaling $N^2$.

## Zero-mean condition and solution constant

At zero wavenumber the eigenvalue is 0, so existence of a periodic solution requires

$$
\widehat f_{0,0}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f\,dx\,dy=0
$$

the condition above: the right side has zero mean.
Any constant can be added to a solution; here choose the zero-mean solution by setting $\widehat u_{0,0}=0$.

For the discrete equation, the condition is zero sample mean of the right side.
Setting the zero-wavenumber bin to 0 solves $-\Delta u=f-\bar f$, with sample mean $\bar f$ subtracted from the right side.
Even for $\bar f\ne0$, computation succeeds and silently returns a solution that does not satisfy the original equation.
Checking the right-side mean is therefore the caller's responsibility.
The following experiment provides an input adding constant $c$ to the right side to examine this behavior.

## Real-spectrum storage and wavenumbers

Transforming a real array of shape $[N,N]$ (row-major, axis 0 = $x$, axis 1 = $y$) with `axes = &[0, 1]` packs the last selected axis, axis 1.
The returned `HermitianSpectrumNd` owns bins of packed shape $[N,\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1]$, logical shape $[N,N]$, and the axes.
Axis 0 retains positive and negative wavenumbers; axis 1 retains only nonnegative wavenumbers.
Edit bins in place through `bins_mut` and borrow the same spectrum into `irfftn` to obtain a real array of logical shape in a new `Vec`.

Obtain wavenumbers by passing sample spacing $1/N$ to `fftfreq` and `rfftfreq`.
This is spacing in normalized coordinate $x/(2\pi)$ of period 1, so returned values are integer wavenumbers directly.
Physical-coordinate spacing $2\pi/N$ instead gives cycles per unit length $k/(2\pi)$, requiring multiplication by $2\pi$ to recover angular wavenumbers.

Multiplier $1/(k_x^2+k_y^2)$ is real and unchanged under wavenumber-sign reversal, preserving Hermitian symmetry $\widehat u_{-k}=\overline{\widehat u_k}$.
`irfftn` checks zero-frequency and Nyquist planes of the packed axis for Hermitian symmetry within rounding tolerance, returning `InvalidInput` on failure.
A multiplier breaking symmetry (for example, a coefficient varying with wavenumber sign) may fail this check.

## Manufactured solutions

The first solution $u_1=\sin(mx)\cos(ny)$ is a single mode, with right side $f_1=(m^2+n^2)u_1$.
For $m,n<N/2$ it is exactly representable on the grid, so error comes only from function evaluation and FFT rounding.
At $m=N/2$, grid-point $\sin(mx_i)=0$ makes both samples and the known solution 0.
Grid-point error remains small, but values between points are unrepresented and invisible to grid-point-only error measurements.

The second solution is

$$
u_2=e^{a\cos(mx)}\cos(ny)
$$

as above; generating function $e^{a\cos\theta}=I_0(a)+2\sum_{k\ge1}I_k(a)\cos(k\theta)$ gives infinitely many modes of wavenumber $km$ along $x$.
Here $I_k$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind, decaying faster than factorial as $I_k(a)\approx(a/2)^k/k!$ for large $k$.
Analytic differentiation gives

$$
f_2=e^{a\cos(mx)}
\left[n^2+a m^2\cos(mx)-a^2m^2\sin^2(mx)\right]\cos(ny)
$$

the expression above.
Coefficients of unrepresented wavenumbers $km\ge N/2$ cause truncation and aliasing error, so error should decay faster than exponentially with $N$ and stop near rounding floor $10^{-15}$.
Increasing $a$ slows coefficient decay and increases the $N$ needed to reach the floor.

Both $f_1$ and $f_2$ have zero mean (because $\cos(ny)$ averages to 0 along $y$).

## Grid-refinement experiment

The following cell increases points per axis as $N=8,12,16,\ldots$ up to the limit, measuring maximum absolute and RMS errors for both manufactured solutions.
Evaluate manufactured solutions and right sides with `f64` trigonometric and exponential functions as references independent of the FFT.
The table shows only power-of-2 $N$ and final-limit rows, alongside right-side and numerical-solution sample means.
The plot shows maximum absolute error versus $N$ on a logarithmic vertical axis, excluding zero-error points and reporting their count.
The heatmap shows the numerical $u_2$ on the finest grid using a diverging color scale centered at 0.

```rust
//| id: fft-spectral-poisson
//| ref: fft-spectral-poisson
//| title: "Periodic Poisson solves with single- and multiple-mode manufactured solutions"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   grid_size: {"type":"range","label":"Maximum points per axis N","min":16,"max":96,"step":16,"value":64}
//|   wave_m: {"type":"range","label":"Fundamental x-wavenumber m","description":"At m = N/2 the single mode disappears from the grid.","min":1,"max":4,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   wave_n: {"type":"range","label":"y-wavenumber n","min":1,"max":4,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   amplitude: {"type":"range","label":"Multimode solution amplitude a","description":"Larger values retain high-wavenumber coefficients and converge more slowly.","min":0.5,"max":4,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//|   rhs_offset: {"type":"range","label":"Constant c added to the right side","description":"Nonzero values violate the zero-mean condition.","min":0,"max":0.5,"step":0.1,"value":0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"steep","label":"a = 4","description":"Coefficients decay slowly, failing to reach the rounding floor even at N = 64.","inputs":{"amplitude":4}}
//|   - {"id":"high-harmonics","label":"m = 4","description":"Harmonic wavenumbers double, leaving error above the rounding floor even at N = 64.","inputs":{"wave_m":4}}
//|   - {"id":"offset","label":"c = 0.3","description":"Even with right-side mean 0.3, error and solution mean stay unchanged.","inputs":{"rhs_offset":0.3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/6/0","equals":64}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/6/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/5","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-14}}
//|   - case: steep
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/2","range":{"min":1e-12}}
//|   - case: high-harmonics
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/2","range":{"min":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/6/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: offset
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/4","approx":{"value":0.3,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/5","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_fft::{Normalization, fftfreq, irfftn, rfftfreq, rfftn};

let (limit, m, q, a, c) = (grid_size as usize, wave_m, wave_n, amplitude, rhs_offset);
let tau = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI;
let names = ["Single mode", "Multiple modes"];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut curves = [Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
let (mut zero_points, mut heat) = (0_usize, Vec::new());
for n in (8..=limit).step_by(4) {
    for model in 0..2 {
        // Reference values: evaluate manufactured u and right side f with intrinsic f64 methods (row-major [x, y]).
        let (mut f, mut exact) = (Vec::with_capacity(n * n), Vec::with_capacity(n * n));
        for i in 0..n {
            let x = tau * i as f64 / n as f64;
            let (s, cx) = ((m * x).sin(), (m * x).cos());
            for j in 0..n {
                let cy = (q * (tau * j as f64 / n as f64)).cos();
                let (u, rhs) = if model == 0 {
                    (s * cy, (m * m + q * q) * s * cy)
                } else {
                    let u = (a * cx).exp() * cy;
                    (u, (q * q + a * m * m * cx - a * a * m * m * s * s) * u)
                };
                exact.push(u);
                f.push(rhs + c);
            }
        }
        let mut spectrum =
            rfftn(&f, &[n, n], &[0, 1], Normalization::Unscaled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        // Passing normalized-coordinate spacing 1/N returns integer wavenumbers.
        let kx = fftfreq(n, 1.0 / n as f64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let ky = rfftfreq(n, 1.0 / n as f64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let width = ky.len();
        for (row, &x) in spectrum.bins_mut().chunks_mut(width).zip(&kx) {
            for (bin, &y) in row.iter_mut().zip(&ky) {
                let k2 = x * x + y * y;
                // Set zero-wavenumber û = 0 (zero-mean solution) and all other coefficients to f̂ / |k|².
                *bin = if k2 == 0.0 {
                    Complex::new(0.0, 0.0)
                } else {
                    *bin / k2
                };
            }
        }
        let solution = irfftn(&spectrum, Normalization::Inverse).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let (mut max_error, mut sum_sq) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
        for (&u, &v) in solution.iter().zip(&exact) {
            max_error = max_error.max((u - v).abs());
            sum_sq += (u - v) * (u - v);
        }
        let points = (n * n) as f64;
        if n.is_power_of_two() || n == limit {
            rows.push((
                n,
                names[model],
                max_error,
                (sum_sq / points).sqrt(),
                f.iter().sum::<f64>() / points,
                solution.iter().sum::<f64>() / points,
            ));
        }
        if max_error > 0.0 {
            curves[model].push([n as f64, max_error]);
        } else {
            zero_points += 1;
        }
        if n == limit && model == 1 {
            heat = (0..n * n)
                .map(|k| {
                    let (x, y) = (
                        tau * (k / n) as f64 / n as f64,
                        tau * (k % n) as f64 / n as f64,
                    );
                    serde_json::json!([x, y, solution[k]])
                })
                .collect();
        }
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Points per axis N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Manufactured solution" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum absolute error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "RMS error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Right-side sample mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Solution sample mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
emit_text!(format!("Zero-error points excluded from the plot: {zero_points}"));
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": names[0],
            "points": curves[0],
            "style": { "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": names[1],
            "points": curves[1],
            "style": { "symbol": "diamond", "showSymbol": true }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Points per axis N [points]",
        "yLabel": "Maximum absolute error [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 8.0,
        "xMax": 96.0
    })
);
// Fix the color range at ±e^a, centered at 0.
let bound = a.exp();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "heatmap",
    "title": "Numerical multimode solution u₂ (finest grid)",
    "data": heat,
    "xLabel": "x [-]",
    "yLabel": "y [-]",
    "colorScale": "diverging",
    "center": 0.0,
    "colorMin": -bound,
    "colorMax": bound,
    "tooltip": true
}));
```

At the defaults $m=2$, $n=3$, $a=2$, maximum absolute single-mode error remains around $2\times10^{-15}$ for every $N$.
For $N=8,16,32,64$, multimode errors are 1.94, $5.27\times10^{-2}$, $5.04\times10^{-6}$, and $1.87\times10^{-14}$, respectively, reaching the same rounding floor as the single mode at $N=64$.
Each doubling of $N$ reduces error by 1.6, 4.0, then 8.4 decimal orders; the number of orders itself increases (the last value reflects stopping at the rounding floor).
This contrasts with algebraic difference-method error decay such as $N^{-2}$.
Increasing $N=64$ to 96 leaves error at $1.9\times10^{-14}$; further refinement gives no accuracy improvement.

At $a=4$, error remains $3.4\times10^{-10}$ even at $N=64$; at $a=0.5$ it falls to $1.4\times10^{-11}$ at $N=32$.
At $m=4$, $x$-direction harmonic wavenumbers double, halving the harmonics represented by the same $N$; error at $N=64$ is $5.2\times10^{-6}$.
Truncation/aliasing error dominates the descending part of each curve, and rounding dominates the flat part.

Right-side sample means are at most $10^{-14}$ for $c=0$, and numerical-solution means at most $10^{-16}$, satisfying both the zero-mean condition and the chosen solution convention.
Setting $c=0.3$ makes the right-side mean 0.3, yet error differs from the $c=0$ case only at rounding level, and solution mean remains 0.
Zeroing the zero-wavenumber bin makes the computed solution solve $-\Delta u=f-0.3$, leaving residual 0.3 everywhere in the original equation $-\Delta u=f$.
Small error alone does not reveal this mismatch.

The heatmap has $m$ peaks along $x$ from factor $e^{a\cos(mx)}$, reaching maximum absolute value $e^a$ on columns where $\cos(mx)=1$.
Along $y$ the sign alternates for $n$ periods following $\cos(ny)$, matching the manufactured solution's shape.

## Summary

The periodic Poisson equation reduces through real FFTs to nonzero-wavenumber divisions $\widehat u=\widehat f/\lvert k\rvert^2$, solved on an $N\times N$ grid in $O(N^2\log N)$ operations.
A zero-mean right side is the solvability condition; zeroing its zero-wavenumber bin silently solves the mean-subtracted problem, so the caller must check the right-side mean.
A grid-representable single mode has rounding-level error; an analytic solution with infinitely many modes converges faster than exponentially under refinement until reaching the rounding floor.
Wavenumber division preserves Hermitian symmetry, so edit `rfftn` packed bins in place and pass them to `irfftn`.
