Vibration and acoustic measurements sometimes require examining nearby frequency components in a narrow band.
`zoom_fft` evaluates a finite sample sequence's discrete-time Fourier transform (DTFT) at equally spaced frequencies in a specified band.
Increasing frequency-point count and extending observation time affect the spectrum differently.

This example synthesizes a 120 Hz sinusoid and a nearby sinusoid, varying observation time, frequency separation, and evaluation spacing.
Check zoom FFT values with DTFT direct sums and a zero-padded FFT.
Examine how resolution is approximately the inverse observation time $1/T$, while zoom FFT only refines the frequency grid.

## Observation time and resolution

Taking $N$ samples at sampling frequency $f_s$ gives observation time $T=N/f_s$ and ordinary DFT bin spacing $f_s/N=1/T$.
This is the spacing of DFT evaluation points, not the minimum spacing at which the DTFT can be evaluated.

Finite-time observation multiplies an infinite signal by a rectangular window of duration $T$.
Even a single sinusoid's spectrum spreads into the window's Fourier-transform shape, with distance $1/T$ from main-lobe center to the first zero.
When component separation $\Delta f$ is smaller than this width, the main lobes overlap and appear as one peak.
$\Delta f\approx1/T$ is a guide to separation.

Whether two peaks appear is not determined by $\Delta f\ge1/T$ alone.
It also depends on window shape, relative amplitude, phase, noise, and the separation criterion; fitting known models may estimate components closer than $1/T$.
Here examine amplitude spectra of two equal-amplitude, equal-initial-phase cosines using a rectangular window.

Zero-padded FFTs and zoom FFTs evaluate the same finite sequence's DTFT at finer points.
They reveal peak shapes in detail but add neither observations nor observation time, leaving main-lobe widths unchanged.
Extending observation time narrows the main lobe itself.

## Calling zoom FFT

At unit-circle frequencies $f_k=\mathrm{start}+k\Delta$ ($0\le k<m$), `zoom_fft(input, start, end, m, sample_rate, endpoint, normalization)` returns

$$
X(f_k)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_j\,e^{-2\pi i jf_k/f_s}
$$

the expression above.
With true `endpoint`, the grid has spacing $\Delta=(\mathrm{end}-\mathrm{start})/(m-1)$ and includes the endpoint.
Internally, [Bluestein's identity](oxref:fft-bluestein-czt) converts the chirp Z-transform (CZT) to convolution with a chirp sequence, computed by FFTs of length $L=$ `next_fast_len`$(N+m-1)$.
One call, including the kernel transform, uses 3 FFTs of length $L$ plus $O(N+m)$ work.
Input is borrowed and results returned in a new `Vec` of length $m$.
For many transforms of the same input length, create `CztPlan` once and reuse workspace to reduce each evaluation to 2 FFTs.

The unit-circle-contour error bound gives each output's absolute error proportional to the sum of absolute input components ([Error bound](oxref:fft-czt-error)).
Outputs near zero can have large relative errors, so below compare absolute differences divided by common scale $N$.

## Normalization and comparison scale

Use unscaled forward transforms throughout and plot $|X(f)|/N$.
A unit-amplitude cosine has peak $|X|/N\approx1/2$ at $f$; do not apply the factor 2 for one-sided amplitude conversion.
The comparison denominator for a zero-padded FFT also uses original sample count $N$.
Dividing by the padded length would make the same signal's amplitude depend on padding amount.

## Comparing three methods

Below set $f_s=1000$ Hz and $f_1=120$ Hz, evaluating the band 115–128 Hz.
Zoom FFT evaluates $26s+1$ band points at spacing $0.5/s$ Hz.
The zero-padded FFT fixes length at 20000 and spacing at 0.05 Hz; measure differences only at frequencies exactly shared with zoom FFT.
Direct sums at 27 points spaced 0.5 Hz provide references independent of the FFT.
Trigonometric functions for synthesis and direct summation use intrinsic methods of `f64`.

The table's “valley-to-peak ratio” divides the minimum $|X|/N$ at evaluation points in $f_1\le f\le f_2$ by the maximum over the whole band.
It is small when two peaks are separated and close to 1 when they appear as one.

```rust
//| id: fft-zoom-tone-resolution
//| ref: fft-zoom-tone-resolution
//| title: "Narrowband spectra with varying observation time and evaluation-point count"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   sample_points: {"type":"range","label":"Samples N (T = N/1000 s)","description":"Change observation time and main-lobe width 1/T.","min":256,"max":1024,"step":256,"value":256}
//|   tone_f2: {"type":"range","label":"Second component frequency f₂ [Hz]","description":"The first component is fixed at 120 Hz.","min":121,"max":126,"step":0.5,"value":122.5}
//|   zoom_factor: {"type":"range","label":"Subdivisions s of a 0.5 Hz grid","description":"Zoom FFT spacing is 0.5/s Hz.","min":1,"max":10,"step":1,"value":10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"long-record","label":"N = 1024","description":"T = 1.024 s gives 1/T ≈ 0.98 Hz < Δf, creating a deep valley between peaks.","inputs":{"sample_points":1024}}
//|   - {"id":"wide-separation","label":"f₂ = 126 Hz","description":"At unchanged T = 0.256 s, Δf = 6 Hz > 1/T also creates a valley.","inputs":{"tone_f2":126}}
//|   - {"id":"coarse-grid","label":"s = 1","description":"Coarse 0.5 Hz grid. Valley depth changes very little.","inputs":{"zoom_factor":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":3.90625,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.05,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0.8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/7","equals":261}
//|   - case: long-record
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":0.3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: wide-separation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":0.5}}
//|   - case: coarse-grid
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.5,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0.8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/7","equals":27}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_fft::{Normalization, fft, zoom_fft};

let (fs, n, f1, f2) = (1000.0_f64, sample_points as usize, 120.0_f64, tone_f2);
let tau = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI;
// Input generation: two cosines of amplitude 1 and initial phase 0 (intrinsic f64 methods).
let input: Vec<Complex<f64>> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| {
        let t = j as f64 / fs;
        Complex::new((tau * f1 * t).cos() + (tau * f2 * t).cos(), 0.0)
    })
    .collect();
// Evaluate 115–128 Hz at 26 s + 1 endpoint-inclusive points (spacing 0.5/s Hz).
let (start, end, subdivisions) = (115.0_f64, 128.0_f64, zoom_factor as usize);
let m = 26 * subdivisions + 1;
let df = (end - start) / (m - 1) as f64;
let zoomed = zoom_fft(&input, start, end, m, fs, true, Normalization::Unscaled)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Comparison: FFT zero-padded to length 20000 (spacing 0.05 Hz).
let padded_len = 20_000_usize;
let mut padded = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); padded_len];
padded[..n].copy_from_slice(&input);
let full = fft(&padded, Normalization::Unscaled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut direct_error, mut padded_error, mut common) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0_usize);
let (mut zoom_curve, mut reference) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut peak, mut valley) = (0.0_f64, f64::INFINITY);
for (k, &value) in zoomed.iter().enumerate() {
    let f = start + k as f64 * df;
    let amplitude = value.norm() / n as f64;
    zoom_curve.push([f, amplitude]);
    peak = peak.max(amplitude);
    if (f1..=f2).contains(&f) {
        valley = valley.min(amplitude);
    }
    if k % subdivisions == 0 {
        // Independent check: DTFT defining sum Σ x_j exp(−2πi f j / f_s).
        let direct: Complex<f64> = input
            .iter()
            .enumerate()
            .map(|(j, &x)| {
                let phase = -tau * f * j as f64 / fs;
                x * Complex::new(phase.cos(), phase.sin())
            })
            .sum();
        direct_error = direct_error.max((value - direct).norm() / n as f64);
        reference.push([f, direct.norm() / n as f64]);
    }
    // Compare only points exactly on the zero-padded FFT grid (do not substitute nearest points).
    let bin = f * padded_len as f64 / fs;
    if (bin - bin.round()).abs() < 1e-8 {
        padded_error = padded_error.max((value - full[bin.round() as usize]).norm() / n as f64);
        common += 1;
    }
}
let first_bin = (start * padded_len as f64 / fs) as usize;
let last_bin = (end * padded_len as f64 / fs) as usize;
let padded_curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (first_bin..=last_bin)
    .map(|k| [k as f64 * fs / padded_len as f64, full[k].norm() / n as f64])
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "zoom FFT", "points": zoom_curve }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Zero-padded FFT (L = 20000)",
            "points": padded_curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "DTFT direct sum",
            "points": reference,
            "style": { "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true, "lineType": "dotted" }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Frequency f [Hz]",
        "yLabel": "Amplitude |X(f)|/N [-]",
        "xMin": 115.0,
        "xMax": 128.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.1,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": f1, "label": "f₁" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": f2, "label": "f₂" }
        ]
    })
);
let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Observation time T [s]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Frequency separation Δf [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Main-lobe guide 1/T [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Zoom spacing [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Valley-to-peak ratio [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Difference from direct sum / N [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Difference from zero-padded FFT / N [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Points shared with zero-padding [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let row = (
    n as f64 / fs,
    f2 - f1,
    fs / n as f64,
    df,
    valley / peak,
    direct_error,
    padded_error,
    common,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
```

At the defaults $N=256$ ($T=0.256$ s, $1/T=3.91$ Hz) and $\Delta f=2.5$ Hz, valley-to-peak ratio is 0.995, making both components appear as one peak.
Even reducing subdivisions to 1, for coarse spacing 0.5 Hz, gives ratio 0.996, nearly unchanged.
Increasing evaluation points tenfold does not separate the peak into two.

At $N=1024$ ($T=1.024$ s, $1/T=0.98$ Hz), the same $\Delta f=2.5$ Hz separation gives ratio 0.20, creating a deep valley.
Keeping $N=256$ and setting $f_2=126$ Hz ($\Delta f=6$ Hz) also gives ratio 0.40.
At $f_2=121$ Hz ($\Delta f=1$ Hz), ratio is 0.97 and the components remain unresolved at $N=256$.
Separation depends on the ratio between $\Delta f$ and $1/T$, not grid spacing.

Direct-sum difference $/N$ is $3.5\times10^{-15}$ at the default and $2.7\times10^{-14}$ at $N=1024$; zero-padded FFT differences are on the scale of $10^{-15}$.
Agreement with the zero-padded FFT curve checks that both evaluate the same finite sequence's DTFT.
Since `zoom_fft` and the zero-padded FFT share FFT components, defining-sum differences are measured separately.
Vertical lines mark both input frequencies, but overlapping peaks need not have amplitude maxima at those positions.

Work is 3 FFTs of length $L=$ `next_fast_len`$(N+m-1)$ for zoom FFT, one length-20000 FFT for zero-padding, and $Nm$ complex-exponential evaluations for direct summation.
At the default $N+m-1=516$, $L$ is 525, the smallest length at least that value with only prime factors 2, 3, 5, and 7.
For detailed evaluation of only a narrow band, zoom FFT uses shorter transforms than full-band zero-padding.

## Summary

Zoom FFT evaluates a finite sequence's DTFT at equally spaced points in a specified band, requiring a few FFTs of length around $N+m-1$.
More evaluation points reveal the curve in detail without changing observation time or the ability to separate nearby components.
Rectangular-window main-lobe width $1/T$ guides separation; making $1/T$ smaller than $\Delta f$ requires longer observation time.
Comparison with both direct sums and zero-padded FFTs distinguishes evaluation-point differences from computation error.
