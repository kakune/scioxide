A Fourier transform represents a signal or field observed in time or space as a superposition of sinusoids (complex exponentials), viewed in frequency space.
The discrete Fourier transform (DFT) converts $N$ samples to frequency components and requires $N^2$ complex multiplications when computed directly from the definition.
The fast Fourier transform (FFT), popularized by Cooley and Tukey, uses the factorization of $N$ to compute the same values in $O(N\log N)$ operations.

## Available transforms

Transforms in `scioxide-fft` operate on scalar types `f32` and `f64` (and their complex forms `Complex<f32>` and `Complex<f64>`).
There are 93 public items, all at the crate root.

- One-dimensional FFTs accept arbitrary lengths (`fft`, `ifft`, real-sequence `rfft` and `irfft`, and Hermitian-symmetric `hfft` and `ihfft`).
  Plans choose mixed-radix Cooley–Tukey, Rader, or Bluestein from the length's prime factors.
- Multidimensional FFTs take a row-major packed slice and shape and transform selected axes (`fftn`, `rfftn`, and others).
  Pass strided arrays to plan `FftNdPlan`.
- Discrete cosine transforms (DCTs) and discrete sine transforms (DSTs) have types I–IV (`dct`, `dst`, multidimensional `dctn` and `dstn`, and their inverses).
- Chirp Z-transforms evaluate the $z$-transform on spirals in the complex plane or parts of the unit circle (`czt`, `zoom_fft`, `CztPlan`).
- Nonuniform Fourier transforms (NUFFTs) approximate Fourier sums at irregular positions to requested accuracy (types 1, 2, and 3: `nufft_type1`, `nufft_type2`, `nufft_type3`, and `NufftPlan`).
- Radial transforms include the discrete Hankel transform on a Bessel-zero grid (`dht`, `idht`) and FFTLog on a logarithmic grid (`fht`, `ifht`).
- Functions for frequency axes and lengths include `fftfreq`, `rfftfreq`, `fftshift`, `ifftshift`, `next_fast_len`, and `prev_fast_len`.
- Repeated computations use plans (such as `FftPlan`), caller-owned workspaces (`FftWorkspace`), and a bounded LRU cache (`FftPlanCache`).
  `FftPlanningContext` handles CPU instruction-set selection and injection of external FFT implementations (`FftProvider`).

Fourier, DCT/DST, chirp Z-transform, and NUFFT functions and plans take normalization (`Normalization`) as an argument.
The discrete Hankel transform and FFTLog have their own integral conventions and do not take `Normalization`.
Every transform uses the supplied input length without implicit zero-padding or truncation.
The caller extends the array when zero-padding is needed.

Functions (such as `fft` and `rfft`) create a plan on every call and return results in a new `Vec` or similar container.
For repeated same-shape transforms, prepare a plan and workspace once and use `execute_into` to write into caller-owned output.
`execute_into` makes no heap allocation when given workspace meeting `workspace_requirements` and output of the correct length.
Plan and workspace preparation allocate; allocations for distributing work in a parallel executor and within an injected external implementation follow their respective contracts.

## Frequencies of two sinusoids

For the signal observed for 1 second ($N = 64$ points) at sampling frequency $f_s = 64$ Hz,

$$
x(t) = \sin(2\pi\cdot 5t) + 0.5\sin(2\pi f_2 t)
$$

compute the transform with `rfft`.
`rfft` returns $\lfloor N/2\rfloor + 1 = 33$ nonnegative-frequency bins $X_k$ in an `HermitianSpectrum`; `rfftfreq(N, Δt)` returns bin frequencies $k f_s/N$.
The forward transform with normalization `Normalization::Inverse` applies no factor, so read one-sided amplitudes as $w_k\lvert X_k\rvert/N$.
Here the one-sided weights $w_k$ are 1 at DC ($k = 0$) and Nyquist ($2k = N$), and 2 elsewhere.

The cell reports bins whose amplitude exceeds $10^{-9}$ in table `peaks`, and the bin count and both sides of Parseval's identity

$$
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n^2 = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2} w_k\lvert X_k\rvert^2
$$

in table `energy`.
The left side is an independent check summed directly from time-domain samples; the right side comes from FFT output.
Input $f_2$ is the second sinusoid's frequency [Hz]; values above 32 Hz (Nyquist) cause aliasing.

```rust
//| id: fft-first-spectrum
//| ref: fft-first-spectrum
//| title: "Read the frequencies and amplitudes of two sinusoids with rfft"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-fft]
//| inputs:
//|   second_hz: {"type":"range","label":"Second sinusoid frequency f₂ [Hz]","description":"Frequency of an amplitude-0.5 sinusoid added to the 5 Hz sinusoid. Above 32 Hz, it aliases to bin 64 − f₂.","min":1,"max":60,"step":1,"value":12}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"same-bin","label":"f₂ = 5 Hz","description":"Both sinusoids fall in the same bin, giving amplitude 1.5.","inputs":{"second_hz":5}}
//|   - {"id":"nyquist","label":"f₂ = 32 Hz","description":"All samples are sin(πn) ≈ 0, eliminating the second component.","inputs":{"second_hz":32}}
//|   - {"id":"aliased","label":"f₂ = 52 Hz","description":"Aliases to bin 64 − 52 = 12 Hz with phase +90 degrees.","inputs":{"second_hz":52}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":12,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":-90,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":40,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: same-bin
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":72,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: nyquist
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":32,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: aliased
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/1/0","equals":12}
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peaks","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":90,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_fft::{Normalization, rfft, rfftfreq};

// Sampling frequency 64 Hz, 64 points (observation time 1 s, bin spacing 1 Hz). Generate input with f64 sin.
let (count, sample_rate) = (64_usize, 64.0_f64);
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let samples: Vec<f64> = (0..count)
    .map(|n| {
        let t = n as f64 / sample_rate;
        (2.0 * pi * 5.0 * t).sin() + 0.5 * (2.0 * pi * second_hz * t).sin()
    })
    .collect();

// 33 nonnegative-frequency bins X_k and their frequencies [Hz].
let spectrum = rfft(&samples, Normalization::Inverse).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let frequencies = rfftfreq(count, 1.0 / sample_rate).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// One-sided weights w_k: 1 for DC and Nyquist, 2 elsewhere.
let weight = |k: usize| if k == 0 || 2 * k == count { 1.0 } else { 2.0 };
let peaks: Vec<(usize, f64, f64, f64)> = spectrum
    .bins()
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, z)| {
        let amplitude = weight(k) * z.norm() / count as f64;
        (k, frequencies[k], amplitude, z.arg() * 180.0 / pi)
    })
    .filter(|row| row.2 > 1e-9)
    .collect();

// Independent check: sum left side Σ x² directly from samples and right side (1/N) Σ w_k |X_k|² from FFT output.
let time_energy: f64 = samples.iter().map(|x| x * x).sum();
let frequency_energy = spectrum
    .bins()
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, z)| weight(k) * z.norm_sqr())
    .sum::<f64>()
    / count as f64;
let relative = ((frequency_energy - time_energy) / time_energy).abs();

let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let digits10 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let peak_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Bin k", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Frequency [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "One-sided amplitude [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Phase [degrees]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    peak_columns,
    peaks.clone(),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "peaks" })
);
let energy_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Logical length N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Bin count", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Bins with amplitude above 10⁻⁹", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Σ x² [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "(1/N) Σ w|X|² [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits10 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    energy_columns,
    vec![(
        spectrum.logical_len(),
        spectrum.bins().len(),
        peaks.len(),
        time_energy,
        frequency_energy,
        relative,
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "energy" })
);
```

At the default $f_2 = 12$ Hz, table `peaks` contains only bin 5 (5 Hz, amplitude 1.000000) and bin 12 (12 Hz, amplitude 0.500000), both with phase $-90$ degrees.
The remaining 31 bin amplitudes are at most $10^{-9}$.
$\sum x_n^2$ in table `energy` is $64\times(1/2 + 0.5^2/2) = 40$, and its relative difference from the FFT-derived right side is on the scale of rounding error.

Selecting cases reveals the following behavior.

- At $f_2 = 5$ Hz both sinusoids share a bin, giving bin-5 amplitude $1 + 0.5 = 1.5$ and $\sum x_n^2$ equal to 72.
- At $f_2 = 32$ Hz the second component's samples are $\sin(\pi n) = 0$ (rounding-scale values in generated input), leaving no sampled second component.
  Only the 5 Hz bin appears in the table, and $\sum x_n^2 = 32$.
- The $f_2 = 52$ Hz sinusoid aliases to the 12 Hz bin by $\sin(2\pi\cdot 52 n/64) = -\sin(2\pi\cdot 12 n/64)$, with phase $+90$ degrees.
  Samples alone cannot distinguish it from a sign-reversed 12 Hz sinusoid.

## Reading results

Bin $k$ has frequency $k f_s/N$; the spacing $f_s/N = 1$ Hz comes from the 1-second observation time.
Integer-Hz sinusoids fall exactly in one bin, with other bins at rounding-scale magnitude.
[Noninteger periods and spectral leakage](examples/spectral-leakage/) measures spreading (spectral leakage) for frequencies between bins.

Read one-sided amplitudes by applying normalization factor $1/N$ and one-sided weight $w_k$.
Since $\sin(2\pi f t)$ is $\cos(2\pi f t - \pi/2)$, its phase is $-90$ degrees.
Normalization changes the amplitude factor, so record the `Normalization` value with the result.

The relative difference in Parseval's identity checks preservation of the sum of squares using a time-domain sum independent of the FFT.
This check does not detect errors in frequency locations (an incorrect frequency axis).
Check the frequency axis separately with `rfftfreq` and known input frequencies.

`HermitianSpectrum` carries logical length 64 with the bins; `irfft` reconstructs a real sequence of that length.
33 bins can come from real sequences of length 64 or 65, so the inverse length cannot be determined without the logical length.
Components above 32 Hz (Nyquist) alias to lower-frequency bins as in the cases above.
[Sampling, aliasing, and the frequency axis](algorithms/sampling-and-frequency-axis/) derives sampling and aliasing.

This cell calls function `rfft` once.
For many transforms of the same length, prepare `FftPlan` and `FftWorkspace` once and repeat `execute_into` ([FFT plans, workspaces, and caches](algorithms/plans-workspaces-and-providers/)).

## Summary

`scioxide-fft` provides complex, real, and multidimensional FFTs, DCT/DST, chirp Z-transforms, nonuniform Fourier transforms, and radial transforms as functions and plans for `f32` and `f64`.
The result of `rfft` is an `HermitianSpectrum` carrying logical length; obtain bin frequencies with `rfftfreq` and read one-sided amplitudes as $w_k\lvert X_k\rvert/N$ (with normalization `Inverse`).
Components above Nyquist alias and cannot be distinguished from lower-frequency components using samples alone.
Zero-padding is explicit; for repeated computations, preparing a plan and workspace lets `execute_into` run without heap allocation.
