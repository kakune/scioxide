Uniform-sample Monte Carlo integration has standard error $\sigma/\sqrt N$, where $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the observations $V f(\boldsymbol X)$.
An integrand concentrated in a narrow region has a large $\sigma$, and most samples are spent where values are nearly 0.
Adaptive Monte Carlo methods examine the integrand with a small training sample and change the placement of evaluation samples to reduce variance.
This page covers VEGAS, which learns an importance-sampling proposal, and MISER, which learns a stratification of the domain.
Both exclude training samples from the evaluation estimator and use fresh random streams for evaluation.

Importance sampling averages observations $V f(\boldsymbol X)/q(\boldsymbol Y)$ at points $\boldsymbol X$ mapped to the box from points $\boldsymbol Y$ drawn from density $q$ on the unit cube; the estimator is unbiased regardless of $q$, with minimum variance when $q \propto |f|$.
[Importance-sampling proposals and effective sample size](../importance-sampling-proposals/) covers proposal conditions and interpreting effective sample size; [Monte Carlo integration estimators and statistics](../monte-carlo-integration/) covers uniform Monte Carlo estimators, standard errors, intervals, and `VarianceBasis`.

## Learning separable importance maps with VEGAS

VEGAS (Lepage) restricts $q$ to a product of one-dimensional axis densities $q(\boldsymbol y)=\prod_k q_k(y_k)$, representing each $q_k$ as a piecewise-constant density with $K$ bins, each having probability $1/K$.
For boundaries $0 = e_0 < e_1 < \dots < e_K = 1$ on axis $k$, a uniform random number $u$ falls in bin $j = \lfloor Ku \rfloor$ and maps linearly to $[e_j, e_{j+1})$.
The point density is

$$
q(\boldsymbol y) = \prod_k \frac{1}{K\,\Delta_k(\boldsymbol y)}
$$

Here $\Delta_k(\boldsymbol y)$ is the width of the bin containing $y_k$.
Narrower bins have higher densities.
The learned map `VegasMap` implements `MonteCarloProposal`, and `boundaries()` returns $K+1$ boundaries per axis in axis order.
The map is defined on the unit cube and used together with the box on which it was trained.

`VegasTrainer::train` starts from a uniform map and performs the specified number of iterations.
Each iteration draws $N$ points from the current map, evaluates $f$, and adds $(\|f\|/q)^2$ to the bin containing $y_k$ on each axis $k$.
$\|f\|$ is the absolute value for a real integrand and the Euclidean norm for multiple real components; all components share one map.
For each axis, update bin boundaries as follows.

1. Divide bin sums by their maximum, average with neighboring bins, and divide by the total to obtain $r_j$ with $\sum_j r_j = 1$.
2. Compress using damping exponent $\alpha$ as $w_j = \bigl((1 - r_j)/\ln(1/r_j)\bigr)^\alpha$ (bound $r_j$ below by machine epsilon).
3. Place new boundaries by linearly interpolating within current bins so each bin has equal total $w$.
4. Average new boundaries with current boundaries.

If an axis has no positive sum or its new boundaries are not strictly increasing, leave that axis unchanged.
Compression and averaging prevent sampling fluctuations in one iteration from drastically changing the map and collapsing bins.
Smaller $\alpha$ changes the map more slowly.

`VegasOptions::default()` uses 50 bins per axis, 5 iterations × 2048 samples, damping exponent $\alpha = 1.5$, and sequential execution; change these with `with_bins` (at least 2), `with_iterations`, `with_samples_per_iteration`, `with_damping` (finite and positive), and `with_execution_policy`.
For $d$ axes, $C$ components, and $K$ bins, one iteration costs $O(N(d + C) + dK)$ plus $N$ evaluations of $f$.
A product density adapts to axis-aligned structure, but for diagonal ridges or multiple peaks, the product of marginals also places density where the original features do not occur.

## Best-first recursive stratification with MISER

Partition the domain into disjoint subdomains (strata) $\Omega_c$ of volumes $V_c$, and independently average $n_c$ uniform samples in each stratum to obtain $\bar f_c$; the stratified estimator $\hat I=\sum_c V_c\bar f_c$ has variance $\sum_c V_c^2\sigma_c^2/n_c$, where $\sigma_c$ is the within-stratum standard deviation of $f$.
For fixed total sample count $n$, variance is minimized by $n_c \propto V_c\sigma_c$ (Neyman allocation).
MISER (Press–Farrar) learns stratum divisions and allocations from pilot samples.

`MiserTrainer::train` starts with the whole box as one stratum assigned $n$ evaluation samples, and repeats the following.

1. Among unexamined strata below the depth limit with at least four samples, choose the stratum with the largest estimated variance contribution $(V_c\sigma_c)^2/n_c$.
   $\sigma_c$ is the standard deviation of the parent stratum's pilot samples falling in that half.
   Initially choose the whole box; break ties in favor of the stratum created earlier.
2. Draw $p$ uniform pilot samples in the selected stratum.
   For each axis, compute sample standard deviations $\sigma_a$ and $\sigma_b$ in the halves split at the midpoint (the Euclidean norm of component standard deviations for multiple components).
3. Bisect on the axis minimizing $\sigma_a + \sigma_b$ (lowest axis index on ties), assign the lower half $n_a = \lfloor n_c\sigma_a/(\sigma_a+\sigma_b) \rfloor$ constrained to $[2, n_c - 2]$, and assign the upper half the remaining samples.
   The halves have equal volumes, so this is Neyman allocation.
   If $\sigma_a + \sigma_b = 0$, split the samples equally.
   If no axis has at least two pilot samples in each half, do not split the stratum.

Stop at the stratum-count limit or when no eligible stratum remains.
There are at most (stratum limit − 1) splits and at most $p$ evaluations per examined stratum.
Each stratum has at least two evaluation samples, and their total equals the evaluation sample count $n$.
This is a best-first search that refines strata with large variance contributions first, rather than a depth-first recursion over the whole domain as in the original Press–Farrar method.

`MiserOptions::default()` uses evaluation settings `MonteCarloOptions::default()` (4096 samples), 256 pilot samples per stratum, up to 16 strata, and depth 8; change these with `with_evaluation`, `with_pilot_samples` (at least 4), `with_max_leaves`, and `with_max_depth`.
The evaluation settings must specify one independent replicate.
The learned `MiserStrata` contains stratum boxes (`strata()`), evaluation sample counts per stratum (`samples()`), stratum count (`len()`), and evaluation settings (`options()`).

`MonteCarloIntegrator::stratified(strata)` evaluates stratum $c$ with $n_c$ uniform observations.
The estimate sums stratum estimates, variance sums stratum variances, the statistical basis is `VarianceBasis::Strata`, and degrees of freedom use the Welch–Satterthwaite effective value

$$
\nu = \frac{\bigl(\sum_c v_c\bigr)^2}{\sum_c v_c^2/(n_c - 1)}
$$

Here $v_c$ is the variance of the estimator in stratum $c$.
`estimator_variance()` returns an extended-exponent `Scaled` value.
The interval is conditional on the strata fixed by training.
If pilot samples never encounter a narrow peak, that stratum's variability is underestimated and its reported standard error is also too small.

## Separating training and evaluation

Training and evaluation are separate stages.

1. Construct a trainer with `VegasTrainer::new(&bounds, VegasOptions)` or `MiserTrainer::new(&bounds, MiserOptions)`.
2. Obtain a map or strata and a `TrainingReport` with `train(f, streams)` (the integrand's value type determines the component count).
3. Construct an evaluation integrator with `MonteCarloIntegrator::importance(&bounds, map, options)` or `MonteCarloIntegrator::stratified(strata)`.
4. Call `integrate_into` with a different random stream and read the workspace's `statistics()`.

Training samples do not enter the estimator, so the evaluation estimate is unbiased conditional on the learned map or strata.
Training and evaluation both draw from random block 0; using the same `stream` number of the same `RandomStreams` makes evaluation reuse training's uniform random numbers, so the map and samples are no longer independent.
Use a different `stream` number for evaluation than for training.

`TrainingReport` describes how training ended.
`is_success()` is true when all prescribed training completes; `termination()` is then `SampleBudgetCompleted`.
`evaluations()` counts training integrand calls, and `updates()` counts completed VEGAS iterations or MISER splits.
Include training evaluations when comparing evaluation costs.
If training stops at an evaluation limit, cancellation, or deadline after at least one update, it returns the last completed update's map or strata as `Ok` and records the stop in `TrainingReport`'s termination status.
Stopping before the first update returns `ConvergenceFailure` or `ExecutionStopped`.
A partially trained map is still a valid proposal usable for evaluation, but distinguish it from one that completed all prescribed training.

`train` is the convenience form that constructs workspace internally.
For repeated training with the same trainer, call `train_into` with the `VegasWorkspace` returned by `VegasTrainer::workspace(ExecutionContext)` and a map from `uniform_map()` as output.
MISER uses the `MiserWorkspace` from `MiserTrainer::workspace(components, ExecutionContext)` and `initial_strata()`.
Both workspaces return the preceding training attempt's record through `last_attempt()`.
Training results do not depend on execution policy or worker count.

For training, the uniform source `UniformSource` accepts only `RandomStreams`.
For evaluation, besides `RandomStreams`, a plan using a learned `VegasMap` proposal can accept `&QmcReplicatePlan` to combine randomized QMC with importance sampling ([Sparse grids and randomized quasi-Monte Carlo](oxref:integrate-qmc-through-monte-carlo-plan)).

## A localized heat source in a box

Estimate the total output [W] of a Gaussian heat source $q_{\mathrm{h}}(\boldsymbol x)=\exp(-|\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol c|^2/(2s^2))$ W/m³ centered at $\boldsymbol c=(0.37,0.52,0.61)$ m inside a cube of side 1 m, using uniform Monte Carlo, VEGAS, and MISER with the same evaluation sample count $2^{14}$.
VEGAS trains its map with the defaults of 50 bins and 5 iterations × 2048 samples; MISER learns up to 64 strata and depth 8 from pilot samples.
Training and the three evaluations use distinct `stream` numbers.
The exact value is the product of $\sqrt{\pi/2}\,s\,[\operatorname{erf}((1-c_j)/(\sqrt2 s))+\operatorname{erf}(c_j/(\sqrt2 s))]$ over axes, computed with `scioxide_special::erf`.

The following definitions block contains the source strength `heat_source` and one-dimensional axis integral `axis_mass` shared by the three cells.
The exact value is the product of `axis_mass` over three axes.
It also contains `evaluate`, which executes a plan on one random stream and returns the estimate, report, and statistics, and `method_row` and `statistics_row`, which construct table rows.

```rust
//| id: integrate-localized-heat-source
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Gaussian heat source in a cube, exact axis integrals, and evaluation helpers"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-random, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    IntegrationReport, MonteCarloIntegrator, MonteCarloProposal, MonteCarloStatistics,
    RandomStreams, TrainingReport,
};
use scioxide_random::Philox4x64;
use scioxide_special::erf;

// Heat-source center c [m] (inside a cube of side 1 m).
pub(crate) const SOURCE_CENTER: [f64; 3] = [0.37, 0.52, 0.61];

// Heat-source strength q(x) = exp(−|x − c|²/(2s²)) [W/m³] (s is source width [m]).
pub(crate) fn heat_source(x: &[f64], s: f64) -> f64 {
    let r2: f64 = x
        .iter()
        .zip(SOURCE_CENTER)
        .map(|(x, c)| (x - c) * (x - c))
        .sum();
    RealScalar::exp(-r2 / (2.0 * s * s))
}

// One-dimensional integral on axis j, √(π/2) s [erf((1 − c_j)/(√2 s)) + erf(c_j/(√2 s))] (SciOxide erf).
pub(crate) fn axis_mass(j: usize, s: f64) -> Result<f64, String> {
    let (c, width) = (SOURCE_CENTER[j], std::f64::consts::SQRT_2 * s);
    let sum = erf((1.0 - c) / width).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        + erf(c / width).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(RealScalar::sqrt(std::f64::consts::PI / 2.0) * s * sum)
}

// Execute a plan on one random stream and return the estimate, report, and workspace statistics.
pub(crate) type Evaluation = (f64, IntegrationReport<f64>, MonteCarloStatistics<f64>);
pub(crate) fn evaluate<P: MonteCarloProposal<f64> + Sync>(
    plan: &MonteCarloIntegrator<f64, P>,
    f: &(dyn Fn(&[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> + Sync),
    streams: RandomStreams<'_, Philox4x64>,
) -> Result<Evaluation, String> {
    let context = ExecutionContext::sequential();
    let mut workspace = plan.workspace(1, context).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut estimate = 0.0_f64;
    let report = plan
        .integrate_into(f, streams, &mut estimate, &mut workspace, context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let statistics = workspace.statistics()[0].clone().ok_or("Missing statistics")?;
    Ok((estimate, report, statistics))
}

// Method-table row: training and evaluation counts, estimate, difference, standard error, difference/standard error, termination status.
pub(crate) type MethodRow = (
    String,
    usize,
    usize,
    f64,
    f64,
    f64,
    f64,
    String,
    Option<String>,
);
pub(crate) fn method_row(
    name: &str,
    (estimate, report, statistics): &Evaluation,
    training: Option<&TrainingReport>,
    exact: f64,
) -> MethodRow {
    let (se, error) = (statistics.standard_error(), estimate - exact);
    let learned = training.map_or(0, |t| t.evaluations());
    let learning = training.map(|t| t.termination().to_string());
    let status = report.termination().to_string();
    let n = report.evaluations();
    (
        name.into(),
        learned,
        n,
        *estimate,
        error,
        se,
        error / se,
        status,
        learning,
    )
}

// Statistics-table row: variance basis, independent-unit count, degrees of freedom, effective sample size.
pub(crate) fn statistics_row(
    name: &str,
    statistics: &MonteCarloStatistics<f64>,
) -> (String, String, usize, Option<f64>, Option<f64>) {
    let basis = format!("{:?}", statistics.basis());
    let (units, dof) = (statistics.units(), statistics.degrees_of_freedom());
    (
        name.into(),
        basis,
        units,
        dof,
        statistics.effective_sample_size(),
    )
}
```

With defaults (128 pilot samples), MISER learns 47 strata with about 5900 evaluations and has standard error $4.9\times10^{-5}$ W, about one sixth of uniform MC; uniform MC would require about 32 times as many samples for the same standard error.
The difference-to-standard-error ratio is 0.83, agreeing with the exact value within a standard error.
MISER's Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom are about 31.
$\nu$ depends on stratum variances $v_c$ and sample counts $n_c$ rather than the stratum count, and never exceeds $\sum_c (n_c - 1)$.
It is only about 31 for 16384 points because some strata have few samples relative to their contributions to estimator variance.
At source width 0.3 m, MISER improves the standard error by only a factor of about 2.

With 16 pilot samples, the MISER estimate differs from the exact value by 16 standard errors.
Too few pilot samples underestimate variability in strata containing the source, assigning them too few evaluation samples and also underestimating standard error.
Standard error alone does not reveal this failure; comparisons across training amounts or against exact values are needed.
Even with 128 pilot samples, changing the first cell's seed to 50 gives a MISER difference of about 5.5 standard errors, while source width 0.02 m gives about 27.
MISER intervals are conditional on the strata fixed by training, so integrands with narrow features can produce standard errors far below the actual error.

The second cell plots the axis densities $q_k$ learned by VEGAS (solid steps) and the ideal separable densities $q_k^* \propto \exp(-(x - c_k)^2/(2s^2))$ (dashed).

```rust
//| id: integrate-vegas-learned-density
//| ref: integrate-vegas-learned-density
//| title: "Compare VEGAS learned axis densities with ideal separable densities"
//| run: button
//| uses: [integrate-localized-heat-source]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
//| dependsOn:
//|   source: integrate-adaptive-monte-carlo-source
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"density","path":"/spec/series/0/points/0/0","equals":0}

use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

let s = source["source_width"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing source width")?;
let bins = source["vegas_bins"].as_u64().ok_or("Missing bin count")? as usize;
let boundaries: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(source["vegas_boundaries"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Plot learned density q_k = 1/(K Δ) as steps, with ideal separable density g_k/∫g_k overlaid as dashed curves.
let mut series = Vec::new();
for k in 0..3 {
    let edges = &boundaries[k * (bins + 1)..(k + 1) * (bins + 1)];
    let mut steps = Vec::new();
    for pair in edges.windows(2) {
        let height = 1.0 / (bins as f64 * (pair[1] - pair[0]));
        steps.extend([[pair[0], height], [pair[1], height]]);
    }
    let (c, mass) = (SOURCE_CENTER[k], axis_mass(k, s)?);
    let ideal: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=200)
        .map(|i| i as f64 / 200.0)
        .map(|x| {
            [
                x,
                RealScalar::exp(-(x - c) * (x - c) / (2.0 * s * s)) / mass,
            ]
        })
        .collect();
    series.push(
        serde_json::json!({ "name": format!("Learned q (axis {})", k + 1), "points": steps }),
    );
    series.push(serde_json::json!({
        "name": format!("Ideal density (axis {})", k + 1),
        "points": ideal,
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }));
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "line",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "series": series,
        "xLabel": "Axis coordinate x_k [m]",
        "yLabel": "Probability density per unit interval q_k [1/m]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 1.0
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "density" })
);
```

The VEGAS learned densities peak at source centers $c_k$ on each axis and are lower and wider than the ideal densities.
Damping and boundary averaging make updates cautious, so five iterations do not reach the ideal shape.
VEGAS has an effective sample size of only about 10 among 16384 points.
This only indicates that the proposal differs greatly from uniform; standard error describes accuracy for this integrand ([Effective sample size](oxref:integrate-effective-sample-size)).

## Summary

For integrands concentrated in narrow regions, learned importance maps (VEGAS) or strata (MISER) can greatly reduce standard error with the same evaluation sample count.
VEGAS is a separable map that accumulates $(\|f\|/q)^2$ in axis bins and updates boundaries; MISER is best-first stratification that bisects strata with large variance contribution $(V\sigma)^2/n$ and uses Neyman allocation; neither includes training samples in its estimator.
Use a different `stream` number for evaluation than for training, and include training evaluations in computational-cost comparisons.
Standard errors and intervals are conditional on the map or strata fixed by training and can be too small when training misses features; check with different seeds or training amounts, exact values, or another method.
