`ProductRule` and `CubaturePlan` in [Tensor-product rules and Genz–Malik adaptive cubature](../multidimensional-and-monte-carlo/) assume a box $[a_1,b_1]\times\cdots\times[a_d,b_d]$.
Embedding a disk or triangle in a box and multiplying by an indicator function makes the integrand discontinuous at the boundary, slowing quadrature convergence.
This page covers two ways to supply domain geometry to quadrature: iterated integration with bounds depending on outer coordinates, and coordinate maps.
It also covers `CubaturePlan::with_breakpoints`, which places known features inside a box on initial-region boundaries, and interpretation of iterated-integration and cubature termination statuses.
[Simplex and sphere quadrature rules](../simplex-and-sphere-rules/) covers fixed rules on triangles, tetrahedra, and spheres.

## Iterated integration with variable bounds

By Fubini's theorem, an integral over a domain with bounds depending on outer coordinates can be written as

$$
\int_\Omega f\,dV = \int_{a_0}^{b_0}\int_{a_1(t_0)}^{b_1(t_0)}\cdots\int_{a_{d-1}(t_0,\dots,t_{d-2})}^{b_{d-1}(t_0,\dots,t_{d-2})} f(t_0,\dots,t_{d-1})\,dt_{d-1}\cdots dt_0
$$


`IteratedDomainPlan::new(d, IteratedOptions)` makes axis 0 outermost; it passes the $k$ outer coordinates $t_0, \dots, t_{k-1}$ to the bound function `bounds(outer)` to obtain the interval for axis $k$.
The axis index is the length $k$ of the supplied slice.
Dimensions range from 0 to 32.
The one-shot function `integrate_iterated` integrates once with default settings; see [One-shot integrate_iterated](#one-shot-integrate_iterated).
Each axis uses globally adaptive G7K15 quadrature, and each node of an outer rule is itself an inner integral.
Since inner integrals are estimates, outer integration propagates their error estimates.

The inner integral $F(t_0)=\int_{a_1(t_0)}^{b_1(t_0)}f\,dt_1$ inherits the smoothness of the bound functions.
Representing a disk of radius $R$ by $t_1\in[-\sqrt{R^2-t_0^2},\sqrt{R^2-t_0^2}]$ gives $F$ a $\sqrt{R-|t_0|}$-type singularity at $t_0=\pm R$, so outer adaptive quadrature refines deeply near the endpoints.
Bound functions describe domain geometry and are not counted as integrand evaluations.
Bounds must be finite, with the lower bound no greater than the upper (zero width is allowed).

### Allocating tolerances to inner integrals

The tolerance in `IteratedOptions::new(SubdivisionOptions)` applies to the outermost integral, while subdivision and evaluation limits count across all axes.
`with_max_iterations` limits G7K15 applications, including those on outer axes.
Each outer region initially allocates half its tolerance to inner integration, distributing it uniformly across the axis interval.
Until outer estimates are available, relative tolerance applies to inner values; afterward, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}|Q|$ determined by the outer estimate is distributed as an absolute quantity.
This gives even small inner integrals affected by cancellation a target independent of their magnitude.
When inner uncertainty dominates a region's error, the region halves this fraction and recomputes the inner integrals.

Even if an inner integral stops at stagnation (for example, a roundoff accuracy floor) or a subdivision limit, its estimate and error pass to the outer rule.
The inner roundoff floor enters the outer roundoff component, while the remaining $\max(\text{discretization}-\text{roundoff},0)+\text{inner}$ enters the outer `IntegrationErrorComponents::inner`.
Regions with error within twice the roundoff floor, and regions dominated by inner integrals that stopped before reaching their targets, are not subdivided further.
When no subdividable region remains, termination is `Stagnation`.
If an inner integral reaches an evaluation or rule-application limit, cancellation, or deadline, the entire call ends with an error.
If the outermost interval has zero width, the result is 0 and termination is `DegenerateDomain`.

The cost is at least $15^d$ evaluations, growing exponentially with dimension.
`with_breakpoints(axis, fractions)` supplies breakpoints as fractions $(0,1)$ of the current interval on each axis, placing boundary layers or interior corners at initial-subdivision endpoints.
The `IteratedWorkspace` from `plan.workspace(components)` stores per-axis region lists in $O(d\,r\,c)$ memory, where $r$ is the region count per axis; `integrate_into` does not allocate.

## Coordinate maps and Jacobians

`CoordinateMap` is a trait that writes physical coordinates $\boldsymbol x=\varphi(\boldsymbol t)$ from integration coordinates $\boldsymbol t$ and returns $J(\boldsymbol t)$.
`plan.integrate_into(f, bounds, &map, &mut out, &mut workspace, scope)` computes

$$
\int f(\varphi(\boldsymbol t))\,J(\boldsymbol t)\,d\boldsymbol t
$$


Return $J = |\det D\varphi|$ to match the change-of-variables formula.
The plan multiplies by $J$ directly without taking its absolute value, so returning the signed determinant $\det D\varphi < 0$ of an orientation-reversing map reverses the integral's sign.
Closures of type `Fn(&[f64], &mut [f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError>` implement this trait; `IdentityMap` is the identity map with $\varphi(\boldsymbol t)=\boldsymbol t$ and $J = 1$.
`plan.integrate(f, bounds)` is the convenience form using the identity map.

Polar coordinates $(r,\theta)\mapsto(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)$ with $J = r$ transform the disk into the rectangle $[0,R]\times[0,2\pi]$, removing endpoint singularities when the integrand is smooth.
The user is responsible for the map and $J$; quadrature error estimates do not include mapping errors.

## The moment of inertia of a disk

For a disk of radius $R=1$ m and surface density $\rho(r)=\rho_0(1+\alpha r^2/R^2)$ with $\rho_0=1$ kg/m², the moment of inertia about the axis through its center perpendicular to the disk is

$$
I = \int_{x^2+y^2\le R^2}\rho(r)\,r^2\,dA = 2\pi\rho_0\left(\frac{R^4}{4}+\frac{\alpha R^4}{6}\right)
$$

Compute it using both Cartesian variable bounds and a polar coordinate map.
The analytical solution on the right is an independent reference.

The following definitions block contains the integrand `inertia_density` ($\rho(r)\,r^2$ in physical coordinates $(x, y)$), Cartesian bound function `disk`, polar rectangle `rectangle`, and analytical solution `exact_inertia` shared by the two cells.

```rust
//| id: integrate-disk-inertia-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Disk surface density, two bound functions, and analytical moment of inertia"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, Interval, RealScalar};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

// Disk radius R [m]. ρ₀ = 1 kg/m².
pub(crate) const RADIUS: f64 = 1.0;

// Integrand ρ(r) r² = (1 + α r²/R²) r² (p is physical coordinates (x, y)).
pub(crate) fn inertia_density(p: &[f64], alpha: f64) -> f64 {
    let r2 = p[0] * p[0] + p[1] * p[1];
    (1.0 + alpha * r2 / (RADIUS * RADIUS)) * r2
}

// Cartesian: outer x ∈ [−R, R], inner y ∈ [−√(R² − x²), √(R² − x²)].
pub(crate) fn disk(outer: &[f64]) -> Result<Interval<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    match outer {
        [] => Interval::new(-RADIUS, RADIUS),
        [x, ..] => {
            let h = RealScalar::sqrt((RADIUS * RADIUS - x * x).max(0.0));
            Interval::new(-h, h)
        }
    }
}

// Polar: (r, θ) ∈ [0, R] × [0, 2π].
pub(crate) fn rectangle(outer: &[f64]) -> Result<Interval<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    Interval::new(0.0, if outer.is_empty() { RADIUS } else { 2.0 * PI })
}

// Analytical solution I = 2π ρ₀ (R⁴/4 + α R⁴/6) [kg·m²].
pub(crate) fn exact_inertia(alpha: f64) -> f64 {
    let r4 = RADIUS * RADIUS * RADIUS * RADIUS;
    2.0 * PI * (r4 / 4.0 + alpha * r4 / 6.0)
}
```

The first cell performs three calculations with the same `IteratedDomainPlan` and workspace, comparing evaluation counts, inner-integral error estimates, and termination statuses.
The third row reverses the azimuth through $\theta = -s$ and returns the signed determinant $-r$, checking that the value becomes $-I$.
The expected-value column includes this sign.

```rust
//| id: integrate-iterated-disk-inertia
//| ref: integrate-iterated-disk-inertia
//| title: "Vary density gradient and tolerance to compare Cartesian and polar-map evaluation counts"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [integrate-disk-inertia-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| inputs:
//|   density_gradient: {"type":"range","label":"Density gradient α [-]","description":"Increase in surface density ρ(r) = ρ₀(1 + α r²/R²) at the outer edge.","min":0,"max":4,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//|   tolerance_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Tolerance exponent q [-]","description":"Set absolute and relative tolerances to 10^q.","min":-12,"max":-6,"step":1,"value":-10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"loose-tolerance","label":"Tolerance 10⁻⁶","description":"Cartesian evaluation count decreases, while the polar map still uses 225 evaluations.","inputs":{"tolerance_exponent":-6}}
//|   - {"id":"steep-density","label":"α = 4","description":"Even for a disk heavier at the edge, the polar integrand is polynomial in r, so evaluation count does not change.","inputs":{"density_gradient":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.6179938779914944,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":2.6179938779914944,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":-2.6179938779914944,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/6","equals":225}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: loose-tolerance
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/6","equals":225}
//|   - case: steep-density
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":5.759586531581287,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    IntegrationReport, IteratedDomainPlan, IteratedOptions, SubdivisionOptions,
};

let sig15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let alpha = density_gradient;
let tol = 10.0_f64.powf(tolerance_exponent);
let tolerance =
    ErrorTolerance::scalar(tol, tol, NormSelection::Infinity).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let limit = Budget::try_from(2000).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = SubdivisionOptions::default()
    .with_tolerance(tolerance)
    .with_max_subdivisions(limit);
let plan =
    IteratedDomainPlan::new(2, IteratedOptions::new(options)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let inertia = |p: &[f64]| Ok(inertia_density(p, alpha));
let exact = exact_inertia(alpha);
// Method 1: Cartesian variable bounds (the inner integral has square-root singularities at x = ±R).
let (cartesian, report) = plan.integrate(&inertia, disk).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut results = vec![("Cartesian, variable bounds", cartesian, exact, report)];
// Methods 2 and 3: map (r, θ) to physical coordinates. σ = −1 reverses orientation with θ = −s and returns −r unchanged.
let mut ws = plan.workspace(1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let context = ExecutionContext::sequential();
for (name, sign) in [
    ("Polar map (J = r)", 1.0_f64),
    ("Reversed map (J = −r)", -1.0),
] {
    let map = |t: &[f64], x: &mut [f64]| {
        x[0] = t[0] * RealScalar::cos(sign * t[1]);
        x[1] = t[0] * RealScalar::sin(sign * t[1]);
        Ok(sign * t[0])
    };
    let mut value = 0.0_f64;
    let report = plan
        .integrate_into(&inertia, rectangle, &map, &mut value, &mut ws, context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    results.push((name, value, sign * exact, report));
}
// Inner-integral error enters the inner component of the outer error estimate.
let row =
    |(name, value, expected, report): &(&'static str, f64, f64, IntegrationReport<f64>)| {
        let inner = report.error_components().and_then(|e| e.inner());
        let (estimate, n) = (report.error_estimate(), report.evaluations());
        let status = report.termination().to_string();
        let error = (value - expected).abs();
        let inner = inner.map(|e| e.magnitude());
        (*name, *value, *expected, error, estimate, inner, n, status)
    };
let rows: Vec<_> = results.iter().map(row).collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "I [kg·m²]", "type": "number", "format": sig15 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Expected value [kg·m²]", "type": "number", "format": sig15 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference from expected value [kg·m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Error estimate [kg·m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Inner-integral component [kg·m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Termination" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })
);
let outcome = if results.iter().all(|r| r.3.is_success()) {
    Outcome::satisfied("All three calculations reached tolerance")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Some calculations stopped before reaching tolerance")
        .explanation("Check the termination-status column to identify which calculations stopped.")
};
let (cartesian, polar) = (&results[0].3, &results[1].3);
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(cartesian.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Cartesian evaluation count", cartesian.evaluations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Polar-map evaluation count", polar.evaluations() as f64, None)
);
```

With defaults ($\alpha=1$, tolerance $10^{-10}$), Cartesian variable bounds use about 19000 evaluations, with difference $1.4\times10^{-13}$ from the expected value and error estimate $2.4\times10^{-10}$.
The polar map uses 225 evaluations (one G7K15 application per axis, $15\times15$ points) with difference $4\times10^{-16}$.
All three terminate with `ToleranceReached`.
Cartesian evaluation counts grow from about 11000 at tolerance $10^{-6}$ to about 23000 at $10^{-12}$.
The integrand is a fourth-degree polynomial in the inner variable $y$, so G7K15 integrates it exactly and the inner component displays as 0.
The increased evaluation count comes from the square-root singularity of the outer integrand $F(x)$ at $x = \pm R$.
In polar coordinates the integrand $\rho r^3$ is polynomial in $r$ and independent of $\theta$, so the initial G7K15 application is accepted.
The reversed map returns $-I$ by multiplying directly by $J = -r$, illustrating that orientation handling is the user's responsibility.

### One-shot integrate_iterated

The one-shot function `integrate_iterated(f, bounds, d)` integrates with `IteratedDomainPlan` using default `IteratedOptions` settings and the identity map.
Defaults are absolute and relative tolerances $\sqrt\varepsilon \approx 1.5\times10^{-8}$, up to 50 subdivisions across all axes, and no G7K15 application limit; workspace is allocated internally and execution is sequential with `ExecutionContext::sequential_native()`.
It returns `Integral` containing the value and an error estimate including inner errors, without a report.
Unsuccessful termination becomes an error.
Since it does not accept `CoordinateMap`, either multiply the integrand by $J$ for a coordinate map or use `IteratedDomainPlan::integrate_into`.
[One-shot functions and configured plans](oxref:integrate-choosing-one-shot) summarizes the correspondence with other one-shot functions.

The following cell uses `integrate_iterated` with Cartesian variable bounds and with a polar integrand multiplied by $J = r$, and displays the evaluation counts and termination statuses alongside those of a plan with identical defaults.

```rust
//| id: integrate-iterated-one-shot
//| ref: integrate-iterated-one-shot
//| title: "Use one-shot integrate_iterated in Cartesian and polar coordinates and compare with a default plan"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [integrate-disk-inertia-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| inputs:
//|   density_gradient: {"type":"range","label":"Density gradient α [-]","description":"Increase in surface density ρ(r) = ρ₀(1 + α r²/R²) at the outer edge.","min":0,"max":4,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"uniform-disk","label":"α = 0","description":"A uniform disk still has singular Cartesian bounds.","inputs":{"density_gradient":0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"one-shot","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"one-shot","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":14175}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: uniform-disk
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"one-shot","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.5707963267948966,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{Integral, IteratedDomainPlan, IteratedOptions, integrate_iterated};

let sig15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let alpha = density_gradient;
let exact = exact_inertia(alpha);
let inertia = |p: &[f64]| Ok(inertia_density(p, alpha));
// The one-shot call accepts no map, so multiply the polar integrand by J = r.
let polar = |t: &[f64]| {
    let x = [t[0] * RealScalar::cos(t[1]), t[0] * RealScalar::sin(t[1])];
    Ok(inertia_density(&x, alpha) * t[0])
};
let one_shot = |name: &str, result: Result<Integral<f64>, SciOxideError>| match result {
    Ok(r) => {
        let error = Some((r.value() - exact).abs());
        (
            name.to_owned(),
            Some(r.value()),
            error,
            Some(r.error_estimate()),
            None,
            "Success".into(),
        )
    }
    Err(e) => (
        name.to_owned(),
        None,
        None,
        None,
        None,
        format!("{:?}", e.kind()),
    ),
};

    one_shot(
        "integrate_iterated (Cartesian)",
        integrate_iterated(&inertia, disk, 2),
    ),
    one_shot(
        "integrate_iterated (polar, J included)",
        integrate_iterated(&polar, rectangle, 2),
    ),

let name = "IteratedDomainPlan (defaults, Cartesian)".to_owned();
rows.push(match plan.integrate(&inertia, disk) {
    Ok((value, report)) => {
        let (estimate, status) = (report.error_estimate(), report.termination().to_string());
        let error = Some((value - exact).abs());
        (
            name,
            Some(value),
            error,
            estimate,
            Some(report.evaluations()),
            status,
        )
    }
    Err(e) => (name, None, None, None, None, format!("{:?}", e.kind())),
});
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Call" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "I [kg·m²]", "type": "number", "format": sig15, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from analytical solution [kg·m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Error estimate [kg·m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Evaluations [count]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "No report" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination status or error" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "one-shot" })
);
// Use the success or failure of both one-shot calls as the conclusion, with the default plan's termination status.
let (status, evaluations) = (rows[2].5.clone(), rows[2].4.unwrap_or(0) as f64);
let outcome = if rows[0].1.is_some() && rows[1].1.is_some() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Both integrate_iterated calls succeeded with default settings")
} else {
    Outcome::failed("Some one-shot calls returned no estimate")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(status)
        .diagnostic("Default-plan evaluation count", evaluations, None)
```

With defaults ($\alpha = 1$), both `integrate_iterated` calls succeed.
Cartesian coordinates give difference $2.6\times10^{-11}$ from the analytical solution and error estimate $4.3\times10^{-8}$; the plan with identical defaults returns the same value with 14175 evaluations and `ToleranceReached`.
Polar coordinates give difference $4\times10^{-16}$ and, as for the mapped plan, need only 225 evaluations.
For this disk, default tolerance $\sqrt\varepsilon$ and 50 subdivisions suffice, so the one-shot call is sufficient.
Use `IteratedDomainPlan` to inspect evaluation counts, change tolerances or limits, retain estimates stopped at a limit, or reuse workspace.

## Presplitting known features inside a box

For a box-domain integrand with known corners or discontinuity locations, those positions can become initial-region boundaries.
Prepare the box, tolerance, and limits with `CubaturePlan::new(&bounds, options)`, then supply per-axis breakpoints with `with_breakpoints(axis, fractions)`.
Breakpoint $s \in (0,1)$ cuts axis $[a,b]$ at $a + s(b-a)$; an axis with $k$ breakpoints divides into $k+1$ intervals.
Initial regions are Cartesian products of intervals across all axes and share a single region list and priority queue.
Refinement uses the same rule as a plan without breakpoints: G7K15 for $d = 1$ and the Genz–Malik degree-7/5 rule for $d \ge 2$.

Peaks narrower than the initial node spacing or discontinuities passing between nodes can be missed without appearing in the embedded-pair difference ([Embedded pairs and globally adaptive quadrature](../adaptive-quadrature/)).
Splitting at a known feature places it on a region boundary instead of in its interior, making each region's integrand smooth so the embedded-pair difference reflects error correctly.
Breakpoints are checked during preparation (finite, inside $(0,1)$, strictly increasing), and the `CubatureWorkspace` returned by `workspace(components)` is allocated to include the initial-region count.
For a plan with more breakpoints, enlarge existing workspace with `workspace.check(&plan.workspace_requirements(c)?)` and `try_reserve`.

## Interpreting iterated-integration and cubature termination

`IteratedDomainPlan` uses error estimates based on G7K15 differences and returns `ToleranceReached` on reaching tolerance.
Errors from inner integrals enter the outer error estimate as the `inner` component.
`Stagnation`, when no subdividable region remains, mainly indicates a roundoff accuracy floor or inner integrals that did not reach their targets.

For $d = 1$, `CubaturePlan` uses G7K15 estimates and returns `ToleranceReached`.
The Genz–Malik error estimate for $d \ge 2$ is an empirical estimate using four times the degree-7/degree-5 difference; it carries `ErrorReliability::Heuristic` and reaches tolerance with `HeuristicToleranceReached` ([Genz–Malik adaptive cubature](oxref:integrate-genz-malik-cubature)).
The error estimate is bounded below by $50\varepsilon\sum|w_7|$ times the magnitude of the integral estimate; if tolerance is below this floor, termination is `Stagnation` once subdivision stops changing the integral estimate.
All-zero samples do not establish convergence over the whole domain; even a truly zero function can be refined until a limit or geometric restriction is reached.

## Summary

For non-box domains, supply geometry to the quadrature method rather than through an indicator function.
Variable-bound `IteratedDomainPlan` handles general domains, but costs at least $15^d$ evaluations, and singular bound functions increase outer refinement.
Use a smoothing `CoordinateMap` when available, returning $|\det D\varphi|$ (a signed determinant reverses the integral's sign).
Inner-integral error enters the outer error estimate as `inner`, so `IntegrationErrorComponents` shows where error remains.
If default tolerance and limits suffice, use one-shot `integrate_iterated`; for coordinate maps, multiply the integrand by $J$.
Place known features inside a box on initial-region boundaries with `CubaturePlan::with_breakpoints`.
