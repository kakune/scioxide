Uniform Monte Carlo integration places samples at the same density where integrand values are small and where they are large.
Importance sampling draws points from a density (proposal) that places more samples where values are large, dividing by the density to remove bias.
This page covers importance-sampling estimators and variance, the proposal contract for `MonteCarloIntegrator::importance`, and effective sample size as a measure of weight imbalance, then uses a custom proposal to examine scale versus standard error.
[Adaptive Monte Carlo (VEGAS and MISER)](../adaptive-monte-carlo/) covers VEGAS proposal learning; [Monte Carlo integration estimators and statistics](../monte-carlo-integration/) covers uniform Monte Carlo standard errors and intervals.

## Importance-sampling estimator and variance

Let $\boldsymbol X_i$ be the affine image in box $B$ of volume $V$ of a point $\boldsymbol Y_i$ drawn from probability density $q > 0$ on the unit cube $[0,1)^d$, and define the observation as

$$
Y_i = \frac{V f(\boldsymbol X_i)}{q(\boldsymbol Y_i)}
$$


Since $\mathbb E_q[V f/q] = \int V f\,d\boldsymbol y = I$, the sample mean is unbiased regardless of $q$.
Its variance is $\operatorname{Var}_q(Y) = \int (Vf)^2/q\,d\boldsymbol y - I^2$, minimized when $q \propto |f|$ (zero if $f$ has constant sign).
If $q$ gives little density where $f$ is large, $f/q$ takes large values and variance can instead increase.

## Proposal contract

`MonteCarloIntegrator::importance(&bounds, proposal, options)` performs importance sampling with `proposal` implementing the `MonteCarloProposal` trait.
The trait has `dimension()` (point dimension $d$), `uniform_count()` (uniform random numbers per point $m$), and `sample(uniforms, point)`.
`sample` receives $m$ uniform random numbers in $[0,1)$, writes a unit-cube point, and returns its density $q$.
Uniforms are passed as `ProposalUniforms`, checked at construction to be finite and in $[0,1)$ (you can also construct them with `ProposalUniforms::new`).
Unbiasedness requires $q$ to integrate to 1 on the cube and be positive wherever $f \ne 0$, but a plan cannot verify this from samples.
The plan only checks that the returned density is finite and positive and that the point lies in $[0,1)$.
Density or coordinate violations return `InvalidInput`; NaN or infinite coordinates return `NonFinite`.
Weights $1/q$ are not self-normalized.
`sample` must be a deterministic function of the supplied uniforms; rejection sampling, adaptive sampling, and Markov chain sampling are not supported.
`MonteCarloIntegrator::new` uses the uniform proposal `UniformProposal::new(d)` ($q = 1$, passing uniforms directly through as point coordinates).

## Effective sample size

Use Kish effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = (\sum_i w_i)^2/\sum_i w_i^2$ ($w_i = 1/q(\boldsymbol Y_i)$) to diagnose weight imbalance.
`MonteCarloStatistics::effective_sample_size()` and `MonteCarloChild::effective_sample_size()` return it; for a uniform proposal it equals $n$.
The more weights $1/q$ concentrate on a few samples, the smaller $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ becomes.
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ depends only on weights, excluding the integrand, so it measures how far the proposal differs from uniform.
When reusing samples for another integrand, it warns that a few samples may dominate the estimate; for the integrand matched by the proposal, assess accuracy through standard error.
This is a proposal diagnostic, not the degrees of freedom for an interval.

## A custom proposal and effective sample size

Consider the same Gaussian heat source $\exp(-|\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol c|^2/(2s^2))$ W/m³ centered at $\boldsymbol c=(0.37,0.52,0.61)$ m in a cube of side 1 m as in [Adaptive Monte Carlo (VEGAS and MISER)](../adaptive-monte-carlo/), and its total output [W].
The exact value is the product of $\sqrt{\pi/2}\,s\,[\operatorname{erf}((1-c_j)/(\sqrt2 s))+\operatorname{erf}(c_j/(\sqrt2 s))]$ over axes, computed with `scioxide_special::erf`.
Implementing `MonteCarloProposal` yourself lets the proposal incorporate knowledge of the integrand's shape.
For this heat source, use a product over axes of Laplace densities centered at $c_k$ with scale $b$, truncated to $[0,1)$:

$$
q_k(y) = \frac{e^{-|y - c_k|/b}}{Z_k}, \qquad Z_k = b\bigl(2 - e^{-c_k/b} - e^{-(1-c_k)/b}\bigr)
$$


The cumulative distribution function has a closed-form inverse, so each coordinate uses one uniform random number (`uniform_count()` is 3).
The following definitions block implements this proposal as `MonteCarloProposal`.
Map the uniform $u$ on each axis to $t = u(A + B)$ ($A = 1 - e^{-c_k/b}$, $B = 1 - e^{-(1-c_k)/b}$, $Z_k = b(A + B)$).
For $t < A$ use the left inverse $y = c_k + b\ln(t + e^{-c_k/b})$, otherwise the right inverse $y = c_k - b\ln(1 - (t - A))$ to construct the coordinate, returning the product of $q_k(y)$ across three axes as the density.

```rust
//| id: integrate-laplace-proposal
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Proposal from a product of truncated Laplace distributions"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{MonteCarloProposal, ProposalUniforms};

// Product of Laplace densities, each centered at c_k with scale b and truncated to [0, 1).
pub(crate) struct LaplaceProposal {
    pub(crate) center: [f64; 3],
    pub(crate) scale: f64,
}
impl MonteCarloProposal<f64> for LaplaceProposal {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        3
    }
    fn uniform_count(&self) -> usize {
        3
    }
    fn sample(
        &self,
        uniforms: ProposalUniforms<'_, f64>,
        point: &mut [f64],
    ) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        let b = self.scale;
        let mut density = 1.0;
        for (k, &u) in uniforms.as_slice().iter().enumerate() {
            let c = self.center[k];
            // Masses left and right of the center (excluding b), and normalization constant Z = b (A + B).
            let left = 1.0 - RealScalar::exp(-c / b);
            let right = 1.0 - RealScalar::exp(-(1.0 - c) / b);
            let t = u * (left + right);
            // Inverse CDF: left side c + b ln(t + e^{−c/b}), right side c − b ln(1 − (t − A)).
            let y = if t < left {
                c + b * RealScalar::ln(t + RealScalar::exp(-c / b))
            } else {
                c - b * RealScalar::ln(1.0 - (t - left))
            };
            point[k] = y;
            density *= RealScalar::exp(-(y - c).abs() / b) / (b * (left + right));
        }
        Ok(density)
    }
}
```

Vary scale $b$ from $0.02$ to $2$ m and plot standard error and effective-sample-size fraction $n_{\mathrm{eff}}/n$ for $2^{14}$ samples.
Both plots share horizontal axis $b$ and are linked, so zooming one gives the same range in the other.
The dashed horizontal line is uniform Monte Carlo's standard error; the vertical line is source width $s$.

With the default $s = 0.06$ m, standard error reaches its minimum $1.4\times10^{-5}$ W at $b = 0.05$ m, about one twentieth of uniform MC's $2.8\times10^{-4}$ W; without training it is comparable to the standard error obtained with the learned VEGAS map ($1.7\times10^{-5}$ W for the same sample count).
Setting $b$ to 0.02 m, narrower than the source width, increases standard error to about 10 times its minimum.
If proposal tails decay faster than integrand tails, rare tail samples have very large $f/q$.
Increasing $b$ brings the proposal closer to uniform, and standard error returns to the uniform MC value.

The effective-sample-size fraction is below 0.002 at $b \le 0.05$ m, increases once $b$ exceeds 0.1 m, and reaches 0.98 at $b = 2$ m.
At $b$, where standard error is minimized, the effective-sample-size fraction is nearly minimal, showing it is not an integrand-specific accuracy measure.
With source width 0.2 m, the estimate at $b = 0.02$ m differs from the exact value by about 20 standard errors.
An overly narrow proposal gives $f/q$ a heavy-tailed distribution, making the sample standard error itself unreliable.

## Summary

The mean of importance-sampling observations $V f/q$ is unbiased if $q$ integrates to 1 on the cube and is positive wherever $f \ne 0$; variance decreases as the density approaches $q \propto |f|$.
`MonteCarloProposal` deterministically constructs a point and density from uniforms; a plan checks only positivity of density and coordinate bounds, and cannot verify normalization.
A proposal too narrow relative to the integrand gives $f/q$ heavy tails, increasing standard error and making the sample standard error itself unreliable.
Effective sample size depends only on weights $1/q$ and diagnoses separation of the proposal from uniform; judge accuracy for a specific integrand through standard error and comparisons with exact values.
