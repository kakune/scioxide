Integrands diverging at endpoints and integrals over infinite intervals slow convergence of Gaussian quadrature and equally spaced Newton–Cotes formulas.
This page explains double-exponential transformations that turn endpoint singularities and infinite intervals into rapid decay, and treatment of known interior singularities.

| Problem | Plan | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Algebraic or logarithmic endpoint singularities, semi-infinite intervals, entire real line | `DoubleExponentialIntegrator` | tanh–sinh, exp–sinh, and sinh–sinh transformations with nested trapezoidal sums |
| Known interior singularities | `singular_points` of `DoubleExponentialDomain::Finite` | tanh–sinh on each piece |
| Nonsmooth interior points such as jumps or corners | `WeightedQuadraturePlan::breakpoints` | Adaptive G7K15 with breakpoints |

Oscillatory integrands and half-line Fourier integrals are covered in [Cancellation in oscillatory integrals and tails of Fourier integrals](../fourier-integrals-and-tails/); endpoint weights and Cauchy principal values in [Weighted quadrature for endpoint weights, principal values, and complex contours](../weighted-rules-and-sensitivities/).
[Choosing numerical integration methods](../../choosing/) collects method-selection guidance.

## The double-exponential transformation idea

Apply the change of variables $x = \phi(t)$ to the integral $I = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$, rewriting it as an integral over the entire real line,

$$
I = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(\phi(t))\,\phi'(t)\,dt = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(t)\,dt
$$


A double-exponential (DE) transformation makes $g(t)$ decay as $|t| \to \infty$ according to

$$
|g(t)| = O\bigl(\exp(-c\,e^{|t|})\bigr) \qquad (c > 0)
$$

by choosing $\phi$ to produce decay at the rate of an exponential of an exponential.
This is called double-exponential decay.

Apply the trapezoidal rule with step $h$ to the decaying $g$,

$$
I_h = h \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} g(kh)
$$


Assuming $g$ is holomorphic in the strip $|\operatorname{Im} t| < d$ and integrable there, trapezoidal discretization error is

$$
|I - I_h| = O\bigl(\exp(-2\pi d / h)\bigr)
$$


In practice, truncate the infinite sum at $|t| \le T$, adding truncation error $|g(T)|$.
With double-exponential decay, $T$ of order $\log(1/h)$ suffices; for sample count $N$, error decreases at rate $\exp(-cN/\log N)$.
This convergence holds even with algebraic or logarithmic endpoint singularities if $f$ is analytic near the open interval.
The endpoint singularity is absorbed into the double-exponential decay of $\phi'(t)$.

| Integration domain | Transformation $x = \phi(t)$ | Jacobian $\phi'(t)$ | Main targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finite interval $[a, b]$ (tanh–sinh) | $\frac{a+b}{2} + \frac{b-a}{2}\tanh\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sinh t\bigr)$ | $\frac{b-a}{2}\cdot\frac{\frac{\pi}{2}\cosh t}{\cosh^2(\frac{\pi}{2}\sinh t)}$ | Endpoint factors such as $(x-a)^{-\alpha}$ or $\log(b-x)$ |
| Half-line $[a, \infty)$ (exp–sinh) | $a + \exp\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sinh t\bigr)$ | $\frac{\pi}{2}\cosh t\,\exp\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sinh t\bigr)$ | Algebraically or exponentially decaying functions |
| Real line (sinh–sinh) | $\sinh\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sinh t\bigr)$ | $\frac{\pi}{2}\cosh t\,\cosh\bigl(\frac{\pi}{2}\sinh t\bigr)$ | Functions decaying in both directions |

DE transformations are unsuitable for slowly decaying oscillatory integrands.
exp–sinh nodes become sparse far away and cannot resolve oscillation signs.
Use the methods in [Cancellation in oscillatory integrals and tails of Fourier integrals](../fourier-integrals-and-tails/) instead.

## Nested trapezoidal sums and tail truncation

`DoubleExponentialIntegrator::new(domain, LevelOptions)` prepares nodes and weights once.
The first level uses step $h = 1$ in $t$; each level halves $h$ and evaluates only newly added odd-indexed points.
Set level count $L$ through `with_max_levels` of `LevelOptions` in the range 4–15; the default is 15 (finest step $2^{-14}$).
Default absolute and relative tolerances are both $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ (about $1.49 \times 10^{-8}$ for `f64`), with the maximum norm.

Truncate sums at $|t| \le 8$ or where the transformation saturates.
Extend both tails outward in unit-width shells $k \le |t| < k+1$ in $t$ until contributions of the latest 3 shells decrease.
The tail estimate divides the last shell contribution by $1 - r$, with $r$ the larger ratio from the latest 2 shells.
This empirically extends observed decay as a geometric series and cannot detect unsampled peaks.

At most $8 \cdot 2^{L-1} + 1$ nodes are prepared, requiring about 4 MiB for `f64` at 15 levels.
No transcendental functions of $t$ are evaluated on each call; cloned Plans share their node table.

The one-shot function `integrate_unbounded(f, domain)` takes an infinite interval as scioxide-core's `ExtendedInterval` and integrates once with default `LevelOptions`.
$[a,\infty)$ uses exp–sinh; $(-\infty,b]$ uses exp–sinh on $[-b,\infty)$ reflected through $x=-t$; the real line uses sinh–sinh.
The integrand receives only coordinate $x$.
Absolute and relative tolerances are both $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ with 15 levels; neither is configurable.
Only success returns a value and error estimate in `Integral`; unsuccessful statuses become errors (`Stagnation` gives `Stagnation` of `ConvergenceFailure`).
Each call builds the node table, so prepare `DoubleExponentialIntegrator` in advance for repeated integration.
Finite and empty intervals are rejected with `InvalidInput` before evaluation; pass finite intervals to `integrate`.

The next cell integrates the Lorentzian $f(x)=1/(1+x^2)$ on three infinite intervals.
The closed forms are $\int_a^\infty f\,dx=\pi/2-\arctan a$, $\int_{-\infty}^a f\,dx=\pi/2+\arctan a$, and $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f\,dx=\pi$.
Although $f$ decays only as $x^{-2}$, the exp–sinh Jacobian decays double exponentially.
The input is finite endpoint $a$; $x$ and the integral are dimensionless.
Each row compares `integrate_unbounded` with a Plan using the same transformation and default `LevelOptions`, showing their difference, closed-form discrepancy, error estimate, and the Plan's evaluation count and termination status.
As with the one-shot function, the $(-\infty,a]$ Plan uses half-line $[-a,\infty)$ reflected by $x=-t$.
Compute reference $\arctan$ with f64 standard functions.

```rust
//| id: integrate-unbounded-one-shot
//| ref: integrate-unbounded-one-shot
//| title: "Compare one-shot integrate_unbounded with DE Plans on three infinite intervals"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| inputs:
//|   endpoint: {"type":"range","label":"Finite endpoint a [-]","description":"Move the endpoint of [a, ∞) and (−∞, a].","min":-5,"max":5,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"far-left","label":"a = −5","description":"[a, ∞) contains most mass; (−∞, a] contains only a small tail.","inputs":{"endpoint":-5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"domains","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"domains","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"domains","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"domains","path":"/rows/2/7","equals":"HeuristicToleranceReached"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: far-left
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"domains","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}

use scioxide_core::{ExtendedInterval, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::DoubleExponentialDomain::{HalfLine, RealLine};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    DoubleExponentialIntegrator, DoubleExponentialPoint, LevelOptions, integrate_unbounded,
};

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let digits15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });

let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut unsuccessful = Vec::new();
let mut last_status = String::new();

    // (One-shot endpoint, Plan domain, whether to reflect with x = −t).
    let (ends, domain, reflect) = match name {
        "[a, ∞)" => ((Some(a), None), HalfLine { lower: a }, false),
        "(−∞, a]" => ((None, Some(a)), HalfLine { lower: -a }, true),
        _ => ((None, None), RealLine, false),
    };
    // Closed form (reference atan uses f64 standard functions).
    let exact = match name {
        "[a, ∞)" => FRAC_PI_2 - a.atan(),
        "(−∞, a]" => FRAC_PI_2 + a.atan(),
        _ => PI,
    };
    // One-shot call using only defaults (tolerance √ε, 15 levels).
    let set = ExtendedInterval::new(ends.0, ends.1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    let plan = DoubleExponentialIntegrator::new(domain, LevelOptions::default())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let point = |p: DoubleExponentialPoint<f64>| {
        let t = p.coordinate();
        lorentz(if reflect { -t } else { t })
    };
    let (value, report) = plan.integrate(&point).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    last_status = report.termination().to_string();
    if !report.is_success() {
        unsuccessful.push(last_status.clone());

    rows.push((

        one_shot.value(),
        value,
        (one_shot.value() - value).abs(),

        report.termination().to_string(),

    { "key": "0", "label": "Integration interval" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "integrate_unbounded value [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits15 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Plan value [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits15 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference between them [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Closed-form discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "One-shot error estimate [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Plan evaluation count [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Plan termination status" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "domains" })
);

// Conclusion: whether Plans succeeded on all 3 intervals. One-shot calls return errors on failure.
let outcome = match unsuccessful.first() {
    None => Outcome::satisfied("Plans succeeded on all 3 intervals, and one-shot calls returned values")
        .status(last_status)
        .explanation("Error estimates are empirical, based on observed decay, and are not upper bounds."),
    Some(status) => {
        Outcome::partial("Some Plans stopped before satisfying the tolerance").status(status.clone())
    }
};
emit_outcome!(outcome.diagnostic("Unsuccessful Plans", unsuccessful.len() as f64, None));
```

At the default $a=1$, `integrate_unbounded` and Plan values agreed bit for bit on all three intervals, differing from closed forms by at most $5 \times 10^{-16}$.
The Plan used 81 evaluations for $[1,\infty)$ and the real line and 145 for $(-\infty,1]$.
Setting $a=-5$ increased evaluations for $[a,\infty)$ to 1041.
Under exp–sinh, $dx/dt=\frac{\pi}{2}\cosh t\,(x-a)$, so near $x=0$, five units from the endpoint, unit width in $t$ spans about 11 units in $x$; resolving a peak of width about 1 requires finer levels.
One-shot calls do not return evaluation counts, so only the Plan exposes this computational cost difference.

## Endpoint saturation in floating-point arithmetic

For tanh–sinh on the standard interval $[-1, 1]$, endpoint distance is

$$
1 - \tanh\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sinh t\bigr) = \frac{2e^{-\pi\sinh t}}{1 + e^{-\pi\sinh t}}
$$


At $t \approx 3.2$ this falls below spacing $2^{-53}$ near 1 in `f64`, rounding coordinate $x$ to the endpoint itself.
The distance-to-width ratio $e^{-\pi\sinh t}/(1+e^{-\pi\sinh t})$ remains representable while it lies within the normal range.
For `f64`, the distance remains representable until $t \approx 6.1$, where $e^{-\pi\sinh t}$ falls below the smallest positive normal; the Plan treats this as saturation and ends the node table there.
Saturation occurs before $|t| = 8$, so it determines truncation for finite intervals in `f64`.

The integrand therefore receives `DoubleExponentialPoint`.
`coordinate()` returns rounded coordinate $x$, which may equal an endpoint.
`nearest_endpoint()` returns the nearest finite endpoint and a positive distance computed without subtracting rounded coordinates.
On finite intervals, endpoints are those of the current piece (including interior singularities); the midpoint returns the lower endpoint.
Half-lines return the lower endpoint; the real line returns `None`.

Evaluate singular endpoint factors using this distance.
Constructing `1.0 - x` from rounded coordinates makes it 0 beyond $t \approx 3.2$, losing distance information.

## Error estimates and termination status

Componentwise error estimates are

$$
E = \max\bigl(4\,|Q_h - Q_{2h}|,\; 50\,\varepsilon A\bigr) + T
$$


Here $Q_h$ is the sum at step $h$, $A$ is the sum from samples of $|f|$ (absolute mass), and $T$ is the tail estimate.
The first term represents changes between levels; the second is the rounding error floor.
Neither is an upper bound, and $T$ is empirical, based on observed decay.

Termination statuses are as follows.

- `HeuristicToleranceReached` is returned from level 3 onward when $E$ satisfies the tolerance, every tail is truncated, a nonzero sample exists, and $4\,|Q_h - Q_{2h}|$ is at most one quarter of $A$.
  DE integration never returns `ToleranceReached`; this is its only converged status.
- `Stagnation` is returned from level 3 onward when above-floor interlevel differences shrink by less than a factor of 4 three times consecutively, the value stops changing, or the difference reaches the error floor.
  This status also covers grids that cannot be refined further and tails that do not decay.
- `RefinementLimit` is returned after exhausting all levels.
- `BudgetLimit` and `ExecutionStopped` are returned when evaluation limits, cancellation, or deadlines stop the next level or tail extension.
- `DegenerateDomain` is for zero-width finite intervals: the value is exactly 0 without calling the integrand.

Integrands that are 0 at every sample do not succeed.
They cannot be distinguished from functions with mass outside the grid.
The fourth condition prevents success based on absolute tolerance alone when only the tails of a peak narrower than the grid are sampled.

`DoubleExponentialWorkspace::component_errors()` returns the error breakdown as a componentwise `IntegrationErrorComponents`.
`discretization` is four times the interlevel difference, `roundoff` the rounding error floor, and `tail` the tail estimate (`ErrorReliability::Heuristic`).
`tail_diagnostics()` returns `TailDiagnostics`; `extent()` is the maximum evaluated $|t|$, `shells()` the completed shell count, and `status()` a `TailStatus`.
`FourierTailPlan` returns tail diagnostics of the same type, but `extent()` and `shells()` have different meanings ([Termination status and tail diagnostics](oxref:integrate-fourier-tail-diagnostics)).

| `TailStatus` | Meaning in DE integration |
|---|---|
| `HeuristicallyTruncated` | Consecutive shell contributions became negligible, and the sum was truncated |
| `Open` | Too few shells have been completed for truncation |
| `Saturated` | No representable nodes remain, or the shell limit was explored |
| `Stagnated` | Shell contributions above tolerance do not decrease, and the rounded sum does not change |

With multiple pieces for one component, return the least reliable piece's status in priority order `Stagnated`, `Saturated`, `Open`, `HeuristicallyTruncated`.
For multiple value components, `IntegrationReport::error_components()` combines diagnostics and retains the weakest status, with reliability decreasing in the order `Open`, `Stagnated`, `Saturated`, `HeuristicallyTruncated`.
For `Stagnated`, the tail estimate is `None`, and `total()` and `error_estimate()` are also `None`.

## Endpoint singularities and tanh–sinh coordinates

Compute the integral with beta-function-type endpoint singularities,

$$
I(\alpha) = \int_0^1 x^{-\alpha}\,(1 - x)^{-0.35}\,dx = B(1 - \alpha,\, 0.65)
$$

using tanh–sinh.
The input is the left-end singularity exponent $\alpha$; $x$ and the integral are dimensionless.
The integrand evaluates singular factors using the distance in `nearest_endpoint()`; after `integrate_into`, read tail diagnostics from the workspace.
The check uses a Beta function value from `scioxide_special::beta`.

The plot shows the transformed integrand $g(t) = f(x(t))\,x'(t)$.
Although $f$ diverges at $x \to 0$, $g$ becomes a peak rapidly decaying on both sides.
For plotting, compute $g(t)$ with f64 standard functions (`sinh`, `tanh`, `cosh`, `powf`); omit points whose coordinates round to endpoints and show their count in the table.

```rust
//| id: integrate-tanh-sinh-singularity
//| ref: integrate-tanh-sinh-singularity
//| title: "Vary endpoint singularity strength to examine the tanh–sinh transformed shape"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   singularity_power: {"type":"range","label":"Left-end singularity exponent α [-]","description":"Change the divergence strength of x^(−α).","min":0.05,"max":0.95,"step":0.05,"value":0.55}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"strong-singularity","label":"α = 0.95","description":"The left tail of g(t) broadens and cannot be truncated, stopping with Stagnation.","inputs":{"singularity_power":0.95}}
//|   - {"id":"weak-singularity","label":"α = 0.05","description":"Weak singularities succeed in fewer levels.","inputs":{"singularity_power":0.05}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: strong-singularity
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/7","equals":"Stagnation"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: weak-singularity
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, Interval, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    DoubleExponentialDomain, DoubleExponentialIntegrator, DoubleExponentialPoint, LevelOptions,
};
use scioxide_special::beta;

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let digits15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let fixed0 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 0 });
let alpha = singularity_power;
let right_power = 0.35_f64;
// f(x) = x^(−α) (1 − x)^(−0.35). Near-endpoint samples use retained endpoint distances instead of rounded coordinates.
let function = |point: DoubleExponentialPoint<f64>| {
    let x = point.coordinate();
    let (left, right) = match point.nearest_endpoint() {
        Some((0.0, distance)) => (distance, 1.0 - x),
        Some((_, distance)) => (x, distance),
        None => (x, 1.0 - x),
    };
    Ok(RealScalar::powf(left, -alpha) * RealScalar::powf(right, -right_power))
};

let domain = DoubleExponentialDomain::Finite {
    interval: Interval::new(0.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    singular_points: &[],
};
let integrator = DoubleExponentialIntegrator::new(domain, LevelOptions::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = integrator.workspace(1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut value = 0.0_f64;
let report = integrator
    .integrate_into(
        &function,
        &mut value,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent reference: B(1 − α, 0.65).
let reference = beta(1.0 - alpha, 1.0 - right_power).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tail = workspace
    .component_errors()
    .and_then(|errors| errors[0].tail_diagnostics());

// Plotting g(t) = f(x(t)) x'(t) (reference computation with f64 standard functions).
// Do not plot points whose x(t) rounds to an endpoint; count them.
let mut curve = Vec::new();
let mut collapsed = 0_usize;
for index in 0..=160 {
    let t = -3.5 + 7.0 * index as f64 / 160.0;
    let u = std::f64::consts::FRAC_PI_2 * t.sinh();
    let x = 0.5 * (1.0 + u.tanh());
    let jacobian = 0.5 * std::f64::consts::FRAC_PI_2 * t.cosh() / u.cosh().powi(2);
    if x > 0.0 && x < 1.0 {
        curve.push([t, x.powf(-alpha) * (1.0 - x).powf(-right_power) * jacobian]);
    } else {
        collapsed += 1;
    }
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({"name": "g(t) = f(x(t)) x′(t)", "points": curve})],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Transformed coordinate t [-]",
        "yLabel": "Transformed integrand g(t) [-]",
        "xMin": -3.5,
        "xMax": 3.5
    })
);

let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Integral [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits15 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "B(1−α, 0.65) [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits15 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Actual error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Error estimate [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "No estimate" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Tail extent |t| [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed0, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Tail status" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Evaluation count [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Termination status" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Plot points rounded to endpoints [points]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        value,
        reference,
        (value - reference).abs(),
        report.error_estimate(),
        tail.map(|t| t.extent()),
        tail.map_or("None".to_string(), |t| format!("{:?}", t.status())),
        report.evaluations(),
        report.termination().to_string(),
        collapsed,
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);

let outcome = if report.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The error estimate satisfies the tolerance")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped before satisfying the tolerance")
        .explanation("Treat the tabulated integral as the estimate from the last level.")
};
let outcome = outcome
    .status(report.termination().to_string())
    .diagnostic("Evaluation count", report.evaluations() as f64, None)
    .diagnostic("Actual error", (value - reference).abs(), None);
emit_outcome!(match report.error_estimate() {
    Some(estimate) => outcome.diagnostic("Error estimate", estimate, None),
    None => outcome,
});
```

With the default $\alpha = 0.55$, 73 evaluations gave `HeuristicToleranceReached`, differing from the Beta function by about rounding error.
The estimate was about $4 \times 10^{-14}$, well below the default tolerance $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
The tail was truncated after shells through $|t| = 5$, with status `HeuristicallyTruncated`.
At $\alpha = 0.05$, 65 evaluations succeeded, truncating the tail at $|t| = 4$.

For $\alpha = 0.95$, $g(t) \approx \exp\bigl(-(1-\alpha)\frac{\pi}{2}e^{|t|}\bigr)$ near $x \to 0$, giving slow left-tail decay.
Even extending to 7 shells through $|t| < 7$ failed truncation conditions (`extent()` is 7, status `Open`); the Plan returned `Stagnation` after 97 evaluations.
For `f64`, representable nodes extend only through $|t| \approx 6.1$, so the final shell is only partially sampled.
Refined finite-interval levels treat unrepresentable nodes as unsampled nodes, giving status `Open` rather than `Saturated`.
The value itself agrees with the reference to about $2 \times 10^{-14}$, but the error estimate is $8 \times 10^{-5}$, so the Plan could not establish this accuracy.
The outcome is `partial`; treat the value as an estimate.
With a singularity exponent near 1, even DE insufficiently absorbs endpoint singularities; analytically handling the singular factor with endpoint weights in [Weighted quadrature for endpoint weights, principal values, and complex contours](../weighted-rules-and-sensitivities/) is preferable.

Omitted plot points are those rounding to endpoints when coordinates are constructed with the ordinary tanh formula.
Quadrature evaluates using retained distances, so this count does not describe quadrature truncation.

## Interior singularities and breakpoints

If the integration interval has an interior singularity or derivative discontinuity at $c \in (a, b)$, whole-interval tanh–sinh leaves $c$ in the middle of transformed coordinates.
This destroys the strip holomorphy of $g(t)$ and invalidates exponential convergence.
Split at known points to make them endpoints of individual pieces.

`DoubleExponentialDomain::Finite { interval, singular_points }` applies a separate tanh–sinh transformation to each piece split at singularities.
Nodes cluster at both ends of each piece, and `nearest_endpoint()` returns distance to the singularity as a piece endpoint.
Singularities may be supplied in any order; duplicates and interval endpoints do not add pieces.
Points outside the interval give `InvalidInput`; NaN or infinity gives `NonFinite`.
The tolerance is divided equally among pieces.
This suits $f$ analytic inside each piece with algebraic or logarithmic endpoint singularities.

`WeightedQuadraturePlan::breakpoints(interval, points, options)` starts adaptive quadrature from the specified breakpoints.
Pieces bounded by breakpoints form initial subdomains; bisect the one with the largest error estimate, using the 15-node Gauss–Kronrod rule (G7K15) on each.
Kronrod nodes are interior, so $f$ is never evaluated at breakpoints or interval endpoints.
`with_extrapolation(true)` extrapolates the bisection sequence with Wynn ε to accelerate convergence at singular piece endpoints.
Extrapolation is disabled by default.
Convergence terminates with `ToleranceReached`; adaptive estimates and statuses follow the same rules as [Embedded pairs and globally adaptive quadrature](../adaptive-quadrature/).
This suits nonsingular but nonsmooth points such as integrand jumps or corners.

The one-shot function `integrate_piecewise(f, a, b, &points)` creates a `breakpoints` Plan with default `WeightedQuadratureOptions` (tolerance $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, 50 bisections, no extrapolation) and integrates once.
Only on success does it return a value and error estimate in `Integral`; it returns neither evaluation count nor termination status.
Reaching a limit returns an error instead of an estimate; use the Plan to change limits or receive a `BudgetLimit` estimate.

The next cell integrates the function $f(x)=e^x$ ($x<c$), $f(x)=e^x/2$ ($x\ge c$), whose value jumps to half at interior point $c$, over $[0,1]$.
The closed form is $(e^c-1)+(e-e^c)/2$.
Compare four methods: `integrate_piecewise`, a `breakpoints` Plan with the same split, `AdaptiveIntegrator` without breakpoints (G7K15, defaults), and tanh–sinh with $c$ supplied to `singular_points`.
The input is jump location $c$; $x$ and the integral are dimensionless.
The reference exponential uses f64 standard functions.
The one-shot row leaves evaluation count and termination status blank because it has neither.
The outcome reports the termination status of the `breakpoints` Plan.

```rust
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]

use scioxide_integrate::{

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let digits15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let c = jump_point;
let interval = Interval::new(0.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Closed form: (e^c − 1) + (e − e^c)/2 (reference using f64 standard functions).
let exact = (c.exp() - 1.0) + (1.0_f64.exp() - c.exp()) / 2.0;

// One-shot call: use the breakpoints Plan once with defaults.
let one_shot = integrate_piecewise(f, 0.0, 1.0, &[c]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let plan =
    WeightedQuadraturePlan::breakpoints(interval, &[c], options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (piecewise, piecewise_report) = plan.integrate(f).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let adaptive =
    AdaptiveIntegrator::new(AdaptiveRule::GaussKronrod, SubdivisionOptions::default())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (plain, plain_report) = adaptive.integrate(f, interval).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let domain = DoubleExponentialDomain::Finite {
    interval,
    singular_points: &[c],
};
let de = DoubleExponentialIntegrator::new(domain, LevelOptions::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let point = |p: DoubleExponentialPoint<f64>| f(p.coordinate());
let (de_value, de_report) = de.integrate(&point).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    "integrate_piecewise".to_string(),
    one_shot.value(),
    (one_shot.value() - exact).abs(),
    Some(one_shot.error_estimate()),
    None,
    None,
)];

    ("breakpoints Plan", piecewise, &piecewise_report),
    ("Adaptive G7K15 without breakpoints", plain, &plain_report),
    ("tanh–sinh（singular_points）", de_value, &de_report),
] {

        name.to_string(),
        value,
        (value - exact).abs(),

    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })

    Outcome::satisfied("The Plan with breakpoints satisfied the tolerance")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("The Plan with breakpoints stopped before satisfying the tolerance")
};

    outcome
        .status(piecewise_report.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Evaluation count", piecewise_report.evaluations() as f64, None)
);
```

At the default $c=0.3$, `integrate_piecewise` and the `breakpoints` Plan returned the same value; the Plan applied G7K15 once to each of two pieces, using 30 evaluations to reach `ToleranceReached`.
Its difference from the closed form was $2 \times 10^{-16}$.
Without breakpoints, `AdaptiveIntegrator` repeatedly bisected the jump-containing interval, using 735 evaluations with a discrepancy still $1.4 \times 10^{-9}$.
tanh–sinh used 106 evaluations with discrepancy $3 \times 10^{-15}$.
At $c=0.5$, the first bisection midpoint coincides with the jump, giving the same value in 45 evaluations even without breakpoints.

## Summary

Use `DoubleExponentialIntegrator` for endpoint singularities and infinite intervals, evaluating singular factors using the distance in `nearest_endpoint()`.
Reconstructing endpoint distances from rounded coordinates loses distance information beyond $t \approx 3.2$.
DE convergence uses only `HeuristicToleranceReached`; tail estimates are empirical, so check the status of `TailDiagnostics` and an independent reference.
Endpoints with singularity exponent $\alpha$ near 1 tend to give `Stagnation`; handle the singular factor analytically using endpoint weights.
Make known interior singularities piece endpoints with `singular_points`, and use `WeightedQuadraturePlan::breakpoints` for jumps and corners.
For one integration with defaults, `integrate_unbounded` and `integrate_piecewise` suffice; use a Plan to change tolerances or obtain evaluation counts, termination statuses, or estimates after reaching a limit.
