Monte Carlo methods place points randomly for high-dimensional integrals or discontinuous integrands for which deterministic rules converge slowly.
Their errors describe statistical uncertainty attached to estimates, differing in nature from error estimates in [Tensor-product rules and Genz–Malik adaptive cubature](../multidimensional-and-monte-carlo/).
This page covers uniform Monte Carlo estimators, standard errors, intervals, replicates, statistics read from `MonteCarloIntegrator` workspace, and reproducibility through sample blocks, then examines rare-event difficulties with high-dimensional ball volumes.
[Importance-sampling proposals and effective sample size](../importance-sampling-proposals/) covers importance sampling, [Adaptive Monte Carlo (VEGAS and MISER)](../adaptive-monte-carlo/) covers learned variance reduction, and [Sparse grids and randomized quasi-Monte Carlo](../sparse-grids-and-qmc/) covers randomized quasi-Monte Carlo.

## Uniform Monte Carlo estimator

Uniform sampling with `MonteCarloIntegrator::new(&bounds, MonteCarloOptions)` draws $n$ independent points $\boldsymbol X_1, \dots, \boldsymbol X_n$ uniformly from box $B$ of volume $V = \prod_k (b_k - a_k)$ and uses the sample mean of observations $Y_i = V f(\boldsymbol X_i)$ as the estimate:

$$
\hat I_n = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n Y_i = \frac{V}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n f(\boldsymbol X_i)
$$


### Unbiasedness and standard error

Since $\boldsymbol X$ has density $1/V$, $\mathbb E[Y] = V \int_B f(\boldsymbol x)\,d\boldsymbol x / V = I$, and linearity of expectation gives $\mathbb E[\hat I_n] = I$.
Thus $\hat I_n$ is an unbiased estimator.
If observation variance $\sigma^2 = \operatorname{Var}(Y)$ is finite, independence gives estimator variance and standard error (SE)

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\hat I_n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}, \qquad \operatorname{SE} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}
$$


The exponent $-1/2$ does not depend on dimension.
Reducing error by a factor of 10 requires 100 times as many samples; this relationship is unchanged by increasing dimension if $\sigma$ stays the same.
However, $\sigma$ depends on integrand variability, and for many functions it grows with dimension.

Since $\sigma$ is unknown, replace it using sample variance $s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_i (Y_i - \hat I_n)^2$ and return $\widehat{\operatorname{SE}} = s/\sqrt n$.
By the central limit theorem, $(\hat I_n - I)/\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}$ approaches a standard normal distribution for large $n$.
By default, `MonteCarloIntegrator` uses a Student-t distribution with $n - 1$ degrees of freedom to return a two-sided confidence interval

$$
\hat I_n \pm t_{(1+p)/2,\,n-1}\,\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}
$$

Here $p$ is the confidence probability, defaulting to 0.95.
`MonteCarloOptions::with_interval_method(MeanIntervalMethod::NormalAsymptotic)` switches to a normal quantile.
This interval has approximately nominal coverage probability.
Contributions from regions never reached by samples do not appear in $s^2$, so the interval cannot capture them.
The reported standard error and interval describe statistical uncertainty, not an error bound.

### Replicates and variance bases

Setting $R \ge 2$ with `with_replicates(R)` constructs independent replicates $\hat I_1, \dots, \hat I_R$ with $n$ points each, using their mean $\bar I$ as the estimate.
Compute standard error from between-replicate variability:

$$
s_R^2 = \frac{1}{R-1} \sum_{r=1}^R (\hat I_r - \bar I)^2, \qquad \widehat{\operatorname{SE}} = \frac{s_R}{\sqrt R}
$$

The interval then has $R - 1$ degrees of freedom.
Replicates give valid standard errors for randomized quasi-Monte Carlo, whose points are correlated ([Sparse grids and randomized quasi-Monte Carlo](../sparse-grids-and-qmc/)); with independent samples, they also directly reveal the distribution of estimates.

`MonteCarloStatistics::basis()` identifies the basis of variance calculation through `VarianceBasis`.

| `VarianceBasis` | Estimate | Estimator variance | Degrees of freedom |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Observations` | Mean of $n$ observations in one run | $s^2/n$ | $n - 1$ |
| `Replicates` | Mean of $R$ replicates | $s_R^2/R$ | $R - 1$ |
| `Strata` | Sum of stratum estimates | Sum of stratum variances | Welch–Satterthwaite effective degrees of freedom |

[Adaptive Monte Carlo (VEGAS and MISER)](../adaptive-monte-carlo/) covers stratification.
For importance-sampling observations with weights $w_i = 1/q(\boldsymbol y_i)$, `effective_sample_size()` returns Kish effective sample size $(\sum_i w_i)^2 / \sum_i w_i^2$.
It equals $n$ for uniform sampling and decreases as weights become more unequal.
This is a proposal diagnostic, not degrees of freedom.
For bases other than `Observations`, it is `None`.
[Importance-sampling proposals and effective sample size](../importance-sampling-proposals/) explains its interpretation.

### Reproducible sample blocks

Samples are drawn in blocks of `SAMPLE_BLOCK` $= 1024$ samples.
Child $c$ (a single run, replicate, or stratum) uses $\lceil n_c/1024 \rceil$ blocks for its $n_c$ observations, numbered consecutively after children $0, \dots, c-1$.
Random block $b$ draws uniforms from the substream extracted by `root.substream(stream, b)` from root generator `root` in `RandomStreams::new(&root, stream)`.
Each block is summarized sequentially; summaries combine in block order and children in index order.
Thus the same root, `stream`, plan, and integrand give bitwise identical estimates, statistics, and evaluation counts regardless of execution policy or worker count.
The root is read without being advanced.
Use a different `stream` number for a different calculation.

Uniform sources implement the sealed `UniformSource` trait, which cannot be implemented externally.
The only implementations are `RandomStreams` for independent pseudorandom samples and `&scioxide_random::QmcReplicatePlan` for randomized quasi-random sequence replicates.

### API and early stops

`MonteCarloOptions::default()` uses 4096 samples per replicate, one replicate, 95% Student-t intervals, and sequential execution.
Change these with `with_samples`, `with_replicates`, `with_confidence_probability`, `with_interval_method`, and `with_execution_policy`; they are checked at plan construction (fewer than two samples or zero replicates give `InvalidInput`).
`plan.workspace(components, ExecutionContext)` constructs `MonteCarloWorkspace`.
Pass an execution context because memory allocation depends on the worker count allowed by the execution policy.
`plan.integrate_into(f, source, &mut out, &mut workspace, scope)` writes the estimate to `out` and leaves statistics in workspace.
Because statistics live in workspace, `MonteCarloIntegrator` has no convenience `integrate` form that internally constructs and discards workspace.
The one-shot `integrate_monte_carlo` estimates a scalar integral once with defaults; see [One-shot integrate_monte_carlo](#one-shot-integrate_monte_carlo).

`workspace.statistics()` returns an `Option<MonteCarloStatistics>` for each real component.
`MonteCarloStatistics` has `estimate()`, `standard_error()`, `estimator_variance()` (an extended-exponent `Scaled` value), `degrees_of_freedom()`, `units()` (independent-unit count), `samples()` (total observation count), and `effective_sample_size()`.
`estimator_variance()` is estimator variance: sample variance divided by unit count for observations and replicates ($s^2/n$ or $s_R^2/R$), or the sum of stratum variances for strata.
This variance is retained separately from `standard_error()` and survives even when standard error is so small that it rounds to 0.
Intervals use the square root of this variance.
The interval quantile is computed and cached on the first `confidence_interval()` or `interval_report()` call, so these methods take `&mut self`.
Call them after applying `clone()` to statistics read from workspace.
`workspace.children()` returns per-child `MonteCarloChild` in index order; inspect each replicate or stratum with `report()`, `values()`, `statistics()`, and `effective_sample_size()`.

Handling of sample or evaluation limits, cancellation, and deadlines depends on the child configuration.
A single run stopped after at least one observation returns the mean so far as `Ok` with termination status `BudgetLimit` or `ExecutionStopped`.
For replicates and strata, the estimate is committed to output only after every child completes; any stopped child returns `ConvergenceFailure` or `ExecutionStopped` as an error.
Even then, `children()` shows how far each child progressed.
Normal completion after all prescribed samples is `SampleBudgetCompleted`.
In solve counts, each observation counts as an evaluation combining one integrand call with the number of uniform random numbers used.
The report's `evaluations()` counts integrand calls.

## High-dimensional balls and rare events

Monte Carlo's convergence exponent is dimension-independent, but high-dimensional integrals are not always easy.
The $d$-dimensional unit ball $B_d = \{\boldsymbol x : \|\boldsymbol x\|_2 \le 1\}$ has volume

$$
V_d = \frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2 + 1)}
$$


The probability $p_d = V_d / 2^d$ that a uniform point from its enclosing cube $[-1, 1]^d$ of volume $2^d$ lies inside the ball falls rapidly: $\pi/4 \approx 0.785$ at $d = 2$, $0.0807$ at $d = 6$, $2.49\times10^{-3}$ at $d = 10$, and $3.6\times10^{-6}$ at $d = 16$.

When integrating the indicator $\mathbf 1_{B_d}$ over the cube to obtain ball volume, $Y_i$ takes value $2^d$ with probability $p$ and 0 otherwise.
The estimate's relative standard error is

$$
\frac{\operatorname{SE}}{V_d} = \sqrt{\frac{1 - p}{N p}} \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{N p}} \quad (p \ll 1)
$$

Here $N$ is the total observation count.
When the expected hit count $Np$ is small, relative error exceeds 1.
If no sample enters the ball, the estimate and sample variance are both 0, giving the zero-width interval $[0, 0]$.
The reported standard error 0 then indicates missing information rather than high precision.
Avoid this problem with importance sampling ([Importance-sampling proposals and effective sample size](../importance-sampling-proposals/)) or coordinates adapted to the domain, such as polar coordinates ([Coordinate maps](oxref:integrate-coordinate-maps)).

## Ball volume and replicate statistics

Estimate unit-ball volume by uniform Monte Carlo integration of its indicator over the cube.
The following definitions block contains the indicator `ball_indicator` and exact volume `ball_volume` shared by three cells.
The exact value is a reference computed with SciOxide `gamma` and f64 `powf`.

```rust
//| id: integrate-ball-volume-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Unit-ball indicator and d-dimensional unit-ball volume"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_special::gamma;

// Unit-ball indicator. Its integral over the cube [−1, 1]^d is the ball volume.
pub(crate) fn ball_indicator(x: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    Ok(if x.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>() <= 1.0 {
        1.0
    } else {
        0.0
    })
}

// V_d = π^(d/2) / Γ(d/2 + 1) (reference from SciOxide gamma and f64 powf).
pub(crate) fn ball_volume(d: usize) -> Result<f64, String> {
    let half = d as f64 / 2.0;
    Ok(std::f64::consts::PI.powf(half) / gamma(half + 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
}
```

The plot shows each replicate's estimate from `children()` and ±1 standard error computed from the observations within that replicate.

This zero standard error is not evidence of precision.

### One-shot integrate_monte_carlo

The one-shot `integrate_monte_carlo(f, &lower, &upper, &rng)` estimates a scalar integral once with `MonteCarloOptions::default()` settings (4096 samples, one replicate, 95% Student-t interval), returning `MonteCarloStatistics`.
It draws uniform samples from stream 0 with generator `rng` as root without advancing the root, so passing the same root gives identical statistics.
It returns neither workspace nor `children()`; use `MonteCarloIntegrator` for replicates, strata, importance sampling, or different sample counts.
Dimension 0, zero-width axes, and mismatched corner lengths are rejected before evaluation; unsuccessful termination becomes an error.
Unlike other functions in [One-shot functions and configured plans](oxref:integrate-choosing-one-shot), it returns statistics directly rather than `Integral`, allowing direct access to standard error and intervals.

The following cell compares `integrate_monte_carlo` with `MonteCarloIntegrator` using identical defaults on streams 0 and 1 of the same root.

let one_shot = integrate_monte_carlo(ball_indicator, &lower, &upper, &root);
let mut results = vec![(
    "integrate_monte_carlo",
    one_shot.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
)];
// Execute the plan with identical defaults on streams 0 and 1.
let bounds = vec![Interval::new(-1.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?; d];
let plan = MonteCarloIntegrator::new(&bounds, MonteCarloOptions::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let context = ExecutionContext::sequential();
for (name, stream) in [
    ("MonteCarloIntegrator (stream 0)", 0),
    ("MonteCarloIntegrator (stream 1)", 1),
] {
    let mut workspace = plan.workspace(1, context).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (streams, mut value) = (RandomStreams::new(&root, stream), 0.0_f64);
    let _ = plan
        .integrate_into(ball_indicator, streams, &mut value, &mut workspace, context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    results.push((name, workspace.statistics()[0].clone().ok_or("Missing statistics")?));
}
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, mut stats) in results {
    let interval = stats.confidence_interval().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (value, se) = (stats.estimate(), stats.standard_error());
    let dof = stats.degrees_of_freedom().map(|v| v.round() as u64);
    let (low, high) = (interval.lower(), interval.upper());
    rows.push((
        name,
        stats.samples(),
        value,
        value - exact,
        se,
        low,
        high,
        dof,
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Call" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample count [points]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Estimated volume [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference from exact value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },

]);
emit_table_with_columns!(

    rows,

With defaults ($d = 4$, seed 4), `integrate_monte_carlo` and the stream-0 plan return the same estimate 5.0391, standard error 0.116, and interval $[4.811, 5.267]$, agreeing in all displayed digits.
The stream-1 plan gives estimate 4.7969, providing another independent estimate from the same root.
Use the one-shot call when the default 4096 samples give sufficient accuracy; use `MonteCarloIntegrator` to change sample or replicate counts, inspect child results, or use vector values or importance sampling.

## Summary

Uniform Monte Carlo estimates are unbiased, with standard error decreasing as $N^{-1/2}$ independently of dimension; the constant depends on integrand variability, and low hit probabilities increase relative error.
`MonteCarloIntegrator` writes the estimate to output, stores standard error, intervals, and degrees of freedom in `MonteCarloWorkspace::statistics()`, and leaves replicate or stratum results in `children()`.
`VarianceBasis` identifies whether variance comes from observations, replicates, or strata, determining degrees of freedom and interval interpretation.
Reported standard errors and intervals describe statistical uncertainty and omit contributions from unsampled regions; check with different seeds or exact values.
Sample blocks and `RandomStreams` make results reproducible across execution policies for the same root and `stream` number.
A scalar integral adequately sampled by the default 4096 points can use one-shot `integrate_monte_carlo`; passing the same root gives the same statistics as a stream-0 plan.
