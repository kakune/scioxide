Newton–Cotes formulas with equally spaced nodes are used to accumulate regularly recorded data (flow rate, power, acceleration, and so on) or obtain a first approximation when integrand properties are unknown.
Their weights follow by integrating the interpolating polynomial through equally spaced nodes.
This page derives the weights, represents error with Peano kernels, examines negative weights in higher-order stencils and composite-rule convergence orders, and outlines `NewtonCotes` usage.
The trapezoidal error expansion and Romberg extrapolation that eliminates its terms are covered in [Euler–Maclaurin expansion and Romberg extrapolation](../romberg-extrapolation/).

## Notation and step conventions

This page denotes the spacing of adjacent nodes (grid spacing) by $h$.
One panel occupied by an $n$-node stencil has width $Wh$: $W=n-1$ for a closed stencil (including both panel endpoints) and $W=n+1$ for an open stencil (excluding both).
For a composite rule dividing $[a,b]$ into $P$ panels, $h=(b-a)/(PW)$.
The `panels` of `NewtonCotes::new(nodes, endpoints, panels)` is this panel count $P$, not the number of grid subintervals.
For example, constructing Simpson's rule ($n=3$, closed) with `panels` $=P$ gives $2P$ subintervals and $2P+1$ evaluations.

## Deriving weights by Lagrange interpolation

The polynomial interpolating values $f(x_j)$ at nodes $x_j=a+jh$ ($j=0,\dots,W$) is

$$
p(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{W} f(x_j)\,\ell_j(x), \qquad \ell_j(x) = \prod_{k\ne j}\frac{x-x_k}{x_j-x_k}
$$


Integrating it over the panel gives the closed Newton–Cotes formula.

$$
\int_{x_0}^{x_W} p(x)\,dx = \sum_{j=0}^{W} w_j\,f(x_j), \qquad w_j = \int_{x_0}^{x_W}\ell_j(x)\,dx .
$$

Introducing the variable $t=(x-x_0)/h$ expresses weights as $h$ times dimensionless coefficients.

$$
w_j = h\,C_j, \qquad C_j = \int_0^{W}\prod_{k\ne j}\frac{t-k}{j-k}\,dt .
$$

Interpolation at $n$ nodes reproduces polynomials through degree $n-1$, so the degree of exactness is at least $n-1$.
If nodes are symmetric about the panel center and $n$ is odd, the integral of the odd function $(x-c)^n$ is also correctly 0, raising the degree of exactness to $n$.
The main stencils are as follows.
The error has the form $E=\int f\,dx - Q = C\,h^{m+1}f^{(m)}(\xi)$, where $\xi$ is some point in the panel and $m$ is the degree of exactness $+1$.

| Rule | Nodes $n$ | Stencil | Panel width | Weights (in units of $h$) | Degree of exactness | One-panel error $E$ |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| Midpoint | 1 | Open | $2h$ | $2$ | 1 | $\frac{1}{3}h^3 f''(\xi)$ |
| Trapezoidal | 2 | Closed | $h$ | $\frac12,\ \frac12$ | 1 | $-\frac{1}{12}h^3 f''(\xi)$ |
| Simpson | 3 | Closed | $2h$ | $\frac13,\ \frac43,\ \frac13$ | 3 | $-\frac{1}{90}h^5 f^{(4)}(\xi)$ |
| Simpson's 3/8 | 4 | Closed | $3h$ | $\frac38,\ \frac98,\ \frac98,\ \frac38$ | 3 | $-\frac{3}{80}h^5 f^{(4)}(\xi)$ |
| Boole | 5 | Closed | $4h$ | $\frac{14}{45},\ \frac{64}{45},\ \frac{24}{45},\ \frac{64}{45},\ \frac{14}{45}$ | 5 | $-\frac{8}{945}h^7 f^{(6)}(\xi)$ |

The error formulas hold when $f$ is $C^m$ (continuously differentiable through order $m$).

## Midpoint and trapezoidal errors from Peano kernels

Only in this section, let a single interval $[a,b]$ have width $H=b-a$ and midpoint $c=(a+b)/2$.
For the trapezoidal rule, $H=h$; for midpoint and Simpson, $H=2h$.
Both midpoint $M=Hf(c)$ and trapezoidal $T=\frac H2\bigl(f(a)+f(b)\bigr)$ integrate linear polynomials exactly.
For a rule exact on linear polynomials, when $f\in C^2[a,b]$, its error is a weighted integral of the second derivative $f''$ (the Peano kernel theorem).

$$
E[f] = \int_a^b K(t)\,f''(t)\,dt .
$$

The kernel $K$ depends on the rule, with

$$
K_M(t)=\begin{cases}\frac12(t-a)^2 & (a\le t<c)\\ \frac12(b-t)^2 & (c\le t\le b)\end{cases},
\qquad
K_T(t) = -\frac12(t-a)(b-t)
$$


Both kernels have constant sign on the interval ($K_M\ge0$, $K_T\le0$), so the integral mean value theorem gives

$$
E_M = \frac{H^3}{24}f''(\xi_M), \qquad E_T = -\frac{H^3}{12}f''(\xi_T)
$$


If $f''$ is nearly constant, trapezoidal error is approximately $-2$ times midpoint error.
For convex functions ($f''>0$), trapezoidal overestimates the true value and midpoint underestimates it.

## Simpson's rule and increased order

Combining the two errors to cancel their leading terms gives

$$
S = \frac{2M+T}{3} = \frac H6\bigl(f(a)+4f(c)+f(b)\bigr)
$$

which is Simpson's rule.
Treating $f''$ as constant makes the error $\frac13\bigl(2\cdot\frac{H^3}{24}-\frac{H^3}{12}\bigr)f''=0$, eliminating the $H^3$ term.
Symmetry of nodes about the center $c$ makes the integral of $(x-c)^3$ correctly 0 as well, giving degree of exactness 3.
Extending the Peano kernel to the fourth derivative gives, for $f\in C^4[a,b]$,

$$
E_S = -\frac{H^5}{2880}f^{(4)}(\xi) = -\frac{h^5}{90}f^{(4)}(\xi)
$$

where $H=2h$.

## Higher-order stencils and weight signs

More nodes increase the degree of exactness, but negative weights appear beyond certain node counts.
A large sum of absolute weights can amplify function-value errors $\delta$ up to $\sum_j|w_j|\,\delta$ in the sum.
`NewtonCotes` provides open stencils with 1–11 nodes and closed stencils with 2–11 nodes; larger stencils are not provided because their weights are large and alternate in sign.
Weights are computed exactly as rational numbers, then rounded once after mapping to the interval.

The next cell tabulates stencil properties for one-panel rules (`panels` $=1$).
`exactness_degree()` returns degree of exactness; `error_order()` returns the derivative order $m$ of the error term; `error_coefficient()` returns coefficient $C$ in the error formula above; and `weight_amplification()` returns $\sum_j|w_j|/W$ (the sum of absolute unit-spacing weights divided by panel width).
`weights()` gives weights at unit spacing $h=1$, and negative weights are counted as well.
Check agreement with the table coefficients (trapezoidal $-1/12$, Simpson $-1/90$, Boole $-8/945$, midpoint $1/3$) and identify stencils with amplification above 1.

```rust
//| id: integrate-newton-cotes-stencils
//| ref: integrate-newton-cotes-stencils
//| title: "Newton–Cotes degree of exactness, error coefficients, and weight amplification by stencil"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| inputs:
//|   endpoint_kind: {"type":"select","label":"Endpoint treatment","description":"Closed stencils include both panel endpoints; open stencils exclude them.","options":["Closed","Open"],"value":"Closed"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"open","label":"Open stencils","description":"Open stencils start with one node (midpoint), and their amplification exceeds 1 sooner than for closed stencils.","inputs":{"endpoint_kind":"Open"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"stencils","path":"/rows/1/4","approx":{"value":-0.011111111111111112,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"stencils","path":"/rows/6/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"stencils","path":"/rows/7/3","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"stencils","path":"/rows/9/5","range":{"min":1.5,"max":10}}
//|   - case: open
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"stencils","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.3333333333333333,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::Budget;
use scioxide_integrate::{NewtonCotes, NewtonCotesEndpoints};

let (endpoints, smallest) = match endpoint_kind.as_str() {
    "Open" => (NewtonCotesEndpoints::Open, 1),
    _ => (NewtonCotesEndpoints::Closed, 2),
};
// Read stencil properties (degree of exactness, error term, weights) from one-panel rules.
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for nodes in smallest..=11 {
    let rule =
        NewtonCotes::<f64>::new(nodes, endpoints, Budget::ONE).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let negative = rule
        .weights()
        .iter()
        .filter(|&&weight| weight < 0.0)
        .count();
    rows.push((
        nodes,
        rule.exactness_degree(),
        rule.error_order(),
        negative,
        rule.error_coefficient(),
        rule.weight_amplification(),
    ));
}
let sci4 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Node count n [nodes]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Degree of exactness [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Error-term derivative order m [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Negative weights [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Error coefficient C [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Weight amplification Σ|w_j|/W [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "stencils" })
);
```


For closed stencils, weights at 2–8 and 10 nodes were all positive, with amplification 1.
The 9-node stencil had 3 negative weights and amplification $1.45$; the 11-node stencil had amplification $3.06$.
For open stencils, negative weights already appeared at 3 nodes (amplification $1.67$), reaching amplification $96$ at 11 nodes.
Error coefficients matched the table (trapezoidal $-0.08333$, Simpson $-0.01111$, 3/8 $-0.0375$, Boole $-0.008466$, midpoint $0.3333$).
For high accuracy, use composite rules repeating low-order stencils over many panels or [Gaussian rules](../gaussian-and-clenshaw-curtis/) with freely chosen nodes, rather than increasing stencil size.

## Composite rules and global convergence order

A composite rule divides $[a,b]$ into $P$ panels, applies the same stencil to each, and sums them.
Closed stencils evaluate shared endpoints only once, giving $P(n-1)+1$ evaluations.
The composite trapezoidal rule is

$$
T(h) = h\Bigl[\frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{P-1}f(a+kh)\Bigr], \qquad h=\frac{b-a}{P}
$$


Summing one-panel error $C h^{m+1}f^{(m)}(\xi_p)$ over $P=(b-a)/(Wh)$ panels gives, for continuous $f^{(m)}$ by the intermediate value theorem,

$$
E = \sum_{p=1}^{P} C\,h^{m+1}f^{(m)}(\xi_p) = \frac{C}{W}(b-a)\,h^m f^{(m)}(\eta)
$$


For trapezoidal, $-\frac{b-a}{12}h^2f''(\eta)$; for Simpson, $-\frac{b-a}{180}h^4f^{(4)}(\eta)$; the global error is $O(h^m)$.
As $h\to0$, panel sums approach an integral, so the leading error term can also be written

$$
E \approx \frac{C}{W}\,h^m\bigl[f^{(m-1)}(b)-f^{(m-1)}(a)\bigr]
$$


The next cell integrates $f(x)=e^{\lambda x}$ over $[0,1]$, plotting the difference from the exact value $(e^\lambda-1)/\lambda$ against the evaluation count $N$.
The input is the growth rate $\lambda$.
Solid lines show actual errors of composite trapezoidal, Simpson, and Boole rules; dashed lines use `error_coefficient()` and the leading-error formula above, with $f^{(m-1)}(1)-f^{(m-1)}(0)=\lambda^{m-1}(e^\lambda-1)$.
On logarithmic axes, the expected slopes are $-2$, $-4$, and $-6$.
The table shows observed orders from error ratios at 16 and 32 panels and error-to-leading-term ratios at 32 panels.
The last column compares the same grid samples passed to `integrate_samples_into` with `integrate`, verifying that stored equally spaced samples can use the same rule.
Errors at most $10^{-14}$ are at rounding level and omitted from the plot, with omitted counts in the final table column.

```rust
//| id: integrate-composite-orders
//| ref: integrate-composite-orders
//| title: "Compare composite trapezoidal, Simpson, and Boole errors by evaluation count and leading-error prediction"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| inputs:
//|   growth_rate: {"type":"range","label":"Exponential growth rate λ [-]","description":"Change the magnitude λ^m e^λ of higher derivatives of e^(λx).","min":0.5,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"steep","label":"λ = 8","description":"Larger higher derivatives increase error for the same evaluation count without changing the slopes.","inputs":{"growth_rate":8}}
//|   - {"id":"gentle","label":"λ = 0.5","description":"Boole error is 0 at 32 panels, making observed order undefined.","inputs":{"growth_rate":0.5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":4.0,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":6.0,"atol":0.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":1,"max":6}}
//|   - case: steep
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":4.0,"atol":0.1}}
//|   - case: gentle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":null}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, Interval, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{FixedRule, FixedRuleNode, NewtonCotes, NewtonCotesEndpoints};

let lambda = growth_rate;
let interval = Interval::new(0.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Reference (closed form using f64 standard functions): ∫₀¹ e^{λx} dx = (e^λ − 1)/λ.
let exact = lambda.exp_m1() / lambda;
let integrand = |x: f64| Ok(RealScalar::exp(lambda * x));

let mut series = Vec::new();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, nodes) in [("Trapezoidal", 2_usize), ("Simpson", 3), ("Boole", 5)] {
    let (mut observed, mut predicted, mut errors) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
    let mut sample_gap = 0.0;
    let mut dropped = 0_usize;
    for level in 0..=9_u32 {
        let panels = Budget::try_from(1_usize << level).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let rule = NewtonCotes::new(nodes, NewtonCotesEndpoints::Closed, panels)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let (value, _) = rule
            .integrate(&integrand, interval)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        // Leading-error prediction (f64 standard functions): (C/W) h^m λ^(m−1) (e^λ − 1), W = n − 1.
        let width = (nodes - 1) as f64;
        let spacing = 1.0 / ((1_usize << level) as f64 * width);
        let m = rule.error_order() as i32;
        let leading = rule.error_coefficient() / width
            * spacing.powi(m)
            * lambda.powi(m - 1)
            * lambda.exp_m1();
        let error = exact - value;
        let evaluations = rule.node_count() as f64;
        // Omit rounding-level and zero-error points from the logarithmic plot and show their counts in the table.
        if error.abs() > 1e-14 {
            observed.push([evaluations, error.abs()]);
        } else {
            dropped += 1;
        }
        predicted.push([evaluations, leading.abs()]);
        errors.push((error, leading));
        if level == 5 {
            // Compare with the value obtained by passing the same grid samples to integrate_samples_into.
            let mut grid = vec![FixedRuleNode::new(0.0, 0.0, None); rule.node_count()];
            rule.map_nodes_into(interval, &mut grid)
                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            let samples: Vec<f64> = grid
                .iter()
                .map(|node| RealScalar::exp(lambda * node.point()))
                .collect();
            let mut from_samples = 0.0;
            rule.integrate_samples_into(&samples, spacing, &mut from_samples, &mut [0.0])
                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            sample_gap = (from_samples - value).abs();
        }
    }
    // Compute observed order from the error ratio at 16 and 32 panels and compare with the leading error term at 32 panels.
    // If rounding makes the error 0, the order is undefined.
    let (coarse, _) = errors[4];
    let (fine, leading) = errors[5];
    let order = (coarse != 0.0 && fine != 0.0).then(|| RealScalar::log2((coarse / fine).abs()));
    rows.push((
        name,
        order,
        fine / leading,
        sample_gap,
        dropped,
    ));
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": observed }));
    series.push(serde_json::json!({
        "name": format!("{name} (leading error term)"),
        "points": predicted,
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Evaluation count N [count]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute error |I − Q| [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 2.0,
        "xMax": 2100.0
    })
);
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Composite rule" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Observed order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Error / leading error term [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference from sampled-data value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Points omitted from plot [points]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })
);
```

At the default $\lambda=2$, observed orders were $2.000$, $3.999$, and $6.003$; error-to-leading-term ratios were $0.9999$ for trapezoidal and Simpson and $0.998$ for Boole.
Dashed and solid lines coincide beyond a few dozen evaluations, and Boole error reaches rounding level after a few hundred.
With defaults, 4 Boole points reached this level and were omitted.
At $\lambda=8$, error at the same evaluation count is several orders of magnitude larger, but observed orders $1.996$, $3.992$, and $5.991$ show unchanged slopes.
Conversely, at $\lambda=0.5$, Boole error falls below rounding level to 0 at 32 panels, leaving observed order undefined.
Values from `integrate_samples_into` agreed with `integrate` within $10^{-13}$.

## Summary

Newton–Cotes weights follow by integrating the interpolant on equally spaced nodes; Peano kernels give error order and sign.
The `panels` of `NewtonCotes::new(nodes, endpoints, panels)` counts panels, grid spacing is $h=(b-a)/(PW)$, and composite-rule error decreases as $O(h^m)$.
`error_coefficient()` and `error_order()` predict the leading error term, agreeing closely with actual error.
Many closed stencils with at least 9 nodes and open stencils with at least 3 have negative weights and `weight_amplification()` above 1, so improve accuracy by increasing panels rather than stencil size.
Since `NewtonCotes` returns no error estimate, check it by comparing panel counts.
