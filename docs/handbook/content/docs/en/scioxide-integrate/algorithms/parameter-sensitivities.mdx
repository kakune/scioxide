Optimization, parameter identification, and uncertainty propagation require both integral values and their dependence on parameters.
This page differentiates the one-dimensional integral depending on parameters $p \in \mathbb{R}^P$:

$$
I(p) = \int_{a(p)}^{b(p)} f(x, p)\,dx \in \mathbb{R}^m
$$


$f$ has $m$ real components, and endpoints $a$ and $b$ may also depend on parameters.
`QuadratureSensitivityPlan` computes the integral together with its derivative as a directional derivative (JVP) or adjoint product (VJP).

---

## Differentiating integrals and Leibniz's rule

If $f$ and $\partial_p f$ are continuous on a domain containing the integration interval and endpoints $a(p)$ and $b(p)$ are differentiable, differentiation and integration commute, giving the derivative in direction $v \in \mathbb{R}^P$:

$$
DI(p)\,v = \int_{a}^{b} J(x, p)\,v\,dx + f(b, p)\,\bigl(\nabla b \cdot v\bigr) - f(a, p)\,\bigl(\nabla a \cdot v\bigr)
$$


This is Leibniz's integral rule.
$J(x, p) = \partial f / \partial p$ is the $m \times P$ Jacobian with respect to parameters at fixed $x$.
The first term describes changes in the integrand; the remaining two describe moving endpoints.
Complex values or parameters count as two real components, their real and imaginary parts.

If $f$ has an interior discontinuity moving with $p$, a jump contribution appears and the formula above no longer holds.
The plan does not correct for this contribution; split the integral with such points as interval endpoints.

---

## Forward (JVP) and reverse (VJP) modes

Compute two types of product without forming the Jacobian $\partial I / \partial p$ ($m \times P$).

- A JVP (Jacobian–vector product) is $(\partial I/\partial p)\,v \in \mathbb{R}^m$ for direction $v \in \mathbb{R}^P$.
- A VJP (vector–Jacobian product) is $w^{\mathsf T}(\partial I/\partial p) \in \mathbb{R}^P$ for cotangent vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^m$.

Using endpoint gradients $\nabla a$ and $\nabla b$, Leibniz's rule for the VJP is

$$
w^{\mathsf T}\frac{\partial I}{\partial p} = \int_a^b J(x, p)^{\mathsf T} w\,dx + \bigl(w \cdot f(b, p)\bigr)\nabla b - \bigl(w \cdot f(a, p)\bigr)\nabla a
$$


The two products satisfy the duality relation $w \cdot \bigl((\partial I/\partial p)\,v\bigr) = \bigl(w^{\mathsf T}\partial I/\partial p\bigr) \cdot v$, useful for checking implementations.

If the whole Jacobian is needed, construct it from $P$ JVPs (one per parameter direction) or $m$ VJPs (one per output component).
Each call costs one integrand evaluation and one Jacobian application per node, plus one value-only evaluation per moving endpoint.
JVPs cost less with few parameters; VJPs cost less with few outputs, such as a scalar loss.

---

## Why discretize before differentiating

There are two approaches to differentiating the quadrature approximation $Q(p) = \sum_i w_i\,f(x_i, p)$.

1. Differentiate, then discretize: approximate $\int J\,v\,dx$ with quadrature separate from $Q$.
2. Discretize, then differentiate: differentiate the computed $Q(p)$ itself with respect to $p$.

When nodes $x_i$ and weights $w_i$ are independent of $p$, $\partial_p Q = \sum_i w_i\,J(x_i, p)$, so both approaches agree.
`QuadratureSensitivityPlan` approximates the integral and integrated Jacobian with the same Gauss–Kronrod rule and nodes; for fixed endpoints it returns the exact derivative of the computed integral up to rounding error.
This keeps values and derivatives consistent in gradient-based optimization and finite-difference checks.

Adaptive quadrature differs.
Refinement decisions compare error estimates, so a small change in $p$ can switch the partition discontinuously, making the computed $Q(p)$ only piecewise smooth in $p$.
At switching points, $Q$ has no derivative, and finite differences capture jumps caused by partition changes.
The plan does not differentiate refinement decisions.
It therefore provides a Frozen mode that fixes an adaptively chosen partition and computes values and derivatives on that same partition for nearby parameters.

When endpoints move with $p$, the partition is defined on the physical interval $[a, b]$.
The plan stores the interval at the current parameters; if $a(p)$ or $b(p)$ changes, prepare a new plan on the new interval.
The endpoint term $f(b)\,(\nabla b \cdot v)$ is added exactly with one value sample.

## Three discretization modes

`SensitivityDiscretization` describes the partition integrated by the plan and is read with `discretization()`.

| Mode | Construction | Partition | Termination status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Fixed` | `QuadratureSensitivityPlan::fixed(&integrand, interval, rule, options)` | One Gauss–Kronrod rule over the whole interval (`GaussKronrodRule`) | `FixedRuleCompleted` |
| `Adaptive` | `QuadratureSensitivityPlan::adaptive(&integrand, interval, options)` | Adaptive G7K15 bisection | `ToleranceReached` on convergence |
| `Frozen` | `QuadratureSensitivityPlan::frozen(&integrand, &partition, options)` | Reuse a previous call's partition without refinement | `FixedRuleCompleted` |

`Fixed` applies the rule once without checking tolerance; a zero-width interval gives `DegenerateDomain`.
The report separately indicates whether error estimates satisfy tolerances.
An interval too short to distinguish rule nodes returns `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).

`Adaptive` normalizes integral and derivative error estimates by their respective tolerances and bisects until the worse one satisfies tolerance.
Reaching limits such as `with_max_subdivisions` returns the current estimate with an unsuccessful termination status.

`plan.partition(&workspace)` extracts the preceding successful call's partition as `SensitivityPartition`; `intervals()` returns subintervals in summation order.
Partitions are independent of derivative mode (JVP or VJP), so a JVP plan's partition can be reused by a VJP `frozen` plan.
If the preceding call failed, this returns `InvalidInput`.

The derivative mode `SensitivityMode::{Jvp, Vjp}` is chosen during preparation with `SensitivityOptions::with_mode` and read with `mode()`.
Passing a VJP query to a JVP plan is an error; an integrand returning false from `supports(mode)` during preparation also gives `InvalidInput`.

## Implementing the integrand

Implement `SensitivityIntegrand`.

- `type Workspace` is integrand scratch space, such as an automatic differentiation tape; provide one per worker on each call.
- `parameter_dimension()` returns $P$, and `value_dimension()` returns $m$.
- `supports(mode)` reports whether JVPs and VJPs are supported.
- `evaluate(x, parameters, &mut sample, &mut workspace, execution)` computes the value and derivative at one node.

In `evaluate`, first write all components of $f(x, p)$ into `sample.value_mut()`, then apply the Jacobian $J(x, p)$ exactly once.
For JVP-only integrands, pass a scioxide-core `LinearOperator` to `sample.apply_forward(&operator, scratch)`; for integrands supporting both modes, pass an `AdjointOperator` to `sample.apply(&operator, scratch)`.
`apply` computes $Jv$ for JVP requests and $J^{\mathsf T}w$ for VJP requests.
Moving-endpoint samples need only values: `sample.mode()` returns `None`, and both application methods do nothing.
A call omitting the application or applying twice returns `InvalidInput`.
`scratch` must be at least as long as the operator's workspace length.

The operator returned by scioxide-core `ClosureOperator::new` implements only `LinearOperator`, so it directly supports only JVPs.
For VJPs, use `ClosureOperator` with a $J^{\mathsf T}w$ closure added through `with_adjoint`, or a small struct implementing both `LinearOperator` and `AdjointOperator`.
Jacobian operators from automatic differentiation can be passed in the same form.
For complex values, $J$ is the real Jacobian of real and imaginary parts; its transpose is the adjoint for inner product $\langle u, v\rangle = \mathrm{Re}(\bar u v)$.

## Options, reports, and error estimates

`SensitivityOptions::new(SubdivisionOptions)` takes primal integral tolerance, subdivision and evaluation limits, and execution policy from `SubdivisionOptions`.
The default derivative mode is JVP, with derivative tolerance using absolute and relative $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ and the root mean square norm.
`with_primal_tolerance` and `with_derivative_tolerance` set the tolerances separately; the latter includes endpoint terms.
The derivative tolerance has $m$ components for JVPs and $P$ for VJPs.
The `with_max_evaluations` limit includes endpoint evaluations.

`jvp_into(&integrand, JvpQuery, (&mut value, &mut jvp), (&mut workspace, &mut pool), scope)` writes the integral and JVP.
For moving endpoints, pass $[\nabla a \cdot v, \nabla b \cdot v]$ to `JvpQuery::new(&parameters, &direction)` through `with_endpoint_tangent(Some([da, db]))`.
`vjp_into` accepts `VjpQuery::new(&parameters, &cotangent)` and `with_endpoint_gradients(Some((&grad_a, &grad_b)))`, writing the integral and a VJP of length $P$.
Zero endpoint tangents or gradients add no integrand evaluations.
NaN or infinite parameters, directions, cotangents, endpoint tangents, or endpoint gradients are rejected before integration as `NonFinite` (origin `Input`).
Nonfinite integrand values or products give `NonFinite` (origin `Callback`).
`pool` is a sequence of integrand workspaces; under parallel execution, each worker uses one.

`SensitivityReport` returns `termination()`, `function_evaluations()` including endpoint evaluations and failed calls, Jacobian application count `derivative_evaluations()`, `subdivisions()`, `primal_tolerance_met()`, and `derivative_tolerance_met()`.
It has no `is_success()`; use `report.termination().is_success()`.
Both outputs hold whole-domain estimates even after unsuccessful termination.

Create `QuadratureSensitivityWorkspace` with `workspace()`; it does not reallocate on every call.
`value_errors()` returns componentwise `IntegrationErrorComponents` for the integral, and `derivative_errors()` returns componentwise `SensitivityErrorComponents` for the derivative.
In `SensitivityErrorComponents`, `quadrature()` estimates quadrature error in $\int J$, `boundary_roundoff()` is roundoff in endpoint terms (0 for fixed endpoints), and `total()` is their sum.
These estimates assume accurate integrand Jacobian products; they are not bounds.
A failed call invalidates error estimates and partitions until the next successful call.
The `SensitivityRequirements` returned by `workspace_requirements()` records interval, mode, rule size, dimensions, and maximum subinterval count; `check` and `try_reserve` check against it.
`last_attempt()` returns the preceding call's record.

The plan handles ordinary one-dimensional integrals on finite intervals.
It does not provide sensitivities for weighted, improper, complex-path, or multidimensional integrals.
For those, assemble Leibniz terms yourself and integrate a vector-valued integrand including $\partial_p f$ with the appropriate plan.

## JVPs and VJPs of a damped-oscillation integral

Integrate oscillations $e^{-kt}(\sin t, \cos t)$ with damping rate $k$ [1/s] from time 0 to $b$ [s]:

$$
I(k, b) = \int_0^b e^{-kt}\begin{pmatrix}\sin t\\ \cos t\end{pmatrix}dt
$$

Compute derivatives with respect to parameters $p = (k, b)$.
Angular frequency is 1 rad/s, and $I$ has units s.
There are $m = 2$ components and $P = 2$ parameters; $b$ does not enter the integrand, appearing only as a moving upper endpoint ($\nabla a = 0$, $\nabla b = (0, 1)$).
The Jacobian is $J(t, p) = \begin{pmatrix} -t f_1 & 0 \\ -t f_2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, where $f_1, f_2$ are the two components.

With $z = k - i$, the closed form is $I_2 + iI_1 = (1 - e^{-zb})/z$ and its derivatives are

$$
\frac{\partial (I_2 + iI_1)}{\partial k} = \frac{b\,e^{-zb} - (I_2 + iI_1)}{z}, \qquad \frac{\partial I}{\partial b} = e^{-kb}\begin{pmatrix}\sin b\\ \cos b\end{pmatrix}
$$


The upper-endpoint derivative equals the integrand's value there, $e^{-zb} = e^{-kb}(\cos b + i\sin b)$.

The following definitions block expresses the model in three Rust items and adds two types passed between cells.
`Jacobian` represents $J$ at node $t$, computing $Jv$ through `LinearOperator` and $J^{\mathsf T}w$ through `AdjointOperator`.
`DampedOscillation` implements `SensitivityIntegrand`, writes $f(t, p)$, then applies `Jacobian` once through `sample.apply`.
`exact_jacobian` uses scioxide-core `Complex` to return the closed-form $2 \times 2$ Jacobian $\partial I/\partial p$, used to check two cells.
`PlanSummary` represents one plan's report, and `Solve` contains values passed by the first cell (parameters, direction, JVP, and two plan reports); the later two cells receive this type.

```rust
//| id: integrate-sensitivity-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Damped-oscillation integrand, Jacobian, and closed form"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
use scioxide_core::{
    AdjointOperator, Complex, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, LinearOperator, RealScalar,
};
use scioxide_integrate::{SensitivityIntegrand, SensitivityMode, SensitivitySample};

// J(t, p) = [[−t f₁, 0], [−t f₂, 0]]. p = (k, b); b does not enter the integrand.
pub(crate) struct Jacobian {
    pub(crate) t: f64,
    pub(crate) values: [f64; 2],
}
impl LinearOperator<f64> for Jacobian {
    fn rows(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn cols(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn apply_workspace_len(&self) -> Result<usize, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(0)
    }
    fn apply_into(&self, v: &[f64], y: &mut [f64], _: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        y[0] = -self.t * self.values[0] * v[0];
        y[1] = -self.t * self.values[1] * v[0];
        Ok(())
    }
}
impl AdjointOperator<f64> for Jacobian {
    fn apply_adjoint_workspace_len(&self) -> Result<usize, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(0)
    }
    fn apply_adjoint_into(
        &self,
        w: &[f64],
        y: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut [f64],
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        y[0] = -self.t * (self.values[0] * w[0] + self.values[1] * w[1]);
        y[1] = 0.0;
        Ok(())
    }
}

// f(t, p) = e^(−kt) (sin t, cos t). Write the value, then apply the Jacobian once.
pub(crate) struct DampedOscillation;
impl SensitivityIntegrand<f64> for DampedOscillation {
    type Workspace = ();
    fn parameter_dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn value_dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn supports(&self, _: SensitivityMode) -> bool {
        true
    }
    fn evaluate(
        &self,
        t: f64,
        p: &[f64],
        sample: &mut SensitivitySample<'_, f64>,
        _: &mut (),
        _: ExecutionContext<'_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let decay = RealScalar::exp(-p[0] * t);
        let values = [decay * RealScalar::sin(t), decay * RealScalar::cos(t)];
        sample.value_mut().copy_from_slice(&values);
        sample.apply(&Jacobian { t, values }, &mut [])
    }
}

// Closed-form ∂I/∂p. Rows: components (sin, cos); columns: parameters (k, b).
pub(crate) fn exact_jacobian(k: f64, b: f64) -> [[f64; 2]; 2] {
    let z = Complex::new(k, -1.0);
    let e = (-(z * b)).exp();
    let integral = (Complex::new(1.0, 0.0) - e) / z;
    let d_k = (e * b - integral) / z;
    [[d_k.im, e.im], [d_k.re, e.re]]
}
/// Plan report passed by the first cell.
#[derive(serde::Serialize, serde::Deserialize)]
pub(crate) struct PlanSummary {
    pub(crate) name: String,
    pub(crate) status: String,
    pub(crate) success: bool,
    pub(crate) evaluations: usize,
    pub(crate) actions: usize,
    pub(crate) subdivisions: usize,
    pub(crate) primal_met: bool,
    pub(crate) derivative_met: bool,
    pub(crate) derivative_error: Option<f64>,
}

/// First-cell exports (parameters, direction, JVP, two plan reports).
#[derive(serde::Deserialize)]
pub(crate) struct Solve {
    pub(crate) parameters: [f64; 2],
    pub(crate) direction: [f64; 2],
    pub(crate) jvp: [f64; 2],
    pub(crate) plans: Vec<PlanSummary>,
}
```

The first cell computes a JVP in direction $v = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ with an adaptive JVP plan, freezes its partition to prepare a VJP plan, and computes a VJP with cotangent $w = (1, 0.5)$.
The table shows each product's difference from its closed form and the duality difference $w \cdot (Jv) - (J^{\mathsf T}w) \cdot v$.
Both primal and derivative absolute and relative tolerances are $10^{-12}$.
Inputs are $k$, $b$, and direction angle $\theta$; parameters, direction, JVP, and two plan reports pass to the later two cells.
This cell only solves; the third cell shows plan termination statuses and the outcome.

```rust

//| ref: integrate-sensitivity-jvp-vjp
//| title: "Compute JVPs and VJPs for an integral with a moving upper endpoint on the same partition"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [integrate-sensitivity-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| exports: [parameters, direction, jvp, plans]
//| inputs:
//|   decay_rate: {"type":"range","label":"Damping rate k [1/s]","description":"Decay rate of integrand e^(−kt).","min":0.1,"max":3,"step":0.1,"value":0.5}
//|   end_time: {"type":"range","label":"Upper endpoint b [s]","description":"Upper integration endpoint, moving as a parameter.","min":0.5,"max":20,"step":0.5,"value":6}
//|   direction_angle: {"type":"range","label":"Direction angle θ [deg]","description":"JVP direction v = (cos θ, sin θ).","min":0,"max":180,"step":15,"value":30}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"long-horizon","label":"b = 20 s","description":"A distant upper endpoint requires finer partitions and has a smaller endpoint contribution.","inputs":{"end_time":20}}
//|   - {"id":"endpoint-direction","label":"θ = 90°","description":"The direction moves only the upper endpoint; the JVP is almost entirely the endpoint term f(b).","inputs":{"direction_angle":90}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sensitivities","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sensitivities","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sensitivities","path":"/rows/4/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: long-horizon
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sensitivities","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-11}}
//|   - case: endpoint-direction
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sensitivities","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sensitivities","path":"/rows/4/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, Interval, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    JvpQuery, QuadratureSensitivityPlan, QuadratureSensitivityWorkspace, SensitivityMode,
    SensitivityOptions, SensitivityReport, SubdivisionOptions, VjpQuery,
};

let sci6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 6 });
let p = [decay_rate, end_time];
let angle = direction_angle.to_radians();
let v = [RealScalar::cos(angle), RealScalar::sin(angle)];
let w = [1.0, 0.5];
let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(1.0e-12_f64, 1.0e-12, NormSelection::Infinity)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options =
    SensitivityOptions::new(SubdivisionOptions::default().with_tolerance(tolerance.clone()))
        .with_derivative_tolerance(tolerance);
let interval = Interval::new(0.0, end_time).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Adaptive JVP. Upper-endpoint tangent ∇b·v = v₂; lower endpoint fixed.
let forward = QuadratureSensitivityPlan::adaptive(
    &DampedOscillation,
    interval,
    options.clone().with_mode(SensitivityMode::Jvp),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut forward_ws = forward.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut value, mut jvp) = ([0.0_f64; 2], [0.0_f64; 2]);
let forward_report = forward
    .jvp_into(
        &DampedOscillation,
        JvpQuery::new(&p, &v).with_endpoint_tangent(Some([0.0, v[1]])),
        (&mut value, &mut jvp),
        (&mut forward_ws, &mut [()]),
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// VJP on the same frozen partition. ∇a = 0, ∇b = (0, 1).
let partition = forward.partition(&forward_ws).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let reverse = QuadratureSensitivityPlan::frozen(
    &DampedOscillation,
    &partition,
    options.with_mode(SensitivityMode::Vjp),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut reverse_ws = reverse.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut reverse_value, mut vjp) = ([0.0_f64; 2], [0.0_f64; 2]);
let reverse_report = reverse
    .vjp_into(
        &DampedOscillation,
        VjpQuery::new(&p, &w).with_endpoint_gradients(Some((&[0.0, 0.0], &[0.0, 1.0]))),
        (&mut reverse_value, &mut vjp),
        (&mut reverse_ws, &mut [()]),
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Closed-form Jv and Jᵀw, and duality w·(Jv) − (Jᵀw)·v.
let j = exact_jacobian(decay_rate, end_time);
let exact_jvp = [
    j[0][0] * v[0] + j[0][1] * v[1],
    j[1][0] * v[0] + j[1][1] * v[1],
];
let exact_vjp = [
    w[0] * j[0][0] + w[1] * j[1][0],
    w[0] * j[0][1] + w[1] * j[1][1],
];
let duality = (w[0] * jvp[0] + w[1] * jvp[1]) - (vjp[0] * v[0] + vjp[1] * v[1]);
let row = |name, computed: f64, exact: f64| (name, computed, exact, (computed - exact).abs());
let rows = vec![
    row("(Jv)₁", jvp[0], exact_jvp[0]),
    row("(Jv)₂", jvp[1], exact_jvp[1]),
    row("(Jᵀw)_k", vjp[0], exact_vjp[0]),
    row("(Jᵀw)_b", vjp[1], exact_vjp[1]),
    row("w·(Jv) − (Jᵀw)·v", duality, 0.0),
];
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Computed value [s]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form [s]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference [s]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sensitivities" })
);

// Reports passed to the next cell: termination, evaluation counts, tolerance checks, and maximum derivative error estimate.
let summary =
    |name: &str, report: &SensitivityReport, ws: &QuadratureSensitivityWorkspace<f64>| {
        let errors = ws.derivative_errors();
        let worst = errors.and_then(|e| {
            e.iter()
                .try_fold(0.0_f64, |acc, e| e.total().map(|t| acc.max(t)))
        });
        PlanSummary {
            name: name.to_string(),
            status: report.termination().to_string(),
            success: report.termination().is_success(),
            evaluations: report.function_evaluations(),
            actions: report.derivative_evaluations(),
            subdivisions: report.subdivisions(),
            primal_met: report.primal_tolerance_met(),
            derivative_met: report.derivative_tolerance_met(),
            derivative_error: worst,
        }
    };
let plans = vec![
    summary("Adaptive JVP", &forward_report, &forward_ws),
    summary("VJP on frozen partition", &reverse_report, &reverse_ws),
];
export!(parameters, &p);
export!(direction, &v);
export!(jvp, &jvp);
export!(plans, &plans);
```

With defaults ($k = 0.5$ /s, $b = 6$ s, $\theta = 30°$), JVP and VJP components agree with closed forms within $10^{-15}$, and the duality difference is also at the roundoff scale.
At $\theta = 90°$ the direction moves only the upper endpoint, so the JVP is almost entirely the endpoint term $e^{-kb}(\sin b, \cos b)$.

The second cell receives the first cell's results and independently checks the JVP without the sensitivity plan.
Compute integral values at $p \pm hv$ using adaptive G7K15 (`AdaptiveIntegrator`, tolerance $10^{-13}$), then form the central difference $(I(p + hv) - I(p - hv))/(2h)$.
Use steps $h = 10^{-1}, \dots, 10^{-10}$ and plot differences from the JVP on logarithmic axes.
Central-difference truncation error is $O(h^2)$; roundoff and quadrature errors in integral values are divided by $h$ and contribute as $O(1/h)$, making the difference smallest at an intermediate $h$.
The first cell's values are received as the definitions-block type `Solve`.

```rust
//| ref: integrate-sensitivity-checks

//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| dependsOn:
//|   solve: {cell: integrate-sensitivity-jvp-vjp, type: Solve}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/5/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":1e-3,"max":1e-1}}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, Interval, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{AdaptiveIntegrator, AdaptiveRule, SubdivisionOptions};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let (p, v, jvp) = (solve.parameters, solve.direction, solve.jvp);

// Independent check: central difference (I(p + hv) − I(p − hv)) / (2h) of integral values from adaptive G7K15.
let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(1.0e-13, 1.0e-13, NormSelection::Infinity)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = SubdivisionOptions::default().with_tolerance(tolerance);
let plan =
    AdaptiveIntegrator::new(AdaptiveRule::GaussKronrod, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let primal = |q: [f64; 2]| -> Result<[f64; 2], String> {
    let f = |t: f64| {
        let decay = RealScalar::exp(-q[0] * t);
        Ok([decay * RealScalar::sin(t), decay * RealScalar::cos(t)])
    };
    let interval = Interval::new(0.0, q[1]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(plan.integrate(&f, interval).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.0)
};
let j = exact_jacobian(p[0], p[1]);
let exact = [
    j[0][0] * v[0] + j[0][1] * v[1],
    j[1][0] * v[0] + j[1][1] * v[1],
];
// Maximum difference across two components.
let gap = |a: [f64; 2], b: [f64; 2]| (a[0] - b[0]).abs().max((a[1] - b[1]).abs());
let (mut rows, mut curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for exponent in 1..=10 {
    let h = RealScalar::powi(10.0_f64, -exponent);
    let plus = primal([p[0] + h * v[0], p[1] + h * v[1]])?;
    let minus = primal([p[0] - h * v[0], p[1] - h * v[1]])?;
    let central = [
        (plus[0] - minus[0]) / (2.0 * h),
        (plus[1] - minus[1]) / (2.0 * h),
    ];
    let to_jvp = gap(central, jvp);
    rows.push((h, to_jvp, gap(central, exact)));
    // Exclude steps with exactly zero difference, which cannot appear on a logarithmic axis.
    if to_jvp > 0.0 {
        curve.push([h, to_jvp]);
    }
}
let style = serde_json::json!({ "showSymbol": true });
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "max|central difference − JVP|", "points": curve, "style": style })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Step h [-]",
        "yLabel": "Largest difference component [s]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Step h [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Difference from JVP [s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from closed form [s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "steps" })
);
```

With defaults, central-difference versus JVP differences decrease from $1.5 \times 10^{-2}$ to $1.5 \times 10^{-10}$ proportionally to $h^2$ as the step moves from $h = 10^{-1}$ to $10^{-5}$, reaching a minimum $4 \times 10^{-11}$ at $h = 10^{-6}$.
For smaller $h$, differences grow proportionally to $1/h$, reaching order $10^{-7}$ at $h = 10^{-10}$.
These differences arise from roundoff, so their values vary by factors of a few across execution environments.
The minimum difference remains far above the JVP's closed-form difference (about $10^{-16}$), making central differences a coarse check of JVP accuracy.

## Summary

A parameter-dependent integral's derivative is the sum of the integrated Jacobian at fixed $x$ and Leibniz terms from moving endpoints.
`QuadratureSensitivityPlan` approximates the integral and integrated Jacobian at the same nodes, so on a fixed partition it returns the derivative of the computed value up to roundoff.
Adaptive partitions are not smooth in $p$; extract them with `partition()`, reuse them through `frozen`, and prepare a new plan when endpoints move.
Choose JVPs for few parameters or VJPs for few outputs; for VJPs, provide an operator implementing `AdjointOperator`.
Read report fields `primal_tolerance_met()` and `derivative_tolerance_met()` separately; small primal error does not establish derivative accuracy.
