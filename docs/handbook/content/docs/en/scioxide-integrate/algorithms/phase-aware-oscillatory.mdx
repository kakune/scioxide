The `WeightedQuadraturePlan::fourier` in [Cancellation in oscillatory integrals and tails of Fourier integrals](../fourier-integrals-and-tails/) requires oscillatory factors with phases linear in position, such as $\cos(\omega x)$ and $\sin(\omega x)$.
This page treats integrals with nonlinear phases,

$$
I_c = \int_a^b f(x)\cos\bigl(\omega g(x)\bigr)\,dx,\qquad
I_s = \int_a^b f(x)\sin\bigl(\omega g(x)\bigr)\,dx
$$


Here $f$ is the amplitude, $g$ the phase function, and $\omega$ the angular frequency.
Examples include $g(x)=x^2$ for Fresnel diffraction by a straight edge, chirp signals with time-varying frequency, and phase integrals in WKB approximations.
The Levin and Filon methods of `OscillatoryQuadraturePlan` integrate using only information about smooth $f$ and $g$, without resolving oscillations.

## Oscillation count and stationary points

Ordinary quadrature needs several nodes or more per wavelength, so evaluations grow roughly proportionally to the oscillation count $|\omega|\,|g'|\,(b-a)/(2\pi)$.
If $g'$ never vanishes on the interval, integration by parts gives

$$
\int_a^b f e^{i\omega g}\,dx = \left[\frac{f}{i\omega g'}e^{i\omega g}\right]_a^b - \frac{1}{i\omega}\int_a^b \left(\frac{f}{g'}\right)' e^{i\omega g}\,dx
$$

and the integral decreases as $O(|\omega|^{-1})$.
Most oscillations cancel, leaving the value determined by endpoint information.
This expansion fails at stationary points where $g'(x_0)=0$.
Stationary phase gives a contribution of $O(|\omega|^{-1/2})$ concentrated in a neighborhood of width approximately $(|\omega||g''(x_0)|)^{-1/2}$.
Stationary neighborhoods must be handled by ordinary quadrature resolving the phase.

## Levin collocation

Levin's method finds a nonoscillatory solution of the ODE for the unknown $p$,

$$
p'(x) + i\omega g'(x)\,p(x) = f(x)
$$


Since $\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(p\,e^{i\omega g}\bigr) = f e^{i\omega g}$, the integral is $I = p(b)e^{i\omega g(b)} - p(a)e^{i\omega g(a)}$.
Smooth $f$ and $g'$ with $g'\neq 0$ admit a smooth $p$.

The implementation maps the interval to a standard interval and collocates a degree-24 polynomial $p$ at 25 Chebyshev extrema.
The complex collocation matrix is solved by Householder QR with column pivoting; since the solution is linear in $f$, its resulting weights dotted with amplitude samples give the integral.
The discretization estimate is the larger of four times the difference from the nested 13-node Levin value and the amplitude-resolution indicator described next; collocation solution error and endpoint-phase rounding are added separately as arithmetic error.
At large $|\omega|$, both 13- and 25-node values approach endpoint asymptotics, so their difference misses interior amplitude jumps, corners, and cusps.
The last two Chebyshev coefficients $c_{23}, c_{24}$ of the amplitude samples therefore give a frequency-independent indicator $2(|c_{23}|+|c_{24}|)\,(\text{width})$.
For a smoothly resolved amplitude, this indicator is near rounding level and does not change the evaluation count; otherwise it triggers subdomain bisection.
No inverse phase function is used.

When $|\omega|\cdot(\text{interval width})\cdot\min|g'|$ is at most 64, the exponential factor $e^{i\omega g}$ is nearly polynomial, making the collocation matrix rank deficient.
In that case, switch to ordinary quadrature as described below.
Even on a strongly oscillatory subdomain, rank deficiency or ill-conditioning returns the product $L\max|f|$ of width $L$ and maximum sampled amplitude as its error, causing bisection rather than failure of the entire call.

## Filon integration in phase coordinates

On an interval with monotonic phase, substitute $\varphi = g(x)$ to obtain

$$
\int_a^b f(x)e^{i\omega g(x)}\,dx = \int_{\min(g(a),g(b))}^{\max(g(a),g(b))} \frac{f(x(\varphi))}{|g'(x(\varphi))|}\,e^{i\omega\varphi}\,d\varphi
$$

which handles both increasing and decreasing phases with the same formula.
Filon's method interpolates the smooth right-hand factor at 25 Chebyshev nodes in $\varphi$ and integrates the interpolant times $e^{i\omega\varphi}$ using analytically computed Fourier–Chebyshev moments.
No amplitude derivative is needed, but each $\varphi$ requires its corresponding $x$: implement the inverse with `OscillatoryPhaseInverse::invert` and provide it as `Some(self)` through `OscillatoryPhase::inverse`.
The integrator checks that returned $x$ lies in the specified interval (branch) and that reevaluating $g(x)$ gives a residual within 64 times working precision; failure gives an error.
Filon uses its phase-coordinate rule for $|\omega|\cdot(\text{interval width})\cdot\min|g'| > 2$.
As in Levin, the error estimate is the larger of four times the nested difference and an amplitude-resolution indicator, whose width is measured in phase.

## Declaring subdomains and switching to ordinary quadrature

Derivative information is declared by the user rather than computed.
`OscillatoryRegion::nonstationary(interval, derivative)` declares a range `derivative` containing all $g'$ values on the interval and excluding zero.
`OscillatoryRegion::stationary` declares a neighborhood containing a stationary point, endpoint stationary point, or higher-order stationary point.
`OscillatoryGeometry::new(interval, omega, regions)` validates and retains an ascending subdomain sequence covering the entire interval without gaps or overlaps, and a signed $\omega$.
Set the ordinary-quadrature panel limit with `OscillatoryOptions`.
The phase function is not called during preparation.

During integration, $g'$ is evaluated at every sample; a value outside its declared range or zero on a nonstationary subdomain stops with an error `InvalidInput`.
Declarations can be checked only at samples, so ranges must be analytically derived enclosures.

Stationary subdomains and those failing collocation conditions use ordinary G7K15 quadrature on equally sized panels.
The panel count doubles until the estimated phase change per panel under the worst-case $|g'|$ is at most 2 rad.
If the required count exceeds `ordinary_panels` of `OscillatoryOptions` (default 4096), the subdomain cannot be evaluated.
Termination handling is described in [API and result interpretation](#api-and-result-interpretation).

Each subdomain is bisected under the tolerances and subdivision limits of `SubdivisionOptions`.
Children inherit the parent's declared range, so narrowing child widths can reduce $|\omega|\cdot\text{width}\cdot\min|g'|$ below the threshold and switch back to ordinary quadrature.
Declaring an interval with a wide $|g'|$ range as multiple subdomains with narrower ranges helps avoid this switch.

## API and result interpretation

`OscillatoryPhase::evaluate` returns phase $g$ and derivative $g'$ together.
A closure `Fn(S) -> Result<(S, S), SciOxideError>` directly supplies a phase without an inverse; a pair `(phase, inverse)` of a closure and `OscillatoryPhaseInverse` supplies a phase with an inverse.
`OscillatoryGeometry::new(interval, omega, regions)` prepares the declared geometry without retaining the phase function itself.
`OscillatoryQuadraturePlan::new(method, phase, weight, options)` retains `OscillatoryMethod::Levin` or `OscillatoryMethod::Filon`, `FourierWeight`, and `OscillatoryOptions`.

The `SubdivisionOptions` of `OscillatoryOptions::new(SubdivisionOptions)` determines tolerances, subdivision and evaluation limits, and execution policy for amplitude sampling.
Tolerances use `ErrorTolerance`, with one absolute tolerance shared by all components or a componentwise vector; the chosen norm combines components for the criterion.
Set execution policy with `SubdivisionOptions::with_execution_policy`; phase evaluation is always sequential.
`OscillatoryOptions::with_ordinary_panels` limits ordinary-quadrature panels per subdomain and defaults to 4096.

Evaluation of the phase–derivative pair counts as one provider call; inverse evaluations at Filon interior nodes count separately.
Passing a provider without an inverse to Filon is rejected with `InvalidInput` before calling callbacks.

`integrate(amplitude, &phase)` returns `(value, IntegrationReport)`, creating a workspace for each call.
For repeated integration, create `OscillatoryWorkspace` once with `workspace(components)` and pass it to `integrate_into(amplitude, &phase, out, &mut workspace, scope)`.
`components` is the real component count, 2 for complex values.
Workspace memory is proportional to the component count and the sum of declared subdomain count and subdivision limit; `component_errors()` returns the preceding call's componentwise error breakdowns.

`FourierWeight::Cosine` and `FourierWeight::Sine` also act on complex amplitudes, so distinguish value type from oscillatory weight.
The reported error estimates nested differences and arithmetic error and is not an upper bound.
Convergence terminates with `ToleranceReached`.
If a declared initial subdomain requires more than `ordinary_panels` panels, no full estimate exists and the call returns error `ConvergenceFailure` (`TruncationLimit`).
If a bisected child exceeds the limit, return the estimate so far with `RefinementLimit`.
Output remains unchanged on error.

## Evaluation counts for a Fresnel-type integral

Compute $\int_0^1\cos(\omega x^2)\,dx$, appearing in straight-edge Fresnel diffraction amplitudes, varying from $\omega=10$ to $10^4$ by factors of $10^{1/2}$.
Here $x$ and $\omega$ are dimensionless.
Since $x=0$ is stationary, declare $[0,\delta]$ a stationary subdomain ($g'\in[0,2\delta]$) and $[\delta,1]$ a nonstationary subdomain ($g'\in[2\delta,2]$).
For comparison, apply ordinary `AdaptiveIntegrator` (G7K15) to the full integrand $\cos(\omega x^2)$ including oscillations, with the same tolerance.
Inputs are the stationary-neighborhood width $\delta$ and absolute tolerance exponent; relative tolerance is 0.

The independent reference is $\sqrt{\pi/(2\omega)}\,C(\sqrt{2\omega/\pi})$ from the normalized Fresnel integral $C(z)=\int_0^z\cos(\pi t^2/2)\,dt$, computed with `scioxide_special::fresnel_c`.
The table has one row per $\omega$ and method; only successful results show reference discrepancies.
Unsuccessful values are not used, and their termination statuses are shown.
The plot shows amplitude evaluation counts on logarithmic axes.
The outcome indicates whether all 14 Levin and Filon integrations met the tolerance; adaptive G7K15 remains in the table for comparison.

//|   - case: wide-stationary
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/18/3","range":{"min":60000,"max":62000}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/18/4","equals":"ToleranceReached"}
//|   - case: loose-tolerance
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/12/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/12/3","range":{"min":200,"max":1000}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, ErrorTolerance, SciOxideError, Interval, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    AdaptiveIntegrator, AdaptiveRule, FourierWeight, OscillatoryGeometry, OscillatoryMethod,
    OscillatoryOptions, OscillatoryPhase, OscillatoryPhaseInverse, OscillatoryQuadraturePlan,
    OscillatoryRegion, SubdivisionOptions,
};
use scioxide_special::fresnel_c;

// Phase g(x) = x² and g'(x) = 2x. The Filon inverse is branch x = √φ on [0, 1].
struct Square;
impl OscillatoryPhase<f64> for Square {
    fn evaluate(&self, x: f64) -> Result<(f64, f64), SciOxideError> {
        Ok((x * x, 2.0 * x))
    }
    fn inverse(&self) -> Option<&dyn OscillatoryPhaseInverse<f64>> {
        Some(self)
    }
}
impl OscillatoryPhaseInverse<f64> for Square {
    fn invert(&self, _: Interval<f64>, phase: f64) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(RealScalar::sqrt(phase))
    }
}

let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let interval = |a: f64, b: f64| Interval::new(a, b).map_err(|e| e.to_string());
let tolerance = RealScalar::powf(10.0, tolerance_exponent);
let options = SubdivisionOptions::default()
    .with_tolerance(
        ErrorTolerance::scalar(tolerance, 0.0, NormSelection::Infinity)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,

let delta = stationary_width;
// [0, δ] contains stationary point x = 0, with g' ∈ [0, 2δ]. [δ, 1] has g' ∈ [2δ, 2], excluding zero.
let regions = [
    OscillatoryRegion::stationary(interval(0.0, delta)?, interval(0.0, 2.0 * delta)?)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    OscillatoryRegion::nonstationary(interval(delta, 1.0)?, interval(2.0 * delta, 2.0)?)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
];

    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

        * fresnel_c(z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut results = Vec::new();

        let plan = OscillatoryQuadraturePlan::new(
            method,
            geometry.clone(),
            FourierWeight::Cosine,
            OscillatoryOptions::new(options.clone()),
        )

            plan.integrate(|_: f64| Ok(1.0), &Square)

        );

    results.push(

            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,

        "yLabel": "Amplitude evaluation count [count]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })

let outcome = match failure {
    None => Outcome::satisfied("Levin and Filon satisfied the tolerance at all 7 ω values")
        .status(last_status)
        .explanation("The error estimate uses nested differences and arithmetic error estimates; it is not an upper bound."),

        .status(status)
        .explanation("Do not use those row values; inspect their termination statuses in the table."),

let evaluations: usize = rows
    .iter()
    .filter(|row| row.1 != "Adaptive G7K15")

With the defaults ($\delta=0.1$, tolerance $10^{-9}$), every successful result differed from the reference by at most $10^{-15}$.
Levin evaluations were 255 ($\omega=10$), 7740 ($\omega=316$), and 605 ($\omega=3162$); Filon used 255, 285, and 705.
Neither grows proportionally to $\omega$.
Adaptive G7K15 required 6075 evaluations at $\omega=1000$ and stopped with `Stagnation` at $\omega\ge 3162$ (stagnation due to rounding), leaving its value unusable.

Levin's 7740 evaluations at $\omega=316$ result from $\omega\cdot 0.9\cdot 0.2\approx 57$ falling below threshold 64 on the nonstationary subdomain, switching to ordinary quadrature with 512 panels.
The 7995 evaluations at $\omega=1000$ arise because bisected children inherit the parent's range $\min|g'|=0.2$ and fall below the threshold.
Setting $\delta=0.5$ makes the stationary subdomain alone require 4096 panels (61440 evaluations) at $\omega=10^4$, giving a total of 61465.
This exactly meets the default `ordinary_panels` limit; wider stationary subdomains or higher frequencies become unevaluable.
Loosening tolerance to $10^{-6}$ reduces Levin's $\omega=1000$ to 315 evaluations, with reference discrepancy about $10^{-12}$.
Keeping stationary-neighborhood widths around $(\omega|g''|)^{-1/2}$ and declaring narrow $|g'|$ ranges per subdomain reduces evaluation counts.

## Summary

For nonlinear-phase oscillatory integrals, pass phase $g$ and derivative $g'$ as an `OscillatoryPhase`, and declare stationary neighborhoods and nonstationary subdomains with enclosures of $g'$ through `OscillatoryRegion`.
Levin needs no inverse; Filon requires an inverse branch and residual checks but can use phase-coordinate rules from smaller phase changes.
Both switch subdomains failing their conditions to ordinary quadrature with at most 2 rad per panel; subdomains requiring more than `ordinary_panels` panels cannot be evaluated.
Evaluation counts depend strongly on declarations; choose stationary widths and $|g'|$ ranges for the problem and check results with `is_success()` and an independent reference.
