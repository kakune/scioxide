The composite trapezoidal rule is the simplest quadrature rule, yet for sufficiently smooth integrands its error has a regular expansion in even powers of the step size $h$.
Knowing this expansion lets combinations of step-halved values eliminate successive leading error terms.
This is Richardson extrapolation; applying it repeatedly to the trapezoidal rule gives Romberg's method.
This page covers conditions for the error expansion, cases where it fails, special behavior for periodic functions, the extrapolation triangle, and error estimates and stopping rules of SciOxide's `Romberg`.
Notation follows [Newton–Cotes formulas and composite rules](../newton-cotes-and-romberg/): $h$ is grid spacing and $T(h)$ the composite trapezoidal rule with spacing $h$.
[Euler–Maclaurin summation and conditional upper bounds](../euler-maclaurin-summation/) applies the same formula to infinite series.

## Euler–Maclaurin expansion of the trapezoidal rule

If $f\in C^{2k+2}[a,b]$, the Euler–Maclaurin formula expands composite trapezoidal error as

$$
T(h) - \int_a^b f\,dx = \sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{B_{2j}}{(2j)!}h^{2j}\bigl[f^{(2j-1)}(b)-f^{(2j-1)}(a)\bigr] + O(h^{2k+2})
$$


Here $B_{2j}$ are Bernoulli numbers ($B_2=1/6$, $B_4=-1/30$, $B_6=1/42$).
Two observations follow.

1. For sufficiently smooth $f$, the error has an asymptotic expansion containing only the even powers $h^2,h^4,h^6,\dots$.
   This justifies Romberg extrapolation.
   If $f$ is nonsmooth at an endpoint, other powers appear.
   For example, $\sqrt{x}$ introduces an $h^{3/2}$ term, making the step-halving error ratio approach $2^{3/2}\approx2.83$ rather than 4.
2. If $f$ is smooth and periodic with period $b-a$, every finite term on the right vanishes.
   If $f$ is $C^\infty$, the error tends to 0 faster than any power of $h$.
   If $f$ is also analytic in the strip $|\mathrm{Im}\,x|<d$, the error decreases geometrically as $O(e^{-2\pi d/h})$.
   For example, $f(x)=1/(2+\cos2\pi x)$ has poles at $\cos2\pi x=-2$ and $d=\ln(2+\sqrt3)/(2\pi)$, so error in the $N=1/h$-node trapezoidal rule decreases proportionally to $(2-\sqrt3)^N\approx0.268^N$.

## Romberg extrapolation

Let $R_{j,0}=T(h_j)$ be trapezoidal values for the step-halving sequence $h_j=(b-a)/2^j$.
The even-power expansion gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{j,0} &= I + c_2h_j^2 + c_4h_j^4 + c_6h_j^6 + \cdots,\\
R_{j-1,0} &= I + 4c_2h_j^2 + 16c_4h_j^4 + 64c_6h_j^6 + \cdots
\end{aligned}
$$


Eliminating the $h_j^2$ term gives

$$
R_{j,1} = \frac{4R_{j,0}-R_{j-1,0}}{3} = I - 4c_4h_j^4 - 20c_6h_j^6 + \cdots
$$

which equals composite Simpson with grid spacing $h_j$.
Repeating this elimination gives the Richardson extrapolation triangle

$$
R_{j,k} = R_{j,k-1} + \frac{R_{j,k-1}-R_{j-1,k-1}}{4^k-1}, \qquad 1\le k\le j
$$


Column $k$ eliminates the $h^2,\dots,h^{2k}$ terms and has error $O(h_j^{2k+2})$.
$R_{j,2}$ equals composite Boole.
The triangle is arranged as follows.

$$
\begin{array}{llll}
R_{0,0} & & & \\
R_{1,0} & R_{1,1} & & \\
R_{2,0} & R_{2,1} & R_{2,2} & \\
R_{3,0} & R_{3,1} & R_{3,2} & R_{3,3}
\end{array}
$$

Row $j$ evaluates only the $2^{j-1}$ midpoints of previous grid subintervals, reusing all existing values.

## SciOxide's `Romberg`

`Romberg::new(LevelOptions)` accepts 6–30 levels and defaults to 15.
At $L$ levels, the finest grid has $2^{L-1}+1$ nodes; the default 15 levels require at most 16,386 evaluations including the off-grid check point.
Both endpoints are evaluated, so use a [double-exponential transformation](../improper-singular-and-oscillatory/) for functions diverging at endpoints.

Sampling only dyadic grid nodes $a+k(b-a)/2^j$ makes functions resonating with that grid, such as $\sin^2(2^m\pi x)$, appear to be 0 at every sample through certain levels.
After completing level 4, one evaluation is therefore made at position $(\sqrt5-1)/2$ in the interval (absent from every dyadic grid).
If its discrepancy from the degree-7 interpolant through 8 nearby grid nodes exceeds four times the difference of degree-7 and degree-5 interpolants, the error estimate is raised to at least four times that discrepancy multiplied by $b-a$.

Each component's error estimate is the maximum of three quantities.

- Four times the change from the preceding level for each of the two highest extrapolation orders
- The error from the check point above
- The rounding error floor $50\,\varepsilon A$ ($A$ is the trapezoidal estimate of $\int_a^b|f|\,dx$)

`ToleranceReached` requires level 5 or later (at least 34 evaluations including the check point), an estimate satisfying the tolerance, a nonzero sample, and an estimate at most $A/4$.
If the estimate reaches the rounding error floor without meeting the tolerance, termination is `Stagnation`; exhausting all levels gives `RefinementLimit`.
On unsuccessful termination, return the completed level with the smallest error estimate (after level 4 is complete, select only among level 4 and later).
The estimate is not an upper bound.
Structure invisible to the nested grids and single check point can be missed.
`RombergWorkspace` retains only the evaluation block size and is independent of level count.
`component_errors()` exposes componentwise estimates.

The next cell shows errors and error ratios for the trapezoidal sequence $T(h_j)$ and results from `Romberg` for four integrands.
The integrands are $e^x$ (smooth, exact value $e-1$), $\sqrt{x}$ (nonsmooth endpoint, $2/3$), $1/(2+\cos2\pi x)$ (periodic, $1/\sqrt3$), and $\sin^2(16\pi x)$ (resonating with the dyadic grid, $1/2$).
Inputs are the integrand and absolute and relative tolerance exponent $q$ for `LevelOptions`.
The error ratio is undefined at levels with zero error.

```rust
//| id: integrate-romberg-integrands
//| ref: integrate-romberg-integrands
//| title: "Compare trapezoidal sequences and Romberg extrapolation under integrand smoothness and periodicity"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| inputs:
//|   integrand_kind: {"type":"select","label":"Integrand","description":"Choose a smooth function, endpoint square root, periodic function, or function resonating with the dyadic grid.","options":["exp","sqrt","periodic","resonant"],"value":"exp"}
//|   tolerance_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Tolerance exponent q [-]","description":"Set absolute and relative tolerances both to 10^q.","min":-14,"max":-4,"step":1,"value":-10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"sqrt","label":"√x","description":"A nonsmooth endpoint makes the error ratio differ from 4, reducing extrapolation effectiveness.","inputs":{"integrand_kind":"sqrt"}}
//|   - {"id":"periodic","label":"Periodic function","description":"The trapezoidal rule itself converges geometrically.","inputs":{"integrand_kind":"periodic"}}
//|   - {"id":"resonant","label":"sin²(16πx)","description":"All grid values through level 4 are 0, but the off-grid check keeps refinement going.","inputs":{"integrand_kind":"resonant"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"trapezoid","path":"/rows/3/3","approx":{"value":4.0,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"romberg","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: sqrt
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"trapezoid","path":"/rows/8/3","approx":{"value":2.81,"atol":0.03}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: periodic
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"trapezoid","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"min":5e-10,"max":1.2e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"trapezoid","path":"/rows/6/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: resonant
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"trapezoid","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"romberg","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, ErrorTolerance, Interval, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{FixedRule, LevelOptions, NewtonCotes, NewtonCotesEndpoints, Romberg};

let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
// Integrand and exact value on [0, 1].
let (integrand, exact): (Box<dyn Fn(f64) -> f64 + Sync>, f64) = match integrand_kind.as_str() {
    "sqrt" => (Box::new(|x| RealScalar::sqrt(x)), 2.0 / 3.0),
    "periodic" => (
        Box::new(move |x| 1.0 / (2.0 + RealScalar::cos(2.0 * pi * x))),
        1.0 / RealScalar::sqrt(3.0),
    ),
    "resonant" => (
        Box::new(move |x| RealScalar::powi(RealScalar::sin(16.0 * pi * x), 2)),
        0.5,
    ),
    _ => (Box::new(|x| RealScalar::exp(x)), RealScalar::exp_m1(1.0)),
};
let f = |x: f64| Ok(integrand(x));
let interval = Interval::new(0.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Composite trapezoidal sequence T(h_j) with step h_j = 2^(−j).
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut previous: Option<f64> = None;
for level in 0..=10_u32 {
    let panels = Budget::try_from(1_usize << level).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let rule =
        NewtonCotes::new(2, NewtonCotesEndpoints::Closed, panels).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (value, _) = rule.integrate(&f, interval).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let error = (value - exact).abs();
    // Error ratio e_{j−1}/e_j. Undefined if either error is 0.
    let ratio = previous
        .filter(|&p| p > 0.0 && error > 0.0)
        .map(|p| p / error);
    rows.push((level, rule.node_count(), error, ratio));
    previous = Some(error);
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let trapezoid_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Level j [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Evaluation count [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Trapezoidal error |T(h_j) − I| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "Error ratio e_{j−1}/e_j [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": sig4,
        "nullText": "Undefined"
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    trapezoid_columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "trapezoid" })
);

// Compute the same function with Romberg extrapolation to tolerance 10^q.
let requested = 10.0_f64.powf(tolerance_exponent);
let options = LevelOptions::default().with_tolerance(
    ErrorTolerance::scalar(requested, requested, NormSelection::Infinity)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
);
let romberg = Romberg::new(options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (value, report) = romberg.integrate(&f, interval).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let romberg_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Romberg value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig15 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Actual error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Error estimate [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Not estimated" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Evaluation count [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    romberg_columns,
    vec![(
        value,
        (value - exact).abs(),
        report.error_estimate(),
        report.evaluations(),
        report.termination().to_string(),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "romberg" })
);

let termination = report.termination();
let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The Romberg error estimate satisfied the tolerance")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Romberg extrapolation stopped before satisfying the tolerance")
        .explanation("The table gives the level with the smallest error estimate; treat its value as an estimate.")
};
let mut outcome = outcome
    .status(termination.to_string())
    .diagnostic("Evaluation count", report.evaluations() as f64, None)
    .diagnostic("Actual error", (value - exact).abs(), None);
if let Some(estimate) = report.error_estimate() {
    outcome = outcome.diagnostic("Error estimate", estimate, None);
}
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

For $e^x$, the error ratio approaches 4, and `Romberg` reached `ToleranceReached` with the minimum 34 evaluations (estimate $5\times10^{-12}$, actual error within rounding accuracy).
For $\sqrt{x}$, the ratio approaches $2.82$, and failure of the even-power expansion reduces extrapolation effectiveness.
`Romberg` exhausted 15 levels and terminated with `RefinementLimit` after 16,386 evaluations, with actual error $3.3\times10^{-8}$ and estimate $2.4\times10^{-7}$.
For the periodic function, the error ratio grows in powers of $2+\sqrt3$, error reaches $8\times10^{-10}$ at 17 nodes, and rounding level at 33.
For the resonant function, all trapezoidal rules through 17 nodes return 0, giving error $0.5$; however, `Romberg` detects resonance at the check point and continues refinement, converging to $1/2$ after 2050 evaluations (`ToleranceReached`).

## Summary

For sufficiently smooth functions, the Euler–Maclaurin formula expands composite trapezoidal error in even powers of $h$, which Romberg extrapolation eliminates successively.
Nonsmooth endpoints introduce other powers and reduce extrapolation effectiveness; use methods such as a [double-exponential transformation](../improper-singular-and-oscillatory/).
For one period of a smooth periodic function, the trapezoidal rule alone converges geometrically.
The error estimate of `Romberg` is the maximum of extrapolation changes, the off-grid check, and the rounding error floor, and is not an upper bound.
Success requires the estimate to satisfy both the tolerance and $A/4$ at level 5 or later; exhausting levels gives `RefinementLimit`.
