Infinite series $S = \sum_{n=n_0}^{\infty} a_n$ arise in Fourier cycle contributions, lattice sums, and statistical-mechanical partition functions.
If partial-sum $S_N = \sum_{n=n_0}^{N} a_n$ remainders $R_N = S - S_N$ decay slowly as $N^{-1}$, continued summation does not attain practical accuracy.
`SeriesSummation` provides two accelerators estimating limits from finitely many terms (Levin u and Wynn ε), and Euler–Maclaurin summation for analytical remainder evaluation.
This page explains both accelerators and the Plans, states, and workspaces shared by all three methods.
Euler–Maclaurin is covered in [Euler–Maclaurin summation and conditional upper bounds](../euler-maclaurin-summation/).

## Types of partial-sum convergence

Classify partial-sum error decay by the ratio $\rho = \lim_{N\to\infty} R_{N+1}/R_N$.
$|\rho| < 1$ is linear convergence, sign alternation near $\rho = -1$ is alternating convergence, and $\rho = 1$ is logarithmic convergence.
$\sum_{n\ge0} (-1)^n/(n+1) = \ln 2$ converges alternatingly and $\sum_{n\ge0} 1/(n+1)^2 = \pi^2/6$ logarithmically; both simple partial-sum errors are $O(N^{-1})$.
Accelerators transform sequences under assumed remainder forms, so they are effective only for matching types.

## The Levin u transformation

Levin u assumes remainders of the form

$$
R_n \approx \omega_n \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \frac{c_j}{x_n^{\,j}}, \qquad x_n = n + 1, \qquad \omega_n = x_n\,a_n
$$


Here $n$ is the term index, and $\omega_n$ is the remainder scale.
Writing this equation for $k+1$ partial sums $S_n, \dots, S_{n+k}$ and eliminating the $k$ coefficients $c_j$ together with the limit gives

$$
L_k^{(n)} = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}\left(\frac{x_{n+j}}{x_{n+k}}\right)^{k-1}\frac{S_{n+j}}{\omega_{n+j}}}{\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}\left(\frac{x_{n+j}}{x_{n+k}}\right)^{k-1}\frac{1}{\omega_{n+j}}}
$$


The alternating numerator and denominator sums are $k$th differences of quantities multiplied by $x^{k-1}$.
The assumption $\omega_n = (n+1)\,a_n$ suits both logarithmic and alternating convergence.

The alternating denominator is prone to cancellation.
SciOxide computes weights, their sum, and the correction to the latest partial sum in double-length arithmetic (a sum of two floating-point numbers), leaving amplified half-ulp rounding of the terms themselves as the remaining rounding error.
This is returned as the floating-point error floor `SeriesError::roundoff`.
At a stage where the denominator is at rounding level relative to the sum of weights, the accelerated value is not used.

Rows with zero terms or terms too small to change the rounded partial sum contain no information and are excluded; the $k$th difference is replaced by divided differences on the retained row coordinates $x_n$.
If every window row is uninformative, this is taken as evidence that the latest partial sum is stationary, allowing convergence checks for terminating series and zero components as well.

## Wynn's ε algorithm

Wynn's ε algorithm computes the Shanks transformation through the recurrence

$$
\varepsilon_{k+1}^{(i)} = \varepsilon_{k-1}^{(i+1)} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_k^{(i+1)} - \varepsilon_k^{(i)}}, \qquad \varepsilon_{-1}^{(i)} = 0, \quad \varepsilon_0^{(i)} = S_i
$$

and uses even-numbered columns to approximate the limit.
It is effective for linear and alternating convergence with remainders expressible as sums of geometric series, but has little effect on logarithmic convergence.
Truncate the table where differences reach rounding level, returning the last usable even-column value.

## Windows, error-estimate evidence, and stopping

Order $k$ of `SeriesOptions::with_order` determines a window of the latest $k+1$ terms and partial sums.
`SeriesSummation::new` accepts orders 1–32 for Levin u, 2–32 for Wynn ε, and 1–12 for Euler–Maclaurin; out-of-range orders give `InvalidInput`.
Higher orders make the transformation more ill-conditioned.
Levin u starts with the first term and increasing order; after filling the window, it slides at the requested order.
Per-term cost is $O(k)$ for Levin u and $O(k^2)$ for Wynn ε, which rebuilds the table from the window at every stage.

Error-estimate evidence `SeriesError::acceleration` is the maximum of changes within the transformation and the latest two changes in accelerated values.
If changes exceed rounding noise and shrink with ratio $\rho < 1$, divide by $1 - \rho$ to account for the remaining geometric series.
Without shrinkage, the evidence becomes `None` (unknown).
The error compared with tolerance is the larger of this evidence and floating-point error floor `SeriesError::roundoff`.
Tolerances use `ErrorTolerance` of `SeriesOptions::with_tolerance`; relative tolerance multiplies the estimate's magnitude.
Because accelerated values can agree by chance, stop with `HeuristicToleranceReached` only when new estimate errors satisfy tolerance at two consecutive stages, meaning three consecutive small changes.
`SeriesTermination::is_success()` is true for this status, but indicates only completion as configured, not proven convergence.

Each stage outputs either the new candidate or the previous candidate with smallest error-estimate evidence (the best estimate).
Output the best estimate only when the new candidate's floating-point error floor exceeds its error; otherwise output the new candidate.
Unaccelerated partial sums also compete for the best estimate using the window's maximum $(n+1)|a_n|$ as a remainder indicator `SeriesError::remainder`, but this indicator never stops summation.
Thus an accelerated value with clearly worse accuracy cannot replace a better estimate.

## Alternating and logarithmically convergent series

Apply Levin u and Wynn ε to the series for $\ln 2$ and $\pi^2/6$ and compare with partial sums using the same term count.
Inputs are term limit $N$ (`with_max_terms`), order $k$ (`with_order`), and absolute tolerance for acceleration evidence.
`sum` adds at most $N$ terms in one call.
The last row per series uses the one-shot function `sum_series` with defaults independent of the inputs ([Plans, states, and workspaces](#plans-states-and-workspaces)).
Reference values come independently from analytical formulas; “Accelerated-value change” in the table is the estimate of `SeriesError::acceleration`.
Rows with unknown evidence show “Unknown” for accelerated-value changes; unaccelerated partial sums show “Not applicable” for termination status.
Each row retains its termination status; comparing methods is this cell's conclusion, so no single outcome is emitted.

```rust
//| id: integrate-series-acceleration-compare
//| ref: integrate-series-acceleration-compare
//| title: "Vary term count and order to compare Levin u and Wynn ε"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| inputs:
//|   term_limit: {"type":"range","label":"Term limit N [terms]","description":"Change the total number of terms obtainable by the plan.","min":8,"max":64,"step":4,"value":32}
//|   tableau_order: {"type":"range","label":"Order k [-]","description":"Use the latest k + 1 partial sums in the transformation.","min":2,"max":32,"step":1,"value":12}
//|   tolerance_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Absolute tolerance exponent q [-]","description":"Set the acceleration-evidence criterion to 10^q.","min":-14,"max":-6,"step":1,"value":-10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"short-window","label":"Term limit N = 8","description":"For π²/6, Levin u changes do not shrink, leaving unknown evidence and terminating with TermLimit.","inputs":{"term_limit":8}}
//|   - {"id":"tight-tolerance","label":"Tolerance 10⁻¹⁴","description":"Levin u for π²/6 advances to 32 terms and TermLimit, but the output's error remains the same as at 15 terms.","inputs":{"tolerance_exponent":-14}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":12,"max":14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/5","equals":"HeuristicToleranceReached"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"min":16,"max":18}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/2/5","equals":"HeuristicToleranceReached"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":11}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/3/5","equals":"HeuristicToleranceReached"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"min":14,"max":16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/5/5","equals":"HeuristicToleranceReached"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/6/2","equals":32}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/6/5","equals":"TermLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/7/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/7/5","equals":"HeuristicToleranceReached"}
//|   - case: short-window
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/5/4","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/5/5","equals":"TermLimit"}
//|   - case: tight-tolerance
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/5/2","equals":32}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/5/3","approx":{"value":4.48e-12,"rtol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/5/5","equals":"TermLimit"}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, ErrorTolerance, NormSelection};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    SeriesMethod, SeriesOptions, SeriesProblem, SeriesResult, SeriesSummation, sum_series,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(
    10.0_f64.powf(tolerance_exponent),
    0.0,
    NormSelection::Infinity,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let terms = Budget::try_from(term_limit as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let order = Budget::try_from(tableau_order as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Term a_n is indexed from n = 0. References are independent analytical values.
let alternating =
    |n: usize| -> f64 { (if n % 2 == 0 { 1.0 } else { -1.0 }) / (n as f64 + 1.0) };
let basel = |n: usize| -> f64 { 1.0 / ((n as f64 + 1.0) * (n as f64 + 1.0)) };
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let series: [(&str, &(dyn Fn(usize) -> f64 + Sync), f64); 2] = [
    ("Σ(−1)ⁿ/(n+1) = ln 2", &alternating, std::f64::consts::LN_2),
    ("Σ1/(n+1)² = π²/6", &basel, pi * pi / 6.0),
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (label, term, exact) in series {
    let row = |name, result: SeriesResult<f64>| {
        let report = result.report();
        let status = Some(report.termination().to_string());
        let error = (result.value() - exact).abs();
        (
            label,
            name,
            report.terms(),
            error,
            result.error().acceleration(),
            status,
        )
    };
    // Show simple partial sums with the same term count as a baseline; they have neither acceleration evidence nor termination status.
    let partial_error = ((0..terms.get()).map(term).sum::<f64>() - exact).abs();
    rows.push((label, "Partial sum", terms.get(), partial_error, None, None));
    for (name, method) in [
        ("Levin u", SeriesMethod::LevinU),
        ("Wynn ε", SeriesMethod::WynnEpsilon),
    ] {
        let options = SeriesOptions::default()
            .with_max_terms(terms)
            .with_order(order)
            .with_tolerance(tolerance.clone());
        let plan =
            SeriesSummation::<f64>::new(method, 0, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let provider = |n: usize, out: &mut [f64]| {
            out[0] = term(n);
            Ok(())
        };
        let result = plan
            .sum(SeriesProblem::Terms(&provider))
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        rows.push(row(name, result));
    }
    // One-shot sum_series uses default Levin u (256 terms, order 8, √ε), independently of inputs.
    let result = sum_series(|n| Ok(term(n)), 0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push(row("sum_series", result));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Series" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Terms used [terms]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Reference discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Accelerated-value change (estimated) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Unknown" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination status", "nullText": "Not applicable" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })
);
```

With defaults, the 32-term partial-sum error for $\ln 2$ is $1.54 \times 10^{-2}$, whereas Levin u reaches $1.55 \times 10^{-15}$ in 13 terms and Wynn ε $7.33 \times 10^{-13}$ in 17, both stopping with `HeuristicToleranceReached`.
For logarithmically convergent $\pi^2/6$, Levin u reaches `HeuristicToleranceReached` with 15 terms and error $4.48 \times 10^{-12}$, while Wynn ε still has error $5.47 \times 10^{-3}$ after 32 terms and terminates with `TermLimit`.
One-shot `sum_series` reaches `HeuristicToleranceReached` for $\ln 2$ in 11 terms with error $1.52 \times 10^{-12}$, and for $\pi^2/6$ in 12 terms with error $3.29 \times 10^{-11}$.
Its default tolerance $\sqrt{\varepsilon} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-8}$ is looser than input $10^{-10}$, so it stops with fewer terms than the Levin u Plan.

At $N = 8$, Levin u changes fail to shrink; evidence stays unknown, giving `TermLimit` with error $1.23 \times 10^{-7}$.
Tightening tolerance to $10^{-14}$ makes Levin u advance to 32 terms and `TermLimit`, yet output error remains $4.48 \times 10^{-12}$ as at 15 terms, without worsening as terms increase.
Loosening tolerance to $10^{-6}$ stops Levin u after 11 terms with error $3.00 \times 10^{-10}$.
With defaults, every row's accelerated-value change exceeds actual error, but for $\pi^2/6$ at $k = 2$, Levin u's change $5.13 \times 10^{-6}$ is smaller than actual error $5.59 \times 10^{-6}$.
Accelerated-value changes are not error upper bounds.

## Plans, states, and workspaces

`SeriesSummation::new(method, first, SeriesOptions)` validates each method's order range.
The defaults of `SeriesOptions` are 256 total terms, order 8, 256 terms per `advance`, absolute and relative tolerances both $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ with the root-mean-square norm, sequential execution, and no provider-call limit.
Out-of-range orders or `first + max_terms` exceeding `usize` give `InvalidInput`.

The one-shot function `sum_series(term, first)` sums a scalar series from term function `term(n)` and starting index using Levin u with default `SeriesOptions`.
`term` is `Fn(usize) -> Result<T, SciOxideError>`, with `T` real or complex.
Only success returns `SeriesResult`; reaching the term limit gives error `ConvergenceFailure` (`TruncationLimit`).
If `first + 256` exceeds `usize`, reject with `InvalidInput` before evaluating terms.
This suffices for a single sum of an alternating or algebraically decaying series at default accuracy.
Use the Plan to change order or tolerance, use Wynn ε or Euler–Maclaurin, sum vector series, receive estimates after reaching limits, or advance incrementally with `advance`.

`SeriesProblem::Terms` supplies Levin u and Wynn ε terms; `SeriesProblem::EulerMaclaurin` supplies tail information.
`sum` and `sum_into` add at most `max_terms` terms per call, ignoring `with_terms_per_advance`.
`sum` internally creates a workspace for scalar series and returns `value()`, `error()`, and `report()` from `SeriesResult`.
`sum_into(problem, out, &mut workspace, scope)` sums vector series componentwise; read estimates from `SeriesWorkspace::component_errors()`.

`advance(problem, &mut state, sink, &mut workspace, scope)` adds at most `terms_per_advance` terms before returning, with status `CallLimit` when that limit is reached.
`SeriesState` retains the latest $k+1$ terms and partial sums, the exact cumulative sum, and componentwise new candidates, best estimates, output estimates, and error-estimate evidence; the next call resumes at subsequent terms.
`sink` receives the state after every added term; `state.value()` and `state.errors()` are `None` before the first term.
State belongs to a method, starting index, order, and component count and does not record the provider; resuming with a different series gives meaningless results.
If the provider or `sink` fails, state retains terms added before failure and can resume.
Parallel execution evaluates batches of at most 32 consecutive terms and adds them in index order, giving the same result as sequential execution.

`SeriesReport` returns `termination()`, total terms added to state `terms()`, provider calls during this call `provider_calls()`, and `accepted_state()` of the last added term.
`SeriesTermination` consists of `BoundedTailReached`, `ExactEmpty`, `HeuristicToleranceReached`, `CallLimit`, `TermLimit`, `ContextBudgetLimit`, and `ExecutionStopped`; `is_success()` is true for the first three.
As with integral termination reasons, success for `HeuristicToleranceReached` means completion as configured, neither proven convergence nor a tail upper bound.
Limits, cancellation, or deadlines before any term is added give errors (`ConvergenceFailure` or `ExecutionStopped`) instead of termination statuses.

`SeriesWorkspace` has stage-processing storage and lookahead for at most 32 terms; memory is not proportional to `max_terms`.
`workspace_requirements(width)` returns `SeriesRequirements` determined by method, starting index, component count, and order; `components()` exposes component count.
Workspaces are reusable for Plans with the same method, starting index, and component count, and an order no larger than prepared.
`check(&requirements)` and `try_reserve(&requirements)` check this and expand if needed; `last_attempt()` returns the preceding call's record.

## Summary

First identify the partial-sum convergence type; use Levin u or Wynn ε for alternating or linear convergence and Levin u for logarithmic convergence.
Order $k$ sets a window of the latest $k+1$ partial sums; Levin u accepts 1–32 and Wynn ε 2–32.
Acceleration stops with `HeuristicToleranceReached` after three consecutive small changes; even a true `is_success()` proves no convergence, so combine it with independent checks.
Accelerated-value change `SeriesError::acceleration` is not an error bound; without shrinkage it is `None` (unknown).
Distinguishing total term limit (`max_terms`) from per-`advance` limit (`terms_per_advance`) and resuming with the same state and workspace allows incremental evaluation of costly terms.
