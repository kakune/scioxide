Domains with fixed shapes, such as triangle or tetrahedral elements and spheres, can use fixed rules constructed directly for those shapes.
Fixed nodes and weights make evaluation counts known in advance and facilitate elementwise integration in computations such as finite element methods.
This page covers positive-weight and Grundmann–Möller simplex rules, mesh-element integration and orientation, and spherical product and Lebedev rules.
[Iterated integration with variable bounds and coordinate maps](../domains-and-geometric-rules/) covers general domains through bounds depending on outer coordinates or coordinate maps.

## Simplex quadrature rules

A $d$-simplex, such as a triangle or tetrahedron (the convex hull of $d+1$ vertices), accepts fixed rules directly without a box transformation.
`SimplexRule` stores a rule on the reference simplex $T = \{\boldsymbol x : x_i \ge 0,\ \sum_i x_i \le 1\}$ of volume $1/d!$, representing nodes in barycentric coordinates $(\lambda_0, \dots, \lambda_d)$.
For a simplex with vertices $\boldsymbol v_0, \dots, \boldsymbol v_d$, map node $j$ to $\boldsymbol x_j = \sum_k \lambda_{jk} \boldsymbol v_k$ and compute

$$
\int_S f\,d\boldsymbol x \approx |\det J| \sum_j w_j f(\boldsymbol x_j), \qquad J = [\boldsymbol v_1 - \boldsymbol v_0, \dots, \boldsymbol v_d - \boldsymbol v_0]
$$


The rule integrates polynomials of total degree at most `exactness_degree()` exactly up to roundoff.
It has no error estimate and terminates with `FixedRuleCompleted`.

There are two families.

- `SimplexRule::positive(d, minimum_degree, options)` constructs rules with positive weights and interior nodes for dimensions 1–3 and requested degrees 0–7.
  It uses Gauss–Legendre rules with the minimum node count for intervals and tabulated Xiao–Gimbutas rules for triangles and tetrahedra.
  A requested triangle degree of 3 uses a six-node degree-4 rule; check actual degree and node count with `exactness_degree()` and `node_count()`.
- `SimplexRule::new(d, s, options)` constructs a Grundmann–Möller rule of index $s$ in any dimension, exactly integrating total degree $2s+1$ with $\binom{d+s+1}{s}$ nodes.
  Weight signs alternate.

Both families' weights sum to the reference simplex volume $1/d!$.
With negative weights, the absolute-weight sum $\Lambda = \sum_j |w_j|$ exceeds the volume and amplifies function-value roundoff by $\Lambda/\text{volume}$.
For Grundmann–Möller rules this ratio grows rapidly with $s$, so subdividing the simplex and using a lower degree is more stable than applying a high degree at once.
Positive-weight rules have $\Lambda$ equal to volume and preserve positivity of discretized mass or diffusion coefficients if coefficients are positive at every node.
However, requesting a high degree does not prove positivity at unsampled points or bound the integration error.

### Mapping, orientation, and mesh elements

`rule.map_nodes_into(&vertices, &mut points, &mut weights, &mut workspace)` writes physical nodes and weights $|\det J|\,w_j$ into caller arrays and returns `SimplexMeasure`.
In `SimplexMeasure`, `measure` is volume $|\det J|/d!$, and `orientation` is the sign of $\det(\boldsymbol v_1 - \boldsymbol v_0, \dots, \boldsymbol v_d - \boldsymbol v_0)$ (`PredicateSign`).
The sign is computed from vertex coordinates using exact geometric predicates, without relying on a rounded determinant's sign.
Integration uses $|\det J|$, so reordering vertices to reverse orientation does not change the value.
Orientation is returned separately to determine boundary normal directions or check element ordering.
Exact orientation requires $(d+1)!$ terms; the `GeometricOptions::with_predicate_terms` default of 4096 suffices through $d \le 5$.
A flat simplex whose vertices lie exactly in one hyperplane returns exact 0 and `DegenerateDomain` without evaluation.
Overflow in edge vectors, mapped nodes, volume, or weights, or underflow of nonzero volume or weights to 0, returns `NumericalInstability`.
The weighted sum $\sum_j w_j f(\boldsymbol x_j)$ uses Neumaier compensated summation, switching to an extended-exponent representation if intermediate products or sums leave the floating-point range.

To integrate precomputed node values, obtain positions with `map_nodes_into` and pass per-node values to `rule.integrate_values_into(&vertices, &values, &mut out, &mut workspace, scope)`.
Arrange $c$ components per node in the same order as `barycentric_nodes()`.
This call does not evaluate the integrand.

Triangle and tetrahedral meshes use `scioxide_spatial::SimplexMesh` (`TriangleMesh`, `TetrahedronMesh`).
Construction checks every element's positive orientation using exact predicates ([Simplex mesh orientation and boundary tags](oxref:spatial-simplex-mesh-orientation-tags)).
`rule.integrate_mesh_cell_into(f, &mesh, cell, &mut out, &mut workspace, scope)` and `rule.map_mesh_cell_into(&mesh, cell, ..)` borrow vertices of mesh element `cell` without owning the mesh, performing the same calculation as when vertices are passed directly.
A mesh dimension differing from the rule's, or other than 2 or 3, gives an error.

### Options and workspace

`GeometricOptions` supplies shared settings for simplex and sphere rules, defaulting to at most $10^6$ nodes, 4096 exact-orientation predicate terms, no evaluation limit, and sequential execution.
Rules exceeding the node limit are rejected during preparation; a call whose node count exceeds the evaluation limit fails before evaluation with `ConvergenceFailure`.
The `GeometricWorkspace` returned by `rule.workspace(components)` stores mapped nodes, weights, and function values in $O(n(d+c)+d^2)$ memory; simplex rules also retain exact-predicate storage and a $d\times d$ LU factorization.
Rules with at most `node_capacity()` nodes and matching dimension and component count can share workspace.
`mapped_points()` and `mapped_weights()` return the last successful mapping's nodes and weights; integration also performs mapping, so those nodes can also be read.

## Plate mass on a triangle mesh

Divide a square plate of side 1 m into $n\times n$ squares, each split into two positively oriented triangles, to construct a `TriangleMesh`.
For plate surface density $\rho(x,y) = 1/(1+x+y)$ kg/m², the mass is

$$
M = \int_0^1\!\!\int_0^1 \frac{dx\,dy}{1+x+y} = 3\ln 3 - 4\ln 2 \ \text{kg}
$$

Compute it by summing `integrate_mesh_cell_into` over elements.
The following definitions block contains surface density `plate_density` and exact square-plate mass `exact_plate_mass` shared by two cells.
Compute the exact value with SciOxide `ln`.

```rust
//| id: integrate-plate-density
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Plate surface density 1/(1 + x + y) and exact square-plate mass"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

// Surface density ρ(x, y) = 1/(1 + x + y) [kg/m²] (x is physical coordinates (x, y) [m]).
pub(crate) fn plate_density(x: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    1.0 / (1.0 + x[0] + x[1])
}

// Square plate of side 1 m, mass 3 ln 3 − 4 ln 2 [kg].
pub(crate) fn exact_plate_mass() -> f64 {
    3.0 * RealScalar::ln(3.0) - 4.0 * RealScalar::ln(2.0)
}
```

The first cell tabulates and plots nodes per element, the absolute-weight-sum to volume ratio $\Lambda/(1/2)$, and mass error for positive-weight rules (requested degrees 0–7) and Grundmann–Möller rules ($s = 0, \dots, 5$, degrees 1–11).

```rust
//| id: integrate-simplex-mesh-mass
//| ref: integrate-simplex-mesh-mass
//| title: "Vary mesh resolution to compare simplex-rule degree and mass error"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [integrate-plate-density]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   cells_per_side: {"type":"range","label":"Subdivisions per side n [-]","description":"Divide the square into n×n squares, giving 2n² elements.","min":1,"max":8,"step":1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fine-mesh","label":"n = 8","description":"Refining elements decreases error as a power of element size even at fixed degree.","inputs":{"cells_per_side":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/7/3","equals":15}
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/7/5","range":{"min":1e-9,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/13/4","range":{"min":30,"max":50}}
//|   - case: fine-mesh
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/7/5","range":{"min":1e-14,"max":1e-11}}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_integrate::{GeometricOptions, SimplexRule};
use scioxide_spatial::{Point, TriangleMesh};

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let n = cells_per_side.round() as usize;
let h = 1.0 / n as f64;
// Grid point (i, j) has index j (n + 1) + i. Split each square into two counterclockwise triangles.
let mut points = Vec::new();
for j in 0..=n {
    for i in 0..=n {
        points.push(Point::new([i as f64 * h, j as f64 * h]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    }
}
let id = |i: usize, j: usize| j * (n + 1) + i;
let mut cells = Vec::new();
for j in 0..n {
    for i in 0..n {
        cells.push([id(i, j), id(i + 1, j), id(i + 1, j + 1)]);
        cells.push([id(i, j), id(i + 1, j + 1), id(i, j + 1)]);
    }
}
let mesh = TriangleMesh::new(points, cells).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let density = |x: &[f64]| Ok(plate_density(x));
let (exact, context) = (exact_plate_mass(), ExecutionContext::sequential());
let options = GeometricOptions::default();
let positive = (0..=7).map(|p| ("Positive weights", p, SimplexRule::positive(2, p, options)));
let signed = (0..=5).map(|s| ("Grundmann–Möller", s, SimplexRule::new(2, s, options)));
let (mut rows, mut curves) = (Vec::new(), [Vec::new(), Vec::new()]);
for (family, request, rule) in positive.chain(signed) {
    let rule: SimplexRule<f64> = rule.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut workspace = rule.workspace(1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (mut mass, mut evaluations) = (0.0_f64, 0);
    for cell in 0..mesh.cell_count() {
        let mut part = 0.0_f64;
        let report = rule
            .integrate_mesh_cell_into(&density, &mesh, cell, &mut part, &mut workspace, context)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        mass += part;
        evaluations += report.evaluations();
    }
    // Λ/volume: divide the absolute-weight sum by reference triangle area 1/2 (1 for positive weights).
    let lambda = rule.weights().iter().map(|w| w.abs()).sum::<f64>() / rule.reference_measure();
    let error = (mass - exact).abs();
    let curve = &mut curves[usize::from(family != "Positive weights")];
    curve.extend(Some([evaluations as f64, error]).filter(|_| error > 0.0));
    let (degree, nodes) = (rule.exactness_degree(), rule.node_count());
    rows.push((family, request, degree, nodes, lambda, error));
}
let [positive, signed] = curves;
let symbols = serde_json::json!({ "showSymbol": true });
let series = vec![
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Positive-weight rule", "points": positive, "style": symbols }),
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Grundmann–Möller rule", "points": signed, "style": symbols }),
];
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Integrand evaluations [count]",
        "yLabel": "Mass error [kg]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Rule" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Requested degree or index s", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Exactness degree", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Nodes per element [points]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Λ/volume [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 4 } },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Mass error [kg]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rules" })
);
```

With defaults ($n = 2$, eight elements), the degree-7 positive-weight rule (15 nodes, 120 evaluations) has error $2.7\times10^{-8}$ kg, while the degree-7 Grundmann–Möller rule (20 nodes, 160 evaluations) has $1.1\times10^{-7}$ kg; at the same degree, the positive-weight rule is more accurate with fewer evaluations.
For Grundmann–Möller, $\Lambda/\text{volume}$ grows rapidly from 2.1 at $s = 1$ to 38 at $s = 5$ (degree 11), potentially amplifying function-value roundoff by those factors.
Requested positive-weight degrees 0 and 1, and 3 and 4, produce identical rules, as the exactness-degree and node-count columns confirm.
At $n = 8$ (128 elements), the degree-7 positive-weight error falls to $7\times10^{-13}$ kg.
Quartering element size $h$ decreases error by about $4\times10^4$, close to the degree-$p$ rule's expected error $O(h^{p+1}) = O(h^8)$ ($4^8 \approx 6.6\times10^4$).

The second cell passes one triangle (vertices $(0,0)$, $(0.5,0)$, $(0.5,0.5)$ m; area $1/8$ m²) to `map_nodes_into` in counterclockwise and clockwise vertex orders.
Evaluate surface density at the mapped nodes, compute mass with `integrate_values_into`, and compare measure, orientation, and mapped-weight sum.
The mass reference is the exact triangle integral $\ln 2 - \tfrac32\ln\tfrac32$ kg.

```rust
//|   - {"id":"degree-zero","label":"Degree 0","description":"Even a one-node centroid rule has measure and weight sum equal to volume.","inputs":{"degree":0}}

//|       - {"artifact":"orientation","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"Negative"}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientation","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.125,"rtol":1e-14}}

//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orientation","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.125,"rtol":1e-14}}

use scioxide_integrate::{GeometricOptions, SimplexRule};

    SimplexRule::positive(2, degree, GeometricOptions::default()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Pass the same triangle (area 1/8 m²) with counterclockwise and clockwise vertex orders.

    let values: Vec<f64> = nodes.chunks(2).map(plate_density).collect();

    let context = ExecutionContext::sequential();

        .integrate_values_into(&vertices, &values, &mut mass, &mut workspace, context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let orientation = format!("{:?}", measure.orientation);
    let (error, evaluations) = ((mass - exact).abs(), report.evaluations());
    rows.push((
        name,
        orientation,
        measure.measure,
        mapped_sum,
        mass,
        error,
        evaluations,

}
let columns = serde_json::json!([

    { "key": "1", "label": "Orientation" },
```

With defaults (requested degree 4, six-node rule), clockwise vertex order gives orientation `Negative`, while measure and mapped-weight sum both remain $0.125$ m² and mass remains $0.0849491$ kg.

## Sphere quadrature rules

For integration on the unit sphere $S^2$, a degree-$p$ rule exactly integrates every spherical harmonic $Y_\ell^m$ ($\ell\le p$) through degree $p$.
Spherical harmonics through degree $p$ span a space of dimension $(p+1)^2$, suggesting any rule needs approximately at least $(p+1)^2/3$ nodes, since each node supplies two position degrees of freedom and one weight.

`SphereProductRule::new(polar_nodes, azimuth_nodes, options)` is a product of an $n_\theta$-node Gauss–Legendre rule in $\cos\theta$ and an $n_\varphi$-node equally spaced azimuthal trapezoidal rule, with nodes and weights

$$
\boldsymbol u_{jk} = (\sin\theta_j\cos\varphi_k,\ \sin\theta_j\sin\varphi_k,\ \cos\theta_j), \qquad w_{jk} = \frac{2\pi a_j}{n_\varphi}
$$

Here $\cos\theta_j$ and $a_j$ are Gauss–Legendre nodes and weights on $[-1,1]$, and $\varphi_k = 2\pi k/n_\varphi$.
Its exactness degree is $\min(2n_\theta - 1,\ n_\varphi - 1)$; degree $p$ uses $n_\theta = (p+1)/2$ and $n_\varphi = p+1$, for about $(p+1)^2/2$ points.
Nodes cluster near the poles.

`LebedevRule::new(degree, options)` is a tabulated Lebedev–Laikov rule invariant under the regular octahedron's 48 rotations and reflections, with 32 degrees from 3 to 131 (odd degrees 3–31, then every 6 from 35–131; `LebedevRule::supported_degrees()`).
Node count is about $(p+1)^2/3$, close to the estimated lower bound; examples are 230 nodes at degree 25, 590 at 41, and 5810 at 131.
Nodes are spread approximately uniformly over the sphere without a preferred direction.

Both rules' weights sum to $4\pi$, neither has an error estimate, and both terminate with `FixedRuleCompleted`.
`rule.integrate(f, &center, radius)` maps to a sphere centered at $\boldsymbol c$ with radius $r$ and computes $\oint f\,dS \approx r^2\sum_j w_j f(\boldsymbol c + r\boldsymbol u_j)$.
`integrate_into` uses `GeometricWorkspace` without allocation; `map_nodes_into(&center, radius, &mut points, &mut weights, &mut workspace)` writes nodes and weights $r^2 w_j$ and returns area $4\pi r^2$.
NaN or infinite center or radius gives `NonFinite`, $r \le 0$ gives `InvalidInput`, and overflow in nodes, area, or weights or underflow of nonzero area or weights to 0 gives `NumericalInstability`.
`nodes()` and `weights()` return unit-sphere nodes (three coordinates per node) and weights.

[Spherical temperature](../../../scioxide-interp/examples/spherical-temperature/) approximates a field from irregular observations and computes its global mean, separating interpolation and quadrature errors.

## Node efficiency of sphere rules

For a distribution concentrated in direction $\boldsymbol n$, $e^{\kappa\,\boldsymbol n\cdot\boldsymbol x}$ (an unnormalized von Mises–Fisher density), the spherical integral is

$$
\int_{S^2}e^{\kappa\,\boldsymbol n\cdot\boldsymbol x}\,d\Omega = \frac{4\pi\sinh\kappa}{\kappa}
$$

Compute it with Lebedev and product rules of the same degree $p$.
Tilt $\boldsymbol n$ away from the coordinate axes so the product rule cannot exploit azimuthal symmetry.
Increasing concentration $\kappa$ requires higher-degree spherical harmonic components.
The reference uses f64 `sinh`.
The plot shows relative error against node count; the table gives node counts and relative errors by degree.

```rust
//| id: integrate-sphere-rules-efficiency
//| ref: integrate-sphere-rules-efficiency
//| title: "Vary concentration to compare Lebedev and spherical-product errors against node counts"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
//| inputs:
//|   concentration: {"type":"range","label":"Concentration κ [-]","description":"Directional concentration of integrand exp(κ n·x).","min":1,"max":30,"step":1,"value":8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"sharp","label":"κ = 30","description":"For a concentrated distribution, degree 41 is needed to reach relative error 10⁻¹².","inputs":{"concentration":30}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/11/1","equals":230}
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/11/3","equals":338}
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/11/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: sharp
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/16/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rules","path":"/rows/11/2","range":{"min":1e-6,"max":1}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{GeometricOptions, LebedevRule, SphereProductRule};

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
// Unit-sphere integral ∫ exp(κ n·x) dΩ = 4π sinh κ / κ (independent of direction n). Tilt n away from axes to prevent symmetry exploitation.
let kappa = concentration;
let (polar, azimuth) = (0.6_f64, 0.3_f64);
let n = [
    RealScalar::sin(polar) * RealScalar::cos(azimuth),
    RealScalar::sin(polar) * RealScalar::sin(azimuth),
    RealScalar::cos(polar),
];
let exact = 4.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * kappa.sinh() / kappa; // Reference (f64 sinh)
let f = |p: &[f64]| {
    Ok(RealScalar::exp(
        kappa * (n[0] * p[0] + n[1] * p[1] + n[2] * p[2]),
    ))
};
let max_nodes = Budget::try_from(6000).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = GeometricOptions::default().with_max_nodes(max_nodes);
let (mut lebedev_curve, mut product_curve, mut rows) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for &degree in LebedevRule::<f64>::supported_degrees()
    .iter()
    .filter(|&&p| p <= 59)
{
    let lebedev = LebedevRule::<f64>::new(degree, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Product rule with the same degree p: (p + 1)/2 polar nodes and p + 1 azimuthal nodes.
    let product = SphereProductRule::<f64>::new((degree + 1) / 2, degree + 1, options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (value, _) = lebedev
        .integrate(&f, &[0.0; 3], 1.0)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (product_value, _) = product
        .integrate(&f, &[0.0; 3], 1.0)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let error = (value - exact).abs() / exact;
    let product_error = (product_value - exact).abs() / exact;
    // Plot only positive errors on logarithmic axes (zero-error points remain in the table).
    let (lebedev_nodes, product_nodes) = (lebedev.node_count(), product.node_count());
    lebedev_curve.extend(Some([lebedev_nodes as f64, error]).filter(|_| error > 0.0));
    product_curve
        .extend(Some([product_nodes as f64, product_error]).filter(|_| product_error > 0.0));
    rows.push((degree, lebedev_nodes, error, product_nodes, product_error));
}
let symbols = serde_json::json!({ "showSymbol": true });
let product_name = "Gauss–Legendre × equally spaced azimuthal product rule";
let series = vec![
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Lebedev rule", "points": lebedev_curve, "style": symbols }),
    serde_json::json!({ "name": product_name, "points": product_curve, "style": symbols }),
];
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Node count [points]",
        "yLabel": "Relative error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Spherical-harmonic degree p", "type": "integer" },
```

At the default $\kappa=8$, degree 25 gives Lebedev 230 nodes and relative error $6.0\times10^{-15}$, compared with the product rule's 338 nodes and $2.7\times10^{-14}$.

## Checking errors

Fixed simplex and sphere rules have no error estimate and terminate with `FixedRuleCompleted` (`DegenerateDomain` for flat simplices).
Check errors by comparing higher-degree results, refined-element results, or known integrals.
If differences do not decrease with degree, suspect nonsmoothness inside elements or roundoff amplified by a large absolute-weight sum.

## Summary

Integrate triangles and tetrahedra with `SimplexRule`; positive-weight rules have absolute-weight sums equal to volume and resist roundoff amplification.
Grundmann–Möller supports arbitrary dimension and odd degrees, but its absolute-weight sum grows with index $s$, so favor element refinement over high degree.
Integration uses $|\det J|$ and is independent of vertex order; orientation is returned separately as `SimplexMeasure::orientation`, and `SimplexMesh` elements can be integrated directly with `integrate_mesh_cell_into`.
For spheres, use `LebedevRule` or `SphereProductRule`; at equal degree, Lebedev uses about 2/3 the product-rule nodes, spread approximately uniformly over the sphere.
No fixed rule provides an error estimate, so check by varying degrees or elements.
