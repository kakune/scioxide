When the integrand's difficult part is known analytically, split the integral as $\int_a^b w(x)\,g(x)\,dx$, treat the known weight $w$ analytically, and sample only the smooth amplitude $g$.
This page covers algebraic and logarithmic endpoint weights, Cauchy principal values, and complex contour integration.

For `WeightedQuadraturePlan`, Fourier weights are covered in [Cancellation in oscillatory integrals and tails of Fourier integrals](../fourier-integrals-and-tails/), and breakpoints in [Double-exponential transformations for improper integrals and endpoint singularities](../improper-singular-and-oscillatory/).
Fixed integrand samples are covered in [Integration of sampled data](../sampled-data-integration/), and differentiation of integrals with respect to parameters in [Parameter sensitivities and integral JVPs and VJPs](../parameter-sensitivities/).

## Separating the weight

For the integral $I = \int_a^b w(x)\,g(x)\,dx$, interpolating $g$ by a polynomial $p_n$ gives

$$
\left| I - \int_a^b w(x)\,p_n(x)\,dx \right| \le \max_{[a,b]}|g - p_n| \int_a^b |w(x)|\,dx
$$


The right-hand side depends only on the smoothness of $g$; singularities and oscillations of $w$ enter only through $\int|w|$.
This is called product integration.

Map the interval to $t \in [-1, 1]$ by $x(t) = \frac{a+b}{2} + \frac{b-a}{2}t$ and expand $p_n = \sum_k c_k T_k(t)$ in Chebyshev polynomials $T_k$ to obtain

$$
\int_a^b w\,p_n\,dx = \frac{b-a}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n} c_k\,\mu_k, \qquad \mu_k = \int_{-1}^{1} T_k(t)\,w(x(t))\,dt
$$


$\mu_k$ are called modified Chebyshev moments.
Coefficients $c_k$ follow by a discrete cosine transform of $g$ at 25 Chebyshev extrema $t_j = \cos(j\pi/24)$; this gives the modified Clenshaw–Curtis rule.
The difference from a nested 13-node rule (degree 12) estimates discretization error.

The basic contract is to return only amplitude $g(x)$ from the callback, without multiplying by weight $w$ again.
Values may be real, complex, or vector-valued; integration acts per real component through scioxide-core's `AffineValue` trait.

## Algebraic and logarithmic endpoint weights

`EndpointWeight::new(alpha, beta, left_log, right_log)` represents the weight

$$
w(x) = (x - a)^{\alpha}\,(b - x)^{\beta}\,[\ln(x - a)]\,[\ln(b - x)]
$$


The logarithmic factors in brackets are included only when selected by `left_log` and `right_log`.
For $\alpha > -1$ and $\beta > -1$, $w$ is integrable on $[a, b]$; $\alpha \le -1$ or $\beta \le -1$ gives `InvalidInput`, and NaN or infinity gives `NonFinite`.

`WeightedQuadraturePlan::endpoint(interval, points, weight, options)` performs weighted quadrature for endpoint singularities.
Without interior breakpoints, the Plan splits the interval at its midpoint so no subdomain contains both singular endpoints.
Only subdomains touching a singular endpoint (a nonzero exponent or logarithmic factor) use moment rules; elsewhere, apply the 15-node Gauss–Kronrod rule (G7K15) to the product $g\,w$.
On moment subdomains, the factor from the opposite endpoint is smooth and multiplies $g$ on the sampled side.
Moment rules use both endpoint Chebyshev extrema, so $g$ is evaluated at $a$ and $b$ as well.
$g$ must be finite and smooth including endpoints.

### Rescaling moments

On a subdomain of width $L$ touching the left endpoint $a$, set $x = a + Ls$ and $s = (1+t)/2$; then $(x-a)^\alpha = L^\alpha s^\alpha$ and $dx = L\,ds$.
The subdomain integral including $dx = L\,ds$ is therefore expressed using canonical unit-width moments,

$$
\nu_k = \int_0^1 s^{\alpha}\,T_k(2s - 1)\,ds, \qquad \lambda_k = \int_0^1 s^{\alpha}\ln s\;T_k(2s - 1)\,ds
$$

multiplied by $L^{\alpha+1}$.
Logarithmic factors split as $\ln(x - a) = \ln L + \ln s$, giving the form $\ln L \cdot \nu_k + \lambda_k$.
The first moments are $\nu_0 = 1/(\alpha+1)$ and $\lambda_0 = -1/(\alpha+1)^2$; higher moments follow a recurrence derived from the three-term recurrence of $T_k$ and integration by parts (Piessens–Branders).

The Plan prepares canonical moments once per endpoint.
The implementation computes integrals of $T_k(t) - T_k(-1)$ by recurrence, removing the common $1/(\alpha+1)$ singularity before rounding.
All moments have magnitude $O(1/(\alpha+1))$; no intermediate powers of 2 are formed, preventing overflow even for large exponents.
Scaling by $L^{\alpha+1}$ uses an extended exponent range, avoiding intermediate overflow and underflow even for exponents such as $\alpha = 1022$ or breakpoints $10^{-100}$ from an endpoint.
Integration fails with `NumericalInstability` only when a subdomain value itself is too large to represent.
For underflowing subdomain values, add the rounded value and include lost accuracy in the rounding error floor.

Each subdomain separately retains discretization estimates from nested differences, the rounding error floor, and propagated moment error.
`component_errors()` returns their sums over all subdomains as `discretization`, `roundoff`, and `moments`.
`error_estimate()` is `total()`: take the larger of discretization and rounding error floor rather than their sum, then add moment error (and extrapolation error if enabled).
The subdomain's $\max(\text{discretization}, \text{rounding}) + \text{moments}$ also determines which one to bisect.
None is an upper bound.

## Cauchy principal values

For an integral with pole $c \in (a, b)$, the Cauchy principal value is the limit obtained by symmetrically excluding both sides of the pole,

$$
\mathrm{PV}\int_a^b \frac{f(x)}{x - c}\,dx = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0^+}\left[\int_a^{c-\epsilon} + \int_{c+\epsilon}^{b}\right]\frac{f(x)}{x - c}\,dx
$$


It does not compute two divergent ordinary integrals separately and add them.
Subtracting and adding $f(c)$ gives

$$
\mathrm{PV}\int_a^b \frac{f(x)}{x - c}\,dx = \int_a^b \frac{f(x) - f(c)}{x - c}\,dx + f(c)\ln\frac{b - c}{c - a}
$$

whose first right-hand term is an ordinary integral if $f$ is smooth near $c$.

`WeightedQuadraturePlan::principal_value(interval, pole, options)` computes Cauchy principal-value integrals.
The callback returns only $f$; the Plan does not divide by $x - c$ near the pole.
Subdomains containing the pole or with the pole within 1.1 half-widths of their center use modified Clenshaw–Curtis with moments of $1/(t - \hat c)$ ($\hat c$ is the pole in standard coordinates).
Other subdomains use G7K15 on $f(x)/(x - c)$.

Moments start at $m_0 = \ln\bigl((1 - \hat c)/(1 + \hat c)\bigr)$; $T_k = 2tT_{k-1} - T_{k-2}$ and $t = (t - \hat c) + \hat c$ give the recurrence

$$
m_k = 2\hat c\,m_{k-1} - m_{k-2} + 2\int_{-1}^{1} T_{k-1}(t)\,dt
$$


Compute $m_0$ as the logarithm of the physical distance ratio $|b - c|/|c - a|$, without going through standard coordinate $\hat c$.
Both distances use extended exponent range, supporting large interval endpoints whose distances overflow.
If distances are nearly equal and the ratio near 1, first combine their difference exactly, then take the logarithm in the form $\ln(1+x)$.
This preserves logarithm values both for poles near the midpoint and poles only one rounding step from an endpoint.
Whether the pole is interior is also determined in physical coordinates.
For exterior poles, the recurrence follows a minimal solution and rounding errors grow by $|\hat c| + \sqrt{\hat c^2 - 1}$ per step; this amplification is included in the error model.
Exterior poles give ordinary integrals; a pole equal to $a$ or $b$ gives `InvalidInput`.

## Complex contour integration

`ContourPlan::new(parameter_interval, breakpoints, SubdivisionOptions)` computes the complex integral for contour $C$ parameterized by $t \in [a, b]$,

$$
\int_C f(z)\,dz = \int_a^b f(z(t))\,z'(t)\,dt
$$


The `map` of `integrate(f, map)` returns $(z(t), z'(t))$ from $t$, and `f` returns $f(z)$.
Orientation is determined by `map`; closed or reversed contours need no special treatment.
Isolated zeros of $z'$ are allowed.
The Plan does not choose branches of multivalued functions, so $f$ must be continuous along $C$.

Real and imaginary parts use adaptive G7K15 with breakpoints, calling `map` and `f` once each per node.
Both calls count toward the evaluation limit; the report's evaluation count is the number of calls to `f`.
The error estimate excludes error in $z'$.
`workspace()` returns `WeightedQuadratureWorkspace`; components of `component_errors()` are ordered as `[real part, imaginary part]`.

## Plans, workspaces, and termination status

The `SubdivisionOptions` of `WeightedQuadratureOptions::new(SubdivisionOptions)` sets tolerances, subdivision and evaluation limits, and execution policy.
Default absolute and relative tolerances are both $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, with criterion $E \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}|Q|$.
`with_moment_levels` is used only for Fourier weights; other weights ignore it.

`workspace(components)` creates a `WeightedQuadratureWorkspace` sized by real component count, reusable in `integrate_into(f, out, &mut workspace, scope)`.
`component_errors()` returns the preceding call's componentwise `IntegrationErrorComponents`, or `None` after failure or without an error estimate.
Zero-width intervals give exactly 0 with status `DegenerateDomain` without calling $f$.
Convergence gives `ToleranceReached`; reaching limits returns the current estimate with `BudgetLimit` or `RefinementLimit`.
Output remains unchanged on error.

## Endpoint weights and the Beta function

Integrate weight $w(x) = x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}$ (also multiplying by $\ln x$ if selected) with amplitude vector $(1, x, e^x)$ using `WeightedQuadraturePlan::endpoint`.
Without a logarithmic factor, the references are

$$
\int_0^1 x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}\,dx = B(\alpha+1, \beta+1), \qquad \int_0^1 x^{\alpha+1}(1-x)^{\beta}\,dx = B(\alpha+2, \beta+1),
$$

$$
\int_0^1 x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}e^{x}\,dx = B(\alpha+1, \beta+1)\,M(\alpha+1;\, \alpha+\beta+2;\, 1)
$$


Here $M$ is Kummer's confluent hypergeometric function; the last formula follows from the integral representation in DLMF 13.4.1.
With a logarithmic factor, $\partial_\alpha B(\alpha+1, \beta+1) = B(\alpha+1,\beta+1)\bigl(\psi(\alpha+1) - \psi(\alpha+\beta+2)\bigr)$ expresses references for the first 2 components through the digamma function $\psi$.
No logarithm-weighted closed form is used for the $e^x$ component; the table marks it as having no reference.
Compute references with `beta`, `digamma`, and `hyp1f1` from `scioxide_special`.

The first 2 amplitudes are polynomials, making Chebyshev interpolation exact and checking moment accuracy itself.
The $e^x$ component also checks interpolation error.
Inputs are the two exponents and logarithmic-factor selection; $x$ and the integral are dimensionless.
Integrate all 3 amplitude components in one call and read componentwise estimates from the workspace.
For comparison, compute the first component's integral with tanh–sinh from [Double-exponential transformations for improper integrals and endpoint singularities](../improper-singular-and-oscillatory/).
The DE integrand evaluates singular factors using the distance in `nearest_endpoint()`.
The table has one row per method and amplitude; the outcome reports the endpoint-weight Plan's termination status.

```rust
//| id: integrate-endpoint-weight
//| ref: integrate-endpoint-weight
//| title: "Vary endpoint-weight exponents and compare with the Beta function"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   alpha: {"type":"range","label":"Left-end exponent α [-]","description":"Exponent of weight x^α. Greater than −1.","min":-0.95,"max":2,"step":0.05,"value":-0.5}
//|   beta_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Right-end exponent β [-]","description":"Exponent of weight (1 − x)^β. Greater than −1.","min":-0.95,"max":2,"step":0.05,"value":0.25}
//|   left_log: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Left-end logarithmic factor ln x","value":false}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"strong-left","label":"α = −0.95","description":"Endpoint weights succeed in a few dozen evaluations even for strong endpoint singularities, while DE stagnates.","inputs":{"alpha":-0.95}}
//|   - {"id":"with-log","label":"With logarithmic factor","description":"Check the weight multiplied by ln x against digamma references.","inputs":{"left_log":true}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/7","equals":"ToleranceReached"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/3/7","equals":"HeuristicToleranceReached"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: strong-left
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/7","equals":"ToleranceReached"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/3/7","equals":"Stagnation"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: with-log
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":null}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, Interval, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    DoubleExponentialDomain, DoubleExponentialIntegrator, DoubleExponentialPoint,
    EndpointWeight, LevelOptions, WeightedQuadratureOptions, WeightedQuadraturePlan,
};
use scioxide_special::{beta, digamma, hyp1f1};

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let digits15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let interval = Interval::new(0.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let weight =
    EndpointWeight::new(alpha, beta_exponent, left_log, false).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plan = WeightedQuadraturePlan::endpoint(
    interval,
    &[],
    weight,
    WeightedQuadratureOptions::default(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// The amplitude has 3 components (1, x, eˣ). The Plan handles weight x^α (1 − x)^β [ln x].
let mut workspace = plan.workspace(3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut value = [0.0_f64; 3];
let report = plan
    .integrate_into(
        |x: f64| Ok([1.0, x, RealScalar::exp(x)]),
        &mut value,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let errors = workspace
    .component_errors()
    .ok_or("No componentwise error estimates")?;

// References: B(α+1, β+1) and B(α+2, β+1). With a logarithmic factor, multiply by differences of ψ.
let moment = |a: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let b = beta(a, beta_exponent + 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if left_log {
        let psi = digamma(a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            - digamma(a + beta_exponent + 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        Ok(b * psi)
    } else {
        Ok(b)
    }
};
// The eˣ component is B(α+1, β+1) M(α+1; α+β+2; 1). No closed form with a logarithmic factor is used.
let kummer = if left_log {
    None
} else {
    let m = hyp1f1(alpha + 1.0, alpha + beta_exponent + 2.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Some(moment(alpha + 1.0)? * m)
};
let exact = [
    Some(moment(alpha + 1.0)?),
    Some(moment(alpha + 2.0)?),
    kummer,
];

// Comparison: integrate the first component with tanh–sinh. Evaluate singular factors using endpoint distances.
let de = DoubleExponentialIntegrator::new(
    DoubleExponentialDomain::Finite {
        interval,
        singular_points: &[],
    },
    LevelOptions::default(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let singular = |point: DoubleExponentialPoint<f64>| {
    let x = point.coordinate();
    let (left, right) = match point.nearest_endpoint() {
        Some((0.0, distance)) => (distance, 1.0 - x),
        Some((_, distance)) => (x, distance),
        None => (x, 1.0 - x),
    };
    let logarithm = if left_log { RealScalar::ln(left) } else { 1.0 };
    Ok(RealScalar::powf(left, alpha) * RealScalar::powf(right, beta_exponent) * logarithm)
};
let (de_value, de_report) = de.integrate(&singular).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Amplitude" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Integral [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits15 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Reference [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits15, "nullText": "No reference" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Actual error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "No reference" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Error estimate [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "No estimate" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Evaluation count [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Termination status" }
]);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (component, name) in ["1", "x", "eˣ"].into_iter().enumerate() {
    rows.push((
        "Endpoint weight".to_string(),
        name.to_string(),
        value[component],
        exact[component],
        exact[component].map(|e| (value[component] - e).abs()),
        errors[component].total(),
        report.evaluations(),
        report.termination().to_string(),
    ));
}
rows.push((
    "DE（tanh–sinh）".to_string(),
    "1".to_string(),
    de_value,
    exact[0],
    exact[0].map(|e| (de_value - e).abs()),
    de_report.error_estimate(),
    de_report.evaluations(),
    de_report.termination().to_string(),
));
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })
);

// Determine the conclusion from the endpoint-weight Plan's termination status. The DE row is for comparison.
let outcome = if report.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The endpoint-weight Plan satisfied tolerances for all 3 components")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("The endpoint-weight Plan stopped before satisfying the tolerance")
};
let outcome = outcome.status(report.termination().to_string()).diagnostic(
    "Evaluation count",
    report.evaluations() as f64,
    None,
);
emit_outcome!(match report.error_estimate() {
    Some(estimate) => outcome.diagnostic("Error estimate", estimate, None),
    None => outcome,
});
```

With the defaults ($\alpha = -0.5$, $\beta = 0.25$), endpoint weighting used a 25-node rule on each of two midpoint-split subdomains, reaching `ToleranceReached` in 50 evaluations.
All 3 components differ from references by about rounding error, with componentwise estimates around $10^{-13}$.
DE reached `HeuristicToleranceReached` in 81 evaluations, also differing by about rounding error.

Even at $\alpha = -0.95$, endpoint weighting still uses 50 evaluations with discrepancies at most $10^{-14}$.
Moments handle the singularity while the smoothness of amplitude $(1, x, e^x)$ remains unchanged.
DE, however, gave `Stagnation` after 97 evaluations; its value is correct, but the estimate remained $8 \times 10^{-5}$.
Adding the logarithmic factor still gave endpoint-weight convergence in 50 evaluations, differing from digamma references by about $10^{-16}$.

## Summary

Put known endpoint singularities and principal values into weights for `WeightedQuadraturePlan::endpoint` and `principal_value`, passing only the smooth amplitude to callbacks.
Error depends only on amplitude smoothness, so evaluation counts do not grow even as singularity exponents approach $-1$.
Modified moments are prepared once on a canonical unit-width interval; rescaling in extended exponent range handles large exponents and small subdomains.
For complex contour integration, pass `ContourPlan` a mapping returning $(z, z')$, remembering that the estimate excludes error in $z'$.
For repeated integration, create `WeightedQuadratureWorkspace` and read componentwise error breakdowns with `component_errors()`.
