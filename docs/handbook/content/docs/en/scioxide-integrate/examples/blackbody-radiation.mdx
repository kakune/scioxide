Integrating blackbody emission at temperature $T$ over wavelength gives $\sigma T^4$ (the Stefan–Boltzmann law).
Constant $\sigma$ comes from a semi-infinite Planck integral; the fraction emitted below wavelength $\lambda$ (band fraction) uses the same integrand.
Compute the integral in three ways and recover $\sigma$ from physical constants.
The peak wavelength (Wien's displacement law) is treated in [Wien's displacement law and the diode equation with Lambert W](../../../scioxide-special/examples/lambert-w-wien-and-diode/); here its value only locates a reference band fraction.

## Planck distribution and a dimensionless integral

Blackbody spectral radiance is

$$
B_\lambda(\lambda, T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5}\,\frac{1}{e^{hc/(\lambda k T)} - 1}
$$


Here $h = 6.62607015 \times 10^{-34}$ J s is Planck's constant, $k = 1.380649 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K Boltzmann's constant, and $c = 299792458$ m/s the speed of light, all exact SI defining values.
Let radiant exitance into a hemisphere be $M = \int_0^\infty \pi B_\lambda\,d\lambda$.
Substitution $x = C_2/(\lambda T)$ and $C_2 = hc/k$ gives

$$
M = \frac{2\pi k^4 T^4}{h^3 c^2}\,I, \qquad I = \int_0^\infty \frac{x^3}{e^x - 1}\,dx = \frac{\pi^4}{15}
$$


The second radiation constant is $C_2 = hc/k = 14387.77\ \mu\mathrm{m\,K}$, and $\sigma = 2\pi k^4 I/(h^3c^2) = 2\pi^5 k^4/(15 h^3 c^2)$ is $5.670374419 \times 10^{-8}$ W m⁻² K⁻⁴ (CODATA 2018).

For wavelengths from $0$ to $\lambda$, $\lambda < \lambda_0$ corresponds to $x > x_0$, giving the fraction

$$
F(0 \to \lambda T) = \frac{15}{\pi^4}\int_{x_0}^\infty \frac{x^3}{e^x - 1}\,dx, \qquad x_0 = \frac{C_2}{\lambda T}
$$


$F$ depends only on product $\lambda T$ of $\lambda$ and $T$, useful for radiation-thermometer band design and estimating solar radiation transmitted through window glass.
At Wien peak wavelength $\lambda_{\max} T = 2897.77\ \mu\mathrm{m\,K}$, $F \approx 0.25$; one quarter of radiation lies at shorter wavelengths.

## Three approaches

**Double-exponential (DE) transformation.**
Use exp–sinh $x = x_0 + \exp(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sinh t)$ on half-line $[x_0, \infty)$ (`DoubleExponentialDomain::HalfLine`).
Each unit increase of transformed variable $t$ makes $x$ grow double exponentially; a few extra tail shells place nodes far beyond $x = 10^3$.
$e^x$ exceeds double-precision range at $x > 709$, so direct computation overflows.
Write $x^3/(e^x - 1) = x^2 e^{-x}/\operatorname{exprel}(-x)$.
$\operatorname{exprel}(y) = (e^y - 1)/y$ is scioxide-core's `exprel`; $\operatorname{exprel}(-x) = (1 - e^{-x})/x$ stays finite both at $x = 0$ and as $x \to \infty$.

**Gaussian quadrature with generalized Laguerre weights.**
For the full integral, place weight $x^{\alpha}e^{-x}$ in the rule and approximate the remaining factor polynomially.

$$
\frac{x^3}{e^x - 1} = x^2 e^{-x}\cdot\frac{x}{1 - e^{-x}}
$$

Splitting this way leaves $x/(1 - e^{-x}) = 1/\operatorname{exprel}(-x)$ smooth and approaching 1 at $x = 0$, allowing generalized Laguerre with $\alpha = 2$ directly.
Splitting as $x^3e^{-x}\cdot 1/(1 - e^{-x})$ with $\alpha = 3$ instead leaves a factor diverging like $1/x$ at $x = 0$, unsuitable for polynomial approximation.
For band integral $[x_0, \infty)$, set $x = x_0 + y$ and write

$$
\int_{x_0}^\infty \frac{x^3}{e^x - 1}\,dx = \int_0^\infty e^{-y}\,\frac{(x_0 + y)^2 e^{-x_0}}{\operatorname{exprel}\bigl(-(x_0 + y)\bigr)}\,dy
$$


The right-hand weight is $e^{-y}$, Laguerre with $\alpha = 0$; `GaussianSupport::HalfLine(x₀)` shifts nodes by $x_0$.
For $x_0 > 0$, the integrand has nonzero lower-end value $x_0^3/(e^{x_0} - 1) \ne 0$, excluding the $\alpha = 2$ split that puts $y^2$ into the weight.

**Series.**
For $x > 0$, $1/(e^x - 1) = \sum_{n \ge 1} e^{-nx}$; termwise integration is allowed because terms are positive, giving

$$
\int_{x_0}^\infty \frac{x^3}{e^x - 1}\,dx = \sum_{n=1}^\infty J_3(n), \qquad
J_m(t) = \int_{x_0}^\infty x^m e^{-tx}\,dx = e^{-t x_0}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{m!}{(m - j)!}\,\frac{x_0^{m-j}}{t^{j+1}}
$$


Here $J_3(n) = e^{-nx_0}\bigl(x_0^3/n + 3x_0^2/n^2 + 6x_0/n^3 + 6/n^4\bigr)$; at $x_0 = 0$, $\sum 6/n^4 = 6\zeta(4) = \pi^4/15$.
For small $x_0$ (large $\lambda T$), terms decrease only as $6/n^4$, requiring acceleration.

The `EulerMaclaurin` method of `SeriesSummation` approximates tail $\sum_{k \ge n} f(k)$ with an integral and odd-derivative endpoint corrections, returning remainder bound $(|B_{2p}|/(2p)!)\int_n^\infty |f^{(2p)}|$ ([Euler–Maclaurin summation and conditional upper bounds](../../algorithms/euler-maclaurin-summation/)).
$f(t) = J_3(t)$ is smooth in $t$, with derivatives given by integrals of the same form.

$$
f^{(k)}(t) = (-1)^k J_{3+k}(t), \qquad \int_n^\infty f(t)\,dt = J_2(n), \qquad \int_n^\infty \bigl|f^{(2p)}(t)\bigr|\,dt = J_{2+2p}(n)
$$

Even derivatives are positive, so absolute-value integrals also have closed forms.
Provided these are returned correctly, a bound below tolerance stops with successful `BoundedTailReached`.
Levin u, used for comparison, extrapolates from terms alone and stops on successive-estimate agreement with successful `HeuristicToleranceReached`, without an upper bound.

The following model definitions are shared by three cells.
`planck(x)` is integrand $x^2e^{-x}/\operatorname{exprel}(-x)$, `moment(m, t, x₀)` is $J_m(t)$, and `BandSeries` supplies term $J_3(n)$ and Euler–Maclaurin tail information.
Provider-supplied errors use rounding scale $64\varepsilon|\text{value}|$ for sums of positive terms.

```rust
//| id: integrate-blackbody-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Planck integrand and Euler–Maclaurin provider"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar, exprel};
use scioxide_integrate::{EulerMaclaurinProvider, SeriesTerms};

/// Exact SI values: Planck constant [J s], Boltzmann constant [J/K], speed of light [m/s].
pub(crate) const PLANCK: f64 = 6.626_070_15e-34;
pub(crate) const BOLTZMANN: f64 = 1.380_649e-23;
pub(crate) const LIGHT: f64 = 299_792_458.0;

/// x³/(eˣ − 1) = x² e^(−x) / exprel(−x). Does not overflow at large x.
pub(crate) fn planck(x: f64) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    Ok(x * x * RealScalar::exp(-x) / exprel(-x)?)
}

/// J_m(t) = ∫_{x₀}^∞ x^m e^(−tx) dx = e^(−t x₀) Σ_j m!/(m−j)! x₀^(m−j) / t^(j+1).
pub(crate) fn moment(m: usize, t: f64, x0: f64) -> f64 {
    let (mut sum, mut falling) = (0.0, 1.0);
    for j in 0..=m {
        sum += falling * RealScalar::powi(x0, (m - j) as i32) / RealScalar::powi(t, j as i32 + 1);
        falling *= (m - j) as f64;
    }
    RealScalar::exp(-t * x0) * sum
}

/// J₃(n) terms and tail integrals, odd derivatives, and even-derivative L¹ norms of f(t) = J₃(t).
pub(crate) struct BandSeries(pub(crate) f64);

impl SeriesTerms<f64> for BandSeries {
    fn term(&self, n: usize, out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        out[0] = moment(3, n as f64, self.0);
        Ok(())
    }
}

impl EulerMaclaurinProvider<f64> for BandSeries {
    fn tail_integral(
        &self,
        at: usize,
        value: &mut [f64],
        error: &mut [f64],
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        value[0] = moment(2, at as f64, self.0);
        error[0] = 64.0 * f64::EPSILON * value[0];
        Ok(())
    }
    fn odd_derivative(
        &self,
        at: usize,
        order: usize,
        value: &mut [f64],
        error: &mut [f64],
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        value[0] = -moment(3 + order, at as f64, self.0);
        error[0] = 64.0 * f64::EPSILON * value[0].abs();
        Ok(())
    }
    fn even_derivative_l1_bound(
        &self,
        at: usize,
        order: usize,
        bound: &mut [f64],
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        bound[0] = moment(2 + order, at as f64, self.0) * (1.0 + 64.0 * f64::EPSILON);
        Ok(())
    }
}

```

The next definitions block groups named methods for the same integral.
`half_line` prepares exp–sinh with lower endpoint 0 once; `by_double_exponential` reuses it through $\int_{x_0}^\infty f(x)\,dx = \int_0^\infty f(x_0 + y)\,dy$.
Node tables do not depend on $x_0$, so changing $x_0$ does not rebuild the Plan.
`by_laguerre` uses shifted Laguerre; `by_series` sums the series.
All collect value, error estimate, evaluation count, and termination status in `Estimate`.
`BandRoutes` selects the six named methods used by both band-fraction cells.

```rust
//| id: integrate-blackbody-routes
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Methods for the semi-infinite integral"
//| uses: [integrate-blackbody-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-poly]
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, SciOxideError, NormSelection, RealScalar, exprel};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    DoubleExponentialDomain, DoubleExponentialIntegrator, DoubleExponentialPoint,
    GaussianRulePlan, GaussianSupport, IntegrationReport, LevelOptions, SeriesMethod,
    SeriesOptions, SeriesProblem, SeriesSummation,
};
use scioxide_poly::OrthogonalFamily;

/// Value, error estimate (None if absent), evaluation count, and termination status.
pub(crate) struct Estimate {
    pub(crate) value: f64,
    pub(crate) error: Option<f64>,
    pub(crate) evaluations: usize,
    pub(crate) status: String,
}

impl Estimate {
    /// Collect quadrature value and report.
    pub(crate) fn integral(value: f64, report: &IntegrationReport<f64>) -> Self {
        Estimate {
            value,
            error: report.error_estimate(),
            evaluations: report.evaluations(),
            status: report.termination().to_string(),
        }
    }
}

/// Prepare half-line exp–sinh with lower endpoint 0 once (node tables are independent of x₀).
pub(crate) fn half_line(
    options: LevelOptions<f64>,
) -> Result<DoubleExponentialIntegrator<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    DoubleExponentialIntegrator::new(DoubleExponentialDomain::HalfLine { lower: 0.0 }, options)
}

/// Compute ∫_{x₀}^∞ x³/(eˣ − 1) dx = ∫₀^∞ f(x₀ + y) dy with prepared exp–sinh.
pub(crate) fn by_double_exponential(
    de: &DoubleExponentialIntegrator<f64>,
    x0: f64,
) -> Result<Estimate, SciOxideError> {
    let (value, report) =
        de.integrate(|p: DoubleExponentialPoint<f64>| planck(x0 + p.coordinate()))?;
    Ok(Estimate::integral(value, &report))
}

/// Compute the same integral by n-node Laguerre (α = 0) shifted through x = x₀ + y.
/// Removing weight e^(−y) leaves factor x² e^(−x₀)/exprel(−x).
pub(crate) fn by_laguerre(x0: f64, nodes: usize) -> Result<Estimate, SciOxideError> {
    let rule = GaussianRulePlan::<f64>::new(OrthogonalFamily::Laguerre, nodes)?;
    let factor = |x: f64| Ok(x * x * RealScalar::exp(-x0) / exprel(-x)?);
    let (value, report) = rule.integrate_weighted(&factor, GaussianSupport::HalfLine(x0))?;
    Ok(Estimate::integral(value, &report))
}

/// Compute the same integral as series Σ J₃(n).
pub(crate) fn by_series(
    x0: f64,
    method: SeriesMethod,
    tolerance: ErrorTolerance<f64>,
) -> Result<Estimate, SciOxideError> {
    let plan = SeriesSummation::<f64>::new(
        method,
        1,
        SeriesOptions::default().with_tolerance(tolerance),
    )?;
    let provider = BandSeries(x0);
    let problem = match method {
        SeriesMethod::EulerMaclaurin => SeriesProblem::EulerMaclaurin(&provider),
        _ => SeriesProblem::Terms(&provider),
    };
    let result = plan.sum(problem)?;
    // Error for stopping: Euler–Maclaurin uses supplied error + remainder bound + rounding; acceleration uses max(extrapolation change, rounding).
    let e = result.error();
    let error = e
        .provider()
        .zip(e.tail_bound())
        .map(|(p, t)| p + t + e.roundoff())
        .or(e.acceleration().map(|a| a.max(e.roundoff())));
    let report = result.report();
    let status = report.termination().to_string();
    Ok(Estimate {
        value: result.value(),
        error,
        evaluations: report.provider_calls(),
        status,
    })
}

/// Select named band methods. Prepare both exp–sinh transforms once and reuse over all x₀.
pub(crate) struct BandRoutes {
    de_default: DoubleExponentialIntegrator<f64>,
    de_relative: DoubleExponentialIntegrator<f64>,
    relative: ErrorTolerance<f64>,
    tight: ErrorTolerance<f64>,
}

impl BandRoutes {
    /// Prepare relative tolerance 10⁻¹² (10⁻¹³ for reference Euler–Maclaurin) and default tolerances.
    pub(crate) fn new() -> Result<Self, SciOxideError> {
        let relative = ErrorTolerance::scalar(0.0, 1e-12, NormSelection::Infinity)?;
        Ok(BandRoutes {
            de_default: half_line(LevelOptions::default())?,
            de_relative: half_line(LevelOptions::default().with_tolerance(relative.clone()))?,
            relative,
            tight: ErrorTolerance::scalar(0.0, 1e-13, NormSelection::Infinity)?,
        })
    }

    /// route is "de-default", "de-relative", "laguerre-8", "laguerre-32", "levin", or
    /// "euler-maclaurin" (reference).
    pub(crate) fn estimate(&self, route: &str, x0: f64) -> Result<Estimate, SciOxideError> {
        match route {
            "de-default" => by_double_exponential(&self.de_default, x0),
            "de-relative" => by_double_exponential(&self.de_relative, x0),
            "laguerre-8" => by_laguerre(x0, 8),
            "laguerre-32" => by_laguerre(x0, 32),
            "levin" => by_series(x0, SeriesMethod::LevinU, self.relative.clone()),
            _ => by_series(x0, SeriesMethod::EulerMaclaurin, self.tight.clone()),
        }
    }
}

/// Second radiation constant C₂ = hc/k [μm K].
pub(crate) fn second_radiation_constant() -> f64 {
    PLANCK * LIGHT / BOLTZMANN * 1e6
}
```

## Total radiation and the Stefan–Boltzmann constant

The next cell computes $I = \int_0^\infty x^3/(e^x - 1)\,dx$ by five methods and compares with $\pi^4/15$.
These are DE, generalized Laguerre at $\alpha = 2$ and $\alpha = 3$ ($n$ nodes), Euler–Maclaurin, and Levin u.
Inputs are Laguerre node count $n$ and DE/series relative tolerance $10^q$; absolute tolerance is 0.
Euler–Maclaurin's estimated-error column is an upper bound conditional on correct provider information.
Other estimates are not bounds; Gauss rules have no estimate.

The second table compares closed form $2\pi^5k^4/(15h^3c^2)$ and $2\pi k^4 I/(h^3c^2)$ using DE integral $I$ with CODATA 2018 value $5.670374419 \times 10^{-8}$.
CODATA rounds a quantity from defining constants to 10 digits, so relative discrepancies are meaningful only to about $10^{-10}$.

Defaults ($n = 16$, $q = -12$) give DE `HeuristicToleranceReached` after 173 evaluations, with relative error $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ against $\pi^4/15$.
Laguerre with $\alpha = 2$ gives $1.0 \times 10^{-10}$ at 16 nodes and rounding level at 64.
With $\alpha = 3$, error is $3.2 \times 10^{-4}$ at 16 nodes and still $6.4 \times 10^{-6}$ at 64: fourfold node count improves error only about fiftyfold.
Changing the weight exponent by one changes remaining-factor smoothness at $x = 0$ and convergence from exponential to algebraic.

Euler–Maclaurin reaches `BoundedTailReached` in 66 provider calls; actual relative error $1.1 \times 10^{-13}$ lies inside stated bound $5.6 \times 10^{-13}$.
Loosening to $q = -6$ stops in 33 calls, with error $1.4 \times 10^{-7}$ inside bound $2.9 \times 10^{-7}$.
Levin u reaches relative error $3.0 \times 10^{-14}$ in 15 calls and stops successfully with `HeuristicToleranceReached`, without an upper bound.
Even matching values leave Levin estimates as indicators from successive-extrapolate changes, not bounds.
Tightening to $q = -14$ puts requests too close to rounding; no adaptive method succeeds.
DE gives `Stagnation` after 327 evaluations; Euler–Maclaurin and Levin u hit term limits with `TermLimit`.
Values agree with $\pi^4/15$ within $3 \times 10^{-15}$, but statuses do not indicate meeting tolerance.

The closed form for $\sigma$ is $5.6703744191844 \times 10^{-8}$ W m⁻² K⁻⁴; relative discrepancy $3.3 \times 10^{-11}$ lies within rounding of CODATA's 10-digit value.
Using DE $I$ matches the closed form to 15 digits.

## Band fractions and λT

Two cells examine band fraction $F(0 \to \lambda T)$.
Both use reference Euler–Maclaurin with relative tolerance $10^{-13}$; `BoundedTailReached` results carry a provider-conditional upper bound.
Five methods are compared.

## Summary

The Stefan–Boltzmann constant follows from $\int_0^\infty x^3/(e^x - 1)\,dx = \pi^4/15$; exact SI constants recover CODATA to 10 digits.
Writing the integrand as $x^2e^{-x}/\operatorname{exprel}(-x)$ avoids overflow even at distant DE nodes.
Choose Laguerre weight factor $x^\alpha$ to leave smooth amplitudes: $\alpha = 2$ for the full integral and shifted $\alpha = 0$ over band domain $[x_0, \infty)$.
Series $\sum J_3(n)$ under Euler–Maclaurin `BoundedTailReached` carries a provider-conditional bound; Levin u can match values but stops with `HeuristicToleranceReached` without a bound.
For fractions varying from $10^{-16}$ to 1 with $\lambda T$, default absolute tolerances impair relative accuracy; scale tolerances to quantity magnitude.
