Fresnel integrals

$$
C(t) = \int_0^t \cos\Bigl(\frac{\pi s^2}{2}\Bigr)\,ds, \qquad S(t) = \int_0^t \sin\Bigl(\frac{\pi s^2}{2}\Bigr)\,ds
$$

appear in Fresnel diffraction amplitudes as light bends around an object's edge.
Plotting points $(C(t), S(t))$ gives the Cornu spiral (also Euler spiral or clothoid).
Compute $C$ and $S$ with `OscillatoryQuadraturePlan` to draw it, compare large-$t$ costs with adaptive Gauss–Kronrod, and calculate straight-edge diffraction intensity.
References are scioxide-special's `fresnel_c` and `fresnel_s`.
[Oscillatory integrals with nonlinear phases (Levin and Filon)](../../algorithms/phase-aware-oscillatory/) explains both methods and evaluation counts with increasing frequency.

## Spiral geometry and asymptotics

Vector $(C'(t), S'(t)) = (\cos(\pi t^2/2), \sin(\pi t^2/2))$ has unit length, so $t$ is arc length along the spiral.
Tangent angle is $\pi t^2/2$ and curvature $\pi t$.
Curvature grows linearly with arc length; as $t \to \pm\infty$, the spiral winds ever more tightly around limit points $(\pm\tfrac12, \pm\tfrac12)$.
$C$ and $S$ are odd; branch $t < 0$ is centrally symmetric about the origin.

At large $t$, integration by parts gives

$$
C(t) \approx \frac12 + \frac{\sin(\pi t^2/2)}{\pi t}, \qquad S(t) \approx \frac12 - \frac{\cos(\pi t^2/2)}{\pi t}
$$


These are points on a circle centered at $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ with radius $1/(\pi t)$; the next term has size $1/(\pi^2t^3)$.

## Declaring stationary points and partitioning intervals

$C$ and $S$ are oscillatory integrals $\int_0^t f\cos(\omega g)\,ds$ and $\int_0^t f\sin(\omega g)\,ds$ with phase $g(s) = s^2$, frequency $\omega = \pi/2$, and amplitude $f = 1$.
$g'(s) = 2s$ vanishes at $s = 0$, making $s = 0$ stationary.
Split through `OscillatoryRegion` and declare each region's range of $g'$.

- $[0, \min(t, 1)]$ contains the stationary point; use `OscillatoryRegion::stationary` and declare $g' \in [0, 2\min(t, 1)]$.
  Stationary regions use ordinary G7K15 on equal panels with phase change at most 2 rad per panel.
- For $t > 1$, divide the remainder into doubling intervals $[1, 2], [2, 4], \dots$; on $[a, b]$ declare $g' \in [2a, 2b]$ through `OscillatoryRegion::nonstationary`.

Narrow per-interval ranges of $|g'|$ benefit Levin collocation and Filon phase-coordinate interpolation.
Levin collocates only where region width $L$ and lower bound $m$ on $|g'|$ satisfy $|\omega|Lm > 64$; otherwise it switches to ordinary quadrature.
On $[a, 2a]$, $|\omega|Lm = \pi a^2$, so collocation starts at intervals with $a \ge 8$.
Filon's threshold is 2, using phase-coordinate rules from intervals with $a \ge 1$.
Also supply inverse phase $s = \sqrt\varphi$ for Filon.

Comparison adaptive Gauss–Kronrod integrates $[\cos(\pi s^2/2), \sin(\pi s^2/2)]$ together as a two-component value.
The integrand evaluates $\cos(\pi x)$ and $\sin(\pi x)$ through scioxide-core's `cos_pi` and `sin_pi`.
No prior rounding of $\pi x$ means no multiplication-rounding error from multiplying by $\pi$.
Rounding in $x = s^2/2$ remains, giving absolute phase error around $\pi\varepsilon s^2/2$.

The next definitions are shared by three cells.
`regions(t)` constructs regions; `fresnel(method, t, tolerance)` computes $C(t)$ and $S(t)$ with `"levin"`, `"filon"`, or `"adaptive"`.
Cosine and sine weights require separate Plans, one each for $C$ and $S$; evaluation counts sum both.
Only absolute tolerance is set.
Result `success` indicates whether both integrals terminated successfully.
`reference(t)` uses scioxide-special `fresnel_c` and `fresnel_s`; `asymptote(t)` is the preceding geometric asymptotic formula.

```rust
//| id: integrate-fresnel-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Computing Fresnel integrals with a declared stationary point"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{
    Budget, ErrorTolerance, SciOxideError, Interval, NormSelection, RealScalar, cos_pi, sin_pi,
};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    AdaptiveIntegrator, AdaptiveRule, FourierWeight, IntegrationReport, OscillatoryGeometry,
    OscillatoryMethod, OscillatoryOptions, OscillatoryQuadraturePlan, OscillatoryRegion,
    SubdivisionOptions,
};
use scioxide_special::{fresnel_c, fresnel_s};

/// Reference (C(t), S(t)) from scioxide-special.
pub(crate) fn reference(t: f64) -> Result<(f64, f64), SciOxideError> {
    Ok((fresnel_c(t)?, fresnel_s(t)?))
}

/// Asymptotics C ≈ 1/2 + sin(πt²/2)/(πt), S ≈ 1/2 − cos(πt²/2)/(πt).
pub(crate) fn asymptote(t: f64) -> Result<(f64, f64), SciOxideError> {
    let radius = 1.0 / (<f64 as RealScalar>::PI * t);
    let phase = 0.5 * t * t;
    Ok((0.5 + radius * sin_pi(phase)?, 0.5 - radius * cos_pi(phase)?))
}

/// C(t), S(t), combined evaluation count, termination status, and whether both succeeded.
pub(crate) struct Fresnel {
    pub(crate) c: f64,
    pub(crate) s: f64,
    pub(crate) evaluations: usize,
    pub(crate) status: String,
    pub(crate) success: bool,
}

/// [0, min(t, 1)] is stationary; successive [a, 2a] intervals are nonstationary with g' ∈ [2a, 2b].
fn regions(t: f64) -> Result<Vec<OscillatoryRegion<f64>>, SciOxideError> {
    let first = t.min(1.0);
    let stationary = OscillatoryRegion::stationary(
        Interval::new(0.0, first)?,
        Interval::new(0.0, 2.0 * first)?,
    )?;
    let mut list = vec![stationary];
    let mut a = first;
    while a < t {
        let b = (2.0 * a).min(t);
        list.push(OscillatoryRegion::nonstationary(
            Interval::new(a, b)?,
            Interval::new(2.0 * a, 2.0 * b)?,
        )?);
        a = b;
    }
    Ok(list)
}

fn summary(c: f64, s: f64, reports: &[&IntegrationReport<f64>]) -> Fresnel {
    let evaluations = reports.iter().map(|r| r.evaluations()).sum();
    let success = reports.iter().all(|r| r.is_success());
    // If either integral fails, show its termination status.
    let worst = reports
        .iter()
        .find(|r| !r.is_success())
        .unwrap_or(&reports[0]);
    Fresnel {
        c,
        s,
        evaluations,
        status: worst.termination().to_string(),
        success,
    }
}

/// method is "levin", "filon", or "adaptive". tolerance is absolute tolerance.
pub(crate) fn fresnel(method: &str, t: f64, tolerance: f64) -> Result<Fresnel, SciOxideError> {
    let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(tolerance, 0.0, NormSelection::Infinity)?;
    let options = SubdivisionOptions::default()
        .with_tolerance(tolerance)
        .with_max_subdivisions(Budget::try_from(1000)?);
    let interval = Interval::new(0.0, t)?;
    if method == "adaptive" {
        let plan = AdaptiveIntegrator::new(AdaptiveRule::GaussKronrod, options)?;
        let integrand = |x: f64| Ok([cos_pi(0.5 * x * x)?, sin_pi(0.5 * x * x)?]);
        let ([c, s], report) = plan.integrate(&integrand, interval)?;
        return Ok(summary(c, s, &[&report]));
    }
    let method = if method == "filon" {
        OscillatoryMethod::Filon
    } else {
        OscillatoryMethod::Levin
    };
    let geometry = OscillatoryGeometry::new(interval, 0.5 * <f64 as RealScalar>::PI, &regions(t)?)?;
    // Phase g(s) = s², g'(s) = 2s; Filon inverse s = √φ.
    let phase = (
        |x: f64| Ok((x * x, 2.0 * x)),
        |_: Interval<f64>, p: f64| Ok(RealScalar::sqrt(p)),
    );
    let solve = |weight| {
        let plan = OscillatoryQuadraturePlan::new(
            method,
            geometry.clone(),
            weight,
            OscillatoryOptions::new(options.clone()),
        )?;
        plan.integrate(|_: f64| Ok(1.0), &phase)
    };
    let (c, cosine) = solve(FourierWeight::Cosine)?;
    let (s, sine) = solve(FourierWeight::Sine)?;
    Ok(summary(c, s, &[&cosine, &sine]))
}
```

## The Cornu spiral

The next cell computes $C(t)$ and $S(t)$ at $t = \pm T_s k/150$ ($k = 1, \dots, 150$) with the selected method and plots the spiral.
Construct $t < 0$ points from $t > 0$ values by odd symmetry.
Dashed curves show asymptotics for $|t| \ge 1$; squares mark limit points $(\pm\tfrac12, \pm\tfrac12)$.
Both axes use fixed range $[-0.8, 0.8]$, though displayed aspect ratio depends on screen width.
Absolute tolerance is $10^{-10}$.

Tabulate maximum discrepancies from `fresnel_c` and `fresnel_s` over all points, combined evaluations, and unsuccessful point count.
The final outcome indicates whether every point succeeded.

```rust

    let point = fresnel(spiral_method.as_str(), t, 1e-10).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let mirror = |points: &[[f64; 2]]| points.iter().map(|[c, s]| [-c, -s]).collect::<Vec<_>>();
let mut curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = mirror(&positive).into_iter().rev().collect();
curve.push([0.0, 0.0]);

};
emit_outcome!(

        .status(status)
        .diagnostic("Maximum discrepancy from special", worst, None)
        .diagnostic("Total evaluations", evaluations as f64, None)
);
```

Defaults ($T_s = 4$, Filon) give `ToleranceReached` for both $C$ and $S$ at all 150 points, with maximum discrepancy $5.6 \times 10^{-16}$ from `fresnel_c` and `fresnel_s`.
The spiral passes through the origin and winds around both limits; agreement with dashed circles for $|t| \ge 1$ improves as $|t|$ grows.
Totals are 29290 evaluations for Filon, 36900 for Levin, and 13620 for adaptive Gauss–Kronrod.
For $|t| \le 4$ there are only a few oscillations; Levin switches all intervals to ordinary quadrature, making adaptive Gauss–Kronrod cheapest in evaluations.
At $T_s = 8$, adaptive Gauss–Kronrod still succeeds everywhere with 44400 evaluations, below about 53,000 for Filon and 140,000 for Levin.
Methods that do not resolve oscillations become advantageous at larger $t$, as below.

## Large-t evaluations and asymptotics

At default tolerance $10^{-10}$, all methods' evaluation counts for $t \le 10$ increase with $t$.
At $t = 10$, Filon uses 680, adaptive Gauss–Kronrod 1245, and Levin 3540 evaluations.
Levin needs more because intervals failing collocation condition $a \ge 8$ use equal-panel ordinary quadrature resolving the phase.
For $t \ge 17.8$, Levin and Filon counts barely increase; Levin succeeds even at $t = 100$ with 2780 evaluations.

Adaptive Gauss–Kronrod succeeds through $t = 17.8$ (4125 evaluations) but stops with `Stagnation` for $t \ge 31.6$.
At selected $t \approx 31.6$, its value $C = -0.077$ disagrees completely with reference $0.4999968$.
Initial panels are too coarse for roughly $t^2/4$ oscillations; repeated bisection does not sufficiently reduce estimates, triggering stagnation termination.
Its unsuccessful status means the value is not used.
At $t = 100$, Filon stops with `Stagnation`; values agree within $2.3 \times 10^{-15}$, but status remains unsuccessful.
Loosening tolerance to $10^{-6}$ makes Filon succeed at all 13 points, with 310 evaluations at $t = 31.6$.
Tightening to $10^{-12}$ leaves even Levin unsuccessful at 2 of 13 points.

Asymptotic errors nearly follow dashed $1/(\pi^2t^3)$ for $t \gtrsim 3$ and equal $3.2 \times 10^{-6}$ at $t = 31.6$.
At small $t$ such as $t = 0.1$, asymptotics match neither $C$ nor $S$.
Asymptotics check or indicate large-$t$ values, but accuracy $10^{-10}$ requires at least $t \approx 1000$.

## Straight-edge diffraction

A plane wave of wavelength $\lambda$ strikes a straight knife edge normally; measure intensity on a screen at distance $z$.
Measure screen coordinate $x$ from the geometric shadow boundary, with illuminated side $x > 0$.
Under the Fresnel approximation, intensity relative to unobstructed $I_0$ is

$$
\frac{I}{I_0} = \frac12\Bigl[\Bigl(C(w) + \frac12\Bigr)^2 + \Bigl(S(w) + \frac12\Bigr)^2\Bigr], \qquad w = x\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda z}}
$$


$w$ is the Fresnel parameter.
The right side is half the squared distance from spiral point $(C(w), S(w))$ to limit point $(-\tfrac12, -\tfrac12)$.
As $w \to \infty$, distance becomes $\sqrt2$ and $I = I_0$; at shadow boundary $w = 0$, distance $1/\sqrt2$ gives $I = I_0/4$.
On the illuminated side, winding around $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ gives oscillating intensity (fringes); in shadow $w < 0$, approach to $(-\tfrac12, -\tfrac12)$ decreases intensity monotonically.

The next cell defaults to He–Ne wavelength $\lambda = 633$ nm and $z = 1$ m, computing intensity at 600 positions $x \in [-1.5, 3]$ mm.
For $w < 0$, use $C(-w) = -C(w)$ and $S(-w) = -S(w)$.
Filon computes the Fresnel integrals.
The table checks intensities at first maximum $w_1 = 1.2172$, first minimum $w = 1.8725$, and shadow point $w = -2$ against `fresnel_c` and `fresnel_s`, plus shadow asymptotic $I/I_0 \approx 1/(2\pi^2w^2)$.

## Summary

Fresnel $C(t)$ and $S(t)$ have phase $s^2$; in `OscillatoryQuadraturePlan`, declare the interval containing stationary point $s = 0$ as `OscillatoryRegion::stationary` and later regions as nonstationary with narrow $|g'|$ ranges.
At tolerance $10^{-10}$, adaptive Gauss–Kronrod draws all points of spirals $T_s = 4$ and $8$ with fewest evaluations.
Pointwise, however, Filon at $t = 10$ uses 680 evaluations versus adaptive Gauss–Kronrod's 1245.
Beyond about $t$ = 30, adaptive Gauss–Kronrod stops with `Stagnation` and an incorrect estimate; Levin and Filon barely increase counts while returning values within $10^{-14}$ of references.
Filon also ends with `Stagnation` at $t = 100$ despite accurate values; only Levin succeeds everywhere at default tolerance.
For every method, check `is_success()` and references; do not use unsuccessful values.
Straight-edge intensity depends on spiral distance to $(-\tfrac12, -\tfrac12)$, with fringe spacing proportional to $\sqrt{\lambda z}$.
