Quantities whose integration endpoint moves with parameters often arise in diffusion and phase-change problems.
For example, as chloride diffuses into concrete, the depth at which concentration reaches the threshold for reinforcing-steel corrosion (the front) advances with time, and the chloride amount before the front also increases.
Differentiating this amount with respect to parameters requires both integrand changes and endpoint motion.
This example uses `QuadratureSensitivityPlan` to compute the penetration amount and its sensitivities with both JVP and VJP, checking them against a closed form and duality.

## Diffusive penetration and the critical-concentration front

Assume chloride diffuses inward from the surface of a thick member, with constant surface concentration $c_s$ and zero initial concentration.
The solution of Fick's second law at depth $x$ [mm], age $t$ [a], and apparent diffusivity $D$ [mm²/a] is

$$
\frac{c(x, t)}{c_s} = \operatorname{erfc}\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{Dt}}\right)
$$


A diffusivity of $D = 30$ mm²/a is approximately $10^{-12}$ m²/s.

For critical corrosion concentration $c_{\mathrm{crit}} = r\,c_s$ ($0 < r < 1$), the depth at which concentration equals the threshold is

$$
s(t, D) = 2\lambda\sqrt{Dt}, \qquad \lambda = \operatorname{erfc}^{-1}(r)
$$

so the front advances in proportion to $\sqrt{t}$.
Define the penetration amount as the chloride amount before the front divided by $c_s$:

$$
M(t, D) = \int_0^{s(t, D)} \operatorname{erfc}\left(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{Dt}}\right) dx \quad [\mathrm{mm}]
$$


The quantity $M$ is chloride amount per unit area divided by surface concentration, and has units of length.

Setting $z = x/(2\sqrt{Dt})$ maps the integral to $z \in [0, \lambda]$; using $\int_0^\lambda \operatorname{erfc} z\,dz = \lambda\operatorname{erfc}\lambda + (1 - e^{-\lambda^2})/\sqrt{\pi}$ gives

$$
M = 2\sqrt{Dt}\,G(\lambda), \qquad G(\lambda) = \lambda\operatorname{erfc}\lambda + \frac{1 - e^{-\lambda^2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}
$$


Since $M$ depends only on $Dt$ and is proportional to $\sqrt{Dt}$,

$$
\frac{\partial M}{\partial t} = \frac{M}{2t}, \qquad \frac{\partial M}{\partial D} = \frac{M}{2D}
$$


Use this closed form for the checks below, computing $\operatorname{erfc}$ and $\operatorname{erfc}^{-1}$ with `scioxide-special`.

## Leibniz's integral rule and endpoint terms

For parameters $\boldsymbol{p} = (t, D)$, integrand $f(x; \boldsymbol{p}) = c/c_s$, and interval $[a(\boldsymbol{p}), b(\boldsymbol{p})]$, Leibniz's integral rule gives

$$
dM = \int_a^b \frac{\partial f}{\partial \boldsymbol{p}}\,d\boldsymbol{p}\,dx + f(b)\,db - f(a)\,da
$$


Take partial derivative $\partial f/\partial \boldsymbol{p}$ at fixed $x$; $db = \nabla b \cdot d\boldsymbol{p}$ represents endpoint motion.
Here $a = 0$ is fixed, $b = s$ has $f(s) = r$, and $\nabla s = (s/(2t),\ s/(2D))$.

Using $\operatorname{erfc}'(z) = -2e^{-z^2}/\sqrt{\pi}$, $\partial z/\partial t = -z/(2t)$, and $\partial z/\partial D = -z/(2D)$ gives the integrand partial derivatives

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = \frac{z\,e^{-z^2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,t}, \qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial D} = \frac{z\,e^{-z^2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,D}
$$


For $t$, the interior integral is $\sqrt{D/(\pi t)}\,(1 - e^{-\lambda^2})$ and the endpoint term is $r\lambda\sqrt{D/t}$; their sum equals $\sqrt{D/t}\,G(\lambda) = M/(2t)$.
Omitting the endpoint term underestimates sensitivity by $r\lambda\sqrt{D/t}$.

## JVP and VJP

For $m$ value components and $P$ parameters, sensitivity is the $m \times P$ Jacobian matrix $J = \partial M/\partial \boldsymbol{p}$.
A JVP (Jacobian–vector product) computes $J\boldsymbol{v}$ ($m$ components) for direction $\boldsymbol{v}$; a VJP (vector–Jacobian product) computes $\boldsymbol{w}^{\mathsf T} J$ ($P$ components) for cotangent vector $\boldsymbol{w}$.
Here $m = 1$ and $P = 2$, so one JVP computes one directional derivative, while one VJP with $w = 1$ computes the entire gradient $(\partial M/\partial t, \partial M/\partial D)$.
Obtaining the gradient through JVP requires two computations with different directions.

Exact sensitivities satisfy the following duality for arbitrary $\boldsymbol{v}$ and $\boldsymbol{w}$.

$$
\boldsymbol{w}^{\mathsf T}(J\boldsymbol{v}) = (\boldsymbol{w}^{\mathsf T}J)\,\boldsymbol{v}
$$


JVP and VJP evaluated at the same nodes with the same weights multiply the same discrete Jacobian, so this identity holds to rounding error.
Duality checks consistency between the implementations rather than discretization error.

## Specify the plan, integrand, and endpoints

`QuadratureSensitivityPlan` approximates $M$ and its sensitivities using the same nodes of the same Gauss–Kronrod rule.
`SensitivityDiscretization` provides three partition choices.

- `fixed`: apply one Gauss–Kronrod rule once over the entire interval.
  Termination is `FixedRuleCompleted`; it does not refine until meeting tolerance, but the report indicates whether the error estimate meets tolerance.
- `adaptive`: bisect using G7K15 until both primal and derivative estimates meet tolerance, then stop with `ToleranceReached`.
- `frozen`: reuse a previous call's partition, obtained through `partition`, without refinement.
  JVP and VJP can share the partition.

In every case the plan differentiates the integral on that partition, without differentiating the adaptive partition-selection procedure.
For `fixed` and `frozen`, nodes are independent of $\boldsymbol{p}$, so the computed $M$ is a smooth function of $\boldsymbol{p}$.
The plan fixes interval $[0, s(\boldsymbol{p})]$; rebuild it whenever changing $\boldsymbol{p}$ changes $s$.

The integrand implements `SensitivityIntegrand`; in `evaluate`, write values into `sample.value_mut()`, then apply the Jacobian exactly once.
For JVP alone, pass a `LinearOperator` to `apply_forward`; VJP needs the transpose action, so pass a type implementing both `LinearOperator` and `AdjointOperator` to `apply`.
The operator returned by scioxide-core's `ClosureOperator::new` implements only `LinearOperator`; for VJP, add a transpose-action closure through `with_adjoint` to obtain an `AdjointOperator`.
Endpoint evaluation requires only values: `sample.mode()` is `None` and `apply` does nothing.
Supply moving endpoints with `JvpQuery::with_endpoint_tangent(Some([da, db]))` for JVP and `VjpQuery::with_endpoint_gradients(Some((&grad_a, &grad_b)))` for VJP.
Each node requires one integrand evaluation and one Jacobian application; each moving endpoint adds one value-only evaluation.
NaN or infinity in parameters, directions, endpoint tangents, or gradients returns `NonFinite` with origin `Input` before computation begins.
Nonfinite values or products returned by the integrand produce `NonFinite` with origin `Callback`.

`SensitivityReport` returns termination, integrand-evaluation and Jacobian-application counts, bisection count, and whether primal and derivative error estimates each meet their tolerances.
Workspace `derivative_errors` returns derivative error-estimate components; `total()` sums the quadrature estimate and estimated rounding error in endpoint terms.
Neither is an upper bound.
They also exclude error in the Jacobian action itself.

The following block defines helpers shared by two cells.
`Ingress` supplies integrand $f(x; t, D)$ and applies its Jacobian $(\partial f/\partial t, \partial f/\partial D)$; `front` gives front depth $s$ and `amount` the closed form of $M$.
`sensitivity_options` sets a common tolerance $10^{-10}$ (absolute and relative) for primal and derivative and selects JVP or VJP mode.
The Jacobian is an $1 \times 2$ matrix: the closure passed to `ClosureOperator::new` writes $J\boldsymbol{v}$, and the closure passed to `with_adjoint` writes $J^{\mathsf T}w$.
`ClosureOperator` first writes the result to workspace, checks finiteness, then copies it to output.
This workspace needs row-count length (1) for JVP and column-count length (2) for VJP, so pass a length-2 array to `apply`.
`prepare_plan` creates a plan with the selected discretization (a `fixed` rule or `adaptive`); `jvp` and `vjp` return each computation's value, product, error estimates, and report together in `Run`.
The `tangent` of `jvp` is upper-endpoint motion $\nabla s \cdot \boldsymbol{v}$; supplying `None` omits the endpoint term.
```rust
//| id: integrate-moving-boundary-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Chloride-penetration integrand, Jacobian, and closed form"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{
    ClosureOperator, ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, Interval, NormSelection,
    RealScalar,
};
use scioxide_integrate::{
    GaussKronrodRule, JvpQuery, QuadratureSensitivityPlan, QuadratureSensitivityWorkspace,
    SensitivityIntegrand, SensitivityMode, SensitivityOptions, SensitivityReport,
    SensitivitySample, SubdivisionOptions, VjpQuery,
};

/// f(x; t, D) = erfc(x / (2√(Dt))), with parameters p = [t, D].
pub(crate) struct Ingress;

impl SensitivityIntegrand<f64> for Ingress {
    type Workspace = ();
    fn parameter_dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn value_dimension(&self) -> usize {
        1
    }
    fn supports(&self, _: SensitivityMode) -> bool {
        true
    }
    fn evaluate(
        &self,
        x: f64,
        p: &[f64],
        sample: &mut SensitivitySample<'_, f64>,
        _: &mut (),
        _: ExecutionContext<'_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let (t, d) = (p[0], p[1]);
        let z = x / (2.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(d * t));
        sample.value_mut()[0] = scioxide_special::erfc(z)?;
        // ∂f/∂t = z e^{−z²}/(√π t), ∂f/∂D = z e^{−z²}/(√π D).
        let common = z * RealScalar::exp(-z * z) / RealScalar::sqrt(core::f64::consts::PI);
        let (by_age, by_diffusivity) = (common / t, common / d);
        // The 1 × 2 Jacobian J and closures writing J v and Jᵀ w. At endpoints, apply does nothing.
        let jacobian = ClosureOperator::new(1, 2, |v: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
            out[0] = by_age * v[0] + by_diffusivity * v[1];
            Ok(())
        })
        .with_adjoint(|w: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
            out[0] = by_age * w[0];
            out[1] = by_diffusivity * w[0];
            Ok(())
        });
        // Workspace needs row-count length 1 for J v and column-count length 2 for Jᵀ w.
        sample.apply(&jacobian, &mut [0.0; 2])
    }
}

/// Common primal/derivative tolerance 10⁻¹⁰ (absolute and relative, infinity norm) and mode.
pub(crate) fn sensitivity_options(
    mode: SensitivityMode,
) -> Result<SensitivityOptions<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-10, 1e-10, NormSelection::Infinity)?;
    Ok(
        SensitivityOptions::new(
            SubdivisionOptions::default().with_tolerance(tolerance.clone()),
        )
        .with_derivative_tolerance(tolerance)
        .with_mode(mode),
    )
}

/// Create a plan from the option name ("fixed G7K15" … "fixed G25K51", "adaptive G7K15").
pub(crate) fn prepare_plan(
    discretization: &str,
    interval: Interval<f64>,
    mode: SensitivityMode,
) -> Result<QuadratureSensitivityPlan<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let options = sensitivity_options(mode)?;
    let rule = match discretization {
        "adaptive G7K15" => {
            return QuadratureSensitivityPlan::adaptive(&Ingress, interval, options);
        }
        "fixed G10K21" => GaussKronrodRule::G10K21,
        "fixed G15K31" => GaussKronrodRule::G15K31,
        "fixed G20K41" => GaussKronrodRule::G20K41,
        "fixed G25K51" => GaussKronrodRule::G25K51,
        _ => GaussKronrodRule::G7K15,
    };
    QuadratureSensitivityPlan::fixed(&Ingress, interval, rule, options)
}

/// One JVP or VJP result: value, product (1 JVP component or 2 VJP components),
/// their error estimates (quadrature estimate plus endpoint-term rounding), and report.
pub(crate) struct Run {
    pub(crate) value: f64,
    pub(crate) products: Vec<f64>,
    pub(crate) value_error: Option<f64>,
    pub(crate) product_errors: Vec<Option<f64>>,
    pub(crate) report: SensitivityReport,
}

impl Run {
    fn read(
        value: f64,
        products: Vec<f64>,
        report: SensitivityReport,
        workspace: &QuadratureSensitivityWorkspace<f64>,
    ) -> Self {
        let value_error = workspace.value_errors().and_then(|e| e[0].total());
        let product_errors = workspace
            .derivative_errors()
            .map_or_else(Vec::new, |e| e.iter().map(|c| c.total()).collect());
        Self {
            value,
            products,
            value_error,
            product_errors,
            report,
        }
    }
}

/// J v at p. tangent is upper-endpoint motion db = ∇s·v (None omits the endpoint term).
pub(crate) fn jvp(
    plan: &QuadratureSensitivityPlan<f64>,
    workspace: &mut QuadratureSensitivityWorkspace<f64>,
    p: &[f64],
    v: &[f64],
    tangent: Option<f64>,
) -> Result<Run, SciOxideError> {
    let (mut value, mut product) = (0.0, 0.0);
    // Endpoint tangents [da, db] = [0, ∇s·v] (lower endpoint a = 0 is fixed).
    let query = JvpQuery::new(p, v).with_endpoint_tangent(tangent.map(|db| [0.0, db]));
    let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
    let outputs = (&mut value, &mut product);
    let report = plan.jvp_into(&Ingress, query, outputs, (&mut *workspace, &mut [()]), cx)?;
    Ok(Run::read(value, vec![product], report, workspace))
}

/// wᵀ J at p (w = 1), with endpoint gradients ∇a = 0 and ∇b = grad_b.
pub(crate) fn vjp(
    plan: &QuadratureSensitivityPlan<f64>,
    workspace: &mut QuadratureSensitivityWorkspace<f64>,
    p: &[f64],
    grad_b: &[f64; 2],
) -> Result<Run, SciOxideError> {
    let (mut value, mut product) = (0.0, [0.0; 2]);
    let grad_a = [0.0; 2];
    let query = VjpQuery::new(p, &[1.0]).with_endpoint_gradients(Some((&grad_a, grad_b)));
    let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
    let outputs = (&mut value, &mut product);
    let report = plan.vjp_into(&Ingress, query, outputs, (&mut *workspace, &mut [()]), cx)?;
    Ok(Run::read(value, product.to_vec(), report, workspace))
}

/// Front depth s = 2λ√(Dt) [mm].
pub(crate) fn front(lambda: f64, t: f64, d: f64) -> f64 {
    2.0 * lambda * RealScalar::sqrt(d * t)
}

/// Closed form M = 2√(Dt) G(λ), G(λ) = λ erfc λ + (1 − e^{−λ²})/√π [mm].
pub(crate) fn amount(lambda: f64, t: f64, d: f64) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    let root_pi = RealScalar::sqrt(core::f64::consts::PI);
    let g = lambda * scioxide_special::erfc(lambda)?
        + (1.0 - RealScalar::exp(-lambda * lambda)) / root_pi;
    Ok(2.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(d * t) * g)
}
```

## JVP and VJP at one parameter point

The next cell selects age $t$, diffusivity $D$, and critical concentration ratio $r = 10^{k}$, then computes penetration amount $M$ and sensitivities.
Choose `fixed`, one application of a Gauss–Kronrod rule, or `adaptive` with tolerance $10^{-10}$ (absolute and relative, common to primal and derivative).
The JVP direction is $\boldsymbol{v} = (1\ \mathrm{a}, 0)$ ($t$ direction) or $(0, 1\ \mathrm{mm^2/a})$ ($D$ direction); the VJP cotangent is $w = 1$.
Extract the JVP partition with `partition` and use it in a `frozen` plan to evaluate VJP at the same nodes, checking duality $w(J\boldsymbol{v}) = (J^{\mathsf T}w)\cdot\boldsymbol{v}$.
The first table compares values and sensitivities with closed forms and gives their error estimates.
The second table gives evaluation counts, tolerance checks, duality difference, and termination for each computation.
The outcome reports JVP termination and whether both primal and derivative error estimates meet tolerance.

```rust
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "runs" })
);
export!(diffusivity, d);
export!(lambda, lambda);

let report = &full.report;
let termination = report.termination();
let met = report.primal_tolerance_met() && report.derivative_tolerance_met();
let outcome = if termination.is_success() && met {
    Outcome::satisfied("Value and sensitivity error estimates meet tolerance")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Error estimates do not meet tolerance, or a limit stopped computation")
        .explanation("Read values and sensitivities as estimates; increase the rule's point count or use adaptive.")
};
let evaluations = report.function_evaluations() as f64;
let applications = report.derivative_evaluations() as f64;
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(termination.to_string())
        .diagnostic("Integrand evaluations", evaluations, None)
        .diagnostic("Jacobian applications", applications, None)
);
```

At the defaults ($t = 10$ a, $D = 30$ mm²/a, $r = 0.1$, `fixed G7K15`, $t$ direction), $\lambda = 1.163$, front depth is $s = 40.3$ mm, and penetration amount is $M = 18.52$ mm.
The JVP $J\boldsymbol{v} = \partial M/\partial t = 0.926$ mm/a and VJP gradient $(0.926\ \mathrm{mm/a},\ 0.309\ \mathrm{a/mm})$ agree with closed forms $M/(2t)$ and $M/(2D)$ to relative differences of order $10^{-16}$.
The duality difference is 0, indicating that JVP and VJP at the same nodes use the same discrete Jacobian.
Omitting the endpoint term gives JVP $0.725$ mm/a, underestimating sensitivity by 22 %.
The difference $0.201$ mm/a equals endpoint term $r\lambda\sqrt{D/t}$.

Both JVP and VJP use 16 evaluations: 15 nodes plus one endpoint evaluation; JVP without the endpoint term uses 15.
VJP returns both gradient components in one computation; JVP needs two computations with different directions for the same gradient.
Choosing the $D$ direction makes $J\boldsymbol{v}$ equal $0.309$, the second VJP component, and the duality difference remains 0.
Increasing Gauss–Kronrod points only increases evaluations for this smooth integrand, whose differences are already at rounding level.

Setting $r = 10^{-6}$ gives $\lambda = 3.459$ and deepens the front to $s = 119.8$ mm.
The `fixed G7K15` result agrees with the closed form to relative difference $3.5 \times 10^{-12}$, but error estimates are $6.1 \times 10^{-4}$ for the derivative and $4.6 \times 10^{-4}$ for the primal integral; neither meets tolerance, so the outcome is partial.
This estimate is based on the difference between two rules and greatly exceeds the actual difference.
With `adaptive G7K15`, two bisections (76 evaluations) meet both tolerances; VJP using `frozen` with the extracted three-subinterval partition returns the same gradient in 46 evaluations without bisection.
A small critical ratio also makes concentration $r$ at the front small, so the endpoint term is only about $6 \times 10^{-6}$ of $J\boldsymbol{v}$.

## Penetration amount and sensitivity versus age

The next cell receives $D$ and $\lambda$ from the previous cell and computes $M$ and $\partial M/\partial t$ with JVP at ages $t = 1, 2, \ldots, 50$ a.
Since interval $[0, s(t)]$ changes with $t$, rebuild the `fixed G15K31` plan for each $t$.
The upper chart gives penetration amount and the lower sensitivity, with closed forms dashed and quadrature values as points; both charts have linked age axes.

```rust
// t = 1, 2, …, 50 a. Rebuild the plan at each t because interval [0, s(t)] changes.

    let t = f64::from(k);

    let interval = Interval::new(0.0, s).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
```

For a `fixed` plan whose nodes do not depend on $t$, computed $M(t)$ is smooth in $t$ and JVP gives its derivative.

## Summary

When integration endpoints move with parameters, sensitivity is the integral of the integrand partial derivative plus Leibniz endpoint terms: endpoint value times endpoint motion.
In `QuadratureSensitivityPlan`, supply endpoint motion through `with_endpoint_tangent` for JVP and `with_endpoint_gradients` for VJP; omitting it omits the entire endpoint term.
When there are few value components and many parameters, VJP returns the whole gradient in one computation.
Extracting the JVP partition with `partition` and reusing it for `frozen` VJP allows duality $\boldsymbol{w}^{\mathsf T}(J\boldsymbol{v}) = (\boldsymbol{w}^{\mathsf T}J)\boldsymbol{v}$ to be checked at the same nodes to rounding error.
The `fixed` termination `FixedRuleCompleted` makes no tolerance claim; read `derivative_tolerance_met` together with error estimates, and increase the rule or use `adaptive` if tolerance is unmet.
Rebuild the plan whenever the interval changes, and check sensitivities against closed forms and duality.
