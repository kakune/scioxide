B-splines represent curves as sums of local bases.
Changed controls affect limited regions; degrees and multiplicities design smoothness.
Controls generally are not curve samples.
Separate evaluating given coefficients from solving sample-interpolating coefficients.

## Basis recurrence and continuity

Supply nondecreasing knots $t_0 \le t_1 \le \dots \le t_{K-1}$ and degree $p$.
Basis/control count is $n = K - p - 1$ and evaluation domain $[t_p, t_n]$.
Degree-zero $B_{i,0}$ is the indicator function of interval $[t_i, t_{i+1})$; the Cox–de Boor recurrence raises the degree.

$$
B_{i,p}(x)=\frac{x-t_i}{t_{i+p}-t_i}B_{i,p-1}(x)
+\frac{t_{i+p+1}-x}{t_{i+p+1}-t_{i+1}}B_{i+1,p-1}(x)
$$

Zero-denominator terms are zero.
Induction yields these properties.

- Local support: $B_{i,p}$ vanishes outside $[t_i, t_{i+p+1})$, with at most $p+1$ nonzero bases per point.
- Nonnegativity and partition of unity: $B_{i,p} \ge 0$ and, on the domain, $\sum_i B_{i,p}(x) = 1$.
  Curve $s(x) = \sum_i c_i B_{i,p}(x)$ is therefore a convex combination of controls with nonzero bases.
- Continuity: at an interior knot of multiplicity $k$, the pieces join with continuity through $C^{p-k}$ for general coefficients.
  Coefficient cancellations may make particular curves smoother.
- Endpoints: repeating end knots $p+1$ times in open knots makes endpoint values equal end controls.

Derivatives use one-lower-degree bases.

$$
B_{i,p}'(x) = p\left(\frac{B_{i,p-1}(x)}{t_{i+p}-t_i} - \frac{B_{i+1,p-1}(x)}{t_{i+p+1}-t_{i+1}}\right)
$$

[`BSplineBasis::new(knots, degree)`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.BSplineBasis.html#method.new) checks at least $2(p+1)$ knots, nondecrease, multiplicity at most $p+1$, and nonempty domains.
Violations give `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`); nonfinite knots `NonFinite`.
Named constructors provide common knots.
`BSplineBasis::clamped(breakpoints, degree)` repeats endpoint breaks $p+1$ times and interior breaks once; odd-degree `BSplineBasis::not_a_knot(nodes, degree)` uses $p = 2k+1$, repeated ends $p+1$ and interior $x_{k+1}, \dots, x_{n-k-2}$.
The latter meets nodal Schoenberg–Whitney conditions and gives not-a-knot cubic spaces at $p = 3$.
Binary-search spans and triangular recurrences for $p+1$ nonzero bases give point cost $O(p^2 + \log n)$.
`evaluate_into(x, order, side, policy, out, workspace)` writes all $n$ basis values or order-`order` derivatives, including $O(n)$ zero-fill; `integrals_into(lower, upper, policy, out, workspace)` writes each basis function's definite integral.
Orders above degree and exterior `Clamp` derivatives vanish.
Construct degree-sized `SplineBasisWorkspace` workspace through `basis.workspace()`, reusable at equal or lower degrees.

Knot values default to right-hand pieces.
`KnotSide::Left` and `KnotSide::Right` select the limit side.
Passing `sides = None` to `BSpline::derivative(point, orders, sides, policy)` requests ordinary derivatives; at knots of multiplicity $k$, it compares the two pieces' derivatives exactly at every order above $p - k$.
Mismatches mean nonexistent derivatives and give `DomainError` (`Undefined`).
Explicit sides return their limits without comparison.
Outward sides at domain ends reject under `Reject` or use constant extensions, zero derivatives, under `Clamp`.
Ordinary existence differs from computing a selected side.

[`cardinal_bspline(degree, x)`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/fn.cardinal_bspline.html) evaluates origin-centered cardinal $B_p$ on uniform unit knots $-(p+1)/2, -(p-1)/2, \dots, (p+1)/2$ through degrees 0–3 using closed polynomial pieces.
Support is half-open $[-(p+1)/2, (p+1)/2)$ with zero exterior values and unit integral, such as $B_1(x) = 1 - |x|$ and $B_3(0) = 2/3$.
Cost $O(1)$ without allocation.
Degrees at least 4 give `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).

## Knot insertion and derivative splines

Univariate `BSpline` can represent the same function differently.

`insert_knot(t)` inserts knot $t \in [t_k, t_{k+1})$ with Boehm's algorithm.
New controls for $k - p + 1 \le i \le k$ are

$$
Q_i = (1 - \alpha_i)\, c_{i-1} + \alpha_i\, c_i, \qquad \alpha_i = \frac{t - t_i}{t_{i+p} - t_i}
$$

with earlier controls unchanged and later indices shifted by one.
The result represents the same function without changing the original.
If $t$ lies outside the evaluation domain, the result is `DomainError`; multiplicity above $p+1$ gives `InvalidInput`.
Refined control polygons approach curves, useful before subdivision or shape editing.

`derivative_spline()` returns derivatives as degree-$p-1$ splines

$$
s'(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-2} q_i B_{i,p-1}(x), \qquad q_i = p\,\frac{c_{i+1} - c_i}{t_{i+p+1} - t_{i+1}}
$$

with one knot removed at each end.
Right-hand knot values lose information about nonexistent ordinary derivatives.
Use `derivative_into` to check existence.
Degree-zero inputs return zero splines.

`BSpline::linear(x, y)` gives piecewise-linear sample interpolation with knots $[x_0, x_0, x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}, x_{n-1}]$ and sample coefficients.
Univariate `BSpline` can prepare repeated-integral primitives through `prepare_antiderivative()`.

## Tensor products and grid interpolation

Represent $D$-dimensional surfaces/fields by axis-basis products:

$$
s(x_1,\ldots,x_D)=\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_D}
 c_{i_1\cdots i_D}\prod_{a=1}^D B^{(a)}_{i_a}(x_a)
$$


[`BSpline<S, V, D>`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.BSpline.html) owns axis `BSplineBasis` and affine controls, with $D = 1$ the usual curve case.
Controls `BSpline::new(bases, coefficients)` follow row-major multiindices, last axis fastest, with real components inside each control.
Counts mismatching axis-control products give `DimensionMismatch`.
Complex values retain real/imaginary components with real coordinates.

Evaluate $p_a + 1$ nonzero bases per axis and contract only corresponding control blocks.
Queries never construct full Cartesian-basis matrices.
Point cost is $O\bigl(\sum_a (p_a^2 + \log n_a) + d\prod_a (p_a + 1)\bigr)$ for $d$ components.
Visited controls $\prod_a (p_a + 1)$ grow exponentially with dimensions; locality does not eliminate dimension dependence.
`BSpline` implements `Differentiable`, `TwiceDifferentiable`, and box integral `Integrable`; higher mixed partials use `derivative_into`.

Solve interpolating coefficients through `TensorSplineInterpolationPlan`.
With per-axis `BSplineBasis::not_a_knot` bases, one-shot `BSpline::interpolate(nodes, values, degree)` constructs a Plan and solves once.
Odd-degree $p$ axes need at least $p+1$ nodes; $p = 1$ gives multilinear and $p = 3$ per-axis not-a-knot cubics.
Axis $a$ collocation $A_a[j][i] = B^{(a)}_i(x^{(a)}_j)$ at $x^{(a)}_j$ is square, requiring nodes equal controls.
For grid $Y$, solve $(A_1 \otimes \dots \otimes A_D)\,\mathrm{vec}(C) = \mathrm{vec}(Y)$ without Kronecker products by applying axis $A_a$ successively to lanes.
Schoenberg–Whitney gives necessary/sufficient nonsingularity: at each $i$, $B_i(x_i) \ne 0$, equivalently $t_i < x_i < t_{i+p+1}$, allowing open-end equality.
`TensorSplineInterpolationPlan::new(bases, nodes)` checks node/control equality, otherwise `DimensionMismatch`, and domain membership, outside `DomainError`; nonpositive diagonal basis $B_i(x_i)$ gives `Singular`.
Band LU preparation costs $O(\sum_a n_a (p_a+1)^2)$ work and $O(\sum_a n_a (p_a+1))$ storage.
Axes may ascend or descend; descending axes reverse internally while sample layouts retain caller directions.

### `TensorSplineInterpolationPlan` and workspace requirements

`fit(samples)` returns `BSpline` from last-axis-fastest row-major grid values, allocating workspace and coefficient arrays per call.
For new values at fixed nodes, prepare workspace once and use `fit_into(values, out, workspace, cx)` to write controls directly.
Output `out` has length controls $\prod_a n_a$ times components $m$, row-major like `BSpline::coefficients` with $m$ components inside each control.
`cx` is `ExecutionContext`, forwarding cancellation/deadlines to band solves.
For sequential work without stops, pass `ExecutionContext::sequential_native()`.

`fit_into` workspace size varies with components $m$.
`TensorSplineInterpolationRequirements` expresses sizes, returned by Plan `workspace_requirements(m)`; inspect target $m$ through `dimension()`.
Requirements include two length-$m\prod_a n_a$ coefficient-sized arrays plus the largest axis band-solve storage.
Axis solves alternate arrays without Kronecker matrices.
$m = 0$ is `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`); uncomputable sizes give `SizeOverflow` or `ResourceLimit`.

Construct `TensorSplineInterpolationWorkspace` through `workspace(m)`, satisfying `workspace_requirements(m)` immediately.
`check(&requirements)` returns `Capacity::Sufficient` if sufficient, otherwise `Capacity::NeedsGrowth`, without allocation.
`try_reserve(&requirements)` grows in place; failed allocation returns `AllocationFailure` retaining prior capacity.
Workspace for more components also serves fewer unchanged.
Insufficient `fit_into` workspace gives `DimensionMismatch`.
For varying component counts, reserve maximum requirements once with `try_reserve` so later `fit_into` allocates nothing.

`preparation_bytes(bases)` returns a preparation memory bound before construction without allocation.
Preparation bands, `fit_into` solve workspace, and local evaluation workspace are distinct costs.

Node locations govern collocation conditioning.
Greville nodes $\xi_i = (t_{i+1} + \dots + t_{i+p})/p$ are common; scioxide-interp has no generator, so compute them from this formula.
For sufficiently smooth $f$, best spline-space errors scale as $O(h^{p+1})$ with knot gaps $h$.
Interpolation approaches this accuracy when collocation is well-conditioned.

## Solving tensor coefficients

The next cell uses cubic bases on both axes, with knots $[0,0,0,0,\,0.5,\,1,1,1,1]$ and five controls per axis, interpolating $f(x, y) = x^3 - 2xy^2 + y$ on the $5 \times 5$ grid of Greville points $0, 1/6, 1/2, 5/6, 1$.
$f$ is at most cubic per variable, so tensor cubics reproduce it within rounding.
Input is $x$, with $y = 0.4$ fixed.
Compare values, gradient $(3x^2 - 2y^2,\ -4xy + 1)$, and unit-square integral $5/12$ with formulas.

Two components store the same $f$ correctly with last $y$ fastest and incorrectly with first $x$ fastest.
One `fit_into` uses shared band factors for both components.
Construct one-component workspace and show `check` against two-component requirements before/after `try_reserve`.
The first table compares values, gradients, integrals, and wrong-order values with formulas; the second workspace checks.

```rust
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"diagonal","label":"x = 0.4","description":"On diagonal x = y, even wrong-order components match and hide layout errors.","inputs":{"position":0.4}}
//|   - {"id":"right-edge","label":"x = 1","description":"Values, gradients, and integrals match at domain ends too.","inputs":{"position":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"recovery","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.335625,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"recovery","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"recovery","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.4166666666666667,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"recovery","path":"/rows/4/3","approx":{"value":0.071625,"atol":1e-12}}

use scioxide_interp::{
    BSpline, BSplineBasis, Differentiable, ExtrapolationPolicy, Integrable, Interpolant,
    TensorSplineInterpolationPlan,

let f = |x: f64, y: f64| x * x * x - 2.0 * x * y * y + y;
let knots = vec![0.0_f64, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0];

// Component 1 uses inner x for f, wrong order

    .iter()

    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    .iter()
    .zip(computed.iter().zip(exact))
    .map(|(name, (&c, e))| (*name, c, e, (c - e).abs()))
    .collect();
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Spline [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Analytic value [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "Error [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": { "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 }
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "recovery" }));
let checks = vec![
    ("Before try_reserve", two.dimension(), format!("{before:?}")),
    ("After try_reserve", two.dimension(), format!("{after:?}")),
    ("After try_reserve", one.dimension(), format!("{smaller:?}")),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    ["Workspace state", "Required components dimension()", "check result"],
    checks,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "workspace" })
);
```

At default $x = 0.25$, values, both partials, and integrals have errors around $10^{-16}$, reproducing $f$ within rounding.
Sweeping $x$ over 0–1 keeps errors around $10^{-15}$; input-independent integrals match $5/12$.
Wrong order interpolates transpose $f(y, x)$, giving error $7.2 \times 10^{-2}$ there.
At $x = 0.4$, $x = y$, hiding the wrong order through matching values.
Symmetric functions or diagonal-only checks miss layout errors; use asymmetric functions.
One-component workspace gives `NeedsGrowth` for two; after `try_reserve` it gives `Sufficient`, still `Sufficient` for one.

## Rational curves and positive weights

[`Nurbs`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.Nurbs.html) is a nonuniform rational B-spline with positive $w_i$, defined by

$$
R(t)=\frac{N(t)}{W(t)}, \qquad N(t) = \sum_i w_i c_i B_i(t), \qquad W(t) = \sum_i w_i B_i(t)
$$

Equivalently, project a polynomial B-spline in a space of one higher dimension with homogeneous controls $(w_i c_i, w_i)$ by dividing by its final component.
Finite-degree polynomials cannot exactly represent circular arcs, but quadratic quotients can.
For central angle $\theta < \pi$, controls at the endpoints and the intersection of their tangents, with weights $1, \cos(\theta/2), 1$, exactly represent a circular arc.
Quarter circles $\theta = \pi/2$ use center weight $1/\sqrt{2}$.
Common scaling of all weights preserves curves except for rounding.
Unit weights give polynomial B-splines.

`Nurbs::new(bases, controls, weights)` checks control/weight counts against tensor counts, mismatch `DimensionMismatch`, and positivity, nonpositive `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Normalize weights by the maximum and retain extended exponents.
Like `BSpline`, visit only nonzero-basis blocks of $\prod_a (p_a + 1)$; supplied-workspace `evaluate_into` and similar forms allocate nothing.
`Nurbs` implements `Differentiable` and `TwiceDifferentiable`, not `Integrable`.

Quotient derivatives include denominator variation.
Differentiating $N = RW$ gives

$$
R'=\frac{N'-RW'}{W},\qquad
R''=\frac{N''-2R'W'-RW''}{W}
$$

these expressions, with $R_{ab} = (N_{ab} - R_a W_b - R_b W_a - R W_{ab})/W$ in multiple dimensions.
Even piecewise-linear $N$ and $W$ generally produce nonlinear $R$, so second derivatives need not vanish.
Ordinary internal-knot derivatives require all adjoining rational values and derivatives through requested order to agree.
scioxide-interp accepts each component difference between the two pieces' values and derivatives if it is at most $64(2 + \sum_a p_a)\varepsilon$ times the sum of both sides' first-order rounding-error bounds; larger differences give `DomainError` (`Undefined`).
Ordinary derivatives can exist when homogeneous-coordinate jumps cancel in quotients.
Normalization and extended exponents enlarge ranges without increasing significand precision.
Unrepresentable final results give structured errors.

## Checking quarter-circle radii and curvature

The next cell constructs quadratic NURBS with controls $(r, 0), (r, r), (0, r)$ and weights $1, m/\sqrt{2}, 1$.
Inputs are radius $r$ [m] and center-weight multiplier $m$; only $m = 1$ gives radius-$r$ quarter circles.
At 101 points, measure radius $|R(t)|$, curvature $\kappa = |x'y'' - y'x''|/(x'^2 + y'^2)^{3/2}$ from `TwiceDifferentiable::hessian`, and parameter speed $|R'(t)|$.
The table shows extrema, circle references radius $r$/curvature $1/r$, and maximum deviations.
Circles have constant $|R(t)| = r$ and $\kappa = 1/r$.
Plot curves and dashed reference arcs computed with `RealScalar::cos` and `RealScalar::sin`.

```rust
//| id: interp-nurbs-circle
//| ref: interp-nurbs-circle
//| title: "Check a NURBS quarter circle and center-weight effects"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   radius: {"type":"range","label":"Radius r [m]","min":0.5,"max":3.0,"step":0.1,"value":1.0}
//|   weight: {"type":"range","label":"Center multiplier m [-]","min":0.5,"max":1.5,"step":0.05,"value":1.0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"large-radius","label":"r = 3 m","description":"Changed radii remain circles, with curvature 1/r = 0.333 [1/m].","inputs":{"radius":3}}
//|   - {"id":"ellipse","label":"m = 0.5","description":"Center weights below 1/√2 give elliptical arcs and curvature 0.5–4 [1/m].","inputs":{"weight":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"hyperbola","label":"m = 1.5","description":"Center weights above 1 give hyperbolic arcs.","inputs":{"weight":1.5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1.4142135623730951,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":null}
//|   - case: large-radius
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.3333333333333333,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: ellipse
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.1082,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.5,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":4,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: hyperbola
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.4444444444444444,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"circle","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":1.5,"rtol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{
    BSplineBasis, Differentiable, ExtrapolationPolicy, Interpolant, Nurbs, TwiceDifferentiable,
};

let basis =
    BSplineBasis::new(vec![0.0_f64, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0], 2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let middle = RealScalar::sqrt(0.5_f64) * weight;
let curve = Nurbs::new(
    [basis],
    vec![[radius, 0.0], [radius, radius], [0.0, radius]],
    vec![1.0, middle, 1.0],
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let policy = ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject;
let (mut points, mut reference) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
// Per-quantity [minimum, maximum]: radius, curvature, speed
let mut ranges = [[f64::INFINITY, f64::NEG_INFINITY]; 3];
for i in 0..=100 {
    let t = f64::from(i) / 100.0;
    let [x, y] = curve.evaluate([t], policy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let [[dx, dy]] = curve.gradient([t], policy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let [[[ddx, ddy]]] = curve.hessian([t], policy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Speed with respect to t and speed-normalized curvature
    let v = RealScalar::hypot(dx, dy);
    let kappa = (dx * ddy - dy * ddx).abs() / (v * v * v);
    for (range, q) in ranges.iter_mut().zip([RealScalar::hypot(x, y), kappa, v]) {
        *range = [range[0].min(q), range[1].max(q)];
    }
    points.push([x, y]);
    // Reference arc, uniformly spaced in angle
    let angle = std::f64::consts::FRAC_PI_2 * t;
    reference.push([
        radius * RealScalar::cos(angle),
        radius * RealScalar::sin(angle),
    ]);
}
// Circle values and maximum deviations; speed has no circular reference
let circle = [Some(radius), Some(1.0 / radius), None];
let names = ["Radius |R(t)| [m]", "Curvature κ [1/m]", "Speed |R'(t)| [m]"];
let rows: Vec<(&str, f64, f64, Option<f64>, Option<f64>)> = names
    .iter()
    .zip(ranges.iter().zip(circle))
    .map(|(name, (r, c))| {
        let deviation = c.map(|c| (r[0] - c).abs().max((r[1] - c).abs()));
        (*name, r[0], r[1], c, deviation)
    })
    .collect();
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 7 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Minimum", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Circle value", "type": "number", "format": digits, "nullText": "None" },
    {
        "key": "4",
        "label": "Maximum circle deviation",
        "type": "number",
        "format": { "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 },
        "nullText": "None"
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "circle" }));
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "NURBS", "points": points }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Radius-r arc, reference",
            "points": reference,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "x [m]",
        "yLabel": "y [m]",
        "xMin": 0,
        "xMax": 3,
        "yMin": 0,
        "yMax": 3
    })
);
```

At defaults $r = 1$ and $m = 1$, the maximum deviation of the distance from the origin is $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$ m, all 101 curvatures match $1/r = 1$ within rounding, and the curve overlaps the reference arc.
Speed $|R'(t)|$ varies from $\sqrt{2} = 1.414$ to $1.657$, so $t$ is not proportional to arc length.
Points at matching $t$ differ from angle-uniform references; judge circles by radius/curvature rather than point correspondence.
At 0.5 or 3 m, deviations remain about $10^{-16}$ m and curvature matches $1/r$, 2 or 0.333 [1/m].
Multiplier $m = 0.5$ gives radius deviation 0.108 m and curvature 0.5–4; $m = 1.5$ gives 0.071 m and 0.444–1.5 [1/m].
Changing center weight from $1/\sqrt{2}$ gives other conics with identical controls.
A quadratic rational Bézier curve with unit endpoint weights is elliptical for central weight $w$ satisfying $w < 1$, circular at $w = 1/\sqrt{2}$, parabolic at $w = 1$, and hyperbolic at $w > 1$.
NURBS parameters need not be arc length; compute curvature with speed factors above.

## Summary

B-spline smoothness depends on degree and multiplicity; multiplicity $k$ joins through $C^{p-k}$.
At knots, specify `KnotSide` through `derivative` or check ordinary existence with `None`.
Distinguish direct-control `BSpline::new` from interpolation-solving `TensorSplineInterpolationPlan`; select nodes meeting Schoenberg–Whitney, such as Greville.
Grid samples are last-axis-fastest; for repeated values reserve `try_reserve` using `workspace_requirements` requirements and solve allocation-free with `fit_into`.
Use positive-weight `Nurbs` for arcs/conics; parameters are not arc length, so curvature includes speed.
