Interpolated functions often need derivatives or integrals as well as values.
Examples include velocity from position tables or cumulative energy from output tables.
Applying finite differences or generic quadrature adds step selection, truncation, cancellation, and extra evaluation costs.
Known piecewise-polynomial or B-spline coefficients permit direct calculus.
This page explains method capabilities, formulas and errors, one-sided knot limits, and extrapolation.
Query workspace and batches appear in [Prepared and batch evaluation](../prepared-evaluation-and-batches/).

## Calculus traits and supported methods

All methods evaluate through shared `Interpolant`.
Calculus is offered only through implemented traits.

- `Differentiable` returns physical first derivatives as `[Value; D]`.
- `TwiceDifferentiable` returns row-major second derivatives as `[[Value; D]; D]`.
- `Integrable` returns definite integrals over box $[\boldsymbol a,\boldsymbol b]$.

Univariate interpolants also offer scalar `Univariate` calling these under `Reject`.
`value_at(x)`, `derivative_at(x)`, and `integrate(a, b)` match bits of `evaluate`, `gradient`, and `integral` and give `DomainError` outside domains.
`derivative_at` and `integrate` require implementations of `Differentiable` and `Integrable` respectively.

| Method | `Differentiable` | `TwiceDifferentiable` | `Integrable` | Other means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BSpline`, univariate or tensor | Yes | Yes | Yes | `derivative` for arbitrary orders/sides, `prepare_antiderivative` |
| `CubicSpline`, `PchipInterpolator`, `AkimaInterpolator` | Yes | — | Yes | `CubicSpline::prepare_antiderivative` |
| `BarycentricInterpolator`, `NewtonInterpolator`, `HermiteInterpolator` | Yes | — | — | `prepare_antiderivative` |
| `Nurbs` | ○ | ○ | — | — |
| `BicubicInterpolator` | ○ | ○ | — | — |
| `SphericalSpline` | Yes | Yes | Yes | `derivative` through second order per axis |
| `SimplexLinearInterpolator`, `CloughTocherInterpolator` | Yes | — | — | — |

Unlisted methods—Neville, Bulirsch–Stoer, rationals, bilinear, RBFs, nearest, natural neighbors, local models—provide values only.
Check implementations in [API guide](../../api/) items rather than assuming universal calculus.
Piecewise-cubic methods do not implement the second-derivative trait.
For cubic second derivatives or curvature, construct a cubic `BSpline` through the same samples with `BSpline::interpolate([x], &y, 3)` ([B-splines, tensor products, and NURBS](../b-splines-tensor-and-nurbs/)).
Its not-a-knot ends match `CubicSpline::new` within rounding for at least four samples.
It does not match `CubicSpline` with other boundaries.
Global `BarycentricInterpolator`, `NewtonInterpolator`, and `HermiteInterpolator` need power conversion costing $O(n^2)$ for integrals; they omit `Integrable` and prepare antiderivatives to convert only once.

## Piecewise-polynomial derivatives

Represent a degree-$d$ piece on interval $[x_i,x_{i+1}]$ of width $h_i=x_{i+1}-x_i$ using normalized local $s=(x-x_i)/h_i\in[0,1]$ as follows.

$$
p_i(x)=P_i(s)=\sum_{k=0}^dc_{i,k}s^k
$$


Both piecewise cubics and B-spline knot pieces admit this form.
Chain rule $ds/dx=1/h_i$ gives derivative order $m$ as

$$
p_i^{(m)}(x)=h_i^{-m}\sum_{k=m}^d\frac{k!}{(k-m)!}c_{i,k}s^{k-m}
$$


For cubics $d=3$, these become $p_i'=h_i^{-1}(c_1+2c_2s+3c_3s^2)$ and $p_i''=h_i^{-2}(2c_2+6c_3s)$.
Horner evaluation takes $O(d)$ multiplies/additions without finite-difference step truncation or cancellation.
Only coefficient errors and evaluation rounding remain.
Factor $h_i^{-m}$ can nevertheless strongly amplify small coefficient errors for high derivatives on narrow intervals.

## Comparison with differences

The next cell samples cubic $p(x)=x^3-2x^2+x$ at five points on $x=0,0.25,\dots,1$ and constructs clamped `CubicSpline` with slopes $p'(0)=1$ and $p'(1)=0$.
Pass `SplineBoundary::Clamped(1.0, 0.0)` to `CubicSpline::with_boundary` for clamping.
Clamped cubics reproduce cubics; `derivative_at` should match analytic $p'(x)=3x^2-4x+1$ within rounding.
Input $x\in[0,1]$ selects the evaluation point; outputs show the difference between `derivative_at` and the analytic derivative, and the error of the central difference $\{S(x+h)-S(x-h)\}/(2h)$ formed from the same spline values.
Sweep $h$ from $10^{-16}$ to $10^{-2}$, extending outermost polynomials through `MethodDefined`.
Since $p$ is cubic, central truncation is exactly $h^2|p'''|/6=h^2$, drawn dashed.

```rust
//| id: interp-analytic-derivative
//| ref: interp-analytic-derivative
//| title: "Vary analytic interpolant derivatives and redifferencing errors"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   evaluation_x: {"type":"range","label":"Evaluation point x [-]","description":"Change differentiation position within the cubic intervals.","min":0,"max":1,"step":0.02,"value":0.4}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"right-end","label":"x = 1","description":"p(1) = p'(1) = 0 makes small-h differences round to zero, with exactly zero errors.","inputs":{"evaluation_x":1}}
//|   - {"id":"left-end","label":"x = 0","description":"derivative_at matches analytic values even at endpoint nodes.","inputs":{"evaluation_x":0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":-0.12,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.395009108229317e-12,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":0}
//|   - case: right-end
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":33}
//|   - case: left-end
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1,"rtol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":31}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{
    CubicSpline, ExtrapolationPolicy, Interpolant, SplineBoundary, Univariate,
};

// Five samples of p(x) = x³ − 2x² + x; clamped cubic splines reproduce p
let p = |x: f64| x * x * x - 2.0 * x * x + x;
// Independent check: p'(x) = 3x² − 4x + 1
let exact = 3.0 * evaluation_x * evaluation_x - 4.0 * evaluation_x + 1.0;
let nodes: Vec<f64> = (0..=4).map(|i| i as f64 / 4.0).collect();
let spline = CubicSpline::with_boundary(
    nodes.clone(),
    nodes.iter().map(|&x| p(x)).collect(),
    SplineBoundary::Clamped(1.0, 0.0),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let analytic = spline
    .derivative_at(evaluation_x)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let value = |x: f64| {
    spline
        .evaluate([x], ExtrapolationPolicy::MethodDefined)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
// Sweep central h logarithmically from 10^-16 to 10^-2
let mut curve = Vec::new();
let mut truncation = Vec::new();
let mut best = (f64::INFINITY, 0.0_f64);
let mut zero_errors = 0;
for index in 0..=64 {
    let h = RealScalar::powf(10.0, -16.0 + 14.0 * index as f64 / 64.0);
    let estimate = (value(evaluation_x + h)? - value(evaluation_x - h)?) / (2.0 * h);
    let error = (estimate - exact).abs();
    // Count and exclude zero errors from logarithmic axes, displaying counts in the table
    if error > 0.0 {
        curve.push([h, error]);
    } else {
        zero_errors += 1;
    }
    // Cubic central truncation h² |p'''| / 6 = h²
    truncation.push([h, h * h]);
    if error < best.0 {
        best = (error, h);
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let digits15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Analytic p'(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits15 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Difference from derivative_at [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Minimum central error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Corresponding h [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Zero-error steps", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(exact, (analytic - exact).abs(), best.0, best.1, zero_errors)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({"name": "Central error", "points": curve}),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Truncation h²",
            "points": truncation,
            "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Difference step h [-]",
        "yLabel": "Analytic-derivative error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": best.1, "label": "Error-minimizing h"}
        ]
    })
);
```

Central error is estimated as the following truncation-plus-rounding sum.

$$
E(h)\approx\frac{h^2}{6}|p'''(x)|+\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}M}{h}
$$


$M$ is the magnitude of $|S|$ near $x$; both evaluations have rounding about $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}M$.
At large $h$, truncation dominates along dashed $h^2$ with slope $+2$.
At small $h$, numerator cancellation divided by $h$ makes rounding dominate with growth slope $-1$.
Errors minimize near balance $h^*=(3\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}M/|p'''|)^{1/3}$.
`derivative_at` (`Differentiable::gradient`) uses no steps and is unrelated to this balance.

At default $x=0.4$, `derivative_at` differs from analytic $-0.12$ by $1.1\times10^{-16}$.
Central differences minimize at $h\approx7\times10^{-7}$ with error $1.4\times10^{-12}$.
This $h$ matches the scale of model $h^*\approx2.5\times10^{-6}$ with $M=|p(0.4)|=0.144$.
The minimum lies an order below estimate $1.5\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}M/h^*\approx2\times10^{-11}$; models indicate magnitude rather than bounds.
At $x=1$, $p(1)=p'(1)=0$, so small $h$ makes both evaluated values equal within rounding, giving an exactly zero difference quotient and error for 33 step sizes.
This is rounding erasing truncation $h^2$, not evidence that differences are correct.
At $x=0$ (`left-end`), $h$ at most about $10^{-9}$ also makes truncation $h^2$ round away relative to analytic 1, giving zero error for 31 step sizes.
`derivative_at` matches analytic 1 even at endpoints.

## Piecewise-polynomial integrals

Within interval $[x_i,x_{i+1}]$, integration over $[\alpha,\beta]$ uses local coordinates $u=(\alpha-x_i)/h_i$ and $v=(\beta-x_i)/h_i$ as follows.

$$
\int_\alpha^\beta p_i(x)\,dx=h_i\int_u^vP_i(s)\,ds
=(\beta-\alpha)\sum_{k=0}^d\frac{c_{i,k}}{k+1}\cdot\frac{v^{k+1}-u^{k+1}}{v-u}
$$


$(v^{k+1}-u^{k+1})/(v-u)=\sum_{j=0}^ku^jv^{k-j}$ is computable without subtraction, avoiding cancellation between close primitive values even over short intervals.
SciOxide uses this factored form per interval and multiplies by $(\beta-\alpha)$ last.
Cubic averages are $c_0+c_1(u+v)/2+c_2(u^2+uv+v^2)/3+c_3(u^3+u^2v+uv^2+v^3)/4$.

For spanning $[\alpha,\beta]$, binary-search endpoint pieces and add partial end integrals and complete middle integrals.
`Integrable::integral` sums middle intervals individually, costing $O(\log n+k)$ for $k$ crossed intervals.
Check finite limits and lower ≤ upper first; reversed limits give `InvalidInput`.

## Preparing antiderivatives

Precomputing cumulative integrals makes repeated same-curve integrals independent of crossed-interval count.
For node $x_0<\dots<x_n$ full integrals $H_j=\int_{x_j}^{x_{j+1}}p$, store cumulative sums

$$
A_0=0,\qquad A_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}H_j=\int_{x_0}^{x_k}p(t)\,dt
$$

For $\alpha\in[x_i,x_{i+1}]$ and $\beta\in[x_j,x_{j+1}]$ ($i<j$),

$$
\int_\alpha^\beta p\,dx=\int_\alpha^{x_{i+1}}p_i\,dx+(A_j-A_{i+1})+\int_{x_j}^\beta p_j\,dx
$$

use this expression, with factored partial end integrals above.
If $i=j$, compute only same-interval partial integrals without cumulative sums.
Difference $A_j-A_{i+1}$ may lose relative accuracy through cancellation when the queried integral is small relative to the full cumulative integral.

### Spline antiderivatives

`BSpline::prepare_antiderivative` and `CubicSpline::prepare_antiderivative` return `PreparedSplineAntiderivative`.
It owns local normalized primitive coefficients and sums independently of the spline, surviving its drop and transferable across threads.
Storage for $m$ intervals, degree $p$, and $\dim$ components is $O(m(p+1)\dim)$.
B-spline preparation converts nonempty knot pieces to local powers through blossoming, equivalent to Bézier extraction, and integrates, costing $O(m\dim)$ through degree 3 or $O(mp^3\dim)$ generally.
`CubicSpline` preparation costs $O(n\dim)$.
Queries cost $O(\log m+p)$ per component.
`integral_into(a, b, policy, out, scratch)` accepts `scratch` at least as long as component count and allocates nothing.
Owned `integral(a, b, policy)` creates workspace internally and allocates dynamic outputs too.
B-splines prepare only univariate curves; use `Integrable` for tensor integrals.
High-degree power conversion can amplify rounding, so results are not accuracy proofs.

### Polynomial antiderivatives

`prepare_antiderivative` of `BarycentricInterpolator`, `NewtonInterpolator`, and `HermiteInterpolator` returns `PreparedPolynomialAntiderivative`.
It converts interpolation to powers of normalized $u=(x-c)/r$, with interval midpoint $c$ and half-width $r$, then stores primitive $F$ coefficients per real component.
Definite $F(b)-F(a)$ integrates constant extensions exactly under `Clamp`, extended polynomials under other policies.
For $n$ nodes and $d$ components, preparation costs $O(n^2d)$ and storage $O(nd)$; integrals cost $O(nd)$ without allocation.
High-degree power conversion may be ill-conditioned and short-range $F(b)-F(a)$ can cancel; check high-degree integrals at nonsample points or by other quadrature.

## Knot smoothness and one-sided limits

Piecewise methods have different nodal continuity orders.

- Piecewise lines are $C^0$, generally with first-derivative jumps.
- PCHIP/Akima cubics are $C^1$, generally with second-derivative jumps.
- Cubic splines are $C^2$, generally with third-derivative jumps.
- Degree-$p$ B-splines are $C^{p-r}$ at multiplicity-$r$ knots.

`BSpline` uses right-hand pieces for knot values.
`derivative(point, orders, sides, policy)` computes derivatives with per-axis orders `orders` and side `sides`.
Setting `sides` to `None` requests ordinary derivatives and checks existence.
At internal multiplicity $r$ knots, compare left/right pieces exactly componentwise for every order above $p-r$ up to both requested order and degree $p$; any mismatch gives `DomainError` (`Undefined`).
Accidentally equal pieces permit that derivative.
`Differentiable::gradient`, univariate `derivative_at`, and `TwiceDifferentiable::hessian` perform the same checks.

Passing `Some([KnotSide::Left])` or `Some([KnotSide::Right])` to `sides` bypasses checks and returns the selected polynomial limit

$$
p'(t_k^-)=\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}p'(t_k-\epsilon),\qquad
p'(t_k^+)=\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}p'(t_k+\epsilon)
$$

These are direct coefficient-based limits, not difference approximations.
At domain ends, outward sides give `DomainError` under `Reject` or zero derivatives of constant extensions under `Clamp`.
Orders above axis degree give zero.

The next cell uses degree-zero piecewise-constant B-splines with knots $[0,1,2]$ and coefficients $[1,H]$.
Input second height $H$ creates a jump at $t=1$ when $H\ne1$.
Outputs are values at $t=1$, ordinary and one-sided derivatives, and full-domain and $[0.5,1.5]$ integrals.
Use `value_at` for values, `derivative_at` for ordinary derivatives, and `derivative` for sides.
Integrate two ways, `integrate` as scalar `Integrable::integral` and prepared primitives, comparing closed $1+H$ and $0.5(1+H)$.

```rust
//| id: interp-spline-jump-calculus
//| ref: interp-spline-jump-calculus
//| title: "Distinguish degree-zero jumps from one-sided derivatives"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   second_height: {"type":"range","label":"Second-piece height H [-]","description":"H = 1 removes the jump.","min":0,"max":5,"step":0.5,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"no-jump","label":"H = 1","description":"Matching pieces make ordinary derivatives defined and zero.","inputs":{"second_height":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/4/1","approx":{"value":4,"rtol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":4,"rtol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/7/1","approx":{"value":2,"rtol":1e-15}}
//|   - case: no-jump
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":2,"rtol":1e-15}}
use scioxide_core::{DomainReason, SciOxideErrorView};
use scioxide_interp::{BSpline, BSplineBasis, ExtrapolationPolicy, KnotSide, Univariate};

// Degree-zero B-spline with knots [0, 1, 2] and coefficients [1, H]
let curve = BSpline::new(
    [BSplineBasis::new(vec![0.0_f64, 1.0, 2.0], 0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?],
    vec![1.0, second_height],
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let policy = ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject;
// Knot values use right-hand pieces
let value = curve.value_at(1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Ordinary derivatives give DomainError (Undefined) if pieces disagree
let ordinary = match curve.derivative_at(1.0) {
    Ok(slope) => Some(slope),
    Err(error) => match error.view() {
        SciOxideErrorView::DomainError {
            reason: DomainReason::Undefined,
            ..
        } => None,
        _ => return Err(error.to_string()),
    },
};
// Selecting a side returns its derivative
let left = curve
    .derivative([1.0], [1], Some([KnotSide::Left]), policy)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let right = curve
    .derivative([1.0], [1], Some([KnotSide::Right]), policy)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Compare integrate (Integrable) with prepared primitives
let primitive = curve.prepare_antiderivative().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let whole = curve.integrate(0.0, 2.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let whole_prepared = primitive
    .integral(0.0, 2.0, policy)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let middle_prepared = primitive
    .integral(0.5, 1.5, policy)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value [-]", "type": "number", "nullText": "DomainError（Undefined）" }
]);
let rows = vec![
    ("Value at t = 1, right piece", Some(value)),
    ("Ordinary derivative at t = 1", ordinary),
    ("Left derivative", Some(left)),
    ("Right derivative", Some(right)),
    ("∫₀² s dt（integrate）", Some(whole)),
    ("∫₀² s dt, primitive", Some(whole_prepared)),
    ("Closed 1 + H", Some(1.0 + second_height)),
    ("∫ s dt on [0.5, 1.5], primitive", Some(middle_prepared)),
    ("Closed 0.5(1 + H)", Some(0.5 * (1.0 + second_height))),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "jump" })
);
```

At default $H=3$, $t=1$ gives right-piece value 3 and ordinary derivative `DomainError` (`Undefined`).
Both one-sided derivatives are zero, reflecting constant pieces.
Both full integrals are 4, matching $1+H$; the $[0.5,1.5]$ integral matches $0.5(1+H)=2$ too.
Jump points have measure zero and do not affect integrals.
At $H=1$, matching pieces make ordinary derivatives defined and zero.

## Extrapolation and calculus

Pass the same `ExtrapolationPolicy` per derivative or integral query as for values.
`Clamp` extends boundaries constantly, making strictly exterior interval/box-axis derivatives zero.
Boundary points count as inside and use internal-piece derivatives.
`Clamp` integrates internal formulas and adds exact constant boundary values times exterior lengths.
`BoundedContinue` and `MethodDefined` extend outermost polynomials, or global polynomials, for differentiation/integration.
Extended polynomials may grow rapidly away from domains; permission to extrapolate guarantees no accuracy.
Outside-hull gradients appear in [Scattered interpolation](oxref:interp-convex-hull-extrapolation).

## Summary

Calculus exists only through implemented traits; check `Differentiable`, `TwiceDifferentiable`, and `Integrable` first.
Direct piecewise/B-spline derivatives avoid difference step selection and cancellation, but narrow-piece high derivatives can amplify coefficient errors.
For repeated integrals, prepare with `prepare_antiderivative`; `integral_into` queries cost $O(\log m+p)$ without allocation.
Undefined B-spline knot derivatives give `DomainError` (`Undefined`); specify one-sided limits with `KnotSide` when needed.
`Clamp` zeroes outside-axis derivatives and includes constant integral extensions.
