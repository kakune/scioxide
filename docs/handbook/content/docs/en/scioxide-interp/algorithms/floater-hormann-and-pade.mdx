AAA selects supports and weights from samples, leaving pole locations unknown until fitted.
Other rational methods predetermine denominator forms to control poles.
This page covers pole-free rational interpolation from real nodes with Floater–Hormann and approximation from Taylor coefficients at one point with Padé.
They use different information: samples versus series coefficients.
[Rational approximation and pole diagnostics](../rational-approximation/) explains barycentric rational forms and poles.

## Floater–Hormann blending

[`FloaterHormannInterpolator`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.FloaterHormannInterpolator.html) is a rational function that interpolates samples at strictly increasing real nodes $x_0 < \dots < x_n$.
Choose degree $d$ and blend local degree-at-most-$d$ polynomials $p_i$ ($i = 0, \dots, n-d$) through consecutive $d+1$ points with these weights.

$$
r(x) = \frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n-d} \lambda_i(x)\, p_i(x)}{\sum_{i=0}^{n-d} \lambda_i(x)},
\qquad
\lambda_i(x) = \frac{(-1)^i}{(x - x_i)(x - x_{i+1}) \cdots (x - x_{i+d})}
$$

As $x$ approaches node $x_k$, only weights of local polynomials containing $x_k$ diverge; all those $p_i$ take $y_k$ at $x_k$, giving $r(x_k) = y_k$ and sample interpolation.
Each $p_i$ reproduces any degree-at-most-$d$ polynomial $f$, so $r$ also reproduces polynomials of degree at most $d$.
At $d = n$ only one local polynomial remains, giving global polynomial interpolation.

Floater and Hormann (2007) used alternating weight signs $\lambda_i$ to prove denominator $\sum_i \lambda_i(x)$ never vanishes on the real axis, so $r$ has no real poles for any $d$.
Moreover, for $d \ge 1$ and $f \in C^{d+2}[x_0, x_n]$, maximum spacing $h$ gives

$$
\max_{x \in [x_0, x_n]} |f(x) - r(x)| = O(h^{d+1})
$$


the bound with constants determined by interval length and the maxima of $f^{(d+1)}$ and $f^{(d+2)}$, independently of node placement.
$d = 0$ is Berrut interpolation, with error $O(h)$ when adjacent-gap ratios are bounded.
Unlike global polynomials, convergence depends on local degree $d$ rather than total nodes.

The rational has a second barycentric form with weights

$$
w_k = (-1)^{k-d} \sum_{i \in J_k} \prod_{j=i,\, j \ne k}^{i+d} \frac{1}{|x_k - x_j|},
\qquad J_k = \{ i : \max(0, k-d) \le i \le \min(k, n-d) \}
$$


where $J_k$ indexes local polynomials containing $x_k$.
Uniform-node Lebesgue constants grow only logarithmically in $n$ but exponentially in $d$ (Bos et al. 2012).
Higher $d$ raises convergence order and sample-error amplification; keep $d$ small.

`FloaterHormannInterpolator::new(nodes, values, degree)` requires $0 \le d < N$ for $N = n + 1$ nodes; invalid degrees give `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Degree defines the result without a neutral default and is an argument alongside samples.
Change separation by passing `SampleOptions` to `with_options(nodes, values, degree, options)`.
Logarithmic sums avoid weight overflow, taking $O(Nd^2)$ work and $O(N)$ storage; normalize largest magnitude to 1 and expose through `weights()`.
Weights underflowing to zero or unrepresentable gaps give `NumericalInstability`.
Affine values and the `BarycentricInterpolator` evaluation kernel match polynomial methods; per-component cost is $O(N)$ independently of degree.
No derivatives or integrals are provided.
Absence of real poles excludes neither complex poles nor rounding-induced ill-conditioning.

### Orders at uniform nodes

The next cell interpolates Runge $f(x) = 1/(1 + 25x^2)$ on uniform $[-1, 1]$, measuring maximum errors for node counts $N = 11, 21, \dots, 641$ and maximum spacing $h = 2/(N-1)$.
Input Floater–Hormann degree $d$ ranges from 0 to 6.
$f$ is smooth on the reals with complex poles $\pm i/5$; uniform global polynomials diverge as nodes increase, the Runge phenomenon.
For $N \le 41$, also compare same-node global `BarycentricInterpolator` errors.
Measure maxima against closed-form $f$ at interval midpoints.
Adjacent $N$ errors give observed order $\log_2(e_{h}/e_{h/2})$, compared with theoretical $d + 1$.
Dashed reference lines pass through $N = 161$ error with slope $d + 1$.

```rust
//| id: interp-floater-hormann-convergence
//| ref: interp-floater-hormann-convergence
//| title: "Measure Floater–Hormann orders at uniform nodes"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Floater–Hormann degree d","description":"Degree of blended local polynomials.","min":0,"max":6,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"berrut","label":"d = 0（Berrut）","description":"Observed order approaches 1.","inputs":{"degree":0}}
//|   - {"id":"degree-six","label":"d = 6","description":"Order approaches 7, then falls when errors reach rounding.","inputs":{"degree":6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/6/0","equals":641}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/6/2","approx":{"value":1.0147160889317774e-11,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/6/3","approx":{"value":4.0,"atol":0.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":1e4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/4","equals":null}
//|   - case: berrut
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/6/3","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/6/2","approx":{"value":9.537558412703318e-5,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: degree-six
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/3","approx":{"value":7.0,"atol":0.3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{BarycentricInterpolator, FloaterHormannInterpolator, Univariate};

// Runge f(x) = 1/(1 + 25x²); measure errors against its closed form
let runge = |x: f64| 1.0 / (1.0 + 25.0 * x * x);
let d = degree as usize;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let (mut fh_curve, mut poly_curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut previous: Option<f64> = None;
for level in 0..7 {
    let intervals = 10_usize << level;
    let h = 2.0 / intervals as f64;
    let x: Vec<f64> = (0..=intervals)
        .map(|i| -1.0 + 2.0 * i as f64 / intervals as f64)
        .collect();
    let y: Vec<f64> = x.iter().map(|&v| runge(v)).collect();
    // Measure errors at each interval midpoint
    let probes: Vec<f64> = x.windows(2).map(|w| 0.5 * (w[0] + w[1])).collect();
    let fh =
        FloaterHormannInterpolator::new(x.clone(), y.clone(), d).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut fh_error = 0.0_f64;
    for &t in &probes {
        fh_error = fh_error.max((fh.value_at(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - runge(t)).abs());
    }
    // Runge divergence limits global polynomials to 41 nodes
    let poly_error = if x.len() <= 41 {
        let poly = BarycentricInterpolator::new(x.clone(), y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let mut error = 0.0_f64;
        for &t in &probes {
            error = error.max((poly.value_at(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - runge(t)).abs());
        }
        poly_curve.push([h, error]);
        Some(error)
    } else {
        None
    };
    // Observed log₂(e_h / e_{h/2})
    let order = previous.map(|coarse| RealScalar::ln(coarse / fh_error) / RealScalar::ln(2.0));
    previous = Some(fh_error);
    fh_curve.push([h, fh_error]);
    rows.push((x.len(), h, fh_error, order, poly_error));
}
// Slope d + 1 reference through N = 161 error
let [anchor_h, anchor_error] = fh_curve[4];
let reference: Vec<[f64; 2]> = fh_curve
    .iter()
    .map(|&[h, _]| {
        [
            h,
            anchor_error * RealScalar::powi(h / anchor_h, d as i32 + 1),
        ]
    })
    .collect();
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Nodes N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Spacing h [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum Floater–Hormann error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Observed order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2, "nullText": "—" },
    {
        "key": "4",
        "label": "Maximum global-polynomial error [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": sci3,
        "nullText": "Not computed"
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": format!("Floater–Hormann（d = {d}）"),
            "points": fh_curve,
            "style": {"showSymbol": true}
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": format!("Slope {} reference", d + 1),
            "points": reference,
            "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Global polynomial interpolation",
            "points": poly_curve,
            "style": {"showSymbol": true}
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Maximum spacing h [-]",
        "yLabel": "Maximum error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
```

At default $d = 3$, maxima fall from $6.42 \times 10^{-2}$ at $N = 11$ to $1.01 \times 10^{-11}$ at $N = 641$; final observed 4.02 agrees with order 4.
Orders from $N = 21$ through $81$ are 4.6, 9.4, and 6.4, away from 4.
Coarse $h$ comparable to variation width $1/5$ of $f$ has not entered asymptotic $O(h^{d+1})$ behavior.
Same-node global errors grow at $N = 11, 21, 41$ through 1.53, 40.0, and $5.74 \times 10^{4}$, showing degree-driven divergence.
At $d = 0$, orders 0.96, 0.98, 0.99, 1.00 approach 1.
At $d = 6$, order 7.19 at $N = 161 \to 321$ approaches 7, but $N = 641$ errors reach rounding $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ and order falls to 4.53.
Compare observed orders with theory only when truncation greatly exceeds rounding.

## Padé from Taylor coefficients

[`PadeApproximant`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.PadeApproximant.html) takes ascending Taylor coefficients $c_k$ at center $z_0$ rather than samples.
Type-$(m, n)$ Padé $P/Q$ uses $\deg P \le m$, $\deg Q \le n$, and $Q(0) = 1$ in local coordinate $u = z - z_0$ to satisfy

$$
Q(u) f(u) - P(u) = O\bigl(u^{m+n+1}\bigr)
$$

Unknowns are $m+1$ in $P$ and $n$ in $Q$ ($q_0 = 1$), matching $m+n+1$ coefficient conditions of $u^0, \dots, u^{m+n}$.
Comparing $u^k$ coefficients, rows $k = m+1, \dots, m+n$ give a $n \times n$ Toeplitz system for $Q$ coefficients $q_1, \dots, q_n$:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{n} q_j\, c_{k-j} = -c_k \qquad (k = m+1, \dots, m+n)
$$

with $k - j < 0$ coefficients zero; rows $k = 0, \dots, m$ then determine $P$ coefficients $p_k = \sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,n)} q_j c_{k-j}$.
scioxide-interp scales coefficients by maximum magnitude and solves using SVD.
Rank deficiency uses truncated-SVD minimum-norm solutions.
Consistent deficient systems are accepted with rank reports and retained common factors of $P$ and $Q$.
Inconsistent systems have residuals in $n$ unused conditions above `PolynomialApproximationPolicy` tolerance, giving `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).
This is a degenerate Padé type unable to meet all matching conditions.

`PadeApproximant::new(coefficients, m, n, center, domain)` requires at least $m + n + 1$ coefficients and ignores extras.
Rank and coefficient tolerance use `PolynomialApproximationPolicy::default()` (rank `DimensionScaledEpsilon`, absolute/relative $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$); pass alternatives to `with_options(coefficients, m, n, center, domain, policy)`.
Too few coefficients give `DimensionMismatch`; NaN/infinite coefficients `NonFinite`; outside centers `DomainError`; $m + n + 1$ not fitting `usize` gives `SizeOverflow`.
Construction takes $O(n^3 + mn)$ work and $O(n^2 + m)$ storage; Horner evaluation $O(m + n)$ without allocation.
`coefficient_residual()` gives the maximum coefficient residual of the matching condition $Qf - P$ through $u^{m+n}$; `denominator_rank()` gives the denominator system's numerical rank; `rational()` gives the rational function $P/Q$ in local coordinates.
`fit_report()` always reports `Converged` and 1 iteration, with denominator-system singular ratio, or 1 for $n = 0$.
`build(.., policy, scope)` first authorizes one scoped iteration; rejection gives `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).
Evaluate with the same one- or two-coordinate forms as `BarycentricRational`.
Zero denominators or denominator estimates at least their magnitudes, permitting true zero, are treated as poles and give `DomainError`.
Series matching near centers implies no distant accuracy.
Poles of $e^x$ Padé are covered in [Exponential Padé approximations](../../../scioxide-poly/examples/pade-exponential-poles/); AAA/closed resonance comparisons in [Resonance poles](../../examples/aaa-resonance-poles/).

## Samples versus Taylor coefficients

The next cell represents $e^x$ with degree-$d$ Floater–Hormann using five irregular samples $x = -1, -0.7, -0.1, 0.4, 1$ and type-$(2,2)$ Padé from Taylor coefficients at 0.
Inputs are position $x$ and degree $d$, 0–4; $d = 4$ matches global polynomials.
Outputs compare point values against `RealScalar::exp` references, Padé diagnostics, and errors over 201 points on $[-1, 1]$.
Also show closed-form Padé $(1 + x/2 + x^2/12)/(1 - x/2 + x^2/12)$ as an independent check.
Both use standard `new` and evaluate inside $[-1, 1]$ through `value_at`.
Different input information prevents interpreting this as an equal-coefficient-count competition.

```rust
//| id: interp-floater-hormann-pade
//| ref: interp-floater-hormann-pade
//| title: "Compare sample interpolation and series approximation"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   position: {"type":"range","label":"Evaluation x [-]","min":-1,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.5}
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Floater–Hormann degree d","min":0,"max":4,"step":1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"berrut","label":"d = 0","description":"Berrut has large errors, exposing degree-zero blending limits.","inputs":{"degree":0}}
//|   - {"id":"near-polynomial","label":"d = 3","description":"At five points, d = 3 blending matches global polynomial interpolation.","inputs":{"degree":3}}
//|   - {"id":"endpoint","label":"x = 1","description":"Endpoints are samples, so Floater–Hormann errors vanish while Padé errors maximize.","inputs":{"position":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":4.649480459325783e-3,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.6486486486486487,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":7.262205147950951e-5,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":3.996114173331122e-3,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"pade","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"pade","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"pade","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: berrut
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.339452127644821,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: near-polynomial
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.6491947871863581,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.7995764959852778e-3,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: endpoint
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"values","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":3.996114173331122e-3,"rtol":1e-9}}

use scioxide_interp::{FloaterHormannInterpolator, PadeApproximant, RationalDomain, Univariate};

let fh = FloaterHormannInterpolator::new(nodes, values, degree as usize)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    &[1.0_f64, 1.0, 0.5, 1.0 / 6.0, 1.0 / 24.0],
    2,
    2,
    0.0,
    domain,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let second = pade.value_at(position).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check: substitute the closed-form (2, 2) Padé by hand

    pade_maximum = pade_maximum.max(errors[1]);

    for (curve, error) in [&mut fh_curve, &mut pade_curve].into_iter().zip(errors) {

            curve.push([x, error]);
        } else {

    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum error on [−1, 1] [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);

    ("Padé (2, 2)", second, (second - exact).abs(), pade_maximum),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(

    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "values" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([

    { "key": "1", "label": "Denominator numerical rank", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Coefficient residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Points excluded from logarithmic axes", "type": "integer" }
]);
let check = (
    (second - closed).abs(),
    pade.denominator_rank(),
    pade.coefficient_residual(),
    omitted,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![check],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "pade" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({"name": format!("Floater–Hormann（d = {degree}）"), "points": fh_curve}),
        serde_json::json!({"name": "Padé (2, 2)", "points": pade_curve}),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Position x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute e^x error [-]",

    })

```

At defaults $x = 0.5$ and $d = 2$, errors are $4.65 \times 10^{-3}$ Floater–Hormann and $7.26 \times 10^{-5}$ Padé.
The Padé/closed-form difference is $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$, the denominator's numerical rank is 2, and the coefficient residual is $2.1 \times 10^{-17}$, confirming agreement of the constructed approximation with the closed form.
On $[-1, 1]$, the maximum Floater–Hormann error is $1.53 \times 10^{-2}$ at $x = 0.78$ between nodes 0.4 and 1; the maximum Padé error is $4.00 \times 10^{-3}$ at endpoint $x = 1$.
Floater–Hormann errors vanish at sample nodes and peak between them; Padé errors vanish at center $x = 0$ and grow farther from the center.
Their different inputs explain these error shapes.
At input $x = 1$, a sample, Floater–Hormann errors vanish and Padé reaches maximum $4.00 \times 10^{-3}$.

Increasing $d$ from 0 through 4 reduces Floater–Hormann maxima through 0.339, $3.01 \times 10^{-2}$, $1.53 \times 10^{-2}$, $1.80 \times 10^{-3}$, and $1.80 \times 10^{-3}$.
Agreement of $d = 3$ and $d = 4$ within rounding is not accidental.
For five points with $d = 3$, two local cubic weights have ratio $\lambda_0 : \lambda_1 = (x - x_4) : -(x - x_0)$; blend $[(x - x_4) p_0 - (x - x_0) p_1]/(x_0 - x_4)$ is exactly Neville recurrence, hence global $d = 4$.
Generally, Floater–Hormann with $N$ points and $d = N - 2$ equals global polynomial interpolation.

## Summary

Floater–Hormann blends consecutive $d+1$-point polynomials into interpolation without real poles for any $d$.
For $d \ge 1$, errors decrease as $O(h^{d+1})$ without global-polynomial uniform-node divergence, making it a first choice for accurate uniform-sample interpolation.
Uniform Lebesgue constants grow exponentially in $d$; start $d$ small and vary degrees to check stability.
Padé depends only on central Taylor coefficients, with high nearby accuracy but no far-field guarantee.
For `PadeApproximant`, inspect `coefficient_residual` and `denominator_rank` for matching residuals and denominator rank.
Neither supports derivatives or integrals; use piecewise polynomials or splines for them.
