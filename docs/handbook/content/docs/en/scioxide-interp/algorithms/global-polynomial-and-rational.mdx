Global interpolation passes one function through every sample.
The degree-at-most-$n$ polynomial through $n+1$ distinct nodes is unique, but representation changes preparation, update, and evaluation complexity and rounding.
This page covers construction and selection of scioxide-interp global interpolants.
[Polynomial interpolation, divided differences, and Chebyshev nodes](../../../scioxide-poly/algorithms/interpolation-and-chebyshev/) derives barycentric formulas, proves remainder formulas, and discusses Lebesgue constants and Runge behavior; their results are used here.

## Global interpolation and node selection

From nodes $x_0 < x_1 < \dots < x_n$ and values $y_i = f(x_i)$, construct degree-at-most-$n$ polynomial $p_n$ satisfying $p_n(x_i) = y_i$.
For $f$ continuously differentiable $n+1$ times, the error is the following remainder.

$$
f(x) - p_n(x) = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}\,\omega(x),
\qquad \omega(x) = \prod_{j=0}^{n}(x - x_j)
$$


$\xi$ lies inside the smallest interval containing $x$ and all nodes and depends on $x$.
Error magnitude is the product of high derivatives of $f$ and node polynomial $\omega$ determined solely by geometry.

Perturbing samples by at most $\eta$ changes interpolants by at most $\Lambda_n \eta$ over the interval.
$\Lambda_n$ is the Lebesgue constant, measuring sample-error amplification.
It grows exponentially in $n$ at uniform nodes and logarithmically at Chebyshev nodes: $\Lambda_n$.
Choose node families before increasing degree.

### Constructing barycentric interpolants

`BarycentricInterpolator::new(x, y)` constructs an interpolant using the second barycentric formula:

$$
p_n(x) = \frac{\sum_{j=0}^{n} \dfrac{w_j}{x - x_j}\, y_j}{\sum_{j=0}^{n} \dfrac{w_j}{x - x_j}},
\qquad w_j = \frac{1}{\prod_{k \ne j}(x_j - x_k)}
$$


Nodes `x` must increase strictly; callers sort them.
To reject near-duplicates too, pass separation with `SampleOptions` to `with_options(x, y, options)`.
General weights $w_j$ take $O(n^2)$ to compute and are stored after division by a common factor.
That factor cancels between numerator and denominator, preserving values while avoiding overflow.

For known families, constructors generate closed-form nodes and weights in $O(n)$ from values and endpoints `lower` and `upper`.

| Constructor | Nodes, at least two | Endpoints | Weights up to common factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| `from_equispaced(values, lower, upper)` | Uniform | Included | $(-1)^j \binom{n}{j}$ |
| `from_chebyshev_roots(values, lower, upper)` | Zeros of Chebyshev $T_{n+1}$ | Excluded | $(-1)^j \sin\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2n+2}$ |
| `from_chebyshev_lobatto(values, lower, upper)` | Extrema of $T_n$, Chebyshev–Lobatto nodes | Included | $(-1)^j \delta_j$, with $\delta_j = 1/2$ at ends and 1 inside |

These weights correspond to $\cos$ ordering on $[-1, 1]$ (Berrut–Trefethen 2004); ascending reordering changes only a common sign.
Affine mapping to $[\mathrm{lower}, \mathrm{upper}]$ likewise multiplies weights by a common factor without changing values.

Generated nodes ascend; arrange values accordingly.
A single value uses the interval midpoint for every family.
Empty values, `lower > upper`, or degenerate one-point intervals with at least two nodes, preventing strict increase, give `InvalidInput`.
Nonfinite ends give `NonFinite`; unrepresentable generated nodes or normalized weights, including underflow to zero, give `NumericalInstability`.
Uniform constructors accelerate weights but do not cure high-degree uniform-node ill-conditioning.

Evaluation domains span minimum to maximum nodes.
Chebyshev-zero families exclude endpoints; evaluation there requires explicit extrapolation such as `MethodDefined`.
In `SampleOptions`, separation `NodeSeparation` defaults to `Exact` rejecting equal adjacent nodes; `Tolerant(tol)` also rejects gaps at most $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\max(|x_i|,|x_{i+1}|)$.
Queries matching nodes under the same separation return their exact samples.
Near-node rejection avoids ill-conditioned inputs but guarantees no accuracy for accepted inputs.

Supply same-shaped `AffineValue` values: real, complex, arrays, or vectors.
Components are stored per node and evaluated by identical real kernels, matching individual scalar interpolation.
One point costs $O(nd)$ for $d$ components without allocation.
Evaluation references the nearest node's term and first subtracts its value, retaining accuracy nearby.
Compensated sums are used; term overflow or underflow triggers recomputation of the full sum with extended exponent range.
`evaluate_with_derivative` returns values and first derivatives in one scan; `evaluate_with_derivative_into` writes the same to caller outputs/workspace.
`Differentiable::gradient` and `derivative_at` return derivatives alone.
Outside-domain `Clamp` gives zero derivative on that axis.
Global polynomials do not implement per-call integral `Integrable`.
Prepare a primitive once with `prepare_antiderivative()`, costing $O(n^2 d)$ work and $O(nd)$ storage, then repeat queries in $O(nd)$.

## Newton divided differences and sequential nodes

Newton form suits samples added as measurements or simulations proceed.

$$
p_n(x) = c_0 + c_1 (x - x_0) + c_2 (x - x_0)(x - x_1) + \dots + c_n \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} (x - x_j)
$$

Coefficients $c_k$ equal divided differences $f[x_0, \dots, x_k]$ defined by this recurrence.

$$
f[x_i] = y_i, \quad f[x_i, \dots, x_{i+k}] = \frac{f[x_{i+1}, \dots, x_{i+k}] - f[x_i, \dots, x_{i+k-1}]}{x_{i+k} - x_i}
$$

The mean-value property $f[x_0,\dots,x_k] = f^{(k)}(\eta)/k!$, with $\eta$ in the smallest interval containing all the nodes, lets coefficient scales indicate high derivative magnitudes.
`NewtonInterpolator::new(x, y)` constructs a divided-difference table from strictly increasing nodes in $O(n^2 d)$; `coefficients()` returns divided differences with components grouped by node.
Horner evaluation of nested products costs $O(nd)$, omitting derivatives for value-only queries.
Intermediate overflow or underflow switches to extended exponents.

Appending node $x_{n+1}$ leaves existing $c_0, \dots, c_n$ unchanged.
Obtain new $c_{n+1} = f[x_0, \dots, x_{n+1}]$ from initial $v = y_{n+1}$ by repeating $v \leftarrow (v - c_k)/(x_{n+1} - x_k)$ in order $k = 0, \dots, n$.
`NewtonInterpolator::insert_point(x, y)` returns a new right-appended model without changing the original.
New nodes must exceed and be separated from the last under construction separation `NodeSeparation` in `SampleOptions`.
Both computing coefficients and copying existing state cost $O(nd)$ work and storage.
The supplied value is consumed as a new shape template, requiring no `Clone` on value types.
Every failure, including allocation, returns `Result`.
Left or interior insertion is not provided; reconstruct with `new`.

## Hermite osculatory interpolation

Some samples include both values $y_i = f(x_i)$ and first derivatives $y_i' = f'(x_i)$, such as orbital positions and velocities.
Hermite interpolation imposes at all $n+1$ nodes

$$
p(x_i) = y_i, \quad p'(x_i) = y_i' \quad (i = 0, \dots, n)
$$

these $2n + 2$ conditions on a polynomial through degree $2n + 1$.
Conditions and coefficients have equal counts.
A degree-at-most-$2n+1$ polynomial with homogeneous $p(x_i) = p'(x_i) = 0$ has $n+1$ double zeros, $2n+2$ in total, so it must vanish; existence and uniqueness follow.

Apply the divided-difference recurrence to repeated nodes $z = (x_0, x_0, x_1, x_1, \dots)$; first-order divided differences of equal nodes have form $0/0$.
Its limit is $f[x_i, x_i] = \lim_{h \to 0} f[x_i, x_i + h] = f'(x_i)$; insert derivative sample $y_i'$ and fill the rest normally.
These are confluent divided differences.
For $f$ continuously differentiable $2n+2$ times, the error follows.

$$
f(x) - p(x) = \frac{f^{(2n+2)}(\xi)}{(2n+2)!} \prod_{i=0}^{n} (x - x_i)^2
$$

The squared node polynomial makes both error and its slope vanish at nodes.

[`HermiteInterpolator::new(x, y, dy)`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.HermiteInterpolator.html#method.new) constructs these doubled-node divided differences in $O(n^2 d)$ and evaluates the Newton form in $O(nd)$.
`dy` matches `y`'s shape; supply coordinate derivatives $dy/dx$ in physical units.
For temperature and heat flux, convert Fourier $q = -k\,dT/dx$ to $dT/dx = -q/k$ first.
Only values and first derivatives are accepted, not higher-derivative samples.
Implements `Differentiable`; prepare integrals through `prepare_antiderivative()`.

### Derivative samples and added points

The next cell interpolates $f(x) = \sin x$ at $n$ uniform nodes on $[0, \pi]$, comparing three interpolants.
Here node count $n$ corresponds to $n+1$ above.
Hermite therefore satisfies $2n$ conditions at degree at most $2n-1$.
First is value-only $n$-point interpolation, built from one-point `NewtonInterpolator` by repeated right-appending `insert_point`.
Second is `HermiteInterpolator` using values and derivatives $\cos x_i$.
Third supplies the same $2n$ conditions with values alone at $2n$ uniform nodes through `from_equispaced`.
Input $n$ controls nodes; outputs give maximum errors over 401 points on $[0, \pi]$ and remainder bounds, insertion checks, and error curves.
All derivatives of $\sin x$ have magnitude at most 1, bounding each error by maximum node-polynomial magnitude divided by a factorial.
Check divided differences of the model extended by `insert_point` against those constructed all at once by `new`, and compare Hermite nodal derivatives with $\cos x_i$.
Compute samples and references with `RealScalar::sin` and `RealScalar::cos`.

```rust
//| id: interp-hermite-newton
//| ref: interp-hermite-newton
//| title: "Check Hermite derivative samples and added nodes"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   node_count: {"type":"range","label":"Nodes n","description":"Uniform nodes on [0, π]; Hermite uses 2n conditions.","min":2,"max":7,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"two-nodes","label":"n = 2","description":"Cubic Hermite uses only endpoint values and slopes.","inputs":{"node_count":2}}
//|   - {"id":"seven-nodes","label":"n = 7","description":"Added nodes rapidly reduce Hermite and 2n-node errors.","inputs":{"node_count":7}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":5.600950026045326e-2,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":2.787065555906243e-3,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":3.090732589464992e-3,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":1.3129573681472662e-3,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: two-nodes
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.21460183660255172,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":4}
//|   - case: seven-nodes
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":1.1774470287662098e-11,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":1.705045826749796e-11,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-15}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{
    BarycentricInterpolator, HermiteInterpolator, NewtonInterpolator, Univariate,
};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let n = node_count as usize;
let grid = |count: usize| -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..count)
        .map(|i| PI * i as f64 / (count - 1) as f64)
        .collect()
};
let x = grid(n);
let y: Vec<f64> = x.iter().map(|&v| RealScalar::sin(v)).collect();
let dy: Vec<f64> = x.iter().map(|&v| RealScalar::cos(v)).collect();
// 1. Build n-node values from a one-node model, appending rightward
let mut grown = NewtonInterpolator::new(vec![x[0]], vec![y[0]]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
for i in 1..n {
    grown = grown.insert_point(x[i], y[i]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
// 2. Degree-2n − 1 Hermite satisfying 2n value/derivative conditions
let hermite =
    HermiteInterpolator::new(x.clone(), y.clone(), dy.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// 3. Uniform 2n-value interpolation supplying the same number of conditions
let wide: Vec<f64> = grid(2 * n).iter().map(|&v| RealScalar::sin(v)).collect();
let lagrange =
    BarycentricInterpolator::from_equispaced(wide, 0.0, PI).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check: node polynomial ω and bound max|ω|/k! from |f^(k)| ≤ 1
let omega = |nodes: &[f64], t: f64| nodes.iter().map(|&v| t - v).product::<f64>();
let factorial = |k: usize| (1..=k).map(|i| i as f64).product::<f64>();
let wide_nodes = grid(2 * n);
let mut maxima = [0.0_f64; 6];
let mut curves = [Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
for i in 0..=400 {
    let t = PI * i as f64 / 400.0;
    let exact = RealScalar::sin(t);
    let errors = [
        (grown.value_at(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - exact).abs(),
        (hermite.value_at(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - exact).abs(),
        (lagrange.value_at(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - exact).abs(),
    ];
    let w = omega(&x, t).abs();
    let products = [w, w * w, omega(&wide_nodes, t).abs()];
    for k in 0..3 {
        maxima[k] = maxima[k].max(errors[k]);
        maxima[k + 3] = maxima[k + 3].max(products[k]);
        // Nodal zero errors cannot be plotted logarithmically; plot positive values only
        if errors[k] > 0.0 {
            curves[k].push([t, errors[k]]);
        }
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Conditions", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Remainder bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
let rows = vec![
    (
        "n values, Newton",
        n,
        maxima[0],
        maxima[3] / factorial(n),
    ),
    (
        "n values and derivatives, Hermite",
        2 * n,
        maxima[1],
        maxima[4] / factorial(2 * n),
    ),
    (
        "2n values, uniform",
        2 * n,
        maxima[2],
        maxima[5] / factorial(2 * n),
    ),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);

// Independent check: insert_point versus new differences, Hermite slopes versus cos x_i
let direct = NewtonInterpolator::new(x.clone(), y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut coefficient_gap = 0.0_f64;
for (a, b) in grown.coefficients().iter().zip(direct.coefficients()) {
    coefficient_gap = coefficient_gap.max((a - b).abs());
}
let mut slope_gap = 0.0_f64;
for (&node, &slope) in x.iter().zip(&dy) {
    let derivative = hermite.derivative_at(node).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    slope_gap = slope_gap.max((derivative - slope).abs());
}
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum divided-difference mismatch [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum nodal-slope mismatch [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![(coefficient_gap, slope_gap)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
let [newton_curve, hermite_curve, lagrange_curve] = curves;
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "n values only", "points": newton_curve }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Hermite, 2n conditions", "points": hermite_curve }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "2n values only", "points": lagrange_curve }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Position x [rad]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute error against sin x [-]",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
```

At default $n = 3$ (nodes $0, \pi/2, \pi$), maximum errors are $5.60 \times 10^{-2}$ for three values, $2.79 \times 10^{-3}$ Hermite, and $1.31 \times 10^{-3}$ for six uniform values, all inside remainder bounds.
Hermite bound $3.09 \times 10^{-3}$ is close to actual error.
Hermite and six-point fits use equal conditions and comparable error scales but differently shaped curves.
Hermite errors are small near nodes, $2.9 \times 10^{-4}$ at $x = \pi/2 - 0.2$, and larger between them, $2.7 \times 10^{-3}$ at $x = \pi/4$.
Its node polynomial $\prod (x - x_i)^2$ decreases quadratically near nodes.
Six uniform conditions spread over the interval, giving small $3.4 \times 10^{-4}$ at $x = \pi/4$ but maxima near endpoints.
Divided differences of the model extended by `insert_point` match those from `new` within $5.6 \times 10^{-17}$; Hermite nodal slopes match $\cos x_i$ within $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$.

At $n = 2$, cubic Hermite interpolation uses only endpoint values and slopes, with maximum error $0.215$ at $x = \pi/2$.
This is one piece of piecewise cubic Hermite ([PCHIP, cubic splines, and Akima](../pchip-and-splines/)).
Two-value interpolation is line $p = 0$ with error 1 at $x = \pi/2$.
At $n = 7$, Hermite errors fall to $1.18 \times 10^{-11}$ and fourteen-point to $1.71 \times 10^{-11}$.
Uniform fourteen-point endpoint errors grow, giving Hermite a smaller maximum.

## Clustered nodes and barycentric weights

The next cell quadratically interpolates $f(x) = e^x$ through three nodes $\{0, \delta, 1\}$ with `BarycentricInterpolator`, varying $\delta$ from 0.001 to 0.5.
Dimensionless spacing $\delta$ controls outputs: maximum errors over 241 points on $[0,1]$, lower/upper remainder bounds, maximum/minimum absolute weight ratio, and error curves.
Weights are $w_0 = 1/\delta$, $w_1 = -1/(\delta(1-\delta))$, and $w_2 = 1/(1-\delta)$, giving exact ratio $1/\delta$.
There are two independent checks.
A hand-built Newton form from the same three divided differences measures evaluation-rounding differences.
Remainders use $f''' = e^x$ satisfying $1 \le f'''(\xi) \le e$ over $[0,1]$, bounding each error between $|\omega(x)|/6$ and $e\,|\omega(x)|/6$.
Samples and references use `RealScalar::exp`.

```rust
//| id: interp-close-nodes
//| ref: interp-close-nodes
//| title: "Vary close-node spacing and global errors"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   node_spacing: {"type":"range","label":"Close-node spacing δ [-]","description":"Change distance between 0 and δ.","min":0.001,"max":0.5,"step":0.001,"value":0.1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"nearly-coincident","label":"δ = 0.001","description":"Weight ratios reach 1000 and representation differences grow.","inputs":{"node_spacing":0.001}}
//|   - {"id":"equispaced","label":"δ = 0.5","description":"Uniform three-node ratios are 2.","inputs":{"node_spacing":0.5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":3.3510954653589664e-2,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.103780864197531e-2,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":5.7186692942080154e-2,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":10.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: nearly-coincident
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":3.821601832241606e-2,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1000.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: equispaced
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1.4420665911631936e-2,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":2.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{BarycentricInterpolator, Univariate};

let nodes = vec![0.0_f64, node_spacing, 1.0];
let values: Vec<f64> = nodes.iter().map(|&x| RealScalar::exp(x)).collect();
let interp =
    BarycentricInterpolator::new(nodes.clone(), values.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check 1: hand-built Newton quadratic divided differences through the same points
let d1 = (values[1] - values[0]) / (nodes[1] - nodes[0]);
let d2 = ((values[2] - values[1]) / (nodes[2] - nodes[1]) - d1) / (nodes[2] - nodes[0]);
let newton = |x: f64| values[0] + d1 * (x - nodes[0]) + d2 * (x - nodes[0]) * (x - nodes[1]);
// Independent check 2: remainder f'''(ξ) ω(x) / 3! and 1 ≤ f'''(ξ) ≤ e
let omega = |x: f64| (x - nodes[0]) * (x - nodes[1]) * (x - nodes[2]);
let e = RealScalar::exp(1.0_f64);

let (mut error_curve, mut lower_curve, mut upper_curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut maximum_error, mut maximum_omega, mut form_gap) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
let mut omitted = 0_usize;
for i in 0..=240 {
    let x = i as f64 / 240.0;
    let p = interp.value_at(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let error = (p - RealScalar::exp(x)).abs();
    let w = omega(x).abs();
    maximum_error = maximum_error.max(error);
    maximum_omega = maximum_omega.max(w);
    form_gap = form_gap.max((p - newton(x)).abs());
    // Count and exclude nodal zero errors and ω from logarithmic axes
    if error > 0.0 && w > 0.0 {
        error_curve.push([x, error]);
        lower_curve.push([x, w / 6.0]);
        upper_curve.push([x, e * w / 6.0]);
    } else {
        omitted += 1;
    }
}
let magnitudes: Vec<f64> = interp.weights().iter().map(|w| w.abs()).collect();
let largest = magnitudes.iter().copied().fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let smallest = magnitudes.iter().copied().fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum e^x error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Lower max|ω|/6 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Upper e·max|ω|/6 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum Newton-form difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Largest/smallest absolute weights [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Points excluded from logarithmic axes", "type": "integer" }
]);
let row = (
    maximum_error,
    maximum_omega / 6.0,
    e * maximum_omega / 6.0,
    form_gap,
    largest / smallest,
    omitted,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
```

## Neville's recursive algorithm

For values at one $x$ alone, Neville avoids constructing coefficients.
Let $P_{i, j}$ be the value at $x$ of degree-$j - i$ interpolation through subset $\{x_i, \dots, x_j\}$; this recurrence holds.

$$
P_{i, j} = \frac{(x_j - x) P_{i, j-1} + (x - x_i) P_{i+1, j}}{x_j - x_i}, \qquad P_{i,i} = y_i
$$

Right-side polynomials agree at shared $x_{i+1}, \dots, x_{j-1}$ and weights retain one each at $x = x_i$ and $x = x_j$, so the left interpolates all $x_i, \dots, x_j$.
The triangular table of $P_{i,j}$ in $j - i$ order is Neville's table.

Following Stoer–Bulirsch and Numerical Recipes, scioxide-interp updates adjacent-degree differences instead of full table values:

$$
C_{m,i} = P_{i,i+m} - P_{i,i+m-1}, \qquad D_{m,i} = P_{i,i+m} - P_{i+1,i+m}
$$


With $w = C_{m-1,i+1} - D_{m-1,i}$, $C_{m,i} = (x_i - x)\,w/(x_i - x_{i+m})$ and $D_{m,i} = (x_{i+m} - x)\,w/(x_i - x_{i+m})$.
Starting from a sample, add one $C$ or $D$ each degree to follow one table path to the all-node value.
$C$ extends right; $D$ extends left.

Start at the nearest node.
Binary-search ascending nodes for bracketing neighbors and choose nearer, left on ties.
Outside queries start at the nearer endpoint.
Only those two distances are compared, correctly ordering even extreme coordinates with one overflowed distance.
Choose paths through the table center.
At each degree, if unused nodes left are fewer than right, add $C$ and extend right; otherwise add $D$ and extend left.
Exact arithmetic is path-independent, but nearby starts and central paths keep corrections and rounding small.

`NevilleInterpolator::new(nodes, values)` stores checked samples only, rebuilding tables per query.
Each point costs $O(n^2 d)$ and uses two $O(n)$ workspace tables.
Supplied-workspace `evaluate_into` and `evaluate_with_error_into` allocate nothing.
Queries matching nodes under `NodeSeparation` return samples directly without tables.
Derivatives/integrals are unavailable; use barycentric or Newton when needed.
For many fixed-sample queries, pointwise $O(nd)$ `BarycentricInterpolator` is faster.

## Neville estimates and rational extrapolation

`evaluate_with_error(point, policy)` returns interpolated values paired with absolute final corrections per component.
This form allocates outputs and workspace per call.
`evaluate_with_error_into(point, policy, value_out, error_out, workspace)` writes both to caller outputs without allocation.
The final correction subtracts interpolation on $n$ nodes omitting one endpoint from the full $n+1$-node value.
The omitted endpoint is the side added last by the path.
This estimates degree-$n-1$ error; using it for full-degree error assumes rapid reduction as degree rises.
It is therefore no upper bound.
Nodes and single samples return zero corrections.
Extrapolation or nearby singularities may greatly underestimate actual errors, as the next cell shows.

[`BulirschStoerInterpolator`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.BulirschStoerInterpolator.html) uses the same table shape, start, and path for diagonal rational rather than polynomial interpolation.
From $N$ samples, numerator and denominator differ in degree by at most one: numerator $\lfloor (N-1)/2 \rfloor$ and denominator $\lceil (N-1)/2 \rceil$.
Sample count determines degrees, so constructors take no numerator/denominator degrees.
Stoer–Bulirsch rational updates use $r = (x - x_i)/(x - x_{i+m})$ and $t = r\,D_{m-1,i}$ as follows.

$$
\phi = \frac{C_{m-1,i+1} - D_{m-1,i}}{t - C_{m-1,i+1}}, \qquad
D_{m,i} = C_{m-1,i+1}\,\phi, \qquad C_{m,i} = t\,\phi
$$


Denominator zeros represent poles, sometimes approximating pole/asymptote behavior with fewer samples than polynomials.
Bulirsch and Stoer used this rational interpolation to extrapolate a sequence of numerical solutions at step sizes $h$ to $h \to 0$.
For the same use, outside-node $h = 0$ requires explicit `MethodDefined` or `BoundedContinue`.

Updates are nonlinear in samples, so each real component has its own table.
Point evaluation costs $O(n^2 d)$ without allocation with supplied workspace.
No derivatives or integrals are provided.
Zero update denominator $t - C_{m-1,i+1}$, at poles or nonexistent rational interpolants of that degree, gives `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).
Default `Exact` recognizes exact zero; `Tolerant` also uses tolerance scaled by the larger magnitude of the two subtracted quantities.
When both inputs $C_{m-1,i+1}$ and $D_{m-1,i}$ vanish for locally constant samples, set new corrections to zero to avoid $0/0$.
For example, type-$(1,1)$ rational $(a + bx)/(1 + cx)$ through $(0,1), (1,-1), (2,1)$ has conditions $x = 0$ and $x = 2$ forcing $a = 1$ and $b = c$, making $r$ constant 1.
Even $b = c = -1$ degenerates to $(1-x)/(1-x)$, unable to take $-1$ at $x = 1$.
This rational interpolant does not exist; evaluation at $x = 0.5$ encounters a zero update denominator at the degree-one stage and gives this error.
Rationals do not make extrapolation inherently safe; interior poles destroy continuity too.
For estimating poles and residues, use [rational approximation](../rational-approximation/), `aaa`, and diagnostics.

## Summary

Choose families before degrees; for Chebyshev nodes construct weights with `from_chebyshev_roots` or `from_chebyshev_lobatto` in $O(n)$.
Sort general nodes strictly for `BarycentricInterpolator::new`; read values/derivatives together with `evaluate_with_derivative`, or allocation-free `evaluate_with_derivative_into`; prepare repeated integrals with `prepare_antiderivative`.
Choose `NewtonInterpolator::insert_point` for right-appended samples and `HermiteInterpolator` for derivative samples, placing Hermite nodes with quadratic near-node error decay in mind.
Neville estimates are not bounds and may greatly understate errors near singularities or in extrapolation; pair them with independent-point checks.
`BulirschStoerInterpolator` supports near-pole interpolation and step extrapolation, but pole queries give `Breakdown`.
