Interpolation imposes $s(x_i) = y_i$ on samples $(x_i, y_i)$.
Approximation and smoothing choose $s$ by balancing residuals and complexity without requiring exact sample agreement.
In either case, sample residuals alone do not determine true errors between samples.
Each page states assumptions and explains mathematical derivations separately from API contracts.

## Notation and input forms

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $x$, $\boldsymbol{x}$ | Real coordinates, univariate or multivariate |
| $y_i$, $f_i$ | Sample values: real, complex, arrays, or vectors |
| $n$ | Sample, node, or control-value count, defined per page |
| $p$ | Polynomial or spline degree |
| $D$ | Coordinate dimension |
| $d$ | Real value components, 2 for complex values; blending degree in Floater–Hormann |
| $h$ | Maximum node spacing |

Coordinates are always real.
Complex values have two components, real and imaginary, without increasing coordinate dimension.
Rational functions in the complex plane are an exception, also offering queries with two coordinates `[real part, imaginary part]`.
Each method fixes value-component order, tensor-axis order, and grid-sample layout in input contracts.

Distinctness, ordering, finiteness, weight signs, and numerical rank are separate conditions.
`NodeSeparation` governs node separation; tolerance-based duplicate tests are distinct from approximation errors.
For univariate and grid methods, put this policy in `SampleOptions` and pass it to options form `with_options`.
Scattered triangulations merging duplicate points also require correspondence between merged points and values.

## Construction forms

Construct interpolants from samples using these forms.

- Standard `new(data)` takes samples only, fixing settings to documented rustdoc defaults.
  For example, `CubicSpline::new` uses not-a-knot ends and `AkimaInterpolator::new` original Akima weights.
- Options form `with_options(data, options)` receives configuration by value.
  Configurations implement `Default`; univariate and grid methods use `SampleOptions` containing only node separation.
- Execution-scope `build(data, options, scope)` accounts for construction within the caller's solve.
  Scope is top-level `ExecutionContext` or outer `&mut SolveContext`; the latter charges work against outer limits.
  `RbfInterpolator`, `GaussianProcessModel`, and `LocalRbfModel` count one proposal; `LocalPolynomialModel` one per tried neighborhood and degree; `PadeApproximant` one iteration; `CloughTocherInterpolator` one proposal plus one iteration per sweep.
  `PenalizedSplinePlan` and `SphericalSplinePlan` factor in child scopes.
  Iterative fitting uses `aaa_into`, `fit_parametric_spline_scoped`, and `fit_spherical_spline_scoped` for the same role.
- Named constructors choose different preparation kinds.
  Examples are `CubicSpline::with_boundary`, `AkimaInterpolator::with_method`, `BicubicInterpolator::monotone`, `BarycentricInterpolator::from_chebyshev_lobatto`, and `BSpline::linear`.
- One-shot functions combine preparation and results.
  `BSpline::interpolate` interpolates tensor samples with equal odd degree $p$ not-a-knot bases on each axis ($p = 3$ gives not-a-knot cubics); `interpolate_scattered` builds scattered Delaunay triangulation and evaluates query sets simplex-linearly.

Model-defining quantities without neutral defaults—RBF kernels, Floater–Hormann blending degree, Hermite derivative samples—are arguments alongside samples rather than settings.
Local-model settings are exceptions: `LocalPolynomialOptions::new` takes degree and neighbors, `LocalRbfOptions::new` neighbors and kernel; neither implements `Default`.
Iterative `aaa`, `fit_parametric_spline`, `fit_spherical_spline`, and `CloughTocherInterpolator::new` return models only on target achievement, otherwise `ConvergenceFailure`.
Their options forms return models with reports `RationalReport`, `AdaptiveSplineReport`, or `CloughTocherReport`, indicating unmet targets through report `termination()`.
Termination types `RationalTermination`, `AdaptiveSplineTermination`, and `CloughTocherTermination` expose target achievement with `is_success()`.

## Domains and extrapolation

Univariate intervals, grid boxes, scattered convex hulls, and spherical angular domains differ.
`RbfInterpolator` uses bounding rectangles rather than convex hulls.
`ExtrapolationPolicy` applies to each method's domain without implicit defaults.
Scalar univariate `value_at`, `values_at`, `derivative_at`, and `integrate` (trait `Univariate`) accept no policy and reject outside points like `Reject`.
Derivatives under `Clamp` differentiate composition with projection, not simple extension of boundary formulas.
Even `BoundedContinue` and `MethodDefined` can error for unsupported extrapolation or numerical breakdown.
Natural neighbors accept only `Clamp` outside hulls; spherical splines do not extend colatitude beyond $[0, \pi]$.
See [Extrapolation and coordinate reports](oxref:interp-extrapolation-report) for policies and [Interpolant derivatives and integrals](./derivatives-integrals-and-workspaces/) for calculus.

## Errors and computational costs

Interpolation errors, rounding errors, estimated conditions, and statistical predictive variances are different quantities.
Theoretical error formulas with unknown high derivatives alone do not give computed a posteriori bounds.
Interpret iterative termination as a test of sample residuals or gradient changes.
Compare univariate continuity and orders in [Properties of one-dimensional methods](oxref:interp-choosing-one-dimensional).

Separate construction, fitting, and query costs.
Plans retaining factorizations speed recomputation at fixed sample positions but require retained memory.
Also distinguish preallocated-workspace queries from convenient owned-output forms.
Shared principles appear in [Workspaces and reproducibility](../../concepts/workspaces-and-reproducibility/).

## Summary

Distinguish sample agreement from proximity to unknown functions; sample residuals do not bound intersample errors.
Choose domain (interval, box, rectangle, hull, sphere), value shape, and needed derivatives before the method; pass extrapolation policies per call.
For iterative fitting, inspect options-form report `termination()` and `is_success()` to check targets.
Compare preparation and repeated-query complexity separately; use `build` or `*_scoped` when solve accounting is needed.
