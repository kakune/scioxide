Dense global models of many scattered points require quadratic storage and cubic preparation.
Local models solve small problems using only neighbors around selected centers.
Kriging, or Gaussian-process prediction, instead treats values as random-field realizations and computes means and variances from fixed covariance.
This page covers local polynomial/RBF preparation, fixed-covariance GP prediction, and variance interpretation.
Distinguish approximation error from uncertainty conditional on assumed probabilistic models.

## Samples and fixed neighborhoods

`LocalSamples::new(&points, &values)` copies and owns $D$-dimensional points and real affine-value components.
`points()` returns borrowed point `PointSet` ($n\times D$); `values()` borrowed `[sample, real component]` values ($n\times m$).
Complex values are ordered real and imaginary components.
Empty points give `InvalidInput`; point/value count or shape mismatch `DimensionMismatch`; nonfinite coordinates/values `NonFinite`.
`LocalModelPlan::new(&points, values)` borrows these and builds KD indexing once in $O(Dn\log n)$ work.

`LocalPolynomialModel::new(&plan, center, options)` and `LocalRbfModel::new(&plan, center, options)` select Euclidean-nearest samples around `center` for preparation.
Distance ties favor smaller indices.
Prepared models fix neighbors without reselection per query.
Queries far from centers do not switch neighborhoods.
For moving least squares or querywise reselection, callers construct a model per center.
Models from separate centers generally have discontinuous joins in values or derivatives.
Extrapolation domains are axis-aligned neighbor bounding boxes.
Local models implement only `Interpolant`, without derivative traits.
Owned `evaluate` returns `Vec` with `LocalModelPlan::components` components.

Settings `LocalPolynomialOptions` and `LocalRbfOptions` take model-defining degree/neighbors or neighbors/kernel as `new` arguments and have no `Default`.
This avoids hiding quantities without neutral defaults.

### Local polynomials

`LocalPolynomialOptions::new(degree, minimum_neighbors, maximum_neighbors)` specifies initial total degree and neighbor-count range.
`with_minimum_degree` sets minimum degree, default 0; `with_numerical` numerical policy; preparation validates first.
`LocalPolynomialModel` centers coordinates and divides by farthest-neighbor distance $\rho$: then performs unweighted least squares with monomials in $\boldsymbol\xi=(\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol x_c)/\rho$.
Centering/scaling makes conditioning independent of coordinate units or origin.
Dimension $D$ and total degree $p$ require $\binom{D+p}{p}$ coefficients.
Enough neighbors alone is insufficient; their geometric rank must determine the polynomial.
For example, collinear points cannot determine even planar affine polynomials.

Preparation doubles neighbors from `minimum_neighbors` through `maximum_neighbors` at requested degree.
Each trial checks numerical rank and reciprocal condition with column-pivoted QR, retrying SVD if rejected.
If maximum neighbors fail, reduce degree and repeat down to `with_minimum_degree`.
No acceptable combination gives `Singular`.
`minimum_degree > degree`, invalid ranges, neighbors exceeding samples, or terms exceeding `max_system_order` give `InvalidInput` before work.
Neighbor/degree adaptation selects solvable systems rather than minimizing unknown-function error.

### Local RBFs

`LocalRbfOptions::new(neighbors, kernel)` fixes neighbor count and [kernel](oxref:interp-rbf-kernels).
Kernel-shape checks make `new` return `Result`, with nonpositive `InvalidInput` and nonfinite `NonFinite`.
`LocalRbfModel` solves once, on selected $k$ neighbors, this polynomial-augmented saddle system:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}K+\lambda I&P\\P^\mathsf T&0\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol w\\\boldsymbol a\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol y\\\boldsymbol0\end{bmatrix}
$$


$K_{ij}=\phi(\|\boldsymbol x_i-\boldsymbol x_j\|)$ is the kernel matrix, $P$ sampled polynomial bases, $\boldsymbol a$ polynomial coefficients, and $\lambda$ regularization.
Polynomial terms depend on kernels: affine for thin plates, constants for multiquadrics, none for positive-definite Gaussian/inverse multiquadrics.
Thin-plate local RBFs reproduce affine functions through these terms.
Global `RbfInterpolator` differs by having no polynomial terms.
Without polynomial terms use Cholesky; otherwise partially pivoted LU; rejected factorizations retry SVD.
With $t$ polynomial terms, preparation costs $O((k+t)^3)$ and per-component queries $O(k+t)$.
`with_regularization(RbfRegularization::Diagonal(λ))` adds diagonal $\lambda$ for smoothing, without automatic jitter.
Unregularized duplicate neighbors, geometry insufficient for polynomials, or poor conditions give `Singular`.

### Numerical policy and reports

`LocalModelOptions` specifies rank threshold default `RankThreshold::default`, minimum reciprocal condition $\sqrt\varepsilon$, dense order limit 1024, and unlimited preparation memory by default.
Also set fallback-SVD iteration limits through `with_factor_iterations`, unlimited by default, and preparation work limits through `with_budgets`, likewise unlimited.
Preparation is one solve, charging one proposal per factored system; proposal limits bound trials.
`LocalFitReport` returns `neighbors()`, `degree()`, `rank()`, `attempts()`, `factorization()` (`FitFactorization`), `used_fallback()`, `condition_norm()` (`FitConditionNorm`), `reciprocal_condition()`, and `residual()`.
`degree()` is total polynomial degree, or `None` without polynomial terms.
`residual()` is maximum solved residual: sample least squares for polynomials, regularization/noise-diagonal systems for RBFs/GPs.
Direct reciprocal conditions use 1-norm estimates and SVD singular ratios use 2-norm; do not compare as identical scales.
Reports describe solved systems, not prediction-error bounds.
`sample_indices()` returns original neighbor indices ordered by distance then index.

## Local polynomial reproduction

The next cell samples quadratic $f(x,y)=2+x^2+3y$ at nine $3\times3$ grid points with coordinates $-1,0,1$ and prepares at the origin.
Inputs are requested degree and query $x$, with $y=0.3$ fixed.
Neighbors range from 6 to 9.
Outputs show the accepted degree, neighbor count, attempt count, least-squares residual, and the difference between the value at the query point and analytic $f$.
Degree at least 2 includes $f$, so errors should be rounding-sized.
Degree 1 cannot express $x^2$; degree 3 has ten coefficients for nine points, triggering reduction.

```rust
//| id: interp-local-polynomial-recovery
//| ref: interp-local-polynomial-recovery
//| title: "Check fixed-neighborhood polynomial degrees and errors"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Requested total degree","min":1,"max":3,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   position: {"type":"range","label":"Query x [-]","min":-0.8,"max":0.8,"step":0.1,"value":0.2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"linear","label":"Degree 1","description":"Missing x² gives residuals and analytic errors around 10⁻¹.","inputs":{"degree":1}}
//|   - {"id":"cubic","label":"Degree 3","description":"Nine points cannot determine ten coefficients; reduction to 2 reproduces f.","inputs":{"degree":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":6}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: linear
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":0.34636363636363665,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: cubic
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_interp::{
    ExtrapolationPolicy, Interpolant, LocalModelPlan, LocalPolynomialModel,
    LocalPolynomialOptions, LocalSamples,
};

// Nine 3 × 3 grid points and f(x, y) = 2 + x² + 3y
let f = |x: f64, y: f64| 2.0 + x * x + 3.0 * y;
let sites: Vec<_> = [-1.0_f64, 0.0, 1.0]
    .iter()
    .flat_map(|&x| [-1.0, 0.0, 1.0].map(|y| [x, y]))
    .collect();
let values: Vec<_> = sites.iter().map(|p| f(p[0], p[1])).collect();
let samples = LocalSamples::new(&sites, &values).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let points = samples.points().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plan = LocalModelPlan::new(&points, samples.values().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Double neighbors from 6 to 9; reduce degree on failure
let options = LocalPolynomialOptions::new(degree as usize, 6, 9);
let model = LocalPolynomialModel::new(&plan, [0.0, 0.0], options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let value = model
    .evaluate([position, 0.3], ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let report = model.fit_report();
// Reports store accepted degrees
let adopted = report.degree().ok_or("Accepted degree unavailable")?;
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Requested degree", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Accepted degree", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Neighbors", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Trials", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Least-squares residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Value [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Analytic error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        degree as usize,
        adopted,
        report.neighbors(),
        report.attempts(),
        report.residual(),
        value[0],
        (value[0] - f(position, 0.3)).abs()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "fit" })
);
```

Requested 2 accepts the first six neighbors with rank 6.
Residual $8.9\times10^{-16}$ and zero analytic error reproduce quadratics within rounding.
Requested 3 (`cubic`) tries six and nine neighbors, then lowers to 2 and accepts its third trial.
Quadratic $f$ keeps errors rounding-sized.
Requested 1 (`linear`) cannot represent $x^2$, giving residual 0.68 and analytic error 0.35 at $x=0.2$.
Read accepted degrees and neighbors to determine whether requested degrees survived.

## Conditional prediction from fixed covariance

`Covariance::new(kernel, variance, length_scales)` constructs a covariance with a `CovarianceKernel`, positive signal variance $\sigma^2$ (variance rather than standard deviation), and positive per-axis length scales $\ell_a$.
Distance is axis-scaled $r=\sqrt{\sum_a((a_a-b_a)/\ell_a)^2}$; covariance $k(\boldsymbol a,\boldsymbol b)=\sigma^2\rho(r)$.
Every correlation $\rho$ is stationary, positive definite in every dimension, and satisfies $\rho(0)=1$.

| `CovarianceKernel` | $\rho(r)$ | Sample-path smoothness |
|---|---|---|
| `SquaredExponential` | $\exp(-r^2/2)$ | Infinitely differentiable |
| `Matern52` | $(1+\sqrt5r+5r^2/3)\exp(-\sqrt5r)$ | Twice differentiable |
| `Matern32` | $(1+\sqrt3r)\exp(-\sqrt3r)$ | Once differentiable |
| `Matern12` | $\exp(-r)$ | Continuous, nondifferentiable |

Sample paths of a Matérn kernel with smoothness $\nu$ are differentiable $\lceil\nu\rceil-1$ times.
Length scales indicate correlation distances; changing them affects curves, covariance conditioning, and variances.
SciOxide GPs do not estimate covariance parameters.
Callers supply scales and variances and validate choices with held-out data or similar checks.

Assume training values $\boldsymbol y$ at $\boldsymbol x_1,\dots,\boldsymbol x_n$ are GP realizations with mean $m$ and covariance $k$ plus independent Gaussian noise variance $s_i$.
With $K=[k(\boldsymbol x_i,\boldsymbol x_j)]+\operatorname{diag}(s)$, covariance vector $\boldsymbol k_*$ between query $\boldsymbol x_*$ and the training points, and $k_{**}=k(\boldsymbol x_*,\boldsymbol x_*)=\sigma^2$, the simple-kriging prediction mean and latent-function variance are

$$
\mu_*=m+\boldsymbol k_*^\mathsf TK^{-1}(\boldsymbol y-m\mathbf1),\qquad
v_*=k_{**}-\boldsymbol k_*^\mathsf TK^{-1}\boldsymbol k_*
$$


These are conditional $f(\boldsymbol x_*)$ moments given observations $\boldsymbol y$ under the assumed model; for $m=0$ they match Rasmussen–Williams §2.2 noisy prediction.
Without noise, at training $\boldsymbol x_*=\boldsymbol x_i$ vector $\boldsymbol k_*$ is column $i$ of $K$, giving $\mu_*=y_i$ and $v_*=0$.
Far from training data, $\boldsymbol k_*\to\mathbf0$ gives $\mu_*\to m$ and $v_*\to\sigma^2$.
Solve with prepared Cholesky factors without forming inverses.

`GaussianProcessModel::new(&points, values, covariance, &noise, mean)` adds nonnegative training-noise variances `noise` diagonally before factoring.
Defaults use `LocalModelOptions`; change through `with_options(.., mean, options)` or prepare within caller scopes through `build(.., options, scope)`.
Cholesky breakdown treats $K$ as non-positive-definite and gives `DomainError`.
Successful factors failing rank/condition tests retry SVD, with further failure giving `Singular`.
No jitter is added automatically to force success.
Negative noise gives `InvalidInput`; noise or mean count mismatches `DimensionMismatch`.

`KrigingMean::Constant(&means)` is simple kriging with supplied componentwise known means.
`KrigingMean::Ordinary` is ordinary kriging estimating unknown componentwise constants through generalized least squares $\hat m=\mathbf1^\mathsf TK^{-1}\boldsymbol y/\mathbf1^\mathsf TK^{-1}\mathbf1$.
Mean-estimation uncertainty adds the following

$$
\frac{(1-\mathbf1^\mathsf TK^{-1}\boldsymbol k_*)^2}{\mathbf1^\mathsf TK^{-1}\mathbf1}
$$

to latent variance.
This is the additional term in Rasmussen–Williams §2.7's limit of a flat prior on the coefficient of basis function $h(\boldsymbol x)=1$.
Far away, $\boldsymbol k_*\to\mathbf0$ makes ordinary variance approach $\sigma^2+1/(\mathbf1^\mathsf TK^{-1}\mathbf1)$, above prior $\sigma^2$.

## Prediction components and complexity

`GaussianProcessModel::prediction(observation_noise)` fixes new-observation noise and returns `GaussianPrediction`; negative noise gives `InvalidInput`.
It is `Interpolant`, with $m+2$ output components for $m$ value components.
Order is componentwise predicted means, latent variance, then new-observation variance.
Observation variance adds `observation_noise` to latent variance, describing new measured-value scatter.
Cancellation can make near-training latent variances slightly negative.
Negatives within $64\varepsilon n(\sigma^2+|\boldsymbol k_*^\mathsf TK^{-1}\boldsymbol k_*|+|\text{ordinary kriging term}|)$ round up to zero; more negative indicates inconsistency and gives `NumericalInstability`.
Construct `LocalPredictionWorkspace` through `prediction.workspace()` and pass to `evaluate_into` to avoid query allocations.
Use `evaluate_many` or `BatchEvaluator` for many predictions.

Dense preparation of $n$ samples costs $O(n^3)$ work and $O(n^2)$ storage.
Mean prediction costs $O(n)$, but solving $K^{-1}\boldsymbol k_*$ for variance makes total $O(n^2)$.
Extrapolation domains are axis-aligned training bounding boxes.
In `fit_report()`, `factorization()` identifies factors, `condition_norm()` condition norm; ordinary kriging has `degree()` equal to `Some(0)`.

`leave_one_out_into(index, out, workspace)` writes leave-one-out means to `out` and returns observation variances without refactoring.
For precision $P=K^{-1}$, means are $y_i-(P\boldsymbol y)_i/P_{ii}$ and variances $1/P_{ii}$ (Dubrule 1983).
Ordinary kriging uses $P=K^{-1}-\boldsymbol u\boldsymbol u^\mathsf T/(\mathbf1^\mathsf T\boldsymbol u)$ with mean estimation removed, setting $\boldsymbol u=K^{-1}\mathbf1$, and requires at least two points.
One call costs $O(n^2)$, or `Singular` if $P_{ii}\le0$.
Leave-one-out errors after full-data scale selection still retain selection effects.

[Surrogate models and acquisition functions](oxref:optimize-surrogate-acquisition) discusses using uncertainty to select future evaluations.

## Noiseless points and far-field variance

The next cell uses three $x=-1,0,1$ points with values $0.5,0,-0.5$ and signal-variance-one `SquaredExponential`.
Choose simple known mean zero or ordinary estimated constants.
Training noise is zero and new-observation noise variance 0.1.
Inputs are scale $\ell$ and mean treatment, predicting 201 points on $x\in[-5,5]$.
Queries leave training range $[-1,1]$, deliberately using `MethodDefined`.
The chart shows predicted means and $\pm2\sqrt{v_*}$ latent bands; the table samples integer points.
A final independent table compares mean/latent variance at $x=0.5$ with direct $3\times3$ Cramer solves of the formulas above.
Check three properties.
Noiseless training points have zero latent variance and observation variance 0.1.
Far away, simple latent variance tends to prior 1 and mean to known 0; ordinary latent variance tends above 1.
Larger scales extend training influence farther.

```rust
//| id: interp-kriging-variance
//| ref: interp-kriging-variance
//| title: "Compare latent and new-observation variances"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   length: {"type":"range","label":"Covariance scale ℓ [-]","min":0.2,"max":2.0,"step":0.1,"value":0.7}
//|   mean_kind: {"type":"select","label":"Mean treatment","options":[{"label":"Known zero mean, simple kriging","value":"simple"},{"label":"Estimated constant mean, ordinary kriging","value":"ordinary"}],"value":"simple"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"long-scale","label":"ℓ = 2","description":"Training influence persists; latent variance remains below 1 even at x = ±5.","inputs":{"length":2}}
//|   - {"id":"ordinary","label":"Ordinary kriging","description":"Mean-estimation uncertainty raises far-field variance above 1.","inputs":{"mean_kind":"ordinary"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"profile","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"profile","path":"/rows/4/3","approx":{"value":0.1,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"profile","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: long-scale
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"profile","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.9299538675745854,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: ordinary
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"profile","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.4806297889044597,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"profile","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{
    Covariance, CovarianceKernel, ExtrapolationPolicy, GaussianProcessModel, Interpolant,
    KrigingMean, LocalSamples,
};

let sites = [-1.0_f64, 0.0, 1.0];
let y = [0.5, 0.0, -0.5];
let data = LocalSamples::new(&sites.map(|x| [x]), &y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let points = data.points().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Signal variance 1, scale ℓ, k(r) = exp(-r²/2)
let covariance = Covariance::new(CovarianceKernel::SquaredExponential, 1.0, [length])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let ordinary = mean_kind == "ordinary";
let mean = if ordinary {
    KrigingMean::Ordinary
} else {
    KrigingMean::Constant(&[0.0])
};
// Training observation noise is zero
let values = data.values().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let model = GaussianProcessModel::new(&points, values, covariance, &[0.0; 3], mean)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Components [predicted mean, latent variance, observation variance]
let prediction = model.prediction(0.1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = prediction.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut predict = |x: f64| -> Result<[f64; 3], String> {
    let mut out = [0.0; 3];
    prediction
        .evaluate_into(
            [x],
            ExtrapolationPolicy::MethodDefined,
            &mut out,
            &mut workspace,
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(out)
};
let mut band = Vec::new();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for i in 0..=200 {
    let x = -5.0 + i as f64 / 20.0;
    let out = predict(x)?;
    let half = 2.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(out[1]);
    band.push([x, out[0], out[0] - half, out[0] + half]);
    if i % 20 == 0 {
        rows.push((x, out[0], out[1], out[2]));
    }
}
// Independent check at x = 0.5: direct Cramer K⁻¹, references using f64 exp
let k = |a: f64, b: f64| (-0.5 * ((a - b) / length).powi(2)).exp();
let gram: [[f64; 3]; 3] = sites.map(|a| sites.map(|b| k(a, b)));
let det = |m: [[f64; 3]; 3]| {
    m[0][0] * (m[1][1] * m[2][2] - m[1][2] * m[2][1])
        - m[0][1] * (m[1][0] * m[2][2] - m[1][2] * m[2][0])
        + m[0][2] * (m[1][0] * m[2][1] - m[1][1] * m[2][0])
};
let solve = |b: [f64; 3]| -> [f64; 3] {
    core::array::from_fn(|c| {
        let mut m = gram;
        for r in 0..3 {
            m[r][c] = b[r];
        }
        det(m) / det(gram)
    })
};
let dot = |a: [f64; 3], b: [f64; 3]| a[0] * b[0] + a[1] * b[1] + a[2] * b[2];
let kstar = sites.map(|b| k(0.5, b));
let (u, w) = (solve([1.0; 3]), solve(kstar));
let m_hat = if ordinary {
    dot(u, y) / dot(u, [1.0; 3])
} else {
    0.0
};
let mean_check = m_hat + dot(w, y.map(|v| v - m_hat));
let trend = if ordinary {
    (1.0 - dot(u, kstar)).powi(2) / dot(u, [1.0; 3])
} else {
    0.0
};
let variance_check = 1.0 - dot(w, kstar) + trend;
let at_half = predict(0.5)?;
let observed: Vec<_> = sites.iter().zip(y).map(|(&x, v)| [x, v]).collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "xLabel": "Coordinate x [-]",
    "yLabel": "Value [-]",
    "yMin": -2.5,
    "yMax": 2.5,
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "layers": [
        { "id": "band", "kind": "band", "name": "Predicted mean ± 2√(latent variance)", "points": band },
        { "id": "samples", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Training points", "points": observed }
    ],
    "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.0, "label": "y = 0" }]
}));
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Coordinate x [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 0 } },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Predicted mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Latent variance [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Observation variance [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "profile" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity at x = 0.5" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "SciOxide [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 12 } },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from direct solve [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![
        ("Predicted mean", at_half[0], (at_half[0] - mean_check).abs()),
        ("Latent variance", at_half[1], (at_half[1] - variance_check).abs()),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "check" })
);
```

At training $x=-1,0,1$, latent variance is zero, observation variance 0.1, and means reproduce data.
Mean/latent variance at $x=0.5$ agree with Cramer solves to about $10^{-16}$.
For default $\ell=0.7$ simple kriging, latent variance is 0.9997 at $x=\pm3$ and nearly 1 at $x=\pm5$; means return to zero.
At $\ell=2$ (`long-scale`), latent variance at $x=\pm5$ remains 0.930, showing longer influence.
Ordinary kriging (`ordinary`) has variance at $x=\pm5$ approaching $1.4806=1+1/(\mathbf1^\mathsf TK^{-1}\mathbf1)$ above prior 1.
Training mean is zero here, so fitted means are also zero and predictions match simple kriging.
Bands vanish at training points and widen toward $\pm2$ away under simple kriging.
With noisy training, latent variance generally remains nonzero at training points.
Variances quantify statistical uncertainty under assumed models and exclude bias from wrong means or covariances.

## Summary

Local models fix neighbors during preparation and reuse factors; querywise reselection requires per-center models.
Local polynomials use unweighted least squares, enlarging neighborhoods or lowering degrees on rank/condition failures; inspect accepted degree and neighbors in `LocalFitReport`.
Local RBF polynomial terms depend on kernels; thin plates reproduce affine functions.
Kriging variances are conditional statistical uncertainty from covariance/noise assumptions, not approximation-error bounds.
Distinguish known/estimated means and latent/observation variance; ordinary far-field variance exceeds prior variance.
