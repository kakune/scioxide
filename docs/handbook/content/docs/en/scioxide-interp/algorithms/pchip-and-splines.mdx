Measurements, property tables, and trajectories often join samples with piecewise polynomials to avoid global Runge behavior.
Piecewise cubics may not simultaneously preserve second-derivative continuity ($C^2$) and shape such as monotonicity or nonnegativity.
Starting from Hermite bases, derive monotone PCHIP conditions, cubic systems, and local Akima slopes.

## Piecewise cubic Hermite bases

On interval $[x_i, x_{i+1}]$ of width $h_i = x_{i+1} - x_i$, cubic $S_i(x)$ is uniquely determined in dimensionless $t = (x - x_i)/h_i \in [0, 1]$ by endpoint values $y_i, y_{i+1}$ and derivatives $d_i, d_{i+1}$.

$$
S_i(x) = h_{00}(t) y_i + h_{10}(t) h_i d_i + h_{01}(t) y_{i+1} + h_{11}(t) h_i d_{i+1}
$$

The four bases follow.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{00}(t) &= 2t^3 - 3t^2 + 1 = (1 + 2t)(1 - t)^2 \\
h_{10}(t) &= t^3 - 2t^2 + t = t(1 - t)^2 \\
h_{01}(t) &= -2t^3 + 3t^2 = t^2(3 - 2t) \\
h_{11}(t) &= t^3 - t^2 = t^2(t - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Each has exactly one unit endpoint value/derivative at $t = 0, 1$ and the others zero, such as $h_{00}(0) = 1$ and $h_{10}'(0) = 1$.
Thus $S_i$ exactly matches endpoint $y_i, y_{i+1}$ and $d_i, d_{i+1}$.
Sharing nodal values/slopes gives globally $C^1$ curves regardless of slope choices $d_i$.
Second derivatives generally jump.
Methods differ only in choosing nodal $d_i$.

scioxide-interp `PchipInterpolator`, `AkimaInterpolator`, and `CubicSpline` precompute slopes and share this interval representation.
Construct with samples through `new(x, y)`; change separation by passing `SampleOptions` to `with_options`.
At least two strictly increasing nodes are required; `slopes()` returns node-ordered component slopes in physical units.
Binary interval search makes point values and derivatives cost $O(\log n)$.
Supplied-workspace `evaluate_into` allocates nothing; owned `evaluate` or `value_at` creates workspace per call ([Prepared and batch evaluation](../prepared-evaluation-and-batches/)).
All three implement `Differentiable` and `Integrable`; in-domain values, slopes, and integrals also use `value_at`, `derivative_at`, and `integrate`.
Per-call integrals add work proportional to crossed intervals to binary search.
`CubicSpline` also offers `prepare_antiderivative()` for repeated integrals.
Complex, array, and vector slopes are computed independently per real component, matching scalar fits.

## PCHIP and Fritsch–Carlson monotonicity conditions

For increasing data $y_0 \le y_1 \le \dots \le y_n$, let interval secants be $\Delta_i = (y_{i+1} - y_i)/h_i \ge 0$.
On $\Delta_i = 0$ intervals, $S_i$ is monotone constant exactly when $d_i = d_{i+1} = 0$.
For $\Delta_i > 0$, normalize endpoint slopes by secants:

$$
\alpha_i = \frac{d_i}{\Delta_i}, \qquad \beta_i = \frac{d_{i+1}}{\Delta_i}
$$


Differentiating Hermite bases gives normalized derivative quadratic in $t$:

$$
g(t) = \frac{S_i'(x)}{\Delta_i} = 3(\alpha_i + \beta_i - 2)\,t^2 - 2(2\alpha_i + \beta_i - 3)\,t + \alpha_i
$$

with $g(0) = \alpha_i$ and $g(1) = \beta_i$.
Monotonicity therefore requires $\alpha_i \ge 0$ and $\beta_i \ge 0$.
Then $g \ge 0$ over $[0, 1]$ holds exactly when one of these holds (Fritsch and Carlson 1980).

1. For $\alpha_i + \beta_i - 2 \le 0$, $g$ is concave or linear and minimizes at endpoints.
2. For $2\alpha_i + \beta_i - 3 \le 0$ or $\alpha_i + 2\beta_i - 3 \le 0$, $g$ is convex but vertex $t^* = (2\alpha_i + \beta_i - 3)/(3(\alpha_i + \beta_i - 2))$ lies outside $[0, 1]$.
3. For $\phi(\alpha_i, \beta_i) = \alpha_i - \dfrac{(2\alpha_i + \beta_i - 3)^2}{3(\alpha_i + \beta_i - 2)} \ge 0$, vertex minimum $g(t^*) = \phi$ is nonnegative.

Practical sufficient subsets include square $0 \le \alpha_i, \beta_i \le 3$ and its quarter-disk subset $\alpha_i^2 + \beta_i^2 \le 9$.
At square corner $\alpha_i = \beta_i = 3$, $t^* = 1/2$ and $\phi = 0$ meet condition 3's boundary.

### Checking monotonicity limits

Examine one interval beyond that corner.
On $[0, 1]$ with $y_0 = 0$, $y_1 = 1$, $h = 1$, and secant $\Delta = 1$, assign equal normalized slopes $s = \alpha = \beta$ to cubic Hermite

$$
p(t) = h_{10}(t)\, s + h_{01}(t) + h_{11}(t)\, s
$$

and construct/plot it through `HermiteInterpolator::new(x, y, dy)`.
For $\alpha = \beta = s$, $g(t) = 6(s - 1)t^2 - 6(s - 1)t + s$; $s > 1$ minimizes at vertex $t^* = 1/2$ with $g(1/2) = (3 - s)/2$, while $s \le 1$ minimizes at ends with $s$.
Minimum $p'$ is therefore $\min(s, (3 - s)/2)$; at $s > 3$ central slopes become negative.
Dimensionless slope $s$ ranges 0–6; the table shows slope $p'(1/2)$ at $t = 1/2$, the minimum $p'$ over 121 grid points, its closed form, minimum/maximum $p$, and the count of intervals between adjacent grid points where values decrease.
Closed forms independently bypass `HermiteInterpolator`.

### PCHIP nodal slopes

[`PchipInterpolator`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.PchipInterpolator.html) selects $d_i$ from adjacent secants alone.

1. Extrema and flat regions Opposite secant signs or either zero ($\Delta_{i-1} \Delta_i \le 0$) mark $x_i$ as an extremum/flat endpoint; set $d_i = 0$.
2. Weighted harmonic means, Fritsch–Butland Equal signs $\Delta_{i-1} \Delta_i > 0$ use interval-width weighted harmonic means.

   $$
   d_i = \frac{w_1 + w_2}{\dfrac{w_1}{\Delta_{i-1}} + \dfrac{w_2}{\Delta_i}}, \qquad w_1 = 2h_i + h_{i-1}, \quad w_2 = h_i + 2h_{i-1}
   $$

   Left secant $\Delta_{i-1}$ receives weight $w_1$ counting right width twice; the right receives $w_2$ counting left width twice.
   Dropping one denominator term gives $d_i \le (w_1 + w_2)\Delta_{i-1}/w_1 \le 3\Delta_{i-1}$, similarly $d_i \le 3\Delta_i$, hence $0 < d_i \le 3 \min(\Delta_{i-1}, \Delta_i)$.
3. Endpoints Use end secants in one-sided three-point $d_0 = \Delta_0 + \frac{h_0}{h_0 + h_1}(\Delta_0 - \Delta_1)$.
   Opposite sign to $\Delta_0$ or $\Delta_0 = 0$ sets $d_0 = 0$; secant sign changes $\Delta_0 \Delta_1 < 0$ limit to $|d_0| \le 3|\Delta_0|$.
   Apply the same right-end formula with $h_{n-1}, h_{n-2}, \Delta_{n-1}, \Delta_{n-2}$.

Rule 2 bounds every interior $\alpha_i, \beta_i$ between 0 and 3, inside the square.
For equal-sign secants, endpoint formulas give $0 \le d_0 < 2\Delta_0$ when $\Delta_0 > 0$; sign-change limiting also stays inside.
Thus monotone real data give monotone intervals.
Componentwise tests preserve each monotone vector component.
Two nodes give a line.

Weights depend only on adjacent width ratios; scaling coordinates by $c$ scales slopes by $1/c$ and preserves normalized $\alpha_i, \beta_i$.
When the magnitudes of nodes, widths, and secants are roughly between $10^{-152}$ and $10^{152}$, evaluate the formulas directly.
Extreme values first normalize the adjacent widths by their maximum.
Scaling by distant-node magnitudes could underflow both local widths and lose their ratio.

For uniform smooth monotone data away from extrema, slopes are $O(h^2)$ accurate and interpolation errors $O(h^3)$.
Expanding secants as $\Delta_{i-1} = f' - h_{i-1} f''/2 + O(h^2)$ and $\Delta_i = f' + h_i f''/2 + O(h^2)$ in the harmonic mean gives $d_i - f'(x_i) = f''(x_i)(h_i - h_{i-1})/6 + O(h^2)$.
Uniform gaps cancel the first-order term, leaving $O(h^2)$; it remains when adjacent-width ratios differ substantially from 1.
Zeroed extrema flatten curves and reduce local accuracy even for smooth data.
Using only adjacent secants gives one order less than cubic-spline $O(h^4)$.

### Checking PCHIP slopes

The next cell compares `PchipInterpolator::slopes()` with direct f64 formulas for seven samples including maxima and plateaus.
Samples $x = (0, 2, 3, 3.5, 5, 6, 8)$ and $y = (0, 4, -1, -1, 0.5, 2, 2.4)$ exercise every branch of all three rules.
Local maximum $x_1$ gives $d_1 = 0$; flat-region endpoints $x_2, x_3$ give $d_2 = d_3 = 0$; slopes at $x_4, x_5$ use weighted harmonic means.
Left secants change sign, $\Delta_0 = 2$ and $\Delta_1 = -5$, limiting formula $6.67$ to $3\Delta_0 = 6$.
The right formula $-0.67$ opposes $\Delta_5 = 0.2$ and becomes zero.
Input $k$ scales all coordinates by $10^k$, checking preserved ratios at extremes.
Reported slopes have been multiplied by $10^k$ to restore the coordinate units for $k = 0$.
The first table compares nodal slopes/direct values; the second gives interval $\alpha_i, \beta_i$, undefined on flat $\Delta_i = 0$.

let intervals: Vec<(f64, f64, Option<f64>, Option<f64>)> = (0..n - 1)

Left limited slope $6 = 3\Delta_0$ puts first-interval $\alpha_0$ on square edge $\alpha = 3$.
At $x_4 = 5$, the slope is the weighted harmonic mean $7.5/(3.5/1 + 4/1.5) = 1.2162$, with weights $w_1 = 3.5$ and $w_2 = 4$.

At $k = \pm 300$ widths are about $10^{\pm 300}$, but slopes multiplied by $10^k$ differ from direct formulas by at most $9 \times 10^{-16}$, matching $k = 0$ within rounding.
Only width ratios determine slopes, retained even at extreme coordinates.

## Cubic-spline tridiagonal equations and boundaries

Cubic splines interpolate with piecewise cubics continuous through second derivatives, $C^2$.
On $n$ intervals, $4n$ coefficients have $2n$ endpoint interpolation conditions and $2(n-1)$ conditions for $S'$/$S''$ continuity at $n - 1$ interior nodes.
Two endpoint conditions complete the system.

Choose nodal second derivatives $M_j = S''(x_j)$ as unknowns.
Within each piece $S''$ is linear, so

$$
S''_j(x) = M_j \frac{x_{j+1} - x}{h_j} + M_{j+1} \frac{x - x_j}{h_j}
$$


integrating twice, setting constants from $S_j(x_j) = y_j$ and $S_j(x_{j+1}) = y_{j+1}$, and enforcing interior $S'_{j-1}(x_j) = S'_j(x_j)$ gives this tridiagonal system.

$$
\frac{h_{j-1}}{6} M_{j-1} + \frac{h_{j-1} + h_j}{3} M_j + \frac{h_j}{6} M_{j+1} = \frac{y_{j+1} - y_j}{h_j} - \frac{y_j - y_{j-1}}{h_{j-1}} \quad (j = 1, \dots, n-1)
$$

Diagonal $(h_{j-1} + h_j)/3$ is twice off-diagonal sum $(h_{j-1} + h_j)/6$, hence strictly dominant.

[`CubicSpline::new(x, y)`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.CubicSpline.html#method.new) constructs not-a-knot cubics.
Pass other [`SplineBoundary`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/enum.SplineBoundary.html) boundaries to `CubicSpline::with_boundary(x, y, boundary)`, or use `with_options(x, y, boundary, options)` to change separation too.
Normalize coordinates by domain half-width before assembly, making systems independent of coordinate scale even for large nodes.
Node-only coefficient matrices factor once with different right sides per real component.
Factorization and component solves cost $O(n)$, making work proportional to nodes times components.
Convert solved $M_j$ to slopes and store Hermite pieces.
Singular systems give `Singular`; periodic endpoint mismatch or fewer than two nodes `InvalidInput`.

### Boundary selection and errors

1. Natural `Natural` imposes $M_0 = M_n = 0$.
   This represents beams without endpoint bending moments.
   Among interpolating functions with square-integrable second derivatives, it minimizes $\int_{x_0}^{x_n} (S''(x))^2 \,dx$, Holladay's theorem.
   Nonzero true endpoint second derivatives limit near-end errors to $O(h^2)$.
2. Clamped `Clamped(v0, vn)` imposes $S'(x_0) = v_0$ and $S'(x_n) = v_n$.
   Supply slopes in value type with physical units per coordinate.
   Convert heat flux through conductivity to temperature gradients.
   Correct slopes and $f \in C^4$ give $\|f - S\|_\infty \le \frac{5}{384} h^4 \|f^{(4)}\|_\infty$ for $h = \max_j h_j$ (Hall and Meyer 1976).
3. `NotAKnot` also makes third derivatives continuous at $x_1$ and $x_{n-1}$.
   The first and last interval pairs each form one cubic, commonly retaining $O(h^4)$ accuracy without endpoint information.
   Eliminating endpoint unknowns generally removes diagonal dominance.
   Nonperiodic SciOxide systems use tridiagonal LU with adjacent-row partial pivoting.
4. `Periodic` matches endpoint values, first derivatives, and second derivatives.
   Solve cyclic tridiagonal systems with unknown $M_0 = M_n$ linked across endpoints.
   Component endpoint differences within $\sqrt{\varepsilon}\max(|y_0|, |y_n|, 1)$ are accepted and final values replaced by initial values.
   Larger discrepancies give `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).

All boundaries support two or more points.
Not-a-knot with two points gives lines and with three a parabola; periodic two-point cases are constant.
Periodic three-point cases solve the two-row cyclic system in closed form.

## Akima and local slopes

Akima (1970) chooses node-$x_i$ slopes from four secants $m_{i-2}, m_{i-1}, m_i, m_{i+1}$, with $m_j$ the secant on $[x_j, x_{j+1}]$.

$$
d_i = \frac{|m_{i+1} - m_i|\, m_{i-1} + |m_{i-1} - m_{i-2}|\, m_i}{|m_{i+1} - m_i| + |m_{i-1} - m_{i-2}|}
$$

Left $m_{i-1}$ receives right-secant differences as weights; right $m_i$ left differences.
Thus slopes approach the side that is more nearly linear.
Abrupt changes affect nearby nodes with limited influence on distant plateaus.

[`AkimaInterpolator::new(x, y)`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.AkimaInterpolator.html#method.new) uses these formulas directly, `AkimaMethod::Akima`.
`AkimaInterpolator::with_method(x, y, method)` selects weights with `AkimaMethod`.
`AkimaMethod::Makima`, modified Akima by Moler and Ionita, uses weights

$$
w_1 = |m_{i+1} - m_i| + \frac{|m_{i+1} + m_i|}{2}, \qquad
w_2 = |m_{i-1} - m_{i-2}| + \frac{|m_{i-1} + m_{i-2}|}{2}
$$

and sets $d_i = (w_1 m_{i-1} + w_2 m_i)/(w_1 + w_2)$.
Added terms resolve consecutive equal slopes with zero original weights and reduce plateau overshoot.
Zero total weights use the adjacent-secant mean; equal adjacent secants use their common value.
At endpoints, extend two secants each way by $m = 2a - b$, with adjacent $a$ and next-interior $b$, then use the same rule.
Two nodes give a line.
Neither guarantees monotonicity for general monotone data; use PCHIP if required.

## Overshoot near abrupt changes

The next cell fits step samples $x = 0, 1, \dots, 7$, zeros then ones, using natural, not-a-knot, Akima, Makima, and PCHIP.
Not-a-knot, Akima, and PCHIP use `new`; natural uses `with_boundary`; Makima `with_method`.
The input is the step position, the first node with value one (2–5); outputs show curves, maximum excursions beyond $[0, 1]$, and the count of intervals between adjacent verification points where values decrease.
The 561 finite verification points cannot prove global monotonicity from absence of decreases.

```rust
//| id: interp-overshoot-comparison
//| ref: interp-overshoot-comparison
//| title: "Compare overshoot of five piecewise cubic methods"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   step: {"type":"range","label":"Step position, first node with value 1","min":2.0,"max":5.0,"step":1.0,"value":3.0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"short-left-plateau","label":"Position 2","description":"Only two left-plateau points make Akima and Makima overshoot too.","inputs":{"step":2}}
//|   - {"id":"mirrored","label":"Position 5","description":"Reflection gives position-3-equivalent samples and values.","inputs":{"step":5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.10938216235292858,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.13075231758373204,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/4/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/4/2","equals":0}
//|   - case: short-left-plateau
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":0.125,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.10992504882812498,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/4/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/4/2","equals":0}
//|   - case: mirrored
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.10938216235292858,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"overshoot","path":"/rows/4/2","equals":0}
use scioxide_interp::{
    AkimaInterpolator, AkimaMethod, CubicSpline, PchipInterpolator, SplineBoundary, Univariate,
};

let step = step as usize;
let x: Vec<_> = (0..8).map(f64::from).collect();
let y: Vec<_> = (0..8).map(|i| if i < step { 0.0 } else { 1.0 }).collect();
let natural = CubicSpline::with_boundary(x.clone(), y.clone(), SplineBoundary::Natural)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// new uses not-a-knot ends
let nak = CubicSpline::new(x.clone(), y.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// new uses original Akima weights
let akima = AkimaInterpolator::new(x.clone(), y.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let makima = AkimaInterpolator::with_method(x.clone(), y.clone(), AkimaMethod::Makima)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let pchip = PchipInterpolator::new(x, y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut curves = [Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
for i in 0..=560 {
    let t = f64::from(i) / 80.0;
    let values = [
        natural.value_at(t),
        nak.value_at(t),
        akima.value_at(t),
        makima.value_at(t),
        pchip.value_at(t),
    ];
    for (curve, value) in curves.iter_mut().zip(values) {
        curve.push([t, value.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
    }
}
let names = ["Natural", "not-a-knot", "Akima", "Makima", "PCHIP"];
let rows: Vec<_> = names
    .iter()
    .zip(&curves)
    .map(|(name, curve)| {
        let outside = curve
            .iter()
            .map(|v| (-v[1]).max(v[1] - 1.0).max(0.0))
            .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
        let decreases = curve
            .windows(2)
            .filter(|w| w[1][1] < w[0][1] - 1e-12)
            .count();
        (*name, outside, decreases)
    })
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    {
        "key": "1",
        "label": "Maximum excursion outside [0, 1] [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": { "significantDigits": 3 }
    },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Decreasing verification intervals", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "overshoot" }));
let series: Vec<_> = names
    .iter()
    .zip(&curves)
    .map(|(name, curve)| serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": curve }))
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Coordinate x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Interpolated value [-]",
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "range", "axis": "y", "from": 0.0, "to": 1.0, "label": "Sample range" }
        ]
    })
);
```

At default position 3, natural overshoots 0.109 and not-a-knot 0.131, with decreases in 246 and 265 of 560 intervals respectively.
$C^2$ conditions transmit step effects globally, producing ripples even on distant plateaus.
Akima, Makima, and PCHIP show neither excursions nor decreases.
Position 2 leaves two plateau points, causing Akima 0.125 overshoot with 40 decreases and Makima 0.110 with 43.
Extended left secants $m_{-1} = 2m_0 - m_1 = -1$ and $m_{-2} = -2$ do not identify a linear plateau, giving slopes $d_0 = -0.5$ and $d_1 = 0.5$ and interval $[0, 1]$ values down to $-0.125$.
Only PCHIP shows no overshoot or decreases at every position.
Reflecting position 5 gives position 3 with zeros/ones exchanged, hence identical excursion and decrease counts.

Spline $C^2$ means continuity through second derivatives, not generally third.
For continuous trajectory jerk, design degrees and multiplicities separately ([B-splines, tensor products, and NURBS](../b-splines-tensor-and-nurbs/)).

## Summary

Piecewise cubics differ in slope selection; point values/derivatives cost $O(\log n)$ and supplied-workspace forms allocate nothing.
Choose PCHIP for monotone property tables or CDFs.
Interior slopes at most three times the secants put each interval's $(\alpha_i, \beta_i)$ in the Fritsch–Carlson square and preserve its monotonicity.
Uniform PCHIP errors $O(h^3)$ are one order below cubic splines; setting slopes to zero at extrema reduces nearby accuracy.
For second-derivative continuity use `CubicSpline`: unknown slopes choose `NotAKnot` of `new`, known slopes `Clamped` of `with_boundary`, periodic data `Periodic`.
Natural splines minimize bending energy but lose endpoint accuracy for nonzero true second derivatives.
Akima/Makima localize abrupt changes without guaranteeing monotonicity; check verification-point overshoot.
