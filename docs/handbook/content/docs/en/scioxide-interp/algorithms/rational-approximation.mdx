Functions with resonances or nearby singularities can sometimes be represented with fewer coefficients by rational functions with a denominator than by increasing the degree of a polynomial.
A polynomial cannot have poles and must follow rapid changes near a pole with high-degree terms, whereas a rational function can represent the pole itself as a zero of its denominator.
However, an approximation that fits the samples well can still develop unwanted poles between samples.
Inspect the approximation residual, denominator conditioning, and pole estimates separately.
This page covers AAA and AAA–Lawson fitting from samples, and diagnostics for estimated poles and residues.
[Floater–Hormann interpolation and Padé approximation](../floater-hormann-and-pade/) covers Floater–Hormann interpolation, which introduces no poles on the real axis, and Padé approximation from Taylor coefficients.

## Barycentric form and support points

Represent a rational function with support points $z_j$ and two sets of coefficients $a_j$ and $b_j$ ($j = 1, \dots, m$) as follows.

$$
r(z) = \frac{n(z)}{d(z)}, \qquad
n(z) = \sum_{j=1}^m \frac{a_j}{z - z_j}, \qquad
d(z) = \sum_{j=1}^m \frac{b_j}{z - z_j}
$$

Multiplying the numerator and denominator by $\ell(z) = \prod_{k}(z - z_k)$ makes $\ell n$ and $\ell d$ polynomials of degree at most $m-1$, so $r$ is a rational function of type $(m-1, m-1)$.
Each term diverges at a support point $z_j$, but if $b_j \ne 0$, the limiting value is $r(z_j) = a_j / b_j$.
Divergence of individual terms in the formula therefore differs from a pole of the rational function.
The poles are the zeros of $\ell d$ that do not cancel with $\ell n$.
AAA chooses $a_j = b_j f_j$, so it interpolates the sample values $f_j$ at the support points.
Multiplying both numerator and denominator by the same nonzero constant leaves the function unchanged.
Compared with expressing type $(m-1, m-1)$ in polynomial coefficients, this form can be better conditioned for the support-point configuration and can be evaluated directly from its coefficients.

[`BarycentricRational`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.BarycentricRational.html) stores the support points `support_points()`, numerator coefficients `numerator_weights()`, and denominator coefficients `weights()` separately.
The same type can therefore represent the result of Lawson iteration, which does not interpolate the samples.
`BarycentricRational::new(nodes, numerator, denominator, domain)` checks that support points are finite, distinct, and within the approximation domain, that coefficients are finite, and that the denominator coefficients are not all zero.
Evaluation costs $O(m)$ for $m$ support points.
The workspace type is `()`, and evaluation does not allocate.
Evaluation sums terms relative to the term for the support point nearest the query point; if a term overflows or underflows, it recomputes with an extended exponent range.
If the evaluation point is a pole and the denominator becomes zero, evaluation returns an error of `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).
`denominator_condition(z, policy)` returns the denominator cancellation indicator $\sum_j |b_j/(z - z_j)| \big/ |\sum_j b_j/(z - z_j)|$.
This value is at least 1 and equals 1 at support points or where all terms have the same sign; larger values indicate more cancellation in the denominator sum.
It is a conditioning indicator, not an estimate of approximation error.

The approximation domain `RationalDomain` has two variants.
`Real(interval)` is a closed real interval; `Complex { real, imaginary }` is a rectangle formed as the product of closed intervals for the real and imaginary parts.
Support points and samples must lie within the approximation domain; otherwise, the result is `DomainError`.
A `Real` domain rejects support points and samples with nonzero imaginary parts with `InvalidInput`.
The approximation domain only determines the coordinates available for evaluation and the reference for extrapolation policy; it guarantees neither the absence of poles nor approximation accuracy within it.

Evaluation goes through `Interpolant`.
Both real and complex models can be evaluated on a `Real` domain with the one-coordinate form `[x]` (`Interpolant<_, 1>`); this form also supports the scalar form `value_at(x)`.
Complex models additionally have a two-coordinate form for `[re, im]` (`Interpolant<_, 2>`).
A `Complex` domain can only be evaluated with the two-coordinate form; a one-coordinate query returns `InvalidInput`.
Using a `Real` domain with the two-coordinate form treats it as the rectangle $[a, b] \times [0, 0]$.
Because a complex model has two implementations, calls that do not pass coordinates must specify the dimension explicitly, as in `Interpolant::<f64, 2>::domains(&model)`.

## Greedy selection and linearization in AAA

The AAA (adaptive Antoulas–Anderson) method fits a rational function to samples $(Z_i, F_i)$ ($i = 1, \dots, N$), selecting support points one at a time.
scioxide-interp follows these steps.

1. Accept the sample mean as a constant approximation and compute the maximum residual over all samples.
2. If the maximum residual is at most the threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} \max_i |F_i|$, terminate with `Converged`.
3. Add the sample with the largest residual among those not yet selected as support points (the earliest in input order in a tie) as the next support point.
4. Determine the weights $b$ through a linearized least-squares problem and set $a_j = b_j f_j$.
5. Recompute the residuals over all samples and return to 2.
   Once the support count reaches `max_terms`, perform only the test in 2; if it fails, terminate with `SupportLimit`.

The linearization in 4 reduces $F_i d(Z_i) - n(Z_i)$ at samples $Z_i$ other than the support points.
Directly reducing the error $F_i - r(Z_i)$ of $r(Z_i) = n(Z_i)/d(Z_i)$ is nonlinear in $b$, so multiplying by the denominator makes it linear.
Substituting $a_j = b_j f_j$ gives this quantity as

$$
F_i d(Z_i) - n(Z_i) = \sum_j \frac{F_i - f_j}{Z_i - z_j}\, b_j = (L b)_i
$$


$L$ is called the Loewner matrix, and the $b$ that minimizes $\|L b\|_2$ subject to $\|b\|_2 = 1$ is the right singular vector corresponding to the smallest singular value of $L$.
If numerical rank deficiency makes the minimizer nonunique, scioxide-interp uses the unit vector obtained by normalizing the sum of an orthonormal basis of the null space, making the result deterministic.
Each component $(Lb)_i = d(Z_i)\,(F_i - r(Z_i))$ of the linearized residual is the true error multiplied by $d(Z_i)$, so steps 3 and 5 reevaluate the residual of the rational function itself.
The minimized quantity is a sum of squares, while the threshold is compared with a maximum, so adding a support point can increase the maximum residual at some stages.
The original paper is by [Nakatsukasa, Sète, and Trefethen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00337).

With $k$ support points, $L$ is approximately an $N \times k$ matrix, and its dense thin SVD costs $O(Nk^2)$ when $N \ge k$.
Increasing the support count to $m$ gives a total SVD cost of $O(Nm^3)$.
Before factorization, divide values by the largest absolute sample value and multiply each entry by the minimum distance between a sample and a support point to scale the problem.
Both are common factors, so the weights are unchanged.

AAA has no quantities to precompute and reuse, so it has no Plan; fitting has the following three forms.

- The one-shot function `aaa(nodes, values)` fits with default settings and returns only the approximation `BarycentricRational`.
  The support limit is $\min(100, N)$, both absolute and relative tolerances are $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ (approximately $1.5 \times 10^{-8}$ for f64), and the approximation domain is the interval enclosing the nodes (if all nodes are real) or their enclosing rectangle.
  If fitting stops before reaching the threshold, it returns `ConvergenceFailure` (`TruncationLimit` for the support limit) without returning an approximation.
- The detailed form `aaa_with_options(nodes, values, domain, options)` takes an approximation domain and `AaaOptions`, allocates workspace and output for sequential execution, and returns an owned `RationalFit`.
  Even an unconverged termination such as `SupportLimit` returns `Ok`, with the approximation accepted by that point in `fit.approximant()`.
- The borrowed form `aaa_into(nodes, values, domain, options, out, workspace, scope)` writes to reserved output and workspace and takes an execution scope.

`AaaOptions::new()` (the same as `Default`) defaults to at most 100 support points, tolerance $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, no Lawson iteration, sequential execution, default SVD settings, and no iteration limit.
Change the support limit with `with_max_terms` and the residual tolerance with `with_tolerance`.
If `max_terms` is zero, the result is `InvalidInput`.
Every form returns `InvalidInput` for duplicate sample nodes, and `NonFinite` if a node or value is NaN or infinite.
The detailed and borrowed forms also return `InvalidInput` if the sample count is less than `max_terms`, and `DomainError` if a sample lies outside the approximation domain.
Read [`RationalReport`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.RationalReport.html) through `fit.report()`.
The `RationalTermination` returned by `termination()` is the sole authority for termination status; `is_success()` is true only for `Converged`.
Statuses other than `Converged` also expose a usable approximation whose maximum residual exceeds the threshold.
`iterations()` is the sum of support-addition stages and attempted Lawson stages, including Lawson stages that were not accepted.
`max_residual()` is the maximum residual at the samples; `singular_ratio()` is the ratio of the smallest to the largest singular value of the system that produced the retained approximation.
This ratio equals $\min_{\|b\|=1}\|Lb\|_2 / \|L\|_2$ and indicates the relative size of the linearized fit, but does not guarantee an error bound.

To solve repeatedly in reserved storage, combine the sample count and settings in `RationalRequest::new(samples, options)`.
Obtain `RationalRequirements` with `RationalWorkspace::workspace_requirements(request)`, prepare workspace with `RationalWorkspace::workspace(request)` and output with `RationalFit::new()` and `try_reserve`, then call `aaa_into` with the same settings.
The requirements include the SVD matrix dimensions (columns equal the support limit for AAA and twice that for Lawson; rows equal the larger of the sample count and column count) and residual-history length; solving after reservation does not allocate.
Immediately after `RationalFit::new()`, nothing has been published, so `approximant()` and `report()` return `None`.
If a solve fails, the previously published approximation and report remain available.
`RationalWorkspace::residual_history()` is the sequence of maximum sample residuals from the initial constant approximation through each accepted approximation.
Set the iteration limit with `AaaOptions::with_budgets`; if the work limit is reached after the constant approximation, the accepted approximation is published with `BudgetExhausted`.

## Lawson iteration and doublets

Setting $l > 0$ with `AaaOptions::with_lawson_iterations(l)` performs at most $l$ weighted least-squares iterations with fixed support points if AAA terminates without converging (except when it reaches the iteration limit): AAA–Lawson.
Each stage minimizes the following over the unit vector $[a; b]$ formed by concatenating the numerator and denominator coefficients:

$$
\sum_i \lambda_i \bigl| n(Z_i) - F_i\, d(Z_i) \bigr|^2
$$

The sample weights are then updated by $\lambda_i \leftarrow \lambda_i |e_i| / \max_k |e_k|$ (where $e_i$ is the residual at that stage) and normalized to have maximum 1.
Samples with larger errors receive more weight, so iteration tends to equalize the maximum residual.
At support-point samples, replace the row with $a_j - f_j b_j$.
Because $a$ and $b$ are independent, the result need not interpolate samples even at support points.
Only stages that strictly reduce the maximum residual are accepted, so the published maximum residual never increases.
A fixed point of this iteration corresponds to the conditions for a best uniform approximation (one minimizing the maximum error), but scioxide-interp does not guarantee convergence to a best uniform approximation.
Termination is `Converged` upon reaching the threshold, `IterationLimit` upon reaching the limit, or `Stagnation` if a trial approximation cannot be evaluated at a sample (a pole forms on a sample) or all weights become zero.
A Lawson stage solves the SVD of a matrix with $2k$ columns for the combined numerator and denominator coefficients.

Reducing sample errors too aggressively can fit noise and produce nearby pairs of unwanted poles and zeros.
Such a pair is called a Froissart doublet.
The pole of a doublet has a small residue and nearly cancels with the nearby zero, leaving little trace in the sample residuals.
This can also happen with noiseless samples if the threshold is lowered to the level of rounding error.

`BarycentricRational::diagnostics(policy, doublet, eigen_options)` multiplies the numerator and denominator by $\ell(z)$ to convert them to polynomials in local coordinates $u = (z - c)/s$, then obtains zeros and poles as eigenvalues of companion matrices.
Here $c$ is the first support point, and $s$ is the distance from $c$ to the farthest support point, placing all support points within the unit disk.
The residue at a pole $\zeta$ is $p(\zeta)/q'(\zeta)$ multiplied by the coordinate scale $s$ to return to the original coordinates; if $q'(\zeta) = 0$, a simple-pole residue is undefined, so `RationalPole::residue()` returns `None`.
The returned [`RationalDiagnostics`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-interp/0.1.0/scioxide_interp/struct.RationalDiagnostics.html) contains the numerator zeros `roots()`, the pole list `poles()`, diagnostics for numerator and denominator roots, counts of leading coefficients removed by tolerance, and the center and scale of the local coordinates.
`RationalPole` contains the position, residue, distance to the nearest zero, and a suspected-doublet flag.
A doublet is suspected if the distance to the nearest zero is at most $\tau(\max(|\zeta|, s))$ and the absolute residue is at most $\tau(v s)$.
Here $\tau(x) = \tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} x$ is the tolerance from the argument `doublet`, and $v = \max_j|a_j| / \max_j|b_j|$ is an indicator of the value scale.
Either condition alone can hold for a genuine pole, so both are required.
Polynomial conversion and eigenvalue computation follow a separate path involving $O(m^3)$ allocation; evaluation does not use it.
Leading coefficients no larger than the `policy` tolerance are removed before finding roots.
If small leading coefficients above the tolerance remain, poles near infinity appear at large absolute values, with uncertain positions.
If `policy` removes every denominator coefficient, the result is `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).
These diagnostics are numerical estimates, not proofs that poles exist.

`cleanup_doublets(nodes, values, policy, doublet, eigen_options)` removes the nearest support point for each pole classified as a doublet, then recomputes interpolatory weights once from the SVD of the Loewner matrix for the remaining support points.
It does not perform greedy stages to select new support points.
Pass the original samples (including every support point); if all support points would be removed, the result is `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).
The returned `RationalCleanup` contains the new approximation `approximant()`, the number of removed support points `removed_supports()`, and the maximum sample residuals before and after removal, `before_residual()` and `after_residual()`; it leaves the original model unchanged.
Removal can discard a genuine pole with a small residue, so check points outside the sample set before deciding whether to use the result.

## Approximating tan and tracing residual history

The following two cells approximate $\tan x$ with AAA from samples on $[-4, 4]$.
The samples are the 153 points satisfying $|\cos x| > 0.08$ on a grid with spacing 0.05, excluding the immediate neighborhoods of the poles $\pm\pi/2$.
Normal noise with standard deviation $\sigma$ can be added to the sample values; the default is no noise.
Both absolute and relative tolerances are $10^{-10}$; with a maximum absolute noiseless sample value of 8.24, the threshold is $9.2 \times 10^{-10}$.
Error is measured against noiseless $\tan x$ at points spaced by 0.025 that do not coincide with samples (again excluding neighborhoods of poles).
Because pole neighborhoods are excluded, this maximum error is not the uniform error over the entire interval.

The following block defines shared code for the two cells.
`tan_samples` returns nodes and noisy sample values, while `nearest_tan_pole` returns the true pole $\pi/2 + k\pi$ nearest $x$.
Noise is generated with SplitMix64 and the normal distribution from `scioxide-random`; changing the seed produces different noise.

```rust
//| id: interp-aaa-tan-samples
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "tan samples and true poles"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};
use std::f64::consts::{FRAC_PI_2, PI};

/// Half-width of the approximation domain [−4, 4].
pub(crate) const HALF_WIDTH: f64 = 4.0;

/// The test |cos x| > 0.08 excludes the immediate neighborhoods of the poles ±π/2.
pub(crate) fn away_from_poles(x: f64) -> bool {
    RealScalar::cos(x).abs() > 0.08
}

/// Nodes spaced by 0.05 and sample values formed by adding normal noise with standard deviation noise to tan x.
pub(crate) fn tan_samples(noise: f64, seed: u64) -> Result<(Vec<f64>, Vec<f64>), SciOxideError> {
    let nodes: Vec<f64> = (0..161)
        .map(|i| -HALF_WIDTH + i as f64 / 20.0)
        .filter(|&x| away_from_poles(x))
        .collect();
    let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed);
    let normal = Normal::new(0.0_f64, 1.0)?;
    let mut values = Vec::with_capacity(nodes.len());
    for &x in &nodes {
        values.push(RealScalar::tan(x) + noise * normal.sample(&mut rng)?);
    }
    Ok((nodes, values))
}

/// The true pole of tan nearest x, π/2 + kπ (a reference for independent checks).
pub(crate) fn nearest_tan_pole(x: f64) -> f64 {
    FRAC_PI_2 + RealScalar::round((x - FRAC_PI_2) / PI) * PI
}
```

The first cell takes the support limit, Lawson iteration limit, noise standard deviation $\sigma$, and noise seed as inputs.
It passes reserved workspace and output to `aaa_into` and outputs a table of termination status, iteration count, support count, maximum sample residual, maximum error between samples, singular-value ratio, and threshold, along with the residual history retained in the workspace.
The residual-history plot shows the threshold as a horizontal line.
Finally, it presents the termination status as a verdict and passes the approximation's support points and coefficients to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: interp-aaa-tan
//| ref: interp-aaa-tan
//| title: "Compare AAA termination status and residual history with Lawson iteration"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [interp-aaa-tan-samples]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| exports: [support, numerator, denominator, noise, seed]
//| inputs:
//|   terms: {"type":"range","label":"Support limit","min":4,"max":30,"step":1,"value":12}
//|   lawson: {"type":"range","label":"Lawson iteration limit","min":0,"max":30,"step":1,"value":0}
//|   noise: {"type":"select","label":"Noise standard deviation σ","options":[{"label":"None","value":"0"},{"label":"1e-8","value":"1e-8"},{"label":"1e-6","value":"1e-6"},{"label":"1e-4","value":"1e-4"}],"value":"0"}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Noise seed","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"support-limit","label":"Limit 6","description":"Stops at the support limit before reaching the threshold.","inputs":{"terms":6}}
//|   - {"id":"lawson","label":"Limit 6, 30 Lawson iterations","description":"Lawson iteration reduces the maximum sample residual but does not reduce the error between samples.","inputs":{"terms":6,"lawson":30}}
//|   - {"id":"noisy","label":"Limit 20, σ = 1e-8","description":"Uses all support points trying to fit noise, producing doublets.","inputs":{"terms":20,"noise":"1e-8"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":10}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":5.202700492645818e-10,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":9.238092752965618e-10,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: support-limit
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"SupportLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.7248159087641657e-3,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: lawson
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"IterationLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":36}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":9.130927013263346e-4,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":2.678151754855307e-3,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: noisy
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"SupportLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":20}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":1e-6,"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, Interval, RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_interp::{
    AaaOptions, RationalDomain, RationalFit, RationalRequest, RationalWorkspace, Univariate,
    aaa_into,
};

let sigma: f64 = noise
    .parse()
    .map_err(|_| format!("Cannot parse noise {noise} as a number"))?;
let (nodes, values) = tan_samples(sigma, seed as u64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tolerance = Tolerance::new(1e-10, 1e-10).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = AaaOptions::new()
    .with_max_terms(terms as usize)
    .with_tolerance(tolerance)
    .with_lawson_iterations(lawson as usize);
let domain =
    RationalDomain::Real(Interval::new(-HALF_WIDTH, HALF_WIDTH).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
// Determine requirements from the sample count and settings, reserve workspace and output, then solve
let request = RationalRequest::new(nodes.len(), options);
let requirements =
    RationalWorkspace::<f64>::workspace_requirements(request).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = RationalWorkspace::workspace(request).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut fit = RationalFit::new();
fit.try_reserve(&requirements).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
aaa_into(
    &nodes,
    &values,
    domain,
    options,
    &mut fit,
    &mut workspace,
    ExecutionContext::sequential_native(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let report = fit.report().ok_or("No solve report")?;
let model = fit.approximant().ok_or("No approximation")?;

    let x = -HALF_WIDTH + (i as f64 + 0.5) / 40.0;

        error = error.max((value - RealScalar::tan(x)).abs());
    }
}

let scale = values.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, v| m.max(v.abs()));
let threshold = tolerance.threshold(scale).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    { "key": "0", "label": "Termination" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Iterations", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Support count", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum sample residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum error between samples [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Singular-value ratio [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Threshold [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }

    format!("{termination:?}"),
    report.iterations(),
    model.support_points().len(),
    report.max_residual(),
    error,
    report.singular_ratio(),
    threshold,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "fit" })
);
let history: Vec<[f64; 2]> = workspace
    .residual_history()
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, &r)| [k as f64, r])
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({
        "name": "Maximum sample residual of accepted approximations",
        "points": history,
        "style": {"showSymbol": true}
    })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Accepted approximation index [-]",
        "yLabel": "Maximum residual at samples [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": threshold, "label": "Threshold"}]
    })
);
// Pass support points as sample indices. Passing floating-point numbers through JSON can change their least significant digits, so
// the later cell regenerates the same samples and retrieves support points exactly by index
let mut support = Vec::new();
for point in model.support_points() {
    support.push(
        nodes
            .iter()
            .position(|node| node == point)
            .ok_or("Support point is not in the samples")?,
    );
}
export!(support, support);
export!(numerator, model.numerator_weights());
export!(denominator, model.weights());
export!(noise, sigma);
export!(seed, seed);

let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The maximum sample residual reached the threshold or below")
        .explanation("The residual is measured only at samples; check the error between samples separately.")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped at the limit before reaching the threshold")
        .explanation("The published approximation is the one available when the limit was reached, with a maximum residual above the threshold.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{termination:?}"))
        .diagnostic("Iterations", report.iterations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Maximum sample residual", report.max_residual(), None)
        .diagnostic("Maximum error between samples", error, None)
);
```

With the default limit of 12, adding 10 support points produces `Converged`; the maximum sample residual is $5.20 \times 10^{-10}$, and the maximum error between samples is nearly the same at $5.20 \times 10^{-10}$.
The residual history contains 11 values, including the constant approximation.
In the first two stages, the maximum residual grows from 8.2 to 21.6, then drops rapidly from the third stage onward.
AAA reduces the sum of squared linearized residuals, not the maximum residual, so the maximum residual can increase at stages with few support points.
With a limit of 6, termination is `SupportLimit`, the maximum sample residual is $1.72 \times 10^{-3}$, the maximum error between samples is $1.73 \times 10^{-3}$, and the verdict is `partial`.
Setting the Lawson iteration limit to 30 here gives termination `IterationLimit` and 36 iterations: 6 support stages plus 30 Lawson stages.
The maximum sample residual falls to $9.13 \times 10^{-4}$, while the maximum error between samples grows to $2.68 \times 10^{-3}$.
Only the 12 accepted stages are added to the residual history; stages that did not reduce the maximum residual are not recorded.
Lawson iteration reduces the maximum residual at samples but need not reduce the error between them, so check the error at validation points separately.
If the support limit is at least 10, AAA converges first and Lawson iteration is not performed.

Adding noise $\sigma = 10^{-8}$ with a limit of 20 leaves the threshold $9.2 \times 10^{-10}$ one order of magnitude below the noise and unreachable; all 20 support points are used, and termination is `SupportLimit`.
In the residual history, the maximum residual falls to $4.8 \times 10^{-8}$ at 9 support points, then fluctuates as it follows noise, reaching $1.84 \times 10^{-5}$ at the twentieth stage.
The error against noiseless $\tan x$ between samples is $3.75 \times 10^{-6}$, far larger than the noise magnitude.
Adding support points does not improve the approximation; doublets appear throughout the approximation domain, as the next cell shows.
For noisy samples, do not set the threshold below the noise level.

### Checking estimated poles and residues

The poles of $\tan x$ are at $\pi/2 + k\pi$ and all have residue $-1$, allowing independent checks of estimated pole positions and residues.
The next cell regenerates the same samples using the noise settings received from the previous cell, reconstructs the same rational function from the received support indices and coefficients with `BarycentricRational::new`, and estimates poles and residues with `diagnostics`.
It does not solve AAA again, so changing the previous cell's inputs makes this cell examine the poles of that approximation.
Its input is the doublet-detection tolerance $\tau$ (the same value for relative and absolute tolerance), defaulting to $10^{-7}$.

The third table shows the result of `cleanup_doublets`: the number of suspected doublets, the number of removed support points, and the maximum sample residuals before and after removal.

## Summary

To fit a rational function from samples, use `aaa` for default settings or `aaa_with_options` to choose limits and tolerances.
`aaa` treats failure to converge as an error; with `aaa_with_options`, check that `report().termination()` is `Converged`.
In either case, separately check errors at points between samples.
Estimate pole positions and residues with `diagnostics`, but do not rely on poles far from the approximation domain or those arising from small leading coefficients.
For noisy samples, setting the threshold below the noise level produces doublets.
Match the threshold to the noise level; if doublets are suspected, compare residuals before and after `cleanup_doublets` and errors outside the sample set before deciding whether to use the result.
To reduce the maximum error, try `with_lawson_iterations` and examine errors at validation points, bearing in mind that the result is no longer interpolatory.
