This table covers rectangular-grid interpolation, scattered interpolation, and local models.
Grid `BilinearInterpolator` and `BicubicInterpolator` accept axis `x` of length $n_x$, axis `y` of length $n_y$, and row-major `ny × nx` values.
Entry `z[j·nx + i]` is the value at $(x_i, y_j)$.

Scattered interpolation has two families.
`RbfInterpolator` solves one dense system over all planar points, using their bounding rectangle as extrapolation domain.
Delaunay methods (nearest, simplex-linear, Clough–Tocher, natural neighbors) borrow and share `Delaunay` from `scioxide-spatial`, taking values as `ScatteredValues` bound to triangulation `MeshToken`.
All have standard `new(&mesh, &values)` construction and detailed `with_options` for settings.
For one query set, one-shot `interpolate_scattered` constructs triangulations and evaluates simplex-linear results.
These domains are convex hulls; nearest and simplex-linear support arbitrary dimensions, Clough–Tocher and natural neighbors only planes.

Local models use only neighbors around a center.
Build a KD tree through `LocalModelPlan` from `LocalSamples` or borrowed tables, then prepare `LocalPolynomialModel` or `LocalRbfModel` per center.
`GaussianProcessModel` prepares Gaussian-process (kriging) predictions from dense all-sample covariance; evaluate `GaussianPrediction` returned by `prediction`.
Local models and Gaussian processes use positive coordinate dimension `D` and return real-component `Vec` in owned evaluations; GP predictions append two variance components.

Dense-system methods (RBFs, local models, GPs) report factorizations and conditioning through `RbfConditioning` or `LocalFitReport`.
Direct reciprocal conditions are 1-norm estimates; SVD uses the 2-norm singular-value ratio, identified by `FitConditionNorm`.
Neither is an error bound.
Rank deficiency or missed minimum reciprocal condition gives `Singular` (non-positive-definite GP covariance gives `DomainError`); preparation or query-workspace excess gives `ResourceLimit`.
Query-workspace limits are checked when constructing interpolants.
Clough–Tocher estimates vertex gradients iteratively; standard `new` gives `ConvergenceFailure` without convergence.
Detailed `with_options` and `build` also return unconverged interpolants; inspect termination through `CloughTocherReport::termination()`.
GP predictive variance is statistical uncertainty, not a numerical-error guarantee.

## Summary

Use bilinear or bicubic for grids; gradients or Hessians require bicubic implementing `Differentiable`.
For scattered points, specify RBF kernels and regularization and inspect reciprocal conditions in `conditioning()`; Delaunay methods share triangulations and `ScatteredValues` within convex hulls.
Standard Clough–Tocher returns only converged gradients; detailed forms require checking `gradient_report().termination()` equals `Converged`.
Prepare local models per center with neighbor and degree ranges, inspect degree, condition, and residual in `fit_report`; specify GP covariances and means and read variances as statistics.
