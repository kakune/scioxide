Small position errors do not necessarily allow accurate recovery of velocity or acceleration.
With difference step $h$, independent position noise of standard deviation $\sigma$ is amplified to $\sqrt2\,\sigma/h$ in the first difference $(y_{i+1}-y_i)/h$ and $\sqrt6\,\sigma/h^2$ in the second difference $(y_{i+1}-2y_i+y_{i-1})/h^2$.
Differentiation amplifies higher-frequency components more strongly, making numerical differentiation of noisy data a typical ill-posed problem.
Use free fall with an analytically known trajectory to compare velocities and accelerations from interpolation through measurements and fitting that smooths noise.

## Model and reference values

Take height $y$ [m] positive upward and neglect air resistance.

$$
y(t)=20+2t-\frac12gt^2,\qquad
v(t)=2-gt,\qquad y''(t)=-g.
$$

$g=9.80665\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ is standard gravitational acceleration, used here as a validation constant.
This is not an experiment estimating actual local gravity.
Take $N$ equally spaced samples on $0\le t\le1\ \mathrm s$ and add independent normal noise with mean zero and standard deviation $\sigma$.
Compare velocities and accelerations at 81 interior points on $[0.1,0.9]\ \mathrm s$.
Near endpoints, every method has samples available on only one side, making derivative errors more likely to grow.

## Noise amplification by an interpolating spline

The second derivative of a cubic spline through measurements linearly connects its node values $M_i=s''(t_i)$.
At interior nodes with equal spacing $h$, $M_i$ satisfies the tridiagonal system

$$
\frac{M_{i-1}+4M_i+M_{i+1}}{6}=\frac{y_{i+1}-2y_i+y_{i-1}}{h^2}
$$

The trajectory is quadratic, so $M_i=-g$ holds exactly without noise.
The noise contribution is linear and can be solved by a discrete Fourier transform at nodes away from the endpoints.
A component of angular frequency $\theta$ is multiplied by $6(2\cos\theta-2)/[(4+2\cos\theta)h^2]$, giving

$$
\operatorname{Var}M_i=\frac{36\sigma^2}{h^4}\cdot\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2+\cos\theta}\right)^2d\theta=\frac{36\sigma^2}{h^4}
$$


To evaluate the integral, rewrite $(1-\cos\theta)/(2+\cos\theta)=3/(2+\cos\theta)-1$; using $\frac1{2\pi}\int d\theta/(2+\cos\theta)=1/\sqrt3$ and $\frac1{2\pi}\int d\theta/(2+\cos\theta)^2=2/(3\sqrt3)$ gives 1.
The same calculation gives correlation coefficient $\rho=3\sqrt3-6\approx-0.804$ between adjacent nodes.
At a point $t=t_i+sh$ ($0\le s\le1$) inside an interval, $s''=(1-s)M_i+sM_{i+1}$, so the acceleration-error variance is

$$
\frac{36\sigma^2}{h^4}\left[(1-s)^2+s^2+2s(1-s)\rho\right]
$$

and the root mean square (RMS) averaged over $s$ is $(6\sigma/h^2)\sqrt{\sqrt3-4/3}\approx3.79\,\sigma/h^2$.
For defaults $\sigma=0.02\ \mathrm m$ and $N=31$ ($h=1/30\ \mathrm s$), this is about $68\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, seven times $g$.
Interpolated acceleration is almost entirely dominated by noise and worsens as $h$ decreases (more samples).

## Three curves

The not-a-knot interpolating spline (`CubicSpline::new`) passes through the measurements.
The not-a-knot boundary condition makes third derivatives continuous at the second node from each end, so the two end intervals use the same cubic.

The penalized spline (`PenalizedSplinePlan`) fixes a cubic B-spline basis $N_j$ with simple knots dividing $[0,1]$ into eight equal intervals and minimizes

$$
\sum_i\bigl(y_i-f(t_i)\bigr)^2+\lambda\,\|\Delta^2c\|^2,\qquad f(t)=\sum_jc_jN_j(t)
$$

over the coefficients $c$ (an Eilers–Marx P-spline).
$\Delta^2c$ is the second difference of coefficients, and $\lambda$ is a dimensionless input.
Minimization solves the normal equations $(B^\top B+\lambda D_2^\top D_2)c=B^\top y$ ($B_{ij}=N_j(t_i)$, with second-difference matrix $D_2$) by banded Cholesky factorization.
The normal matrix depends only on sample times and penalty, so a Plan prepared once can be reused through `fit` for measurements with different noise.
Coefficients of a linear polynomial satisfy $\Delta^2c=0$ but coefficients of a quadratic polynomial do not, so the quadratic trajectory is also penalized, leaving bias at $\lambda>0$ even without noise.

Adaptive fitting passes scalar position values to the detailed form `fit_parametric_spline_with_options` and supplies times through `CurveParameterization::Supplied`.
With scalar values, this function fits position as a function of time.
Use the detailed form here because the one-shot function `fit_parametric_spline` supports only chord-length parameters and returns an error without a termination report if the target is unmet.
Fitting starts with one cubic polynomial piece; for target `SplineFitTarget::Residual(S)`, knots bisecting the interval with the largest residual sum are inserted until the residual sum of squares is at most $S$ plus tolerance.
It then increases the penalty from $\lambda=1$ by factors of 16 until finding a value that fails the condition, and bisects to find the largest penalty satisfying it.
Choosing $S=N\sigma^2$ matches the residual sum of squares to the expected noise sum of squares (Morozov's discrepancy principle).
With zero noise, use $S=10^{-16}\ \mathrm{m^2}$ as the lower bound; residual tests use absolute tolerance $10^{-14}\ \mathrm{m^2}$ and relative tolerance $10^{-5}$.
This choice assumes known noise variance and serves as a guide; it need not give statistically optimal smoothing strength.
For termination status `AdaptiveSplineTermination`, `is_success()` is true at `ResidualSatisfied`, indicating that the target was met and the penalty search finished.

`CubicSpline` does not implement the second-derivative trait `TwiceDifferentiable`.
Compute interpolated acceleration by centered differences of the gradient `derivative_at` using `physical_difference_derivative` from `scioxide-differentiate`.
The cubic-spline gradient is quadratic on each interval, so centered differences with a step of $10^{-5}$ times the interval width are exact apart from rounding.
The spline is $C^2$, so crossing a node with difference points does not change the result.
Both B-splines evaluate second derivatives directly with `hessian`.

The following block defines the model and fitting of the three curves shared by this page's two cells.
`height` and `velocity` define $y(t)$ and $v(t)$; `generator` and `noisy_heights` generate noisy samples from the random seed; `penalized_plan` prepares the eight-interval basis and penalty Plan; `adaptive_options` sets residual target $S=\max(N\sigma^2,10^{-16})$; `Curves::fit` fits the three curves; `Curves::derivatives` gives each curve's velocity and acceleration.

```rust
//| id: interp-noisy-velocity-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Free-fall model and three curves"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-differentiate, scioxide-interp, scioxide-random]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, Tolerance};
use scioxide_differentiate::physical_difference_derivative;
use scioxide_interp::{
    AdaptiveSplineOptions, BSpline, BSplineBasis, CubicSpline, CurveParameterization,
    ExtrapolationPolicy, ParametricSplineFit, PenalizedSplinePlan, SplineFitTarget, SplinePenalty,
    TwiceDifferentiable, Univariate, fit_parametric_spline_with_options,
};
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

/// Standard gravitational acceleration g [m/s²].
pub(crate) const GRAVITY: f64 = 9.80665;

/// Height y(t) = 20 + 2t − g t²/2 [m] and velocity v(t) = 2 − g t [m/s].
pub(crate) fn height(t: f64) -> f64 {
    20.0 + 2.0 * t - 0.5 * GRAVITY * t * t
}
pub(crate) fn velocity(t: f64) -> f64 {
    2.0 - GRAVITY * t
}

/// count equally spaced points on [0, 1] s and 81 comparison points on [0.1, 0.9] s.
pub(crate) fn sample_times(count: usize) -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..count).map(|i| i as f64 / (count - 1) as f64).collect()
}
pub(crate) fn evaluation_times() -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..81).map(|i| 0.1 + 0.8 * i as f64 / 80.0).collect()
}

/// Use seeded random numbers to add N(0, σ²) noise to the height at each time.
pub(crate) fn generator(seed: f64) -> SplitMix64 {
    SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64)
}
pub(crate) fn noisy_heights(
    times: &[f64],
    noise: f64,
    rng: &mut SplitMix64,
) -> Result<Vec<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let normal = Normal::new(0.0_f64, 1.0)?;
    let mut heights = Vec::with_capacity(times.len());
    for &t in times {
        heights.push(height(t) + noise * normal.sample(rng)?);
    }
    Ok(heights)
}

/// Cubic B-spline basis dividing [0, 1] into eight equal intervals, and Plan for penalty λ‖Δ²c‖².
pub(crate) fn penalized_plan(
    times: &[f64],
    strength: f64,
) -> Result<PenalizedSplinePlan<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let mut knots = vec![0.0; 4];
    knots.extend((1..8).map(|i| i as f64 / 8.0));
    knots.extend([1.0; 4]);
    let basis = BSplineBasis::new(knots, 3)?;
    PenalizedSplinePlan::new(
        basis,
        times.to_vec(),
        None,
        SplinePenalty::new(strength, 2)?,
    )
}

/// Residual-sum-of-squares target S = max(Nσ², 10⁻¹⁶) m² and its tolerances (absolute 10⁻¹⁴, relative 10⁻⁵).
pub(crate) fn adaptive_options(
    count: usize,
    noise: f64,
) -> Result<AdaptiveSplineOptions<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let target = (count as f64 * noise * noise).max(1e-16);
    Ok(AdaptiveSplineOptions::new()
        .with_target(SplineFitTarget::Residual(target))
        .with_residual_tolerance(Tolerance::new(1e-14, 1e-5)?))
}

/// Three curves fitted to the same samples: interpolation, fixed penalty, and adaptive residual target.
pub(crate) struct Curves {
    pub(crate) interpolation: CubicSpline<f64>,
    pub(crate) penalized: BSpline<f64>,
    pub(crate) adaptive: ParametricSplineFit<f64>,
    step: f64,
}

impl Curves {
    pub(crate) fn fit(
        times: &[f64],
        heights: &[f64],
        plan: &PenalizedSplinePlan<f64>,
        options: AdaptiveSplineOptions<f64>,
    ) -> Result<Self, SciOxideError> {
        let supplied = CurveParameterization::Supplied(times);
        Ok(Self {
            interpolation: CubicSpline::new(times.to_vec(), heights.to_vec())?,
            penalized: plan.fit(heights)?.into_spline(),
            adaptive: fit_parametric_spline_with_options(heights, supplied, None, options)?,
            step: 1e-5 * (times[1] - times[0]),
        })
    }

    /// Each curve's [velocity, acceleration]. Interpolation acceleration uses centered gradient differences; B-splines use Hessians.
    pub(crate) fn derivatives(&self, t: f64) -> Result<[[f64; 2]; 3], SciOxideError> {
        let policy = ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject;
        let h = self.step;
        let difference = physical_difference_derivative(
            |q| self.interpolation.derivative_at(q),
            t,
            &[t - h, t + h],
        )?;
        let adaptive = self.adaptive.spline();
        Ok([
            [self.interpolation.derivative_at(t)?, difference],
            [
                self.penalized.derivative_at(t)?,
                self.penalized.hessian([t], policy)?[0][0],
            ],
            [
                adaptive.derivative_at(t)?,
                adaptive.hessian([t], policy)?[0][0],
            ],
        ])
    }
}
```

## Velocity and acceleration from one measurement

The next cell constructs the three curves from one set of noisy samples and compares velocity and acceleration.
Inputs are noise standard deviation $\sigma$ [m], sample count $N$, fixed penalty strength $\lambda$, and random seed.
The first table shows each method's velocity and acceleration RMS errors; the second shows adaptive-fit termination status, residual sum of squares and target, inserted knots, and penalty.
The upper plot shows velocity and the lower plot acceleration, with linked horizontal time axes.
The theoretical values $v(t)=2-gt$ and $y''=-g$ appear as dashed lines.
This cell's four inputs are also passed to the repetition experiment below.

```rust
//| id: interp-noisy-velocity-smoothing
//| ref: interp-noisy-velocity-smoothing
//| title: "Compare velocities from interpolation and smoothing"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [interp-noisy-velocity-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-differentiate, scioxide-interp, scioxide-random]
//| exports: [noise, samples, strength, seed]
//| inputs:
//|   noise: {"type":"range","label":"Position-noise standard deviation σ [m]","min":0.0,"max":0.1,"step":0.005,"value":0.02}
//|   samples: {"type":"range","label":"Sample count N [-]","min":21.0,"max":61.0,"step":10.0,"value":31.0}
//|   strength: {"type":"range","label":"Fixed penalty strength λ [-]","min":0.0,"max":2.0,"step":0.05,"value":0.1}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1.0,"max":100.0,"step":1.0,"value":7.0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"noise-free","label":"σ = 0","description":"Interpolation reproduces the trajectory at rounding level, while fixed-penalty fitting retains only bias.","inputs":{"noise":0}}
//|   - {"id":"dense-samples","label":"N = 61","description":"Increasing samples increases interpolation acceleration errors as h⁻².","inputs":{"samples":61}}

//|       - {"artifact":"adaptive","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":4}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/4/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: noise-free
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.09625,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/4/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: dense-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":281.9,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":0.02330,"rtol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

let count = samples as usize;

let mut rng = generator(seed);
let heights = noisy_heights(&times, noise, &mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let curves = Curves::fit(&times, &heights, &plan, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Evaluate velocity and acceleration at 81 points and compute RMS errors against theoretical values
let mut velocity_lines = [Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
let mut acceleration_lines = [Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
let mut squares = [[0.0; 2]; 3];
for t in evaluation_times() {
    let methods = curves.derivatives(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    for (j, [v, a]) in methods.into_iter().enumerate() {
        velocity_lines[j].push([t, v]);
        acceleration_lines[j].push([t, a]);
        squares[j][0] += (v - velocity(t)) * (v - velocity(t)) / 81.0;
        squares[j][1] += (a + GRAVITY) * (a + GRAVITY) / 81.0;

    velocity_lines[3].push([t, velocity(t)]);
    acceleration_lines[3].push([t, -GRAVITY]);
}
let names = ["Interpolant", "Fixed penalty", "Adaptive residual target"];
let rows: Vec<_> = (0..3)
    .map(|j| {
        let [v, a] = squares[j];
        (names[j], RealScalar::sqrt(v), RealScalar::sqrt(a))
    })
    .collect();

let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Velocity RMS error [m/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },

]);

    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);

let report = curves.adaptive.report();
let target = (count as f64 * noise * noise).max(1e-16);
let report_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Termination" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Residual sum of squares [m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Target S [m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Inserted knots [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Penalty λ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
let report_rows = vec![(
    format!("{:?}", report.termination()),
    report.residual(),
    target,
    report.refinements(),
    report.penalty(),
)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    report_columns,
    report_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "adaptive" })
);

let series = |lines: &[Vec<[f64; 2]>; 4], theory: &str| {
    let mut list: Vec<_> = (0..3)
        .map(|j| serde_json::json!({ "name": names[j], "points": lines[j] }))
        .collect();
    list.push(serde_json::json!({
        "name": theory,
        "points": lines[3],
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }));
    list
};
emit_line_chart!(
    &series(&velocity_lines, "Theoretical v = 2 − gt"),
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "fall-time" },
        "xLabel": "Time t [s]",
        "yLabel": "Velocity v [m/s]"
    })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &series(&acceleration_lines, "Theoretical y'' = −g"),
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "fall-time" },
        "xLabel": "Time t [s]",
        "yLabel": "Acceleration y'' [m/s²]"
    })
);
export!(noise, noise);
export!(samples, samples);
export!(strength, strength);
export!(seed, seed);

let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Adaptive fitting met the residual-sum-of-squares target")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Adaptive fitting stopped before meeting the target")
        .explanation("The returned curve is the last accepted one; reconsider the target or limit.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{termination:?}"))
        .diagnostic("Residual sum of squares", report.residual(), Some("m²"))
        .diagnostic("Target", target, Some("m²"))
        .diagnostic("Inserted knots", report.refinements() as f64, None)
);
```

With defaults ($\sigma=0.02\ \mathrm m$, $N=31$, $\lambda=0.1$, seed 7), interpolation has velocity RMS error 1.27 m/s and acceleration RMS error 86.8 m/s²; acceleration is buried in noise about nine times $g$.
Velocity errors for fixed-penalty and adaptive residual-target fitting are 0.140 and 0.135 m/s, about one ninth of interpolation's error; acceleration errors also fall to 2.1 and 2.5 m/s².
Adaptive fitting inserts four knots and uses penalty $\lambda\approx4.7\times10^{-3}$ to match the residual sum of squares to the target 0.0124 m², terminating with `ResidualSatisfied`.
The lower plot shows interpolated acceleration fluctuating strongly around the dashed $-g$ line while both smoothing methods stay nearby, allowing comparisons at the same times as in the velocity plot above.

At $\sigma=0$, the interpolating spline reproduces the quadratic trajectory at rounding level (velocity error $6\times10^{-14}$ m/s), while adaptive fitting has velocity error $8\times10^{-8}$ m/s.
Fixed-penalty fitting retains velocity RMS error 0.096 m/s and acceleration error 1.34 m/s² even without noise, showing penalty-induced bias.
Setting $N=61$ increases interpolation acceleration error to 282 m/s².
The theoretical RMS $3.79\,\sigma/h^2$ quadruples (from 68 to 273 m/s²) when $h$ is halved; individual measurements fluctuate around it.
Changing penalty strength or seed can reverse the two smoothing methods' ranking (with seed 1, fixed-penalty velocity error 0.086 m/s is smaller than adaptive fitting's 0.095 m/s).
A single-measurement comparison depends on the noise sequence; the next section compares repeated measurements.

## Estimating gravitational acceleration through repetition

Average each method's second derivative over 81 points on $[0.1,0.9]\ \mathrm s$ and reverse its sign to estimate gravitational acceleration as $\hat g$.
A single measurement's $\hat g$ is a noise-dependent random variable, so repeat independent measurements $R$ times and compare the mean and standard error (SE) with the true value.
SE is the sample standard deviation of the $R$ estimates divided by $\sqrt R$; use mean ±1.96 SE as an approximate 95% confidence interval.
For acceleration RMS error, average mean squared errors across $R$ repetitions before taking the square root.
For the interpolating spline, also report the theoretical variance formula derived above, averaged over the same 81 points.
The estimated mean minus $g$ represents bias, SE represents variance magnitude, and mean squared error includes both.

The next cell receives $\sigma$, $N$, $\lambda$, and random seed from the previous cell; its sole input is repetition count $R$.
Starting from the same seed makes the first repetition's samples identical to the previous cell's samples.
The penalized-spline Plan depends only on sample times and penalty, so prepare it once and reuse it for each repetition's `fit`.
The table shows each method's mean $\hat g$, 1.96 SE, difference between the mean and $g$, acceleration RMS error, interpolation's theoretical value, and fraction of adaptive fits meeting the target; the plot shows mean $\hat g$ and 95% intervals.

```rust
//| id: interp-noisy-velocity-gravity
//| ref: interp-noisy-velocity-gravity
//| title: "Check gravitational-acceleration estimates and noise amplification through repetition"
//| run: button
//| uses: [interp-noisy-velocity-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-differentiate, scioxide-interp, scioxide-random]
//| dependsOn:
//|   single: interp-noisy-velocity-smoothing
//| inputs:
//|   replicates: {"type":"range","label":"Repetition count R [-]","min":10.0,"max":100.0,"step":10.0,"value":40.0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"many-replicates","label":"R = 100","description":"More repetitions shrink 1.96 SE as 1/√R, making bias clearer.","inputs":{"replicates":100}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gravity","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":69.513,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gravity","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":69.329,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gravity","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.4695,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gravity","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":9.4785,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gravity","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":-0.32815,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gravity","path":"/rows/2/6","equals":1}
//|   - case: many-replicates
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gravity","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.03964,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gravity","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":69.505,"rtol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

let number = |key: &str| single[key].as_f64().ok_or(format!("Missing {key}"));
let (noise, strength) = (number("noise")?, number("strength")?);
let count = number("samples")? as usize;
let times = sample_times(count);
let h = times[1] - times[0];
// The Plan depends only on sample times and penalty, so reuse it across repetitions
let plan = penalized_plan(&times, strength).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = adaptive_options(count, noise).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let evaluation = evaluation_times();
let mut rng = generator(number("seed")?);
// Each method's [ĝ, acceleration mean squared error] per repetition, and adaptive-fit success count
let mut records = [Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
let mut satisfied = 0;
for _ in 0..replicates as usize {
    let heights = noisy_heights(&times, noise, &mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let curves = Curves::fit(&times, &heights, &plan, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if curves.adaptive.report().termination().is_success() {
        satisfied += 1;
    }
    let mut sums = [[0.0; 2]; 3];
    for &t in &evaluation {
        let methods = curves.derivatives(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        for (sum, [_, a]) in sums.iter_mut().zip(methods) {
            sum[0] -= a / 81.0;
            sum[1] += (a + GRAVITY) * (a + GRAVITY) / 81.0;
        }
    }
    for (record, sum) in records.iter_mut().zip(sums) {
        record.push(sum);
    }
}
// Independent check: theoretical interpolation acceleration-error RMS (approximation for nodes away from endpoints, ρ = 3√3 − 6)
let rho = 3.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(3.0) - 6.0;
let mut shape = 0.0;
for &t in &evaluation {
    let s = RealScalar::fract(t / h);
    shape += ((1.0 - s) * (1.0 - s) + s * s + 2.0 * s * (1.0 - s) * rho) / 81.0;
}
let predicted = 6.0 * noise / (h * h) * RealScalar::sqrt(shape);
let r = replicates;
let names = ["Interpolant", "Fixed penalty", "Adaptive residual target"];

for (i, record) in records.iter().enumerate() {
    let mean = record.iter().map(|x| x[0]).sum::<f64>() / r;
    let deviations = record.iter().map(|x| (x[0] - mean) * (x[0] - mean));
    let half = 1.96 * RealScalar::sqrt(deviations.sum::<f64>() / (r - 1.0) / r);
    let rms = RealScalar::sqrt(record.iter().map(|x| x[1]).sum::<f64>() / r);
    let theory = (i == 0).then_some(predicted);
    let success = (i == 2).then_some(satisfied as f64 / r);
    rows.push((names[i], mean, half, mean - GRAVITY, rms, theory, success));
    bars.push([(i + 1) as f64, mean, mean - half, mean + half]);
}
```

With defaults ($\sigma=0.02\ \mathrm m$, $N=31$, $\lambda=0.1$, $R=40$, seed 7), interpolation acceleration RMS error is 69.5 m/s², agreeing with the theoretical 69.3 m/s² within 0.3%.
Setting $N=21$ and $N=61$ in the previous cell gives observed and theoretical values 31.8 and 31.5 m/s², and 285.7 and 283.2 m/s², differing by less than 1% and confirming growth proportional to $\sigma/h^2$.
Interpolation gives $\hat g$ of $9.77\pm0.47\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ (mean ±1.96 SE); the interval includes $g$ but is more than five times as wide as the smoothing intervals.
The standard deviation of a single measurement's $\hat g$ is SE $\times\sqrt R\approx1.5\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, much smaller than the acceleration error at one point.
Adjacent-node $M_i$ values are negatively correlated ($\rho\approx-0.80$), so positive and negative contributions cancel when averaged over 81 points.

Fixed-penalty $\hat g$ is $9.48\pm0.06\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, with an interval excluding $g$.
The difference $-0.33\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ is bias from penalty $\lambda\|\Delta^2c\|^2$ reducing curvature.
Setting $\sigma=0$ in the previous cell leaves the difference unchanged at $-0.328\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, so this bias comes from the penalty rather than noise.
Setting $\lambda=0$ gives $9.87\pm0.18$, and the interval includes $g$.
The adaptive residual-target $\hat g$ is also $9.65\pm0.08$, significantly below $g$, and all 40 repetitions meet the target.
Its acceleration RMS error of 6.45 m/s² exceeds the fixed-penalty value of 1.86 m/s²; less bias does not necessarily imply smaller mean squared error.
Setting $R=100$ shrinks 1.96 SE by a factor of $1/\sqrt{2.5}\approx0.63$ (interpolation from 0.47 to 0.32, fixed penalty from 0.064 to 0.040 m/s²).
Smoothing reduces variance at the cost of bias; inspect both the mean and SE to judge which error dominates.

## Summary

Position noise is amplified at rates $1/h$ by first differentiation and $1/h^2$ by second differentiation; acceleration-error RMS for an equally spaced interpolating spline is $3.79\,\sigma/h^2$.
The interpolating spline passes through measurements and is unbiased, but noise dominates acceleration and more samples actually worsen it.
Penalized splines and residual-target fitting greatly reduce variance, but penalties also bias quadratic trajectories, making gravitational-acceleration estimates smaller than the true value.
When choosing smoothing strength, check bias and SE together on a known trajectory rather than judging only from residual sums of squares.
For adaptive fitting, inspect termination status, residual sum of squares and target, inserted knots, and penalty in the report.
