In geological surveys and environmental observations, measurement points often scatter across a plane rather than lying on a grid.
Bilinear and bicubic grid interpolation based on cell boundaries cannot be applied directly to these scattered points.
This example builds terrain from 16 scattered elevations using radial basis functions (RBFs) and measures errors at unused points against the true terrain.
Examine separately how shape parameter, diagonal regularization, and subtraction of the observation mean change sample-point fitting, unused-point errors, and matrix conditioning.

The radial basis function (RBF) method constructs a surface by superposing kernels $\phi(r)$ depending only on distance $r = \|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_j\|_2$ from each sample point.
It constructs smooth surfaces from scattered points without geometric preprocessing such as triangulation.
`RbfInterpolator` takes two-dimensional points `[x, y]`, their values, and a kernel.
Set regularization and the numerical-rank threshold through the setters on `RbfOptions` and pass it to `RbfInterpolator::with_options(points, values, kernel, options)`.

## The RBF system and positive-definite kernels

Given $n$ sample points $\mathbf{p}_j = (x_j, y_j)$ and elevations $z_j$, the RBF interpolating surface is

$$
s(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{j=1}^n c_j \phi(\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_j\|_2)
$$


Imposing $s(\mathbf{p}_i) = z_i$ at every sample point gives the coefficients $\mathbf{c}$ through the linear system

$$
\Phi \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{z}, \qquad \Phi_{ij} = \phi(\|\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j\|_2)
$$

this system.
$\Phi$ is a symmetric dense $n\times n$ matrix; preparation requires $O(n^2)$ memory and $O(n^3)$ computation.
Evaluation costs $O(n)$ per point.

This example uses the Gaussian kernel

$$
\phi(r) = \exp\left( - (\varepsilon r)^2 \right)
$$


$\varepsilon > 0$ is the shape parameter, with $1/\varepsilon$ setting the basis width.
The argument $c$ of `RbfKernel::Gaussian(c)` is this $\varepsilon$.
The Gaussian Fourier transform is positive, so Bochner's theorem makes $\Phi$ positive definite for distinct points (Wendland, Chapter 6).
`RbfInterpolator` therefore solves Gaussian-kernel matrices by Cholesky factorization.
If Cholesky breaks down or fails numerical-rank and reciprocal-condition checks, it refactors using singular value decomposition (SVD).

`RbfInterpolator` uses only the kernel sum, without adding constant or linear terms.
A sum of Gaussian kernels approaches zero far from sample points, so directly fitting terrain near elevation 120 m tends to pull values toward zero between and outside the points.
Fitting $z_j-\bar z$ after subtracting the observation mean $\bar z$ and adding $\bar z$ to evaluated values makes distant values approach $\bar z$ instead of zero.
The extrapolation region is defined by the rectangle of coordinate minima and maxima, rather than the sample convex hull.

## Shape parameter and reciprocal condition number

The shape parameter $\varepsilon$ determines both surface shape and matrix conditioning.

| Shape parameter | Basis width | Effects on matrix and approximation |
|---|---|---|
| Large | Localized near samples | The matrix approaches the identity and becomes well conditioned, but values tend to dip between samples |
| Small | Flat and overlapping over long distances | Similar columns make the matrix ill conditioned, and coefficients become sensitive to rounding error and noise |

A reciprocal condition number $\kappa^{-1}(\Phi)=1/(\|\Phi\|\,\|\Phi^{-1}\|)$ near 1 indicates good conditioning; near zero, the solution becomes sensitive to input errors.
Its value depends on the chosen norm.
The `RbfConditioning` returned by `conditioning()` reports a 1-norm estimate for direct Cholesky or LU factorization, or the 2-norm value $\sigma_{\min}/\sigma_{\max}$ for SVD.
It describes the matrix actually solved ($\Phi+\lambda I$ from the next section when regularized).
Check `condition_norm()` and `factorization()` to identify which value is reported.
The 1-norm value is an estimate, not a proof of an error bound.

The numerical-rank threshold `RankThreshold::new(atol, rtol)` determines whether a direct factorization is accepted.
If the direct factorization's reciprocal condition number falls below $\mathrm{rtol}$ (when $\mathrm{atol}=0$), it switches to SVD; if fewer than $n$ singular values exceed $\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\,\sigma_{\max}$ in SVD, construction stops with an error of `Singular`.
The default threshold $\mathrm{atol}=\mathrm{rtol}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\approx2.2\times10^{-16}$ rejects only matrices singular at working precision.
This example sets $\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-12}$, rejecting matrices with condition numbers above $10^{12}$.

## Diagonal regularization

If observed elevations contain measurement errors $e_i$, a surface obtained by solving $\Phi \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{z}$ exactly passes through those errors too.
`RbfRegularization::Diagonal(λ)` adds $\lambda>0$ to the kernel matrix diagonal and solves

$$
(\Phi + \lambda I) \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{z}
$$


If $\Phi$ is positive definite, this is the solution of

$$
\min_{\mathbf{c}} \left( \|\Phi \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{z}\|_2^2 + \lambda\, \mathbf{c}^\mathsf{T} \Phi \mathbf{c} \right)
$$


Indeed, setting the objective's gradient to zero gives $\Phi\bigl((\Phi+\lambda I)\mathbf c-\mathbf z\bigr)=\mathbf 0$; since $\Phi$ is nonsingular, this agrees with the system above.
The first term is the sample-point fitting residual; the second is the squared norm $\|s\|^2$ in the function space defined by the kernel (a reproducing kernel Hilbert space), representing the magnitude of surface variation.
Increasing $\lambda$ permits larger sample residuals $|s(\mathbf{p}_i) - z_i|$ in exchange for smaller surface variations in response to noise.
This trades increased bias for reduced variance.
The eigenvalues of $\Phi+\lambda I$ are those of $\Phi$ plus $\lambda$, placing the smallest eigenvalue at least at $\lambda$ and improving conditioning.
Gaussian-kernel matrix entries are at most 1, so the largest eigenvalue is at most $n$, and the 2-norm condition number is at most $(n+\lambda)/\lambda$.
For this example's $n=16$ and $\lambda\ge10^{-10}$, this bound is at most $1.6\times10^{11}$, below the threshold $10^{12}$ above.
Specify $\lambda$ in the same units as the kernel matrix (dimensionless for the Gaussian kernel).

## Terrain and observations for independent checks

The true terrain is a sum of a hill, a valley, and a gentle slope on the unit square $[0,1]^2$:

$$
z(x,y)=120+45e^{-18[(x-0.32)^2+(y-0.62)^2]}-28e^{-24[(x-0.76)^2+(y-0.36)^2]}+8x-4y\quad[\mathrm m]
$$


$x$ and $y$ are dimensionless east–west and north–south coordinates.
The hill and valley widths (distances at which they become $e^{-1}$) are about 0.24 and 0.20, respectively, comparable to the spacing of the 16 points (about 0.3).
Add an error $a\sin(1.7k+0.3)$ determined by point index $k$ to each observation.
$a$ is the error amplitude, and the root mean square error across the 16 points is approximately $a/\sqrt2$.
Since the true terrain is known, errors can be measured on a $31\times31$ validation grid that, except for its four corners, does not coincide with samples.
This provides the example's independent check.

The following block defines quantities shared by this page's three cells.
`sample_points` gives the 16 coordinates; `terrain` defines the true terrain $z(x,y)$; `observations` gives observations with added errors; `validation_grid` supplies the validation grid; `fit_terrain` fits with mean subtraction and regularization.
Terrain and observations are generated with `RealScalar::exp` and `RealScalar::sin`, without f64 inherent methods.

```rust
//| id: interp-rbf-terrain-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Scattered points, true terrain, observations, and RBF fitting"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RankThreshold, RealScalar};
use scioxide_interp::{RbfInterpolator, RbfKernel, RbfOptions, RbfRegularization};

/// 16 scattered observation coordinates [x, y] (x east–west, y north–south, dimensionless).
pub(crate) fn sample_points() -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    let xs = [
        0.0, 0.32, 0.68, 1.0, 0.0, 0.24, 0.61, 1.0, 0.0, 0.39, 0.76, 1.0, 0.0, 0.29, 0.71, 1.0,
    ];
    let ys = [
        0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.34, 0.28, 0.38, 0.31, 0.67, 0.58, 0.72, 0.64, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0,
    ];
    xs.into_iter().zip(ys).map(|(x, y)| [x, y]).collect()
}

/// True terrain z(x, y) [m]: a hill, a valley, and a gentle slope.
pub(crate) fn terrain(x: f64, y: f64) -> f64 {
    let hill = RealScalar::exp(-18.0 * ((x - 0.32) * (x - 0.32) + (y - 0.62) * (y - 0.62)));
    let valley = RealScalar::exp(-24.0 * ((x - 0.76) * (x - 0.76) + (y - 0.36) * (y - 0.36)));
    120.0 + 45.0 * hill - 28.0 * valley + 8.0 * x - 4.0 * y
}

/// Observations [m] = true terrain + point-dependent error a sin(1.7k + 0.3).
pub(crate) fn observations(noise: f64) -> Vec<f64> {
    sample_points()
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(k, p)| terrain(p[0], p[1]) + noise * RealScalar::sin(1.7 * k as f64 + 0.3))
        .collect()
}

/// 31×31 validation grid [x, y] (x varies fastest).
pub(crate) fn validation_grid() -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    (0..31 * 31)
        .map(|k| [(k % 31) as f64 / 30.0, (k / 31) as f64 / 30.0])
        .collect()
}

/// Fit after subtracting the observation mean z̄ (if subtract_mean is true). Add z̄ to evaluated values.
pub(crate) fn fit_terrain(
    shape: f64,
    lambda: f64,
    observed: &[f64],
    subtract_mean: bool,
) -> Result<(RbfInterpolator<f64>, f64), SciOxideError> {
    let offset = if subtract_mean {
        observed.iter().sum::<f64>() / observed.len() as f64
    } else {
        0.0
    };
    let shifted: Vec<f64> = observed.iter().map(|z| z - offset).collect();
    let options = RbfOptions::new()
        .with_regularization(RbfRegularization::Diagonal(lambda))
        .with_rank_threshold(RankThreshold::new(0.0, 1.0e-12)?);
    let kernel = RbfKernel::Gaussian(shape);
    let rbf = RbfInterpolator::with_options(sample_points(), shifted, kernel, options)?;
    Ok((rbf, offset))
}
```

```rust

//| ref: interp-rbf-terrain
//| title: "Vary RBF kernel width, regularization, measurement noise, and cross-section position"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [interp-rbf-terrain-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]

//|   terrain_noise_m: {"type":"range","label":"Observation-error amplitude a [m]","description":"Amplitude of the point-dependent error a sin(1.7k + 0.3).","min":0,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":2}

//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":4.8e-4,"rtol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.39,"atol":0.01}}

//|       - {"artifact":"conditioning","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":16}
//|   - case: strong-regularization
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":3.8,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":5.82,"atol":0.01}}
//|   - case: narrow-kernel
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":43.9,"atol":0.1}}
//|   - case: narrow-kernel-mean
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":6.69,"atol":0.01}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{ExtrapolationPolicy, Interpolant};

let lambda = RealScalar::powf(10.0, regularization_exponent);
let observed = observations(terrain_noise_m);
let (rbf, offset) = fit_terrain(rbf_shape_parameter, lambda, &observed, subtract_mean)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let at = |x: f64, y: f64| {
    rbf.evaluate([x, y], ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject)
        .map(|v| v + offset)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};

// Reconstruction and true terrain on the east–west cross section y = y*
let (mut section, mut exact) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for i in 0..=160 {
    let x = f64::from(i) / 160.0;
    section.push([x, at(x, cross_section_y)?]);
    exact.push([x, terrain(x, cross_section_y)]);
}
// Independent check: measure signed errors against true terrain on the validation grid
let mut error_map = Vec::new();
for p in validation_grid() {
    error_map.push([p[0], p[1], at(p[0], p[1])? - terrain(p[0], p[1])]);
}
let squared: f64 = error_map.iter().map(|e| e[2] * e[2]).sum();
let worst = error_map.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, e| m.max(e[2].abs()));
// Sample-point fitting residuals (differences from observations) and differences from true terrain
let (mut fit_residual, mut node_squared) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
for (p, &z) in sample_points().iter().zip(&observed) {
    let fitted = at(p[0], p[1])?;
    fit_residual = fit_residual.max((fitted - z).abs());
    let node_error = fitted - terrain(p[0], p[1]);
    node_squared += node_error * node_error;
}
let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Value [m]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 }

    vec![

        ("Sample-point RMSE against true terrain", RealScalar::sqrt(node_squared / 16.0)),
        ("Validation-grid RMSE", RealScalar::sqrt(squared / error_map.len() as f64)),
        ("Validation-grid maximum error", worst),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "accuracy" })
);
let report = rbf.conditioning();
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Factorization" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Norm" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Reciprocal condition [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Numerical rank", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    vec![(
        format!("{:?}", report.factorization()),
        format!("{:?}", report.condition_norm()),
        report.reciprocal_condition_number(),
        report.numerical_rank(),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "conditioning" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "RBF reconstruction", "points": section }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "True terrain",
            "points": exact,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({ "xLabel": "East–west position x [-]", "yLabel": "Elevation z [m]" })
);
export!(error_map, &error_map);
```

With defaults ($\varepsilon = 2$, $\lambda = 10^{-6}$, $a=2$ m, no mean subtraction), the maximum sample-point fitting residual is $4.8 \times 10^{-4}$ m, validation-grid RMSE is 5.97 m, and maximum error is 16.7 m.
The sample-point RMSE against true terrain, 1.39 m, nearly equals the added errors' RMS $a/\sqrt2\approx1.41$ m, showing that the surface passes through samples including their errors.
Cholesky solves the matrix, with 1-norm reciprocal-condition estimate $1.76\times10^{-3}$ and numerical rank 16.

Setting $\lambda = 10^{-10}$ lowers residuals to $4.8 \times 10^{-8}$ m but leaves validation-grid RMSE unchanged at 5.97 m.
At $\lambda = 10^{-2}$, residuals grow to 3.8 m and sample-point RMSE against true terrain to 1.85 m, but validation-grid RMSE decreases slightly to 5.82 m.
Even with errors set to $a=0$, validation-grid RMSE is 5.92 m; at $a=8$ m, it becomes 7.90 m.
For this point configuration, most unused-point error comes from 16 points inadequately representing the hill and valley, rather than from noise.

At $\varepsilon=6$, the basis width of about 0.17 is narrower than point spacing, pulling the surface toward zero between points; validation-grid RMSE becomes 43.9 m and maximum error 100 m.
The reciprocal condition number rises to 0.70, and the matrix is close to the identity.
Mean subtraction lowers RMSE to 6.69 m at the same $\varepsilon=6$; even at the default $\varepsilon=2$, it reduces RMSE from 5.97 m to 4.94 m.
Cross-section position $y^*$ changes only the plot, leaving table values unchanged.

## Scanning shape parameters and validation errors

The kernel matrix $\Phi+\lambda I$ depends only on point configuration, kernel, and $\lambda$, not on observation values.
Here we verify that good conditioning and accuracy at unused points are separate properties.
The next cell varies $\varepsilon$ from 0.8 to 6.0 in increments of 0.4, builds an RBF from observations at each value, and measures reciprocal condition number, maximum sample residual, and validation-grid RMSE.
Inputs are regularization exponent $q$, error amplitude $a$, and whether to subtract the mean; this experiment is independent of the cell above.
Both plots have logarithmic vertical axes, with linked horizontal axes for $\varepsilon$.
The first cell of [RBF cross-validation with the Franke function](../station-cross-validation/) also examines how shape parameter and regularization affect reciprocal condition number.

```rust
//| id: interp-rbf-shape-accuracy
//| ref: interp-rbf-shape-accuracy
//| title: "Sweep the shape parameter to compare sample residuals and validation errors"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [interp-rbf-terrain-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   regularization_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Regularization exponent q (λ=10ᑫ)","description":"Change λ added to the diagonal.","min":-10,"max":-2,"step":0.5,"value":-6}
//|   terrain_noise_m: {"type":"range","label":"Observation-error amplitude a [m]","description":"Amplitude of the point-dependent error a sin(1.7k + 0.3).","min":0,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//|   subtract_mean: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Subtract the observation mean before fitting","value":false}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"tiny-regularization","label":"λ = 10⁻¹⁰","description":"Residuals for small ε decrease, but RMSE does not improve.","inputs":{"regularization_exponent":-10}}
//|   - {"id":"mean-removed","label":"Subtract mean","description":"Minimum RMSE decreases, and optimal ε moves upward. Reciprocal condition numbers are unchanged.","inputs":{"subtract_mean":true}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/3/3","approx":{"value":5.97,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.70,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/13/3","approx":{"value":43.9,"atol":0.1}}
//|   - case: tiny-regularization
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":8.99,"atol":0.01}}
//|   - case: mean-removed
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/7/3","approx":{"value":2.67,"atol":0.01}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{ExtrapolationPolicy, Interpolant};

let lambda = RealScalar::powf(10.0, regularization_exponent);
let observed = observations(terrain_noise_m);
let grid = validation_grid();
let (mut rows, mut rmse_curve, mut rcond_curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for step in 0..=13 {
    let shape = f64::from(8 + 4 * step) / 10.0;
    let (rbf, offset) =
        fit_terrain(shape, lambda, &observed, subtract_mean).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let at = |p: &[f64; 2]| {
        rbf.evaluate(*p, ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject)
            .map(|v| v + offset)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
    };
    let mut residual = 0.0_f64;
    for (p, &z) in sample_points().iter().zip(&observed) {
        residual = residual.max((at(p)? - z).abs());
    }
    // Independent check: measure errors against true terrain on the validation grid
    let mut squared = 0.0_f64;
    for p in &grid {
        let error = at(p)? - terrain(p[0], p[1]);
        squared += error * error;
    }
    let rmse = RealScalar::sqrt(squared / grid.len() as f64);
    let rcond = rbf.conditioning().reciprocal_condition_number();
    rmse_curve.push([shape, rmse]);
    rcond_curve.push([shape, rcond]);
    rows.push((shape, rcond, residual, rmse));
}
```

With defaults ($\lambda=10^{-6}$, $a=2$ m, no mean subtraction), validation-grid RMSE is minimized at $\varepsilon=2$ with 5.97 m.
At smaller $\varepsilon$, the bases are too flat to represent the hill and valley; at larger $\varepsilon$, values dip between points, increasing RMSE to 43.9 m at $\varepsilon=6$.
The reciprocal condition number increases monotonically from $6.8\times10^{-7}$ at $\varepsilon=0.8$ to 0.70 at $\varepsilon=6$, but RMSE reaches its minimum at $\varepsilon=2$ and then increases.
The residual of 0.70 m at $\varepsilon=0.8$ occurs because $\lambda=10^{-6}$ is no longer negligible relative to the matrix's smallest eigenvalue (estimated from the reciprocal condition number to be around $10^{-5}$).
At $\lambda=10^{-10}$, this residual falls to $7.4\times10^{-5}$ m, but RMSE remains unimproved at 8.99 m.
Conversely, $\lambda=10^{-2}$ suppresses surface variation at small $\varepsilon$, reducing RMSE at $\varepsilon=1.6$ to 5.35 m.

Mean subtraction lowers minimum RMSE to 2.67 m at $\varepsilon=3.6$ and shifts the optimal shape parameter upward.
The reciprocal-condition column is unchanged by mean subtraction, so this improvement comes from a different approximation model rather than conditioning.
At $a=8$ m with mean subtraction, minimum RMSE is 4.58 m at $\varepsilon=4.0$.
Good conditioning and accuracy at unused points are separate properties; examine both when choosing a shape parameter.

## Summary

RBFs build surfaces from nongridded elevation samples by superposing kernels.
`RbfInterpolator` has no constant term, so with a Gaussian kernel, subtracting the observation mean before fitting prevents between-point values from dropping toward zero.
Increasing shape parameter moves the reciprocal condition number toward 1 but worsens between-point approximation; choose by examining `conditioning()` values alongside unused-point errors.
Diagonal regularization permits sample residuals to reduce noise sensitivity and bounds the condition number by $(n+\lambda)/\lambda$, but does not greatly change unused-point errors when point placement is coarse.
The 1-norm reciprocal condition number is an estimate, not an error guarantee.
