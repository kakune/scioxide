Weather observations and photovoltaic estimates use global horizontal irradiance $G_i$ [W/m²] recorded at discrete times $t_i$ [h] to determine daily irradiation

$$
E = \int_0^{24} G(t)\,dt\quad[\mathrm{Wh/m^2}]
$$


Because time is measured in hours [h], integrating irradiance in W/m² gives Wh/m².
Samples $(t_i,G_i)$ alone do not determine $G(t)$ between samples.
To obtain irradiation, choose a model connecting the samples and integrate that model.
An interpolant's integral is the integral of the chosen model, not of the true irradiance.
This example integrates the same observation table with three models—a natural cubic spline, PCHIP, and a piecewise-linear curve (the trapezoidal rule)—and uses formulas to examine where their differences arise.

## Observation table and units

The table contains illustrative values resembling horizontal irradiance on a clear summer day, rather than sourced measurements.
Records are spaced four hours apart at night and two hours apart during the day.

| Time $t_i$ [h] | 0 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 20 | 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clear-sky irradiance [W/m²] | 0 | 0 | 35 | 260 | 610 | 850 | 790 | 510 | 135 | 0 | 0 |

To represent afternoon clouds, multiply values from 14 h onward by $1-f\,\min\bigl(1,(t-14)/6\bigr)$.
$f$ is the attenuation fraction at 20 h; the attenuation increases linearly from zero at 14 h to $f$ at 20 h.
Apply the overall scale factor $s$ to every value.

The following block defines quantities shared by this page's three cells.
`HOURS` and `CLEAR_SKY` contain the table above; `irradiance` gives samples $G_i$ with scale factor $s$ and attenuation fraction $f$ applied; `solar_models` constructs a natural cubic spline and PCHIP from those same samples.

```rust
//| id: interp-solar-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Irradiance observation table and two interpolants"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_interp::{CubicSpline, PchipInterpolator, SplineBoundary};

/// Observation times t_i [h].
pub(crate) const HOURS: [f64; 11] = [
    0.0, 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0, 12.0, 14.0, 16.0, 18.0, 20.0, 24.0,
];

/// Clear-sky horizontal irradiance [W/m²] (illustrative values).
pub(crate) const CLEAR_SKY: [f64; 11] = [
    0.0, 0.0, 35.0, 260.0, 610.0, 850.0, 790.0, 510.0, 135.0, 0.0, 0.0,
];

/// G_i = s · G_clear · (1 − f · min(1, max(0, (t − 14)/6))) [W/m²].
pub(crate) fn irradiance(scale: f64, cloud: f64) -> Vec<f64> {
    HOURS
        .iter()
        .zip(CLEAR_SKY)
        .map(|(&t, g)| scale * g * (1.0 - cloud * ((t - 14.0) / 6.0).clamp(0.0, 1.0)))
        .collect()
}

/// Natural cubic spline (S″ = 0 at both ends) and PCHIP for the same samples.
pub(crate) fn solar_models(
    scale: f64,
    cloud: f64,
) -> Result<(CubicSpline<f64>, PchipInterpolator<f64>), SciOxideError> {
    let samples = irradiance(scale, cloud);
    let spline =
        CubicSpline::with_boundary(HOURS.to_vec(), samples.clone(), SplineBoundary::Natural)?;
    let pchip = PchipInterpolator::new(HOURS.to_vec(), samples)?;
    Ok((spline, pchip))
}
```

## Interpolant integrals and the trapezoidal rule

The simplest daily integral is the integral of piecewise-linear interpolation joining the samples, namely the composite trapezoidal rule

$$
E_{\text{trap}} = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \frac{h_i}{2} (G_i + G_{i+1}), \qquad h_i = t_{i+1} - t_i
$$


Cubic splines and PCHIP represent each interval by a cubic Hermite polynomial determined by node values $G_i,G_{i+1}$ and slopes $d_i,d_{i+1}$.
Integrating the Hermite basis in local coordinates $u=(t-t_i)/h_i$ over $[0,1]$ gives $1/2$ for both value basis functions and $h_i/12$ and $-h_i/12$ for the slope basis functions.
The integral over each interval is therefore

$$
\int_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}} p_i(t)\,dt = \frac{h_i}{2}(G_i+G_{i+1}) + \frac{h_i^2}{12}(d_i-d_{i+1})
$$

and the difference between daily integrals from cubic interpolation and the trapezoidal rule is

$$
E_{\text{cubic}} - E_{\text{trap}} = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{h_i^2}{12}(d_i-d_{i+1})
$$

determined only by node slopes.
This difference is neither rounding error nor discretization error from general-purpose quadrature; it comes from modeling between samples with lines or cubics.
Determining which better represents reality requires knowledge beyond the samples, such as finer observations or a physical irradiance model.

Across equally spaced consecutive intervals, the sum $\sum(d_i-d_{i+1})$ telescopes, leaving only $h^2(d_{\text{first}}-d_{\text{last}})/12$.
This table has two nighttime intervals of width 4 h and eight daylight intervals of width 2 h, so only slopes at 0, 4, 20, and 24 h determine the difference.

The trapezoidal and spline daily integrals are linear in sample values $G_i$.
PCHIP slopes involve harmonic means and are nonlinear, but scaling all samples by the same positive factor scales slopes and the daily integral by that factor.

The definite integrals of `CubicSpline` and `PchipInterpolator` (`Integrable::integral` and its scalar form `integrate`) sum these Hermite-piece integrals over intervals.
Cost is proportional to the number of intervals in the integration range.
For repeated integration of the same spline, use `CubicSpline::prepare_antiderivative` to prepare per-interval antiderivatives and cumulative sums once, making each query $O(\log n)$.
`PchipInterpolator` has no API for this preparation.

## Daily-integral experiment

The next cell takes overall scale factor $s$ and afternoon attenuation fraction $f$, computes 24-hour integrals of a natural cubic spline (passing `Natural` to `CubicSpline::with_boundary`) and PCHIP with `integrate`, and compares them with the trapezoidal rule.
As an independent check, compute the difference formula above from each interpolant's node slopes `slopes()` and compare the trapezoidal value plus this difference with `integrate`.
The table's “Difference from reconstruction” is this check; a difference at rounding-error scale confirms the formula.
This cell also passes $s$ and $f$ to the two cells below.

```rust
//| id: interp-solar-energy
//| ref: interp-solar-energy
//| title: "Vary irradiance scale and afternoon clouds to compare daily integrals with the trapezoidal rule"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [interp-solar-model]

let trapezoid: f64 = (0..HOURS.len() - 1)

        .map(|i| {
            let h = HOURS[i + 1] - HOURS[i];

        .sum()

    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative difference from trapezoidal rule [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "Σh²(d_i − d_{i+1})/12 [Wh/m²]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": sci3,
        "nullText": "—"
    },

        "key": "4",
        "label": "Difference from reconstruction [Wh/m²]",
        "type": "number",

        "nullText": "—"
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "daily" }));
export!(irradiance_scale, irradiance_scale);

```

With defaults $s=1$ and $f=0.35$, the natural-spline daily integral is 6177.2 Wh/m² and the trapezoidal value is 6198.0 Wh/m², with relative difference $-0.34\%$.
The trapezoidal value plus the difference formula (whose default value is $-20.8$ Wh/m²) differs from the spline's `integrate` by zero with defaults and at most $4\times10^{-12}$ Wh/m² for any input, confirming the formula within rounding error.
Setting $s=1.6$ leaves the relative difference unchanged, showing that all three daily integrals are proportional to the sample scale factor.
The relative difference is $-0.55\%$ at $f=0$ and $+0.03\%$ at $f=0.9$; lowering afternoon samples with clouds can even change its sign.

The PCHIP daily integral equals the trapezoidal value.
All PCHIP slopes at 0, 4, 20, and 24 h that remain in the difference formula are zero.
At 4 and 20 h, one adjacent secant is zero, so the interior rule sets the slope to zero; at 0 and 24 h, the first and last interval secants are zero, so the endpoint rule does likewise.
Spline slopes at nighttime nodes are nonzero, and these endpoint terms produce the difference.

## Nonnegativity and overshoot

A natural cubic spline imposes $S''(0) = S''(24) = 0$ at both endpoints.
This is the natural boundary condition of the variational problem minimizing the integral of the squared second derivative; it does not express zero nighttime irradiance or zero irradiance slope at sunrise.

Physical irradiance satisfies $G(t)\ge0$, but the $C^2$ cubic spline does not know this constraint.
Where intervals of nighttime zeros adjoin rapid daylight changes, second-derivative continuity can pull the curve below zero.
Negative irradiance lowers the daily integral and temporarily decreases cumulative irradiation $E(t)$.

PCHIP constructs monotone Hermite pieces on each interval and sets slopes to zero at nodes where the data have extrema.
Each interval's values therefore stay between its endpoint samples; nonnegative samples give a nonnegative curve.
In exchange, second derivatives jump at nodes.

The next cell receives $s$ and $f$ from the cell above, evaluates both interpolants every 0.1 h with `value_at`, and tabulates minimum values, their times, and counts of negative evaluation points.
Values below the $G=0$ line in the plot are negative irradiance introduced by interpolation.
The horizontal time axis is linked to the next cell's cumulative-irradiation plot.

## Cumulative irradiation and the fundamental theorem of calculus

Cumulative irradiation from 0 h to time $t$,

$$
E(t) = \int_0^t S(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

satisfies $E'(t) = S(t)$ by the fundamental theorem of calculus.
The cumulative curve's slope equals the interpolated irradiance at that time, so the curve decreases where irradiance is negative.

The theorem can also be checked with differences.
Compare the average $\bigl(E(t+\tau)-E(t-\tau)\bigr)/(2\tau)$ over interval $[t-\tau,t+\tau]$ of width $2\tau$ with midpoint irradiance $S(t)$; Taylor expansion gives

$$
\frac{E(t+\tau)-E(t-\tau)}{2\tau} - S(t) = \frac{S''(t)}{6}\tau^2 + O(\tau^3)
$$


The $O(\tau^3)$ term comes from jumps in $S'''$ at nodes.
If the difference remains of order $\tau^2$, this checks that prepared antiderivative values and interpolated irradiance are linked by the fundamental theorem.

The next cell receives $s$ and $f$ from above and takes time $t^*$ [h] as input.
Using the antiderivative prepared with `prepare_antiderivative`, it plots the cumulative curve every 0.1 h and tabulates irradiance $S(t^*)$, slope $S'(t^*)$, and cumulative irradiation $E(t^*)$ at $t^*$.
For checks, it shows the difference between $E(t^*)$ computed directly with `integrate` and the prepared antiderivative value, plus the difference between the interval average with $\tau=0.01$ h and midpoint irradiance, and its expected value $S''(t^*)\tau^2/6$.
Compute $S''$ from the endpoint `derivative_at` difference $\bigl(S'(t+\tau)-S'(t-\tau)\bigr)/(2\tau)$.
Within one cubic piece, $S'$ is quadratic, so this difference equals $S''$ at the midpoint.
Clip the interval to $[0,24]$; if $t^*$ is at an endpoint, its midpoint shifts, and the expectation is computed at that shifted midpoint.

//| title: "Plot cumulative irradiation with a prepared antiderivative and check the fundamental theorem"

//| crates: [scioxide-interp]
//| dependsOn:
//|   day: interp-solar-energy

//|   - {"id":"after-sunset","label":"t* = 22 h","description":"At a time with negative spline irradiance, the cumulative curve also has negative slope.","inputs":{"solar_query_hour":22}}
//|   - {"id":"end-of-day","label":"t* = 24 h","description":"The cumulative value equals the spline daily integral in the first cell.","inputs":{"solar_query_hour":24}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default

//|       - {"artifact":"cumulative","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":-1.4e-3,"atol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: after-sunset
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"strength","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":0}}
//|   - case: end-of-day
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"cumulative","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":6177.2,"atol":0.1}}

    .as_f64()
    .ok_or("Missing overall scale factor")?;
let cloud = day["afternoon_cloud_fraction"]
    .as_f64()
    .ok_or("Missing cloud attenuation fraction")?;
let (spline, _) = solar_models(scale, cloud).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Prepare per-interval antiderivatives and cumulative sums only once
let primitive = spline.prepare_antiderivative().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let reject = ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject;
let energy = |t: f64| {
    primitive
        .integral(0.0, t, reject)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};

let mut cumulative = Vec::new();
for i in 0..=240 {
    let t = 0.1 * f64::from(i);
    cumulative.push([t, energy(t)?]);
}
let t = solar_query_hour;
let direct = spline.integrate(0.0, t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let value = spline.value_at(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let slope = spline.derivative_at(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Fundamental-theorem check: interval average with half-width τ = 0.01 h, midpoint irradiance, and expected S″τ²/6
let (a, b) = ((t - 0.01).max(0.0), (t + 0.01).min(24.0));
let middle = 0.5 * (a + b);
let average = (energy(b)? - energy(a)?) / (b - a);
let midpoint = spline.value_at(middle).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Since S′ is quadratic on a cubic piece, the difference of endpoint S′ values equals midpoint S″
let slope_right = spline.derivative_at(b).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let slope_left = spline.derivative_at(a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let half = 0.5 * (b - a);

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
emit_table_with_columns!(

        {
            "key": "0",
            "label": "S(t*) [W/m²]",
            "type": "number",
            "format": { "fractionDigits": 2 }
        },
        {
            "key": "1",
            "label": "S′(t*) [W/(m²·h)]",
            "type": "number",
            "format": { "fractionDigits": 2 }
        }

);

    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Time t* [h]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 2 } },
        {
            "key": "1",
            "label": "E(t*) (prepared antiderivative) [Wh/m²]",
            "type": "number",
            "format": fixed1
        },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from integrate [Wh/m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Interval average − midpoint irradiance [W/m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Expected S″τ²/6 [W/m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }

At the default $t^*=12$ h, the value is $E(t^*)=2645.6$ Wh/m², and the difference between the prepared antiderivative and `integrate` is zero.
The interval-average minus midpoint-irradiance difference is $-1.4\times10^{-3}$ W/m², nearly equal to the expectation $S''\tau^2/6$ and consistent with the fundamental theorem.
At $t^*=0$ and 24 h, the interval is clipped to one side, but natural boundary conditions set endpoint $S''$ to zero, leaving a difference at most $10^{-7}$ W/m².
At $t^*=22$ h, $S(t^*)$ is negative and the cumulative curve is decreasing.
The cumulative curve falls by about 29 Wh/m², from 6206.3 Wh/m² at 20 h to 6177.2 Wh/m² at 24 h, corresponding to the negative irradiance at 20.1–23.9 h in the nonnegativity experiment.
Conversely, the spline is slightly positive at 0–4 h, adding 4.2 Wh/m² during a nighttime interval with all-zero samples.
The cumulative value at 24 h equals the spline daily integral of 6177.2 Wh/m² from the daily-integral experiment.

## Summary

Integrated irradiation depends on the model chosen to connect samples.
The difference between cubic interpolation and the trapezoidal rule is $\sum h_i^2(d_i-d_{i+1})/12$, a model difference calculable from node slopes; it vanishes for PCHIP on this table.
A natural cubic spline can produce negative irradiance before sunrise or after sunset; if nonnegativity is required, use PCHIP or define nighttime irradiance as zero.
For repeated integration of the same interpolant, prepare an antiderivative with `prepare_antiderivative` and check agreement with `integrate`.
This observation table has no known true daily integral, so the compared table values are model differences rather than integration errors.
