The observation table in [Integrating an irradiance table](../solar-energy/) has no known true daily integral, so that example compared differences between models.
This page samples an analytically integrable irradiance model and measures integration error itself as the difference from the known integral.
Determine convergence orders from error ratios when sample spacing is halved, and examine how spline endpoint conditions and corners where slopes jump at sunrise and sunset change the order.

## A sinusoidal irradiance model from solar elevation

Solar elevation $\alpha$ is determined by latitude $\varphi$, solar declination $\delta$, and hour angle $\omega=15^\circ\,(t-12)$ (where $t$ is local solar time [h]) as follows:

$$
\sin\alpha = \sin\varphi\sin\delta + \cos\varphi\cos\delta\cos\omega
$$


At the equinoxes ($\delta=0$), $\sin\alpha=\cos\varphi\cos\omega$, with sunrise at 6 h and sunset at 18 h.
Ignoring atmospheric attenuation, set the irradiance on a horizontal surface to $G=G_n\sin\alpha$, where $G_n$ is the irradiance on a surface normal to the Sun.
Setting $G_n=1000$ W/m² and $\varphi=36.87^\circ$ ($\cos\varphi=0.8$) gives

$$
G(t)=800\cos\frac{\pi(t-12)}{12}=800\sin\frac{\pi(t-6)}{12}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}\quad(6\le t\le18),\qquad G(t)=0\quad(\text{otherwise})
$$


The integral over $[a,b]$, with $a'=\max(a,6)$ and $b'=\min(b,18)$, is

$$
E(a,b)=\int_{a'}^{b'}800\sin\frac{\pi(t-6)}{12}\,dt=\frac{9600}{\pi}\left[\cos\frac{\pi(a'-6)}{12}-\cos\frac{\pi(b'-6)}{12}\right]\ \mathrm{Wh/m^2}
$$


The daylight daily integral is $E(6,18)=19200/\pi\approx6111.55$ Wh/m².
This model represents only solar geometry and includes neither atmospheric transmittance nor diffuse light.

## Expected error orders

For equally spaced samples with spacing $h$, the Euler–Maclaurin formula gives the trapezoidal-rule error as

$$
E_{\text{trap}}-E=\frac{h^2}{12}\bigl[G'(b)-G'(a)\bigr]+O(h^4)
$$

when the integrand is smooth on $[a,b]$.
The leading term depends only on the endpoint slopes, and the trapezoidal rule has order 2.

As derived in [Integrating an irradiance table](oxref:interp-solar-integral-trapezoid), the difference between the cubic-spline integral and the trapezoidal rule is $\sum h_i^2(d_i-d_{i+1})/12$, reducing to $h^2(d_0-d_n)/12$ for equal spacing.
The spline integration error is therefore

$$
E_{\text{spline}}-E=\frac{h^2}{12}\Bigl[\bigl(d_0-G'(a)\bigr)-\bigl(d_n-G'(b)\bigr)\Bigr]+O(h^4)
$$

and the error in the endpoint slopes $d_0,d_n$ determines its order.

- With not-a-knot endpoints (requiring the two end intervals to form one cubic), the endpoint slope error for smooth $G$ is $O(h^3)$ (de Boor, Chapter IV), and the integration error is $O(h^4)$.
- With natural endpoints ($S''=0$), if $G''(a)\ne0$, the endpoint slope error is $O(h)$, reducing the integration error to $O(h^3)$.
  If $G''(a)=0$, the endpoint conditions match the true function, and the integration error remains $O(h^4)$.
- If an interior corner where $G'$ jumps lies at a node, the Euler–Maclaurin formula applies separately to the intervals divided at the corner.
  On $[0,24]$, $G'=0$ on nighttime intervals, so the trapezoidal-rule leading term is $h^2[G'(18)-G'(6)]/12$, the same as on $[6,18]$.
  A spline joins both sides of a corner with one $C^2$ curve, giving an error of $O(h)$ near the corner.

The model's $G$ satisfies $G=0$ and $G''=0$ at $t=6$ and 18 h.
Sampling $[6,18]$ exactly satisfies natural endpoint conditions, while sampling $[8,16]$ gives $G''=-800(\pi/12)^2\sin(\pi/6)\ne0$ at the endpoints.

## Measuring the order

The following cell selects the sampling interval and endpoint conditions, varies the equally spaced sample count $N$ from 5 to 129 (halving the spacing each time), and compares spline and trapezoidal-rule integration errors against $E(a,b)$.
Even on $[0,24]$, the sample count is $2^k+1$, so $t=6$ and 18 h are always nodes.
Table errors are the signed quantity $E_{\text{model}}-E$; the observed order is computed as $\log_2(|e_{N}|/|e_{2N-1}|)$ from the ratio of absolute errors whenever spacing is halved.
The last column is the leading Euler–Maclaurin term for the trapezoidal-rule error; on $[0,24]$, it uses the endpoint slopes of $[6,18]$.
The plot uses logarithmic scales on both axes: points show observed errors, while dashed lines show the trapezoidal-rule leading term and a line with the theoretical-order slope through the finest sample set.
The model's sine and cosine are computed with `RealScalar::sin` and `RealScalar::cos`.

```rust
//| id: interp-solar-sine-convergence
//| ref: interp-solar-sine-convergence
//| title: "Compare integration errors for an analytic irradiance model by sample count"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp]
//| inputs:
//|   sampling_window: {"type":"select","label":"Sample range","options":[{"label":"Daylight [6, 18] h (G = G″ = 0 at endpoints)","value":"day"},{"label":"Part of the day [8, 16] h (G″ ≠ 0 at endpoints)","value":"inner"},{"label":"Full day [0, 24] h (corners at 6 and 18 h)","value":"full"}],"value":"day"}
//|   spline_ends: {"type":"select","label":"Spline endpoint conditions","options":[{"label":"Natural (S″ = 0)","value":"natural"},{"label":"not-a-knot","value":"not-a-knot"}],"value":"natural"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"inner-natural","label":"[8, 16] h, natural","description":"Since G″ ≠ 0 at the endpoints, the natural spline drops to order 3.","inputs":{"sampling_window":"inner"}}
//|   - {"id":"inner-not-a-knot","label":"[8, 16] h, not-a-knot","description":"Changing endpoint conditions to not-a-knot restores order 4.","inputs":{"sampling_window":"inner","spline_ends":"not-a-knot"}}
//|   - {"id":"full-day","label":"[0, 24] h","description":"Sunrise and sunset corners make both the spline and trapezoidal rule order 2.","inputs":{"sampling_window":"full"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-3.9926,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/3","approx":{"value":4,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/5","approx":{"value":2,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/6","approx":{"value":-0.30680,"atol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: inner-natural
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/3","approx":{"value":3,"atol":0.05}}
//|   - case: inner-not-a-knot
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"min":3.5,"max":4.2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"min":-1e-6,"max":1e-6}}
//|   - case: full-day
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/3","approx":{"value":2,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/2","approx":{"value":-1.22723,"atol":1e-4}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{CubicSpline, SplineBoundary, Univariate};

let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
// Horizontal irradiance G(t) [W/m²] and G′(t) [W/(m²·h)] at the equinoxes, cos φ = 0.8, G_n = 1000 W/m²
let model = |t: f64| {
    if (6.0..=18.0).contains(&t) {
        800.0 * RealScalar::sin(pi * (t - 6.0) / 12.0)
    } else {
        0.0
    }
};
let model_slope = |t: f64| 800.0 * pi / 12.0 * RealScalar::cos(pi * (t - 6.0) / 12.0);
let (start, end) = match sampling_window.as_str() {
    "inner" => (8.0, 16.0),
    "full" => (0.0, 24.0),
    _ => (6.0, 18.0),
};
// Sunlit part [a′, b′] and closed-form E(a, b) (reference value)
let (lit_start, lit_end) = (start.max(6.0), end.min(18.0));
let phase = |t: f64| RealScalar::cos(pi * (t - 6.0) / 12.0);
let exact = 9600.0 / pi * (phase(lit_start) - phase(lit_end));
let natural = spline_ends == "natural";
let boundary = if natural {
    SplineBoundary::Natural
} else {
    SplineBoundary::NotAKnot
};
// Theoretical spline order from the expectations in the text
let theory = match (sampling_window.as_str(), natural) {
    ("full", _) => 2.0,
    ("inner", true) => 3.0,
    _ => 4.0,
};

let (mut rows, mut curves) = (Vec::new(), [Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new()]);
let mut previous: Option<(f64, f64)> = None;
for count in [5_usize, 9, 17, 33, 65, 129] {
    let h = (end - start) / (count - 1) as f64;
    let times: Vec<f64> = (0..count).map(|i| start + h * i as f64).collect();
    let values: Vec<f64> = times.iter().map(|&t| model(t)).collect();
    let trapezoid: f64 = values.windows(2).map(|g| 0.5 * h * (g[0] + g[1])).sum();
    let spline =
        CubicSpline::with_boundary(times, values, boundary).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let integral = spline.integrate(start, end).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let errors = (integral - exact, trapezoid - exact);
    // Leading Euler–Maclaurin term of trapezoidal-rule error: h²[G′(b′) − G′(a′)]/12
    let leading = h * h / 12.0 * (model_slope(lit_end) - model_slope(lit_start));
    // Compute observed order from the error ratio when spacing is halved. Leave the first row blank.
    let order = |now: f64, before: f64| RealScalar::log2(before.abs() / now.abs());
    let orders = previous.map(|(s, t)| (order(errors.0, s), order(errors.1, t)));
    previous = Some(errors);
    curves[0].push([h, errors.0.abs()]);
    curves[1].push([h, errors.1.abs()]);
    curves[2].push([h, leading.abs()]);
    rows.push((
        count,
        h,
        errors.0,
        orders.map(|o| o.0),
        errors.1,
        orders.map(|o| o.1),
        leading,
    ));
}
// Line with theoretical-order slope through the finest sample set
let [h_last, e_last] = curves[0][curves[0].len() - 1];
let guide: Vec<[f64; 2]> = curves[0]
    .iter()
    .map(|p| [p[0], e_last * RealScalar::powf(p[0] / h_last, theory)])
    .collect();

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Sample count N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Spacing h [h]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 4 } },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Spline error [Wh/m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "Observed spline order",
        "type": "number",
        "format": fixed2,
        "nullText": "—"
    },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Trapezoidal-rule error [Wh/m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    {
        "key": "5",
        "label": "Observed trapezoidal-rule order",
        "type": "number",
        "format": fixed2,
        "nullText": "—"
    },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Trapezoidal-rule leading term [Wh/m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" }));
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "line",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "series": [
            {
                "name": "Spline error",
                "points": curves[0],
                "style": { "showSymbol": true, "symbol": "circle" }
            },
            {
                "name": "Trapezoidal-rule error",
                "points": curves[1],
                "style": { "showSymbol": true, "symbol": "diamond" }
            },
            {
                "name": "Trapezoidal-rule leading term (Euler–Maclaurin)",
                "points": curves[2],
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
            },
            {
                "name": format!("Slope for theoretical order {theory}"),
                "points": guide,
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
            }
        ],
        "xLabel": "Spacing h [h]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute daily-integral error [Wh/m²]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
```

With the default daylight samples on $[6,18]$ and natural endpoints, spline error falls from $-3.99$ Wh/m² at $N=5$ to $-3.1\times10^{-6}$ Wh/m² at $N=129$, and the observed order converges to 4.00.
The observed trapezoidal-rule order is 2.00, with an error of $-0.307$ Wh/m² remaining even at $N=129$.
The trapezoidal-rule error agrees with the leading Euler–Maclaurin term $h^2[G'(18)-G'(6)]/12=-(h^2/12)(400\pi/3)$ to within $3\times10^{-6}$ Wh/m² at $N=129$.
The remaining difference is an $O(h^4)$ term.
Here the model's $G''=0$ matches the natural endpoint conditions, so the spline's integration error has order 4.

On $[8,16]$, $G''\ne0$ at the endpoints, and the natural spline's observed order converges from 3.05 to 3.00.
With not-a-knot endpoints, the error at $N=129$ is $-4.7\times10^{-7}$ Wh/m², about 1/700 of the natural-endpoint error $-3.2\times10^{-4}$ Wh/m².
However, the not-a-knot error changes sign between $N=9$ and 17, yielding meaningless observed orders such as 10.4 and $-1.21$ around that change.
The ratio $|e_N|/|e_{2N-1}|$ does not express order where the error crosses zero, so read the order from fine sample sets with a consistent error sign.
The observed orders in the last two rows are 3.48 and 3.81, approaching 4.

On $[0,24]$ including nighttime, both observed orders become 2.00.
The spline error $-1.2272$ Wh/m² at $N=129$ agrees with the trapezoidal-rule error to within $2\times10^{-12}$ Wh/m².
In the formula above, the spline–trapezoidal difference is $h^2(d_0-d_n)/12$, and the slopes $d_0,d_n$ at 0 and 24 h are nearly zero because the corner's influence decays by about a factor of $2-\sqrt3\approx0.27$ per node.
The spline integral therefore has the same $O(h^2)$ error as the trapezoidal rule, with the same leading Euler–Maclaurin term.
This result shows that, for samples including nighttime zeros such as the observation table in [Integrating an irradiance table](../solar-energy/), the daily integral of cubic interpolation need not be more accurate than the trapezoidal rule.

## Summary

An analytically integrable model lets us measure interpolation integration error as a difference from a known value.
For smooth integrands, the trapezoidal-rule error is second order, and its leading term is predicted by the Euler–Maclaurin formula $h^2[G'(b)-G'(a)]/12$.
The integration order of a cubic spline depends on the accuracy of endpoint slopes: it is 4 with not-a-knot endpoints or natural endpoints when the function's $G''$ vanishes there, and 3 with other natural endpoints.
Sampling nighttime zeros as well makes both the spline and trapezoidal rule order 2 because slopes jump at sunrise and sunset.
Read observed orders from fine sample sets whose errors have a consistent sign; before using cubic interpolation on measured tables, check whether corners or endpoint conditions reduce convergence order.
