When interpolating global temperatures on a map, treating latitude and longitude degrees directly as planar distances causes severe distortion near the poles.
A spherical spline satisfies longitude periodicity and a unique value at each pole through its basis construction.
Use an artificial temperature field with an exactly known global mean to examine map-fitting errors and global-mean errors separately.

## Synthetic field and area mean

On the unit sphere, define the following function of colatitude $\theta$ (angle measured from the North Pole) and longitude $\phi$:

$$
T(\theta,\phi)=15+8\cos\theta+3\sin\theta\cos\phi
\quad[{}^\circ\mathrm C]
$$


It has directional temperature contrasts, warmer toward the North Pole and toward longitude zero.
The second and third terms are proportional to the degree-one spherical harmonic $Y_1^0$ and the real part of $Y_1^1$; the constant term is a constant multiple of $Y_0^0=1/\sqrt{4\pi}$.
Spherical harmonics are mutually orthogonal, so the global integrals of the two degree-one terms vanish.
The global mean $\overline T=(4\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\pi T\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$ is therefore exactly the constant coefficient $15\ {}^\circ\mathrm C$.
This is a field for testing poles, the longitude seam, and area means, rather than an empirical climate model.

Generate observation points with independent uniform random values for $z=\cos\theta$ in $[-1,1]$ and longitude in $[0,2\pi)$.
By Archimedes' theorem, the area of a spherical band is proportional to its width in $z$, so these points are uniformly distributed with respect to spherical area.
Making colatitude $\theta$ itself uniform instead concentrates points near the poles, which have small areas.
Hold out one in every five generated points from fitting, using temperature differences at the held-out points for validation.
This measures an error separate from residuals at samples used for fitting.

## Spherical splines and residual-target fitting

`SphericalSpline` combines a tensor product of cubic B-splines in colatitude and periodic cubic B-splines in longitude with six pole modes determining values and tangent planes at the poles.
The tensor-product basis excluding pole modes has zero value and slope at both poles, so the surface is $C^1$ everywhere, including the poles, with a unique longitude-independent value at each pole.
Point coordinates are ordered as `[colatitude, longitude]` (rad), and longitude wraps with period $2\pi$.

The detailed form `fit_spherical_spline_with_options` starts with a basis containing only pole modes (colatitude breakpoints $[0,\pi]$ and four longitude intervals).
For target `SplineFitTarget::Residual(S)`, it inserts breakpoints bisecting the interval with the largest residual sum, alternating colatitude and longitude (starting with colatitude), until the weighted residual sum of squares is at most $S$ plus tolerance.
It then increases the penalty from $\lambda=1$ by factors of 16 until finding a value that fails the condition, and bisects to find the largest penalty $\lambda$ satisfying it.
Each trial is a `SphericalSplinePlan` fit with fixed breakpoints, minimizing the weighted residual sum of squares plus $\lambda$ times a penalty on second differences of coefficients.
Use the detailed form rather than the one-shot function `fit_spherical_spline` to raise the penalty-trial limit from its default 32 to 64 and inspect termination status in the report.
Set the penalty-search limit with `SplineFitLimits::with_max_penalty_iterations(64)`.

The input smoothing scale $\tau$ [°C] indicates the allowed residual RMS; for $n$ fitting samples, set the target to $S=n\tau^2$.
$\tau$ controls how closely to fit this artificial field; it does not estimate measurement error.
A smaller target requires additional breakpoints and a smaller penalty.
Residual tests use absolute tolerance $10^{-10}$ and relative tolerance $10^{-4}$.
Termination status `AdaptiveSplineTermination` equal to `ResidualSatisfied` means the target was met and the penalty search finished within tolerance; `is_success()` is true.

From errors $e_j$ at the $m$ held-out points, compute the mean square $\overline{e^2}$ and its standard error $\mathrm{SE}=s_{e^2}/\sqrt m$.
Approximate uncertainty in RMSE $=\sqrt{\overline{e^2}}$ by the delta method as $\mathrm{SE}/(2\,\mathrm{RMSE})$.
With only 20 held-out points by default, this uncertainty is not negligible.

The following block defines the synthetic field and observations shared by the two cells on this page.
`temperature` defines $T(\theta,\phi)$; `observations` supplies fitting points and values and held-out validation points; `fit_options` defines the residual target $S=n\tau^2$ and search limits.

```rust
//| id: interp-spherical-temperature-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Synthetic temperature field and observation points"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp, scioxide-random]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_interp::{AdaptiveSplineOptions, SplineFitLimits, SplineFitTarget};
use scioxide_random::{Rng, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

/// T(θ, φ) = 15 + 8 cos θ + 3 sin θ cos φ [°C] (θ is colatitude, φ is longitude, in rad).
pub(crate) fn temperature(theta: f64, phi: f64) -> f64 {
    15.0 + 8.0 * RealScalar::cos(theta) + 3.0 * RealScalar::sin(theta) * RealScalar::cos(phi)
}

/// Generate count points uniformly in area and hold out one in five for validation.
/// Returns (fitting [θ, φ], their values, held-out [θ, φ]).
pub(crate) fn observations(
    count: usize,
    seed: f64,
) -> Result<(Vec<[f64; 2]>, Vec<f64>, Vec<[f64; 2]>), SciOxideError> {
    let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
    let (mut sites, mut values, mut held) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
    for i in 0..count {
        let uniform_z: f64 = rng.next_float()?;
        let uniform_phi: f64 = rng.next_float()?;
        // Make z = cos θ and longitude uniform
        let point = [
            RealScalar::acos(2.0 * uniform_z - 1.0),
            std::f64::consts::TAU * uniform_phi,
        ];
        if i % 5 == 0 {
            held.push(point);
        } else {
            sites.push(point);
            values.push(temperature(point[0], point[1]));
        }
    }
    Ok((sites, values, held))
}

/// Residual target S = nτ², tolerances (absolute 10⁻¹⁰, relative 10⁻⁴), and 64 penalty trials.
pub(crate) fn fit_options(
    fitted: usize,
    strength: f64,
) -> Result<AdaptiveSplineOptions<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    Ok(AdaptiveSplineOptions::new()
        .with_target(SplineFitTarget::Residual(
            fitted as f64 * strength * strength,
        ))
        .with_limits(SplineFitLimits::new().with_max_penalty_iterations(64))
        .with_residual_tolerance(Tolerance::new(1e-10, 1e-4)?))
}
```

## Fitting and mapping

The next cell takes the number of observations to generate, smoothing scale $\tau$, and random seed as inputs.
The first table shows fit termination status, residual sum of squares and target, inserted breakpoints, and penalty; the second shows counts of fitting and held-out points, held-out RMSE, and the 95% interval half-width (1.96 times the approximate SE).
Evaluate the map at the centers of a $10^\circ$ grid in geographic latitude $90^\circ-\theta$ and longitude $[-180^\circ,180^\circ)$.
Convert map coordinates to `[colatitude, longitude]` (rad) before passing them.
Fix the color range at 6–24 °C, encompassing the synthetic field's range $[15-\sqrt{73},15+\sqrt{73}]\approx[6.5,23.5]$ °C.
This cell passes observations, breakpoints, penalty, and coefficients to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: interp-spherical-temperature
//| ref: interp-spherical-temperature
//| title: "Fit a spherical temperature field and check held-out observations"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [interp-spherical-temperature-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp, scioxide-random]
//| exports: [sites, values, theta_breaks, longitude_breaks, penalty, coefficients]
//| inputs:
//|   samples: {"type":"range","label":"Number of observations to generate [-]","min":80.0,"max":160.0,"step":20.0,"value":100.0}
//|   strength: {"type":"range","label":"Allowed residual RMS scale τ [°C]","min":0.02,"max":0.08,"step":0.01,"value":0.04}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1.0,"max":100.0,"step":1.0,"value":7.0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"loose-target","label":"τ = 0.08 °C","description":"A looser target reduces breakpoints and increases the penalty.","inputs":{"strength":0.08}}
//|   - {"id":"more-stations","label":"160 points","description":"Increasing observations also increases held-out points to 32.","inputs":{"samples":160}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"ResidualSatisfied"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.128,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"holdout","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":20}
//|       - {"artifact":"holdout","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.04237,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"holdout","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.01204,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: loose-target
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"holdout","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.09272,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: more-stations
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"holdout","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":128}
//|       - {"artifact":"holdout","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":32}
//|       - {"artifact":"holdout","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.04933,"rtol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_interp::{
    ExtrapolationPolicy, Interpolant, SphericalSamples, fit_spherical_spline_with_options,
};

let (sites, values, held) = observations(samples as usize, seed).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = fit_options(values.len(), strength).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let grid = SphericalSamples::Scattered(&sites);
// Use the detailed form returning a report so unmet-target termination statuses are also shown
let fit = fit_spherical_spline_with_options(grid, &values, None, options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let spline = fit.spline();
let policy = ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject;
let mut squares = Vec::new();
for &[t, p] in &held {
    let error = spline.evaluate([t, p], policy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - temperature(t, p);
    squares.push(error * error);
}
// Mean squared error and SE, and RMSE SE from the delta method
let m = squares.len() as f64;
let mse = squares.iter().sum::<f64>() / m;
let variance = squares.iter().map(|s| (s - mse) * (s - mse)).sum::<f64>() / (m - 1.0);
let rmse = RealScalar::sqrt(mse);
let half = 1.96 * RealScalar::sqrt(variance / m) / (2.0 * rmse);
let mut map = Vec::new();
for i in 0..18 {
    let latitude = -85.0 + 10.0 * i as f64;
    for j in 0..36 {
        let longitude = -175.0 + 10.0 * j as f64;
        let degree = std::f64::consts::PI / 180.0;
        let point = [(90.0 - latitude) * degree, longitude * degree];
        let value = spline.evaluate(point, policy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        map.push([longitude, latitude, value]);
    }
}
let report = fit.report();
let target = values.len() as f64 * strength * strength;
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fit_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Termination" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Residual sum of squares [°C²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Target S [°C²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Inserted breakpoints [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Penalty λ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
let fit_rows = vec![(
    format!("{:?}", report.termination()),
    report.residual(),
    target,
    report.refinements(),
    report.penalty(),
)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    fit_columns,
    fit_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "fit" })
);
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let holdout_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Fitting points [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Held-out points [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Held-out RMSE [°C]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "1.96 SE of RMSE [°C]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
let holdout = vec![(sites.len(), held.len(), rmse, half)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    holdout_columns,
    holdout,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "holdout" })
);
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "heatmap",
    "data": map,
    "xLabel": "Longitude [°]",
    "yLabel": "Geographic latitude [°]",
    "title": "Spherical-spline temperature [°C]",
    "colorMin": 6.0,
    "colorMax": 24.0
}));
// Breakpoints: colatitude knot vector with endpoint multiplicity four; periodic longitude knot vector extended by three knots at each end
let [theta_knots, longitude_knots] = spline.knots();
let mut theta_breaks = theta_knots.to_vec();
theta_breaks.dedup();
let longitude_breaks = longitude_knots[3..longitude_knots.len() - 3].to_vec();
export!(sites, sites);
export!(values, values);
export!(theta_breaks, theta_breaks);
export!(longitude_breaks, longitude_breaks);
export!(penalty, report.penalty());
export!(coefficients, spline.coefficients().to_vec());

let termination = report.termination();
let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The residual sum of squares met the target and the penalty search finished")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Fitting stopped before meeting the target")
        .explanation("The returned surface is the last accepted one; reconsider the target or trial limit.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{termination:?}"))
        .diagnostic("Residual sum of squares", report.residual(), Some("°C²"))
        .diagnostic("Target", target, Some("°C²"))
        .diagnostic("Held-out RMSE", rmse, Some("°C"))
);
```

With defaults (100 points, $\tau=0.04$ °C, seed 7), fitting inserts three breakpoints and uses penalty $\lambda\approx0.049$ to match the residual sum of squares to the target 0.128 °C², terminating with `ResidualSatisfied`.
The RMSE at the 20 held-out points is 0.042 °C, comparable to the RMS scale $\tau$ for fitting points.
The 95% interval half-width is 0.012 °C, 30% of the RMSE; this interval width largely explains the RMSEs of 0.031 and 0.043 °C with seeds 1 and 100.
Setting $\tau$ to 0.02 °C increases inserted breakpoints to five and lowers held-out RMSE to 0.035 °C, but not in proportion to $\tau$.
Residuals at fitting points need not equal errors at points excluded from fitting.
At $\tau=0.08$ °C, one breakpoint is inserted and RMSE is 0.093 °C.
Rectangular map pixels do not represent equal spherical areas (pixel area is proportional to the cosine of latitude), so do not use a simple pixel-value average for the global mean.

## Checking the global mean

The spline's global mean can be computed in two ways.
One is area integration with `Integrable::integral`.
The integral provided by `SphericalSpline` is with respect to the area element $\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$; bounds from `[0, 0]` to `[π, 2π]` give the whole-sphere integral.
On each colatitude interval, a 32-term Taylor series computes the integral of $\theta^n\sin\theta$ with truncation error below `f64` rounding, so this includes no discrete quadrature error.

The other method is Lebedev quadrature from `scioxide-integrate`.
A Lebedev rule is an octahedrally symmetric quadrature rule exactly integrating spherical polynomials of degree at most $d$ (restrictions to the sphere of polynomials in $x,y,z$ of degree at most $d$, equivalently sums of spherical harmonics of degree at most $d$); its weights sum to $4\pi$.
`LebedevRule::new(d, options)` accepts 32 degrees: odd integers from 3 to 31 and degrees from 35 to 131 in increments of 6 ([Quadrature rules on the sphere](oxref:integrate-sphere-rules)).
Lebedev rules provide no error estimate.
The integrand receives Cartesian coordinates $(x,y,z)$, so convert them to $\theta=\arccos z$ and $\phi=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$ before evaluating the spline.
The spline is piecewise cubic in $\theta$ and $\phi$, rather than a spherical polynomial, so degree-$d$ exactness does not make its integral exact.
The difference between Lebedev quadrature and the spline area integral is quadrature error; the difference between the spline area integral and 15 °C is fitting error.

The next cell receives observations, breakpoints, and penalty from the previous cell and reconstructs the same surface with `SphericalSplinePlan` using fixed breakpoints and penalty.
This is one linear least-squares solve; it does not repeat breakpoint or penalty searches.
It also checks that the reconstructed surface's coefficients match the previous cell's coefficients.
Its input is the maximum Lebedev degree; it computes global means for degrees from 5 through that degree.
The first table shows the area-integral global mean, fitting error, and coefficient difference; the second shows each degree's node count, global mean, and difference from the area integral (quadrature error).
The plot shows quadrature error on a logarithmic scale and fitting error as a reference line because it is independent of degree.

```rust
//| id: interp-spherical-global-mean
//| ref: interp-spherical-global-mean
//| title: "Compare the global mean from area integration and Lebedev quadrature"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [interp-spherical-temperature-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-random]
//| dependsOn:
//|   fitted: interp-spherical-temperature
//| inputs:
//|   max_degree: {"type":"select","label":"Maximum Lebedev degree d","options":["17","41","83","131"],"value":"41"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"highest-degree","label":"d ≤ 131","description":"Even at degree 131 (5810 nodes), quadrature error remains around 10⁻⁹ °C.","inputs":{"max_degree":"131"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"global","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.5078e-3,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"global","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"lebedev","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":14}
//|       - {"artifact":"lebedev","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.1211e-3,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"lebedev","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":3.436e-5,"rtol":1e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"lebedev","path":"/rows/6/3","range":{"max":1e-6}}
//|   - case: highest-degree
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"lebedev","path":"/rows/9/1","equals":5810}
//|       - {"artifact":"lebedev","path":"/rows/9/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_integrate::{GeometricOptions, LebedevRule};
use scioxide_interp::{
    ExtrapolationPolicy, Integrable, Interpolant, SphericalSamples, SphericalSplinePlan,
    SplineFitLimits,
};

let list = |key: &str| -> Result<Vec<f64>, String> {
    serde_json::from_value(fitted[key].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let sites: Vec<[f64; 2]> =
    serde_json::from_value(fitted["sites"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (values, previous) = (list("values")?, list("coefficients")?);
let penalty = fitted["penalty"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing penalty")?;
// One fit with fixed breakpoints and penalty (no search)
let limits = SplineFitLimits::new().with_max_penalty_iterations(64);
let plan = SphericalSplinePlan::new(
    &list("theta_breaks")?,
    &list("longitude_breaks")?,
    SphericalSamples::Scattered(&sites),
    None,
    penalty,
    limits,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let spline = plan.fit(&values).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.into_spline();
let coefficient_gap = spline
    .coefficients()
    .iter()
    .zip(&previous)
    .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let policy = ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject;
let four_pi = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::TAU;
// Whole-sphere integral with area element sin θ dθ dφ. Divide by 4π for the global mean
let area_mean = spline
    .integral(
        [0.0, 0.0],
        [std::f64::consts::PI, std::f64::consts::TAU],
        policy,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    / four_pi;
let fit_error = (area_mean - 15.0).abs();
// Lebedev integrand: convert Cartesian coordinates to [colatitude, longitude] and evaluate
let integrand = |xyz: &[f64]| -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    let t = RealScalar::acos(xyz[2].clamp(-1.0, 1.0));
    spline.evaluate([t, RealScalar::atan2(xyz[1], xyz[0])], policy)
};
let largest: usize = max_degree.parse().map_err(|_| "Degree is not an integer")?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut errors = Vec::new();
for degree in [5, 7, 11, 17, 23, 29, 41, 59, 83, 131] {
    if degree > largest {
        break;
    }
    let rule =
        LebedevRule::new(degree, GeometricOptions::default()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (integral, _) = rule
        .integrate(&integrand, &[0.0; 3], 1.0)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let quadrature_error = (integral / four_pi - area_mean).abs();
    rows.push((
        degree,
        rule.node_count(),
        integral / four_pi,
        quadrature_error,
    ));
    // Omit zero differences from the logarithmic plot but retain them in the table
    if quadrature_error > 0.0 {
        errors.push([degree as f64, quadrature_error]);
    }
}
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let global_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Global mean from area integration [°C]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Fitting error |mean − 15| [°C]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum coefficient difference [°C]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let global = vec![(area_mean, fit_error, coefficient_gap)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    global_columns,
    global,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "global" })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Lebedev degree d [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Node count [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Global mean [°C]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference from area integral [°C]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "lebedev" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Quadrature error |Lebedev − area integral|", "points": errors })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Lebedev degree d [-]",
        "yLabel": "Global-mean difference [°C]",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{
            "kind": "line",
            "axis": "y",
            "value": fit_error,
            "label": "Fitting error |area integral − 15|",
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }]
    })
);
```

With defaults, the spline's area-integral global mean is 15.0015 °C, with fitting error $1.5\times10^{-3}$ °C.
Coefficients reconstructed with fixed breakpoints and penalty agree with the previous cell's coefficients within $7\times10^{-15}$, confirming integration of the same surface.
The fitting error is much smaller than the held-out RMSE of 0.042 °C, showing that positive and negative pointwise errors cancel in the global integral.
Quadrature error is $1.1\times10^{-3}$ °C at degree 5 (14 nodes), comparable to fitting error, but falls to $3.4\times10^{-5}$ °C at degree 11 (50 nodes) and around $10^{-6}$ °C at degree 17 and above.
The decrease is not monotonic: error grows from $9.8\times10^{-7}$ °C at degree 17 to $2.5\times10^{-6}$ °C at degree 23.
Even increasing the degree to 131 (5810 nodes) leaves a difference around $2\times10^{-9}$ °C rather than reducing it to the rounding level.
The spline is piecewise cubic with discontinuous higher derivatives at joins, so higher-degree exactness does not directly translate to decreasing error.
For this field, degree 11 or higher gives quadrature error at most 1/40 of fitting error, so fitting determines global-mean error.
Setting $\tau=0.08$ °C in the previous cell increases fitting error to $1.0\times10^{-2}$ °C, but actually reduces degree-5 quadrature error to $2.2\times10^{-5}$ °C.
With just one inserted breakpoint and a larger penalty, the surface is smoother and easier to integrate with few nodes.

## Summary

Although spherical fields use colatitude–longitude coordinates, handle sample placement and means according to the area element $\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$.
Uniform $z=\cos\theta$ and longitude give area-uniform observations; a simple average of map pixels does not give the global mean.
Assess fitting accuracy with held-out-observation RMSE and its standard error, separately from the residual-sum-of-squares target.
For the global mean, the spline area integral minus the exact value is fitting error, while Lebedev quadrature minus the area integral is quadrature error; check them separately.
For this synthetic field, a Lebedev rule of about degree 11 makes quadrature error sufficiently smaller than fitting error, but the required degree changes with field smoothness.
