Joining a straight line directly to a circular arc makes curvature jump from zero to $1/R$.
A vehicle traveling at constant speed $v$ has lateral acceleration $v^2\kappa$, so a curvature jump causes an abrupt change in lateral acceleration.
A transition curve avoids this abrupt change by varying curvature gradually with arc length.
This example uses clothoid geometry to validate interpolation, without addressing individual railway-design standards.

## Clothoids and Fresnel integrals

A clothoid has curvature $\kappa(s)=s/A^2$ proportional to arc length $s$; $A$ [m] is called the clothoid parameter.
Its tangent angle is $\psi(s)=\int_0^s\kappa\,du=s^2/(2A^2)$, so its position is

$$
x(s)=\int_0^s\cos\frac{u^2}{2A^2}\,du,
\qquad y(s)=\int_0^s\sin\frac{u^2}{2A^2}\,du
$$


Using the normalized Fresnel integrals $C(z)=\int_0^z\cos(\pi t^2/2)\,dt$ and $S(z)=\int_0^z\sin(\pi t^2/2)\,dt$ (DLMF 7.2.7) and substituting $u=A\sqrt\pi\,t$ gives

$$
x(s)=A\sqrt\pi\,C\!\left(\frac{s}{A\sqrt\pi}\right),
\qquad y(s)=A\sqrt\pi\,S\!\left(\frac{s}{A\sqrt\pi}\right)
$$


Samples of the reference alignment are generated with `fresnel_c` and `fresnel_s` from `scioxide-special`.

Set $A=80\ \mathrm m$ and clothoid length $L=60\ \mathrm m$, with a 30 m straight segment before it and a 30 m circular arc after it.
The arc radius is $R=A^2/L\approx106.7\ \mathrm m$; position and tangent angle $\psi(L)=L^2/(2A^2)$ match at the join $s=L$.
The reference alignment has curvature zero on the straight segment, $s/A^2$ on the transition, and $L/A^2$ on the arc; it is continuous in arc length, but its slope changes at $s=0$ and $s=L$.
The maximum curvature is $L/A^2\approx9.4\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$.
Because curvature $\kappa=\psi'$ is continuous while $\kappa'$ jumps, position is $C^2$ but not $C^3$.

## Parameterization and curvature

To connect a point sequence $\boldsymbol r_0,\dots,\boldsymbol r_{N-1}$ with a curve, assign each point a parameter $u_i$.
Uniform parameterization uses $u_i=i$; chord-length parameterization uses cumulative point distances $u_{i+1}=u_i+\|\boldsymbol r_{i+1}-\boldsymbol r_i\|$; centripetal parameterization uses cumulative square roots of distances $u_{i+1}=u_i+\|\boldsymbol r_{i+1}-\boldsymbol r_i\|^{1/2}$.
`CurveParameterization::ChordLength` divides cumulative chord lengths by the total length, normalizing them to $[0,1]$.
Uniform and centripetal parameterizations are not provided directly; construct an array and pass it to `CurveParameterization::Supplied`.
None of these parameters necessarily equals true arc length.
Chord length approximates arc length from below and approaches it as point spacing becomes small relative to the radius of curvature.

Curvature is independent of parameterization and is computed from first and second position derivatives as

$$
\kappa=\frac{x'y''-y'x''}{\|\boldsymbol r'\|^3}
$$


$\boldsymbol r'$ and $\boldsymbol r''$ are derivatives with respect to the parameter; the denominator $\|\boldsymbol r'\|^3$ cancels the parameter scale.
Plotting only the second parameter derivative $y''$ mistakes differences in parameter scale for curvature.

Use two fitting methods.
The first is a cubic B-spline curve fitted with the detailed form `fit_parametric_spline_with_options`, target `SplineFitTarget::Residual(0)`, and absolute tolerance $10^{-12}\ \mathrm{m^2}$, inserting knots until the residual sum of squares is at most $10^{-12}\ \mathrm{m^2}$.
Since the target is zero, no penalty search is performed, and the penalty remains zero.
Use the detailed form rather than the one-shot function `fit_parametric_spline` to select a parameterization and inspect the parameters `parameters()` used and the report.
All knots are simple, so the curve is $C^2$, and the report's `continuity()` also returns 2.
Curvature is also continuous at knots as long as velocity $\boldsymbol r'$ is nonzero.

The second method is `PchipInterpolator` with the same parameterization, interpolating each component with Fritsch–Butland weighted-harmonic-mean slopes.
PCHIP is $C^1$ cubic Hermite interpolation preserving each component's monotonicity, so second derivatives and curvature can jump at nodes.
`PchipInterpolator` does not implement the second-derivative trait `TwiceDifferentiable`, so compute second derivatives by centered differences of gradients using `physical_difference_derivative` from `scioxide-differentiate`.
If the difference points lie in the same polynomial piece, centered differences of the quadratic gradient are exact apart from rounding.

## Measuring curvature errors

Use reference arc-length samples $s_i=-30+120\,(i/(N-1))^{1.3}$ [m], with spacing increasing toward the end to make parameterization differences visible.
Evaluate curvature at each interval's parameter midpoint.
Points on the fitted curve do not lie on the reference alignment, so evaluate the analytic comparison curvature at the arc length $\tilde s$ obtained by projecting each point onto the reference alignment.
Projection applies the first-order Newton approximation $\tilde s\leftarrow\tilde s+(\boldsymbol r-\boldsymbol p(\tilde s))\cdot\boldsymbol t(\tilde s)$ (where $\boldsymbol p$ is the reference-alignment point and $\boldsymbol t$ its unit tangent) three times, starting from the midpoint of the endpoint arc lengths.
This projection and analytic curvature are calculated explicitly for independent checks; the main fitting and curvature calculations use `scioxide-interp`.

The following block provides reference-alignment and fitting helpers shared by the page's two cells.
`Track::point` gives the point and tangent angle at arc length $s$; `Track::curvature` gives analytic curvature; `Track::project` performs projection; `sample_arcs` gives sample arc lengths; `fit_curves` fits both methods with the selected parameterization; `spline_curvature` and `pchip_curvature` give their curvatures.

```rust
//| id: interp-track-clothoid-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Straight–clothoid–circular-arc reference alignment and two curves"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-differentiate, scioxide-interp, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_differentiate::physical_difference_derivative;
use scioxide_interp::{
    AdaptiveSplineOptions, CurveParameterization, Differentiable, ExtrapolationPolicy,
    ParametricSplineFit, PchipInterpolator, SplineFitTarget, TwiceDifferentiable,
    fit_parametric_spline_with_options,
};
use scioxide_special::{fresnel_c, fresnel_s};

/// Clothoid parameter A [m] and clothoid length L [m].
pub(crate) const CLOTHOID_A: f64 = 80.0;
pub(crate) const CLOTHOID_LENGTH: f64 = 60.0;

/// Reference alignment joining a straight line (s < 0), clothoid (0 ≤ s ≤ L), and circular arc (s > L).
pub(crate) struct Track {
    scale: f64,
    radius: f64,
    angle: f64,
    end: [f64; 2],
}

impl Track {
    pub(crate) fn new() -> Result<Self, SciOxideError> {
        let (a, length) = (CLOTHOID_A, CLOTHOID_LENGTH);
        let scale = a * RealScalar::sqrt(std::f64::consts::PI);
        let end = [
            scale * fresnel_c(length / scale)?,
            scale * fresnel_s(length / scale)?,
        ];
        let (radius, angle) = (a * a / length, length * length / (2.0 * a * a));
        Ok(Self {
            scale,
            radius,
            angle,
            end,
        })
    }

    /// Point [m] and tangent angle ψ(s) [rad] at arc length s.
    pub(crate) fn point(&self, s: f64) -> Result<([f64; 2], f64), SciOxideError> {
        if s < 0.0 {
            return Ok(([s, 0.0], 0.0));
        }
        if s <= CLOTHOID_LENGTH {
            let p = [
                self.scale * fresnel_c(s / self.scale)?,
                self.scale * fresnel_s(s / self.scale)?,
            ];
            return Ok((p, s * s / (2.0 * CLOTHOID_A * CLOTHOID_A)));
        }
        let psi = self.angle + (s - CLOTHOID_LENGTH) / self.radius;
        let x = self.end[0] + self.radius * (RealScalar::sin(psi) - RealScalar::sin(self.angle));
        let y = self.end[1] - self.radius * (RealScalar::cos(psi) - RealScalar::cos(self.angle));
        Ok(([x, y], psi))
    }

    /// Analytic curvature κ(s) = clamp(s, 0, L)/A² [1/m].
    pub(crate) fn curvature(s: f64) -> f64 {
        s.clamp(0.0, CLOTHOID_LENGTH) / (CLOTHOID_A * CLOTHOID_A)
    }

    /// Arc length of the projection of point r onto the reference alignment (three iterations of s ← s + (r − p(s))·t(s)).
    pub(crate) fn project(&self, r: [f64; 2], guess: f64) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        let mut arc = guess;
        for _ in 0..3 {
            let (p, psi) = self.point(arc)?;
            arc += (r[0] - p[0]) * RealScalar::cos(psi) + (r[1] - p[1]) * RealScalar::sin(psi);
        }
        Ok(arc)
    }
}

/// Sample arc lengths s_i = −30 + 120 (i/(N − 1))^1.3 [m].
pub(crate) fn sample_arcs(count: usize) -> Vec<f64> {
    let last = (count - 1) as f64;
    (0..count)
        .map(|i| -30.0 + 120.0 * RealScalar::powf(i as f64 / last, 1.3))
        .collect()
}

/// Select a parameterization ("uniform", "chord", or "centripetal") and fit a spline curve and PCHIP.
pub(crate) fn fit_curves(
    points: &[[f64; 2]],
    mode: &str,
) -> Result<
    (
        ParametricSplineFit<f64, [f64; 2]>,
        PchipInterpolator<f64, [f64; 2]>,
    ),
    SciOxideError,
> {
    let mut parameters = vec![0.0];
    for pair in points.windows(2) {
        let distance = RealScalar::hypot(pair[1][0] - pair[0][0], pair[1][1] - pair[0][1]);
        let step = if mode == "uniform" {
            1.0
        } else {
            RealScalar::sqrt(distance)
        };
        parameters.push(parameters[parameters.len() - 1] + step);
    }
    let choice = if mode == "chord" {
        CurveParameterization::ChordLength
    } else {
        CurveParameterization::Supplied(&parameters)
    };
    let options = AdaptiveSplineOptions::new()
        .with_target(SplineFitTarget::Residual(0.0))
        .with_residual_tolerance(Tolerance::new(1e-12, 0.0)?);
    let fit = fit_parametric_spline_with_options(points, choice, None, options)?;
    let pchip = PchipInterpolator::new(fit.parameters().to_vec(), points.to_vec())?;
    Ok((fit, pchip))
}

/// Signed planar-curve curvature (x'y'' − y'x'')/‖r'‖³.
pub(crate) fn plane_curvature(v: [f64; 2], acc: [f64; 2]) -> f64 {
    (v[0] * acc[1] - v[1] * acc[0]) / RealScalar::powi(RealScalar::hypot(v[0], v[1]), 3)
}

/// Spline-curve point and curvature (analytic Hessian).
pub(crate) fn spline_curvature(
    fit: &ParametricSplineFit<f64, [f64; 2]>,
    q: f64,
) -> Result<([f64; 2], f64), SciOxideError> {
    let policy = ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject;
    let spline = fit.spline();
    let point = scioxide_interp::Interpolant::evaluate(spline, [q], policy)?;
    let v = spline.gradient([q], policy)?[0];
    Ok((
        point,
        plane_curvature(v, spline.hessian([q], policy)?[0][0]),
    ))
}

/// PCHIP point and curvature. Compute second derivatives by centered differences of gradients at q ± step.
pub(crate) fn pchip_curvature(
    pchip: &PchipInterpolator<f64, [f64; 2]>,
    q: f64,
    step: f64,
) -> Result<([f64; 2], f64), SciOxideError> {
    let policy = ExtrapolationPolicy::Reject;
    let mut acc = [0.0; 2];
    for (component, out) in acc.iter_mut().enumerate() {
        *out = physical_difference_derivative(
            |x| Ok(pchip.gradient([x], policy)?[0][component]),
            q,
            &[q - step, q + step],
        )?;
    }
    let point = scioxide_interp::Interpolant::evaluate(pchip, [q], policy)?;
    let v = pchip.gradient([q], policy)?[0];
    Ok((point, plane_curvature(v, acc)))
}
```

With the default chord-length parameterization and $N=25$, the spline curve inserts 17 knots before its residual sum of squares $1.0\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm{m^2}$ drops below the target $10^{-12}\ \mathrm{m^2}$, terminating with `ResidualSatisfied`.
The knot-vector length 25 is shorter than the $N+4=29$ required for interpolation, so the curve does not pass exactly through the samples (residual RMS is about $6\times10^{-8}$ m).
The maximum curvature error is $1.5\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, about 1.6% of the maximum curvature.
Errors concentrate in intervals containing $s=0$ and $s=L$, where the curvature slope changes; excluding those intervals and their neighbors gives $4.9\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$.
The reference alignment's third position derivative jumps at the joins, so a cubic cannot fully approximate intervals spanning them.
The PCHIP maximum error $1.4\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ is comparable, but errors of the same magnitude persist away from the joins.
PCHIP is local interpolation determined only by values and slopes at each interval's endpoints, so curvature is exactly zero on eight intervals of the straight segment; they do not appear in the lower logarithmic plot.
The spline curve fits globally, leaving small curvatures even on the straight segment.

Setting $N=41$ lowers the spline maximum error to $4.2\times10^{-5}$, below PCHIP's $9.9\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$.
The maximum spline error is $2.3\times10^{-4}$ with uniform parameterization and $1.8\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ with centripetal parameterization; chord length gives the smallest error for these irregularly spaced samples.
Uniform and centripetal parameterizations insert 21 knots to give a knot-vector length of 29, and the curves interpolate the samples.

## Curvature continuity at nodes and knots

Midpoint errors do not directly demonstrate continuity.
Evaluate curvature on both sides of each method's polynomial-piece joins and measure the difference.
The spline curve joins at interior knots, while PCHIP joins at interior nodes $u_1,\dots,u_{N-2}$; these do not coincide.
Compare curvature at two points $\pm h$ from each join, taking $h$ as $10^{p}$ times the minimum parameter spacing.
A difference at finite separation does not prove continuity itself.
For a $C^2$ curve, the difference is about the curvature slope $\times2h$, decreasing in proportion to $h$; for a $C^1$ curve, a second-derivative jump remains regardless of $h$.

Its input is the exponent $p$ of $h$.
The table shows the join count and maximum curvature difference across joins for each parameterization and method, along with spline-fit termination status.

```rust
let track = Track::new().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut points = Vec::new();
for arc in sample_arcs(count) {
    points.push(track.point(arc).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.0);
}
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, mode) in [
    ("Uniform", "uniform"),
    ("Chord length", "chord"),
    ("Centripetal", "centripetal"),
] {
    let (fit, pchip) = fit_curves(&points, mode).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let u = fit.parameters();
    let gap = u
        .windows(2)
        .map(|w| w[1] - w[0])
        .fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
    let h = scioxide_core::RealScalar::powi(10.0, offset as i32) * gap;
    // An open cubic basis has knots of multiplicity four at both ends
    let knots = fit.spline().bases()[0].knots();
    let interior = &knots[4..knots.len() - 4];
    let mut spline_jump = 0.0_f64;
    for &k in interior {
        let left = spline_curvature(&fit, k - h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.1;
        let right = spline_curvature(&fit, k + h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.1;
        spline_jump = spline_jump.max((right - left).abs());
    }
    // For PCHIP, use centered differences with step h/4 within each polynomial piece on either side of the node
    let mut pchip_jump = 0.0_f64;
    for &node in &u[1..count - 1] {
        let left = pchip_curvature(&pchip, node - h, h / 4.0)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            .1;
        let right = pchip_curvature(&pchip, node + h, h / 4.0)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            .1;
        pchip_jump = pchip_jump.max((right - left).abs());
    }
    let status = format!("{:?}", fit.report().termination());
    rows.push((
        name,
        "Cubic spline curve (knots)",
        interior.len(),
        spline_jump,
        Some(status),
    ));
    rows.push((name, "PCHIP (nodes)", count - 2, pchip_jump, None));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Parameterization" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Method (joins)" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Join count [-]", "type": "integer" },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "Maximum curvature difference across joins [1/m]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": { "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 }
    },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Fit termination status", "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "jumps" })
);
```

With the defaults ($N=25$ and $p=-6$), curvature differences across spline knots range from $7\times10^{-10}$ to $1.9\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ for every parameterization.
Changing $p$ to $-8$ and $-2$ gives chord-length differences of $6.9\times10^{-12}$ and $6.9\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, varying in proportion to $h$.
This agrees with curvature being continuous at knots and the difference across them being determined by the product of curvature slope and $2h$.
Differences across PCHIP nodes range from $3.7\times10^{-3}$ to $4.0\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, nearly independent of $p$, reaching 40% of the maximum curvature $9.4\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$.
As the sample count in the previous cell increases, the PCHIP differences decrease from $5.3\times10^{-3}$–$5.7\times10^{-3}$ at $N=17$ to $2.4\times10^{-3}$–$2.6\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ at $N=41$, but they do not approach the level of spline differences.
Even with comparable midpoint errors, PCHIP curvature jumps at each node, making it unsuitable for avoiding abrupt changes in lateral acceleration.
PCHIP preserves monotonicity of each coordinate component, a separate property from smoothly joining curvature.

## Summary

A clothoid has curvature proportional to arc length, varying curvature continuously between a straight line and a circular arc.
Curvature is independent of parameterization: compute it with $\kappa=(x'y''-y'x'')/\|\boldsymbol r'\|^3$ rather than judging from the second parameter derivative alone.
When comparing with analytic curvature, use the arc length obtained by projecting the fitted point onto the reference alignment.
A cubic spline curve with simple knots has continuous curvature; PCHIP curvature jumps at nodes even when midpoint errors are comparable.
Parameterization changes error magnitudes but does not change this difference in continuity.
