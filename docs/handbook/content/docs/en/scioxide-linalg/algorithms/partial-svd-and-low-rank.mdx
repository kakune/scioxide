Even when a full singular value decomposition of a large matrix or operator is infeasible, a few leading singular values and vectors or a low-rank approximation can be computed from products alone.
Examples include principal components of a data matrix and estimates of numerical rank.
This page explains the two method objects `PartialSvdSolver` and `RandomizedLowRank`.
Norm and condition-number estimation are covered in [Estimating the 1-norm and exact operator norms](../norm-estimation/).
Shared conventions for method objects, workspace requirements, APIs returning owned values and accepting caller-provided output, and the termination reason `SpectralTermination` are in [Applying matrix functions to vectors and method objects](oxref:linalg-action-conventions).

## Partial SVD

### Thick-restart Golub–Kahan bidiagonalization

`PartialSvdSolver` computes the `count` leading singular triplets $(\sigma_j, u_j, v_j)$ ($Av_j = \sigma_j u_j$, $A^{\mathsf H}u_j = \sigma_j v_j$) of an $m \times n$ operator $A$.
$A$ is used only through products $Ax$ and $A^{\mathsf H}y$; $A^{\mathsf H}A$ is never formed.
Starting from a right vector, Golub–Kahan bidiagonalization constructs orthonormal bases $V$ and $U$ of dimension up to $m_b$ and an upper bidiagonal matrix $B$ satisfying $AV = UB$.
Each new vector is orthogonalized against the entire basis by classical Gram–Schmidt, with another pass if its norm drops below $1/\sqrt 2$ times its previous value.
The SVD of $B$ gives Ritz triplets (approximate singular triplets formed from the projected problem).
A restart retains $\max(\text{count}, d/2)$ Ritz vectors, capped at $d - 1$, together with their coupling coefficients to the residual (the augmented restart of Baglama and Reichel, 2005).
Triplets already meeting the tolerance are retained first and checked again in later cycles.

A triplet is accepted only when both residuals computed with fresh products by $A$ and $A^{\mathsf H}$ meet the tolerance.

```text
max(‖A v − σ u‖₂, ‖Aᴴ u − σ v‖₂) ≤ τ_abs + τ_rel σ
```

A single residual can be small even for a poor triplet.
This check runs in the first and last cycles, when the basis becomes invariant, and in any cycle where the estimate $\beta \lvert e_{m_b}^{\mathsf T} \tilde u \rvert$ meets the tolerance for a requested triplet ($\tilde u$ is a left singular vector of $B$, and $\beta$ is the final coefficient from extending the bidiagonalization).
A cycle costs about $2(m_b - \text{retained count})$ products plus two products per checked triplet.

### PartialSvdSolver settings, results, and reports

Defaults for `PartialSvdSolver::new(count)` are basis dimension 30 (`with_basis`, at least `count + 2` or at least $\min(m, n)$), at most 100,000 total forward and adjoint applications including residual checks, and tolerances $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}} = \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} = \sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
`count` exceeding $\min(m, n)$ gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), and an insufficient basis dimension gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).
The execution options' iteration limit counts restart cycles (100 by default); the dense SVD of the projected matrix is solved without an iteration limit.

Call the API returning owned values `solve(&a, &initial)` or accepting caller-provided output `solve_into(&a, &initial, &mut buffer, &mut workspace, cx)`; the starting vector `initial` is a nonzero vector of length $n$.
The result `PartialSvdBuffer` (prepared with `PartialSvdBuffer::new(m, n, count)` for caller-provided output) holds descending singular values `singular_values()`, $m \times r$ `left_vectors()` with left singular vectors as columns, $n \times r$ `right_vectors()` with right singular vectors as columns ($V$ rather than $V^{\mathsf H}$; $r$ is the number of returned triplets), and the two residuals `residuals()` for each triplet.
It may contain fewer triplets than requested; all meet the tolerance only on `Converged`.
On a limit or cancellation, it contains the most recent triplets with both residuals computed, possibly none.
The report `PartialSvdReport` holds the termination reason, application count, and cycle number `cycles()` of the returned triplets (counted from 1, or 0 if none).
The workspace `PartialSvdWorkspace` holds $O((m + n) m_b)$ scalars and storage for the projected-matrix SVD, reserved using `PartialSvdRequirements`.

### Test matrix with known singular values

To compare against exact values, construct an $m \times n$ matrix ($m = 200$, $n = 120$) with known singular values.
The discrete sine transform matrix $S^{(N)}_{ij} = \sqrt{2/(N+1)}\,\sin(ij\pi/(N+1))$ is orthogonal; using the first $n$ columns of $U = S^{(m)}$ and $V = S^{(n)}$, form

```text
A = U diag(σ₁, …, σₙ) Vᵀ,   σⱼ = j^(−α)
```

whose singular values are exactly $\sigma_j$.
Larger $\alpha$ gives faster singular-value decay.
By the Eckart–Young–Mirsky theorem, the best rank-$k$ approximation in the 2-norm is the truncated SVD $A_k$, with error $\lVert A - A_k \rVert_2 = \sigma_{k+1}$.

The following definitions block shares this test matrix between two cells.
`test_matrix` provides $A$, `singular_value` provides $\sigma_j$, `gaussian` provides seeded standard-normal pseudorandom values (xorshift64* and Box–Muller), and `truncation_error` verifies the error $\lVert A - \tilde A \rVert_2$ of the approximation $\tilde A = \sum_j \sigma_j u_j v_j^{\mathsf H}$ using a dense SVD of the difference.

```rust
//| id: linalg-low-rank-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Test matrix with known singular values and verification"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_linalg::{Matrix, MatrixView, SpectralTermination, SvdValues};

/// Singular values σⱼ = j^(−α) (reference values).
pub(crate) fn singular_value(j: usize, alpha: f64) -> f64 {
    (j as f64).powf(-alpha)
}

/// A = U diag(σ) Vᵀ (m ≥ n). U and V are the first n columns of orthonormal discrete sine transforms.
pub(crate) fn test_matrix(m: usize, n: usize, alpha: f64) -> Result<Matrix<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
    let basis = |size: usize, i: usize, j: usize| {
        let scale = (2.0 / (size + 1) as f64).sqrt();
        scale * (pi * ((i + 1) * (j + 1)) as f64 / (size + 1) as f64).sin()
    };
    let mut a = Matrix::zeros(m, n)?;
    for (idx, value) in a.as_mut_slice().iter_mut().enumerate() {
        let (i, l) = (idx / n, idx % n);
        *value = (0..n)
            .map(|j| basis(m, i, j) * singular_value(j + 1, alpha) * basis(n, l, j))
            .sum();
    }
    Ok(a)
}

/// Seeded standard-normal pseudorandom numbers (xorshift64* and Box–Muller).
pub(crate) fn gaussian(seed: u64, len: usize) -> Vec<f64> {
    let mut state = seed.wrapping_mul(0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15) | 1;
    let mut uniform = move || {
        state ^= state >> 12;
        state ^= state << 25;
        state ^= state >> 27;
        ((state.wrapping_mul(0x2545_F491_4F6C_DD1D) >> 11) as f64 + 0.5) / (1u64 << 53) as f64
    };
    (0..len)
        .map(|_| {
            let (u, v) = (uniform(), uniform());
            (-2.0 * u.ln()).sqrt() * (2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * v).cos()
        })
        .collect()
}

/// Compute the error ‖A − Ã‖₂ of the rank-r approximation Ã = Σⱼ σⱼ uⱼ vⱼᴴ as the largest singular value in a dense SVD of the difference
/// (independent verification). `v` is an n × r matrix with columns vⱼ.
pub(crate) fn truncation_error(
    a: &Matrix<f64>,
    u: MatrixView<'_, f64>,
    sigma: &[f64],
    v: MatrixView<'_, f64>,
) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    let n = a.cols();
    let mut difference = a.clone();
    for (idx, value) in difference.as_mut_slice().iter_mut().enumerate() {
        let (i, l) = (idx / n, idx % n);
        for (j, &s) in sigma.iter().enumerate() {
            *value -= u.get(i, j)? * s * v.get(l, j)?;
        }
    }
    Ok(SvdValues::compute(&difference)?.singular_values()[0])
}

/// Convert a termination reason to its variant name.
pub(crate) fn termination_name(termination: SpectralTermination) -> &'static str {
    match termination {
        SpectralTermination::Converged => "Converged",
        SpectralTermination::EvaluationLimit => "EvaluationLimit",
        SpectralTermination::IterationLimit => "IterationLimit",
        SpectralTermination::InvariantSubspace => "InvariantSubspace",
        SpectralTermination::AccuracyLimit => "AccuracyLimit",
        SpectralTermination::ExecutionStopped(_) => "ExecutionStopped",
    }
}
```

## Randomized low-rank approximation

### A sketch capturing the range

`RandomizedLowRank` uses the method of Halko, Martinsson, and Tropp (2011) to capture the range of $A$ with a few test vectors and form a low-rank approximation.
The caller supplies an $n \times w$ test matrix $\Omega$ (a sketch, usually with independent standard-normal entries) and at least one probe vector unused in construction.
`RandomizedLowRank` generates no random numbers, so identical inputs produce identical results; the caller controls sketch selection and reproducibility.

The method proceeds as follows.

1. Construct an orthonormal basis $Q$ for the range of $A\Omega$ by Gram–Schmidt.
   Discard a vector as dependent if its post-orthogonalization norm is at most the threshold determined from its original norm by the rank policy `RankPolicy`.
2. Perform $q$ subspace iterations, each replacing $Q$ with an orthonormal basis of $A(A^{\mathsf H}Q)$.
3. Use adjoint products to form the small matrix $Q^{\mathsf H}A$ (at most $w \times n$).
4. `svd`/`svd_into` computes $Q^{\mathsf H}A = \tilde U \Sigma V^{\mathsf H}$ and sets $U = Q\tilde U$; `interpolative`/`interpolative_into` uses column-pivoted QR $Q^{\mathsf H}AP = \tilde Q [R_{11}\ R_{12}]$ to select $r$ columns of $A$ and forms $A \approx CZ$ with $Z = [I,\ R_{11}^{-1}R_{12}]P^{\mathsf T}$ ($C$ contains the selected columns, each computed with one product $Ae_j$).

The cost is at most $w(2q + 2)$ products, plus one per probe and an additional $r$ for a column interpolative decomposition.
The resulting rank $r$ is the minimum of the target rank, the rank of $Q$, and the numerical rank according to the small factorization's rank policy.
Each probe $g$ gives a residual $\lVert Ag - \tilde Ag \rVert_2$; if $\lVert Ag - \tilde Ag \rVert_2 \le \tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} \lVert Ag \rVert_2$ holds for all $g$, the result is `Converged`, otherwise `AccuracyLimit`.
The probes are independent of the sketch, so their residuals independently check the approximation.
These residuals give only a lower bound on the error through $\lVert (A - \tilde A)g \rVert_2 \le \lVert A - \tilde A \rVert_2 \lVert g \rVert_2$; they give no upper bound.

### Bounds on expectations and subspace iteration

Accuracy depends on the sketch column count $w = k + p$ (rank $k$ plus $p$ extra columns) and singular-value decay.
If $\Omega$ has independent standard-normal entries and $p \ge 2$, the basis $Q$ obtained without subspace iteration satisfies

```text
E ‖A − Q Qᴴ A‖₂ ≤ (1 + √(k/(p − 1))) σₖ₊₁ + (e √(k + p) / p) (Σ_{j>k} σⱼ²)^{1/2}
```

where $e$ is the base of natural logarithms (Halko, Martinsson, and Tropp, 2011, Theorem 10.6).
Furthermore, the approximation $\tilde A_k$ obtained by truncating the SVD of $Q^{\mathsf H}A$ to rank $k$ satisfies $\lVert A - \tilde A_k \rVert_2 \le \sigma_{k+1} + \lVert A - QQ^{\mathsf H}A \rVert_2$ (ibid., Section 9.3).
The second term on the right grows for matrices with slowly decaying singular values.
With $q$ subspace iterations, the range of $(AA^{\mathsf H})^qA$ is captured instead of $A$, changing the singular values to $\sigma_j^{2q+1}$ and accelerating decay.
Replacing $\sigma_j$ with $\sigma_j^{2q+1}$ on the right above and raising the result to the power $1/(2q+1)$ then bounds $E\lVert A - QQ^{\mathsf H}A \rVert_2$ from above (ibid., Corollary 10.10).
These bounds on expectations apply only when the caller supplies a Gaussian sketch; a single result need not fall within them, so verify it using probe residuals.

### RandomizedLowRank settings, results, and reports

Defaults for `RandomizedLowRank::new(rank)` are rank policy `RankPolicy::Exact` (`with_rank_policy`), no subspace iterations (`with_power_steps`), at most 100,000 applications, and probe tolerances $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}} = \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} = \sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
The sketch column count $w$ is read from `sketch.cols()` on each call; a target rank exceeding $w$ or $w$ exceeding $\min(m, n)$ gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), and zero probes gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Prepare the workspace with `workspace(&a, w)`; `RandomizedLowRankWorkspace` holds $O((m + n)w)$ scalars and storage for small SVD and QR factorizations (requirements `RandomizedLowRankRequirements`).
`RandomizedSvdBuffer` returns $m \times r$ `u()`, $r \times n$ `vt()` ($V^{\mathsf H}$), and descending `singular_values()`; `InterpolativeBuffer` returns selected columns `columns()`, an $r \times n$ interpolation matrix `coefficients()`, and column indices `column_indices()`.
Column `column_indices()[j]` of $Z$ is the unit vector $e_j$, so the selected columns are reproduced exactly.
The report `LowRankReport` holds the termination reason, rank `rank()`, maximum probe residual `probe_residual()` and maximum relative residual `relative_probe_residual()` (`None` if every probe has $Ag = 0$), number `completed_probes()` of probes with computed residuals, and application count.
On a limit or cancellation, the output contains the approximation if construction completed, otherwise zeros (rank 0).
Residuals then cover only completed probes; with zero completed probes, zero residual fields provide no accuracy information.

```rust
let probes = Matrix::from_vec(n, 2, gaussian(seed + 1000, n * 2)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tolerance = tolerance.parse::<f64>().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let method = RandomizedLowRank::new(k)
    .with_power_steps(q)
    .with_tolerance(ResidualTolerance::new(0.0, tolerance).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
let (approx, report) = method
    .svd(&op, sketch.view(), probes.view())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let values = approx.singular_values();
// vt() is Vᴴ (r × n), so transpose it to put vⱼ in columns.
let error = truncation_error(&a, approx.u(), values, approx.vt().transpose())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let worst = values
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, &v)| (v - sigma(j + 1)).abs() / sigma(j + 1))
    .reduce(f64::max);

// HMT bound on the expectation: the sum of σₖ₊₁ and a bound on range error E‖A − QQᴴA‖₂.
// With q subspace iterations, replace σⱼ by σⱼ^(2q+1) and take the 1/(2q+1) power (Corollary 10.10).
let p = (w - k) as f64;
let order = (2 * q + 1) as i32;
let tail = (k + 1..=n)
    .map(|j| sigma(j).powi(2 * order))
    .sum::<f64>()
    .sqrt();
let range_bound = ((1.0 + (k as f64 / (p - 1.0)).sqrt()) * sigma(k + 1).powi(order)
    + std::f64::consts::E * (w as f64).sqrt() / p * tail)
    .powf(1.0 / order as f64);
let bound = sigma(k + 1) + range_bound;
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Termination reason", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Applications [count]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "‖A − Ãₖ‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum relative error of σ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative probe residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Rank r", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" }
]);
let rows = vec![
    (
        "Randomized SVD",
        Some(termination_name(report.termination())),
        Some(report.applications()),
        Some(error),
        worst,
        report.relative_probe_residual(),
        Some(report.rank()),
    ),
    (
        "Optimal rank-k approximation σₖ₊₁",
        None,
        None,
        Some(sigma(k + 1)),
        None,
        None,
        None,
    ),
    (
        "Bound on expectation (HMT)",
        None,
        None,
        Some(bound),
        None,
        None,
        None,
    ),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "randomized" })
);

let exact: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (1..=w + 5).map(|j| [j as f64, sigma(j)]).collect();
let indexed = |v: &[f64]| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    v.iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(j, &x)| [(j + 1) as f64, x])
        .collect()
};
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "xLabel": "Index j [-]",
    "yLabel": "Singular value σⱼ [-]",
    "yType": "log",
    "layers": [
        {
            "id": "exact",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "σⱼ = j^(−α) (exact)",
            "points": exact,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
        },
        { "id": "partial", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Partial SVD", "points": indexed(&partial) },
        {
            "id": "randomized",
            "kind": "scatter",
            "name": "Randomized SVD",
            "points": indexed(values),
            "style": { "symbol": "diamond" }
        }
    ],
    "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": k as f64 + 0.5, "label": "Rank k" }]
}));

let outcome = match report.termination() {
    SpectralTermination::Converged => Outcome::satisfied("Probe residuals met the criterion"),
    SpectralTermination::AccuracyLimit => {
        Outcome::failed("Probe residuals did not meet the criterion")
            .explanation("Reconsider the rank, subspace-iteration count, and tolerance.")
    }
    _ => Outcome::partial("Stopped at a limit"),
};
let mut outcome = outcome
    .status(termination_name(report.termination()))
    .diagnostic("Applications", report.applications() as f64, None)
    .diagnostic("‖A − Ãₖ‖₂", error, None);
if let Some(residual) = report.relative_probe_residual() {
    outcome = outcome.diagnostic("Relative probe residual", residual, None);
}
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

With the defaults $p = 5$, $q = 0$, $\tau_{\mathrm{rel}} = 0.2$, and seed 1, randomized SVD reaches `Converged` in 22 applications (20 forward and adjoint products for 10 sketch columns, plus 2 probes).
The error $\lVert A - \tilde A_5 \rVert_2 = 0.0362$ is 1.3 times the optimum $\sigma_6 = 0.0278$ and lies within the expectation bound $0.163$.
The maximum relative error of the leading singular values is nevertheless large at $0.18$; `Converged` means only that the relative probe residual $0.14$ fell below $\tau_{\mathrm{rel}} = 0.2$.
Setting $q = 2$ increases applications to 62 but reduces singular-value relative error to $4.3 \times 10^{-7}$; the approximation error matches $\sigma_6$ with a relative difference of $3 \times 10^{-11}$.
The bound on the expectation also falls to $0.0642$ and approaches $2\sigma_6$ as $q$ increases.
Even this near-optimal approximation retains a relative probe residual $0.13$ because the tail singular values $\sigma_6, \sigma_7, \dots$ contribute to $Ag$.
A tolerance below the tail, such as $\tau_{\mathrm{rel}} = 10^{-8}$, therefore yields `AccuracyLimit` regardless of the quality of the approximation.
With seed 2, the error $0.0281$ is even nearer the optimum, but the relative probe residual is $0.28$ and the result is `AccuracyLimit`.
A decision based on two probes depends on their directions, so interpret probe residuals as an independent check of the approximation.

## Summary

`PartialSvdSolver` uses Golub–Kahan bidiagonalization to compute leading singular triplets and returns `Converged` only when both left and right residuals meet the tolerance.
`RandomizedLowRank` captures the range using a caller-provided sketch and constructs a low-rank approximation with few products, but `Converged` means only that the probe residuals met the criterion.
For slowly decaying singular values, increase extra columns $p$ and subspace iterations $q$ to approach the optimal error $\sigma_{k+1}$, estimating the added products with $w(2q + 2)$.
Set the probe tolerance to match the singular-value tail, and remember that decisions using only about two probes depend on their directions.
Choose partial SVD for accurate leading singular values themselves, or randomized SVD to capture the range with few products.
