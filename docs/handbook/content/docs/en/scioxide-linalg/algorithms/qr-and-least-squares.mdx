For an overdetermined system $Ax \approx b$ with more observations $m$ than unknowns $n$ ($A \in \mathbb{K}^{m \times n}$, where $\mathbb{K}$ is $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$), seek $x$ minimizing the residual 2-norm $\lVert b - Ax \rVert_2$.
This page explains QR factorization, which solves this least-squares problem by orthogonal transformations, its error and sensitivity, treatment of rank deficiency, trust-region subproblems, and QR updates for gradually changing matrices.
The symbols $u$ (unit roundoff) and $\kappa_2$ follow [Mathematics of linear algebra and shared conventions](../); general background on backward error and condition numbers is in [Floating-point arithmetic and conditioning](../../../concepts/floating-point-and-conditioning/).
The call-form names—standard form, form with options, API with caller-provided output, and one-shot function—are defined in [Call forms](oxref:linalg-execution-forms); solution acceptance is specified in [Separating factorization from solution acceptance](oxref:linalg-factorization-acceptance).

## Householder reflections

For a nonzero vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^m$, the reflection $H = I - 2 v v^{\mathsf T} / (v^{\mathsf T} v)$ is a symmetric orthogonal matrix reflecting across the hyperplane perpendicular to $v$.
To zero all but the first component of a given $x$, that is, obtain $Hx = \beta e_1$, choose $v = x - \beta e_1$ with $\lvert \beta \rvert = \lVert x \rVert_2$.
Indeed, $v^{\mathsf T} x = \beta^2 - \beta x_1$ and $v^{\mathsf T} v = 2(\beta^2 - \beta x_1)$ imply $Hx = x - v = \beta e_1$.

Either sign of $\beta$ is possible, but numerically choose $\beta = -\operatorname{sign}(x_1) \lVert x \rVert_2$.
Then $v_1 = x_1 + \operatorname{sign}(x_1) \lVert x \rVert_2$ is a sum of numbers with the same sign.
The opposite sign gives $v_1 = x_1 - \operatorname{sign}(x_1) \lVert x \rVert_2$; when $x$ is almost parallel to $e_1$, cancellation makes the relative error of $v_1$ large and the computed $H$ loses orthogonality.
`scioxide-linalg` stores reflections in the form $H = I - \tau v v^{\mathsf H}$ ($v_1 = 1$), scaling components by powers of two before computing the norm to avoid overflow and underflow.
For complex scalars, take complex $\tau$ and real $\beta$ (then $H$ is unitary but not Hermitian).

## QR factorization and least-squares solutions

Construct a reflection $H_k$ on column $k$ from the diagonal downward and multiply from the left; repeating for $k = 1, \dots, n$ makes $A$ upper triangular and yields

$$
A = Q R = \begin{bmatrix} Q_1 & Q_2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R_1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad Q = H_1 \cdots H_n,\ Q^{\mathsf H} Q = I
$$

where $Q_1 \in \mathbb{K}^{m \times n}$ and $R_1$ is $n \times n$ upper triangular.
Orthogonal transformations preserve the 2-norm, so

$$
\lVert b - Ax \rVert_2^2 = \lVert Q_1^{\mathsf H} b - R_1 x \rVert_2^2 + \lVert Q_2^{\mathsf H} b \rVert_2^2
$$

The least-squares solution follows by back substitution in $R_1 x = Q_1^{\mathsf H} b$, and the residual norm is $\lVert Q_2^{\mathsf H} b \rVert_2$.

Count real-arithmetic operations as follows.
At step $k$, apply $v$ of length $m - k + 1$ to the remaining $n - k$ columns; $w^{\mathsf T} = v^{\mathsf T} A_{k:m,\,k+1:n}$ and the update $A_{k:m,\,k+1:n} - \tau v w^{\mathsf T}$ each require about $2(m-k)(n-k)$ operations.
The total is $\sum_{k=1}^{n} 4(m-k)(n-k) \approx 4 \int_0^n (m-t)(n-t)\,dt = 2mn^2 - \tfrac{2}{3} n^3$.
Compute $Q^{\mathsf H} b$ without forming $Q$ by applying the reflections in sequence, adding only $O(mn)$ work.

`HouseholderDecomposition::factor(&a, orientation, pivoting)` groups reflections in compact-WY form $I - VTV^{\mathsf H}$ and uses matrix products for trailing-column updates and the action of $Q$.
$Q$ remains stored as reflections and is formed only when needed with `to_q_matrix()` (`apply_q` applies it without forming it).
The factorization also retains a copy of the original matrix for residual measurement.
Factorization does not fail for rank-deficient matrices; rank is assessed at solve time.

Householder QR is backward stable.
Under the standard rounding model, for the computed $\hat R$ there exist an exactly orthogonal $Q$ and a $\Delta A$ satisfying $A + \Delta A = Q \hat R$ and $\lVert \Delta a_j \rVert_2 \le \tilde\gamma_{mn} \lVert a_j \rVert_2$ ($j = 1, \dots, n$) (Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, Theorem 19.4).
$\tilde\gamma_{k} = cku/(1 - cku)$ contains a small constant $c$ and assumes $cku < 1$.
This is a columnwise relative-error bound, and the computed $\hat Q$ is also only of order $\tilde\gamma_{mn}$ away from an orthogonal matrix.
The computed least-squares solution $\hat x$ is likewise the exact solution of $\min \lVert (A + \Delta A) x - (b + \Delta b) \rVert_2$ ($\lVert \Delta b \rVert_2 \le \tilde\gamma_{mn} \lVert b \rVert_2$), with perturbations of the same columnwise size (ibid., Theorem 20.3).
These are a priori bounds describing the algorithm's properties, rather than estimates returned by `scioxide-linalg` on each execution.

## Least-squares sensitivity and normal equations

Converting backward error to forward error requires the sensitivity of the least-squares solution to perturbations.
Let the residual be $r = b - Ax$ and the angle between $b$ and the range of $A$ be $\theta$ ($\sin\theta = \lVert r \rVert_2 / \lVert b \rVert_2$).
If $A$ has full column rank, $\sin\theta < 1$, the perturbations satisfy $\lVert \Delta A \rVert_2 \le \epsilon \lVert A \rVert_2$ and $\lVert \Delta b \rVert_2 \le \epsilon \lVert b \rVert_2$, and $\epsilon\,\kappa_2(A) < 1$, the perturbed solution $x + \Delta x$ satisfies the following bound:

$$
\frac{\lVert \Delta x \rVert_2}{\lVert x \rVert_2} \le \epsilon \left( \frac{2\kappa_2(A)}{\cos\theta} + \kappa_2(A)^2 \tan\theta \right) + O(\epsilon^2)
$$

This is Wedin's bound (Golub and Van Loan, *Matrix Computations*, Theorem 5.3.1).
To derive it, differentiate the normal equations with respect to perturbation size; the first-order term is $\Delta x \approx A^{+}(\Delta b - \Delta A\, x) + (A^{\mathsf H} A)^{-1} \Delta A^{\mathsf H} r$.
Using $\lVert A^{+} \rVert_2 = 1/\sigma_n$, $\lVert (A^{\mathsf H} A)^{-1} \rVert_2 = 1/\sigma_n^2$, and $\lVert Ax \rVert_2 = \lVert b \rVert_2 \cos\theta$, the first term gives $2\kappa_2/\cos\theta$, and the second, proportional to the residual, gives $\kappa_2^2 \tan\theta$.
This bound measures relative error: with zero residual the condition number is $\kappa_2$, while a large residual makes it proportional to $\kappa_2^2$.

Substituting a backward error $\epsilon \approx u$ gives a QR forward error of roughly $u(\kappa_2 + \kappa_2^2 \tan\theta)$.
Solving the normal equations $A^{\mathsf H} A x = A^{\mathsf H} b$ by Cholesky approximately halves the operation count, but forming $A^{\mathsf H} A$ creates a problem with $\kappa_2(A^{\mathsf H} A) = \kappa_2(A)^2$, giving an error of order $u\kappa_2^2$ regardless of the residual.
The difference between the two is greatest for problems with small residuals and small $\tan\theta$.

The QR factorization's standard form `qr.solve(&b)` returns the least-squares solution $x = P R_1^{-1} (Q^{\mathsf H} b)_1$ from a QR factorization with $m \ge n$.
It uses the default policy (`RankPolicy::Exact`), so only an exactly zero diagonal entry of $R$ causes a `Singular` error; an LQ factorization or QR with $m < n$ gives `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).
The form with options `solve_with_options(&b, &options)` determines acceptance from a numerical-rank threshold or reciprocal-condition lower bound; without full column rank it returns an `Ok` report whose `termination()` is `RankDeficient` and does not commit a solution to the output.

The one-shot function `least_squares(&a, &b)` combines column-pivoted QR with the minimum-norm preparation in the next section and returns the minimum-norm least-squares solution for tall, square, or wide $A$.
The numerical rank counts diagonal entries of $R$ exceeding $\tau = \min(\max(m, n)\,\varepsilon,\ 1/2) \cdot \max_i \lvert r_{ii} \rvert$ (`RankPolicy::DimensionScaledEpsilon`); rank-deficient $R$ is handled by a complete orthogonal decomposition, so rank deficiency is not an error.
For $m \ge n$ the operation count is about $2mn^2 - \tfrac{2}{3}n^3$, using the sequential context `ExecutionContext::sequential_native()`.
Failures are `DimensionMismatch`, `NonFinite`, `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`) for solution overflow, and `AllocationFailure` for allocation failure.
For another rank policy or reciprocal-condition lower bound, use `HouseholderDecomposition::into_minimum_norm`.

The three cells in this section and the trust-region section use the same fitting problem.
`monomial_matrix` constructs an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ with $a_{ij} = t_i^{\,j}$ at $m$ points $t_i = i/(m-1)$ in $[0, 1]$.
With true coefficients $c = (1, \dots, 1)$, `fitting_rhs` constructs the right-hand side $b = Ac + s\,w/\lVert w \rVert_2$.
$w = g - A x_g$ is a least-squares residual for a vector $g$ and is therefore orthogonal to the range of $A$; the least-squares solution is $c$ for every $s$, with residual norm $s$.

```rust
//| id: linalg-polynomial-fit-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Polynomial fitting in a monomial basis"
//| crates: [scioxide-linalg]
use scioxide_linalg::{
    HouseholderDecomposition, HouseholderOrientation, HouseholderPivoting, Matrix,
};

/// An m × n matrix with a_ij = t_i^j at m points t_i = i/(m − 1) in [0, 1].
pub(crate) fn monomial_matrix(m: usize, n: usize) -> Result<Matrix<f64>, String> {
    let entries = (0..m * n).map(|k| ((k / n) as f64 / (m - 1) as f64).powi((k % n) as i32));
    Matrix::from_vec(m, n, entries.collect()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())
}

/// b = A c + s w / ‖w‖₂ (c = (1, …, 1), w orthogonal to the range of A).
pub(crate) fn fitting_rhs(a: &Matrix<f64>, s: f64) -> Result<Vec<f64>, String> {
    let (m, n) = (a.rows(), a.cols());
    // w = g − A x_g is the least-squares residual for g and is orthogonal to the range of A.
    let g: Vec<f64> = (0..m).map(|i| ((i * 37 % 11) as f64 - 5.0) / 5.0).collect();
    let qr =
        HouseholderDecomposition::factor(a, HouseholderOrientation::Qr, HouseholderPivoting::None)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let xg = qr.solve(&g[..]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let ag = a.view().mul_vec(&xg).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let w: Vec<f64> = g.iter().zip(ag.as_slice()).map(|(p, q)| p - q).collect();
    let w_norm = w.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
    let ac = a
        .view()
        .mul_vec(&vec![1.0_f64; n][..])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(ac
        .as_slice()
        .iter()
        .zip(&w)
        .map(|(p, q)| p + s * q / w_norm)
        .collect())
}
```

The following cell fits a degree-$d$ polynomial at $m = 40$ points ($n = d + 1$).
Inputs are degree $d$ and exponent $s'$ of the residual magnitude $s = 10^{s'}$; outputs are a table of problem quantities and a table of forward errors (maximum norm) for three methods.
The methods are QR's standard `solve`, the one-shot `least_squares`, and normal equations formed as $A^{\mathsf T}A$ and solved by Cholesky, compared with reference scales $u\kappa_2$ and $u\kappa_2^2$.

With defaults $d = 8$ ($\kappa_2 \approx 6.2 \times 10^{5}$) and $s = 10^{-12}$, QR's `solve` has error $9.3 \times 10^{-12}$ no greater than $u\kappa_2 = 6.8 \times 10^{-11}$, while the normal-equation error $7.2 \times 10^{-6}$ has the same order as $u\kappa_2^2 = 4.2 \times 10^{-5}$; the two differ by about 6 orders of magnitude.
The error $4.1 \times 10^{-11}$ of `least_squares` is also at most $u\kappa_2$.
`least_squares` includes column pivoting and minimum-norm preparation, so its error differs from QR's `solve`.
Setting $s = 1$ gives $\tan\theta \approx 0.044$ and increases QR error through the $\kappa_2^2 \tan\theta$ term to $6.9 \times 10^{-9}$ (`least_squares` gives $1.5 \times 10^{-7}$); the gap from the normal-equation error $1.7 \times 10^{-5}$ narrows to about 3 orders for QR's `solve` and 2 for `least_squares`.
Both QR errors remain below the first-order estimate $u(\kappa_2 + \kappa_2^2 \tan\theta) \approx 1.9 \times 10^{-6}$.
At $d = 12$, $u\kappa_2^2$ exceeds 1, the rounded $A^{\mathsf T} A$ loses positive definiteness, and Cholesky fails with `DomainError`, but both QR methods solve with error around $2 \times 10^{-8}$.

## Column pivoting, minimum-norm solutions, LQ, and RQ

When column dependence is suspected, use column-pivoted QR (`HouseholderPivoting::Column`, the default).
Each step moves the remaining column of largest 2-norm to the front, placing the absolute diagonal entries of $R$ in descending order; the number of small entries indicates numerical rank.
This ordering does not establish rank: examples such as the Kahan matrix have very small singular values despite no small diagonal entries, so use [SVD](../svd-and-rank/) for a definitive assessment.
Column norms are updated by subtraction at each step and recomputed only when cancellation makes them unreliable (Drmač–Bujanović).

QR's `solve` requires full column rank; to allow rank deficiency, convert a column-pivoted factorization to `HouseholderMinimumNormPlan` with `into_minimum_norm(&options)` (factors without column pivoting are rejected with `InvalidInput`).
For rank $r$ selected by `options`, this preparation further factors the trapezoid formed by the first $r$ rows of $R$ as $\begin{bmatrix} T & 0 \end{bmatrix} Z$ ($T$ is upper triangular and $Z$ unitary).
This is the complete orthogonal decomposition $AP = Q \begin{bmatrix} T & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} Z$ of the truncated matrix $A_r$, performed once in $O(r^2 n)$ operations.
Subsequent `solve(&b)` calls return the least-squares solution of $A_r$ having minimum 2-norm, $x = P Z^{\mathsf H} [T^{-1}(Q^{\mathsf H} b)_1;\ 0]$, in $O(mn + rn + r^2)$ per right-hand side.
Rank is determined once during preparation and read with `rank()`.
`solve` returns `DirectSolveResult`; rank deficiency is not a reason for rejection.
A rank-deficient Plan has estimated reciprocal condition number 0, so `options` with a positive reciprocal-condition lower bound rejects it with `IllConditioned`.
`options` also controls backward-error diagnostics and execution limits for each solve.

`HouseholderOrientation::Lq` is an LQ factorization $A[p, :] = LQ$ of the rows of $A$ ($L$ is lower trapezoidal and $Q$ is $n \times n$ unitary), computed as QR of $A^{\mathsf H}$ and used for underdetermined systems with more unknowns than equations.
A row-pivoted LQ can also be converted to minimum-norm preparation by `into_minimum_norm`.
`RqDecomposition::factor(&a)` gives $A = RQ$ ($Q$ is $n \times n$ unitary, and $R$ is $m \times n$ upper trapezoidal with its diagonal ending at the bottom-right corner); it explicitly holds both factors, read with `r_matrix()` and `q_matrix()`.
It uses reversal permutations $J$ and an unpivoted Householder QR of $J_n A^{\mathsf H} J_m$.

Solve the damped problem $\min \lVert Ax - b \rVert_2^2 + \lambda \lVert x \rVert_2^2$ from real QR with $m \ge n$ using `qr.solve_damped(&b, λ)` (standard form) or `solve_damped_with_options(&b, λ, &options)` (form with options).
This is least squares for the augmented system $\begin{bmatrix} A \\ \sqrt\lambda I \end{bmatrix} x \approx \begin{bmatrix} b \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$: compute $Q^{\mathsf H}b$, then incorporate the rows $\sqrt\lambda I$ into $R$ with the next section's Givens rotations, costing $O(mn + n^3)$.
It forms neither a second factorization nor the normal equations $(A^{\mathsf H}A + \lambda I)x = A^{\mathsf H}b$, whose condition number squares that of $A$.
$\lambda = 0$ gives the ordinary least-squares solution.

## Givens rotations

Householder reflections eliminate many column components at once; Givens rotations eliminate them one at a time by rotations in a plane.
For real $(a, b)$, set $\lvert r \rvert = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$, $c = a/r$, $s = b/r$, and $G = \begin{bmatrix} c & s \\ -s & c \end{bmatrix}$; then $G (a, b)^{\mathsf T} = (r, 0)^{\mathsf T}$, and $c^2 + s^2 = 1$ makes $G$ orthogonal.
Application to a pair of vectors costs four multiplications and two additions per component; the local changes suit elimination of a few nonzeros introduced into Hessenberg or triangular matrices.
Direct evaluation of $a^2 + b^2$ can overflow or underflow, so `scioxide-linalg` scales extreme inputs by powers of two before computing $r$; $r$ overflows only if $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ itself overflows.

`givens_rotation(a, b)` constructs a rotation with the real BLAS xROTG convention and returns `GivensRotationResult` (the scoped API accepting a context for cancellation or a BLAS provider is `givens_rotation_scoped(a, b, &options, context)`).
`r()` is the first component after rotation; its sign follows $a$ if $\lvert a \rvert > \lvert b \rvert$, and $b$ otherwise.
`z()` is the BLAS reconstruction value encoding the rotation in one number: $s$ if $\lvert a \rvert > \lvert b \rvert$; otherwise $1/c$ when $c \ne 0$, and 1 when $c = 0$.
`GivensRotation` returned by `rotation()` is a value type holding coefficients $c$ (`cosine()`) and $s$ (`sine()`).
For $a = b = 0$, it returns the identity rotation and $r = z = 0$.

`GivensRotation::new(c, s)` checks only finiteness and does not normalize to $c^2 + s^2 = 1$, so manually constructed values must specify a unit rotation.
Apply a rotation to two vectors with `VectorViewMut::rotate_in_place(rotation, &options, x, y, &mut workspace, context)`, replacing each pair by $x_i \leftarrow c x_i + s y_i$ and $y_i \leftarrow c y_i - s x_i$ (BLAS xROT); reserve workspace with `DenseWorkspaceRequest::vector_pair`.
Neither vector changes on failure.
The types and functions in this section handle only real scalars; complex plane rotations are used internally by QR updates.

## Trust-region subproblems

At each iteration, nonlinear least squares with Levenberg–Marquardt solves the following subproblem for Jacobian $A$, residual $b$, diagonal scaling matrix $D$, and trust-region radius $\Delta$:

$$
\min_p \lVert Ap - b \rVert_2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \lVert Dp \rVert_2 \le \Delta
$$

Nonlinear least squares in scioxide-optimize also calls `solve_trust_region_into`, described in this section.
The optimality conditions give a solution of the following form, parametrized by $\lambda \ge 0$:

$$
p(\lambda) = (A^{\mathsf T} A + \lambda D^{\mathsf T} D)^{-1} A^{\mathsf T} b, \qquad \lambda \bigl(\lVert D p(\lambda) \rVert_2 - \Delta\bigr) = 0
$$


If the unconstrained solution $p(0)$ satisfies $\lVert Dp(0) \rVert_2 \le \Delta$, use $\lambda = 0$; otherwise seek $\lambda > 0$ (the Levenberg–Marquardt parameter) satisfying $\lVert D p(\lambda) \rVert_2 = \Delta$.
$\phi(\lambda) = \lVert Dp(\lambda) \rVert_2 - \Delta$ decreases monotonically with $\lambda$ but has a pole-like shape, so applying Newton directly to $\phi$ converges slowly.
Moré's method applies Newton to the nearly linear $1/\lVert Dp(\lambda) \rVert_2 - 1/\Delta$, using the update

$$
\lambda^{+} = \lambda - \frac{\phi(\lambda)}{\phi'(\lambda)} \cdot \frac{\lVert D p(\lambda) \rVert_2}{\Delta}
$$

Repeated updates are safeguarded in $[\ell, \upsilon]$ using Moré's method.
The upper bound is $\upsilon = \lVert D^{-1} A^{\mathsf T} b \rVert_2 / \Delta$, and the lower bound is the Newton value at $\lambda = 0$ (or 0 if unavailable).
Each iteration replaces $\ell$ or $\upsilon$ by the current $\lambda$ according to the sign of $\phi$ and sets the next value to $\max(\ell, \lambda^{+})$.
If the update is non-finite, use Moré's safeguard $\max(\upsilon/1000, \sqrt{\ell\upsilon})$.

`HouseholderDecomposition::solve_trust_region_into(&b, &diagonal, radius, hint, &options, x, &mut ws, cx)` is an API with caller-provided output solving the subproblem with $D = \operatorname{diag}(\mathrm{diagonal})$ from real QR with $m \ge n$.
If $\lVert Dp(0) \rVert_2 \le 1.1\Delta$, set $\lambda = 0$; otherwise start Moré iteration from `hint` (for example, $\lambda$ from the previous iteration) clipped to $[\ell, \upsilon]$ and continue until $\lVert Dp(\lambda) \rVert_2$ is within 10% of $\Delta$, no further improvement is possible, or 10 iterations are reached.
Each $p(\lambda)$ reuses $R$ and $Q^{\mathsf T}b$ and eliminates rows $\sqrt\lambda D$ by Givens rotations, avoiding normal equations and costing $O(n^2)$ per iteration.
Overflow in $Q^{\mathsf T}b$ or in a component of the step $p(\lambda)$ for $\lambda > 0$ gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
A factorization failing the rank or condition checks in `options` returns a report with that termination reason without writing a solution, leaving the output unchanged.
No allocation is needed if `HouseholderWorkspaceRequest::damped_solve` has been reserved in the workspace.

`ThinSvdDecomposition::solve_trust_region_into(&b, radius, hint, rank, &options, x, &mut ws, cx)` solves the subproblem with $D = I$ from a thin SVD $A = U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$.
It discards singular values at or below the threshold from rank policy `rank` and selects $\lambda$ for $p(\lambda) = \sum_i \sigma_i (u_i^{\mathsf T} b) / (\sigma_i^2 + \lambda)\, v_i$ using the same safeguarded iteration.
This solve is always accepted; its report includes rank but no condition estimate or backward error.

Both return `LeastSquaresTrustRegionReport`.
`termination()` returns `DirectSolveTermination`; `solve_report()` returns a `DirectSolveReport` with rank, condition, and backward-error diagnostics; `damping()` returns the selected $\lambda \ge 0$; and `step_norm()` returns the step measured in the trust-region norm ($\lVert Dp \rVert_2$ for QR and $\lVert p \rVert_2$ for SVD), the latter two being `None` on rejection.
A step with $\lambda = 0$ has norm at most $1.1\Delta$.
A radius that is not finite and positive, `hint` that is not finite and nonnegative, or a component of `diagonal` that is not finite and positive gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`); an LQ factorization or QR with $m < n$ gives `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).

## QR updates

When rows or columns are added or removed one at a time, as in moving-window regression or sequential observations, there is no need to repeat a $O(mn^2)$ factorization each time.
`QrUpdateDecomposition` stores the complete column-pivoted QR factorization $A\Pi = QR$ explicitly as $m \times m$ $Q$ and $m \times n$ $R$.
`modify(edit)` and its form with options `modify_with_options(edit, &options)` return a new factorization and report incorporating one change; the API with caller-provided output `modify_in_place` modifies the factorization in place.
A new factorization (`factor(&a)`, `factor_with_options(&a, &options)`, or `from_householder` from an existing `HouseholderDecomposition`) costs $O(m^2 n)$ operations and $O(m^2 + mn)$ storage.
Nonzeros introduced into $R$ by a change are eliminated by plane rotations between adjacent rows (including complex scalars); the rotations are also applied together to $Q$, costing $O(m^2 + mn)$ per elementary change.
The change `QrModification` supports row insertion `InsertRows`, row deletion `DeleteRows`, column insertion `InsertColumns`, column deletion `DeleteColumns`, and low-rank addition or subtraction `RankUpdate` ($A + UV^{\mathsf H}$) and `RankDowndate` ($A - UV^{\mathsf H}$); block changes are applied as sequences of elementary changes for each row, column, or column of $U$.
Column indices refer to columns of $A$, not the pivoted order of $R$.
Changes preserve the column permutation without choosing new pivots.

`QrUpdateOptions::new(policy)` holds the rank policy, column-pivoting choice (`with_pivoting`, column pivoting by default), and execution limits; `QrUpdateOptions::default()` uses `RankPolicy::Exact` and column pivoting.
Each factorization or change determines the rank of $R$ using that rank policy and returns the before-and-after assessments in `QrUpdateReport` (`before()`, `after()`, and `rank_lost()` indicating whether rank decreased).
Assessment does not truncate the factorization.
Assessment uses incremental condition estimation for $R$ (Bischof's method) in $O(mn)$ operations.
If the estimate is at least a factor of 16 from the threshold, use it; otherwise compute the singular values of $R$ to decide.
When the estimate decides, the Frobenius-norm bound $\sigma_{r+1} \le \lVert R_{22} \rVert_F$ shows that no more than $r$ singular values exceed the threshold, but the claim that $r$ values exceed it is an empirical assessment based on estimating the smallest singular value.
The `QrRankSource` returned by `QrRankDecision::source()` records which was used: `Estimator` for the estimate or `SingularValues` for singular values.
To apply the same estimate to a caller-owned triangular factor, use `TriangularRankWorkspace` from [Structured linear systems](oxref:linalg-triangular-systems).

};

            QrRankSource::Estimator => "Estimator",

            step,
            x[0],

            diff,
            s[0] / s[1],

            source,
        ));
    }
}

let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([

## Summary

Householder QR solves least squares backward stably; with full column rank and $\epsilon\kappa_2 < 1$, forward error is roughly $u(\kappa_2 + \kappa_2^2 \tan\theta)$.
Normal equations incur errors of order $u\kappa_2^2$ regardless of the residual, so use QR for ill-conditioned problems with small residuals.
The one-shot `least_squares` returns minimum-norm solutions, including rank-deficient cases, using column-pivoted QR and complete orthogonal decomposition; use `HouseholderMinimumNormPlan` of `into_minimum_norm` for another rank policy.
Levenberg–Marquardt subproblems can be solved from QR or SVD with `solve_trust_region_into`; the report's `damping()` and `step_norm()` show the selected $\lambda$ and step norm.
For gradually changing rows or columns, `QrUpdateDecomposition` incorporates one change with plane rotations in $O(m^2 + mn)$ and returns before-and-after rank assessments and their basis.
