In one-dimensional finite-difference problems, spline interpolation, and time-series autocorrelation, many coefficient-matrix entries are zero or determined by a few repeated values.
Using this structure reduces dense factorization work $O(n^3)$ and storage $O(n^2)$ to $O(n)$ for tridiagonal matrices, quantities proportional to $n$ and products of bandwidths for banded matrices, and $O(n)$ coefficient storage with $O(n^2)$ work per right-hand side for Toeplitz matrices.
This page describes the structured matrix types of `scioxide-linalg` and the assumptions, costs, and failure handling of their direct methods.
Acceptance uses the same `DirectSolveOptions` as dense factorizations; its contract is explained in [Separating factorization from solution acceptance](oxref:linalg-factorization-acceptance).
Call-form names—standard form, form with options, API with caller-provided output, and one-shot function—are defined in [Call forms](oxref:linalg-execution-forms).

## LU factorization of tridiagonal matrices

A tridiagonal matrix $T$ is determined by its subdiagonal $l_i$, diagonal $d_i$, and superdiagonal $u_i$.
`TridiagonalMatrix::new(lower, main, upper)` owns vectors of lengths $n - 1, n, n - 1$, and `TridiagonalMatrixView::new(&lower, &main, &upper)` borrows the same three vectors.
The owned `TridiagonalMatrix` is not an operator; the view obtained with `view()` implements core's `LinearOperator` and `AdjointOperator`, so the view can serve as an iterative-method operator.

`TridiagonalLuDecomposition::factor(&matrix)` performs LU with adjacent pivoting, selecting the larger absolute pivot from the current and next rows at each step; every multiplier has magnitude at most 1.
A row exchange brings the next row's superdiagonal into the pivot row, introducing a second superdiagonal in $U$.
The factorization holds the diagonal, superdiagonal, and second superdiagonal `second_superdiagonal()` of $U$ (length $n - 2$), multipliers in elimination order, and exchanged rows `pivots()` (`pivots()[k]` is $k$ or $k+1$).
The unpivoted Thomas algorithm is the special case without row exchanges and can break down at zero or small pivots on matrices without diagonal dominance.
Column $k$ has nonzeros only in rows $k$ and $k+1$, so adjacent pivoting equals ordinary partial pivoting; in exact arithmetic a nonsingular $T$ never has a zero pivot.
Apart from input checks, factorization fails only with `Singular` for an exactly zero pivot or `NumericalInstability` for overflow in an entry of $U$.
Multipliers cannot overflow because their magnitudes are at most 1; as in xGTTRF, multipliers below the normal range round to subnormals or 0 without an error.
Factorization and solution both cost $O(n)$; the factors also hold copies of the original three diagonals (about $3n$ entries) for residual measurement, giving about $7n$ scalars and $n$ indices of storage.

The first argument `StructuredOperation` of `factor.solve(op, &b)` selects $T x = b$ (`Normal`), $T^{\mathsf T} x = b$ (`Transpose`), or $T^{\mathsf H} x = b$ (`Adjoint`).
All solves with tridiagonal, banded, and triangular structured factors take this argument before the right-hand side; standard-form `solve(op, &b)` assesses acceptance with the default policy (`RankPolicy::Exact`) and returns the solution.
The form with options `solve_with_options(op, &b, &options)` assesses acceptance with the same `DirectSolveOptions` as dense factorizations (rank policy, optional reciprocal-condition lower bound, optional backward-error diagnostics), returning an `Ok` `DirectSolveResult` even on rejection.
Substitution scales by powers of two to avoid overflow.
For APIs with caller-provided output, obtain workspace requirements by passing `StructuredWorkspaceRequest::factor(n, 1, 1, &options)` for factorization or `StructuredWorkspaceRequest::solve(n, &options)` for solution to `workspace_requirements`.

## Cyclic tridiagonal matrices with periodic boundaries

One-dimensional periodic problems add corner entries $\beta, \alpha$ at the top-right $(0, n-1)$ and bottom-left $(n-1, 0)$ of a tridiagonal matrix.
This cyclic tridiagonal matrix $A$ is not banded, but reordering rows and columns in the same order $0, n-1, 1, n-2, \dots$ makes it banded with lower and upper bandwidths 2.
In that order, originally adjacent indices $i$ and $i+1$ differ in position by at most 2, while $0$ and $n-1$ joined by the corner entries become adjacent.

`CyclicTridiagonalPlan::new(view, corner_upper, corner_lower, &options)` constructs this banded matrix during preparation and factors it by band LU with partial pivoting (`BandedLuDecomposition`).
With bandwidth 2, partial-pivoting growth is bounded by a constant independent of $n$, making elimination backward stable for every nonsingular $A$ (Golub and Van Loan, Section 4.3).
A solve reorders the right-hand side, substitutes with the band factors, and restores the solution order, costing $O(n)$ per right-hand side.
Rank, condition estimates, and backward error also use these same band-LU factors.
Factorization fails with `Singular` for an exactly zero pivot during band elimination or `NumericalInstability` for overflow in a factor entry.
The order must be at least 3 (at $n = 2$, corner entries overlap ordinary off-diagonal entries).
Preparation and solution both cost $O(n)$, with $O(n)$ storage.
The Plan retains only the preparation byte limit; provide solution-acceptance conditions on each call through `DirectSolveOptions`.

Another well-known approach splits $A$ into $T' + uv^{\mathsf T}$, the sum of a tridiagonal matrix $T'$ with only the first and last diagonal entries changed and a rank-one correction, then uses Sherman–Morrison.
This split can make $T'$ or $1 + v^{\mathsf T}T'^{-1}u$ ill-conditioned even if $A$ is well-conditioned, so the Plan and low-level factorization in `scioxide-linalg` do not use it.

The following cell discretizes and solves the periodic problem $-u'' + c\,u = f$ by second differences on $N$ points in $[0, 2\pi)$.
Inputs are grid-point count $N$, wavenumber $k$ of the right-hand side $f(x) = \sin(kx)$, and reaction coefficient $c > 0$.
Since $\sin(kx)$ is an eigenvector of the discrete Laplacian, the exact discrete solution is $u_i = \sin(k x_i) / \lambda_k$ with $\lambda_k = \tfrac{4}{h^2}\sin^2(kh/2) + c$.
The difference between the numerical and exact discrete solutions measures rounding error alone; the difference from the continuous solution $\sin(kx)/(k^2 + c)$ measures the discretization error $O(h^2)$.
Use the form with options `solve_with_options` to record backward error in the report, and also compare with a solution from the low-level `CyclicTridiagonalFactor` described below.
The chart shows the numerical and continuous solutions, with position $x$ horizontally and solution $u$ vertically.

```rust
let h = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI / n as f64;
let off = -1.0 / (h * h);
let (lower, main, upper) = (
    vec![off; n - 1],
    vec![2.0 / (h * h) + reaction; n],
    vec![off; n - 1],
);
// Corner entries (0, N−1) and (N−1, 0) are also −1/h².
let view = TridiagonalMatrixView::new(&lower, &main, &upper).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plan = CyclicTridiagonalPlan::new(view, off, off, &LinalgExecutionOptions::new())

// Grid points and right-hand side f(x_i) = sin(k x_i). Use standard f64 functions for these reference values.

    .solve_with_options(StructuredOperation::Normal, &f[..], &options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Solve the same system with the low-level factorization too (without acceptance checks or diagnostics).
let factor =

factor.solve_in_place(&mut raw).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    continuum.push([t, exact]);

    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },

        ("Maximum difference from continuous solution", Some(discretization)),
        ("Relative discretization error", Some(discretization * (k * k + reaction))),

        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Numerical solution", "points": numeric, "style": { "showSymbol": true } }),

            "name": "Continuous solution sin(kx)/(k²+c)",
            "points": continuum,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }

    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Position x [rad]",
```

With defaults $N = 64$, $k = 3$, and $c = 1$, the difference from the exact discrete solution is rounding error $1.9 \times 10^{-16}$, and the reported backward error is $9.1 \times 10^{-17}$.
The relative difference from the continuous solution is $6.5 \times 10^{-3}$; at $N = 128$ it becomes $1.6 \times 10^{-3}$, nearly one quarter, agreeing with the $O(h^2)$ accuracy of second differences.
At $N = 8$, relative discretization error grows to 0.53, and the plotted numerical amplitude exceeds that of the continuous solution.
As $k$ approaches $N/2$, $hk$ grows and relative discretization error increases ($8.3 \times 10^{-2}$ for $N = 64$ and $k = 10$); as $c$ approaches 0, the constant-mode eigenvalue $c$ shrinks and $A$ approaches singularity (at $c = 0.05$, the difference from the exact discrete solution grows to $1.0 \times 10^{-15}$).
The Plan and low-level `CyclicTridiagonalFactor` use the same reordering and band LU, so their solutions differ by 0 for the default and both cases.

## Banded and packed matrices

`BandedMatrix::new(rows, cols, lower, upper, leading_dimension, data)` holds a banded matrix $A$ with lower bandwidth $p_l$ and upper bandwidth $p_u$ (entries with $i > j + p_l$ or $j > i + p_u$ are zero) in column-major band storage.
Entry $(i, j)$ is stored at `data[j * leading_dimension + upper + i - j]`; the trailing `leading_dimension − lower − upper − 1` entries of each column and positions outside the matrix are unread padding.
`BandedLuDecomposition::factor(&a)` performs band LU with partial pivoting, selecting the largest absolute entry in rows $k, \dots, k + p_l$ of column $k$ at step $k$.
Row exchanges expand the upper bandwidth of $U$ to $p_l + p_u$; factors use compact band storage (`factors()`) with $2p_l + p_u + 1$ scalars per column, so input `BandedMatrix` needs no extra space.
Factorization costs $O(n\,p_l (p_l + p_u))$ and each right-hand-side solve $O(n(2p_l + p_u))$; with fixed bandwidths these are proportional to $n$.
Bands with lower bandwidth at least 16 are divided into panels and updated with matrix products.
Factorization fails only for exactly zero pivots or overflow in $U$; multipliers below the normal range round to subnormals or 0 as in xGBTRF, preserving normwise backward stability of the factors.
Obtain workspace requirements with `StructuredWorkspaceRequest::factor(n, p_l, p_u, &options)`.

Store a Hermitian positive-definite band matrix in `HermitianBandMatrix::new(n, k, triangle, data)`, which holds one triangle with $k + 1$ entries per column, and factor it as $A = L L^{\mathsf H}$ with `BandedCholeskyDecomposition::factor(&a)`.
The operation count is $O(n k^2)$; read factor $L$ as a band-matrix view with `lower()`.
Failure of positive definiteness gives `DomainError`.

`PackedMatrix::new(n, triangle, kind, data)` holds one triangle of a square matrix in $n(n+1)/2$ entries packed columnwise; `PackedKind` selects whether the unstored side is zero (`Triangular`) or its conjugate reflection (`Hermitian`).
Owned banded, Hermitian-banded, and packed types are not operators; their views `BandedMatrixView`, `HermitianBandMatrixView`, and `PackedMatrixView` obtained with `view()` implement products `mul_vec` and core's operator contracts.
Views can also borrow caller slices directly with `new`, checking shape, bandwidth, leading dimension, and length at construction.
For entry values, only Hermitian bands and `Hermitian` packed matrices check diagonal imaginary parts at construction; finiteness is checked during numerical computation.
`PackedMatrixViewMut` is a mutable packed-storage view; `set(i, j, value)` rejects writes to the unstored side and values with imaginary parts on a `Hermitian` diagonal.

## Triangular substitution and numerical rank

Solve $Tx = b$ for a triangular matrix $T$ in $n^2$ operations by forward substitution for a lower triangle or back substitution for an upper triangle.
Substitution is componentwise backward stable: the computed solution exactly satisfies $(T + \Delta T)\hat x = b$ with $\lvert \Delta T \rvert \le \gamma_n \lvert T \rvert$ (Higham, Chapter 8).
The one-shot function `solve_triangular(&a, triangle, &b)` reads only `triangle` (scioxide-core's `Triangle::Lower` or `Upper`) of dense `a`, treating an exactly zero diagonal as a `Singular` error.

For unit diagonals, banded or packed triangles, transposed systems, or repeated solves with the same $T$, use `TriangularPlan`.
`TriangularPlan::from_dense(a, triangle, diagonal)` accepts values convertible to dense-matrix views (`&Matrix` or `MatrixView`); `from_band(view, triangle, diagonal)` and `from_packed(view, diagonal)` accept banded and packed views (rejecting the packed `Hermitian` interpretation).
Each borrows without copying the matrix; `diagonal` (`DiagonalKind::Explicit` or `Unit`) determines whether to read diagonal entries.
Preparation reads each used entry once to check finiteness and nonzero diagonals (an exactly zero `Explicit` diagonal gives `Singular`) and records the maximum entry magnitude.
Solves cost $O(n^2)$ for dense or packed storage and $O(nk)$ for bands of bandwidth $k$; substitution is retried with power-of-two scaling on overflow or accuracy loss from underflow before division by a small pivot.
Solve forms match tridiagonal factors: standard `solve(op, &b)` and with options `solve_with_options(op, &b, &options)`.
Products with triangular matrices, including singular ones, use each storage view's `mul_vec` or `MatrixView::triangular_mul_mat`.

`TriangularRankWorkspace` is a workspace for estimating numerical rank of a caller-owned upper-trapezoidal factor $R$ (such as QR's $R$).
Allocate once with `TriangularRankWorkspace::new(rows, cols)`; `estimate(&r, policy)` returns `Option<RankReport>` in $O(mn)$ operations.
The estimate uses the same incremental condition estimation as [QR updates](oxref:linalg-qr-update); if at least a factor of 16 from the threshold, it determines rank, otherwise it returns `None` as undecided.
On `None`, use a rank-revealing factorization such as SVD.
A $R$ with nonzero entries below the diagonal is rejected with `InvalidInput`.

## Toeplitz, Hankel, and circulant matrices

Toeplitz matrices have constant diagonals, as in $t_{ij} = c_{i-j}$, and are determined by $2n - 1$ values.
A real symmetric (complex Hermitian) Toeplitz system $T_n x = b$ can be solved by Levinson–Durbin recurrence in $O(n^2)$ operations.
From the solution of the Yule–Walker equations $T_k y^{(k)} = -(c_1, \dots, c_k)^{\mathsf T}$ for the leading $k \times k$ principal submatrix $T_k$, update to order $k+1$ using the reflection coefficient

$$
\kappa_{k} = -\frac{c_{k+1} + \sum_{i=1}^{k} c_{k+1-i}\, y^{(k)}_i}{c_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{k} c_i\, y^{(k)}_i}
$$

The same recurrence also solves general right-hand sides.
The denominator is the prediction-error variance.
This recurrence assumes all leading principal submatrices are nonsingular and becomes unstable as the denominator approaches 0.

A Toeplitz matrix $T$ has displacement structure: $Z_{+} T - T Z_{-}$ has rank at most 2 for the cyclic shifts $Z_{+}$ and $Z_{-}$ whose top-right corners are $+1$ and $-1$.
The GKO (Gohberg–Kailath–Olshevsky) algorithm uses discrete Fourier transforms to move this displacement to diagonal shifts and form a Cauchy-like matrix with entries $g_i^{\mathsf T} h_j / (t_i - s_j)$ ($g_i, h_j \in \mathbb{C}^2$ are generator rows).
Row exchanges preserve this structure, allowing Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting on the generators even when principal submatrices are singular.
The cost is $O(n^2)$ per right-hand side; discrete Fourier transforms are evaluated by direct summation rather than FFT.

`StructuredSolvePlan::new(view, &StructuredSolveOptions::new())` accepts a square view constructed with `StructuredMatrixView::toeplitz`, `hankel`, or `circulant` and stores $O(n)$ values for power-of-two-scaled coefficients, twiddle factors, and circulant eigenvalues.
The operation `StructuredOperation` is specified per solve.
Real symmetric (Hermitian) Toeplitz systems first try Levinson–Durbin; a column is recomputed by pivoted GKO if prediction error falls to at most $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ relative to the largest coefficient, the recurrence becomes non-finite, or the solution fails the backward-error check.
Nonsymmetric Toeplitz and Hankel systems use GKO from the start; a Hankel matrix $A$ is reduced to the column-reversed Toeplitz matrix $AJ$.
Circulant matrices diagonalize under the discrete Fourier transform, so division by eigenvalues $\lambda_k$, the transform of the first column, solves them; direct summation again costs $O(n^2)$ per right-hand side.
If these $O(n^2)$ algorithms are also rejected, the solve is retried by $O(n^3)$ dense LU with partial pivoting only when `with_dense_fallback()` includes its storage in the workspace request.
LU is factored at most once per call and used only for rejected columns.
An exactly zero circulant eigenvalue, GKO elimination pivot, or dense-LU pivot fails with `Singular`; a zero GKO pivot does not trigger a dense-LU retry even with reserved storage.

`solve_into(op, &b, x, &mut ws, cx)` measures each column's rowwise backward error below before committing a solution to the output:

$$
\eta = \max_i \frac{\lvert b - \operatorname{op}(A) x \rvert_i}{\lvert b_i \rvert + \lVert x \rVert_\infty \sum_j \lvert \operatorname{op}(A)_{ij} \rvert}
$$


With tolerances $(\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}, \tau_{\mathrm{rel}})$ supplied by `StructuredSolveOptions::with_tolerance`, acceptance requires $\eta \le \tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}}$; the default `Tolerance::from_sqrt_epsilon()` sets $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}} = \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} = \sqrt{\varepsilon}$ and thus requires $\eta \le 2\sqrt{\varepsilon} \approx 3.0 \times 10^{-8}$.
No rank policy is used.
If a finite solution's backward error exceeds tolerance with no alternative algorithm, `termination()` in the `Ok` report becomes `DirectSolveTermination::ResidualRejected` and the output is unchanged.
Without a finite candidate that can be checked, the call fails with `NumericalInstability`.

$\eta$ is the smallest $\eta$ among perturbations making $\hat x$ exact and satisfying $\lVert \Delta A_{i,:} \rVert_1 \le \eta \lVert A_{i,:} \rVert_1$ and $\lvert \Delta b_i \rvert \le \eta \lvert b_i \rvert$ in every row (writing $\operatorname{op}(A)$ as $A$).
This $\Delta A$ generally does not preserve Toeplitz structure, so backward error restricted to structure-preserving perturbations may exceed $\eta$.
For positive-definite matrices, Levinson–Durbin is known to give residuals comparable to Cholesky (Cybenko, 1980).
For indefinite matrices, small prediction errors can destabilize the recurrence; GKO can have large residuals from generator growth, so the Plan measures $\eta$ per column regardless of algorithm.
Acceptance assesses backward error and is not a forward-error bound.
Forward error also depends on the conditioning of $A$; the Plan's `condition_estimate()` computes $\lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$ columnwise from $n$ structured solves on unit vectors and returns $1/(\lVert A \rVert_1 \lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1)$ (total $O(n^3)$).

Construct the workspace with `plan.workspace(&StructuredWorkspaceRequest::solve(n, &options))`; for Toeplitz and Hankel it includes a packed complex upper-triangular factor and working vectors for GKO.
The report `StructuredSolveReport` returns termination reason `termination()` (`Accepted` or `ResidualRejected`), maximum columnwise backward error `backward_error()`, and counts `levinson_solves()`, `gko_solves()`, `dense_lu_solves()`, and `circulant_solves()` by the algorithm used for accepted columns.

The one-shot function `solve_structured(matrix, &b)` constructs a Plan with default `StructuredSolveOptions` and solves without reserved dense-LU storage, returning an accepted solution.
Rejection becomes a `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`) error through `DirectSolveTermination::require_accepted`; views other than Toeplitz, Hankel, and circulant are rejected with `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).
Use `StructuredSolvePlan` for multiple right-hand sides, another tolerance, or dense-LU retries.

The following cell solves a Kac–Murdock–Szegő matrix $t_{ij} = \rho^{\lvert i - j \rvert}$.
Its inverse has the tridiagonal closed form $\tfrac{1}{1-\rho^2}\operatorname{tridiag}(-\rho,\ 1+\rho^2,\ -\rho)$ (with endpoint diagonal entries 1), allowing independent computation of $x = T^{-1} b$.
Inputs are correlation $\rho$, order $n$, whether to zero the leading entry $t_0$, and whether to reserve a dense-LU retry.
Zeroing the leading entry makes the first principal submatrix singular and prevents Levinson–Durbin; form the reference by materializing a dense matrix and using the one-shot `solve`.
Outputs are a table of algorithm counts and workspace bytes, an accuracy table, and an acceptance outcome.

```rust
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":0}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: zero-leading
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: near-unit-correlation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-11}}
//|   - case: reserved-fallback
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":100000}}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_linalg::{
    LinalgExecutionOptions, StructuredMatrixView, StructuredOperation, StructuredSolveOptions,
    StructuredSolvePlan, StructuredWorkspaceRequest, VectorViewMut, solve, solve_structured,
};

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let n = order.round() as usize;
let rho = correlation;
let options = LinalgExecutionOptions::new();
let mut column: Vec<f64> = (0..n).map(|k| rho.powi(k as i32)).collect();
if zero_leading {
    column[0] = 0.0;
}
let matrix = StructuredMatrixView::toeplitz(&column, &column).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// The operation op(A) is a per-solve argument, not a Plan setting.
let plan = StructuredSolvePlan::new(matrix, &StructuredSolveOptions::new())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let b: Vec<f64> = (0..n).map(|i| 1.0 + (i % 3) as f64).collect();
// When enabling a dense-LU retry, include its storage in the request and reserve it beforehand.
let mut request = StructuredWorkspaceRequest::solve(n, &options);
if dense_fallback {
    request = request.with_dense_fallback();
}
let bytes = plan
    .workspace_requirements(&request)
    .and_then(|r| r.required_bytes::<f64>())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut ws = plan.workspace(&request).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut x = vec![0.0_f64; n];
let report = plan
    .solve_into(
        StructuredOperation::Normal,
        &b[..],
        VectorViewMut::from_slice(&mut x),
        &mut ws,
        ExecutionContext::sequential_native(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let accepted = report.termination().is_success();
// Independent reference: the closed-form tridiagonal inverse for KMS; LU of the materialized matrix for t₀ = 0.
let reference: Vec<f64> = if zero_leading {
    let dense = matrix.materialize().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    solve(&dense, &b[..])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .as_slice()
        .to_vec()
} else {
    let s = 1.0 / (1.0 - rho * rho);
    (0..n)
        .map(|i| {
            let diagonal = if i == 0 || i == n - 1 {
                1.0
            } else {
                1.0 + rho * rho
            };
            let left = if i > 0 { b[i - 1] } else { 0.0 };
            let right = if i + 1 < n { b[i + 1] } else { 0.0 };
            s * (diagonal * b[i] - rho * (left + right))
        })
        .collect()
};
let max_diff = |p: &[f64], q: &[f64]| {
    p.iter()
        .zip(q)
        .fold(0.0_f64, |m, (u, v)| m.max((u - v).abs()))
};
let scale = reference.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, v| m.max(v.abs()));
let error = accepted.then(|| max_diff(&x, &reference) / scale);
// The one-shot function converts rejection to an error.
let one_shot = solve_structured(matrix, &b[..])
    .ok()
    .map(|y| max_diff(y.as_slice(), &x));
let bound = (!zero_leading).then(|| ((1.0 + rho.abs()) / (1.0 - rho.abs())).powi(2));
let method_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Levinson–Durbin [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "GKO [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Dense LU [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Workspace [B]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    method_columns,
    vec![(
        report.levinson_solves(),
        report.gko_solves(),
        report.dense_lu_solves(),
        bytes
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })
);
let accuracy_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Backward error η (report) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Relative difference from reference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "Not committed" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Upper bound on κ₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "Difference from solve_structured [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": sci2,
        "nullText": "Error"
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    accuracy_columns,
    vec![(report.backward_error(), error, bound, one_shot)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "accuracy" })
);
let outcome = if accepted {
    Outcome::satisfied("Backward error is below tolerance 2√ε; the solution was accepted")
} else {
    Outcome::failed("Backward error exceeds tolerance; the solution was not committed to the output")
        .explanation("Reserve a dense-LU retry or reconsider the tolerance.")
};
let status = match report.termination() {
    scioxide_linalg::DirectSolveTermination::Accepted => "Accepted",
    _ => "ResidualRejected",
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(status)
        .diagnostic("Backward error", report.backward_error(), None)
        .diagnostic("Levinson–Durbin", report.levinson_solves() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("GKO", report.gko_solves() as f64, None)
);
```

With defaults $\rho = 0.9$ and $n = 50$, Levinson–Durbin is used once; reported backward error is $1.0 \times 10^{-16}$, relative difference from the closed-form solution is $5.2 \times 10^{-15}$, and the outcome is satisfied with `Accepted`.
The one-shot `solve_structured` performs the same computation, so its solution differs by 0.
Even at $\rho = 0.99$, Levinson–Durbin solves; the relative difference grows to $1.0 \times 10^{-13}$ along with the condition-number bound $((1+\lvert\rho\rvert)/(1-\lvert\rho\rvert))^2 \approx 4.0 \times 10^{4}$.
Zeroing the leading entry makes Levinson–Durbin's first denominator 0, so pivoted GKO is used once, giving relative difference $1.6 \times 10^{-14}$ from dense LU.
In every case, a solution is committed only after its backward error falls below tolerance $2\sqrt{\varepsilon} \approx 3.0 \times 10^{-8}$.

GKO is not rejected for this matrix, so reserving dense-LU retry storage leaves it unused and only grows the workspace for $n = 50$ from 27,216 to 158,344 bytes.
The default 27,216 bytes comprise $n(n+1)/2 + 7n = 1625$ complex values for GKO's packed upper-triangular factor and working vectors (two `f64` values each), plus $3n + 2$ entries for staged right-hand-side storage.
Retry storage includes an $O(n^2)$ dense matrix and LU factors, so reserve it only when the ability to retry after rejection is needed.
Byte counts depend on the width of `usize`; reserved-storage values on 64-bit native environments exceed these Wasm values.

## Structured matrix generators and operator assembly

`StructuredMatrixView` is a structured matrix that borrows coefficients or holds only its order without storing entries; read entries with `get(i, j)`, form a dense matrix with `materialize()` (`materialize_with_options(&options)` checks result size against the `LinalgExecutionOptions` byte limit before allocation), and use products through core's `LinearOperator` and `AdjointOperator`.
Products evaluate each entry once, costing $O(mn)$ for an $m \times n$ matrix.
Pascal matrices and inverse Hilbert matrices are exceptions: each entry uses $O(n)$ binomial-coefficient work, making an order-$n$ product cost $O(n^3)$.
`StructuredMatrix::from_view(view)` copies and owns only Toeplitz, Hankel, or circulant coefficients; borrow it with `view()` for operator use.

| Generator | Matrix | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `toeplitz(column, row)`, `hankel(column, last_row)`, `circulant(column)` | Constant diagonals, constant antidiagonals, circulant | Solved by `StructuredSolvePlan` and `solve_structured` |
| `hilbert(n, form)` | $h_{ij} = 1/(i + j + 1)$ and its inverse (`MatrixForm::Direct`, `Inverse`) | Inverse has integer entries |
| `pascal(n, kind, form)` | Binomial coefficients (`PascalKind::Lower`, `Upper`, `Symmetric`) and their inverse | |
| `hadamard(n)`, `helmert(n, kind)` | A constant multiple of an orthogonal matrix; an orthogonal matrix (`HelmertKind::Full`, or `Reduced` omitting the constant row) | |
| `leslie(fertility, survival)`, `fiedler(values)`, `companion(coefficients, kind)` | Population matrix, $\lvert v_i - v_j \rvert$, polynomial companion matrix (`CompanionKind::Frobenius` or pentadiagonal `Fiedler`; coefficients in ascending powers) | |
| `convolution(kernel, input_len, mode)` | Convolution matrix (`ConvolutionMode::Full`, `Same`, `Valid`) | |
| `dft(n, direction, normalization)` | Complex Fourier matrix (`DftDirection::Forward`, `Inverse`, `DftNormalization::None`, `ByOrder`, `Unitary`) | Complex scalars only |

There are also types combining operators.
`KroneckerOperator` applies $A \otimes B$ through $(A \otimes B)\operatorname{vec}(X) = \operatorname{vec}(B X A^{\mathsf T})$, and `BlockDiagonalOperator` applies a block-diagonal collection, without forming entries.
Both solve through `solve_into` using the constituent inverse operators and report via `termination()` of `CompositeSolveReport` whether the original-system residual satisfies $\lVert b - Ax \rVert_\infty \le \tau_{\mathrm{abs}} s + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} (\lVert b \rVert_\infty + \lVert Ax \rVert_\infty)$ ($s = \max(\lVert b \rVert_\infty, \lVert Ax \rVert_\infty)$; tolerances from `CompositeSolveOptions`, both $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ by default).
`khatri_rao(&a, &b)` materializes the columnwise Kronecker product; `khatri_rao_with_options` and the API with caller-provided output `khatri_rao_into` are also available.
`operator_norm(op, norm)` computes operator 1-, $\infty$-, and Frobenius norms (`MatrixNorm`) exactly apart from rounding, using $n$ applications to basis vectors (the scoped API `operator_norm_scoped` takes workspace and context and uses one iteration from the limit per column).
For a square-matrix 1-norm estimate from few products, use `OneNormEstimator` in [Estimating the 1-norm and exact operator norms](../norm-estimation/).
`materialize_operator_into` forms a dense matrix from an operator; `apply_operator_matrix_into` applies it to each column of a matrix.
Obtain their workspace requirements by passing `OperatorWorkspaceRequest` requests to `LinalgWorkspace::workspace_requirements`.

## Low-level factorizations

`scioxide_linalg::backend` exposes low-level factorizations called directly by other SciOxide crates.
They may partially modify right-hand sides or working storage on failure, perform no rank-policy checks, residual or condition diagnostics, or iteration accounting, and leave acceptance to the caller.
Applications normally use `TridiagonalLuDecomposition` or `CyclicTridiagonalPlan` described above.

`backend::tridiagonal::TridiagonalFactor` is real tridiagonal LU with adjacent pivoting; `factor(lower, main, upper, execution)` takes three vectors and uses their storage directly for factors.
For a symmetric matrix with nonnegative off-diagonals, each diagonal at least twice the off-diagonal sum, and entries well inside the floating-point range, it skips pivoting, stores reciprocal pivots, and solves using multiplication alone.
`refactor(&lower, &main, &upper, execution)` refactors while reusing storage; `solve_in_place(values, execution)` overwrites the right-hand side with the solution.
`execution` (`ExecutionContext`) checks only cancellation and deadlines and does not count iterations.
Failures include shape mismatch, non-finite entries, `Singular` for exactly zero pivots, and `NumericalInstability` for multiplier or pivot overflow; solving after a failed `refactor` gives `InvalidInput`.
The factors hold neither a matrix copy nor diagnostics; factorization and solution both cost $O(n)$.

`backend::tridiagonal::CyclicTridiagonalFactor` is real cyclic-tridiagonal LU, using the same reordering as `CyclicTridiagonalPlan` to obtain bandwidth 2 and eliminate with partial pivoting.
`factor(lower, main, upper, corner_upper, corner_lower)` factors without a context, and `solve_in_place(values)` solves in $O(n)$.
Failures are `InvalidInput` for order below 3, shape mismatch, non-finite entries, `Singular` for exactly zero pivots during band elimination, and `NumericalInstability` for overflow in factors or solution entries; no diagnostics are computed.

`backend::bordered_bidiagonal::BorderedBidiagonalFactor` is LU with partial pivoting for bordered block-bidiagonal systems arising in Newton iterations for multiple shooting and collocation in boundary-value problems.
The $Nn + k$ unknowns consist of states at $N$ nodes ($n$ each) in node order, followed by $k$ parameters.
The first $(N-1)n$ rows couple adjacent nodes and parameters; the last $n + k$ rows are boundary conditions coupling endpoint nodes and parameters, each row holding $2n + k$ values including explicit zeros.
`new(n, k, nodes)` allocates storage together; `factor(&values, execution)` selects pivots over the entire current interval and remaining border rows, so individual interval blocks need not be nonsingular.
The final border block uses dense LU with partial pivoting, and `solve_in_place(&mut rhs, execution)` overwrites the right-hand side with the solution.
Storage is proportional to the node count (available in advance from `required_bytes(n, k, nodes)`); `resize(nodes)` changes the node count and invalidates factors without shrinking capacity.
A solve after failed factorization is rejected with `InvalidInput`; working-storage contents after failed factorization are unspecified.

## Summary

Tridiagonal systems are solved in $O(n)$ with `TridiagonalLuDecomposition`; adjacent pivoting avoids zero-pivot breakdown even for nonsingular matrices without diagonal dominance.
Pass periodic systems to `CyclicTridiagonalPlan` for a backward-stable solve by reordered bandwidth-2 band LU with $O(n)$ work and storage.
Band matrices factor with `BandedLuDecomposition` in $O(n\,p_l(p_l + p_u))$ operations; choose `solve_triangular` for a single triangular solve or `TriangularPlan` for banded or packed storage and repeated solves.
For Toeplitz, Hankel, and circulant systems, choose `solve_structured` for a single solve or `StructuredSolvePlan` for many right-hand sides, tolerance selection, or dense-LU retries; report counts identify which algorithm solved each column.
The acceptance measure $\eta$ is an unstructured backward error, so combine it with conditioning from `condition_estimate()` when a forward-error estimate is needed.
