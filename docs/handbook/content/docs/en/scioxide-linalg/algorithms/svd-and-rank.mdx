Singular value decomposition (SVD) factors any rectangular matrix into two unitary matrices and a nonnegative diagonal matrix.
Rank, condition number, minimum-norm solutions, and best low-rank approximations all follow from singular values, providing a standard for questions that LU or QR diagonals cannot settle.
This page explains SVD existence and perturbation theory, computational algorithms and the three SVD types in `scioxide-linalg`, numerical rank, condition-number computation and estimation, regularization of ill-conditioned problems, and best low-rank approximation.
Notation follows [Mathematics of linear algebra and shared conventions](../); call forms (standard, with options, with caller-provided output, and scoped) follow [Workspaces, execution contexts, and attempt records](oxref:linalg-execution-forms).

## Existence of singular value decomposition

Every $A \in \mathbb{K}^{m \times n}$ has the following factorization with unitary $U \in \mathbb{K}^{m \times m}$ and $V \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times n}$ and an $m \times n$ matrix $\Sigma$ with diagonal $\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_p \ge 0$ ($p = \min(m, n)$):

$$
A = U \Sigma V^{\mathsf H} = \sum_{i=1}^{p} \sigma_i\, u_i v_i^{\mathsf H}
$$


Existence follows by induction.
The unit sphere is closed and bounded, so a unit vector $v_1$ maximizes $\lVert A v \rVert_2$; set $\sigma_1 = \lVert A v_1 \rVert_2$ and $u_1 = A v_1 / \sigma_1$ (the case $A = 0$ is trivial).
Transforming by unitary matrices with first columns $u_1, v_1$ gives the form $\begin{bmatrix} \sigma_1 & w^{\mathsf H} \\ 0 & B \end{bmatrix}$.
Multiplying this matrix by $(\sigma_1, w)$ gives first component $\sigma_1^2 + \lVert w \rVert_2^2$, so its 2-norm is at least $(\sigma_1^2 + \lVert w \rVert_2^2)^{1/2}$.
Unitary transformations preserve the 2-norm, whose value is $\sigma_1$; thus $w = 0$, and the argument repeats on the remaining $B$.
Singular values depend only on $A$; vectors for simple singular values are unique up to phase (sign for real scalars).
For repeated singular values, only the subspaces spanned by corresponding singular vectors are determined.

Geometrically, $A$ maps the unit ball to an ellipsoid with semiaxes $\sigma_i$; $v_i$ gives preimage directions and $u_i$ axis directions.
We have $\lVert A \rVert_2 = \sigma_1$ and $\lVert A \rVert_F^2 = \sum_i \sigma_i^2$, and for nonsingular square matrices $\kappa_2(A) = \sigma_1 / \sigma_n$; the number of nonzero singular values gives rank.

## Perturbations of singular values and singular subspaces

Singular values are stable under perturbations.
The min-max characterization $\sigma_i(A) = \max_{\dim S = i} \min_{x \in S,\, \lVert x \rVert_2 = 1} \lVert Ax \rVert_2$ and inequality $\lVert (A + E)x \rVert_2 \le \lVert Ax \rVert_2 + \lVert E \rVert_2$ give Weyl's inequality below for every $E$, without additional assumptions:

$$
\lvert \sigma_i(A + E) - \sigma_i(A) \rvert \le \lVert E \rVert_2 \qquad (i = 1, \dots, p)
$$


This bounds absolute error; in relative terms it becomes $\lVert E \rVert_2 / \sigma_i$.
A backward-stable algorithm gives exact singular values of a matrix perturbed by $\lVert E \rVert_2 \lesssim c(m, n)\, \varepsilon\, \sigma_1$ ($c(m, n)$ is a low-degree polynomial in dimensions and $\varepsilon$ machine epsilon), so computed singular values have absolute error of order $\varepsilon\,\sigma_1$.
Singular values many orders below $\sigma_1$ lose relative accuracy; those below $\varepsilon\,\sigma_1$ cannot be distinguished from 0.
Rounding a general dense matrix's entries to floating-point numbers alone introduces perturbations of this size, so this limitation often already exists when the input is rounded.
Although $\sigma_i^2$ are eigenvalues of $A^{\mathsf H} A$, forming $A^{\mathsf H} A$ buries small singular values in error $\varepsilon \sigma_1^2$, so compute SVD directly from $A$.

Singular-vector accuracy depends on gaps to neighboring singular values.
Let $\mathcal{U}_k, \mathcal{V}_k$ be subspaces spanned by the leading $k$ left and right singular vectors, and assume $\delta = \sigma_k(A) - \sigma_{k+1}(A) - \lVert E \rVert_2 > 0$.
Let $\Theta$ be a diagonal matrix of [principal angles](oxref:linalg-principal-angles) with the corresponding subspaces of $A + E$ ($\lVert \sin\Theta \rVert_2$ is the sine of the largest principal angle); Wedin's $\sin\Theta$ theorem gives

$$
\max\bigl(\lVert \sin\Theta(\mathcal{U}_k, \hat{\mathcal{U}}_k) \rVert_2,\ \lVert \sin\Theta(\mathcal{V}_k, \hat{\mathcal{V}}_k) \rVert_2\bigr) \le \frac{\lVert E \rVert_2}{\delta}
$$


If the gap is large relative to the perturbation, range and null-space bases are stable; if comparable or smaller, individual singular vectors may rotate substantially.

## Computational algorithms

`scioxide-linalg` computes SVD in two stages.
Below, let $k = \min(m, n)$.

1. Scale the input by powers of two and apply Householder reflections from both sides to obtain $A = U_1 B V_1^{\mathsf H}$ ($B$ is real upper bidiagonal): Golub–Kahan bidiagonalization.
   Through order 64, apply reflections individually; above that, group them into width-32 panels and apply by matrix products.
   Wide matrices are reduced in the adjoint orientation.
   For jobs not requesting square $U$ and $V$, matrices satisfying $\max(m, n) \ge 2k$ first undergo unpivoted Householder QR, and only the $k \times k$ triangular factor is bidiagonalized.
2. Compute the bidiagonal matrix's SVD.
   Values-only jobs use dqds, the differential qd recurrence with shifts, computing bidiagonal singular values without square roots.
   Jobs also requesting singular vectors use implicitly shifted bidiagonal QR iteration for $k \le 64$ and divide-and-conquer above it.
   QR iteration chooses a chasing direction from the larger endpoint diagonal to the smaller for each block (Demmel–Kahan), converging for graded blocks in either orientation.
   Divide-and-conquer splits the bidiagonal matrix into small subproblems and recovers global singular values from roots of a connecting secular equation; the smallest subproblems use QR at order 32.

The cost is $O(mnk)$; switching depends only on matrix shape and job, not the CPU instruction set.
Small relative perturbations of bidiagonal entries cause only small relative changes in singular values (Demmel–Kahan), so even small singular values of the bidiagonal matrix itself are meaningful.
dqds uses this property to compute singular values of $B$ with high relative accuracy.
Dense Householder reduction guarantees only normwise backward error, however, so the preceding perturbation discussion still applies to singular values of $A$.
Values-only jobs preserve relative accuracy of small singular values for inputs whose reduction introduces no rounding, such as diagonal matrices.
An exception is values becoming subnormal after scaling (below the product of $\sigma_1$ and the smallest positive normal number): even originally normal values can lose relative accuracy or round to 0.
Set the iteration limit with `LinalgExecutionOptions::with_iteration_budget`.
Without an explicit limit, dqds allows $100k$ transformations including rejected shifts, bidiagonal QR $30k$ sweeps, and divide-and-conquer $30k\,(1 + \lceil \log_2 \lceil k/32 \rceil \rceil)$ combined sweeps and secular-equation iterations.
Reaching the limit gives `ConvergenceFailure`, overflow on undoing scaling gives `NumericalInstability`, and NaNs or infinities in input give `NonFinite`.

| Type | Result | Main uses |
|---|---|---|
| `SvdValues` | Singular values only | Rank, condition number, norms |
| `ThinSvdDecomposition` | Thin SVD: $U_k$ ($m \times k$), $\sigma$, $V_k^{\mathsf H}$ ($k \times n$) | Least squares, regularized solutions, pseudoinverse, low-rank approximation |
| `SvdDecomposition` | Square $U$, $\sigma$, square $V^{\mathsf H}$ | Complete range and null-space bases |

All provide standard-form `compute(&a)`, a form with options `compute_with_options(&a, &options)` accepting execution settings `LinalgExecutionOptions` last, and an API `compute_into` borrowing output, workspace, and execution context.
Factorization uses request `SvdWorkspaceRequest::new(m, n, &options)`; solves and pseudoinverses from an existing factorization use `SvdWorkspaceRequest::solve` and `pseudoinverse` (`SvdValues` rejects the latter two with `InvalidInput`).
Singular values are committed in descending order as raw values, with zero represented by `+0.0`.
Since singular vectors are determined up to phase, make the largest-magnitude component of each column of $U$ positive real (first in a tie), adjusting the corresponding row of $V^{\mathsf H}$ accordingly.
`analyze_rank(policy)` returns rank report `RankReport`, and `condition_estimate()` returns $\sigma_k / \sigma_1$ as `ConditionEstimate` (confidence `ModelBased`, based on a numerical model).

## Numerical-rank assessment

With rounding or measurement error, rank depends on singular-value magnitudes and a threshold $\tau$ below which they are treated as zero.
By the Eckart–Young theorem below, the minimum rank of a matrix $B$ satisfying $\lVert A - B \rVert_2 \le \tau$ equals the number of indices $i$ satisfying $\sigma_i > \tau$.
This is the numerical rank at $\tau$; `analyze_rank` likewise counts singular values exceeding $\tau$.
`RankReport` returns numerical rank `rank()`, maximum rank from the shape `maximum_rank()`, threshold `threshold()`, and largest singular value `scale()` used to scale the relative threshold.

Choose the threshold based on error already present in the matrix: for rounding alone, $c(m, n)\, \varepsilon\, \sigma_1$ from the preceding section.
Following this idea, `RankPolicy::DimensionScaledEpsilon` uses threshold $\min(\max(m, n)\,\varepsilon, 1/2)\,\sigma_1$.
`RankPolicy::Exact` counts every singular value that is not exactly zero.
For measured matrices with error $E$ ($\lVert E \rVert_2 \approx \eta$), $\eta$ exceeds rounding error; use `Threshold(RankThreshold::new(atol, rtol)?)` to choose $\tau = \mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}\,\sigma_1$ at least $\eta$.
If the observed $A + E$ has singular values satisfying $\sigma_r > \tau + \lVert E \rVert_2$ and $\sigma_{r+1} < \tau - \lVert E \rVert_2$, Weyl's inequality also gives numerical rank $r$ at threshold $\tau$ for error-free $A$.
A large singular-value gap clearly determines rank regardless of threshold details; without a gap, numerical rank merely reflects the chosen threshold.

## Computing and estimating condition numbers

A nonsingular square matrix's condition number measures possible amplification of relative right-hand-side or matrix perturbations into relative solution error.
SVD gives the 2-norm condition number $\kappa_2(A) = \sigma_1/\sigma_n$ but costs $O(n^3)$; absolute error $\varepsilon\,\sigma_1$ in $\sigma_n$ gives relative error of order $\kappa_2\,\varepsilon$.
If LU is already available for solving a system, the 1-norm condition number $\kappa_1(A) = \lVert A \rVert_1 \lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$ can be estimated with $O(n^2)$ extra work.
The norm relation $\lVert x \rVert_2 \le \lVert x \rVert_1 \le \sqrt{n}\,\lVert x \rVert_2$ gives $\kappa_2(A)/n \le \kappa_1(A) \le n\,\kappa_2(A)$, so either condition number indicates the scale of digit loss.

$\lVert A \rVert_1$ follows directly from entries as the maximum absolute column sum; estimate $\lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$ without forming $A^{-1}$.
Hager–Higham uses at most 17 products by $A^{-1}$ and $A^{-\mathsf H}$ (each an $O(n^2)$ LU solve), independent of $n$, returning a lower bound on $\lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$ apart from rounding.
The procedure, product counts, and accuracy are explained in [Hager–Higham estimation](oxref:linalg-hager-higham).
`LuDecomposition::condition_estimate()` returns reciprocal condition number $1/\kappa_1$ from this estimate and $\lVert A \rVert_1$ computed exactly during factorization.
The [method object](oxref:linalg-method-values) `OneNormEstimator` applies the same estimate to arbitrary square operators; passing the LU inverse operator `LuDecomposition::inverse_operator()` gives $\lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$.

The following cell computes conditioning of the Vandermonde matrix $V_{ij} = t_i^{\,j}$ ($i, j = 0, \dots, n-1$) arising in polynomial interpolation by three methods.
Inputs are order $n$ (4–16), node selection $t_i \in [0, 1]$ (equally spaced or Chebyshev nodes $t_i = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12 \cos\frac{(2i+1)\pi}{2n}$), and the application limit for `OneNormEstimator` (1–64).
The table places $\kappa_2$ from `SvdValues`, exact $\kappa_1$ from `operator_norm` applying $A^{-1}$ to every unit vector, the `OneNormEstimator` estimate, and the reciprocal of LU's `condition_estimate()` side by side, using exact $\kappa_1$ as an independent reference.
Call `OneNormEstimator` through the scoped API after reserving workspace.
`workspace_requirements(&op)` reads only shape and workspace needs without applying the operator and returns `OneNormRequirements`, including required bytes `required_bytes()` for a new workspace and execution byte limit `byte_limit()`.
`OneNormWorkspace::try_reserve` grows the workspace accordingly, and `estimate_scoped(&op, &mut workspace, context)` returns report `OneNormReport`.
The estimation termination reason determines the outcome: satisfied for `Converged`, partial for `EvaluationLimit` at the application limit, with diagnostics for application count, estimated/exact ratio, and `required_bytes()`.

```rust
// Nodes t_i ∈ [0, 1]: equally spaced or Chebyshev nodes mapped to [0, 1] (standard f64 functions).
let node = |i: usize| match nodes.as_str() {

        &mut workspace,
```

Against an independent 50-digit $\kappa_2$, SVD's $\kappa_2$ has relative error $7.9 \times 10^{-12}$ for the same matrix with entries rounded to double precision and $2.3 \times 10^{-11}$ for the matrix at unrounded nodes $t_i = i/9$.
Both are below $\kappa_2\,\varepsilon \approx 3.4 \times 10^{-9}$ estimated from absolute error $\varepsilon\,\sigma_1$ in $\sigma_n$.
`OneNormEstimator` reaches `Converged` in 7 applications, matching the exact value (ratio 1); LU's `condition_estimate()` returns the same value.
Exact `operator_norm` uses $n = 10$ applications, a small difference at this size, but estimation never exceeds 17 even as $n$ grows.
The required `required_bytes()` is 560 B.

Chebyshev nodes give $\kappa_2 = 3.980 \times 10^{6}$ and $\kappa_1 = 7.916 \times 10^{6}$, respectively 3.8 and 5.4 times smaller in $\kappa_2$ and $\kappa_1$ than equally spaced nodes at the same order.
With an application limit of 1, estimation stops after the first product with `EvaluationLimit` and a partial outcome.
The first product applies $A^{-1}$ to $x = (1, \dots, 1)/n$.
Column 0 of $V$ is all ones, so $A^{-1}(1, \dots, 1)^{\mathsf T} = e_0$; the estimate is $1/n$ and its product with $\lVert A \rVert_1 = n$ is 1, only $2.4 \times 10^{-8}$ times the exact value.
Stopping a lower-bound estimate can severely underestimate the value, so check the termination reason before use.

## Regularized solutions

The minimum-norm least-squares solution of $Ax = b$ is $x = \sum_i (u_i^{\mathsf H} b / \sigma_i)\, v_i$, but small $\sigma_i$ amplify errors in $b$ by $1/\sigma_i$.
`ThinSvdDecomposition::solve(&b, policy, damping)` uses $r$ singular values above threshold $\tau$ from `policy`, with damping $\lambda \ge 0$ (`damping`), to return

$$
x_{r,\lambda} = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_i^2 + \lambda}\, (u_i^{\mathsf H} b)\, v_i
$$


The inner products $u_i^{\mathsf H} b$ and the sum over $i$ use compensated inner products.
$\lambda = 0$ gives truncated SVD (TSVD); with $r = p$, $\lambda > 0$ gives Tikhonov regularization $\min \lVert Ax - b \rVert_2^2 + \lambda \lVert x \rVert_2^2$.
TSVD discards components with $\sigma_i \le \tau$; Tikhonov multiplies each by $\sigma_i^2/(\sigma_i^2 + \lambda)$, smoothly suppressing those with $\sigma_i \ll \sqrt{\lambda}$.
Solution components are meaningful only while coefficients $\lvert u_i^{\mathsf H} b \rvert$ decay faster than $\sigma_i$ (the discrete Picard condition).
Negative `damping` gives `InvalidInput`, NaN or infinity `NonFinite`; overflow of coefficients such as reciprocals of subnormal $\sigma_i$ retained by `RankPolicy::Exact` gives `NumericalInstability`.
`pseudoinverse(policy)` forms the pseudoinverse $\sum_{\sigma_i > \tau} \sigma_i^{-1} v_i u_i^{\mathsf H}$ ($n \times m$) with the same truncation.
This is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of the truncated matrix $A_r$ and equals $A^+$ only when $\tau$ is below the smallest nonzero singular value.
The API `solve_into` counts one iteration, and `pseudoinverse_into` one per output column, against the iteration limit.

```rust
};
let entries = (0..n * n)
    .map(|k| h * kernel(node(k / n), node(k % n)))
    .collect();
let a = Matrix::from_vec(n, n, entries).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let f: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| (pi * node(j)).sin() + 0.5 * (3.0 * pi * node(j)).sin())
    .collect();
let g = a.view().mul_vec(&f[..]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Scale deterministic pseudorandom noise to 2-norm ratio 10^e.
let z: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|i| ((i * 7919 + 17) % 1013) as f64 / 1013.0 - 0.5)
    .collect();
let norm = |v: &[f64]| v.iter().map(|x| x * x).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
let scale = 10.0_f64.powf(noise_exponent) * norm(g.as_slice()) / norm(&z);
let b: Vec<f64> = g
    .as_slice()
    .iter()
    .zip(&z)
    .map(|(p, q)| p + scale * q)
    .collect();

let svd = ThinSvdDecomposition::compute(&a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sigma = svd.singular_values();
let level = 10.0_f64.powf(regularization_exponent) * sigma[0];
let threshold = RankThreshold::new(level, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let truncation = RankPolicy::Threshold(threshold);
let methods = [
    ("Unregularized", RankPolicy::Exact, 0.0),
    ("TSVD", truncation, 0.0),
    ("Tikhonov", RankPolicy::Exact, level * level),
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, policy, damping) in methods {
    let x = svd
        .solve(&b[..], policy, damping)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let rank = svd.analyze_rank(policy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.rank();
    let ax = a.view().mul_vec(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let residual: Vec<f64> = ax.as_slice().iter().zip(&b).map(|(p, q)| p - q).collect();
    let error: Vec<f64> = x.as_slice().iter().zip(&f).map(|(p, q)| p - q).collect();
    rows.push((
        name,
        rank,
        norm(&residual) / norm(&b),
        norm(&error) / norm(&f),
    ));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Solver" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Singular values used", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative error against f [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "solutions" })
);

// Picard plot: σ_i and |u_iᵀ b|. Omit zeros from the logarithmic axis.
let coefficients = svd
    .u_matrix()
    .view()
    .transpose()
    .mul_vec(&b[..])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut s_points, mut c_points) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for i in 0..n {
    if sigma[i] > 0.0 {
        s_points.push([(i + 1) as f64, sigma[i]]);
    }
    let c = coefficients.as_slice()[i].abs();
    if c > 0.0 {
        c_points.push([(i + 1) as f64, c]);
    }
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "σ_i", "points": s_points }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "|u_iᵀ b|", "points": c_points }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Singular-value index i [-]",
        "yLabel": "Magnitude [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{
            "kind": "line",
            "axis": "y",
            "value": level,
            "label": "TSVD threshold 10^α σ₁"
        }]
    })
);
```

Singular values decay rapidly from $\sigma_1 = 0.989$; computed $\sigma_{64}$ is $1.9 \times 10^{-18}$.
At 50-digit precision, $\sigma_{64}$ of the unrounded kernel matrix is $5.2 \times 10^{-21}$, whereas $\sigma_{64}$ of the matrix with entries rounded to double precision is of order $10^{-18}$; rounding entries alone shifts the smallest singular value by three orders of magnitude.
Singular values below $\varepsilon\,\sigma_1 \approx 2 \times 10^{-16}$ move by this amount under input rounding or algorithmic error and therefore have no relative accuracy and cannot be distinguished from 0.
Noise-free coefficients $u_i^{\mathsf T} g$ decay with singular values, but noise coefficients are nearly independent of index, so the Picard plot of $\lvert u_i^{\mathsf T} b \rvert$ levels off at the noise floor (of order $10^{e} \lVert g \rVert_2 / \sqrt{n}$, defaults $10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}$).
The unregularized solution divides this flat tail by small $\sigma_i$, giving relative error $7.6 \times 10^{12}$ against $f$ at default $e = -4$.
At default $\alpha = -2$, TSVD uses 20 singular values for relative error $5.7 \times 10^{-3}$ and Tikhonov gives $4.1 \times 10^{-3}$; relative residuals $1.1 \times 10^{-4}$ and $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ are comparable to the noise magnitude $10^{-4}$.
TSVD counts (14, 20, 42, and 51 in this section) agree with counts obtained by comparing 50-digit singular values with the threshold.

Lowering $\alpha$ picks up noise; at $\alpha = -12$ TSVD uses 51 singular values and error reaches $6.9 \times 10^{6}$.
Raising it discards too much of the solution; at $\alpha = -1$ TSVD uses 14 values with error $1.7 \times 10^{-2}$.
Selecting $\alpha$ to make the residual equal the noise magnitude is Morozov's discrepancy principle.
The TSVD relative residual is $1.1 \times 10^{-4}$ at $\alpha = -2$ and $9.7 \times 10^{-5}$ at $\alpha = -2.5$, so this principle selects $\alpha$ between them.
The TSVD error there, $3.2 \times 10^{-3}$–$5.7 \times 10^{-3}$, is smaller than $6.0 \times 10^{-2}$ at $\alpha = -3$ or $1.7 \times 10^{-2}$ at $\alpha = -1$.
Reducing noise to $e = -10$ with $\alpha = -8$ makes TSVD use 42 singular values and lowers its error to $1.3 \times 10^{-3}$, and Tikhonov's to $7.3 \times 10^{-4}$.

## Best low-rank approximation

By the Eckart–Young–Mirsky theorem, the closest matrix to $A$ of rank at most $k$ is $A_k = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^{\mathsf H}$, retaining the leading $k$ terms, with errors

$$
\lVert A - A_k \rVert_2 = \sigma_{k+1}, \qquad \lVert A - A_k \rVert_F = \Bigl( \sum_{i > k} \sigma_i^2 \Bigr)^{1/2}
$$


The proof for the 2-norm is short.
A rank-at-most-$k$ matrix $B$ has null-space dimension at least $n - k$, so it shares a unit vector $z$ with the span of $v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}$, giving $\lVert (A - B) z \rVert_2 = \lVert A z \rVert_2 \ge \sigma_{k+1}$.

The following cell forms $A_k$ from thin SVD of the $30 \times 20$ Gaussian-kernel matrix $a_{st} = \exp(-(s - t)^2/(2w^2))$ ($s, t$ are equally spaced points in $[0, 1]$).
The input is kernel width $w$ (0.05–0.5).
Independently compute the 2-norm of $A - A_k$ using `SvdValues` of the difference matrix and its Frobenius norm by summing squared entries; compare with $\sigma_{k+1}$ and $(\sum_{i>k} \sigma_i^2)^{1/2}$ on the right above.
The table includes $k \le 7$ and multiples of 4 for $k$; the chart shows points for $\lVert A - A_k \rVert_2$ and a dashed theoretical curve $\sigma_{k+1}$.

```rust
//| id: linalg-eckart-young
//| ref: linalg-eckart-young
//| title: "Truncated-SVD approximation errors and singular values"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-linalg]
//| inputs:
//|   width: {"type":"range","label":"Kernel width w","description":"Smaller widths give slower singular-value decay.","min":0.05,"max":0.5,"step":0.05,"value":0.2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"narrow-kernel","label":"w = 0.05","description":"Slower singular-value decay increases the rank needed for a given error.","inputs":{"width":0.05}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":10.43063883312876,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":1.8056314664818141,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/9/1","approx":{"value":8.9763890322932371e-7,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":13.488112644642527,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|   - case: narrow-kernel
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/8/1","approx":{"value":1.3019538677074704,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_linalg::{Matrix, SvdValues, ThinSvdDecomposition};

let (m, n) = (30_usize, 20_usize);
let entries = (0..m * n)
    .map(|k| {
        let (s, t) = (
            (k / n) as f64 / (m - 1) as f64,
            (k % n) as f64 / (n - 1) as f64,
        );
        (-(s - t).powi(2) / (2.0 * width * width)).exp()
    })
    .collect();
let a = Matrix::from_vec(m, n, entries).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let svd = ThinSvdDecomposition::compute(&a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sigma = svd.singular_values();
let (u, vt) = (svd.u_matrix().as_slice(), svd.vt_matrix().as_slice());
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let (mut points, mut theory) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for k in 0..n {
    // Form A − A_k = Σ_{i≥k} σ_i u_i v_iᵀ entrywise (indices start at 0).
    let diff: Vec<f64> = (0..m * n)
        .map(|idx| {
            let (r, c) = (idx / n, idx % n);
            (k..n)
                .map(|i| sigma[i] * u[r * n + i] * vt[i * n + c])
                .sum()
        })
        .collect();
    let frobenius = diff.iter().map(|x| x * x).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
    let difference = Matrix::from_vec(m, n, diff).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let two_norm = SvdValues::compute(&difference)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .singular_values()[0];
    let tail = sigma[k..].iter().map(|s| s * s).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
    // Omit zero values, which cannot appear on a logarithmic axis.
    if two_norm > 0.0 {
        points.push([k as f64, two_norm]);
    }
    if sigma[k] > 0.0 {
        theory.push([k as f64, sigma[k]]);
    }
    if k < 8 || k % 4 == 0 {
        rows.push((k, two_norm, sigma[k], frobenius, tail));
    }
}
let sci4 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "k", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "‖A − A_k‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "σ_(k+1) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "‖A − A_k‖_F [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "(Σ_(i>k) σ_i²)^(1/2) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "xLabel": "Approximation rank k [-]",
    "yLabel": "2-norm error [-]",
    "yType": "log",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "layers": [
        {
            "id": "observed",
            "kind": "scatter",
            "name": "‖A − A_k‖₂",
            "points": points,
            "style": { "symbol": "circle" }
        },
        {
            "id": "theory",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "σ_(k+1) (theory)",
            "points": theory,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
        }
    ]
}));
```

For every $k$, the difference's 2-norm agrees with $\sigma_{k+1}$ and its Frobenius norm with the square root of the remaining squared singular-value sum to all 4 displayed digits.
$\sigma_{k+1}$ also agrees with independent 50-digit values; for $\sigma_{13}$ at default $w = 0.2$, relative difference is $7.5 \times 10^{-11}$.
At default $w = 0.2$, adjacent ratios $\sigma_k/\sigma_{k+1}$ gradually grow from 1.4 to 36; rank 8 suffices for $\lVert A - A_k \rVert_2 / \sigma_1 \le 10^{-3}$ ($\sigma_9 / \sigma_1 = 2.2 \times 10^{-4}$).
At $w = 0.05$ decay slows; even rank 8 has error $\sigma_9 = 1.30$ (45% of $\sigma_1$).
The final plotted point $k = 19$ has error $2.6 \times 10^{-15}$, about 4% different from the 50-digit $\sigma_{20} = 2.5 \times 10^{-15}$.
$\sigma_{20}$ is comparable to the absolute singular-value error scale $\varepsilon\,\sigma_1 \approx 2.3 \times 10^{-15}$, making relative error a few percent.

## Summary

Computed singular values have absolute errors of order $\varepsilon\,\sigma_1$, so treat values near or below $\varepsilon\,\sigma_1$ as indistinguishable from 0 rather than reliable values.
Choose numerical-rank thresholds at least as large as error already in the matrix (`DimensionScaledEpsilon` for rounding alone, or `Threshold` at the measurement-error magnitude), and check whether a singular-value gap crosses the threshold.
With SVD available, read $\kappa_2$; with only LU, obtain a lower-bound estimate of $\kappa_1$ through `condition_estimate()` in $O(n^2)$ work as a guide to digit loss.
For ill-conditioned solves, inspect where Picard coefficients reach the noise floor and choose truncation or damping in `solve(&b, policy, damping)` to make residuals match the noise magnitude.
The rank needed for low-rank approximation is determined by the first $\sigma_{k+1}$ below the permitted error.
