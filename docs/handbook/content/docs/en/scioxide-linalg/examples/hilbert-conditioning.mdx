The error in a linear system solution depends on both the stability of the method and the condition number of the problem.
A backward-stable method returns the exact solution of a slightly different problem, but for an ill-conditioned problem that slight difference can substantially change the solution.
This example observes these two factors separately using Hilbert matrices, whose condition numbers increase rapidly with order.
Set the true solution to $x^\ast = (1, \dots, 1)$ and solve the systems $H_n x = b$ of orders $n = 2, \dots, 13$ in double precision (`f64`).
Also examine up to which order iterative refinement combining a single-precision (`f32`) factorization with double-precision residuals reaches the same accuracy as double-precision LU factorization.
General background on errors and condition numbers is collected in [Floating point and conditioning](../../../concepts/floating-point-and-conditioning/).

## Hilbert matrices and condition numbers

The Hilbert matrix of order $n$ is symmetric positive definite with entries (using zero-based indices)

$$
(H_n)_{ij} = \frac{1}{i + j + 1}, \qquad 0 \le i, j < n
$$


It is the Gram matrix of $L^2$ inner products of the monomials $1, x, \dots, x^{n-1}$ on $[0, 1]$, and is the coefficient matrix of the normal equations for least-squares approximation of a function on $[0, 1]$ by a polynomial of degree $n-1$.
High-degree monomials have similar shapes on the interval, making the columns nearly linearly dependent.
Up to a constant factor, the 2-norm condition number $\kappa_2(H_n) = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$ grows as

$$
\kappa_2(H_n) \propto \frac{(1+\sqrt2)^{4n}}{\sqrt{n}} \approx \frac{e^{3.5 n}}{\sqrt n}
$$

It increases by approximately a factor of $(1+\sqrt2)^4 \approx 34$ per increment of the order ($4.8 \times 10^{5}$ at $n = 5$ and $1.6 \times 10^{13}$ at $n = 10$).
The inverse has a closed form with integer entries, available from `StructuredMatrixView::hilbert(n, MatrixForm::Inverse)` ([Structured matrices](../../algorithms/structured-systems/)).
Calling `materialize()` produces a dense `Matrix` for either form.
At $n = 12$, the largest absolute entry of the inverse is $3.7 \times 10^{15}$, below $2^{53} \approx 9.0 \times 10^{15}$, so it is representable without rounding; at $n = 13$, it reaches $1.1 \times 10^{17}$ and some entries incur relative rounding of order $u$.

## Forward error and backward error

The backward error of a computed solution $\hat x$ is the relative distance to a nearby problem for which $\hat x$ is the exact solution:

$$
\eta(\hat x) = \frac{\lVert b - H\hat x \rVert_\infty}{\lVert H \rVert_\infty \lVert \hat x \rVert_\infty + \lVert b \rVert_\infty}
$$


For LU factorization with partial pivoting, $\eta$ is a small multiple of unit roundoff $u = 2^{-53} \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-16}$ as long as the growth factor is small.
Forward error is the relative difference in the solution itself; for this $\eta$, which measures perturbations to both $H$ and $b$, the following holds when $\kappa_\infty(H)\,\eta < 1$:

$$
\frac{\lVert \hat x - x \rVert_\infty}{\lVert x \rVert_\infty} \le \frac{2\,\kappa_\infty(H)\,\eta}{1 - \kappa_\infty(H)\,\eta}
$$

Here $x$ is the exact solution of the stored system.
Since $H$ is symmetric, $\kappa_\infty(H) = \kappa_1(H)$.
For $\kappa_\infty \eta \ge 1$ this bound is uninformative, and the solution may have no correct digits.
Most entries $1/(i+j+1)$ of $H_n$ have no finite binary representation, so merely storing the matrix introduces componentwise relative perturbations of order $u$.
The exact solution of the stored system can consequently differ from $x^\ast$ by order $\kappa u$, so any method can have forward error of order $\kappa u$ relative to $x^\ast$.

`LuDecomposition::factor(&h)` performs LU factorization with partial pivoting; the only numerical cause of factorization failure is an exactly zero pivot (`Singular`).
Solution acceptance is judged separately at solve time using the conditions in `DirectSolveOptions` ([Solution acceptance](oxref:linalg-factorization-acceptance)).
To read reports, this page uses the [form with options](oxref:linalg-execution-forms), `solve_with_options(&b, &options)`, for solves.
The form with options returns a `DirectSolveResult` combining the solution and report; a rejected solution still returns `Ok` but is not published, and `solution()` is `None`.
Adding `with_residual(Estimation::Estimate)` makes the report's `residual()` return the above $\eta$ for the original matrix.
Providing a reciprocal condition threshold with `with_minimum_reciprocal_condition` estimates the reciprocal condition number before solving using the method below; if the estimate falls below the threshold, the termination reason becomes `DirectSolveTermination::IllConditioned` and the solution is not published.
The rank policy is `RankPolicy::Exact` (only exactly zero pivots indicate rank deficiency); without a threshold, solutions are accepted even at ill-conditioned orders.

The next cell sets the right-hand side to $b = Hx^\ast$ and examines LU solutions from order 2 to $n_{\max}$ (4–13).
It computes condition numbers in three ways.
These are $\kappa_2$ from the singular value ratio computed by `SvdValues::compute`, $\kappa_1 = \lVert H \rVert_1 \lVert H^{-1} \rVert_1$ from the closed-form inverse (an independent check computed with `MatrixView::norm(MatrixNorm::One)`), and the reciprocal of the $\mathrm{rcond}_1 = 1/\kappa_1$ estimate returned by the LU factors' `condition_estimate()`.
The input “Reciprocal condition threshold” offers “None”, $10^{-14}$, and $10^{-10}$.
For each order, the table shows the three condition numbers, termination reason, backward error $\eta$, forward error relative to $x^\ast$, and guide $\kappa_2 u$; errors of solutions not written to the output are marked “Output unchanged”.
The plot shows $\kappa_2$, forward error, and $\kappa_2 u$ (dashed) on logarithmic axes.

        DirectSolveTermination::RankDeficient => "RankDeficient",

With the defaults ($n_{\max} = 12$, no threshold), the backward error $\eta$ in the table is at most $1.6 \times 10^{-16}$ at every order, so the LU computation is backward stable.
Meanwhile, forward error increases from $2.1 \times 10^{-11}$ at $n = 5$ to $3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ at $n = 10$ and $0.52$ at $n = 12$, staying below the dashed $\kappa_2 u$ at every order.
The bound's condition $\kappa_1 \eta < 1$ holds for $n \le 11$; for example, at $n = 10$, $\kappa_1 \eta = 5.4 \times 10^{-3}$ gives a bound of $1.1 \times 10^{-2}$.
At $n = 12$, $\kappa_1 \eta \approx 4.8$ makes the bound inapplicable, and $\kappa_2 u$ also exceeds 1 (1.9).
With $n_{\max} = 13$, the forward error at $n = 13$ is 12, and the computed solution has no correct digits relative to the true solution.
The termination reason is still `Accepted` and $\eta$ is $2.9 \times 10^{-17}$, so the report's backward error alone cannot establish solution accuracy.
Forward error is also sensitive to rounding order: running with the native matrix product kernel yields values several times to 10 times different even at the same order (the numbers in this text are browser results).
Since the plot uses logarithmic axes, it omits any orders whose forward error is exactly 0.

The SVD-based $\kappa_2$ is 0.42–0.71 times $\kappa_1$ for $n \le 12$, and both increase together.
At $n = 13$, the ratio jumps to 1.1 and departs from this pattern.
Singular values computed by a backward-stable SVD incur absolute errors of order $u\,\sigma_{\max}$.
For matrices with $\kappa_2 > 1/u$, where $\sigma_{\min}$ falls below this level, the computed $\sigma_{\min}$ has no correct relative digits.
Storage rounding also changes the matrix by relative order $u$, changing $\sigma_{\min}$ by the same magnitude.
Condition numbers exceeding $1/u \approx 9 \times 10^{15}$ therefore provide only an order-of-magnitude guide even with SVD.

## Interpreting condition estimates

`condition_estimate()` estimates $\lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$ using Hager's method with Higham's improvements and returns $\mathrm{rcond}_1 = 1/(\lVert A \rVert_1 \lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1)$ in the original matrix coordinates as `ConditionEstimate`.
$\lVert A \rVert_1$ is computed exactly during factorization.
The estimate does not form $A^{-1}$; it uses the retained LU factors to solve $A^{-1}v$ and $A^{-\mathsf T}v$ only a few times, taking the maximum ratio $\lVert A^{-1}v \rVert_1 / \lVert v \rVert_1$ over the trial vectors.
Each solve costs $O(n^2)$, less than the $O(n^3)$ factorization cost.
For orders up to 4, $\lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$ is computed exactly.
For every trial vector the ratio is at most $\lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$, so in exact arithmetic the estimate is a lower bound on $\lVert A^{-1} \rVert_1$, and “Estimate of 1/rcond₁” in the table is a lower bound on $\kappa_1$.
It is often within a factor of 10 of the true value, but this is not guaranteed.
In floating point, errors from solving $A^{-1}v$ using the LU factors mean it need not be a lower bound; the reliability of `ReliableEstimate` returned by `reciprocal()` is `Heuristic` (an estimate with no guaranteed upper or lower bound).

The table's “Estimate of 1/rcond₁” agrees with exact $\kappa_1$ to 2–3 significant digits for $n \le 12$ (both are $3.54 \times 10^{13}$ at $n = 10$).
At $n = 13$, $\kappa_1 u > 1$, so the inverse of stored $H$ may differ substantially from the closed-form inverse, and applying the LU factors incurs errors of the same magnitude.
This explains why the reciprocal of the estimate is 1.7 times $\kappa_1$, losing the lower-bound property.
Although this example agrees to 2–3 significant digits for $n \le 12$, generally interpret the estimate as an order-of-magnitude guide.
To examine the condition number itself, use singular values ($\kappa_2$) or, when the structure is known, the closed-form inverse.

Setting the reciprocal condition threshold to $10^{-10}$ gives termination reason `IllConditioned` from $n = 8$ onward, where the estimated $\mathrm{rcond}_1$ is $2.95 \times 10^{-11}$, and neither the solution nor $\eta$ is written to the output (“Output unchanged” in the table).
With a threshold of $10^{-14}$, rejection starts at $n = 11$, where $\mathrm{rcond}_1$ is $8.1 \times 10^{-16}$.
This rejection returns a result in `Ok`, retaining the factorization so the same factors can be used again with a different threshold.
To treat rejection as an error, use the result's `into_accepted()` to convert it to an `NumericalInstability` (`IllConditioned`) error.
The standard form `solve(&b)` uses the default conditions (`RankPolicy::Exact`, no threshold).
Since an exactly zero pivot produces a `Singular` error during factorization, the standard `solve` always accepts a solution for LU factors returned by `factor`.

## Mixed-precision iterative refinement

Mixed-precision iterative refinement performs the $\tfrac23 n^3$ operations of LU factorization in single precision (`f32`, unit roundoff $u_f = 2^{-24} \approx 6 \times 10^{-8}$) ([Iterative refinement](../../algorithms/iterative-refinement/)).
Only the residual $r_k = b - H x_k$ is computed in double precision, and the correction $d_k$ solved using single-precision factors is added as $x_{k+1} = x_k + d_k$.
Solving the correction equation by backward-stable single-precision LU gives a correction satisfying $(H + E_k)\,d_k = r_k$ for some $E_k$.
Let $\lVert E_k \rVert_\infty \le c_n \rho_n u_f \lVert H \rVert_\infty$ (where $c_n$ is a small polynomial in $n$ and $\rho_n$ is the LU growth factor) and $\theta = \lVert H^{-1} E_k \rVert_\infty < 1$; ignoring residual rounding, the error at each step satisfies

$$
\lVert x_{k+1} - x \rVert_\infty \lesssim \frac{\theta}{1 - \theta}\, \lVert x_k - x \rVert_\infty, \qquad \theta \le c_n \rho_n\, \kappa_\infty(H)\, u_f
$$


The contraction factor $\theta/(1-\theta)$ is below 1 when $\theta < 1/2$; as $\theta$ approaches $1/2$, more corrections are needed, and above it this bound no longer shows a decrease in error.
Thus, $\kappa_\infty(H) u_f$ being well below 1 is a guide to convergence.
For Hilbert matrices, $\kappa_2 u_f$ exceeds 1 at $n = 7$.
The converged solution's forward error relative to $x^\ast$ remains of order $\kappa u$, as with `f64` LU factorization.
Refinement solves the system with $H$ and $b$ stored in `f64`, whose storage rounding moves the solution by order $\kappa u$.

`IterativeRefinement<L, H>` is a [method object](oxref:linalg-execution-forms) that refines with a pair of factor precision `L` and residual precision `H` (`f32`/`f32`, `f64`/`f64`, `f32`/`f64`, and their complex counterparts); see [IterativeRefinement inputs and results](oxref:linalg-refinement-inputs) for the complete inputs and results.
The value holds only `RefinementOptions` settings, borrowing the original system and inverse operator on each call as `RefinementProblem::new(system, inverse, scaling, fallback)`.
In this cell, `system` is the original `f64` matrix `RefinementSystem::Dense` and `inverse` is `inverse_operator()` of the `f32` LU factors; refinement does not perform factorization itself.
It stops with `Converged` when backward error is at most `atol + rtol`; a correction that does not strictly decrease backward error is discarded and stops with `Stagnation`.
The correction limit is provided through `LinalgExecutionOptions::with_iteration_budget` and defaults to 10 per right-hand side.
Selecting `RefinementFallback::OnFailure` switches once to the `f64` inverse operator supplied as `fallback` (fallback) if the residual cannot be converted to factor precision, the lower-precision inverse operator fails numerically, or refinement stagnates.
The correction count includes discarded corrections and corrections after fallback.
`refine(&problem, &b, &x0)` returns the refined solution and `RefinementReport`; even unsuccessful refinement returns the best accepted iterate in `Ok`.
Among `RefinementTermination` reasons, only `Converged` means success; `Stagnation`, `IterationLimit`, `EvaluationLimit`, and `ExecutionStopped` indicate stopping due to stagnation or a limit, and `CorrectionFailed` indicates a correction failure without fallback.

For each order $n = 3, \dots, 10$, the next cell factorizes a `f32` Hilbert matrix and uses `IterativeRefinement::<f32, f64>` to refine from initial guess $x_0 = 0$ to a double-precision solution.
The inputs are the maximum correction count (1–20) and whether to enable fallback.
The table compares termination reason, correction count, backward error, and refined solution forward error with solving using only `f32` LU and with `f64` LU factorization (both using the standard `solve`).
The tolerances are $\mathrm{atol} = 0$ and $\mathrm{rtol} = 10^{-15}$, tested using normwise backward error $\eta$.
The outcome summarizes all orders: `satisfied` if every order is `Converged`, `partial` if any order stops early, and `failed` if any has `CorrectionFailed`.

//| title: "Iterative refinement of Hilbert systems with single-precision LU and double-precision residuals"

//|   max_iterations: {"type":"range","label":"Maximum corrections [count]","description":"Maximum number of corrections at one order.","min":1,"max":20,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   use_fallback: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Fall back to f64 LU","description":"Switch once to an f64 inverse operator when refinement stops progressing.","value":false}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fallback","label":"Fallback enabled","description":"Stagnating orders also become Converged after switching to the f64 inverse operator.","inputs":{"use_fallback":true}}
//|   - {"id":"one-correction","label":"Maximum corrections 1","description":"IterationLimit occurs after just one solve with the f32 factorization.","inputs":{"max_iterations":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/3/3","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.89,"rtol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/4/3","equals":"Stagnation"}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/2/6","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: fallback
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/7/3","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/7/8","equals":"Stagnation"}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/7/6","range":{"max":1e-2}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: one-correction
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":"IterationLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinement","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":1e-7,"max":1e-4}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, LinearOperator, ResidualTolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::{
    IterativeRefinement, LinalgExecutionOptions, LuDecomposition, MatrixForm,
    RefinementFallback, RefinementFallbackReason, RefinementOptions, RefinementProblem,
    RefinementSystem, RefinementTermination, StructuredMatrixView, SvdValues,
};

let tolerance = ResidualTolerance::new(0.0, 1e-15).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Supply the correction limit as the iteration budget of the composed execution options.
let budget = Budget::try_from(max_iterations.round() as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let policy = if use_fallback {
    RefinementFallback::OnFailure
} else {
    RefinementFallback::Disabled
};
let options = RefinementOptions::new(tolerance)
    .with_execution_options(LinalgExecutionOptions::new().with_iteration_budget(budget))
    .with_fallback(policy);
// The method object holds only settings; the problem (original system and inverse operator) is borrowed on each call.
let refinement = IterativeRefinement::<f32, f64>::new(options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let error = |v: &[f64]| v.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, &xi| m.max((xi - 1.0).abs()));
let single_unit = f64::from(f32::EPSILON) / 2.0;
let (mut rows, mut stopped, mut failed, mut hardest) = (Vec::new(), 0, 0, "");
for n in 3..=10_usize {
    let h = StructuredMatrixView::<f64>::hilbert(n, MatrixForm::Direct)
        .and_then(|view| view.materialize())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let h32 = StructuredMatrixView::<f32>::hilbert(n, MatrixForm::Direct)
        .and_then(|view| view.materialize())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let sigma = SvdValues::compute(&h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let kappa2 = sigma.singular_values()[0] / sigma.singular_values()[n - 1];
    let ones = vec![1.0_f64; n];
    let b = h.view().mul_vec(&ones[..]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let lu32 = LuDecomposition::factor(&h32).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let lu64 = LuDecomposition::factor(&h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (inverse, fallback_inverse) = (lu32.inverse_operator(), lu64.inverse_operator());
    let fallback: Option<&(dyn LinearOperator<f64> + Sync)> = if use_fallback {
        Some(&fallback_inverse)
    } else {
        None
    };
    let system = RefinementSystem::Dense(h.view());
    let problem =
        RefinementProblem::new(system, &inverse, None, fallback).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let zero = vec![0.0_f64; n];
    let (x, report) = refinement
        .refine(&problem, &b, &zero[..])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Direct solutions for comparison: f64 LU and a solve using only f32 LU (standard solve).
    let direct = lu64.solve(&b).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let b32: Vec<f32> = b.as_slice().iter().map(|&v| v as f32).collect();
    let single = lu32.solve(&b32[..]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let single: Vec<f64> = single.as_slice().iter().map(|&v| f64::from(v)).collect();
    let name = match report.termination() {
        RefinementTermination::Converged => "Converged",
        RefinementTermination::Stagnation => "Stagnation",
        RefinementTermination::IterationLimit => "IterationLimit",
        RefinementTermination::EvaluationLimit => "EvaluationLimit",
        RefinementTermination::ExecutionStopped(_) => "ExecutionStopped",
        RefinementTermination::CorrectionFailed => "CorrectionFailed",
    };
    let reason = report.fallback_reason().map(|r| match r {
        RefinementFallbackReason::Conversion => "Conversion",
        RefinementFallbackReason::NumericalFailure => "NumericalFailure",
        RefinementFallbackReason::Stagnation => "Stagnation",
    });
    stopped += usize::from(!report.is_success());
    failed += usize::from(report.termination() == RefinementTermination::CorrectionFailed);
    hardest = name;
    rows.push((
        n,
        kappa2 * single_unit,
        error(&single),
        name,
        report.iterations(),
        report.final_error().map(|e| e.normwise()),
        error(x.as_slice()),
        error(direct.as_slice()),
        reason,
    ));
}
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Degree n [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "κ₂ u_f [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Forward error using only f32 LU [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Corrections [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Backward error η [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Refined solution forward error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "f64 LU forward error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Fallback reason", "nullText": "No fallback" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "refinement" })
);

    Outcome::failed("Corrections failed without fallback at some orders")
} else if stopped > 0 {
    Outcome::partial("Refinement stopped before reaching tolerance at some orders").explanation(
        "Solutions at stopped orders are the best accepted iterates; examine their forward errors in the table.",
    )
} else {
    Outcome::satisfied("Backward error fell below tolerance 10⁻¹⁵ at every order")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(hardest)
        .diagnostic("Number of stopped orders", stopped as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Maximum corrections", max_iterations, None)
);

With the defaults (maximum corrections 10, no fallback), $n \le 6$ ($\kappa_2 u_f < 1$) reaches `Converged` in 3–7 corrections, and the refined solution's forward error has the same order $\kappa u$ as `f64` LU factorization.
The number of corrections grows as $\kappa_2 u_f$ approaches 1, reaching 7 at $n = 6$ ($\kappa_2 u_f = 0.89$).
Convergence occurs at $n = 6$ even though $\kappa_1 u_f$ is 1.7, above 1; $\kappa u_f < 1$ is a rough guide that omits the constant $c_n \rho_n$.
At $n \ge 7$, corrections cease reducing backward error and stop with `Stagnation`, leaving backward error at the single-precision level $10^{-10}$–$10^{-8}$.
The refined solution's forward error is 0.21 at $n = 7$ and exceeds 1 at $n \ge 8$, leaving almost no correct digits, as with the solution using only `f32` LU.
At $n = 9$ and $10$, the second correction is discarded and the value after the first correction is returned unchanged.
The outcome is `partial`, with status name `Stagnation`, the termination reason at $n = 10$.
Even with maximum corrections 20, stagnation occurs within 6 corrections, so convergence for $n \le 6$ and stagnation for $n \ge 7$ are unchanged.
With maximum corrections 1, the result is just one solve using the `f32` factorization, and every order has termination reason `IterationLimit`.

Enabling fallback switches to the `f64` inverse operator upon stagnation (fallback reason `Stagnation`), and $n = 7$–$10$ reaches `Converged` in 4–7 total corrections before and after the switch.
The outcome becomes `satisfied`.
Forward errors for $n = 7$ through 10 are $2.1 \times 10^{-8}$, $3.7 \times 10^{-7}$, $9.9 \times 10^{-6}$, and $6.5 \times 10^{-4}$, at the same orders of magnitude as `f64` LU factorization.
Since refinement stops when backward error falls below tolerance $10^{-15}$, forward error can generally remain of order $2\kappa_1 \times 10^{-15}$ (0.07 at $n = 10$), as in the bound above.
In this example, backward error after the switch decreases to order $10^{-17}$, reaching the same level as `f64` LU factorization.
To reduce the forward error bound to the same level as LU factorization, reduce the tolerance to a few multiples of $u$.

## Summary

The Hilbert matrix condition number increases by about a factor of 34 with each order and exceeds the reciprocal of double-precision unit roundoff $1/u \approx 9 \times 10^{15}$ at $n = 12$.
The backward error of LU factorization with partial pivoting remains at the scale of unit roundoff at every order, whereas forward error grows with $\kappa u$ and stays within bound $2\kappa_1\eta/(1-\kappa_1\eta)$ over $\kappa_1 \eta < 1$.
Therefore, a small $\eta$ in `residual()` alone does not establish an accurate solution.
The Hager–Higham estimate, computed from LU factors in $O(n^2)$ work, agrees well with exact $\kappa_1$ for $n \le 12$ in this example, but must be interpreted as an unguaranteed guide.
To stop on conditioning before publishing a solution, supply a threshold with `with_minimum_reciprocal_condition` and receive rejection through termination reason `IllConditioned` in the form with options.
Iterative refinement with single-precision factors reaches the same accuracy as double-precision LU while $\kappa u_f$ is roughly below 1, and stops with `Stagnation` above that range; check the termination reason and enable fallback or solve with double-precision factors.
