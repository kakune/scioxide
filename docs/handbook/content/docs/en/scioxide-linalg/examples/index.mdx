The examples for `scioxide-linalg` reduce concrete problems in mechanics, stochastic processes, quantum mechanics, statistics, control, and heat conduction to linear equations, eigenvalue problems, singular value decomposition, matrix functions, matrix equations, or iterative methods.
Every example compares SciOxide API results against independently obtained values such as closed-form solutions, a second algorithmic path, or statistical theory, and discusses the magnitudes of residuals, forward errors, and condition numbers.

The examples are ordered from top to bottom through direct methods (tridiagonal and SVD), eigenvalue problems, matrix functions, ill-conditioning and iterative refinement, structured solvers, iterative methods, matrix equations, and execution control.
Cells use ordinary forms and one-call functions when reports are not inspected, and detailed forms and plans when termination reasons, residuals, or ranks are inspected.
Only computations inspecting workspace or attempt records (the cancellation example and Rabi-oscillation actions) and structured plans exposing only borrowed solves (Yule–Walker) use borrowed forms (see [Workspaces, execution contexts, and attempt records](oxref:linalg-execution-forms) for the distinction).
When a solver's termination reason determines a cell's conclusion, that conclusion appears as a computation outcome at the start of the output.
When reading values, examine both the error magnitudes discussed in each example (relative residuals, forward errors, differences between two paths) and whether conditioning and the algorithm's properties predict those magnitudes.
Algorithm derivations and result-acceptance conventions are collected in [Mathematics and shared conventions of linear algebra](../algorithms/).

## Summary

Start with Hilbert matrices and spring chains for linear-system errors and conditioning, SVD for rank decisions, and vibrating systems and Markov chains for eigenvalues and stationary distributions.
Rabi oscillations cover matrix exponentials and their actions, LQR covers matrix equations, and the heat-conduction and cancellation examples cover preconditioner selection and results from stopped iterative methods.
Every example compares results against a closed-form solution or a second algorithmic path; likewise, provide an independent check for your own problem instead of inferring accuracy from a small residual alone.
Before using results, inspect the termination reason and estimate forward-error magnitudes from backward error and estimated conditioning.
