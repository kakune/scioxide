Near-resonant electromagnetic driving of two energy levels in an atom, ion, or superconducting circuit makes probability oscillate periodically between ground and excited states (Rabi oscillations).
Qubit operations and nuclear magnetic resonance pulses are designed from the period and amplitude of these oscillations.
This example computes time evolution as a complex matrix exponential and compares it with the analytical Rabi formula and closed-form state.
It also advances only the state using the action $\exp(-\mathrm{i}HT)\psi_0$ through another algorithm, without forming the matrix exponential, and compares the two algorithms.

## Hamiltonian and time evolution

Take ground state $\lvert 0 \rangle = (1, 0)$, excited state $\lvert 1 \rangle = (0, 1)$, and units with $\hbar = 1$.
Energy is measured as angular frequency [rad/μs] and time in μs ($\Omega/2\pi$ is then frequency in MHz).
In a frame rotating at the drive frequency, the rotating-wave approximation gives Hamiltonian

$$
H = \frac12 \begin{bmatrix} -\Delta & \Omega \\ \Omega & \Delta \end{bmatrix} \quad [\mathrm{rad}/\mu\mathrm{s}]
$$


$\Omega$ is the Rabi frequency proportional to drive strength, and $\Delta$ is the difference between drive and transition frequencies (detuning).
Since $H$ is time independent, the Schrödinger equation $\mathrm{i}\,\dot\psi = H\psi$ has solution $\psi(t) = U(t)\psi(0)$ with $U(t) = \exp(-\mathrm{i}Ht)$, and $U$ is unitary ($U^{\mathsf H} U = I$).

Since $H$ has trace 0 and satisfies $H^2 = (\Omega_R/2)^2 I$, the exponential series reduces to a closed form.

$$
U(t) = \cos\frac{\Omega_R t}{2}\, I - \mathrm{i}\,\frac{2}{\Omega_R} \sin\frac{\Omega_R t}{2}\, H, \qquad \Omega_R = \sqrt{\Omega^2 + \Delta^2}
$$

Here $\Omega_R$ is the generalized Rabi frequency.
Starting from $\psi(0) = \lvert 0 \rangle$, the state is

$$
\psi(t) = \left( \cos\frac{\Omega_R t}{2} + \mathrm{i}\,\frac{\Delta}{\Omega_R} \sin\frac{\Omega_R t}{2},\; -\mathrm{i}\,\frac{\Omega}{\Omega_R} \sin\frac{\Omega_R t}{2} \right)
$$

The excited-state probability is given by the Rabi formula

$$
P_e(t) = \lvert \psi_1(t) \rvert^2 = \frac{\Omega^2}{\Omega_R^2} \sin^2\frac{\Omega_R t}{2}
$$


On resonance ($\Delta = 0$), the amplitude is 1, and a “$\pi$ pulse” of duration $t_\pi = \pi/\Omega$ transfers the state completely to the excited state.
Detuning reduces the amplitude to $\Omega^2/\Omega_R^2$ and shortens the period $2\pi/\Omega_R$.
Since $P_e$ sees only the magnitude of excited-state probability amplitude $\psi_1$, check the state phase against $\psi(t)$ above.

## Computing and checking the exponential

`matrix_function(&a, MatrixFunction::Exp)` is a [one-shot function](oxref:linalg-execution-forms) returning the exponential $\exp(A)$ of square matrix $A$ as a new matrix; internally it constructs and evaluates a `MatrixFunctionPlan` Plan with default settings ([Matrix functions](../../algorithms/matrix-functions-and-equations/)).
It uses Al-Mohy and Higham's (2009) scaling and squaring: divide $A$ by $2^s$, apply a Padé approximation of degree 3, 5, 7, 9, or 13, then square the result $s$ times.
The degree and $s$ are chosen so the exact-arithmetic Padé backward error does not exceed double-precision unit roundoff $u = 2^{-53}$; no Schur decomposition is used.
For an order-$n$ matrix, the cost is $O(n^3)$.
To also compute Fréchet derivatives or condition estimates for the same matrix, construct and reuse `MatrixFunctionPlan`.

Padé approximation keeps backward error at most $u$ but is not designed to return a unitary matrix, so computed $U$ is unitary only to rounding accuracy.
Compute $U(\Delta t) = \exp(-\mathrm{i}H\Delta t)$ once and repeat $\psi_{k+1} = U(\Delta t)\psi_k$ $N$ times to track evolution and examine three quantities:

1. The difference between $P_e$ and the Rabi formula at each time, and between state $\psi_k$ and closed-form $\psi(t_k)$.
2. Entry magnitudes of $U^{\mathsf H} U - I$ (unitarity) and $\lVert \psi_k \rVert_2^2 - 1$ (probability conservation).
3. The final state compared with a single evaluation of $\exp(-\mathrm{i}HT)$ and an action that avoids forming the matrix exponential (next section).

The following definitions are shared by two cells.
`generator` supplies the entries of generator $-\mathrm{i}Ht$, and `exact_state` returns closed-form $\psi(t)$.
The closed form is a check, so it uses inherent `f64` methods (`sin_cos`, `hypot`).

```rust
//| id: linalg-two-level-rabi-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Two-level generator and closed-form state"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg]
use scioxide_core::{Complex, SciOxideError};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;

/// Generator −iHt. H = ½[[−Δ, Ω], [Ω, Δ]], Ω and Δ in [rad/μs], t in [μs].
pub(crate) fn generator(
    rabi: f64,
    detuning: f64,
    t: f64,
) -> Result<Matrix<Complex<f64>>, SciOxideError> {
    let (d, w) = (0.5 * detuning * t, 0.5 * rabi * t);
    Matrix::from_rows(&[
        [Complex::new(0.0, d), Complex::new(0.0, -w)],
        [Complex::new(0.0, -w), Complex::new(0.0, -d)],
    ])
}

/// Closed-form ψ(t) = U(t)|0⟩ (reference using inherent f64 methods).
pub(crate) fn exact_state(rabi: f64, detuning: f64, t: f64) -> [Complex<f64>; 2] {
    let omega_r = rabi.hypot(detuning);
    let (s, c) = (0.5 * omega_r * t).sin_cos();
    [
        Complex::new(c, detuning / omega_r * s),
        Complex::new(0.0, -rabi / omega_r * s),
    ]
}
```

The next cell selects Rabi frequency $\Omega$ (0.5–20 rad/μs), detuning $\Delta$ (−20–20 rad/μs), pulse duration $T$ (0.5–10 μs), and step count $N$ (10–1000), then multiplies $U(T/N)$ $N$ times to compare excitation probability evolution with the formula.
The table shows generalized Rabi frequency, period, amplitude, and maximum errors for checks 1 and 2 above; the plot overlays computed $P_e$ and the formula (dashed).
This cell passes $\Omega$, $\Delta$, $T$, $N$, and final state $\psi_N$ to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: linalg-two-level-rabi
//| ref: linalg-two-level-rabi
//| title: "Rabi time evolution using the matrix exponential"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [linalg-two-level-rabi-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg]
//| exports: [rabi, detuning, duration, steps, final_state]
//| inputs:
//|   rabi: {"type":"range","label":"Rabi frequency Ω [rad/μs]","description":"Driving strength.","min":0.5,"max":20,"step":0.5,"value":6}
//|   detuning: {"type":"range","label":"Detuning Δ [rad/μs]","description":"Resonance at 0.","min":-20,"max":20,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//|   duration: {"type":"range","label":"Pulse duration T [μs]","description":"Duration to track.","min":0.5,"max":10,"step":0.5,"value":3}
//|   steps: {"type":"range","label":"Step count N [-]","description":"Number of multiplications by U(T/N).","min":10,"max":1000,"step":10,"value":400}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"resonance","label":"Δ = 0","description":"On resonance, amplitude is 1 and the state transfers completely to the excited state at t = π/Ω.","inputs":{"detuning":0}}
//|   - {"id":"far-detuned","label":"Ω = 0.5, Δ = −20 rad/μs","description":"Large detuning reduces amplitude to 6 × 10⁻⁴ and produces fast oscillations.","inputs":{"rabi":0.5,"detuning":-20}}
//|   - {"id":"long-pulse","label":"T = 10 μs, N = 1000","description":"More multiplications accumulate rounding errors in proportion to their count.","inputs":{"duration":10,"steps":1000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":6.324555320336759,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.9,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: resonance
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: far-detuned
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":6.24609618988132e-4,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: long-pulse
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rabi-summary","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-11}}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_linalg::{Matrix, MatrixFunction, Vector, matrix_function};

let n = steps.round() as usize;
let dt = duration / n as f64;
// Compute one-step evolution U(Δt) = exp(−iHΔt) with the one-shot function.
let generator_step = generator(rabi, detuning, dt).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let step = matrix_function(&generator_step, MatrixFunction::Exp).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Unitarity error max |(UᴴU − I)ᵢⱼ|.
let gram = step
    .view()
    .adjoint()
    .mul_mat(&step)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let identity = Matrix::<Complex<f64>>::identity(2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let unitarity = gram
    .as_slice()
    .iter()
    .zip(identity.as_slice())
    .map(|(g, i)| (*g - *i).norm())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);

// From ψ₀ = |0⟩, repeat ψ_{k+1} = U(Δt) ψ_k N times, comparing with the closed form at every time.
let mut psi = Vector::from_vec(vec![Complex::new(1.0, 0.0), Complex::new(0.0, 0.0)]);
let (mut numeric, mut formula) = (Vec::with_capacity(n + 1), Vec::with_capacity(n + 1));
let (mut formula_error, mut state_error, mut norm_error) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
for k in 0..=n {
    let t = k as f64 * dt;
    let state = psi.as_slice();
    let exact = exact_state(rabi, detuning, t);
    let (p, p_exact) = (state[1].norm_sqr(), exact[1].norm_sqr());
    formula_error = formula_error.max((p - p_exact).abs());
    state_error = state_error
        .max((state[0] - exact[0]).norm())
        .max((state[1] - exact[1]).norm());
    norm_error = norm_error.max((state[0].norm_sqr() + p - 1.0).abs());
    numeric.push([t, p]);
    formula.push([t, p_exact]);
    if k < n {
        psi = step.view().mul_vec(&psi).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
}

let omega_r = rabi.hypot(detuning);
let amplitude = (rabi / omega_r).powi(2);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Ω_R [rad/μs]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Period 2π/Ω_R [μs]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Amplitude Ω²/Ω_R² [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum difference of P_e from formula [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum state difference max|ψ_k − ψ(t_k)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Unitarity max|UᴴU − I| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Probability conservation max|‖ψ_k‖² − 1| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        omega_r,
        2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI / omega_r,
        amplitude,
        formula_error,
        state_error,
        unitarity,
        norm_error,
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rabi-summary" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "P_e from matrix exponential", "points": numeric }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Rabi formula",
            "points": formula,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Time t [μs]",
        "yLabel": "Excitation probability P_e [-]",
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": amplitude, "label": "Amplitude Ω²/Ω_R²" }
        ]
    })
);

let last = psi.as_slice();
export!(rabi, rabi);
export!(detuning, detuning);
export!(duration, duration);
export!(steps, n);
export!(
    final_state,
    [last[0].re, last[0].im, last[1].re, last[1].im]
);
```

With the defaults ($\Omega = 6$ rad/μs, $\Delta = 2$ rad/μs, $T = 3$ μs, $N = 400$), $\Omega_R = 6.325$ rad/μs, the period is 0.9935 μs, amplitude is 0.9, and the two plotted curves overlap.
The one-step $U$ unitarity error is $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$, of the order of unit roundoff.
After 400 multiplications, the $P_e$ formula difference reaches $2.7 \times 10^{-14}$, state difference $1.8 \times 10^{-14}$, and $\lvert \lVert \psi_k \rVert_2^2 - 1 \rvert$ reaches $3.6 \times 10^{-14}$.

Varying the step count shows that errors come from accumulated rounding rather than a time-stepping approximation.
At $N = 10$ all three errors fall to at most $2 \times 10^{-15}$; at $N = 1000$ they grow to $3 \times 10^{-14}$–$6 \times 10^{-14}$.
$U(\Delta t)$ is computed to rounding accuracy regardless of step size, so table errors accumulate relative rounding of order $u$ in each step's $U(\Delta t)$ and products over the multiplication count.
However, multiplication count alone does not determine error: even with the same $N = 1000$, $T = 10$ μs gives $2.3 \times 10^{-13}$.

At $\Delta = 0$ the amplitude is 1, reaching $P_e = 1$ at $t_\pi = \pi/6 \approx 0.524$ μs.
Large detuning such as $\Omega = 0.5$ rad/μs and $\Delta = -20$ rad/μs reduces amplitude to $6.2 \times 10^{-4}$, giving fast, small oscillations of period 0.314 μs.
Then the $P_e$ formula difference is $4.0 \times 10^{-17}$, well below amplitude $6.2 \times 10^{-4}$, and state difference remains $1.4 \times 10^{-14}$.

## Comparison with an action without forming the matrix

`ExponentialAction` is a [method object](oxref:linalg-method-values) computing the action $\exp(tA)b$ on vector $b$ using only products with $A$, without forming matrix $\exp(tA)$.
It uses Al-Mohy and Higham's (2011) truncated Taylor method, dividing $[0, t]$ into $s$ stages and applying a degree-$m$ Taylor polynomial at each stage ([Exponential action using truncated Taylor series](oxref:linalg-truncated-taylor)).
$(m, s)$ is chosen from a caller-supplied upper bound on $\lVert A - \mu I \rVert_1$ (shift $\mu$ defaults to 0); for a large bound, it also uses 1-norm estimates of $\lVert (t(A - \mu I))^p \rVert_1^{1/p}$.
The method cannot verify the bound; underestimation can make the result inaccurate.
This cell passes $\lVert -\mathrm{i}H \rVert_1 = (\lvert \Omega \rvert + \lvert \Delta \rvert)/2$ computed directly from the definition using `MatrixView::norm(MatrixNorm::One)` rather than estimated.

When the operator application count reaches its limit (including adjoint products for 1-norm estimation), the termination reason is `SpectralTermination::EvaluationLimit` and the result returns in `Ok`.
The default limit is 100 000, changed with `with_applications`.
On a budget stop, the output contains the value $\exp(f\,tA)b$ through completed stages.
$f$ is the fraction of $[0, t]$ covered by completed stages, read by calling `ExponentialActionAccepted::progress()` on the value returned by workspace `last_accepted()`.
To read this fraction, the cell uses the [destination-taking API](oxref:linalg-execution-forms) `apply_into` accepting output and workspace (the owned-result API `apply` suffices when workspace diagnostics are unnecessary).

The next cell receives $\Omega$, $\Delta$, $T$, $N$, and $\psi_N$ from the previous cell, computes the final state through three paths, and compares them with the closed form.
The paths are $N$ products of $U(\Delta t)$, $U(T)\psi_0$ evaluated once by `matrix_function`, and $\exp(-\mathrm{i}HT)\psi_0$ from `ExponentialAction`.
The input is the operator application limit (1–1000); the table shows comparison time, closed-form difference, operator application count, and termination reason.
A budget-limited action is compared with the closed form at time $fT$.
The outcome uses the action's termination reason: `satisfied` for `Converged`, `partial` for a budget stop or cancellation, and `failed` for `AccuracyLimit`.

```rust
//| id: linalg-two-level-rabi-action
//| ref: linalg-two-level-rabi-action
//| title: "Final state from a single exponential and a Taylor action"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [linalg-two-level-rabi-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg]
//| dependsOn:
//|   evolution: linalg-two-level-rabi
//| inputs:
//|   applications: {"type":"range","label":"Operator application limit [count]","description":"Number of Ax products available to the Taylor action.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":1000}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"budget-stop","label":"Limit 40 applications","description":"A budget stop gives EvaluationLimit and output at time fT through completed stages.","inputs":{"applications":40}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":3.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: budget-stop
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"EvaluationLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1.5,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
use scioxide_core::{BlockVectorView, BlockVectorViewMut, Budget, Complex, ExecutionContext};
use scioxide_linalg::{
    ExponentialAction, MatrixFunction, MatrixNorm, PhiFunction, SpectralTermination,
    matrix_function,
};

let read = |key: &str| evolution[key].as_f64().ok_or(format!("Missing {key}"));
let (rabi, detuning, duration) = (read("rabi")?, read("detuning")?, read("duration")?);
let steps = read("steps")?;
let product: Vec<f64> = (0..4)
    .map(|i| evolution["final_state"][i].as_f64().ok_or("ψ_N is missing"))
    .collect::<Result<_, _>>()?;
let product = [
    Complex::new(product[0], product[1]),
    Complex::new(product[2], product[3]),
];
// Closed-form ψ(t) difference max|ψ − ψ(t)|.
let error = |state: [Complex<f64>; 2], t: f64| {
    let exact = exact_state(rabi, detuning, t);
    (state[0] - exact[0])
        .norm()
        .max((state[1] - exact[1]).norm())
};

// Path 2: compute U(T) = exp(−iHT) once and extract its first column U(T)|0⟩.
let whole = generator(rabi, detuning, duration).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let total = matrix_function(&whole, MatrixFunction::Exp).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let pade = [total.as_slice()[0], total.as_slice()[2]];

// Path 3: action exp(TA)|0⟩ for A = −iH. Supply exactly computed upper bound ‖A‖₁.
let a = generator(rabi, detuning, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let bound = a.view().norm(MatrixNorm::One).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let budget = Budget::try_from(applications.round() as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let action = ExponentialAction::new(bound).with_applications(budget);
let operator = a.view().try_finite().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let input = [Complex::new(1.0, 0.0), Complex::new(0.0, 0.0)];
let mut output = [Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); 2];
let mut workspace = action.workspace(&operator, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let report = action
    .apply_into(
        &operator,
        PhiFunction::exp(duration),
        BlockVectorView::new(&input, 2, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
        BlockVectorViewMut::new(&mut output, 2, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential_native(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let progress = workspace
    .last_accepted()
    .map_or(0.0, |accepted| accepted.progress()[0]);
let reached = progress * duration;
let termination = report.termination();
let name = match termination {
    SpectralTermination::Converged => "Converged",
    SpectralTermination::EvaluationLimit => "EvaluationLimit",
    SpectralTermination::IterationLimit => "IterationLimit",
    SpectralTermination::InvariantSubspace => "InvariantSubspace",
    SpectralTermination::AccuracyLimit => "AccuracyLimit",
    SpectralTermination::ExecutionStopped(_) => "ExecutionStopped",
};

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Path" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Comparison time t [μs]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 4 } },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed-form difference max|ψ − ψ(t)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Operator applications [count]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Termination reason", "nullText": "None" }
]);
let (product_error, pade_error) = (error(product, duration), error(pade, duration));
let (action_error, applied) = (error(output, reached), Some(report.applications()));
let rows: Vec<(&str, f64, f64, Option<usize>, Option<&str>)> = vec![
    (
        "N products of U(Δt)",
        duration,
        product_error,
        Some(steps as usize),
        None,
    ),
    (
        "Single exponential U(T)ψ₀ (Padé)",
        duration,
        pade_error,
        None,
        None,
    ),
    (
        "Action exp(−iHT)ψ₀ (Taylor)",
        reached,
        action_error,
        applied,
        Some(name),
    ),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "routes" })
);

let outcome = match termination {
    SpectralTermination::Converged => Outcome::satisfied("Taylor action converged to the final time")
        .explanation("Compare the three paths using the closed-form differences in the table."),
    SpectralTermination::AccuracyLimit => {
        Outcome::failed("Taylor action failed the accuracy check")
    }
    _ => Outcome::partial("Taylor action stopped at the budget")
        .explanation("The output is exp(fT·(−iH))ψ₀ through completed stages, interpreted as the state at time fT."),
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(name)
        .diagnostic("Operator applications", report.applications() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Completed fraction f", progress, None)
        .diagnostic("Closed-form difference", action_error, None)
);
```

With the defaults, $N = 400$ repeated products differ by $1.8 \times 10^{-14}$, while the single exponential agrees with the closed form to $4.5 \times 10^{-16}$ and the Taylor action to $2.3 \times 10^{-15}$ using 70 operator applications.
If only the final state is needed, a single exponential or action incurs less rounding than repeated step products.
Even at $\Omega = \lvert\Delta\rvert = 20$ rad/μs and $T = 10$ μs, where $\lVert A \rVert_1 T$ is largest over the previous cell's input range, the action converges in 823 applications, within this cell's default limit of 1000.
The closed-form difference there is $1.8 \times 10^{-12}$, larger than the single exponential's $3.6 \times 10^{-15}$.

With limit 40, the action stops with `EvaluationLimit` and outcome `partial`.
The default action divides $[0, T]$ into two stages, so the completed fraction is $f = 0.5$ and output is the state at time $fT = 1.5$ μs.
This output agrees with closed-form $\psi(1.5)$ to difference $5.0 \times 10^{-16}$, but is not $\psi(3)$.
With limit 34 or less, no stage completes, $f = 0$, and the output remains initial state $\psi_0$.
Check termination reason and $f$ to avoid treating a stopped action as a final-time result.

## Resonance curves and pulse sequences

Amplitude $\Omega^2/(\Omega^2 + \Delta^2)$ as a function of detuning $\Delta$ is Lorentzian, falling to $1/2$ at $\lvert\Delta\rvert = \Omega$, so its full width at half maximum is $2\Omega$.
In experiments sweeping drive frequency to find resonance, this width sets the frequency resolution.
For piecewise-constant Hamiltonians, pulse sequences changing $\Omega$ or $\Delta$ in each interval can be handled by recomputing and multiplying $U$ successively.
For fine changes in a continuously time-dependent Hamiltonian, an ordinary differential equation solver ([scioxide-ode](../../../scioxide-ode/)) is more appropriate.

## Summary

For a time-independent Hamiltonian, the evolution operator $U(t) = \exp(-\mathrm{i}Ht)$ can be computed as a complex matrix exponential with `matrix_function(&a, MatrixFunction::Exp)`.
Scaling and squaring does not structurally preserve unitarity, so check $U^{\mathsf H}U - I$ and $\lVert\psi\rVert_2^2 - 1$ (both were at rounding scale here).
The error from repeated step products does not decrease with finer steps; it is rounding that grows with multiplication count, so compute a single exponential or action if only the final state is needed.
`ExponentialAction` computes the action using only products with $A$, with the caller supplying an upper bound on $\lVert A - \mu I \rVert_1$.
When its termination reason is `EvaluationLimit`, interpret output as the state at time $fT$ rather than a result at $T$.
