A chain of particles connected by springs is a basic model for crystal lattice vibrations and nanometer-scale mechanical resonators.
A single atom of different mass (an impurity) changes the distribution of vibration frequencies.
In particular, a light impurity creates a high-frequency mode absent from a uniform chain, with amplitude exponentially localized around the impurity.
This example solves vibrations as a generalized eigenproblem and checks the results against analytical uniform-chain solutions and localized-mode theory for an infinitely long chain.

---

## Equations of motion and generalized eigenproblem

Connect $N$ particles between fixed walls at both ends with springs of stiffness $k$ [N/m].
All particles have mass $m$ [kg], except the central particle $c = (N+1)/2$ ($N$ is odd), whose mass is $m_d = r\,m$.
For displacement $u_i(t)$ of particle $i$, the equation of motion is $m_i \ddot u_i = k(u_{i-1} - 2u_i + u_{i+1})$; setting $u_i = \phi_i e^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}$ gives

$$
K\phi = \omega^2 M\phi, \qquad K = k \operatorname{tridiag}(-1, 2, -1), \quad M = \operatorname{diag}(m_1, \dots, m_N)
$$


Since $K$ is symmetric positive definite and $M$ is positive diagonal, all eigenvalues $\omega^2$ are positive and eigenvectors can be chosen orthonormal with respect to $M$ ($\Phi^{\mathsf T} M \Phi = I$).

`GeneralizedHermitianEigenDecomposition::decompose(&k, &m, HermitianInput::Exact, HermitianInput::Exact)` computes the entire spectrum in $O(N^3)$ operations ([Generalized eigenproblems and QZ decomposition](../../algorithms/generalized-eigenproblems/)).
It divides $K$ and $M$ by their respective largest absolute entries, reduces to the standard eigenproblem $L^{-1}KL^{-\mathsf T}w = \omega^2 w$ using Cholesky factorization $M = LL^{\mathsf T}$ of $M$, then tridiagonalizes $L^{-1}KL^{-\mathsf T}$ with Householder transformations and solves.
This is the [standard form](oxref:linalg-execution-forms) using default `HermitianEigenOptions`; to change eigenvalue selection, order, or accuracy, pass settings to the form with options `decompose_with_options`.
`HermitianInput::Exact` requests checking that the supplied matrix is exactly symmetric; a nonsymmetric matrix gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).

Because $M$ is diagonal, setting $\psi = M^{1/2}\phi$ also gives a standard eigenproblem with symmetric tridiagonal matrix

$$
\tilde K = M^{-1/2} K M^{-1/2}, \qquad \tilde K_{ii} = \frac{2k}{m_i}, \quad \tilde K_{i,i+1} = -\frac{k}{\sqrt{m_i m_{i+1}}}
$$

The standard eigenproblem is $\tilde K \psi = \omega^2 \psi$.
Passing this $\tilde K$ to `HermitianEigenDecomposition::tridiagonal` bypasses Cholesky reduction and Householder transformations; comparing the two paths reveals errors introduced by reduction and back-transformation.
Both paths share the tridiagonal eigensolver (implicit QL through order 32, divide-and-conquer above), so use the analytical uniform-chain solution and infinite-chain theory as independent checks of the complete algorithm.

## Dispersion relation of a uniform chain

For $r = 1$, eigenvectors satisfying the boundary conditions are $\phi_i = \sin(ij\pi/(N+1))$, with natural angular frequencies

$$
\omega_j = 2\sqrt{k/m}\, \sin\frac{j\pi}{2(N+1)}, \qquad j = 1, \dots, N
$$


All natural angular frequencies lie below $\omega_{\max} = 2\sqrt{k/m}$; this range is the propagating-wave band of the uniform chain.

The following definitions block shares this model between two cells.
`masses` supplies the mass sequence with a central impurity, while `chain` assembles stiffness matrix $K$ and mass matrix $M$ as dense matrices.

```rust
//| id: linalg-chain-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Stiffness and mass matrices of a spring–mass chain with an impurity"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;

/// Mass sequence. Only central particle c = (N + 1)/2 (zero-based N/2) has mass r m.
pub(crate) fn masses(n: usize, mass: f64, ratio: f64) -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..n)
        .map(|i| if i == n / 2 { ratio * mass } else { mass })
        .collect()
}

/// Stiffness matrix K = k tridiag(−1, 2, −1) and mass matrix M = diag(mᵢ) (both exactly symmetric).
pub(crate) fn chain(
    spring: f64,
    masses: &[f64],
) -> Result<(Matrix<f64>, Matrix<f64>), SciOxideError> {
    let n = masses.len();
    let mut stiff = Matrix::zeros(n, n)?;
    let mut mass = Matrix::zeros(n, n)?;
    for (idx, value) in stiff.as_mut_slice().iter_mut().enumerate() {
        let (i, j) = (idx / n, idx % n);
        *value = match i.abs_diff(j) {
            0 => 2.0 * spring,
            1 => -spring,
            _ => 0.0,
        };
    }
    for (i, &mi) in masses.iter().enumerate() {
        mass.as_mut_slice()[i * n + i] = mi;
    }
    Ok((stiff, mass))
}
```

The next cell selects particle count $N \in [11, 81]$ (odd), stiffness $k \in [10, 1000]$ N/m, mass $m \in [0.1, 10]$ kg, and impurity mass ratio $r = m_d/m \in [0.2, 3]$, then solves the generalized eigenproblem and compares with the tridiagonal path.
The first table compares frequencies $f_j = \omega_j/(2\pi)$ [Hz] of the three lowest and three highest modes with analytical uniform-chain values ($r = 1$) at the same $N$, $k$, and $m$.
At $r = 1$, check analytical agreement; at $r \ne 1$, read the impurity-induced frequency changes.
The second table shows the maximum relative difference in $\omega^2$ between paths, $M$-orthogonality error $\max\lvert \Phi^{\mathsf T}M\Phi - I \rvert$, the largest residual indicator over all eigenpairs, and band edge $2\sqrt{k/m}/(2\pi)$ [Hz].
The residual indicator returned by `residuals()` is $\lVert Kv - \lambda Mv \rVert_\infty / (N(\lVert K \rVert_{\max} + \lvert\lambda\rvert \lVert M \rVert_{\max})\lVert v \rVert_{\max})$ for each eigenpair $(\lambda, v)$, where $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\max}$ is the magnitude of the largest absolute entry.

```rust
//| id: linalg-vibration-modes
//| ref: linalg-vibration-modes
//| title: "Natural frequencies of a spring–mass chain through two paths"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [linalg-chain-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg]
//| inputs:
//|   nodes: {"type":"range","label":"Particle count N","description":"Odd. The central particle is the impurity.","min":11,"max":81,"step":2,"value":31}
//|   spring: {"type":"range","label":"Spring constant k [N/m]","description":"The same for every spring.","min":10,"max":1000,"step":10,"value":100}
//|   mass: {"type":"range","label":"Mass m [kg]","description":"Mass of every particle except the impurity.","min":0.1,"max":10,"step":0.1,"value":1}
//|   ratio: {"type":"range","label":"Impurity mass ratio r = m_d/m","description":"r < 1 is a light impurity; r > 1 is a heavy impurity.","min":0.2,"max":3,"step":0.1,"value":0.4}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"uniform","label":"r = 1","description":"Uniform chain: every mode agrees with analytical values.","inputs":{"ratio":1}}
//|   - {"id":"heavy","label":"r = 3","description":"A heavy impurity lowers odd-numbered modes without producing a mode outside the band.","inputs":{"ratio":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":3.98,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: uniform
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: heavy
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/5/1","range":{"max":3.1831}}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":0}}
use scioxide_linalg::{
    GeneralizedHermitianEigenDecomposition, HermitianEigenDecomposition, HermitianInput,
};

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let hz = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let (n, k, m) = (nodes.round() as usize, spring, mass);
let masses = masses(n, m, ratio);
let (stiff, mass_matrix) = chain(k, &masses).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// K and M were assembled exactly symmetric, so pass both as Exact.
let eigen = GeneralizedHermitianEigenDecomposition::decompose(
    &stiff,
    &mass_matrix,
    HermitianInput::Exact,
    HermitianInput::Exact,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Separate path: eigenvalues of symmetrized tridiagonal M^{-1/2} K M^{-1/2}.
let diag: Vec<f64> = masses.iter().map(|&mi| 2.0 * k / mi).collect();
let off: Vec<f64> = (0..n - 1)
    .map(|i| -k / (masses[i] * masses[i + 1]).sqrt())
    .collect();
let tri = HermitianEigenDecomposition::tridiagonal(&diag, &off).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let routes = eigen
    .eigenvalues()
    .iter()
    .zip(tri.eigenvalues())
    .fold(0.0_f64, |d, (p, q)| d.max((p - q).abs() / q));
// Compute M-orthogonality ΦᵀMΦ − I using two products.
let phi = eigen.eigenvectors();
let m_phi = mass_matrix.view().mul_mat(phi).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let gram = phi
    .view()
    .transpose()
    .mul_mat(&m_phi)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let orthogonality = gram
    .as_slice()
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(idx, &g)| (g - if idx / n == idx % n { 1.0 } else { 0.0 }).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let residual = eigen.residuals().iter().copied().fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);

// Uniform-chain analytical f_j = 2√(k/m) sin(jπ/(2(N+1)))/(2π) (reference using standard f64 functions).
let two_pi = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI;
let uniform = |j: usize| {
    let angle = j as f64 * std::f64::consts::PI / (2.0 * (n + 1) as f64);
    2.0 * (k / m).sqrt() * angle.sin() / two_pi
};
let rows: Vec<(usize, f64, f64, f64)> = [0, 1, 2, n - 3, n - 2, n - 1]
    .into_iter()
    .map(|idx| {
        let f = eigen.eigenvalues()[idx].sqrt() / two_pi;
        (idx + 1, f, uniform(idx + 1), f / uniform(idx + 1) - 1.0)
    })
    .collect();
let mode_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Mode j", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Frequency f [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": hz },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Uniform-chain analytical value [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": hz },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    mode_columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "modes" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum relative ω² difference between paths [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "max |ΦᵀMΦ − I| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum eigenpair residual indicator [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Band edge 2√(k/m)/(2π) [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": hz }
]);
let band_edge = 2.0 * (k / m).sqrt() / two_pi;
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![(routes, orthogonality, residual, band_edge)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
```

With the defaults $N = 31$, $k = 100$ N/m, $m = 1$ kg, and $r = 0.4$, the two paths' $\omega^2$ agree to relative difference $5.8 \times 10^{-15}$, while $M$-orthogonality error $1.6 \times 10^{-15}$ and residual indicator $7.0 \times 10^{-17}$ are at unit roundoff.
Even-numbered modes ($j = 2, 30$) have a central node where the impurity remains still, so their frequencies match the uniform chain.
Odd-numbered modes increase due to the impurity; the highest reaches 3.98 Hz, above the band edge 3.18 Hz.
This agrees with the infinite-chain theoretical value $\omega_L/(2\pi) = 3.979$ Hz in the next section.
With $r = 1$, all tabulated modes agree with analytical values to order $10^{-14}$.
For $r = 3$ (heavy impurity), odd modes decrease ($-5.9\%$ for the lowest), and even the highest stays below the band edge at 3.168 Hz.
The two paths' difference widens to $9.4 \times 10^{-14}$ at $r = 3$, still at most $10^{-13}$ in relative terms.
$k$ and $m$ merely scale frequencies by $\sqrt{k/m}$, leaving relative differences, $M$-orthogonality, and residual indicators unchanged.
The difference between paths varies slightly with scaling, reaching $1.2 \times 10^{-13}$ at $k = 10$ N/m.
With $N = 81$, differences outside the highest mode shrink (0.74% for the lowest), while the difference between paths widens to $4.3 \times 10^{-13}$.

## Localized mode caused by a light impurity

On an infinite chain with the impurity at the origin, set $q = e^{-1/\xi}$ and substitute a solution of the form $u_n \propto (-q)^{\lvert n \rvert}$ into the equations of motion.
The non-impurity equations give $m\omega^2/k = (1+q)^2/q$ and the impurity equation gives $r m \omega^2/k = 2(1+q)$; solving them together gives a solution with $0 < q < 1$ only for $r < 1$:

$$
\omega_L = \frac{2\sqrt{k/m}}{\sqrt{r(2-r)}}, \qquad e^{-1/\xi} = \frac{r}{2-r}
$$


$\omega_L$ exceeds band edge $2\sqrt{k/m}$, and the amplitude changes sign and decays by a factor $r/(2-r)$ at each neighboring particle.
As $r \to 1$, $\xi \to \infty$ and the mode joins the band edge; as $r \to 0$, only the impurity oscillates.

The next cell fixes $k = 1$ N/m and $m = 1$ kg, selecting particle count $N$ and mass ratio $r \in [0.1, 0.9]$.
It compares the highest angular frequency with $\omega_L$ and the log-amplitude slope for $\lvert \sqrt{m_i}\,\phi_i \rvert$ at particles 1–6 away from the impurity with $-1/\xi$.
Finite-chain results approach infinite-chain theory when localization length $\xi$ is much shorter than half the chain length.
The plot shows highest- and lowest-mode amplitudes versus position relative to the impurity.

```rust
//| id: linalg-localized-mode
//| ref: linalg-localized-mode
//| title: "Frequency and decay length of a light-impurity localized mode"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [linalg-chain-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg]
//| inputs:
//|   nodes: {"type":"range","label":"Particle count N","description":"Odd. The central particle is the impurity.","min":11,"max":81,"step":2,"value":41}
//|   ratio: {"type":"range","label":"Impurity mass ratio r","description":"A localized mode appears below 1.","min":0.1,"max":0.9,"step":0.05,"value":0.4}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"weak-impurity","label":"r = 0.9","description":"Localization length extends to 5 particles, slightly departing from infinite-chain theory.","inputs":{"ratio":0.9}}
//|   - {"id":"short-chain","label":"N = 11, r = 0.9","description":"Amplitude reaches both walls, making the slope depart substantially from theory.","inputs":{"nodes":11,"ratio":0.9}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"localized","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"localized","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":2.5,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"localized","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":0.7213,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: weak-impurity
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"localized","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":1e-7,"max":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"localized","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":-0.2007,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: short-chain
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"localized","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"localized","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":-0.3}}
use scioxide_linalg::{GeneralizedHermitianEigenDecomposition, HermitianInput};

// Set k = 1 N/m and m = 1 kg to express frequencies in units of √(k/m).
let n = nodes.round() as usize;
let c = n / 2;
let masses = masses(n, 1.0, ratio);
let (stiff, mass_matrix) = chain(1.0, &masses).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let eigen = GeneralizedHermitianEigenDecomposition::decompose(
    &stiff,
    &mass_matrix,
    HermitianInput::Exact,
    HermitianInput::Exact,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let top = eigen.eigenvalues()[n - 1].sqrt();
// Infinite-chain theoretical values (reference using standard f64 functions).
let omega_l = 2.0 / (ratio * (2.0 - ratio)).sqrt();
let decay = (ratio / (2.0 - ratio)).ln();
// Mass-weighted amplitude √m_i φ_i of mode j (φ is row-major with eigenvectors as columns).
let vectors = eigen.eigenvectors().as_slice();
let weighted = |j: usize| -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..n)
        .map(|i| masses[i].sqrt() * vectors[i * n + j])
        .collect()
};
let amplitude = weighted(n - 1);
// Mean log-amplitude slope 1–6 particles from the impurity (average of both sides).
let reach = 6.min(c);
let side_slope = |side: usize| {
    let at = |d: usize| {
        let index = if side == 0 { c - d } else { c + d };
        amplitude[index].abs().ln()
    };
    (at(reach) - at(1)) / (reach - 1) as f64
};
let slope = (side_slope(0) + side_slope(1)) / 2.0;
let fixed8 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 8 });
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Highest mode ω/√(k/m) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed8 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Infinite chain ω_L/√(k/m) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed8 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Log-amplitude slope [1/particle]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "−1/ξ = ln(r/(2−r)) [1/particle]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Localization length ξ [particles]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
let row = (
    top,
    omega_l,
    (top - omega_l).abs() / omega_l,
    slope,
    decay,
    -1.0 / decay,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "localized" })
);
let position = |values: Vec<f64>| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    values
        .into_iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(i, a)| [i as f64 - c as f64, a])
        .collect()
};
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Highest mode", "points": position(amplitude.clone()) }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Lowest mode", "points": position(weighted(0)) })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Position from impurity i − c [particles]",
        "yLabel": "Mass-weighted displacement √m_i φ_i [-]"
    })
);
```

With the defaults $N = 41$ and $r = 0.4$, localization length is short at $\xi = 0.72$ particles, and the highest angular frequency and log-amplitude slope agree with infinite-chain theory to the displayed digits.
The highest mode is sharply peaked at the impurity and decays with alternating signs, while the lowest extends across the whole chain.
At $r = 0.9$, localization length extends to 5 particles, relative angular-frequency difference grows to $4.4 \times 10^{-6}$, and slope $-0.2010$ is slightly steeper than theoretical $-0.2007$.
At the same $r$ with $N = 11$, the amplitude reaches both walls and slope $-0.42$ departs substantially from theory, showing finite-chain boundary effects.
At $r = 0.1$, $\omega_L$ rises to 2.3 times the band edge, confining vibration almost entirely to the impurity.

## Summary

Vibration problems with stiffness matrix $K$ and positive definite mass matrix $M$ can be solved by passing both matrices directly to `GeneralizedHermitianEigenDecomposition::decompose`.
Check results using $\Phi^{\mathsf T}M\Phi = I$ and `residuals()`; if $M$ is diagonal, compare with solving $M^{-1/2}KM^{-1/2}$ through `tridiagonal` to measure reduction error.
The two paths here differ by relative $5.8 \times 10^{-15}$ at defaults and $4.3 \times 10^{-13}$ at $N = 81$; for a uniform chain they also agree with the analytical dispersion relation.
A light impurity ($r < 1$) creates a localized mode outside the band, whose frequency and decay length are estimated by infinite-chain theory $\omega_L = 2\sqrt{k/m}/\sqrt{r(2-r)}$ and $e^{-1/\xi} = r/(2-r)$.
For a finite chain, this estimate applies when localization length is much shorter than half the chain length.
