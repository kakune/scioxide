Autoregressive (AR) models, which predict the current value from a linear combination of past values, are widely used for time-dependent data such as temperature, stock prices, and speech.
AR coefficients are determined by symmetric Toeplitz equations formed from autocorrelations (Yule–Walker equations).
Levinson–Durbin recursion exploits Toeplitz structure to solve in $O(P^2)$ operations, also producing all lower-order solutions along the way.
This example checks coefficient recovery from theoretical autocorrelations within rounding error and compares simulated-data coefficient errors with asymptotic standard errors.
Time is counted by sample index, in units of time points.

## AR(2) processes and autocorrelation

An AR(2) process is the time series defined using independent noise $\varepsilon_t$ with mean 0 and variance $\sigma^2$ by

$$
X_t = \phi_1 X_{t-1} + \phi_2 X_{t-2} + \varepsilon_t
$$


The process is stationary if roots of characteristic equation $z^2 - \phi_1 z - \phi_2 = 0$ lie inside the unit circle; here take roots $r e^{\pm \mathrm{i}\theta}$ ($0 < r < 1$), setting $\phi_1 = 2r\cos\theta$ and $\phi_2 = -r^2$.
Correlation persists longer as $r$ approaches 1, while $\theta$ represents quasiperiodic oscillations with period $2\pi/\theta$ time points.

Multiplying both sides by $X_{t-k}$ and taking expectations shows that autocorrelation $\rho(k) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_t, X_{t-k}) / \operatorname{Var}(X_t)$ satisfies

$$
\rho(0) = 1, \qquad \rho(1) = \frac{\phi_1}{1 - \phi_2}, \qquad \rho(k) = \phi_1 \rho(k-1) + \phi_2 \rho(k-2) \quad (k \ge 2)
$$


Substituting $\rho(1)$ and $\rho(2)$ into $\gamma(0) = \phi_1\gamma(1) + \phi_2\gamma(2) + \sigma^2$ ($\gamma(k) = \operatorname{Cov}(X_t, X_{t-k})$), obtained by multiplying both sides by $X_t$ and taking expectations, gives the ratio of noise variance to process variance $\gamma(0) = \operatorname{Var}(X_t)$:

$$
\frac{\sigma^2}{\gamma(0)} = \frac{(1+\phi_2)\bigl((1-\phi_2)^2 - \phi_1^2\bigr)}{1-\phi_2}
$$


## Yule–Walker equations and partial autocorrelation

Choosing coefficients of the order-$P$ AR model $X_t = \sum_{j=1}^{P} a_j X_{t-j} + e_t$ to minimize prediction-error variance gives the Yule–Walker equations

$$
R_P\, a = (\rho(1), \dots, \rho(P))^{\mathsf T}, \qquad (R_P)_{ij} = \rho(\lvert i - j \rvert)
$$


$R_P$ is symmetric positive definite Toeplitz; the last coefficient $a_P^{(P)}$ is called the partial autocorrelation $\alpha(P)$.
The normalized prediction-error variance is $E_P = 1 - \sum_{j=1}^{P} a_j \rho(j)$.
If the true process is AR(2), the solution for $P \ge 2$ is $(\phi_1, \phi_2, 0, \dots, 0)$, and partial autocorrelation is $\alpha(1) = \rho(1)$, $\alpha(2) = \phi_2$, and zero for $P \ge 3$ (cutoff).
Then $E_P$ equals the above $\sigma^2/\gamma(0)$ for $P \ge 2$.

Levinson–Durbin recursion computes the reflection coefficient from the order-$k-1$ solution as

$$
\kappa_k = \frac{\rho(k) - \sum_{j=1}^{k-1} a_j^{(k-1)} \rho(k-j)}{E_{k-1}}, \qquad E_k = E_{k-1}(1 - \kappa_k^2), \quad E_0 = 1
$$

It then updates $a^{(k)}_j = a^{(k-1)}_j - \kappa_k a^{(k-1)}_{k-j}$ and $a^{(k)}_k = \kappa_k$.
The reflection coefficient is the partial autocorrelation itself, and $E_k$ is the normalized prediction-error variance above.
Each order increment takes $O(k)$ operations, giving total $O(P^2)$ through order $P$, below dense LU's $O(P^3)$.

Solve by passing structured matrix description `StructuredMatrixView::toeplitz(&column, &row)` to the [Plan](oxref:linalg-execution-forms) `StructuredSolvePlan` ([Toeplitz, Hankel, and circulant matrices](oxref:linalg-toeplitz-systems)).
The Plan uses safeguarded Levinson–Durbin for symmetric Toeplitz matrices.
Columns whose prediction error falls to at most $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ relative to the largest coefficient, or whose recursion produces nonfinite values or a rejected solution, are solved again by pivoted GKO elimination (Gohberg–Kailath–Olshevsky).
Both take $O(P^2)$ operations per right-hand side and do not require positive definite $R_P$.
Acceptance uses the rowwise backward error

$$
\eta = \max_i \frac{\lvert b - A x \rvert_i}{\lvert b_i \rvert + \lVert x \rVert_\infty \sum_j \lvert a_{ij} \rvert}
$$

For tolerances $(\mathrm{atol}, \mathrm{rtol})$ in `StructuredSolveOptions`, solutions satisfying $\eta \le \mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}$ are accepted.
The defaults are $\mathrm{atol} = \mathrm{rtol} = \sqrt{\varepsilon} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-8}$, giving acceptance condition $\eta \le 2\sqrt{\varepsilon} \approx 3.0 \times 10^{-8}$.
The Plan offers only the [destination-taking API](oxref:linalg-execution-forms), accepting output, workspace, and execution context: use `solve_into(operation, b, output, workspace, context)` for one right-hand side and `solve_matrix_into` for multiple right-hand sides.
In report `StructuredSolveReport`, `termination()` returns `Accepted` or `ResidualRejected`, `backward_error()` returns $\eta$, and `levinson_solves()` and `gko_solves()` return the number of columns accepted by each method.
A rejected solution still returns `Ok` with `ResidualRejected`, leaving output unwritten, so the cell checks the termination reason.
Dense LU fallback is used only when workspace was preallocated with `StructuredWorkspaceRequest::with_dense_fallback`; this page does not use it.
If no report is needed, the [one-shot function](oxref:linalg-execution-forms) `solve_structured(matrix, &b)` constructs and solves with the same Plan at defaults, returning rejected solutions as `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`) errors.

The following AR(2) model definitions are shared by the three cells on this page.
`ar2_coefficients` returns $(\phi_1, \phi_2)$ from roots $re^{\pm\mathrm{i}\theta}$; `autocorrelation` returns $\rho(0), \dots, \rho(P_{\max})$ using the recurrence above; and `variance_ratio` returns $\sigma^2/\gamma(0)$.

```rust
//| id: linalg-yule-walker-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "AR(2) coefficients, autocorrelations, and prediction-error variance"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

/// AR(2) coefficients (φ₁, φ₂) = (2r cos θ, −r²) for roots r e^{±iθ}. θ is in degrees.
pub(crate) fn ar2_coefficients(radius: f64, angle_degrees: f64) -> (f64, f64) {
    let cosine = RealScalar::cos(angle_degrees.to_radians());
    (2.0 * radius * cosine, -radius * radius)
}

/// Theoretical autocorrelations ρ(0), …, ρ(p_max). ρ(1) = φ₁/(1 − φ₂), ρ(k) = φ₁ρ(k−1) + φ₂ρ(k−2).
pub(crate) fn autocorrelation(phi1: f64, phi2: f64, p_max: usize) -> Vec<f64> {
    let mut rho = vec![1.0, phi1 / (1.0 - phi2)];
    for k in 2..=p_max {
        rho.push(phi1 * rho[k - 1] + phi2 * rho[k - 2]);
    }
    rho
}

/// Normalized prediction-error variance σ²/γ(0) = (1 + φ₂)((1 − φ₂)² − φ₁²)/(1 − φ₂).
pub(crate) fn variance_ratio(phi1: f64, phi2: f64) -> f64 {
    (1.0 + phi2) * ((1.0 - phi2).powi(2) - phi1 * phi1) / (1.0 - phi2)
}
```

## Condition numbers and spectral density

The process has spectral density $S(\omega) \propto 1/\lvert A(e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega}) \rvert^2$ with $A(z) = 1 - \phi_1 z - \phi_2 z^2$.
Eigenvalues of $R_P$ lie between the minimum and maximum of appropriately scaled $S$ (a Toeplitz matrix property established by Grenander and Szegő).
Thus $\kappa_2(R_P) \le \max S / \min S$ regardless of order; for $r \to 1$, a sharp peak near $\omega = \theta$ makes the bound large.

The next cell receives $(\phi_1, \phi_2)$ and $P_{\max}$, materializes $R_P$ for $P = 1, \dots, P_{\max}$ as a dense matrix with `materialize()`, and computes $\kappa_2(R_P)$ from the singular value ratio returned by `SvdValues::compute`.
Bound $\max S / \min S$ is computed by evaluating $\lvert A(e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega}) \rvert^2$ at 4001 points in $[0, \pi]$ (using inherent `f64` methods as a checking reference).
The table shows $\kappa_2$, the bound, and their ratio at $P_{\max}$; the plot shows $\kappa_2(R_P)$ on a logarithmic axis with the bound as a horizontal line.

```rust
    let (s1, c1) = w.sin_cos();
```

With defaults, $\kappa_2(R_{12}) = 40$, or 0.66 times the spectral-density bound 61.
The plotted $\kappa_2(R_P)$ increases with order toward the bound without exceeding it.
$R_P$ is the leading principal submatrix of $R_{P+1}$, so Cauchy's eigenvalue interlacing theorem means increasing order cannot decrease the largest eigenvalue or increase the smallest.
Therefore $\kappa_2(R_P)$ increases monotonically with $P$.
Extreme eigenvalues of Toeplitz matrices approach the minimum and maximum of $S$ as $P \to \infty$, so the bound is also the limiting condition number at large order.

With $r = 0.99$, $\theta = 5^\circ$, and $P_{\max} = 60$, the bound is $5.2 \times 10^{6}$ and $\kappa_2(R_{60}) = 1.5 \times 10^{6}$.
At $\theta = 0^\circ$, the bound is $1.6 \times 10^{9}$ and $\kappa_2(R_{60}) = 2.3 \times 10^{8}$.
The previous cell's coefficient differences $1.2 \times 10^{-11}$ and $9.4 \times 10^{-10}$ lie below guides $3.5 \times 10^{-11}$ and $8.3 \times 10^{-9}$ obtained by multiplying maximum backward error by $\kappa_2$.
Forward error of a backward-stable solution increases with conditioning and can be estimated at the scale of backward error times condition number.

## Estimation from simulated data

For actual data, estimate autocorrelation from the sample.
For a series $x_1, \dots, x_T$ of length $T$, the sample autocorrelation is

$$
\hat\rho(k) = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T-k} (x_t - \bar x)(x_{t+k} - \bar x)}{\sum_{t=1}^{T} (x_t - \bar x)^2}
$$

This estimator uses a common denominator based on $T$; its Toeplitz matrix is always positive semidefinite, keeping Levinson–Durbin reflection coefficients within $\lvert\kappa_k\rvert \le 1$.
For large $T$, both AR(2) Yule–Walker estimates $(\hat\phi_1, \hat\phi_2)$ have standard error $\sqrt{(1 - \phi_2^2)/T}$, and estimated partial autocorrelations of orders $P \ge 3$ fluctuate around 0 with approximate standard deviation $1/\sqrt{T}$.
Thus, if the true process is AR(2), about 5% of orders on average exceed the band $\pm 1.96/\sqrt{T}$.

```rust
//| uses: [linalg-yule-walker-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   radius: {"type":"range","label":"Root magnitude r [-]","description":"Closer to 1 gives longer-lasting correlation.","min":0.1,"max":0.99,"step":0.01,"value":0.8}
//|   angle: {"type":"range","label":"Root argument θ [degrees]","description":"Quasiperiod is 360/θ time points.","min":0,"max":180,"step":5,"value":60}
//|   length: {"type":"range","label":"Series length T [time points]","description":"Number of time points used for sample autocorrelations.","min":100,"max":20000,"step":100,"value":2000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","description":"Change the noise sequence.","min":1,"max":50,"step":1,"value":7}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"long-series","label":"T = 20000","description":"Longer series reduce coefficient differences in proportion to 1/√T.","inputs":{"length":20000}}
//|   - {"id":"near-unit-root","label":"r = 0.99, θ = 5°, T = 20000","description":"Near-unit-circle roots make Yule–Walker bias greatly exceed standard error.","inputs":{"radius":0.99,"angle":5,"length":20000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":-3.0,"max":3.0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"min":-3.0,"max":3.0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.4499121951219512,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|   - case: long-series
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.0054,"rtol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":-3.0,"max":3.0}}
//|   - case: near-unit-root
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":-5.0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"min":5.0}}
use scioxide_linalg::{StructuredMatrixView, solve_structured};
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let (phi1, phi2) = ar2_coefficients(radius, angle);
let t_len = length.round() as usize;
let normal = Normal::new(0.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
// X_t = φ₁X_{t−1} + φ₂X_{t−2} + ε_t. Discard the first 500 time points to remove initialization effects.
let (mut x, mut previous) = (Vec::with_capacity(t_len), [0.0_f64; 2]);
for t in 0..t_len + 500 {
    let noise = normal.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let next = phi1 * previous[0] + phi2 * previous[1] + noise;
    previous = [next, previous[0]];
    if t >= 500 {
        x.push(next);
    }
}
let mean = x.iter().sum::<f64>() / t_len as f64;
let p_max = 12_usize;
// Sample autocorrelation ρ̂(k). The denominator Σ (x_t − x̄)² is independent of k.
let lagged = |k: usize| -> f64 {
    (0..t_len - k)
        .map(|t| (x[t] - mean) * (x[t + k] - mean))
        .sum()
};
let gamma: Vec<f64> = (0..=p_max).map(lagged).collect();
let r: Vec<f64> = gamma.iter().map(|g| g / gamma[0]).collect();
// Independent path: directly implement Levinson–Durbin recursion for reflection coefficients κ_k and E_k.
let (mut a, mut e, mut reflection) = (Vec::<f64>::new(), 1.0_f64, Vec::new());
for k in 1..=p_max {
    let kappa = (r[k] - (0..k - 1).map(|j| a[j] * r[k - 1 - j]).sum::<f64>()) / e;
    let previous = a.clone();
    for j in 0..k - 1 {
        a[j] = previous[j] - kappa * previous[k - 2 - j];
    }
    a.push(kappa);
    e *= 1.0 - kappa * kappa;
    reflection.push(kappa);
}
// Main path: solve R̂_P a = (ρ̂(1), …, ρ̂(P)) at each order using the one-shot function.
let (mut pacf, mut route_difference, mut phi_hat) = (Vec::new(), 0.0_f64, [0.0_f64; 2]);
for p in 1..=p_max {
    let matrix = StructuredMatrixView::toeplitz(&r[..p], &r[..p]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let coefficients = solve_structured(matrix, &r[1..=p]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let c = coefficients.as_slice();
    if p == 2 {
        phi_hat = [c[0], c[1]];
    }
    route_difference = route_difference.max((c[p - 1] - reflection[p - 1]).abs());
    pacf.push([p as f64, c[p - 1]]);
}
let standard_error = ((1.0 - phi2 * phi2) / t_len as f64).sqrt();
let band = 1.96 / (t_len as f64).sqrt();
let outside = pacf[2..].iter().filter(|p| p[1].abs() > band).count();
let estimate_row = |name, estimate: f64, truth: f64| {
    let z = (estimate - truth) / standard_error;
    (name, estimate, truth, estimate - truth, standard_error, z)
};
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let estimate_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Coefficients" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Estimate [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "True value [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Standard error √((1−φ₂²)/T) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Difference / standard error [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    estimate_columns,
    vec![
        estimate_row("φ₁", phi_hat[0], phi1),
        estimate_row("φ₂", phi_hat[1], phi2)
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "estimates" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum difference in α(P) between paths [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Orders outside band for P ≥ 3 [orders]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Orders P ≥ 3 [orders]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "E_12 (estimate) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "σ²/γ(0) (theory) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![(
        route_difference,
        outside,
        p_max - 2,
        e,
        variance_ratio(phi1, phi2)
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Estimated α(P)", "points": pacf })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Order P [-]",
        "yLabel": "α(P) [-]",
        "yMin": -1.0,
        "yMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "range", "axis": "y", "from": -band, "to": band, "label": "±1.96/√T" }
        ]
    })
);
```

With default $T = 2000$ time points and seed 7, $(\hat\phi_1, \hat\phi_2) = (0.8233, -0.6414)$, with true-value differences of 1.36 and 0.08 times standard error 0.0172.
Partial autocorrelations from the two paths agree to difference $6 \times 10^{-17}$.
Of 10 estimated orders from 3 upward, one exceeds band $\pm 1.96/\sqrt{T}$, a count consistent with the 5% level.
The estimate of $E_{12}$ is 0.438, close to theoretical 0.450.
Increasing $T$ through 100, 1000, and 20000 time points decreases standard errors through 0.077, 0.024, and 0.0054 in proportion to $1/\sqrt{T}$, and differences in $\hat\phi_1$ also shrink through 0.050, 0.033, and 0.0039.
Changing the seed moves estimates by about a standard error; seeds 1 and 2 give $\hat\phi_1$ differences of 0.68 and −1.20 standard errors.

With $r = 0.99$ and $\theta = 5^\circ$, even $T = 20000$ time points gives coefficient differences 15 times standard error 0.0014 and 3 of 10 orders outside the band.
The estimate $6.1 \times 10^{-4}$ of $E_{12}$ is also twice theoretical $3.0 \times 10^{-4}$.
Using a common $T$ denominator in sample autocorrelation systematically reduces large-lag values for persistent processes, and ill-conditioned $R_P$ amplifies this bias.
The difference between paths increases to $1.3 \times 10^{-12}$ but remains much smaller than the bias, showing that the bias comes from the estimator rather than the solver.
For processes with roots near the unit circle, conditional least squares and Burg's method are known to have less bias than Yule–Walker estimation (Tjøstheim and Paulsen, 1983).
SciOxide does not provide Burg's method for AR estimation.
Conditional least squares is obtained by solving with one-shot `least_squares` using rows of lagged values $(x_{t-1}, x_{t-2})$ and a column of $x_t$.

## Summary

AR coefficients are determined by symmetric positive definite Toeplitz autocorrelation equations, solved by `StructuredSolvePlan` using Levinson–Durbin recursion in $O(P^2)$ operations.
Use the Plan's `solve_into` for termination reasons and backward errors, or one-shot `solve_structured` for just the solution.
Theoretical autocorrelations recover AR(2) coefficients within rounding error, partial autocorrelation vanishes from order 3, and prediction-error variance $E_P$ agrees with $\sigma^2/\gamma(0)$.
The condition number of $R_P$ is bounded independently of order by the ratio of maximum to minimum spectral density; roots near the unit circle make it large, increasing coefficient forward error despite small backward error.
Coefficients estimated from sample autocorrelations fluctuate on the standard-error scale $\sqrt{(1-\phi_2^2)/T}$; for roots near the unit circle, bias greatly exceeds standard error, so compute conditional least squares with `least_squares` for comparison.
