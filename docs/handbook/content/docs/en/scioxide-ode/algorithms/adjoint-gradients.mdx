Parameter identification and optimal control require gradients $\partial J/\partial y_0$ and $\partial J/\partial p$ of scalar objective $J(y_0, p)$ depending on ODE solution $y(t; y_0, p)$.
[Forward sensitivity](../forward-sensitivities-and-ensembles/) integrates $\partial y/\partial p$ alongside state, adding state-sized equations for each extra parameter.
Adjoints add one backward integration after the forward integration, computing the full gradient at cost independent of parameter count.
The backward integration instead needs the forward trajectory in reverse order.

The crate `scioxide-ode` provides ODE `OdeAdjointSolver` (forward Dormand–Prince 5(4)) and residual-form index-1 DAE `DaeAdjointSolver` (forward BDF, backward Euler, or implicit trapezoidal).
Both record forward accepted steps and traverse them backward, recomputing portions to fit memory limits.

## Continuous adjoint equations

Let the objective over $[t_0, T]$ be

$$
J(y_0, p) = \Phi\bigl(T, y(T), p\bigr) + \int_{t_0}^{T} L\bigl(t, y(t), p\bigr)\,dt + \sum_k G_k\bigl(y(t_k), p\bigr)
$$


Here $\Phi$ is the terminal term, $L$ the integral term, and $G_k$ the term at observation time $t_k$.
Integrating a Lagrangian constrained by $y' = f(t, y, p)$ by parts in time gives the adjoint variable (costate) $\lambda(t)$ equations

$$
\lambda' = -\Bigl(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\Bigr)^{\mathsf T}\lambda - \Bigl(\frac{\partial L}{\partial y}\Bigr)^{\mathsf T}, \qquad \lambda(T) = \Bigl(\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial y}\Bigr)^{\mathsf T}
$$

to solve backward from $T$ to $t_0$.
Observation terms add jumps to $\lambda$ at times $t_k$.
The gradients are

$$
\frac{\partial J}{\partial y_0} = \lambda(t_0), \qquad \frac{\partial J}{\partial p} = \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial p} + \int_{t_0}^{T} \Bigl(\frac{\partial L}{\partial p} + \lambda^{\mathsf T}\frac{\partial f}{\partial p}\Bigr)\,dt
$$


Only Jacobian–adjoint products $(\partial f/\partial y)^{\mathsf T}w$ and $(\partial f/\partial p)^{\mathsf T}w$ (pullbacks) are needed, without forming full Jacobians.

The system implements `OdeAdjointSystem` (extending `OdeSystem` with `parameters`, `is_replayable`, and `pullback`); `pullback(t, y, w, …)` writes both products.
Products may use dense or sparse matrices, matrix-free actions, or scioxide-ad reverse-mode automatic differentiation.
Objectives implement `AdjointObjective`; `evaluate(part, t, y, p, …)` returns value `AdjointObjectiveValue { value, time_derivative }` for terminal (`AdjointObjectivePart::Terminal`) or integral (`Running`) terms and writes state and parameter gradients.
Observation terms use `observation_times` and `observation`, available only under `AdjointMode::Discrete` without terminal events.
States may be complex; state gradients are then cotangents $\bar y$ satisfying $dJ = \operatorname{Re}(\bar{y}^{\mathsf T}\,dy)$.
Time and parameters are real.

## Discrete and continuous adjoints

The setting `AdjointMode` selects what is differentiated.

| `AdjointMode` | Quantity computed | Error properties |
|---|---|---|
| `Discrete` | Fix the forward accepted-step sequence and apply transposed step-map derivatives in reverse order | Exact derivative of computed $J$ at fixed steps, apart from rounding. Excludes changes in step selection with $p$ |
| `Continuous` | Integrate the adjoint equations backward along forward dense output | Estimate depending on adjoint and integral-term local tolerances (`with_adjoint_error`, `with_quadrature_error`) and forward tolerances |

Both differ from true $J$ gradients by forward discretization error.
Discrete adjoints give gradients consistent with the computed objective, suiting optimization that combines objective values and gradients.
Continuous adjoints independently select backward steps, integrating within each recorded forward step using Dormand–Prince 5(4).
Gradient differences are roughly of tolerance magnitude and do not prove either is correct.

## Checkpoints and recomputation

Backward integration needs forward states in reverse order.
Storing all accepted states uses memory proportional to step count.
Storing only some states (checkpoints) and recomputing between them reduces memory at the cost of extra forward steps.
Choose storage through `CheckpointPolicy`.

| `CheckpointPolicy` | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `StoreAll` (default) | Store restart states at all accepted steps, without recomputation |
| `MemoryBudget` | Store all steps if total workspace fits `with_max_workspace_bytes`; otherwise choose a binomial schedule (Griewank 1992) with the most snapshots (up to 64) fitting both workspace and `with_max_replay_steps` recomputation limits |
| `Explicit(schedule)` | Use scioxide-ad's `CheckpointSchedule`; it must cover recorded-step capacity `with_tape_capacity` and fit recomputation within `with_max_replay_steps` |

[scioxide-ad checkpoints](../../../scioxide-ad/algorithms/checkpointing/) covers binomial-schedule theory.
Recomputation must reproduce stored states correctly; systems declare identical outputs for identical inputs through `is_replayable`.
Systems returning `false` give `InvalidInput` during workspace preparation.
Recomputed-state differences are checked against `with_replay_error` tolerances (default: no difference allowed); exceeding them fails with `ReplayMismatch`.
Changing system parameters during computation also gives `ReplayMismatch`.
The recomputation limit `with_max_replay_steps` is validated at Plan construction against recomputation for full recorded capacity, so it never stops execution at runtime.

## Plans, problems, and workspaces

Settings have two levels: shared ODE/DAE `AdjointOptions` and family-specific settings.
The constructor `AdjointOptions::new(mode, initial_step)` accepts adjoint method and initial-step magnitude.
Defaults are adaptive steps, forward/adjoint/integral local tolerances $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ (absolute and relative, maximum norm), exact recomputation agreement, `StoreAll`, recorded-step capacity 1024, and no other limits.
The setting `with_fixed_step(true)` disables error control, fixing all forward steps to the initial size.
Resource limits are:

| Setting | Quantity limited | Termination on reaching the limit |
|---|---|---|
| `with_tape_capacity` | Recorded accepted forward steps (default 1024); workspace is estimated for this count | `BudgetLimit(Iteration)` |
| `with_max_steps` | Attempted integration steps (forward plus continuous-adjoint backward steps) | `BudgetLimit(Proposal)` |
| `with_max_replay_steps` | Recomputed forward steps | Validated at Plan construction; never stops runtime execution |
| `with_max_reverse_steps` | Backward steps (one per recorded step for discrete adjoints; one per attempted substep for continuous adjoints) | `BudgetLimit(Iteration)` |
| `with_max_workspace_bytes` | Numerical workspace bytes (including linear-solver memory, excluding callback-owned memory) | `InvalidInput` or `DimensionMismatch` at Plan or workspace preparation |
| `with_max_evaluations` | Callback evaluations (right-hand side or residual, pullback, objective, events, linear solvers) | `BudgetLimit(Evaluation)` |

ODE settings `OdeAdjointOptions::new(common)` add forward Dormand–Prince 5(4) tolerances (`with_primal_error`), terminal-event search and localization (`with_event_options`), and continuous-adjoint interpolation-defect measurements (`with_interpolation_defect_sampling`).
DAE settings `DaeAdjointOptions::new(common, numerics)` add numerical settings shared with `DaeBdf` (`DaeNumericalOptions`) and the relative residual for backward transposed linear systems (`with_linear_residual`, default $64\varepsilon$).
Adjoint tolerances and interpolation-defect measurements apply only to continuous adjoints; supplying them for discrete adjoints gives `InvalidInput` at Plan construction.

The Plan `OdeAdjointSolver::new(dimension, parameter_dimension, options)` validates only dimensions and settings, without borrowing callbacks.
The problem `OdeAdjointProblem::new(&system, &objective, &initial, (t0, t1))` borrows the system, objective, initial values, interval, and optional terminal event.
The condition `t1 < t0` requests backward time integration.
The method `workspace_requirements(&problem)` validates system reproducibility, mass-matrix presence (`OdeAdjointSolver` rejects them), dimensions, and observation times, estimating workspace including callback memory, observation times, and checkpoints.
The returned `OdeAdjointRequirements` holds gradient lengths (`initial_gradient_len`, `parameter_gradient_len`) and memory bound `required_bytes()`.
The method `workspace(&problem)` allocates matching `OdeAdjointWorkspace`, returning an error before system or objective evaluation if limits are exceeded.
Existing workspace can also be checked or grown through `OdeAdjointWorkspace::check` and `try_reserve`.

There are two computation APIs.

- `solve_into(&problem, &mut initial_gradient, &mut parameter_gradient, &mut workspace, scope)` writes both gradients only on success, returning `AdjointReport`.
  Failed-attempt reports remain in workspace `last_attempt()`.
  With prepared workspace and nonallocating callbacks, no allocation occurs even with terminal events.
- `solve(&problem)` allocates new workspace and gradient storage, returning `AdjointResult` (`initial_gradient()`, `parameter_gradient()`, `report()`, `into_parts()`).
  Failure returns `PartialFailure` carrying the failed attempt's `AdjointReport`.

Neither returns partial gradients on failure.

### Oral-dosing model AUC gradients

In a one-compartment pharmacokinetic model, let gastrointestinal amount $A_g$ and body amount $A_c$ [mg] satisfy

$$
A_g' = -k_a A_g, \qquad A_c' = k_a A_g - k_e A_c, \qquad A_g(0) = D,\ A_c(0) = 0
$$


With absorption constant $k_a = 1\ \mathrm{h^{-1}}$, elimination constant $k_e = 0.2\ \mathrm{h^{-1}}$, and dose $D = 100$ mg, compute gradients of 24-hour body-amount integral (AUC) $J = \int_0^{24} A_c\,dt$ [mg·h].
Analytical solution $A_c = D k_a (e^{-k_e t} - e^{-k_a t})/(k_a - k_e)$ gives closed forms for $J$ and four partial derivatives.
Use them for independent verification.

The following block defines helpers shared by two cells.
The type `OralDose` supplies the system and pullback, `Auc` the integral term $L = A_c$, `auc_closed_form` closed-form $J$ and gradients, and `worst_relative` the maximum relative error against the closed forms across four partial derivatives.

```rust
//| id: ode-adjoint-oral-dose-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Oral-dosing system, pullback, AUC, and closed forms"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SolveContext};
use scioxide_ode::{
    AdjointObjective, AdjointObjectivePart, AdjointObjectiveValue, OdeAdjointSystem, OdeSystem,
};

/// One-compartment model with state y = (A_g, A_c) [mg], parameters p = (k_a, k_e) [1/h].
pub(crate) struct OralDose(pub(crate) [f64; 2]);
impl OdeSystem<f64> for OralDose {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn rhs(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let [ka, ke] = self.0;
        out[0] = -ka * y[0];
        out[1] = ka * y[0] - ke * y[1];
        Ok(())
    }
}
impl OdeAdjointSystem<f64> for OralDose {
    fn parameters(&self) -> &[f64] {
        &self.0
    }
    fn is_replayable(&self) -> bool {
        true
    }
    /// Write (∂f/∂y)ᵀw and (∂f/∂p)ᵀw for seed w.
    fn pullback(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        w: &[f64],
        dy: &mut [f64],
        dp: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let [ka, ke] = self.0;
        dy.copy_from_slice(&[ka * (w[1] - w[0]), -ke * w[1]]);
        dp.copy_from_slice(&[y[0] * (w[1] - w[0]), -y[1] * w[1]]);
        Ok(())
    }
}
/// J = ∫ A_c dt. Integral term L = A_c has state gradient (0, 1); terminal term is zero.
pub(crate) struct Auc;
impl AdjointObjective<f64> for Auc {
    fn evaluate(
        &self,
        part: AdjointObjectivePart,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        _: &[f64],
        dy: &mut [f64],
        dp: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<AdjointObjectiveValue<f64>, SciOxideError> {
        let running = if part == AdjointObjectivePart::Running {
            1.0
        } else {
            0.0
        };
        dy.copy_from_slice(&[0.0, running]);
        dp.fill(0.0);
        Ok(AdjointObjectiveValue {
            value: running * y[1],
            time_derivative: 0.0,
        })
    }
}
/// Closed forms (J, ∂J/∂A_g(0), ∂J/∂A_c(0), ∂J/∂k_a, ∂J/∂k_e). Exponentials use f64 references.
pub(crate) fn auc_closed_form(dose: f64, ka: f64, ke: f64, horizon: f64) -> [f64; 5] {
    let (ea, ee) = ((-ka * horizon).exp(), (-ke * horizon).exp());
    let (c, q) = (dose * ka / (ka - ke), (1.0 - ee) / ke - (1.0 - ea) / ka);
    let dq_dka = ((1.0 - ea) - ka * horizon * ea) / (ka * ka);
    let dq_dke = (ke * horizon * ee - (1.0 - ee)) / (ke * ke);
    let scale = dose / (ka - ke).powi(2);
    [
        c * q,
        q * ka / (ka - ke),
        (1.0 - ee) / ke,
        -scale * ke * q + c * dq_dka,
        scale * ka * q + c * dq_dke,
    ]
}
/// Maximum relative error across four closed-form partial derivatives.
pub(crate) fn worst_relative(gradient: [f64; 4], exact: [f64; 5]) -> f64 {
    let pairs = gradient.iter().zip(&exact[1..]);
    pairs.fold(0.0_f64, |m, (a, b)| m.max(((a - b) / b).abs()))
}
```

The next cell solves this model with discrete and continuous `OdeAdjointSolver::solve` adjoints, comparing with closed forms.
Forward, adjoint, and integral tolerances share value $10^q$; checkpoints use `MemoryBudget` with an input memory bound.
“Maximum relative error” is the maximum of the relative errors in $\partial J/\partial A_g(0)$, $\partial J/\partial A_c(0)$, $\partial J/\partial k_a$, and $\partial J/\partial k_e$; “Workspace bound” is `OdeAdjointRequirements::required_bytes()`.

## Termination statuses and diagnostics

The enum `AdjointTermination` is the authoritative termination status.
Gradients are written only on success, so successful `AdjointReport` always has status `ReachedEnd` or `TerminalEvent`.
Other variants classify failed-attempt reports (workspace `last_attempt()`, `PartialFailure` of `solve`).

| Termination status | Meaning | Error returned |
|---|---|---|
| `ReachedEnd`, `TerminalEvent` | Reached interval end or terminal event and computed both gradients | None |
| `BudgetLimit(kind)` | Reached a limit in the table above. Outer-solve evaluation limits also give `Evaluation` | `ConvergenceFailure` |
| `StepSizeLimit` | No representable step advances time | `ConvergenceFailure` |
| `ExecutionStopped(reason)` | Stopped by cancellation or deadline | `ExecutionStopped` |
| `ReplayMismatch` | Recomputation failed to reproduce stored states | `ConvergenceFailure` |
| `EventDerivativeUnavailable` | Terminal-event gradient unavailable | `InvalidInput` |

The method `AdjointReport::termination()` returns `Option`, or `None` for failures outside these classifications, such as callback errors.
The method `is_success()` is true only when gradients were computed.
The value `objective()` is the objective after forward integration; `final_time()` is the last accepted forward time.
The fields `accepted_steps()` and `rejected_steps()` count only initial forward steps; recomputed and backward steps use `replayed_steps()` and `reverse_steps()` separately.
The field `reverse_steps()` counts recorded steps fully processed backward, counting one even when continuous adjoints use multiple substeps within a recorded step.
The field `workspace_bytes()` gives the numerical-memory bound; `counts()` and `accounting()` count evaluations.

The `AdjointErrorReport` returned by `errors()` records the following quantities separately.

- `primal()`: maximum normalized local error estimate over accepted forward steps, for methods providing estimates
- `adjoint()`: maximum normalized local error estimate over accepted continuous-adjoint steps
- `quadrature()`: maximum normalized local error estimate for integral-term and parameter-gradient quadrature
- `replay()`: maximum difference between recomputed and stored states
- `interpolation_defect()`: maximum sampled defect $|u'(t) - f(t, u(t))|$ of forward dense output $u$ (only under `with_interpolation_defect_sampling(true)`; one extra right-hand-side evaluation per sample)
- `linear_residual()`: maximum relative residual of backward transposed linear systems (DAE adjoints)

None bounds global gradient error.

For objectives stopping at terminal events, supply `AdjointTerminalEvent` through `OdeAdjointProblem::with_terminal_event(Some(&event))`.
The first root of event function $g(t, y, p)$ becomes terminal time $T$; gradients include dependence of $T$ on $y_0$ and $p$.
The caller declares a unique root in the interval (true `unique_branch`) and a lower bound (`transversality_threshold`) for total-derivative magnitude $|dg/dt| = |\partial g/\partial t + (\partial g/\partial y) f|$.
Finite-point root finding cannot prove uniqueness, so this declaration is the caller's responsibility.
An initial-time root, false `unique_branch`, or derivative magnitude at most the bound leaves gradients nonexistent, giving `EventDerivativeUnavailable`.
Event search and localization default to initial value problem settings, configurable through `OdeAdjointOptions::with_event_options`.
Events have no state updates (reset maps).

## Implicit forward methods and DAE adjoints

`DaeAdjointSolver` computes gradients for residual-form index-1 DAE $F(t, y, y', p) = 0$ ([Index-1 DAEs](../dae-index-one/)).
Initial-value gradients contain only differential coordinates; initial algebraic values follow from constraints and are not independent variables.
Systems implement `DaeAdjointSystem`, with `pullback` writing $(\partial F/\partial y)^{\mathsf H}w$, $(\partial F/\partial y')^{\mathsf H}w$, and $(\partial F/\partial p)^{\mathsf T}w$.
DAE adjoints have no terminal events.
One solve is represented by `DaeAdjointProblem::new(system, objective, initial, derivative, interval)`, borrowing the initial state/derivative pair and interval.
Select the forward method through constructors.

| Constructor | Forward method | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| `DaeAdjointSolver::new(partition, parameter_dimension, backend, options)` | Variable-order BDF, orders 1–5 (same as `DaeBdf`) | Specify differential and algebraic variables through `DaePartition` |
| `DaeAdjointSolver::backward_euler(dimension, parameter_dimension, backend, options)` | Order-1 BDF (backward Euler) | All coordinates differential |
| `DaeAdjointSolver::trapezoid(dimension, parameter_dimension, backend, options)` | Fixed-step implicit trapezoidal | All coordinates differential. Requires `AdjointMode::Discrete` and `with_fixed_step(true)`, without `with_quadrature_error` |

Backward integration solves transposed systems combining $\partial F/\partial y$ and $\partial F/\partial y'$ with the Plan's linear solver (dense, sparse, or Krylov in `OdeStageBackend`).
Discrete adjoints fix accepted steps and orders, differentiating all multistep-history dependencies.
Continuous adjoints integrate the adjoint DAE backward, estimating local error from one step $h$ versus two steps $h/2$ (step doubling), then correcting with the difference (local extrapolation).

The adapter `OdeAdjointResidual::new(&system)` supplies `OdeAdjointSystem` to `DaeAdjointSolver` as residual $F = y' - f(t, y, p)$.
Use this with backward Euler or BDF adjoints for stiff ODE gradients.
Use analytical ODE Jacobians when available; otherwise difference the residual according to DAE derivative settings.
ODEs with mass matrices cannot use the adapter; implement `DaeAdjointSystem` directly.
One implicit trapezoidal step solves $F(t_0 + h, y_1, y'_1) = 0$ by Newton, using $(t_0, y_0, y'_0)$ to give $y'_1 = 2(y_1 - y_0)/h - y'_0$.
For $F = y' - f$, this equals $y_1 - y_0 = \tfrac{h}{2}(f_0 + f_1)$, with integral terms also integrated by trapezoidal rule $\tfrac{h}{2}(L_0 + L_1)$.
Without error estimates, report `primal()` and `quadrature()` are `None`.
The types `DaeAdjointRequirements` and `DaeAdjointWorkspace` have the same roles as for ODEs; initial-value gradient length is the differential-variable count.

The next cell converts the same oral-dosing model to a residual through `OdeAdjointResidual`, computing fixed-step discrete adjoints with implicit trapezoidal (second-order) and backward Euler (first-order), halving step $h = 24/2^k$ successively.
Rebuild Plans per method and step size, reusing one `DaeAdjointWorkspace` workspace through `solve_into`.
Gradients include forward discretization error, so closed-form differences should decrease at the order of $h$.
Table “Observed order” is $\log_2$ of the error ratio after halving steps.
For steps exceeding recorded capacity, no gradients are returned; read status from workspace `last_attempt()` reports.

```rust
//| id: ode-adjoint-implicit-convergence
//| ref: ode-adjoint-implicit-convergence
//| title: "Convergence orders of discrete adjoints for implicit trapezoidal and backward Euler methods"
//| run: button
//| uses: [ode-adjoint-oral-dose-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   finest_level: {"type":"range","label":"Exponent k of finest step 24/2^k h","description":"Halve step size with each increment from k = 4.","min":6,"max":10,"step":1,"value":9}
//|   tape_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Recorded-step capacity [steps]","description":"with_tape_capacity. Computations exceeding this step count fail.","min":128,"max":1024,"step":128,"value":1024}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"small-tape","label":"k = 8, capacity 128 steps","description":"The step size requiring 256 steps fails at recorded capacity.","inputs":{"finest_level":8,"tape_capacity":128}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"convergence","path":"/rows/5/4","approx":{"value":2,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"convergence","path":"/rows/11/4","approx":{"value":1,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"convergence","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"max":1e-4}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: small-tape
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"convergence","path":"/rows/4/6","equals":"BudgetLimit(Iteration)"}
//|       - {"artifact":"convergence","path":"/rows/4/3","equals":null}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"failed"}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_ode::{
    AdjointMode, AdjointOptions, DaeAdjointOptions, DaeAdjointProblem, DaeAdjointSolver,
    DaeAdjointWorkspace, DaeNumericalOptions, OdeAdjointResidual, OdeStageBackend,
};

let (dose, ka, ke, horizon) = (100.0, 1.0, 0.2, 24.0);
let exact = auc_closed_form(dose, ka, ke, horizon);
let model = OralDose([ka, ke]);
// Supply the ODE to DAE adjoints as residual F = y' − f(t, y, p). Initial derivative is f(0, y₀).
let residual = OdeAdjointResidual::new(&model).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (initial, derivative) = ([dose, 0.0], [-ka * dose, ka * dose]);
let problem = DaeAdjointProblem::new(&residual, &Auc, &initial, &derivative, (0.0, horizon));
// Reuse one workspace across all Plans with different methods and steps.
let mut workspace = DaeAdjointWorkspace::new();
let (mut rows, mut series) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut failures, mut failure) = (0, None);
for name in ["trapezoid", "backward_euler"] {
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    let mut previous: Option<f64> = None;
    for level in 4..=(finest_level as i32) {
        // h = 24/2^k is exactly representable in binary, so 2^k steps reach the endpoint exactly.
        let step = horizon / 2.0_f64.powi(level);
        let common = AdjointOptions::new(AdjointMode::Discrete, step)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            .with_fixed_step(true)
            .with_tape_capacity(tape_capacity as usize);
        let options = DaeAdjointOptions::new(common, DaeNumericalOptions::new())
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let backend = OdeStageBackend::Dense;
        let plan = match name {
            "trapezoid" => DaeAdjointSolver::trapezoid(2, 2, backend, options),
            _ => DaeAdjointSolver::backward_euler(2, 2, backend, options),
        }
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let requirements = plan
            .workspace_requirements(&problem)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let (mut initial_gradient, mut parameter_gradient) = ([0.0; 2], [0.0; 2]);
        let solved = plan.solve_into(
            &problem,
            &mut initial_gradient,
            &mut parameter_gradient,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        );
        let gradient = [
            initial_gradient[0],
            initial_gradient[1],
            parameter_gradient[0],
            parameter_gradient[1],
        ];
        let (error, status, steps) = match solved {
            Ok(report) => (
                Some(worst_relative(gradient, exact)),
                report.termination(),
                report.accepted_steps(),
            ),
            // Failed-attempt reports remain in workspace last_attempt.
            Err(_) => {
                failures += 1;
                let record = workspace.last_attempt().and_then(|a| a.progress());
                let report = record.ok_or("No failed-attempt report")?;
                (None, report.termination(), report.accepted_steps())
            }
        };
        // Observed order: log₂ of the error ratio after halving steps.
        let order = match (previous, error) {
            (Some(coarse), Some(fine)) => Some((coarse / fine).log2()),
            _ => None,
        };
        previous = error;
        if let Some(e) = error {
            points.push([step, e]);
        }
        let status = status.map(|t| format!("{t:?}"));
        if error.is_none() {
            failure = status.clone();
        }
        rows.push((
            name,
            step,
            steps,
            error,
            order,
            requirements.required_bytes(),
            status,
        ));
    }
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points, "kind": "scatter" }));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Step size h [h]",
        "yLabel": "Maximum relative gradient error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Forward method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Step size h [h]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 4 } },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Accepted steps [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum relative gradient error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Observed order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Workspace bound [B]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination status", "nullText": "-" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "convergence" })
);
let outcome = if failures == 0 {
    Outcome::satisfied("Gradients obtained at every step size")
} else {
    Outcome::failed("No gradients for step sizes exceeding recorded capacity").explanation(
        "Reaching recorded capacity fails with BudgetLimit(Iteration), returning no gradients.",
    )
};
let status = failure.unwrap_or_else(|| "ReachedEnd".to_string());
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(status)
        .diagnostic("Failed computations", failures as f64, None)
);
```

With defaults ($k = 4$–$9$, capacity 1024), implicit-trapezoidal maximum relative gradient error falls from $3.6 \times 10^{-2}$ at $h = 1.5$ h to $3.5 \times 10^{-5}$ at $h = 0.046875$ h; observed orders 1.99–2.00 match second-order convergence.
Backward Euler decreases only from 0.83 to $2.3 \times 10^{-2}$ over the same range, with observed order approaching 1 from 1.09 to 1.01.
Workspace estimates depend on recorded capacity, not step size: 289 008 B for trapezoidal and 404 128 B for backward Euler.
Capacity 128 makes the $h = 0.09375$ h computation needing 256 steps fail with `BudgetLimit(Iteration)`, leaving error columns blank.
The accepted-step column then shows 128 from the failed-attempt report.
Table errors reflect forward discretization, distinct from the discrete adjoint returning derivatives of the computed objective to within rounding error.

## Summary

Adjoints compute objective gradients with one forward and one backward integration at cost independent of parameter count.
The method `AdjointMode::Discrete` returns derivatives consistent with the computed objective; `Continuous` returns estimates from independently error-controlled adjoint equations.
Verify both against closed forms or forward sensitivities.
Memory depends on `CheckpointPolicy` and `AdjointOptions` limits; workspace preparation compares estimates with them.
Insufficient memory appears as recomputed steps in reports; exceeding recorded capacity `with_tape_capacity` fails.
Gradients are available only on success; inspect failure causes through termination statuses in workspace `last_attempt()` or `PartialFailure` reports.
Convert stiff ODEs to residuals through `OdeAdjointResidual` and solve with implicit-method adjoints in `DaeAdjointSolver`.
