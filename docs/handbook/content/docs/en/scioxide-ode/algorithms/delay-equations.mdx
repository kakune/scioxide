Phenomena whose rates depend on earlier states, such as population growth, physiological control, and infectious-disease latency, are modeled by delay differential equations (DDEs).
DDE initial conditions are functions over a delay-length interval (history), rather than values at one point.
This page explains constant-delay DDEs, the method of steps, derivative-discontinuity propagation and schedules, and using `DdePlan` and `solve_dde`, then checks how delay length changes delayed-logistic equilibria into oscillations.
Explicit Runge–Kutta error control and dense output follow [Explicit Runge–Kutta methods and step-size control](../explicit-runge-kutta-and-control/) and [Dense output and events](../dense-output-and-events/).

## Constant-delay differential equations

The solver `scioxide-ode` handles retarded equations with constant delays $\tau_1, \dots, \tau_k > 0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y'(t) &= f\bigl(t,\, y(t),\, y(t - \tau_1),\, \dots,\, y(t - \tau_k)\bigr), && t_0 \le t \le t_{\mathrm{end}}, \\
y(t) &= \varphi(t), && t_0 - \tau_{\max} \le t \le t_0
\end{aligned}
$$


Here $y(t) \in \mathbb R^n$ is state, $\varphi$ initial history, and $\tau_{\max}$ maximum delay.
Right-hand sides use only past state values; neutral equations with past derivatives, advanced equations with future values, and state-dependent delays are unsupported.
States must be real.

The trait `DdeRhs` represents the right-hand side; `delayed` of `evaluate(t, y, delayed, out)` is a matrix with row $j$ equal to $y(t - \tau_j)$ and columns representing state components.
Rows follow delays supplied to `DdePlan::new`, preserving positions even for duplicate delays.
The trait `DdeHistory` writes history $\varphi(t)$.
Both can be implemented by closures (`Fn(t, y, delayed, out)` and `Fn(t, out)`), paired through `DdeProblem::new(&rhs, &history)`.
History must be continuous and return identical values at identical times regardless of call order.
History calls count as `Function` evaluations, right-hand-side calls as `RightHandSide` evaluations.

## Method of steps

If step size $h$ is at most the smallest delay $\tau_{\min}$, then $t' - \tau_j \le t$ for every time $t'$ within $[t, t + h]$, so delayed values are already known.
Each step is therefore an ordinary ODE step with a known right-hand-side function.
This is the method of steps.
On the first interval $[t_0, t_0 + \tau_{\min}]$, past values come from history $\varphi$; later they come from accepted-step continuous extensions.

The Plan `DdePlan` uses adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) (`DormandPrince5`), limiting steps to the smallest delay.
Delayed values therefore always come from known values, requiring no within-step iteration.
Values between accepted steps use Shampine's fourth-order continuous extension.
Supply error control, step-size bounds, output times, events, and work limits through `OdeOptions`, passed by `DdeOptions::with_ode_options`.
Local error estimates exclude history-interpolation error, so choose tolerances accounting for interpolation accuracy too.

## Propagation of derivative discontinuities

At initial time $t_0$, the history's right-end derivative $\varphi'(t_0^-)$ generally differs from equation-supplied $y'(t_0^+) = f(t_0, \varphi(t_0), \dots)$.
Then $y'$ jumps at $t_0$; at time $t_0 + \tau_j$ when the right-hand side receives this jump through $y(t - \tau_j)$, $y''$ jumps.
Generally, a derivative of order $m$ jumping at $s$ produces a derivative jump of order $m + 1$ at $s + \tau_j$ for each delay $\tau_j$.
Jumps propagate to higher derivative orders, making the solution smoother over time.

Runge–Kutta error estimation assumes smoothness within steps; low-order jumps inside them disrupt estimates and cause repeated rejection.
The Plan `DdePlan::new` precomputes discontinuity times and forces steps to end exactly there.
The time $t_0$ is always scheduled as a first-derivative jump; declared history discontinuities are additional origins.
Jumps propagate through derivative order 5; higher orders do not reduce fifth-order accuracy and are not scheduled.
Times within $8\varepsilon\max(|a|, |b|)$ are merged.
For $b$ origins and $d$ delays, schedule construction needs $O(bd)$ insertions.
The method `DdePlan::discontinuities` returns scheduled times after $t_0$.

For history derivative jumps (such as piecewise-defined histories), declare time and the lowest discontinuous derivative order through `DdeHistoryBreak::new(time, derivative_order)`.
The constructor `DdeHistoryBreak::first_derivative(time)` is shorthand for a first-derivative jump.
History must be continuous, so order 0 gives `InvalidInput`.
Declared times must lie in $[t_0 - \tau_{\max}, t_0]$.
Declared orders above 5 do not force steps.

## History memory and limits

At time $t$, the method retains continuous extensions over $[t - \tau_{\max}, t]$ and discards older steps.
Retained steps number roughly $\tau_{\max}$ divided by step size, increasing with longer delays and tighter tolerances.
The setting `DdeOptions::with_history_capacity` limits retained steps, and `with_schedule_capacity` limits scheduled discontinuities; both default to unlimited.
Limits are compared only, without allocation.
Exceeded schedule limits make `DdePlan::new` return `ResourceLimit`.
If the next step needs more history than allowed, integration stops with `HistoryCapacity`.

The requirement `DdeWorkspaceRequest::new(events)` specifies monitored event functions, and `with_history_segments(count)` preallocated history steps.
The `DdeRequirements` returned by `DdePlan::workspace_requirements` reports bytes through `required_bytes`, counting six scalars per state component (four interpolation coefficients and two endpoint values) for requested history steps plus the current step.
Integration doubles insufficient history capacity up to the limit, retaining memory in workspace.
Later same-dimension integrations reuse it; with sufficient history workspace and nonallocating callbacks, `advance` does not allocate.

## Plans, reports, and termination statuses

The constructor `DdePlan::new(dimension, span, delays, history_breaks, options)` builds a schedule from interval $(t_0, t_{\mathrm{end}})$, delays, and history discontinuities.
The condition $t_0 \ge t_{\mathrm{end}}$, missing or nonpositive delays, or delays too small to advance floating-point time give `InvalidInput`; nonfinite values give `NonFinite`.
Each `advance(problem, state, sink, workspace, scope)` call restarts from history, initializing state to $\varphi(t_0)$ before integration.
Stopped integration cannot resume from where it stopped.
The method `solve(problem)` creates workspace and returns `DdeSolution` (final state, report, and output `OdeTrajectory`).
Events are supplied through `DdeProblem::with_events` but depend only on $(t, y(t))$, cannot use delayed values, and cannot change state.

The one-shot `solve_dde(rhs, history, delays, t0, t1, dimension)` integrates with default `DdeOptions` (absolute tolerance $10^{-6}$, relative tolerance $10^{-3}$, no limits), returning only $y(t_1)$.
All unsuccessful termination becomes an error carrying last-accepted progress through `DdeProgress`.

In `DdeTermination`, `ReachedEnd` and `TerminalEvent` are successful; `BudgetLimit`, `StepSizeLimit`, and `ExecutionStopped` have the same meanings as in other time-evolution solvers.
The status `HistoryCapacity` indicates a history-limit stop.
For these stops, `advance` returns `Ok` with an unsuccessful report, leaving the last accepted point in state.
The report `DdeReport` holds accepted and rejected steps, evaluations, last-step local error estimate, event count, and maximum simultaneously retained history steps (`peak_history_segments`).
The record `DdeWorkspace::last_attempt` stores latest-call progress as `DdeProgress`.

## Comparison with a piecewise polynomial analytical solution

Consider the linear delay equation with constant delay $\tau = 1$:

$$
y'(t) = y(t - 1) \quad (t \ge 0), \qquad y(t) = 1 \quad (t \le 0)
$$


On $[0, 1]$, $y(t - 1) = 1$, giving $y = 1 + t$; on $[1, 2]$, integrating $y' = 1 + (t - 1)$ gives $y = 1 + t + (t - 1)^2/2$.
Repeating gives the piecewise polynomial solution below.

$$
y(t) = 1 + \sum_{k = 1}^{\infty} \frac{(t - k + 1)_+^k}{k!}, \qquad (x)_+ = \max(x, 0)
$$

The solution on interval $[k - 1, k]$ has degree $k$.
At $t = 0$, $y'$ jumps from 0 to 1, producing a derivative jump of order $k + 1$ at $t = k$.
The schedule extends through $t = 1, 2, 3, 4$ (derivative orders 2–5).

The next cell solves through $T$ at absolute and relative tolerance $10^{-10}$ with maximum step 0.125, comparing 101 output points with the analytical solution.
Vertical dotted lines mark scheduled discontinuities.
The table shows maximum error per interval and a summary including $y(T)$ from `solve_dde` at default tolerances.
A small history limit causes termination with `HistoryCapacity`.

## Delayed logistic equation and Hopf bifurcation

Hutchinson's (1948) delayed logistic equation,

$$
y'(t) = r\, y(t)\left(1 - \frac{y(t - \tau)}{K}\right)
$$

models population growth rates determined by population $\tau$ time units earlier.
Here $r > 0$ is the intrinsic growth rate and $K > 0$ carrying capacity.
Setting $s = t/\tau$ and $u = y/K - 1$ gives $\dot u(s) = -r\tau\, u(s - 1)\bigl(1 + u(s)\bigr)$; behavior depends only on the product $r\tau$ (Wright's equation).
Substituting $u = e^{\lambda s}$ into the linearization $\dot u(s) = -r\tau\, u(s - 1)$ around equilibrium $u = 0$ gives the characteristic equation

$$
\lambda + r\tau\, e^{-\lambda} = 0
$$


The equilibrium is asymptotically stable when every characteristic root has negative real part.
Substitution of $\lambda = i\omega$ gives $\omega = \pi/2$ and $r\tau = \pi/2$ from $\cos\omega = 0$ and $\omega = r\tau\sin\omega$, so equilibrium is stable for $r\tau < \pi/2$ and a root pair crosses the imaginary axis at $r\tau = \pi/2$.
At $r\tau > 1/e$, the rightmost root is complex, giving damped oscillations toward equilibrium.
When $r\tau$ exceeds $\pi/2$, equilibrium destabilizes and a periodic solution (limit cycle) emerges through Hopf bifurcation.
Just beyond bifurcation, period is $2\pi/\omega = 4\tau$, with amplitude approximated by the Hopf normal form as

$$
A \approx \sqrt{\frac{40\,(r\tau - \pi/2)}{3\pi - 2}}
$$

($A$ is the oscillation half-amplitude of $y/K$).

The following block supplies computations shared by two cells.
The function `hutchinson_path` solves $t \in [0, T]$ with `DdePlan`, using $\tau = 1$, $K = 1$, and history $y(t) = 1 + \delta$ ($t \le 0$), returning $(t, y)$ every 0.05.
The independent `characteristic_root` finds the rightmost characteristic root $\lambda = \mu + i\omega$ with complex Newton from $\lambda_0 = 1.5i$; `half_range` returns a sequence's half-amplitude $(\max - \min)/2$.

```rust
//| id: ode-hutchinson-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Delayed-logistic numerical solutions and characteristic roots"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, NormSelection, Tolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::{MatrixView, Vector};
use scioxide_ode::{
    DdeOptions, DdePlan, DdeProblem, DdeReport, DdeWorkspaceRequest, OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule,
    OdeState, OdeTrajectory,
};

/// Solve y'(t) = rτ y(t)(1 − y(t − 1)) with history y = 1 + δ over t ∈ [0, horizon],
/// returning (t, y) every 0.05 after start and the report. Absolute and relative tolerances are both 10⁻⁹.
pub(crate) fn hutchinson_path(
    rate_delay: f64,
    perturbation: f64,
    start: f64,
    horizon: f64,
) -> Result<(Vec<(f64, f64)>, DdeReport<f64>), SciOxideError> {
    let rhs = |_: f64, y: &[f64], delayed: MatrixView<'_, f64>, out: &mut [f64]| {
        out[0] = rate_delay * y[0] * (1.0 - delayed.get(0, 0)?);
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    };
    let history = |_: f64, out: &mut [f64]| {
        out[0] = 1.0 + perturbation;
        Ok(())
    };
    let count = ((horizon - start) / 0.05).round() as usize;
    let times = (0..=count).map(|i| start + 0.05 * i as f64).collect();
    let tolerance =
        ErrorTolerance::from_tolerance(Tolerance::new(1e-9, 1e-9)?, NormSelection::RootMeanSquare);
    let ode = OdeOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(tolerance)
        .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::times(times));
    let options = DdeOptions::new().with_ode_options(ode);
    let plan = DdePlan::new(1, (0.0, horizon), &[1.0], &[], options)?;
    let mut workspace = plan.workspace(DdeWorkspaceRequest::new(0))?;
    let mut state = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![0.0]));
    let mut path = OdeTrajectory::new();
    let problem = DdeProblem::new(&rhs, &history);
    let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
    let report = plan.advance(&problem, &mut state, &mut path, &mut workspace, cx)?;
    let samples = path.times().iter().zip(path.states());
    Ok((
        samples.map(|(&t, y)| (t, y.as_slice()[0])).collect(),
        report,
    ))
}

/// Rightmost root (μ, ω) of λ + rτ e^{−λ} = 0. Newton from λ₀ = 1.5i (f64 standard functions).
pub(crate) fn characteristic_root(rate_delay: f64) -> (f64, f64) {
    let (mut mu, mut omega) = (0.0_f64, 1.5_f64);
    for _ in 0..100 {
        // e = rτ e^{−λ}, g = λ + e, g' = 1 − e.
        let scale = rate_delay * (-mu).exp();
        let (er, ei) = (scale * omega.cos(), -scale * omega.sin());
        let (gr, gi, dr, di) = (mu + er, omega + ei, 1.0 - er, -ei);
        let norm = dr * dr + di * di;
        mu -= (gr * dr + gi * di) / norm;
        omega -= (gi * dr - gr * di) / norm;
    }
    (mu, omega)
}

/// Sequence half-amplitude (max − min)/2.
pub(crate) fn half_range(values: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    let max = values.iter().copied().fold(f64::MIN, f64::max);
    let min = values.iter().copied().fold(f64::MAX, f64::min);
    (max - min) / 2.0
}
```

```rust
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"stability","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-0.0817037,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"stability","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-0.0817,"atol":3e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"stability","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":4.1419,"atol":2e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"stability","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-4}}
//|   - case: oscillating
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"stability","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"stability","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0.9,"max":1.2}}
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let (path, report) =
    hutchinson_path(rate_delay, perturbation, 0.0, 200.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
if !report.is_success() {
    return Err(format!("Did not reach the endpoint: {:?}", report.termination()));
}
let (mu, omega) = characteristic_root(rate_delay);
let deviation = |a: f64, b: f64| {
    path.iter()
        .filter(|p| p.0 >= a && p.0 <= b)
        .fold(0.0_f64, |m, p| m.max((p.1 - 1.0).abs()))
};
let (early, late) = (deviation(20.0, 40.0), deviation(60.0, 80.0));
let measurable = |d: f64| d < 0.05 && d > 1e-6;
let rate = (measurable(early) && measurable(late)).then(|| (late / early).ln() / 40.0);
// Upward crossings of y = 1 (linear interpolation).
let crossings: Vec<f64> = path
    .windows(2)
    .filter(|w| w[0].0 >= 100.0 && w[0].1 < 1.0 && w[1].1 >= 1.0)
    .map(|w| w[0].0 + (1.0 - w[0].1) * (w[1].0 - w[0].0) / (w[1].1 - w[0].1))
    .collect();
let count = crossings.len();
let period = (count >= 2).then(|| (crossings[count - 1] - crossings[0]) / (count - 1) as f64);
let tail: Vec<f64> = path.iter().filter(|p| p.0 >= 180.0).map(|p| p.1).collect();
let linear_period = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI / omega;
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "rτ [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 2 } },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Characteristic-root real part μ [1/τ]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Observed decay rate [1/τ]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Out of range" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Linearized period 2π/ω [τ]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Observed period [τ]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Half-amplitude for t ≥ 180 [K]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        rate_delay,
        mu,
        rate,
        linear_period,
        period,
        half_range(&tail)
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "stability" })
);
let series: Vec<[f64; 2]> = path.iter().map(|p| [p.0, p.1]).collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": format!("y(t)（rτ = {rate_delay:.2}）"), "points": series })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Time t [τ]",
        "yLabel": "Population y [K]",
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 1.0, "label": "Equilibrium y = K" }]
    })
);
```

At default $r\tau = 1.4$, the characteristic root is $\mu = -0.0817$, $2\pi/\omega = 4.1419$; numerical decay rate $-0.0832$ and period $4.1420$ agree within 2% and $10^{-4}$, respectively.
The half-amplitude over $t \ge 180$ falls to $1.9 \times 10^{-8}$, with stable equilibrium.
At $r\tau = 0.5$, rapid decay takes the equilibrium deviation below integration error before $t = 20$, so the out-of-range decay rate is not displayed.
At $r\tau = 1.8$, $\mu = 0.097 > 0$ destabilizes equilibrium, and the solution settles into a periodic orbit of half-amplitude 1.05.
Its period 4.18 differs from linearized 3.85 and near-bifurcation 4, reflecting nonlinear effects.

At default $T = 200$, half-amplitudes are at most $2 \times 10^{-8}$ for $r\tau \le 1.4$ and $1.4 \times 10^{-4}$ for $r\tau = 1.5$, growing rapidly above $\pi/2$.

## Summary

The Plan `DdePlan` integrates constant-delay retarded DDEs by the DP5(4) method of steps with steps no larger than the smallest delay, reading past values from fourth-order continuous extensions.
Jumps from initial time and declared history discontinuities propagate through derivative order 5 and are scheduled as step boundaries, avoiding low-order jumps within steps of piecewise smooth solutions.
Retained history depends on maximum delay and step size, stopping with `HistoryCapacity` if `with_history_capacity` is exceeded.
Local error estimates omit history-interpolation error; check accuracy against known solutions or solutions at different tolerances.
Delay length changes stability; delayed-logistic equilibrium becomes periodic at $r\tau = \pi/2$.
