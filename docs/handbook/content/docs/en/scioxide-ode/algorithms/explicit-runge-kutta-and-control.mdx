With fixed steps, sizing them for rapid changes wastes evaluations in smooth intervals, while sizing them for smooth intervals produces large errors during rapid changes.
Adaptive step-size control estimates local error each step and adjusts the step size within tolerance requirements.

This page covers explicit Runge–Kutta construction and order conditions, embedded pairs for local error estimation, FSAL reuse of the final stage as the next first stage, componentwise normalization, step-size controllers, and initial-step estimation.
The final two cells examine controller effects on rejections and evaluations, and relations between tolerances and evaluation counts for different methods.
Notation follows [Error order, absolute stability, and stiffness](../error-order-and-stability/).

## Butcher tableaux and order conditions

An $s$-stage explicit Runge–Kutta method computes one step from time $t_n$ to $t_{n+1} = t_n + h$ as follows.

$$
k_i = f\Bigl( t_n + c_i h,\; y_n + h \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} a_{ij} k_j \Bigr), \quad i = 1, \dots, s,
\qquad
y_{n+1} = y_n + h \sum_{i=1}^s b_i k_i
$$

The values $k_i$ are called stage slopes.
Coefficients are represented by the Butcher tableau

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
0 & & & & & \\
c_2 & a_{21} & & & & \\
c_3 & a_{31} & a_{32} & & & \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & & \\
c_s & a_{s1} & a_{s2} & \dots & a_{s,s-1} & \\
\hline
& b_1 & b_2 & \dots & b_{s-1} & b_s \\
& \hat{b}_1 & \hat{b}_2 & \dots & \hat{b}_{s-1} & \hat{b}_s
\end{array}
$$


The last row $\hat{b}$ gives the second set of weights for the embedded pairs in the next section.

### Order conditions and rooted trees

Order conditions are coefficient constraints making $y_{n+1}$ agree with the true solution's Taylor expansion through terms in $h^p$.
Butcher's rooted-tree theory derives them systematically; under $c_i = \sum_j a_{ij}$, the low-order conditions are as follows.

| Order | Condition |
|---|---|
| 1 | $\sum_i b_i = 1$ |
| 2 | $\sum_i b_i c_i = 1/2$ |
| 3 | $\sum_i b_i c_i^2 = 1/3$, $\sum_{i,j} b_i a_{ij} c_j = 1/6$ |
| 4 | $\sum_i b_i c_i^3 = 1/4$, $\sum_{i,j} b_i c_i a_{ij} c_j = 1/8$, $\sum_{i,j} b_i a_{ij} c_j^2 = 1/12$, $\sum_{i,j,k} b_i a_{ij} a_{jk} c_k = 1/24$ |

Their number increases rapidly with order, reaching 200 through order 8.
High-order formulas satisfy these conditions, then use remaining coefficient freedom to tune error constants and stability regions.

## Local error estimation with embedded pairs

An embedded pair uses the same stage slopes $k_1, \dots, k_s$ with weights $b$ and $\hat{b}$ to form solutions of different orders.

$$
y_{n+1} = y_n + h \sum_{i=1}^s b_i k_i, \qquad \hat{y}_{n+1} = y_n + h \sum_{i=1}^s \hat{b}_i k_i
$$

For methods advancing with the higher-order solution (local extrapolation), the difference

$$
e = y_{n+1} - \hat{y}_{n+1} = h \sum_{i=1}^s (b_i - \hat{b}_i) k_i
$$

estimates the lower-order solution's local error and is proportional to $h^{\hat{p}+1}$ ($\hat{p}$ is the lower order).
Because the estimate concerns a lower-order solution than the advancing one, the accepted step's actual local error is often smaller than estimated.
This is asymptotic, however; the estimate is not an upper bound.

### Reducing evaluations with FSAL

The seven-stage `DormandPrince5` is constructed with final-stage time $c_7 = 1$ and coefficients $a_{7j} = b_j$.
The final-stage slope is then

$$
k_7 = f\Bigl( t_n + h,\; y_n + h \sum_{j=1}^{6} b_j k_j \Bigr) = f(t_{n+1}, y_{n+1})
$$

which equals the next step's first stage $k_1 = f(t_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$ if the step is accepted.
This property is called FSAL (first same as last).
Skipping the next first-stage evaluation leaves six evaluations per step after the first.
The method `DormandPrince853` also uses $f(t_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$ at an accepted point as the next first stage, needing 13 evaluations initially and 12 thereafter.
The methods `Rkf45` and `Verner65` evaluate their first stage once per accepted point and reuse it for rejected-step retries from that point.

When a system has accepted-point step-size bounds (`OdeSystem::step_limit`, local bounds such as CFL conditions), the bound and right-hand side $f(t_n, y_n)$ may use the same intermediate quantities, such as fluxes or characteristic speeds.
Implementing `OdeSystem::rhs_with_step_limit` lets explicit Runge–Kutta obtain both the bound and derivative in one call, use the derivative as its first stage, and reuse it on retries from the same accepted point.
The default implementation returns `OdeRhsStepLimit::Unavailable`, causing separate calls to `step_limit` and `rhs`.
This handoff only copies values and introduces no approximation.
Immediately before a step, use the minimum of this bound, the `OdeOptions::with_maximum_step` bound, the controller proposal, and the remaining time to the endpoint.

### DOP853 error estimation

The 12-stage `DormandPrince853` advances with an eighth-order solution and combines fifth- and third-order error estimates $E_5$ and $E_3$ componentwise.

$$
e_i = h\, \frac{E_{5,i}\, |E_{5,i}|}{\sqrt{E_{5,i}^2 + (0.1\, E_{3,i})^2}}
$$

Using only $E_5$ can underestimate error at large steps; adding $E_3$ to the denominator limits this.
Hairer's DOP853 combines them at the norm level, while `scioxide-ode` combines them componentwise.
The combined estimate uses the same normalization and norm as other methods.

### Stages and evaluations by method

| Method | Order | Stages | Evaluations per accepted step | Power $k$ of $h$ in the error estimate | Stable real-axis interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Rkf45` | 5(4) | 6 | 6 | 5 | $[-3.6777, 0]$ |
| `DormandPrince5` | 5(4) | 7 | 6 (FSAL; 7 for the first step) | 5 | $[-3.3065, 0]$ |
| `Verner65` | 6(5) | 8 | 8 | 6 | $[-4.0647, 0]$ |
| `DormandPrince853` | 8(5,3) | 12 | 12 (13 for the first step) | 8 | $[-6.3936, 0]$ |

Table evaluation counts assume no rejections and no dense output.
Rejected steps also consume evaluations, so total evaluations are not simply a constant times accepted steps.
Initial-step estimation (below) also uses two evaluations.
[Dense output and event detection](../dense-output-and-events/) explains additional evaluations for dense output.

## Error normalization and acceptance

Components can have different units and magnitudes, such as orbital radius $10^{11}$ m and attitude angle $10^{-3}$ rad.
One absolute tolerance for all components either misses errors in small components or demands excessive accuracy in large ones.
For each component $i$, construct the scale

$$
s_i = \mathrm{atol}_i + \mathrm{rtol} \cdot \max(|y_{n,i}|, |y_{n+1,i}|)
$$

and compute the normalized error

$$
r = \Bigl\| \Bigl( \frac{e_i}{s_i} \Bigr)_{i=1}^d \Bigr\|, \qquad
\|v\|_{\mathrm{RMS}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{d} \sum_{i=1}^d v_i^2}
$$


The defaults for `OdeOptions::new` are $\mathrm{atol} = 10^{-6}$, $\mathrm{rtol} = 10^{-3}$, and RMS norm; `ErrorTolerance` supplied to `OdeOptions::with_error_tolerance` also permits componentwise absolute tolerances and maximum norm (see [Tolerances and residuals](../../../concepts/tolerances-and-residuals/) for general tolerance definitions).
If $r \le 1$, accept the step and advance state to $y_{n+1}$; if $r > 1$, reject it and retry from the same time with a smaller step.
Rejected-step values do not enter the accepted state.
The value $r$ scales one-step local error; it does not bound terminal global error.

## Step-size controllers

Normalized error approximately follows $r \propto h^{k}$.
Here $k$ is the $h$ power in the error estimate, determined by method as tabulated above (4 for `RadauIia`; current order plus 1 for variable-order multistep methods).
A controller chooses the next step size to make $r \approx 1$.
Select the controller with `OdeStepControl` supplied to `OdeOptions::with_step_control`.

| `OdeStepControl` | Contents | Methods using it |
|---|---|---|
| `MethodDefault` (default) | Method-specific default | I control with a deadband for explicit embedded pairs, predictive control for `RadauIia`, default PI control for other adaptive methods |
| `ProportionalIntegral(c)` | PI control with `ProportionalIntegralController` coefficients (no deadband) | Adaptive methods |
| `Radau` | Gustafsson predictive control | Adaptive methods |

For fixed-step methods, specifying anything except `MethodDefault` makes `OdeMethod::validate` return `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).

### PI and I control

The step-size formula for `ProportionalIntegralController` is

$$
h_{\text{new}} = h \cdot \operatorname{clamp}\bigl( s \cdot r_n^{-\alpha/k} \cdot r_{n-1}^{\beta/k},\; f_{\min},\; f_{\max} \bigr)
$$


Here $s$ is the safety factor, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are exponents, $r_{n-1}$ is the last accepted-step error (1 initially), and $f_{\min}$ and $f_{\max}$ bound the multiplier.
Construct with `ProportionalIntegralController::new(safety, max_factor, min_factor, alpha, beta)`; `Default` uses $s = 0.9$, $f_{\max} = 5$, $f_{\min} = 0.2$, $\alpha = 0.7$, and $\beta = 0.4$.
Setting $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 0$ gives classical I control $h_{\text{new}} = h \cdot s \cdot r_n^{-1/k}$ based only on current error.
The value $r_{n-1}$ updates only after accepted steps, remaining unchanged on rejection.
An exactly zero-error step uses multiplier $f_{\max}$ and resets $r_{n-1}$ to 1.

PI control (Gustafsson, Hairer–Wanner) moderates step-size changes through the previous-error term $r_{n-1}^{\beta/k}$.
In stability-limited intervals, I-controlled steps oscillate across the stability boundary, with steps beyond it rejected.
The previous-error term suppresses this oscillation, reducing rejections under PI control (checked in the next cell).

### Deadband in default I control

Explicit embedded pairs with `MethodDefault` use I control with $s = 0.9$, $f_{\min} = 0.2$, and $f_{\max} = 5$, augmented by a deadband.
For $0.5 < r \le 0.75$, the step is accepted without changing size; otherwise the formula above adjusts it.
This prevents small error-estimate fluctuations from changing the step size.
I control explicitly selected through `ProportionalIntegral` with $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 0$ has no deadband.

### Radau IIA predictive control

The default for `RadauIia` and `OdeStepControl::Radau` uses the same Gustafsson predictive control as Hairer–Wanner's RADAU5.
The multiplier is

$$
q = \min\Bigl( \frac{s_N}{r_n^{1/4}},\; 0.9 \cdot \frac{h_n}{h_{n-1}} \cdot \frac{r_{n-1}^{1/4}}{r_n^{1/2}} \Bigr), \qquad
s_N = \min\Bigl(0.9,\; 0.9 \cdot \frac{15}{14 + N_{\text{Newton}}}\Bigr)
$$

restricted to $0.2 \le q \le 8$.
Here $N_{\text{Newton}}$ is the step's Newton iteration count; more iterations lower safety factor $s_N$.
The second (predictive) term applies only to accepted steps with a preceding accepted step; $r_{n-1}$ is stored with lower bound 0.01.
If $1 \le q \le 1.2$ on an accepted step, set $q = 1$ to avoid rebuilding the matrix factorization.

### Rejection and retries

A rejected step is retried with the smaller of its size and the controller's proposal.
Even after accepting a step following rejection, the next step is no larger than that accepted step.
With `OdeOptions::with_minimum_step` specified, smaller proposals are raised to that lower bound.
If a minimum-size step is rejected, or the step becomes too small to advance floating-point time, integration stops with `OdeTermination::StepSizeLimit`, retaining the last accepted point in state.
Each `advance` initializes controller history for a fresh integration.

### Initial-step estimation

Without an initial step supplied through `OdeOptions::with_initial_step`, adaptive methods estimate it by Hairer, Nørsett, and Wanner's procedure (I §II.4).
Using $\|\cdot\|$ for the normalized norm above:

1. Compute $d_0 = \|y_0\|$ and $d_1 = \|f(t_0, y_0)\|$, setting $h_0 = 0.01\, d_0/d_1$ (if $d_0$ or $d_1$ is below $10^{-5}$, use $h_0 = 10^{-6}$).
2. Evaluate $f(t_0 + h_0, y_1)$ after the forward Euler step $y_1 = y_0 + h_0 f(t_0, y_0)$ and set $d_2 = \|f(t_0 + h_0, y_1) - f(t_0, y_0)\|/h_0$.
3. Set $h_1 = \bigl(0.01/\max(d_1, d_2)\bigr)^{1/p}$ ($p$ is method order) and choose $h = \min(100\, h_0,\; h_1)$ as the initial step.

This uses two evaluations, reusing the first $f(t_0, y_0)$ as the initial step's first stage.
Fixed-step methods also use this estimate when no step is supplied.

## Comparing controller responses

The following cell solves the Prothero–Robinson problem $y' = \lambda(y - \cos t) - \sin t$, $y(0) = 1$ (exact solution $y = \cos t$) with `DormandPrince5` over $[0, 10]$.
The three controllers are default I control with a deadband, I control with $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = 0$, and default PI control.
Inputs are $|\lambda|$ and tolerance (absolute and relative both $10^q$); outputs show $h|\lambda|$ over accepted steps and a table of step counts, evaluations, and terminal errors.
Even after the solution smoothly follows $\cos t$, explicit methods must keep $h|\lambda|$ within stable interval $[0, 3.3065]$.
The plot's horizontal line is this boundary returned by `OdeMethod::linear_stability`.

With the defaults ($|\lambda| = 200$, $q = -3$), all three controllers use steps averaging $h|\lambda| \approx 3.30$ over $t \ge 5$, remaining near the stability boundary.
I control repeatedly rejects steps beyond the boundary: 105 rejections with the default deadband and 93 without it.
PI control has no rejections and uses 3668 right-hand-side evaluations, 14% fewer than the default 4274.
Terminal error is $7.0 \times 10^{-4}$ by default and $6.7 \times 10^{-5}$ with PI control, both below tolerance $10^{-3}$.
At $|\lambda| = 1000$, the two I controllers reject 475 and 504 steps, while PI rejects none; evaluation counts are 20984, 21152, and 18164.

When accuracy determines steps, as with $|\lambda| = 10$ or $q = -6$, all controllers have at most six rejections.
PI control then selects somewhat smaller steps.
At $q = -6$, mean $h|\lambda|$ is 1.66 with PI control and 2.08 by default; PI uses 7160 evaluations versus the default 5792, but its terminal error $9.9 \times 10^{-8}$ is smaller than the default $3.4 \times 10^{-7}$.
The deadband does not reduce rejections for this problem.
For problems with long stability-limited intervals, explicitly selecting PI control through `ProportionalIntegralController::default()` reduces evaluations.
For greater stiffness, switch from explicit to implicit methods.

## Comparing method efficiency

Compare efficiency by the right-hand-side evaluations needed for the same accuracy.
The next cell solves the Arenstorf orbit of the Earth–Moon restricted three-body problem (Hairer, Nørsett, and Wanner I §II.0) with four embedded pairs.
With mass ratios $\mu = 0.012277471$ and $\mu' = 1 - \mu$, the equations are

$$
\begin{aligned}
x'' &= x + 2y' - \mu' \frac{x + \mu}{D_1} - \mu \frac{x - \mu'}{D_2}, &
y'' &= y - 2x' - \mu' \frac{y}{D_1} - \mu \frac{y}{D_2}, \\
D_1 &= \bigl((x + \mu)^2 + y^2\bigr)^{3/2}, &
D_2 &= \bigl((x - \mu')^2 + y^2\bigr)^{3/2}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the four state components are ordered as $(x, y, x', y')$.
The trajectory returns from initial values $(0.994, 0, 0, -2.00158510637908)$ to the same point after period $T = 17.0652165601580$, so the maximum-norm difference between the state after $m$ periods and the initial state measures global error.
Absolute and relative tolerances are both $10^{-3}, \dots, 10^{-12}$; the input is period count $m$.
The plot places evaluation count horizontally and global error vertically; methods closer to the lower left are more efficient.

```rust
//| id: ode-explicit-work-precision
//| ref: ode-explicit-work-precision
//| title: "Compare evaluation counts and global errors of four embedded pairs on the Arenstorf orbit"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   periods: {"type":"range","label":"Period count m [-]","description":"Number of periods to integrate. Longer integrations accumulate more global error.","min":1,"max":3,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"three-periods","label":"m = 3","description":"Global error exceeds that for one period even at the same tolerance.","inputs":{"periods":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"efficiency","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"efficiency","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"efficiency","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":8000}}
//|   - case: three-periods
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"efficiency","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"max":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, EvaluationRole, ExecutionContext, NormSelection};
use scioxide_linalg::Vector;
use scioxide_ode::{
    DormandPrince5, DormandPrince853, OdeFn, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeProblem, OdeState,
    OdeSystem, Rkf45, Verner65,
};

// Pairs (right-hand-side evaluations, global error) for tolerances 10^(−q), q = 3, …, 12.
fn sweep<M: OdeMethod<f64> + Clone, Sys: OdeSystem<f64>>(
    method: M,
    problem: &OdeProblem<'_, f64, Sys>,
    initial: &[f64],
) -> Result<Vec<(usize, f64)>, String> {
    let mut results = Vec::new();
    for q in 3..=12 {
        let epsilon = 10.0_f64.powi(-q);
        let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(epsilon, epsilon, NormSelection::RootMeanSquare)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let options = OdeOptions::new().with_error_tolerance(tolerance);
        let mut workspace = method
            .workspace(problem, &options)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let mut state = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(initial.to_vec()));
        let report = method
            .advance(
                problem,
                &options,
                &mut state,
                &mut (),
                &mut workspace,
                ExecutionContext::sequential(),
            )
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if !report.is_success() {
            return Err(format!("{:?}", report.termination()));
        }
        let error = state
            .values()
            .iter()
            .zip(initial)
            .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
            .fold(0.0, f64::max);
        results.push((
            report.counts().provider(EvaluationRole::RightHandSide),
            error,
        ));
    }
    Ok(results)
}

let (mu, nu) = (0.012277471, 1.0 - 0.012277471);
```

For the default one period at tolerance $10^{-6}$, global errors are 0.17 for `Rkf45`, 0.016 for `DormandPrince5`, and 0.0044 for `DormandPrince853`, all more than three orders of magnitude above tolerance.
Tolerances concern one-step local error, which is amplified around the close approach to the Moon.
At tolerance $10^{-3}$, all four methods have global error above 1 and fail to reproduce the orbit's shape.
At tolerance $10^{-12}$, `DormandPrince853` reaches global error $2.7 \times 10^{-10}$ with 5366 evaluations, less than half the evaluations and two orders less error than `DormandPrince5` (11954 evaluations, $4.0 \times 10^{-8}$).
The method `Verner65` uses fewer evaluations than `DormandPrince5`, but has larger global error $5.8 \times 10^{-7}$ at the same tolerance.
Compare methods by evaluations for equal global error on the plot, rather than equal tolerance values.
The higher-order `DormandPrince853` gains an advantage as tolerances tighten.

At $m = 3$, even tolerance $10^{-12}$ gives global error $5.8 \times 10^{-5}$ with `DormandPrince853` and at least $3.5 \times 10^{-3}$ with other methods.
As periods accumulate, error amplification compounds (factor $e^{L(T - t_0)}$ in [Global error bound](oxref:ode-local-global-error)); local tolerances therefore cannot predict endpoint accuracy over long integrations.

## Summary

Explicit Runge–Kutta local error is estimated from embedded-pair differences; a step is accepted when the norm normalized by componentwise scales is at most 1.
With FSAL, `DormandPrince5` uses six evaluations per accepted step, while `DormandPrince853` uses 12.
By default, explicit embedded pairs use I control with a deadband, `RadauIia` predictive control, and other adaptive methods PI control; `OdeOptions::with_step_control` selects PI control with modified `ProportionalIntegralController` coefficients.
I control incurs more rejections in stability-limited problems; choose PI control or, depending on stiffness, switch to an implicit method.
Step-size control uses local error estimates; check global error against known solutions or solutions at different tolerances.

[Error order, absolute stability, and stiffness](../error-order-and-stability/) explains small explicit steps for stiff problems; [Dense output and event detection](../dense-output-and-events/) explains values within accepted steps and event-time computation.
