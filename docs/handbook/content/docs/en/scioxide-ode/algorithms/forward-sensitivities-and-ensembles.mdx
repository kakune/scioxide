Parameter estimation, experimental design, and uncertainty propagation require how solution $y(t; p)$ changes with parameters $p$ or initial values $y_0$.
For few parameters, forward sensitivity straightforwardly integrates derivatives alongside the solution.
Solving many parameter or initial-value variants (an ensemble) also examines finite-change sensitivity or propagates uncertainty by Monte Carlo.
This page explains forward-sensitivity equations, using `ForwardSensitivitySystem`, terminal-event sensitivities, and executing many problems through `solve_ensemble_into`.
For many parameters and only a scalar-objective gradient, [Adjoints](../adjoint-gradients/) cost less.

## Variational equations and augmented systems

Differentiating the solution of $y' = f(t, y, p)$, $y(t_0) = y_0(p)$ in parameter direction $d \in \mathbb R^m$ gives $s(t) = \partial y(t)/\partial p \cdot d$, satisfying the variational equation obtained by differentiating with respect to $p$:

$$
s' = f_y(t, y, p)\, s + f_p(t, y, p)\, d, \qquad s(t_0) = \frac{\partial y_0}{\partial p}\, d
$$


Here $f_y$ is the state Jacobian of $n \times n$, and $f_p$ the parameter Jacobian of $n \times m$.
Sensitivities to initial value $y_{0,i}$ follow from the same equation with $d = 0$ and $s(t_0) = e_i$ (a unit vector).
Since variational equations depend on $y$, integrate sensitivities for $k$ directions together with $y$ in the dimension-$N = n(k + 1)$ augmented system

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix} y \\ s_1 \\ \vdots \\ s_k \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} f(t, y, p) \\ f_y s_1 + f_p d_1 \\ \vdots \\ f_y s_k + f_p d_k \end{bmatrix}
$$


This is an ordinary ODE, solvable by any method.
Sensitivity components enter error control, so steps depend on accuracy of both $y$ and sensitivities (coupled control).

One augmented right-hand-side evaluation calls $f$ once and directional derivative $f_y s_j + f_p d_j$ $k$ times.
For explicit methods, cost grows approximately with direction count.
Implicit methods (BDF, Radau IIA, backward Euler) form augmented Jacobians by forward differences; dense solvers require about $N$ augmented right-hand-side evaluations and store $N^2$ entries per Jacobian.
Krylov solvers use directional differences without forming matrices ([Jacobians and linear stage equations](../jacobians-and-linear-stages/)).

## Constructing sensitivity systems

The trait `OdeSensitivitySystem` supplies state dimension $n$ (`dimension`), real parameter count $m$ (`parameter_dimension`), right-hand side $f(t, y, p)$ (`rhs`), and directional derivative $f_y\,\delta y + f_p\,\delta p$ (`tangent_rhs`).
Directional derivatives may use dense or sparse Jacobians, matrix-free actions, or forward-mode automatic differentiation.
States may be real or complex; parameters are real.
Directions follow real paths, so nonholomorphic right-hand sides can return real-linear products.
Return callback workspace scalar count through `workspace_len`.

The constructor `ForwardSensitivitySystem::bind(provider, parameters, parameter_directions, workspace)` accepts parameter values and a $k \times m$ matrix with one direction per row, returning augmented `OdeSystem`.
Direction values are copied into `ForwardSensitivityWorkspace` in $O(km)$ work, growing workspace only as needed.
The constructor `ForwardSensitivityRequirements::new(provider, directions)` returns the direction-value count $mk$ (`packed_directions`) and workspace scalar count (`provider_scratch`), permitting preallocation through workspace `check` and `try_reserve`.
The bound system borrows workspace, making it unavailable for other use until the system is released.

Build augmented initial values through `initialize_into(state, tangents, output)`.
The input `tangents` is a $n \times k$ matrix whose column $j$ is $s_j(t_0)$; zero columns give parameter sensitivities and unit-vector columns initial-value sensitivities.
After integration, separate state and sensitivities through `extract_into(augmented, state, tangents)`.
The constructor `error_tolerance(primal_absolute, sensitivity_absolute, relative, norm)` creates augmented-system tolerances with separate absolute tolerances for $y$ and sensitivities, supplied to `OdeOptions::with_error_tolerance`.
If sensitivity and state magnitudes differ greatly, match sensitivity absolute tolerance to its scale.

## Event-time sensitivities

Stopping at event $g(t, y, p) = 0$ makes stopping time $t^*$ itself parameter-dependent.
The augmented $s_j(t^*)$ is a fixed-time sensitivity, excluding event-time changes.
Differentiating $g(t^*(p), y(t^*(p); p), p) = 0$ in direction $j$ gives event-time and event-state sensitivities

$$
\tau_j = \frac{\partial t^*}{\partial p}\, d_j = -\frac{g_y s_j + g_p d_j}{g_t + g_y f}, \qquad
\sigma_j = s_j + f\, \tau_j
$$


Denominator $g_t + g_y f$ is the event function's total derivative ($dg/dt$); near zero (a tangency), sensitivities become ill-conditioned and are undefined at zero.
Condition numbers grow as $1/|dg/dt|$.

The function `ForwardSensitivitySystem::event_sensitivities_into(input, time_output, state_output)` performs this conversion in $O(nk)$ work.
Supply `EventSensitivityInput` with fixed-time sensitivity matrix (`fixed_time`), event-state slope $f$ (`state_slope`), per-direction numerator $g_y s_j + g_p d_j$ (`numerators`), total derivative $dg/dt$ (`event_slope`), transversality threshold (`minimum_slope`), and root-isolation statement (`isolation`).
The caller computes these from event-function derivatives.
For `EventIsolation::Ambiguous` or $|dg/dt| \le$ `minimum_slope`, it writes nothing and returns `EventSensitivityStatus::Ambiguous` or `NonTransverse`, respectively; otherwise it returns `Available`.
This status describes only sensitivity-conversion availability; read integration success separately through `OdeTermination`.
Sensitivity corrections for state jumps at events are unavailable; the caller must apply the jump-map derivative.

## Forward-sensitivity experiment

Logistic growth,

$$
y' = r\, y \left(1 - \frac{y}{K}\right), \qquad y(0) = y_0
$$

has solution $y(t) = K/D$, $D = 1 + A e^{-rt}$, and $A = (K - y_0)/y_0$, with closed forms for all three sensitivities.

$$
\frac{\partial y}{\partial r} = \frac{K A t\, e^{-rt}}{D^2}, \qquad
\frac{\partial y}{\partial K} = \frac{1}{D} - \frac{K e^{-rt}}{y_0 D^2}, \qquad
\frac{\partial y}{\partial y_0} = \frac{K^2 e^{-rt}}{y_0^2 D^2}
$$

Directional derivatives come from $f_y = r(1 - 2y/K)$, $f_r = y(1 - y/K)$, and $f_K = r y^2/K^2$.

The following block supplies a model shared by two cells.
The function `logistic_rhs` is right-hand side $f$; `Logistic` implements `OdeSensitivitySystem` (parameters $p = (r, K)$); `logistic_exact` gives closed forms for solution and three sensitivities.

```rust
//| id: ode-logistic-sensitivity-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Logistic-growth right-hand side, directional derivatives, and closed forms"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SolveContext};
use scioxide_ode::OdeSensitivitySystem;

/// f(y; r, K) = r y (1 − y/K).
pub(crate) fn logistic_rhs(r: f64, k: f64, y: f64) -> f64 {
    r * y * (1.0 - y / k)
}

/// Logistic growth with parameters p = (r, K).
pub(crate) struct Logistic;

impl OdeSensitivitySystem<f64> for Logistic {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        1
    }

    fn parameter_dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }

    fn rhs(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        p: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        out[0] = logistic_rhs(p[0], p[1], y[0]);
        Ok(())
    }

    // f_y δy + f_r δr + f_K δK. f_y = r(1 − 2y/K), f_r = y(1 − y/K), f_K = r y²/K².
    fn tangent_rhs(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        p: &[f64],
        dy: &[f64],
        dp: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let (r, k, y) = (p[0], p[1], y[0]);
        let f_y = r * (1.0 - 2.0 * y / k);
        out[0] = f_y * dy[0] + y * (1.0 - y / k) * dp[0] + r * y * y / (k * k) * dp[1];
        Ok(())
    }
}

/// Closed forms [y, ∂y/∂r, ∂y/∂K, ∂y/∂y₀] (references from f64 standard functions).
pub(crate) fn logistic_exact(r: f64, k: f64, y0: f64, t: f64) -> [f64; 4] {
    let (a, e) = ((k - y0) / y0, (-r * t).exp());
    let d = 1.0 + a * e;
    [
        k / d,
        k * a * t * e / (d * d),
        1.0 / d - k * e / (y0 * d * d),
        k * k * e / (y0 * y0 * d * d),
    ]
}
```

The next cell models cultured bacteria, with $K = 100$ ($10^6$ cells/mL), $y_0 = 2$, $t \in [0, 20]$ h, and input growth rate $r$.
Solve three augmented directions with `DormandPrince5`: parameter directions $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$, plus an initial-value direction (parameter direction 0, initial tangent 1).
The plot expresses three sensitivities as elasticities $(p/y)\,\partial y/\partial p$ (relative solution changes per relative parameter change), with numerical symbols and dashed closed forms.
The table shows terminal sensitivities and differences from closed forms.

With defaults ($r = 0.6$ h⁻¹, $\varepsilon = 10^{-9}$), 153 steps reach `ReachedEnd`; at $t = 20$ h, the three sensitivities agree with closed forms to differences $1.5 \times 10^{-9}$ ($\partial y/\partial r$), $3.7 \times 10^{-12}$ ($\partial y/\partial K$), and $7.7 \times 10^{-11}$ ($\partial y/\partial y_0$).
There are 926 right-hand-side evaluations and 2778 directional-derivative evaluations, three times as many for three directions.
In the elasticity plot, elasticity with respect to $y_0$ decreases from 1, elasticity with respect to $r$ peaks during growth, and after saturation elasticity with respect to $K$ approaches 1 while the other two approach zero.
Saturated solutions depend only on $K$ and lose initial-value and growth-rate information; the plot shows why observations only at $t = 20$ h poorly identify $r$.
At $\varepsilon = 10^{-4}$, only 18 steps are taken and the $\partial y/\partial r$ difference grows to $8.6 \times 10^{-5}$.
At $r = 2$ h⁻¹, numerical $\partial y/\partial r$ is $6.0 \times 10^{-10}$ versus true $4.2 \times 10^{-13}$, with difference remaining of the order of absolute sensitivity tolerance $10^{-9}$.
Relative errors of near-zero sensitivities are not meaningful; assess them using absolute tolerances.

## Ensembles

The function `solve_ensemble_into(parameters, run, policy, members, outcomes, workspace, scope)` calls `run(i, parameters_i, member_i, cx)` for each member $i = 0, 1, \dots$, stores results in `outcomes[i]`, and returns `EnsembleReport`.
The `cx` passed to `run` is that member's nested-solve context; pass it directly to the member solver's `advance`.
The input `members` holds per-member mutable state (workspace or result storage); members own numerical memory.

The type `EnsembleParameters` selects parameter delivery.
The variant `Shared(values)` supplies the same sequence to all members without copying; `Independent(matrix)` supplies row $i$ of the $M \times m$ matrix to member $i$.
Contiguous rows are borrowed directly; other layouts are copied into workspace per execution.
Nonfinite parameters return `NonFinite` without running any member.
The method `EnsembleRequirements::new(members, parameters)` returns member and copied-parameter counts, allocatable through `check` and `try_reserve` of `EnsembleWorkspace`.
With sufficient workspace, the function itself does not allocate.

The trait `EnsembleOutcome` returns whether a member succeeded; ODE, SDE, DDE, DAE, dynamical-system, BVP, and adjoint reports implement it.
Custom types pairing reports with solution-derived values can implement it too.
Member errors remain in their results (`Some(Err(e))`), without stopping other members.
The report `EnsembleReport` gives executed members (`completed`), successful members (`successful`), and total work (`accounting`); `is_success` is true only if every member ran and succeeded.
If execution itself stops, for example by cancellation, completed results remain, others stay `None`, and the report remains in `EnsembleWorkspace::last_attempt`.

Under `ExecutionPolicy::Sequential`, members run in index order.
Other policies distribute members across execution-context executor workers, with at most 64 partitions.
Parallel execution requires `run` to be `Sync` and members and results to be `Send`.
Outer solve work limits (such as shared evaluation limits) are allocated among members in index order before execution; unused allowances are not redistributed.
There are no separate per-member iteration or proposal limits; member solver settings determine them.
Wasm cells use sequential execution contexts, so every policy runs members in index order on one worker.

## Growth-rate ensembles and stiffness

The next cell solves the same logistic equation ($K = 100$, $y_0 = 2$, $T = 20$ h) for nine members with growth rates $r = 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, \dots, 1000$ h⁻¹.
Parameters are supplied as a $9 \times 1$ matrix of `Independent`; each member solves by passing `cx` to `advance` of `DormandPrince5`.
All members receive the same step limit $N_{\max}$.
Large $r$ makes the problem stiff through equilibrium $y = K$ Jacobian $f_y = -r$, restricting explicit steps to approximately $3.3/r$ for stability ([Error, order, and stability](../error-order-and-stability/)).
The table gives each member's terminal value, closed-form difference, accepted steps, and termination status.

```rust
//| id: ode-ensemble-growth-rates
//| ref: ode-ensemble-growth-rates
//| title: "Observe step-limit termination in an ensemble of growth rates"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [ode-logistic-sensitivity-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   step_limit: {"type":"range","label":"Step limit N_max [count]","description":"Attempted-step limit per member.","min":500,"max":20000,"step":500,"value":2000}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"generous","label":"N_max = 20000","description":"Every member reaches the endpoint.","inputs":{"step_limit":20000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"members","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"members","path":"/rows/8/5","equals":"Some(BudgetLimit(Proposal))"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: generous
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"members","path":"/rows/8/5","equals":"Some(ReachedEnd)"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}

let digits8 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 8 });

    { "key": "1", "label": "Last time [h]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Last y [10⁶/mL]", "type": "number", "format": digits8 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Terminal error [10⁶/mL]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Not reached" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Accepted steps [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination status" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "members" })
);
let outcome = if ensemble.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("All members reached the endpoint")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Some members stopped at the step limit")
        .explanation("Stopped members' values are at their last accepted points, not the endpoint.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!(
            "Successful {} / executed {}",
            ensemble.successful(),
            ensemble.completed()
        ))
        .diagnostic("Successful members", ensemble.successful() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Executed members", ensemble.completed() as f64, None)
);
```

At the default $N_{\max} = 2000$, seven members $r \le 100$ reach the endpoint; the two with $r = 300$ and $1000$ stop with `BudgetLimit(Proposal)`.
The ensemble report gives “successful 7, executed 9”; stopped members still have results and do not prevent others from running.
For $r \ge 10$, accepted steps are approximately proportional to $r$; reaching the endpoint for $r = 1000$ needs 6066 steps ($h \approx 3.3/r$).
Steps increase despite nearly constant post-equilibrium solutions because stability rather than accuracy determines step size; choose [Implicit methods](../implicit-runge-kutta-and-mass-matrices/) or stiffness-detecting [Automatic switching](../rosenbrock-and-stiffness-switching/) for stiff problems.
The two stopped members have different last times, 18.0 h and 5.41 h, but identical last y and accepted-step counts.
The logistic equation with growth rate $r$ becomes the $r = 1$ equation in time $rt$; under absolute tolerance, `DormandPrince5` selects the same steps with rescaled time, giving identical values after equal step counts.

## Summary

Forward sensitivity couples variational equations $s' = f_y s + f_p d$ with the solution in dimension $n(k + 1)$; `ForwardSensitivitySystem::bind` constructs an ordinary `OdeSystem` from `OdeSensitivitySystem`, solvable by any method.
Sensitivity components enter error control; use `error_tolerance` to match their absolute tolerances to their scales.
Cost grows approximately with direction count, with extra augmented-Jacobian difference costs for implicit methods; choose adjoints for many parameters and only gradients.
At terminal events, use `event_sensitivities_into` to convert fixed-time to event-time sensitivities, avoiding conversion when $|dg/dt|$ is small.
The function `solve_ensemble_into` solves many problems while retaining per-member results; assess success from `EnsembleReport` and each member's termination status.
