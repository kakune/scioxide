Long integration of conservative systems such as planetary orbits, molecular dynamics, or rigid-body rotation can gradually increase or decrease energy and distort trajectories despite small one-step errors.
Symplectic integrators preserve the exact flow's geometric structure in discrete maps, keeping energy error bounded over long times.
This page covers Hamiltonian symplecticity, modified-Hamiltonian error interpretation, Störmer–Verlet for separable Hamiltonians, and RATTLE for constrained systems.
The APIs are `solve_hamiltonian`, `StormerVerlet`, `Rattle`, and their state, settings, report, and workspace types.
[Implicit Runge–Kutta methods and mass matrices](../implicit-runge-kutta-and-mass-matrices/) covers implicit Gauss–Legendre (`GaussRk`) formulas; here we compare only long-term behavior.

## Hamiltonian systems and symplecticity

Generalized coordinates $q \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and canonical momenta $p \in \mathbb{R}^d$ evolve under Hamiltonian $H(q, p)$ according to

$$
q' = \nabla_p H(q, p), \qquad p' = -\nabla_q H(q, p)
$$


If $H$ is time-independent, $\frac{d}{dt} H = \nabla_q H \cdot q' + \nabla_p H \cdot p' = 0$, making $H$ (total energy) conserved.

Let $y = (q, p)$ and let $\Psi$ be the time-$h$ flow (mapping initial values to values after $h$).
The flow Jacobian $\Psi'$ satisfies

$$
\Psi'(y)^{\mathsf T}\, \mathcal{J}\, \Psi'(y) = \mathcal{J}, \qquad
\mathcal{J} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & I \\ -I & 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$


This property is called symplecticity.
For one degree of freedom, it is equivalent to preservation of phase-plane area under the flow.
A numerical method is symplectic if its one-step map $y_n \mapsto y_{n+1}$ satisfies the same equation.
Explicit Runge–Kutta and linear multistep methods are not symplectic.
Gauss–Legendre, Störmer–Verlet, and RATTLE are symplectic.

## Modified Hamiltonians and long-term energy error

One step of an order-$p$ numerical method differs from the original flow by $O(h^{p+1})$, but can agree much more closely with the flow of a slightly modified equation,

$$
\tilde y' = \tilde f(\tilde y) = f(\tilde y) + h^p f_{p+1}(\tilde y) + h^{p+1} f_{p+2}(\tilde y) + \cdots
$$


This is the modified equation; studying numerical errors through its properties is called backward error analysis.
For symplectic methods, the modified equation is also Hamiltonian, with

$$
\tilde H = H + h^p H_{p+1} + h^{p+1} H_{p+2} + \cdots
$$

called the modified Hamiltonian.
The series generally diverges, but truncation at an appropriate term lets the numerical solution preserve $\tilde H$ to error $O(e^{-c/h})$ over exponentially long time $t \le e^{c/h}$ (Hairer–Lubich–Wanner, §IX.8).
Thus $|H(y_n) - H(y_0)| \le |H - \tilde H| + |\tilde H(y_n) - \tilde H(y_0)| = O(h^p)$ holds over long times, and energy error oscillates within bounds.
Nonsymplectic methods introduce dissipative or source terms into modified equations, causing energy error to grow linearly with time.

This explanation assumes constant step size.
With steps adapted from error estimates, even symplectic individual maps no longer follow one modified Hamiltonian flow overall.
This is why scioxide-ode's `StormerVerlet`, `Rattle`, and `GaussRk` use fixed steps.
Even with bounded energy error, phase error (position along the trajectory) grows linearly with time; preserved energy does not imply an accurate trajectory.

## Störmer–Verlet

For a separable Hamiltonian split into kinetic and potential energies,

$$
H(q, p) = T(p) + V(q)
$$

the equations of motion are $q' = \nabla T(p)$ and $p' = -\nabla V(q)$.
One `StormerVerlet` step proceeds with a momentum half-step (kick), position full-step (drift), then momentum half-step.

$$
p_{n+1/2} = p_n - \frac{h}{2}\nabla V(q_n), \qquad
q_{n+1} = q_n + h\,\nabla T(p_{n+1/2}), \qquad
p_{n+1} = p_{n+1/2} - \frac{h}{2}\nabla V(q_{n+1}).
$$

Each stage is an exact flow of subsystem $H = V$ or $H = T$, so their composition is symplectic.
The formula is unchanged under time reversal (time-symmetric), explicit, and second-order.
Each step evaluates $\nabla T$ and $\nabla V$ once; reuse of the preceding final $\nabla V(q_{n+1})$ at the next start requires one extra $\nabla V(q_0)$ evaluation only on each call's first step.
For central-force problems without rotational terms, invariants of the form $p^{\mathsf T} D q$, such as angular momentum $q \times p$, are also preserved to within rounding error (Hairer–Lubich–Wanner, §IV.2).

For unit-mass $T(p) = |p|^2/2$, the first modified-Hamiltonian term is

$$
\tilde H = H + \frac{h^2}{12}\, p^{\mathsf T} \nabla^2 V(q)\, p - \frac{h^2}{24}\, |\nabla V(q)|^2 + O(h^4)
$$


Numerical solutions nearly preserve $\tilde H$ rather than $H$, making $H$ error oscillate at size $O(h^2)$, while $\tilde H$ error falls to $O(h^4)$.

### scioxide-ode APIs

The type `StormerVerlet` is a stateless unit struct, with problems supplied through `SeparableHamiltonian<T>`.
The trait provides position count `dimension()`, `velocity` writing $\nabla T(p)$, and `gradient` writing $\nabla V(q)$ (force with its sign reversed).
The constructor `HamiltonianFn::new(dimension, velocity, gradient)` builds this trait implementation from two closures.
Callbacks must write every output component; unwritten entries, NaN, or infinity give `NonFinite` (`Callback`).
For complex states, each component represents two real coordinates; return gradients with respect to those real and imaginary coordinates.

State `PhaseSpace<T>` holds positions $q$, momenta $p$, and time.
The constructor `PhaseSpace::new(position, momentum)` creates a state at time 0, returning `DimensionMismatch` for mismatched lengths or `NonFinite` (`Input`) for nonfinite components.
The second argument is canonical momentum $p$ rather than velocity $q'$.
Time advances by $h$ per accepted step, and successive `advance` calls continue one trajectory.

| Call style | Return value | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `solve_hamiltonian(velocity, gradient, q0, p0, step, steps)` | `PhaseSpace<T>` | Advance a closure-based system for `steps` steps and receive only the final state |
| `StormerVerlet.solve(&problem, step, steps, &options, initial)` | `MechanicalSolution<T>` | Receive final state and report |
| `StormerVerlet.advance(&problem, step, steps, &options, &mut state, &mut sink, &mut workspace, scope)` | `MechanicalReport` | Advance state in place, send every step's state to `sink`, and reuse workspace |

Step $h$ (`step`) is signed; negative values integrate backward.
Zero or nonfinite $h$, or $h/2$ rounding to zero, returns an error without changing state.
Step size is a call argument; `MechanicalOptions` configures only work limits.
Limits are `with_max_steps` (attempted steps), `with_max_iterations` (steps and RATTLE Newton iterations), and `with_max_evaluations` (gradients, velocities, constraints, applications of `M⁻¹`, factorizations, and solves).
The function `solve_hamiltonian` runs without limits; if not every step is accepted, it returns `PartialFailure` containing a report at the last accepted point.

The sink `MechanicalOutputSink<T>` receives `emit(step, position, momentum)` per accepted step.
The sink `()` discards output; a `FnMut(usize, &[T], &[T]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError>` closure can also be a sink.
Sink errors stop integration there, leaving that step accepted.

The report `MechanicalReport` returns termination status `termination()`, accepted steps `accepted_steps()`, time `final_time()`, evaluation counts `counts()` and `accounting()`, and final-state trust level `trust()` (`Admissible` for Störmer–Verlet).
The fields `position_residual()` and `momentum_residual()` give RATTLE constraint residuals, or `None` for Störmer–Verlet.
The value `MechanicalSolution` pairs final state `state()` with report `report()`.

Termination status `MechanicalTermination` includes `ReachedEnd` for all steps accepted, `BudgetLimit(kind)` for limit stops, `ExecutionStopped(reason)` for cancellation or deadline, `ProjectionLimit` for failed RATTLE constraints, and `StepSizeLimit` when adding a step no longer changes time.
Only `ReachedEnd` is `is_success()`; without events, `TerminalEvent` is never returned.
At limits, `advance` returns a report as `Ok`, leaving the last accepted step in state.

Workspace `StormerVerletWorkspace<T>` holds four vectors for positions, momenta, gradients, and velocities and is reusable for all same-dimension problems.
Allocate through `StormerVerlet.workspace(&problem)` or add capacity to `StormerVerletWorkspace::new()` through `try_reserve(&requirements)`.
Required capacity is `StormerVerletRequirements` returned by `workspace_requirements(&problem)`, where `dimension()` is the position count.
The method `check` returns whether capacity suffices (`Capacity`); `last_attempt` returns the latest call record.
With sufficient capacity and nonallocating callbacks and sinks, `advance` does not allocate.

### Long-term pendulum integration

For a pendulum of length $L = 1$ m and mass 1 kg, angle $q$ [rad] from downward vertical and angular momentum $p$ give, with gravitational acceleration $g = 9.81$ m/s²,

$$
H(q, p) = \frac{p^2}{2} - g \cos q
$$

in joules.
The following cell releases the pendulum from rest at amplitude $q_0$, integrating with common step $h$ through time $T$ using `StormerVerlet` (`advance` and a sink), `ClassicalRk4`, and `GaussRk`.
The same system is supplied to `ClassicalRk4` and `GaussRk` as `OdeFn` of $y = (q, p)$; `GaussRk` stages are solved to tolerance $10^{-12}$.
The logarithmic plot shows $|H(y_n) - H(y_0)|$, adding dashed modified-Hamiltonian error $|\tilde H(y_n) - \tilde H(y_0)|$ for Störmer–Verlet.
Subsample each method to at most 2000 plotted points.
The table shows maximum and terminal energy errors, evaluations per step, and termination statuses.
Evaluation counts combine $\nabla T$ and $\nabla V$ for Störmer–Verlet and count right-hand side $f$ for the others.

Modified-Hamiltonian error is at most $3.4 \times 10^{-3}$ J, about one-fifty-fifth of $|\Delta H|$.
Classical RK4's $|\Delta H|$ grows with time to terminal $0.33$ J.
At $h = 0.05$ s, maximum Störmer–Verlet $|\Delta H|$ is $0.047$ J (about one-quarter) and modified-Hamiltonian error $2.1 \times 10^{-4}$ J (about one-sixteenth), matching $O(h^2)$ and $O(h^4)$ in the $\tilde H$ formula.
At the same $h = 0.05$ s, RK4 error is $0.011$ J, below Störmer–Verlet over 500 s.
Fourth-order RK4 error depends strongly on step size; its ranking against Störmer–Verlet changes with step and integration duration.
Over long times, differences widen because RK4 error grows linearly in time while Störmer–Verlet error does not grow.
The $|\Delta H|$ of `GaussRk` is at most $1.2 \times 10^{-4}$ J, smallest as a fourth-order symplectic method; however, 11.8 right-hand-side evaluations per step are about six times Störmer–Verlet's 2.0.

## Holonomic constraints and RATTLE

Systems with $m$ position constraints $g(q) = 0$ (pendulum rods, molecular bond lengths, multibody joints) may need solving in Cartesian coordinates rather than independent coordinates.
Constraining Hamiltonian $H = p^{\mathsf T} M^{-1} p/2 + V(q)$ with mass matrix $M$ (constant, symmetric positive definite) gives equations

$$
q' = M^{-1} p, \qquad p' = -\nabla V(q) - G(q)^{\mathsf T} \lambda, \qquad g(q) = 0, \qquad G(q) = \frac{\partial g}{\partial q}
$$

where Lagrange multipliers $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^m$ determine constraint-force magnitudes.
Differentiating $g(q(t)) = 0$ in time gives hidden constraint $G(q) M^{-1} p = 0$ (velocity tangent to the constraint surface).
If $G(q)$ has rank $m$ near the solution, this is an index-3 DAE.

One `Rattle` step adds two projections to Störmer–Verlet.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{n+1/2} &= p_n - \tfrac{h}{2}\nabla V(q_n) - G(q_n)^{\mathsf T}\lambda, \\
q_{n+1} &= q_n + h M^{-1} p_{n+1/2}, \qquad g(q_{n+1}) = 0, \\
p_{n+1} &= p_{n+1/2} - \tfrac{h}{2}\nabla V(q_{n+1}) - G(q_{n+1})^{\mathsf T}\mu, \qquad G(q_{n+1}) M^{-1} p_{n+1} = 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Position constraints are nonlinear in $\lambda$ and solved with Newton.
Each iteration factors $G(\tilde q) M^{-1} G(q_n)^{\mathsf T}$ at current position $\tilde q$ by LU; the iteration limit is `RattleOptions::with_newton_iterations` (default 10).
Momentum constraints are linear in $\mu$ and solved by $LDL^{\mathsf T}$ factorization of $G(q_{n+1}) M^{-1} G(q_{n+1})^{\mathsf T}$.
The method is second-order, symplectic on the constraint surface, and time-symmetric (Hairer–Lubich–Wanner, §VII.1).
Each step applies $M^{-1}$ $2m + 3$ times, evaluates $\nabla V$, $g$, and $G$ once each, and evaluates $g$ and $G$ once per Newton iteration; linear algebra costs $O(m^2 d + m^3)$ per iteration.
Each call initially evaluates the initial state's $g$, $G$, $M^{-1} p$, and $\nabla V(q_0)$.

Step acceptance uses residuals.
Accept if $\|g(q_{n+1})\|_\infty$ and $\|G(q_{n+1}) M^{-1} p_{n+1}\|_\infty$ are each at most their threshold $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}} \cdot s$.
The caller supplies scale $s$; the threshold has units of $g$.
Defaults use the default `ResidualTolerance` ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}} = \sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$), with scale 1.
A step failing thresholds is rejected; the call ends with `ProjectionLimit`, leaving the last accepted step in state.
At call start, check both initial-state thresholds; failure returns `InvalidInput`.
The initial state is not projected onto the constraint surface, so the caller must construct consistent values.

### scioxide-ode APIs

Supply the system through `HolonomicConstraints<S>`.
The trait provides position count `dimension()`, constraint count `constraints()` ($m \le d$), `gradient` writing $\nabla V(q)$, `values` writing $g(q)$, and `jacobian` writing $G(q)$ of $m \times d$ rowwise.
States must be real.
The problem `RattleProblem::new(&system, &inverse_mass)` borrows the system and `LinearOperator` applying $M^{-1}$.
The matrix $M^{-1}$ must be symmetric positive definite; nonpositive-definite $G M^{-1} G^{\mathsf T}$ gives `InvalidInput`, and singularity gives `Singular`.

The settings `RattleOptions` configure work limits `with_integration(MechanicalOptions)`, position tolerance and scale `with_position_tolerance(tolerance, scale)`, momentum tolerance and scale `with_momentum_tolerance(tolerance, scale)`, and Newton iteration limit `with_newton_iterations`.
Setters do not validate; `advance` validates them with the step size.
Negative scales or zero thresholds give `InvalidInput`; nonfinite scales give `NonFinite`.
A zero iteration limit accepts only steps whose unprojected positions meet the threshold.

Calls follow `StormerVerlet`, with `Rattle.solve(&problem, step, steps, &options, initial)` and `Rattle.advance(...)`.
Workspace `RattleWorkspace<S>` holds stage vectors, $G$ and $M^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}$, two factorizations, and workspace for $M^{-1}$, retaining $O(md + m^2)$ values.
The requirements `RattleRequirements` return `dimension()`, `constraints()`, and `operator_scratch()` (workspace length for $M^{-1}$).
Report `trust()` is `ResidualChecked`; `position_residual()` and `momentum_residual()` are $\|g(q)\|_\infty$ and $\|G M^{-1} p\|_\infty$ at the last accepted state.

### Cartesian pendulum

Writing the same pendulum in Cartesian coordinates $q = (x, y)$ ($y$ upward, in meters) gives $M = I$, $V(q) = g y$, and constraint

$$
g_{\mathrm c}(q) = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - L^2}{2} = 0, \qquad G(q) = (x, \; y)
$$

(denoted $g_{\mathrm c}$ to distinguish it from gravitational acceleration $g$).
For comparison, differentiate the constraint twice and eliminate the multiplier, solving the resulting ODE

$$
x'' = -\lambda x, \qquad y'' = -g - \lambda y, \qquad \lambda = \frac{x'^2 + y'^2 - g y}{L^2}
$$

with `DormandPrince5` (tolerance $10^{-6}$).
Its exact solution remains on the constraint surface for consistent initial values, but numerical deviations have no restoring force, so $|g_{\mathrm c}|$ grows with time (drift-off).
The next cell releases from rest at amplitude $\theta_0$, solving `Rattle` with step $h$ and threshold $10^{-12}$, and the comparison ODE through the same time $T$.
The upper plot shows $|g_{\mathrm c}(q_n)|$ and the lower energy error $|H(q_n, p_n) - H_0|$, with linked horizontal axes.
Residuals are computed from each step's state in the sink, independently checking report values.
The table gives maximum $|g_{\mathrm c}|$, maximum $|G M^{-1} p|$, maximum energy error, step count, termination status, and mean RATTLE Newton iterations per step.

```rust
//| id: ode-rattle-pendulum
//| ref: ode-rattle-pendulum
//| title: "Compare Cartesian-pendulum constraint residuals with RATTLE and a constraint-eliminated ODE"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   rattle_amplitude: {"type":"range","label":"Amplitude θ₀ [rad]","description":"Initial angle from downward vertical.","min":0.1,"max":3,"step":0.1,"value":1.5}
//|   rattle_step: {"type":"range","label":"Step size h [s]","description":"Fixed RATTLE step.","min":0.01,"max":0.1,"step":0.01,"value":0.05}
//|   rattle_span: {"type":"range","label":"Integration duration T [s]","description":"Duration over which to track constraint residual drift.","min":20,"max":200,"step":20,"value":100}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"near-top","label":"θ₀ = 3 rad","description":"Near-inverted amplitudes make the constraint-eliminated ODE's residual grow faster.","inputs":{"rattle_amplitude":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"constraints","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"constraints","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"constraints","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":1e-8}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: near-top
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"constraints","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{
    Budget, ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, NormSelection, ResidualTolerance,
    SolveContext,
};
use scioxide_linalg::{Matrix, MatrixViewMut, Vector};
use scioxide_ode::{
    DormandPrince5, HolonomicConstraints, OdeFn, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule,
    OdeProblem, OdeState, PhaseSpace, Rattle, RattleOptions, RattleProblem,
};

/// Pendulum of length L and mass 1: V = g y, g_c(q) = (x² + y² − L²)/2, G = (x, y).
struct Pendulum {
    gravity: f64,
    length: f64,
}
impl HolonomicConstraints<f64> for Pendulum {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn constraints(&self) -> usize {
        1
    }
    fn gradient(
        &self,
        _: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        out[0] = 0.0;
        out[1] = self.gravity;
        Ok(())
    }
    fn values(
        &self,
        q: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        out[0] = 0.5 * (q[0] * q[0] + q[1] * q[1] - self.length * self.length);
        Ok(())
    }
    fn jacobian(
        &self,
        q: &[f64],
        out: &mut MatrixViewMut<'_, f64>,
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        out.set(0, 0, q[0])?;
        out.set(0, 1, q[1])
    }
}

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let (gravity, length) = (9.81_f64, 1.0_f64);
let h = rattle_step;
let steps = (rattle_span / h).round() as usize;
let end = steps as f64 * h;
let start = [
    length * rattle_amplitude.sin(),
    -length * rattle_amplitude.cos(),
];
let energy0 = gravity * start[1];
// Each state's |g_c(q)|, |G M⁻¹ p|, and |H − H₀| (independent checks).
let residuals = move |q: &[f64], p: &[f64]| {
    [
        (0.5 * (q[0] * q[0] + q[1] * q[1] - length * length)).abs(),
        (q[0] * p[0] + q[1] * p[1]).abs(),
        (0.5 * (p[0] * p[0] + p[1] * p[1]) + gravity * q[1] - energy0).abs(),
    ]
};
let stride = steps.div_ceil(2000);

let pendulum = Pendulum { gravity, length };
let identity = Matrix::identity(2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let inverse_mass = identity.view().try_finite().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = RattleProblem::new(&pendulum, &inverse_mass);
let tolerance = ResidualTolerance::new(1e-12, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = RattleOptions::new()
    .with_position_tolerance(tolerance, 1.0)
    .with_momentum_tolerance(tolerance, 1.0);
let mut state = PhaseSpace::new(start.to_vec(), vec![0.0, 0.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = Rattle.workspace(&problem).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut rattle_points, mut rattle_worst) = (Vec::new(), [0.0_f64; 3]);
let report = Rattle
    .advance(
        &problem,
        h,
        steps,
        &options,
        &mut state,
        &mut |n: usize, q: &[f64], p: &[f64]| {
            let r = residuals(q, p);
            (0..3).for_each(|k| rattle_worst[k] = rattle_worst[k].max(r[k]));
            if n % stride == 0 {
                rattle_points.push((n as f64 * h, r));
            }
            Ok(())
        },
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let counts = report.counts();
let newton =
    (counts.iterations() - report.accepted_steps()) as f64 / report.accepted_steps() as f64;

// Comparison: ODE eliminating the multiplier after differentiating constraints twice, y = (x, y, x′, y′).
let reduced = OdeFn::new(4, move |_: f64, y: &[f64], dydt: &mut [f64]| {
    let lambda = (y[2] * y[2] + y[3] * y[3] - gravity * y[1]) / (length * length);
    dydt[0] = y[2];
    dydt[1] = y[3];
    dydt[2] = -lambda * y[0];
    dydt[3] = -gravity - lambda * y[1];
    Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
})
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let ode_tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-6, 1e-6, NormSelection::RootMeanSquare)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let ode_options = OdeOptions::new()
    .with_error_tolerance(ode_tolerance)
    .with_max_steps(Budget::try_from(50_000_usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
    .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::AcceptedSteps);
let ode_problem = OdeProblem::new(&reduced, end);
let method = DormandPrince5::new();
let mut ode_workspace = method
    .workspace(&ode_problem, &ode_options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut ode_state = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![start[0], start[1], 0.0, 0.0]));
let (mut ode_all, mut ode_worst) = (Vec::new(), [0.0_f64; 3]);
let ode_report = method
    .advance(
        &ode_problem,
        &ode_options,
        &mut ode_state,
        &mut |t: f64, y: &[f64]| {
            let r = residuals(&y[..2], &y[2..]);
            (0..3).for_each(|k| ode_worst[k] = ode_worst[k].max(r[k]));
            ode_all.push((t, r));
            Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
        },
        &mut ode_workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let ode_stride = ode_all.len().div_ceil(2000).max(1);
let ode_points: Vec<_> = ode_all.into_iter().step_by(ode_stride).collect();

let rows = vec![
    (
        "RATTLE",
        rattle_worst[0],
        rattle_worst[1],
        rattle_worst[2],
        report.accepted_steps(),
        report
            .termination()
            .map_or_else(|| "None".to_string(), |t| format!("{t:?}")),
        Some(newton),
    ),
    (
        "Constraint-eliminated ODE (DP5)",
        ode_worst[0],
        ode_worst[1],
        ode_worst[2],
        ode_report.accepted_steps(),
        ode_report
            .termination()
            .map_or_else(|| "None".to_string(), |t| t.to_string()),
        None,
    ),
];
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum |g_c(q)| [m²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum |G M⁻¹ p| [m²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum |ΔH| [J]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Accepted steps", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Newton iterations / step", "type": "number", "format": fixed2, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "constraints" })
);
// Omit zero values from logarithmic axes.
let curve = |points: &[(f64, [f64; 3])], k: usize| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    points
        .iter()
        .filter(|(_, r)| r[k] > 0.0)
        .map(|(t, r)| [*t, r[k]])
        .collect()
};
for (k, label) in [
    (0, "Constraint residual |g_c(q)| [m²]"),
    (2, "Energy error |ΔH| [J]"),
] {
    emit_line_chart!(
        &vec![
            serde_json::json!({
                "name": "RATTLE",
                "points": curve(&rattle_points, k),
                "style": { "showSymbol": false }
            }),
            serde_json::json!({
                "name": "Constraint-eliminated ODE (DP5)",
                "points": curve(&ode_points, k),
                "style": { "showSymbol": false }
            })
        ],
        options = &serde_json::json!({
            "link": { "group": "rattle-time" },
            "xLabel": "Time t [s]",
            "yLabel": label,
            "yType": "log"
        })
    );
}

let termination = report.termination();
let outcome = if report.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("RATTLE met constraint thresholds at every step")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("RATTLE did not reach the final step")
        .explanation("Use states through the last accepted step and reconsider step size or Newton iteration limits.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(termination.map_or_else(|| "None".to_string(), |t| format!("{t:?}")))
        .diagnostic("Maximum |g_c|", rattle_worst[0], Some("m²"))
        .diagnostic("Newton iterations / step", newton, None)
);
```

With defaults $\theta_0 = 1.5$ rad, $h = 0.05$ s, and $T = 100$ s, RATTLE's $|g_{\mathrm c}|$ stays at most $1.0 \times 10^{-12}$ m² for every step, and $|G M^{-1} p|$ is $4.4 \times 10^{-16}$ m²/s, satisfying threshold $10^{-12}$ and terminating with `ReachedEnd`.
Position-projection Newton iterations average 2.9 per step.
Maximum energy error is $0.082$ J, oscillating within bounds as a second-order method with step $0.05$ s.
The constraint-eliminated ODE uses Dormand–Prince at tolerance $10^{-6}$ and has small energy error $9.6 \times 10^{-3}$ J, but $|g_{\mathrm c}|$ grows to $5.6 \times 10^{-6}$ m², producing a rising line in the upper plot.
At $\theta_0 = 3$ rad, that ODE departs substantially from the constraint surface, shrinks steps, and stops with `BudgetLimit(Proposal)` at the 50,000-step limit.
RATTLE maintains $|g_{\mathrm c}| \le 9.9 \times 10^{-13}$ m² under the same conditions.
Differentiating away constraints does not restore them in the equations, so long integrations need residual projection or DAE solvers.

## Comparison with Gauss–Legendre

The method `GaussRk` is two-stage fourth-order Gauss–Legendre, a symplectic implicit Runge–Kutta method.
It supports nonseparable Hamiltonians and preserves all quadratic invariants, beyond forms $p^{\mathsf T} D q$ such as angular momentum, to within rounding error provided stage equations are solved.
In return, each step solves nonlinear equations with twice the state dimension in Newton, making per-step work much greater than Störmer–Verlet.
For the pendulum above, `GaussRk` used more right-hand-side evaluations per step than Störmer–Verlet's two evaluations of $\nabla T$ and $\nabla V$.

| Property | `StormerVerlet` | `Rattle` | `GaussRk` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applies to | Separable $H = T(p) + V(q)$ | $H = p^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}p/2 + V(q)$ and $g(q) = 0$ | General $y' = f(t, y)$ |
| Implicit? | Explicit | Newton only for position constraints | Newton stage solves |
| Order | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| Preserved quantities | Approximate preservation of $\tilde H$; exact preservation of quadratic quantities of form $p^{\mathsf T}Dq$ | Constraint residuals below thresholds | Quadratic invariants exactly |
| API | `solve_hamiltonian`, `StormerVerlet` | `Rattle` | `OdeMethod`（`GaussRk::new()`） |

All three use fixed steps, without dense output or event detection.
Since `GaussRk` is `OdeMethod`, use right-hand side `OdeSystem`, fixed step `OdeOptions`, and `OdeSolveReport`.
The methods `StormerVerlet` and `Rattle` return `MechanicalReport` and do not use `OdeOptions`.
Use Störmer–Verlet when energy preservation matters and force evaluation is costly, as in molecular dynamics; use Gauss when both high accuracy and long-term stability are needed.
For problems requiring variable steps (such as near-collision celestial trajectories), give up symplecticity and use a high-order adaptive method (`DormandPrince853`) with tight tolerances.

## Summary

Hamiltonian flows are symplectic; symplectic numerical solutions nearly preserve modified Hamiltonian $\tilde H$, so energy error keeps oscillating at magnitude $O(h^p)$ over long times.
The method `StormerVerlet` is explicit and second-order for separable Hamiltonians, evaluating $\nabla T$ and $\nabla V$ once per step; obtain only the final state with `solve_hamiltonian`, or each step's state using `advance` and a sink.
The method `Rattle` enforces position and momentum constraints with Newton and linear projection, accepting steps by comparing residuals with thresholds.
Symplecticity determines long-term fixed-step behavior, but phase error keeps growing; verify trajectories against known solutions or comparisons at different steps.
