For stiff systems arising in chemical reactions (combustion, Oregonator), circuit transients, and spatially semidiscrete PDEs (method of lines), explicit methods require small steps for stability ([Stiffness](../error-order-and-stability/)).
Implicit Runge–Kutta trades coupled stage solves at each step for stable integration with larger steps.

The crate `scioxide-ode` provides implicit Runge–Kutta methods `BackwardEuler`, `ImplicitTrapezoid`, `Sdirk`, `GaussRk`, and `RadauIia`, all handling $M y' = f(t, y)$ with constant nonsingular mass matrices.
Only `RadauIia` is adaptive, with error estimation, dense output, and event detection.
The other four use the fixed step supplied through `OdeOptions::with_initial_step` (or an estimated initial step if absent), without error estimates, dense output, or event detection.
This page explains stage equations and Newton iteration, factorization reuse, collocation construction, SDIRK, mass matrices, and replacing stage solvers.
[Jacobians and linear stage equations](../jacobians-and-linear-stages/) covers Jacobian evaluation and linear-solver selection.
Other adaptive stiff methods are `Bdf` ([Linear multistep methods](../multistep-methods/)) and `RosenbrockW` ([Rosenbrock methods and automatic stiffness switching](../rosenbrock-and-stiffness-switching/)).

## Stage equations and iteration matrices

An $s$-stage Runge–Kutta method is implicit when coefficient matrix $A = (a_{ij})$ has nonzero entries on or above the diagonal.
With mass matrix $M$, stage values $Y_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ satisfy

$$
M\,(Y_i - y_n) = h \sum_{j=1}^s a_{ij}\, f(t_n + c_j h,\, Y_j), \qquad i = 1, \dots, s
$$


Combining stages gives the $sd$-dimensional nonlinear system $\mathcal{R}(Y) = 0$ with Jacobian

$$
\frac{\partial \mathcal{R}_i}{\partial Y_j} = \delta_{ij} M - h\, a_{ij}\, J_j, \qquad J_j = \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(t_n + c_j h,\, Y_j)
$$


Using the same $J$ for all stages gives the Newton iteration matrix in Kronecker-product form

$$
E = I_s \otimes M - h\, A \otimes J
$$


For fully implicit methods (`GaussRk`, `RadauIia`), dense LU of this $sd \times sd$ matrix costs $O((sd)^3)$ per factorization.
The method `GaussRk` solves both stages as one $2d \times 2d$ system.
The method `RadauIia` transforms stage variables using eigenvalues of $A$, separating one real $d \times d$ system $M - h\gamma J$ and one complex $d \times d$ system, reducing factorization cost to about one-fifth of a $3d \times 3d$ system (three complex systems for complex states).

### Newton convergence checks

Let the Newton correction at iteration $k$ be $\Delta_k$ and the contraction factor $\theta = \|\Delta_k\| / \|\Delta_{k-1}\|$.
The norm is normalized by tolerance scale $s_i = \mathrm{atol}_i + \mathrm{rtol} \cdot \max(|x_i|, |y_{n,i}|)$, then measured in units of one-tenth of tolerance.
The iteration is considered divergent if $\theta \ge 1$ and converged if $\theta/(1 - \theta) \cdot \|\Delta_k\| \le 1$ (Hairer and Wanner II §IV.8).
This estimates the remaining error after convergence as $\theta/(1-\theta)\,\|\Delta_k\|$, requiring at most one-tenth of the local error tolerance.

On stage-iteration failure or a singular iteration matrix, adaptive `RadauIia` rejects the step and retries at half size.
Fixed-step methods cannot change size, so they stop and return the error (such as `ConvergenceFailure` or `Singular`).
Tolerances near rounding-error magnitude can make stage residuals rounding-limited and prevent convergence checks from passing, causing fixed-step methods to return `ConvergenceFailure`.

### Simplified and full Newton

The policy `NewtonMode` selects when to rebuild the Jacobian and iteration-matrix factorization.

| `NewtonMode` | Contents |
|---|---|
| `Simplified` (default) | Retain and reuse Jacobians and factorizations across iterations and steps |
| `Full` | Recompute the Jacobian and factor the iteration matrix for every correction |

The methods `BackwardEuler`, `ImplicitTrapezoid`, `Sdirk`, and `GaussRk` select either through `with_newton_mode`.
The method `RadauIia` always uses simplified Newton, as do iterations with replacement stage solvers.

Simplified Newton rebuilds the Jacobian when:

- Time or any state component $x$ moves by more than $0.5 \max(1, |x_{\text{old}}|, |x_{\text{new}}|)$ from the previous Jacobian evaluation point.
  This check is skipped for systems with true `OdeSystem::jacobian_is_constant`.
- More than two corrections are needed with the current factorization and the contraction factor exceeds 0.3.
- Iteration fails.
  First rebuild only the factorization; if failure persists, rebuild the Jacobian before rejecting the step.

Coupled systems of at least two stages reuse factorizations after step changes when the effective step ratio lies in $[0.8, 1.2]$; residuals always use the current step's equations.
The factorization and error estimate of `RadauIia` are always rebuilt for the current step.
Reuse is confined to one `advance` call and assumes the same differentiation policy (`DerivativeStrategy`).
With an analytical constant Jacobian and a current direct-solver factorization, fixed-step methods solve each stage with one correction.

Full Newton converges quadratically, reducing iteration counts for strongly nonlinear problems but requiring Jacobian evaluation and factorization per correction.
The [Replacing stage solvers](#replacing-stage-solvers) cell compares their evaluation and factorization counts.

## Gauss and Radau IIA collocation

Collocation determines a degree-$s$ polynomial $u(t)$ on $[t_n, t_{n+1}]$ satisfying differential equation $u'(t_n + c_i h) = f(t_n + c_i h, u(t_n + c_i h))$ at $s$ collocation points $t_n + c_i h$ and initial condition $u(t_n) = y_n$.
With Lagrange basis polynomials $\ell_j(\tau)$ at points $c_1, \dots, c_s$, the Butcher coefficients are

$$
a_{ij} = \int_0^{c_i} \ell_j(\tau)\, d\tau, \qquad b_j = \int_0^1 \ell_j(\tau)\, d\tau
$$


Method order follows the quadrature rule at those points; stage order, the approximation order of stage values $Y_i$, is $s$.

### Gauss methods

Gauss methods choose Gauss–Legendre quadrature nodes on $(0, 1)$ as collocation points.
The method `GaussRk` has two stages, points $c_{1,2} = 1/2 \mp \sqrt{3}/6$, and order $2s = 4$ (the highest attainable by an $s$-stage Runge–Kutta method).
It is A-stable but not L-stable because $R(-\infty) = 1$.
Gauss methods are symplectic; fixed-step integration with converged stage equations preserves the symplectic structure of Hamiltonian flows.
Nonquadratic invariants such as energy are not preserved exactly, but their errors oscillate within bounded ranges rather than growing continually over long times.
Quadratic invariants, such as harmonic-oscillator energy, are preserved to stage-solve accuracy.
[Geometric integrators](../geometric-integrators/) covers long-term Hamiltonian integration.

### Radau IIA

Radau IIA chooses Radau quadrature nodes including endpoint $c_s = 1$ as collocation points.
The method `RadauIia` has three stages, points $c_1 = (4 - \sqrt{6})/10$, $c_2 = (4 + \sqrt{6})/10$, and $c_3 = 1$, order $2s - 1 = 5$, and stage order 3.
With $c_3 = 1$, the final stage equals new state $y_{n+1} = Y_3$ (stiffly accurate), giving $R(-\infty) = 0$ and L-stability.

The method `RadauIia` advances steps as follows.

- Solve stage equations with simplified Newton using a shared Jacobian for all stages.
  Each iteration evaluates $f$ at all three stages, with at most seven iterations per step.
- Estimate local error from the difference with a third-order embedded formula, filtering through the real iteration matrix $M - h\gamma J$ to remain bounded for stiff components.
  The estimate scales as $h^4$ and does not bound fifth-order solution error.
  On the first step or immediately after rejection, filter estimates exceeding tolerance again, requiring one extra $f$ evaluation.
- Select step size with Gustafsson predictive control, also using Newton iteration counts ([Radau IIA predictive control](oxref:ode-step-controllers)).
- Use the accepted-step cubic collocation polynomial for dense output, event detection, and initial stage predictions for the next step.

The method `RadauIia` cannot solve DAEs with singular mass matrices; use `DaeBdf` from [Index-1 DAEs](../dae-index-one/).

## SDIRK

To avoid the coupled $sd$-dimensional system of fully implicit methods, singly diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta (SDIRK) makes $A$ lower triangular with shared diagonal value $a_{ii} = \gamma$.
Each stage can be solved sequentially as an $d$-dimensional system, with the same iteration matrix for all stages,

$$
M - h\gamma J
$$


The method `Sdirk` is Alexander's three-stage third-order L-stable method; $\gamma \approx 0.435866521508459$ is the root of $6\gamma^3 - 18\gamma^2 + 9\gamma - 1 = 0$ giving L-stability.
Its stiffly accurate coefficients make the final stage equal the solution; simplified Newton reuses one factorization of $d \times d$ for all three stages.
Stage order is 1, so strongly stiff problems can reduce observed order ([Observed order by step halving](oxref:ode-step-halving)).

The method `BackwardEuler` is single-stage, first-order, and L-stable; `ImplicitTrapezoid` (Crank–Nicolson) is second-order and A-stable but not L-stable.
Since implicit trapezoidal has $R(-\infty) = -1$, stiff components alternate sign each step without damping.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BackwardEuler` | 1 | 1 | Fixed | L-stable | None | Supported |
| `ImplicitTrapezoid` | 1 (implicit) | 2 | Fixed | A-stable | None | Supported |
| `Sdirk` | 3 | 3 | Fixed | L-stable | None | Supported |

Mass matrices require the default dense LU solver.

## Equations with mass matrices

Finite-element and electrical circuit models produce systems $M y' = f(t, y)$ with constant mass matrices $M \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$.
Computing $M^{-1}$ and reformulating as $y' = M^{-1} f$ destroys sparsity and increases rounding error when $M$ is ill-conditioned.

Implicit methods construct iteration matrices directly as $M - h\gamma J$ (a matrix pencil), without forming $M^{-1}$.
Supply a mass matrix through the revision returned by `OdeSystem::mass_matrix_revision` and through `OdeSystem::mass_matrix`.
Revision `None` (default) means identity; the same matrix must be written while `Some(r)`.
A changed revision causes implicit methods to read the new matrix at the next step.
Calls to `mass_matrix` count as `LinearOperator` evaluations.
Mass matrices must be nonsingular; sparse LU and Krylov solvers permit only identity (otherwise `InvalidInput` is returned when reading the matrix at the first step).
Handle singular $M$ through residual-form DAEs (`DaeSystem`).
Explicit methods do not support mass matrices; a system returning a revision gives `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).

The next cell solves $m\,y' = -100\,y$, $y(0) = 1$ through $60h$ with supplied mass matrix $M = [m]$ using four implicit methods.
Inputs are mass coefficient $m$ (timescale $m/100$ s) and step $h$, together determining $z = -100h/m$.
The method `RadauIia` starts with $h$ and adjusts steps to tolerances; the other three use fixed steps.

```rust
    system: &MassDecay,
    h: f64,

        .with_initial_step(h)
        .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::AcceptedSteps);
    let problem = OdeProblem::new(system, 60.0 * h);
    let mut workspace = method
        .workspace(&problem, &options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut state = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![1.0]));

        .advance(
            &problem,
            &options,
            &mut state,
            &mut path,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),

        .times()
        .iter()
        .zip(path.states())
        .map(|(&t, y)| [t, y.as_slice()[0]])
        .collect();
    Ok((points, report.termination()))
```

## Replacing stage solvers

With a suitable linear or nonlinear solver, pass `OdeStageSolver` through `OdeProblem::with_stage_solver` to replace implicit Runge–Kutta stage solving.
Only `BackwardEuler`, `ImplicitTrapezoid`, `Sdirk`, `GaussRk`, and `RadauIia` accept replacement; other methods return `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).

| `OdeStageSolver` | Contents |
|---|---|
| `Native` (default) | Use the method's own Newton iteration and linear solver |
| `Linear(&solver)` | Solve only the simplified-Newton linear system $E c = r$ with `StageLinearSolver` |
| `Nonlinear(&solver)` | Solve the entire nonlinear stage iteration with `NonlinearStageSolver` |

The solver `StageLinearSolver::solve_into(operator, state, residual, correction, accuracy, cx)` receives iteration matrix $E$ as matrix-free operator `operator`, writing correction $c$ into `correction`.
Apply `operator` with `LinearOperator::apply_into_scoped` under `cx`.
Replacement solvers count their own work: call `StageAccuracy::admit_iteration` before each iteration and `StageAccuracy::admit_provider` before each operator application (with roles such as `EvaluationRole::LinearOperator`).
Returning `Ok(())` claims convergence; the integrator applies the operator once per correction to check residuals, rejecting corrections exceeding stage linear-system tolerance.
If inner iteration fails, return an error such as `ConvergenceFailure` or `Singular`; the integrator propagates it unchanged.
Prepare workspace before integration; one application of `operator` needs `apply_workspace_len()` workspace values.

The solver `NonlinearStageSolver::solve_in_place(residual_fn, operator, state, residual, correction, accuracy, cx)` solves stage equations from initial values `state`, writing the solution to `state`.
The callback `residual_fn` evaluates stage residuals; `operator` is iteration matrix $E$.
Use `StageAccuracy::correction_norm` for convergence (correction norm in units of one-tenth of tolerance; at most 1 is the integrator's own Newton stopping test).
The returned `NonlinearStageReport::new(iterations, linear_solves, factorizations)` gives iteration, linear-solve, and factorization counts, added to the integrator's totals.
Returning `Ok` claims convergence to requested accuracy; the integrator checks only finite states.
A zero-iteration report requires an exactly zero residual.
Errors from replacement solvers do not cause even `RadauIia` to retry with smaller steps.

The value `StageAccuracy` holds the step-start state, tolerances, and iteration limit for one stage solve.
The method `component_scale(index, candidate)` returns component scale $s_i = \mathrm{atol}_i + \mathrm{rtol} \cdot \max(|y_{n,i}|, |\mathrm{candidate}|)$; `iteration_limit()` returns the iteration limit.
For coupled methods with at least two stages, vectors concatenate one state vector per stage; `correction_norm` returns the maximum separately normalized stage norm.
With replacement solvers, the integrator forms no stage factorizations (`RadauIia` still factorizes the real matrix for error estimation).

The following cell solves the $d = 8$-component nonlinear stiff system with diagonal Jacobian,

$$
y_i' = -k_i\, y_i - y_i^3, \qquad y_i(0) = 1, \qquad k_i = 10^{K i/7} \quad (i = 0, \dots, 7)
$$

using `Sdirk` over $[0, 1]$.
The exact solution is $y_i(t) = \bigl((1 + 1/k_i)\, e^{2k_i t} - 1/k_i\bigr)^{-1/2}$.
Compare simplified Newton, full Newton, and `StageLinearSolver` exploiting the diagonal iteration matrix.
The replacement solver applies $E$ once to an all-ones vector to extract its diagonal, then computes corrections with $c_i = r_i / E_{ii}$ (unsuitable for nondiagonal $E$).
Inputs are step $h$ and stiffness exponent $K$; the table gives terminal error and counts of Jacobian evaluations, factorizations, Newton corrections (linear solves), operator applications, and right-hand-side evaluations.

```rust
//| tests:

//|       - {"artifact":"work","path":"/rows/2/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"work","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":100}}
//|   - case: stiff
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"work","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-6}}
use scioxide_core::{
    ErrorTolerance, EvaluationRole, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, Invocation, LinearOperator,
    NormSelection, OperatorContext, SolveContext,
};
use scioxide_linalg::{MatrixViewMut, Vector};

use std::cell::RefCell;

// Apply diagonal iteration matrix E to an all-ones vector to obtain its diagonal, then solve E c = r.
struct DiagonalSolver {
    ones: Vec<f64>,
    diagonal: RefCell<Vec<f64>>,
}
impl StageLinearSolver<f64> for DiagonalSolver {
    fn solve_into(
        &self,
        operator: &dyn LinearOperator<f64>,
        _: &[f64],
        residual: &[f64],
        correction: &mut [f64],
        accuracy: StageAccuracy<'_, f64>,
        cx: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let mut diagonal = self.diagonal.borrow_mut();
        // Count operator applications yourself before applying them.
        accuracy.admit_provider(EvaluationRole::LinearOperator, cx)?;
        operator
            .apply_into_scoped(
                &self.ones,
                &mut diagonal,
                &mut [],
                &mut OperatorContext::new(cx),
            )
            .and_then(Invocation::require)?;
        for ((c, r), d) in correction.iter_mut().zip(residual).zip(diagonal.iter()) {
            *c = r / d;
        }
        Ok(())
    }
}

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let n = 8_usize;
let rates: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|i| 10.0_f64.powf(stiffness_exponent * i as f64 / 7.0))
    .collect();
let (k, kj) = (rates.clone(), rates.clone());
let system = OdeFn::new(n, move |_: f64, y: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
    for ((out, &y), &k) in out.iter_mut().zip(y).zip(&k) {
        *out = -k * y - y * y * y;
    }
    Ok(())
})
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
.with_jacobian(move |_: f64, y: &[f64], j: &mut MatrixViewMut<'_, f64>| {
    for row in 0..y.len() {
        for col in 0..y.len() {
            let value = if row == col {
                -kj[row] - 3.0 * y[row] * y[row]
            } else {
                0.0
            };
            j.set(row, col, value)?;
        }
    }
    Ok(())
});
let solver = DiagonalSolver {
    ones: vec![1.0; n],
    diagonal: RefCell::new(vec![0.0; n]),
};
let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-8, 1e-8, NormSelection::RootMeanSquare)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Increase the step by 4ε to avoid an extra short step from rounding.
let options = OdeOptions::new()
    .with_error_tolerance(tolerance)
    .with_initial_step(step_size + 4.0 * f64::EPSILON);
let native = OdeProblem::new(&system, 1.0);
let injected = native.with_stage_solver(OdeStageSolver::Linear(&solver));
let cases = [
    ("Simplified Newton", NewtonMode::Simplified, &native),
    ("Full Newton", NewtonMode::Full, &native),
    (
        "Replacement diagonal linear solver",
        NewtonMode::Simplified,
        &injected,
    ),
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, mode, problem) in cases {
    let method = Sdirk::new().with_newton_mode(mode);
    let mut workspace = method
        .workspace(problem, &options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut state = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![1.0; n]));
    let report = method
        .advance(
            problem,
            &options,
            &mut state,
            &mut (),
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if !report.is_success() {
        return Err(format!("{name}: {:?}", report.termination()));
    }
    // Independent check: maximum componentwise difference from the exact solution (f64 standard functions).
    let error = state
        .values()
        .iter()
        .zip(&rates)
        .map(|(&y, &k)| (y - ((1.0 + 1.0 / k) * (2.0 * k).exp() - 1.0 / k).powf(-0.5)).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max);
    let counts = report.accounting().total().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        name,
        error,
        counts.provider(EvaluationRole::Jacobian),
        counts.provider(EvaluationRole::Factorization),
        counts.provider(EvaluationRole::LinearSolve),
        counts.provider(EvaluationRole::LinearOperator),
        counts.provider(EvaluationRole::RightHandSide),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Stage solver" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Terminal error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Jacobians [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Factorizations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Newton corrections [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Operator applications [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Right-hand-side evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "work" })
);
```

With defaults ($h = 0.01$, $K = 4$), all three have terminal error $1.6 \times 10^{-7}$, and the replacement diagonal solver gives the same values as simplified Newton.
Simplified Newton completes 100 steps (300 stage solves) with five Jacobian evaluations, five factorizations, and 827 corrections.
Full Newton rebuilds the Jacobian and factorization for every correction, giving 607 of each.
Its 607 corrections and 907 right-hand-side evaluations are fewer than simplified Newton's 827 and 1128, but per-factorization cost $O(d^3)$ dominates for large $d$.
The replacement solver forms no factorizations and applies the operator 1654 times, twice per correction (once inside the solver and once for the integrator's residual check).
Even at $K = 6$, all three errors lie between $9.1 \times 10^{-8}$ and $1.0 \times 10^{-7}$.

## Summary

Implicit Runge–Kutta solves stage equations with Newton; default simplified Newton reuses Jacobians and factorizations across iterations and steps.
Only `RadauIia` is adaptive; other implicit Runge–Kutta methods use fixed steps without error estimates, dense output, or event detection.
L-stable `RadauIia`, `Sdirk`, and `BackwardEuler` damp fast components at large $|z|$; A-stable `GaussRk` and implicit trapezoidal barely damp them.
Supply nonsingular mass matrices with revisions and use dense LU.
Structure-aware linear or nonlinear solvers can replace stages through `OdeStageSolver`, but the integrator cannot fully verify their claimed convergence; work counting and convergence checks must follow `StageAccuracy`.
