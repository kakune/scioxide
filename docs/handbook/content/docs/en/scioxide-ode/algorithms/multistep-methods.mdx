Runge–Kutta evaluates right-hand side $f$ repeatedly within each step and discards information from earlier steps.
Linear multistep methods interpolate past states or right-hand sides, limiting new evaluations to one or two per step.
This saves evaluations for costly right-hand sides but requires history and recomputing coefficients after step changes.
This page covers general formulas and stability theory, and scioxide-ode's `AdamsBashforth`, `AdamsMoulton`, and `Bdf` formulas, order selection, and starting procedures.
All three are variable-step implementations deriving coefficients from actual accepted times, with orders 1–5.
Notation follows [Mathematics and shared conventions](../).

## General form and order

An $k$-step linear multistep method with equally spaced steps $h$ is written as

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{k} \alpha_j\, y_{n+1-j} = h \sum_{j=0}^{k} \beta_j\, f_{n+1-j}, \qquad \alpha_0 = 1
$$


Here $f_m = f(t_m, y_m)$.
If $\beta_0 = 0$, the method is explicit, determining $y_{n+1}$ only from known values; if $\beta_0 \ne 0$, it is implicit, with $y_{n+1}$ on both sides.
Two generating polynomials describe its properties:

$$
\rho(\xi) = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \alpha_j\, \xi^{k-j}, \qquad \sigma(\xi) = \sum_{j=0}^{k} \beta_j\, \xi^{k-j}
$$


The method has order $p$ if substituting the exact solution gives residual $O(h^{p+1})$.
Order at least 1 (consistency) is equivalent to $\rho(1) = 0$ and $\rho'(1) = \sigma(1)$.

For variable steps, $\alpha_j$ and $\beta_j$ depend on step ratios.
scioxide-ode recomputes coefficients each step from interpolation at actual accepted times $t_{n+1-j}$; unlike reusing equally spaced tables, this preserves order under step changes.

## Adams methods

Integrating $y' = f(t, y)$ over $[t_n, t_{n+1}]$ gives

$$
y_{n+1} = y_n + \int_{t_n}^{t_{n+1}} f(t, y(t))\,dt
$$


Adams replaces the integrand with a polynomial through past right-hand-side values.

Order-$q$ Adams–Bashforth extrapolates and integrates a degree-$q - 1$ polynomial through $f$ at the latest $q$ accepted points $t_n, \ldots, t_{n-q+1}$.
It is explicit, requiring only one new evaluation $f_{n+1}$ per step.
Equally spaced second- and fourth-order formulas are

$$
y_{n+1} = y_n + \frac{h}{2}\,(3 f_n - f_{n-1}), \qquad
y_{n+1} = y_n + \frac{h}{24}\,(55 f_n - 59 f_{n-1} + 37 f_{n-2} - 9 f_{n-3})
$$


Order-$q$ Adams–Moulton integrates a degree-$q - 1$ polynomial through $f$ at $q$ points $t_{n+1}, \ldots, t_{n-q+2}$, including new point $t_{n+1}$.
Equally spaced second-order (trapezoidal) and fourth-order formulas are

$$
y_{n+1} = y_n + \frac{h}{2}\,(f_{n+1} + f_n), \qquad
y_{n+1} = y_n + \frac{h}{24}\,(9 f_{n+1} + 19 f_n - 5 f_{n-1} + f_{n-2})
$$


Compared with Adams–Bashforth of the same order, it has a smaller error constant ($19/720$ versus $251/720$ at fourth order) and larger stability region, but is implicit because $f_{n+1}$ depends on unknown $y_{n+1}$.

### Predictor–corrector

The method `AdamsMoulton` avoids iterating the implicit formula, taking four phases per step (PECE).

1. Obtain prediction $y_{n+1}^{(0)}$ with Adams–Bashforth of the same order (P).
2. Evaluate $f(t_{n+1}, y_{n+1}^{(0)})$ (E).
3. Substitute it for $f_{n+1}$ in Adams–Moulton to obtain correction $y_{n+1}$ (C).
4. Evaluate $f(t_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$ for the next step's history (E).

Only one correction makes the overall method explicit, with two evaluations per step.
Unlike correcting to convergence, its stability region is smaller than Adams–Moulton's and unsuitable for stiff problems.
Neither `AdamsBashforth` nor `AdamsMoulton` supports mass matrices.

## Backward differentiation and numerical differentiation formulas

BDF constructs a degree-$q$ interpolant through states $y_{n+1}, y_n, \ldots, y_{n+1-q}$ and equates its derivative at $t_{n+1}$ to $f(t_{n+1}, y_{n+1})$.
Including mass matrix $M$, the order-$q$ formula for `Bdf` is

$$
M \sum_{j=0}^{q} \alpha_j\, y_{n+1-j} = h\, f(t_{n+1}, y_{n+1})
$$

with $\alpha_j$ derived from actual steps.
Equally spaced orders 1–3 are

$$
y_{n+1} - y_n = h f_{n+1}, \qquad
\tfrac{3}{2} y_{n+1} - 2 y_n + \tfrac{1}{2} y_{n-1} = h f_{n+1}, \qquad
\tfrac{11}{6} y_{n+1} - 3 y_n + \tfrac{3}{2} y_{n-1} - \tfrac{1}{3} y_{n-2} = h f_{n+1}
$$

(order 1 is backward Euler).
Evaluating the right-hand side only at the new point strongly damps fast-decaying components and suits stiff problems.

Each step solves a nonlinear equation in $y_{n+1}$ using Newton.
The iteration matrix is $M - (h/\alpha_0) J$, starting from a history prediction.
Simplified Newton reuses LU while $h/\alpha_0$ remains within $[0.8, 1.2]$ times its last factorization value.
The default iteration limit is 20 (`with_max_iterations`); under automatic order selection, unconverged steps are retried with smaller steps.
[Jacobians and linear stage equations](../jacobians-and-linear-stages/) covers linear backends (`with_backend`), Jacobian evaluation (`with_derivative_strategy`), and Newton types (`with_newton_mode`).
Only dense backends support mass matrices; sparse and Krylov backends require $M = I$.

NDF (Klopfenstein and Shampine–Reichelt) adds one term to BDF, selected through `with_formula(BdfFormula::Ndf)`.
Order-$q$ NDF subtracts $\kappa\,\gamma_q\,(y_{n+1} - y_{n+1}^{(0)})$ from the BDF left-hand side.
Here $y_{n+1}^{(0)}$ is the predictor, $\gamma_q = \sum_{j=1}^{q} 1/j$, and $\kappa$ is $-0.185$, $-1/9$, $-0.0823$, $-0.0415$, and $0$ for orders 1–5.
This reduces error constants at the cost of slightly narrower stability angles for orders 3–4.
At order 5, $\kappa = 0$, so NDF equals BDF.
The NDF term is added once $q$ history points are available.

## Zero-stability and Dahlquist barriers

To avoid error amplification in the limit $h \to 0$, the roots of $\rho(\xi) = 0$ must satisfy:

- Every root satisfies $|\xi| \le 1$.
- Roots with $|\xi| = 1$ are simple.

A method satisfying this root condition is zero-stable.
Consistency and zero-stability together are necessary and sufficient for convergence as $h \to 0$ over finite intervals (Dahlquist equivalence theorem).
Violating the root condition makes parasitic solutions corresponding to extra roots of $\rho$ grow each step, amplifying even rounding error.

Zero-stable $k$-step methods have an order bound (Dahlquist's first barrier).
For odd $k$, $p \le k + 1$; for even values, $p \le k + 2$; for explicit methods, $p \le k$.
Equally spaced BDF is zero-stable through order 6, but not from order 7 onward.
scioxide-ode's `Bdf` supports orders 1–5.

For stiff problems, stability at large $z = h\lambda$ matters.
A method stable throughout the left half-plane $\mathrm{Re}\,z < 0$ is A-stable; A-stable linear multistep methods have order at most 2 (Dahlquist's second barrier).
Among second-order A-stable multistep methods, trapezoidal has the smallest error constant.
BDF of order at least 3 is not A-stable but is stable in the sector $|\arg(-z)| \le \alpha$ within angle $\alpha$ of the negative real axis, called $A(\alpha)$-stability.
Equally spaced BDF and NDF angles are as follows (Shampine–Reichelt 1997).

| Order $q$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BDF angle $\alpha$ | 90° | 90° | 86.03° | 73.35° | 51.84° |
| NDF angle $\alpha$ | 90° | 90° | 80° | 66° | 51° |

Since $\alpha > 0$, all orders 1–5 are stable on the whole negative real axis, permitting large steps for stiff problems with eigenvalues near it.
Oscillatory components with eigenvalues near the imaginary axis restrict stable steps at orders at least 3.
These angles concern equally spaced steps; variable-step stability also depends on step ratios.

## Order policies and starting procedures

Multistep methods cannot start without past values.
The policy `MultistepOrder` determines order selection and corresponding starting procedures.

| Policy | Order | Step size | Starting procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adams with `MultistepOrder::Fixed(q)` | $q$ once history is available | Constant | Initial steps use classical RK4 for $q \le 4$, Dormand–Prince 5(4) for $q = 5$ |
| BDF with `MultistepOrder::Fixed(q)`, $q = 1, 2$ | Increase from 1 to $q$ | Constant | Start with backward Euler |
| BDF with `MultistepOrder::Fixed(q)`, $q = 3$–$5$ | $q$ | Constant | Initial steps use backward Euler extrapolated to order $q$ |
| `MultistepOrder::Automatic(q)` | Automatically select orders 1 through $q$ | Adjusted to tolerances | Start at order 1 |

The constructor `new(q)` accepts `Fixed(q)`; `with_order_policy` accepts any policy, returning `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`) for orders outside 1–5.
Fixed-order methods keep step size from `OdeOptions::with_initial_step` (or its estimate), shortening only the last step to the endpoint.
High-order starting procedures prevent low-order starting errors from persisting in global error.

Under `Automatic`, compare neighboring-order local error estimates within the same attempt to select the next order.
Order changes by at most one per step, retaining the current order if estimates tie.
After an order change, wait for $q + 1$ acceptances before comparing again; do not raise order immediately after rejection.
PI control determines step size.
The type `AutoStiff` pairs only adaptive methods, so both methods in `AdamsBdf` use `Automatic(5)`.

Each `advance` call starts afresh, without inheriting history or step size from the preceding call.
After a right-hand-side discontinuity or state jump, stop there and restart in a new call.

The following cell uses four constant-step order-$q$ methods (`AdamsBashforth::new(q)`, `AdamsMoulton::new(q)`, `Bdf::new(q)`, and `Bdf::new(q)` with NDF) to solve the nonlinear limit-cycle system

$$
x' = -y + x\,(1 - r^2), \qquad y' = x + y\,(1 - r^2), \qquad r^2 = x^2 + y^2
$$

from $(x, y) = (0.5, 0)$ to $t = 4$.
In polar coordinates, $r' = r(1 - r^2)$ and $\theta' = 1$, giving exact solution $r(t) = \bigl(1 + (r_0^{-2} - 1)e^{-2t}\bigr)^{-1/2}$ and $\theta = t$.
For steps $h = 4/N$ ($N = 16, 32, \ldots, 512$), the first table and log–log plot show terminal error $\|y_N - y(4)\|_2$; the second table shows observed orders from the finest two steps and right-hand-side evaluations at the finest step.
BDF Newton receives an analytical Jacobian and solves stages to tolerance $10^{-13}$.

use scioxide_linalg::{MatrixViewMut, Vector};

/// Solve through t = 4 with step h, returning terminal error and right-hand-side evaluations.
fn solve_fixed<M: OdeMethod<f64> + Clone>(method: M, h: f64) -> Result<(f64, usize), String> {
    let system = OdeFn::new(2, |_: f64, y: &[f64], dydt: &mut [f64]| {
        let shrink = 1.0 - y[0] * y[0] - y[1] * y[1];
        dydt[0] = -y[1] + y[0] * shrink;
        dydt[1] = y[0] + y[1] * shrink;
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    })
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .with_jacobian(|_: f64, y: &[f64], jac: &mut MatrixViewMut<'_, f64>| {
        let (x, v) = (y[0], y[1]);
        let shrink = 1.0 - x * x - v * v;
        jac.set(0, 0, shrink - 2.0 * x * x)?;
        jac.set(0, 1, -1.0 - 2.0 * x * v)?;
        jac.set(1, 0, 1.0 - 2.0 * x * v)?;
        jac.set(1, 1, shrink - 2.0 * v * v)
    });
    let end = 4.0_f64;
    let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-13, 1e-13, NormSelection::RootMeanSquare)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let options = OdeOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(tolerance)
        .with_initial_step(h);
    let initial = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![0.5, 0.0]));
    let solution = method
        .solve(&OdeProblem::new(&system, end), &options, initial)
        .map_err(|e| SciOxideError::from(e).to_string())?;
    // Exact solution r(t) = (1 + (r₀⁻² − 1)e^{−2t})^{−1/2}, θ = t (for verification).
    let radius = 1.0 / RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 + 3.0 * RealScalar::exp(-2.0 * end));
    let (sin, cos) = RealScalar::sin_cos(end);
    let y = solution.state().values();
    let error = RealScalar::hypot(y[0] - radius * cos, y[1] - radius * sin);
    let evaluations = solution
        .report()
        .counts()
        .provider(EvaluationRole::RightHandSide);
    Ok((error, evaluations))
}

At the default $q = 4$, finest-two-step observed orders are 4.00 for Adams–Bashforth, 3.96 for Adams PECE, and 4.00 for both BDF and NDF, all converging at fourth order.
At $h = 4/512$, Adams PECE has the smallest error $1.0 \times 10^{-9}$, about one-fourteenth of Adams–Bashforth's $1.4 \times 10^{-8}$.
Adams–Bashforth and PECE use 524 and 1033 right-hand-side evaluations, approximately one and two per step.
BDF and NDF evaluate the right-hand side per Newton iteration, needing 1655 evaluations.
Even at $q = 5$, observed orders are 4.94–4.95; Dormand–Prince 5(4) starting for Adams and extrapolated backward Euler for BDF preserve fifth-order accuracy.
At order 5 the NDF term vanishes, making BDF and NDF errors identical.
At $q = 2$, NDF error $9.6 \times 10^{-6}$ is about one-quarter of BDF's $3.9 \times 10^{-5}$, demonstrating its smaller error constant.
For this nonstiff problem, explicit Adams achieves the same order with fewer evaluations.

## Stability for complex eigenvalues

The $A(\alpha)$-stability angle can be checked with a complex-eigenvalue test equation.
Setting $\lambda = |\lambda|\,e^{i(\pi - \varphi)}$ makes $\varphi$ the angle of $\lambda$ from the negative real axis; $\varphi = 0$ is the negative real axis and $\varphi = 90°$ the imaginary axis.
Writing $y' = \lambda y$ as two real components $(u, v)$ gives

$$
\begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \end{pmatrix}' =
\begin{pmatrix} \mathrm{Re}\,\lambda & -\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda \\ \mathrm{Im}\,\lambda & \mathrm{Re}\,\lambda \end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \end{pmatrix}
$$

with exact-solution magnitude decaying as $|y(t)| = e^{t\,\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda}$.
The following cell selects $|z| = |h\lambda|$ and $\varphi$, takes 300 steps with $h = 0.1$ from $(1, 0)$ using four order-$q$ methods, and plots $|y_n|$ logarithmically.
The table shows $|y_{300}|$, exact $|y(30)|$, and BDF and NDF angles $\alpha$ from the table above.
Even with $\varphi > \alpha$, sufficiently small or large $|z|$ can be stable; instability occurs over an intermediate $|z|$ range.
For methods stopping with overflow errors, plot points through the stop and leave table $|y_{300}|$ blank.
Absolute tolerance $10^{-200}$ allows tracking small values.
BDF stage equations are solved to local tolerance, so retaining default absolute tolerance $10^{-6}$ makes $|y_n|$ stop decreasing near that magnitude.

use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::{MatrixViewMut, Vector};
use scioxide_ode::{
    AdamsBashforth, AdamsMoulton, Bdf, BdfFormula, OdeFn, OdeMethod, OdeOptions,
    OdeOutputSchedule, OdeProblem, OdeState, OdeTrajectory,

/// Take `steps` steps with h = 0.1, returning each |y_n| and |y_N| if the endpoint is reached.
fn amplitudes<M: OdeMethod<f64> + Clone>(
    method: M,
    lambda: (f64, f64),
    steps: usize,
) -> Result<(Vec<[f64; 2]>, Option<f64>), String> {
    let (re, im) = lambda;
    let system = OdeFn::new(2, move |_: f64, y: &[f64], dydt: &mut [f64]| {
        dydt[0] = re * y[0] - im * y[1];
        dydt[1] = im * y[0] + re * y[1];
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    })
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .with_jacobian(move |_: f64, _: &[f64], jac: &mut MatrixViewMut<'_, f64>| {
        jac.set(0, 0, re)?;
        jac.set(0, 1, -im)?;
        jac.set(1, 0, im)?;
        jac.set(1, 1, re)
    });
    let h = 0.1_f64;
    let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-200, 1e-12, NormSelection::RootMeanSquare)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let options = OdeOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(tolerance)
        .with_initial_step(h)
        .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::AcceptedSteps);
    let problem = OdeProblem::new(&system, h * steps as f64);
    let mut workspace = method
        .workspace(&problem, &options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut state = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![1.0, 0.0]));
    let mut path = OdeTrajectory::new();
    // Overflowing divergence stops with an error, but earlier points remain in path.
    let finished = method
        .advance(
            &problem,
            &options,
            &mut state,
            &mut path,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .is_ok_and(|report| report.is_success());
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = path
        .states()
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(n, y)| {
            [
                n as f64,
                RealScalar::hypot(y.as_slice()[0], y.as_slice()[1]),
            ]
        })
        .filter(|p| p[1] > 0.0)
        .collect();
    let last = finished.then(|| RealScalar::hypot(state.values()[0], state.values()[1]));
    Ok((points, last))
}

## Workspaces and history

Allocate multistep workspace `OdeWorkspace` through `method.workspace(&problem, &options)` and reuse it by passing to `advance`.
For maximum order $q$, it holds state and right-hand-side history for $q + 1$ points, interpolation coefficients, and, for BDF, Jacobians, LU, and Newton work vectors.
Only capacity checks (`check`), growth (`try_reserve`), and latest-call records (`last_attempt`) are public; history contents and current order are unreadable.
Passing the same workspace to the next `advance` does not transfer history; the method restarts.
With sufficient capacity and nonallocating callbacks and sinks, `advance` does not allocate.
[Solving procedure and contracts](../solve-lifecycle-and-contracts/) covers workspace and call contracts in full.

## Summary

Linear multistep methods use past states and right-hand sides, needing one new right-hand-side evaluation per Adams–Bashforth step, two for PECE, and one per Newton iteration for BDF.
scioxide-ode computes coefficients from accepted times, preserving orders 1–5 under step changes; all four fixed-order methods in the experiment had observed order matching $q$.
Explicit Adams and single-correction PECE are unsuitable for stiff problems; use `Bdf` for stiffness.
BDF through order 2 is A-stable, while orders 3–5 are $A(\alpha)$-stable; NDF trades smaller error constants for narrower order-3–4 angles.
For components with eigenvalues near the imaginary axis, reduce order or choose L-stable `RadauIia`.
