Implicit Runge–Kutta and BDF solve nonlinear equations with Newton at each stiff-problem step.
Rosenbrock incorporates only the first Newton iteration into the method formula, solving one linear system per stage.
Without iteration convergence checks, per-step work is predictable and often lower than implicit Runge–Kutta at moderate accuracy.
This page covers scioxide-ode's `RosenbrockW` formulas and two coefficient sets, why approximate Jacobians preserve order, and when stability can nevertheless be lost.
The second half covers indicators and rules used by `AutoStiff` and `AdamsBdf` to switch between nonstiff and stiff methods as stiffness changes.
Notation follows [Mathematics and shared conventions](../), with right-hand-side Jacobian $J = \partial f/\partial y$ and step size $h$.
[Error order, stability, and stiffness](../error-order-and-stability/) gives the basics of stiffness and stability functions.

## Rosenbrock-W stage equations

An $s$-stage Rosenbrock-type method advances one step from $y_n$ to $y_{n+1}$ as follows.

$$
(I - h\gamma A)\,k_i = h\,f\!\left(t_n + \alpha_i h,\; y_n + \sum_{j<i} a_{ij} k_j\right) + hA\sum_{j<i}\gamma_{ij} k_j + h^2 \gamma_i\, \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(t_n, y_n),
$$

$$
y_{n+1} = y_n + \sum_{i=1}^{s} b_i k_i, \qquad \hat y_{n+1} = y_n + \sum_{i=1}^{s} \hat b_i k_i .
$$

Here $k_i$ is a stage increment; $a_{ij}$, $\gamma_{ij}$, $b_i$, and $\hat b_i$ are method coefficients, with $\alpha_i = \sum_{j<i} a_{ij}$ and $\gamma_i = \sum_{j\le i}\gamma_{ij}$ and shared diagonal coefficient $\gamma_{ii} = \gamma$.
The matrix $A$ is Jacobian $J(t_n, y_n)$ itself or an approximation.
A method satisfying order conditions assuming $A = J$ is called a Rosenbrock method; one satisfying them for arbitrary $A$ is a W method.

The left-hand matrix $I - h\gamma A$ is shared across stages, requiring one LU factorization per step and only forward/back substitution per stage.
Right-hand sides depend only on known stages, making stage equations linear without Newton iteration.
The error estimate is the solution difference $y_{n+1} - \hat y_{n+1}$ obtained with weights $b_i$ and $\hat b_i$; acceptance and next-step selection follow explicit Runge–Kutta rules (PI control by default).

The method `RosenbrockW` selects two coefficient sets through `RosenbrockMethod`.

| Coefficient set | Stages $s$ | Order | Embedded order | $\gamma$ | Reference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| `RosenbrockMethod::Ros2` | 2 | 2 | 1 | $1 + 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 1.7071$ | Verwer et al. (1999) |
| `RosenbrockMethod::Ros34Pw2` | 4 | 3 | 2 | $0.435866521508459$ | Rang–Angermann（2005） |

Both coefficient sets are constructed as W methods and are L-stable when $A = J$.
One attempt of an $s$-stage method needs $s - 1$ right-hand-side evaluations, one LU factorization, and $s - 1$ Jacobian–vector products.
The quantities $f(t_n, y_n)$ and $\partial f/\partial t(t_n, y_n)$ are evaluated only once per accepted step and reused on retries with smaller steps after rejection.
Dense output is cubic Hermite interpolation from endpoint values and slopes; the accepted endpoint slope becomes the next step's $f(t_n, y_n)$.
Events and scheduled output are supported, but mass matrices are not; systems with mass matrices are rejected with `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).

Jacobians are rebuilt after five consecutive accepted steps, a rejection, or a change in time or any state component exceeding half $\max(1, |\text{old}|, |\text{new}|)$.
Systems whose `OdeSystem::jacobian_is_constant` returns `true` never rebuild them.
LU always uses the current step's $h$.
Select stage linear solvers through `with_backend`: dense LU by default, with sparse LU and restarted GMRES also available.
Select Jacobian evaluation through `with_derivative_strategy`.
[Jacobians and linear stage equations](../jacobians-and-linear-stages/) covers these choices and costs.

## Approximate Jacobians and order

Rosenbrock order conditions follow from Taylor expansion; using $A = J$ reduces their number.
W coefficients expand $A$ and $J$ as separate quantities and satisfy conditions for every $A$.
W methods retain their order as step size approaches zero with old Jacobians, finite-difference approximations, or Jacobians containing only some components.
This permits `RosenbrockW` to rebuild Jacobians only every five steps and accept difference approximations by default.

Stability, however, depends on $A$.
Applying $A = \mu$ to test equation $y' = \lambda y$ gives amplification $R(z, w)$ depending on two variables $z = h\lambda$ and $w = h\mu$; $|R(z, z)| \le 1$ need not imply the same for $w \ne z$.
For example, with $A = 0$, every stage matrix is identity, giving the form of explicit Runge–Kutta.
Thus every W method diverges on stiff problems with large $|h\lambda|$ if the approximate Jacobian misses fast-component eigenvalues.

This and the next section use the Prothero–Robinson-type test equation

$$
y' = \lambda(t)\,\bigl(y - \cos t\bigr) - \sin t, \qquad y(0) = 1
$$


Its exact solution is $y(t) = \cos t$ independently of $\lambda(t)$; the magnitude of $\lambda(t) < 0$ determines stiffness.
The nonautonomous equation requires the time derivative

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = \lambda'(t)\,(y - \cos t) + \lambda(t)\sin t - \cos t
$$

for Rosenbrock-W.
The following block defines the model used by two cells.
The parameter `peak` is maximum $\Lambda$ of $|\lambda|$; if `varying` is `false`, it is constant at $\lambda = -\Lambda$; if `true`, it follows $\lambda(t) = -\Lambda^{\sin^2(\pi t/10)}$, growing from $-1$ at $t = 0$ to $-\Lambda$ at $t = 5$, then returning to $-1$ at $t = 10$.
The parameter `jacobian_scale` is multiplier $c$ changing the supplied Rosenbrock-W Jacobian to $c\lambda$; $c = 1$ gives the correct Jacobian.
The method `time_derivative` returns $\partial f/\partial t$ above.

```rust
//| id: ode-prothero-robinson-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Prothero–Robinson-type test problem"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar, SolveContext};
use scioxide_linalg::MatrixViewMut;
use scioxide_ode::OdeSystem;

/// y′ = λ(t)(y − cos t) − sin t. The exact solution for y(0) = 1 is y(t) = cos t.
pub(crate) struct ProtheroRobinson {
    /// Maximum |λ|, Λ.
    pub(crate) peak: f64,
    /// If true, λ(t) = −Λ^{sin²(πt/10)}; otherwise λ = −Λ.
    pub(crate) varying: bool,
    /// Multiplier c for the supplied Jacobian c λ (1 gives the correct Jacobian).
    pub(crate) jacobian_scale: f64,
}

impl ProtheroRobinson {
    /// Return λ(t) and λ′(t).
    pub(crate) fn lambda(&self, t: f64) -> (f64, f64) {
        if !self.varying {
            return (-self.peak, 0.0);
        }
        let phase = std::f64::consts::PI * t / 10.0;
        let log_peak = RealScalar::ln(self.peak);
        let lambda = -RealScalar::exp(log_peak * RealScalar::powi(RealScalar::sin(phase), 2));
        // d/dt sin²(πt/10) = (π/10) sin(πt/5).
        let slope = lambda * log_peak * std::f64::consts::PI / 10.0 * RealScalar::sin(2.0 * phase);
        (lambda, slope)
    }
}

impl OdeSystem<f64> for ProtheroRobinson {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        1
    }
    fn rhs(
        &self,
        t: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        dydt: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let (lambda, _) = self.lambda(t);
        dydt[0] = lambda * (y[0] - RealScalar::cos(t)) - RealScalar::sin(t);
        Ok(())
    }
    fn jacobian(
        &self,
        t: f64,
        _: &[f64],
        jac: &mut MatrixViewMut<'_, f64>,
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<Option<()>, SciOxideError> {
        jac.set(0, 0, self.jacobian_scale * self.lambda(t).0)?;
        Ok(Some(()))
    }
    fn time_derivative(
        &self,
        t: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        output: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<Option<()>, SciOxideError> {
        let (lambda, slope) = self.lambda(t);
        let (sin, cos) = RealScalar::sin_cos(t);
        output[0] = slope * (y[0] - cos) + lambda * sin - cos;
        Ok(Some(()))
    }
}
```

The following cell solves the constant-$\lambda = -10^k$ problem with $[0, 2]$, computing terminal error $|y_N - \cos 2|$ against step $h = 2/N$ ($N = 8, 16, \ldots, 512$) for each Jacobian multiplier $c = 1, 1/2, 0$.
Since `RosenbrockW` is adaptive, tolerance $10^6$ prevents error-based rejections; setting initial, minimum, and maximum step sizes all to $h$ enforces fixed-step integration.
Steps are negative powers of two, reaching $t = 2$ in exactly $N$ steps.
Observed orders in the table are adjacent-step error ratios $\log_2(e_{h}/e_{h/2})$.
Combinations diverging beyond floating-point range or stopping after a rejected step are displayed as “Divergent.”

With default ROS34PW2 and $\lambda = -1$, all three multipliers' observed orders approach 3; at the finest step, $c = 1, 1/2, 0$ errors are $9.5 \times 10^{-10}$, $8.2 \times 10^{-10}$, and $5.8 \times 10^{-9}$.
The case $c = 0$ computes the equivalent of an explicit method without a Jacobian, yet retains third-order convergence.
Error constants vary with $A$; the error for $c = 0$ is about six times that for $c = 1$.

With $\lambda = -10^3$, $c = 1$ remains stable even at $h = 1/4$, with error $3.4 \times 10^{-6}$.
The case $c = 0$ diverges for every step size with $|h\lambda| \ge 3.9$; $c = 1/2$ also diverges at $h \ge 1/64$, first reducing error to $3.0 \times 10^{-3}$ at $h = 1/128$.
For ROS2, $c = 1/2$ remains stable at every tested step size, but observed order falls to 1.04–1.13.
Even with $c = 1$, ROS34PW2 observed orders of 2.0–2.4 do not reach 3.
This is known Prothero–Robinson order reduction: while $|h\lambda|$ is large, local errors decrease only as lower powers of step size.
Order preservation concerns the limit $h \to 0$; errors and stability at large $|h\lambda|$ depend on Jacobian approximation quality.
For stiff problems, supply the correct Jacobian or at least an approximation capturing fast-component eigenvalues correctly.

## Time derivatives in nonautonomous systems

When $f$ explicitly depends on $t$, Rosenbrock-type methods need the $\partial f/\partial t$ term to retain order.
The method `RosenbrockW` obtains this term from `OdeSystem::time_derivative` if available, otherwise through finite differences in $t$.
The perturbation scale is $\max(|t|, 1)$; forward differences add one right-hand-side evaluation per accepted step.
Automatic differentiation is unavailable for $\partial f/\partial t$.
The preference order selected through `with_derivative_strategy` applies to both Jacobians and time derivatives.

For autonomous $y' = f(y)$, writing 0 in `time_derivative` and returning `Ok(Some(()))` avoids difference evaluations.
Closure-based `OdeFn` has no `time_derivative`, so time derivatives always use differences.
To reduce evaluations or improve on difference accuracy, implement `OdeSystem` and supply `time_derivative`.

## Stiffness indicator

Explicit stability regions are bounded, with negative-real-axis interval $|h\lambda| \le c$ ($c \approx 3.3$ for Dormand–Prince 5(4)).
When this condition rather than accuracy determines steps, the problem is stiff for that method.
The type `AutoStiff` detects this through the product of step size and spectral radius

$$
\sigma = |h|\,\rho(J)
$$


Here $\rho(J)$ is the largest absolute Jacobian eigenvalue; when $\sigma$ exceeds roughly 1, explicit steps are likely stability-limited rather than accuracy-limited.

The quantity $\rho(J)$ is estimated by power iteration without forming Jacobians.
Directions persist across steps; the geometric mean of the last two ratios $\|Jv\|/\|v\|$ is the estimate, allowing convergence when equal-magnitude $\pm\lambda$ pairs or complex conjugate pairs dominate.
Each iteration uses one product $Jv$.
A current stiff-method Jacobian gives this product without extra evaluations; otherwise use `OdeSystem::jacobian_product` or forward differences of $f$ (one right-hand-side evaluation).
At most one product is used per accepted step; estimation intervals double from one step after each estimate, capped at 12 steps.
If the estimate is within a factor of two of the switching threshold and differs from the previous estimate by more than one-quarter, the interval resets to one step.
The quantity $\sigma$ is estimated and does not prove stiffness.
No indicator is computed for systems with mass matrices.

## Switching rules

The type `AutoStiff<S, N, F>` starts with adaptive nonstiff `N`, switches to stiff `F` under `StiffnessSwitchOptions` rules, and switches back.
It switches from `N` to `F` when accepted-step $\sigma$ exceeds threshold `enter` for `enter_steps` consecutive steps, or after `rejections` consecutive rejections.
It returns from `F` to `N` after at least `residence` acceptances in `F` and `leave_steps` consecutive values of $\sigma$ below threshold `leave`.
While integrating with `F`, the $h$ in $\sigma$ uses the largest step taken by `F` since switching.
This avoids returning to `N` solely because of rejections or temporary step reductions.

| Settings | Default | Setter |
|---|---|---|
| `enter`, `leave` | $1$, $1/4$ | `with_thresholds(enter, leave)` |
| `enter_steps`, `leave_steps` | 2, 6 | `with_evidence_steps(enter, leave)` |
| `residence` | 8 | `with_residence(accepted_steps)` |
| `rejections` | 3 | `with_rejections(consecutive)` |

Setters do not validate; `AutoStiff::new` validates the combined values.
Nonpositive or nonfinite `enter`, `leave` failing $0 \le \text{leave} < \text{enter}$, or any zero count returns `InvalidInput`.
If either `N` or `F` is not adaptive (fixed-step methods or multistep methods with `MultistepOrder::Fixed`), it returns `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).
Separating `enter` and `leave` and requiring repeated satisfaction gives hysteresis, preventing oscillation between methods near thresholds.

A newly active method starts at the switching time with empty history and restarted step-size control.
Multistep history is not transferred, so BDF as `F` starts at order 1.
With mass matrices, the first step switches to `F` (reason `MassMatrix`), without switching back.
Thus `F` must support mass matrices, and pairings using `F` for `RosenbrockW` cannot handle them.
Scheduled output and events use dense output from both methods.

The type `AdamsBdf` aliases `AutoStiff<S, AdamsMoulton<S>, Bdf<S>>`; `AdamsBdf::adams_bdf()` constructs an Adams predictor–corrector (PECE) and BDF pair with automatic orders up to 5 and default switching rules.
Construct other pairs, such as `DormandPrince5` and `RosenbrockW`, through `AutoStiff::new`.
[Linear multistep methods](../multistep-methods/) covers the numerics of both methods.

Switching records remain in `method_switches()` (count) and `last_method_switch()` (latest switch) of `OdeSolveReport`.
The record `OdeMethodSwitch` returns time `time()` when the new method starts its first step, method names `from()` and `to()`, reason `reason()`, and estimate `indicator()` of $\sigma$ at that time.
The value `indicator()` is `None` when no estimate exists, such as with mass matrices.
The four reasons `OdeSwitchReason` are:

| `OdeSwitchReason` | Switch | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| `Stiffness` | `N` → `F` | $\sigma >$ `enter` for `enter_steps` consecutive steps |
| `Rejections` | `N` → `F` | `N` rejected `rejections` consecutive steps |
| `Nonstiffness` | `F` → `N` | After `residence` acceptances, $\sigma <$ `leave` for `leave_steps` consecutive steps |
| `MassMatrix` | `N` → `F` | System has a mass matrix |

Switches are not sent to the output sink (`OdeOutputSink`), and only the latest remains in the report.
To list all switches, solve the same problem again with a terminal event just before the recorded switching time and read that report's latest switch.
Events only stop integration without changing earlier steps, so the repeated integration follows the original step sequence.
The following cell repeats this until no switches remain.

/// Return the full-interval report, accepted times, and switches traced from last to first.
fn switch_log<M: OdeMethod<f64> + Clone>(
    method: &M,
    system: &ProtheroRobinson,
    options: &OdeOptions<f64>,
    end: f64,
) -> Result<
    (
        OdeSolveReport<f64>,
        f64,
        Vec<f64>,
        Vec<OdeMethodSwitch<f64>>,
    ),
    String,
> {
    let initial = || OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![1.0]));
    let accepted = options
        .clone()
        .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::AcceptedSteps);
    let full = method
        .solve(&OdeProblem::new(system, end), &accepted, initial())
        .map_err(|e| SciOxideError::from(e).to_string())?;
    let report = *full.report();
    let final_value = full.state().values()[0];
    let mut switches = Vec::new();
    let mut last = report.last_method_switch();
    while let Some(switch) = last {
        switches.push(switch);
        // Terminal event stopping just before the recorded switch. Earlier steps match the original integration.
        let stop = switch.time() - 1e-9 * switch.time().abs().max(1.0);
        let event = EventFn::new(move |t: f64, _: &[f64]| Ok(t - stop)).with_terminal(true);
        let events: [&dyn EventFunction<f64>; 1] = [&event];
        let problem = OdeProblem::new(system, end).with_events(&events);
        let earlier = method
            .solve(&problem, options, initial())
            .map_err(|e| SciOxideError::from(e).to_string())?;
        last = earlier.report().last_method_switch();
    }
    switches.reverse();
    Ok((
        report,
        final_value,
        full.trajectory().times().to_vec(),
        switches,
    ))
}

## Choosing a method

Rosenbrock-W has no Newton convergence checks; stage count determines per-step work.
It suits moderately sized stiff problems with analytically available or inexpensive difference Jacobians and tolerances around $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-6}$.
Third-order ROS34PW2 needs small steps at tight tolerances; fifth-order `RadauIia` or `Bdf` up to order 5 often needs fewer steps then.
For stiffness confined to part of an interval or unknown stiffness, switch using `AutoStiff` or `AdamsBdf`.
With `AdamsBdf`, larger BDF steps make the indicator harder to reduce, preventing a return from the stiff method in the example above.
For problems with a mass matrix, $M y' = f$, `RosenbrockW` is unavailable; use `Bdf` or `RadauIia`.
[Choosing a method](../../choosing/) collects overall selection guidance.

## Summary

The method `RosenbrockW` solves linear systems with matrix $I - h\gamma A$ at each stage, using only one LU factorization per step.
As W methods, ROS2 and ROS34PW2 retain orders 2 and 3 at $h \to 0$ even with approximate Jacobians; the experiment also converged at order 3 with $A = 0$.
Stability at large $|h\lambda|$ still depends on approximation quality; at $\lambda = -10^3$, $A = 0$ and $A = J/2$ diverged for large steps.
For nonautonomous systems, supply $\partial f/\partial t$ through `time_derivative`; for autonomous systems, returning 0 avoids difference evaluations.
The types `AutoStiff` and `AdamsBdf` switch using estimated $|h|\rho(J)$ and rejection counts; reports retain switch count and the latest switch.
