Systems combining deterministic change with irregular fluctuations, such as stock prices, turbulent particles, and circuits with thermal noise, are modeled by stochastic differential equations (SDEs).
An SDE solution is a stochastic process; numerical errors have different meanings for individual paths and expectations over many paths.
This page explains Itô and Stratonovich interpretations, the two `scioxide-ode` solvers, caller-supplied Wiener increments, strong and weak convergence, and ensemble expectation estimation.
scioxide-random's [Hierarchical stochastic paths with Brownian bridges](../../../scioxide-random/examples/brownian-bridge/) covers random generation and Wiener-path construction.

## Stochastic differential equations and Wiener processes

The SDE in `scioxide-ode` is

$$
dX = a(t, X)\,dt + B(t, X)\,dW
$$


Here $X(t) \in \mathbb K^n$ is a real or complex state, $a(t, X) \in \mathbb K^n$ drift, $B(t, X)$ a $n \times m$ diffusion matrix, and $W$ a $m$-dimensional real Wiener process with independent components.
A Wiener process has $W(0) = 0$ and independent increments on disjoint intervals; increment $W(t + h) - W(t)$ is normal with mean 0 and variance $h$.
Paths are almost surely continuous but nowhere differentiable; squared increments over finer partitions of $[0, T]$ (quadratic variation) sum to $T$ in the limit.

The trait `SdeSystem` supplies state dimension $n$ (`dimension`), Wiener dimension $m$ (`noise_dimension`), drift (`drift`), and diffusion-increment product $B(t, X)\,\Delta W$ (`diffusion_product`).
Supplying diffusion as a product rather than a matrix permits dense, sparse, or matrix-free forms.
The product must be linear in the increments.
Use `SdeFn::new(n, m, drift, diffusion_product)` for closure implementations.
Drift calls count as `RightHandSide` evaluations and diffusion calls as `LinearOperator` evaluations.

## Itô and Stratonovich integrals

Approximating $\int_0^T b(X)\,dW$ by sums over partition $t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_N$ gives different limits depending on where the integrand is evaluated.
Itô evaluates at left endpoint $b(X(t_k))$; Stratonovich $\int b(X) \circ dW$ averages endpoint values $\tfrac12\bigl(b(X(t_k)) + b(X(t_{k+1}))\bigr)$.
The sum difference is $\tfrac12 \sum_k \bigl(b(X(t_{k+1})) - b(X(t_k))\bigr)\Delta W_k$; by $b(X(t_{k+1})) - b(X(t_k)) \approx b'(X)\,b(X)\,\Delta W_k$ and $\sum_k \Delta W_k^2 \to T$ it does not approach zero.
Thus identical coefficients give different Itô and Stratonovich solutions.

The Stratonovich equation $dX = a\,dt + B \circ dW$ has the same solution as an Itô equation with drift replaced by

$$
a_i^{\mathrm{I}} = a_i + \frac12 \sum_{j=1}^{m} \sum_{k=1}^{n} B_{kj}\,\frac{\partial B_{ij}}{\partial X_k}
$$


For scalars, $a^{\mathrm I} = a + \tfrac12 b\,b'$.
Conversely, solving an Itô equation as Stratonovich requires subtracting the same drift term.
For additive noise with state-independent $B$, correction vanishes and both interpretations coincide.

Itô stochastic integrals have zero expectation (martingales), making this interpretation natural in statistical and financial models.
Stratonovich preserves the ordinary chain rule and arises as a smooth-noise limit, making it natural in physical models.
Interpretation is part of the model and cannot be inferred from coefficients alone.

The enum `SdeInterpretation` has values `Ito` and `Stratonovich`; `SdeMethod::interpretation` returns a solver's interpretation.
Solvers do not convert drift between interpretations; supply drift matching the chosen solver.

## Euler–Maruyama and stochastic Heun

For step $h = t_{k+1} - t_k$ and increment $\Delta W_k = W(t_{k+1}) - W(t_k)$, the two `SdeMethod` methods are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Euler–Maruyama:} && X_{k+1} = X_k + h\,a(t_k, X_k) + B(t_k, X_k)\,\Delta W_k, \\
&\text{Stochastic Heun:} && \tilde X = X_k + h\,a(t_k, X_k) + B(t_k, X_k)\,\Delta W_k, \\
& && X_{k+1} = X_k + \tfrac{h}{2}\bigl(a(t_k, X_k) + a(t_{k+1}, \tilde X)\bigr) + \tfrac12\bigl(B(t_k, X_k) + B(t_{k+1}, \tilde X)\bigr)\Delta W_k
\end{aligned}
$$

The method `EulerMaruyama` evaluates diffusion at left endpoints, solving Itô equations; `StochasticHeun` uses the same increment in predictor and corrector, averaging endpoints to solve Stratonovich equations.
Both are explicit one-step methods: Euler–Maruyama evaluates drift and diffusion once each per step, stochastic Heun twice each.
Explicit methods need small steps for stability with stiff drift.

## Strong and weak convergence

SDE numerical errors have two measures.
The expected difference from the solution on the same Wiener path,

$$
e_{\mathrm s}(h) = \mathbb E\,\lvert X(T) - X_N \rvert \le C h^{\gamma}
$$

defines strong order $\gamma$ when the bound holds; the expectation difference for a smooth function $g$,

$$
e_{\mathrm w}(h) = \bigl\lvert \mathbb E\, g(X(T)) - \mathbb E\, g(X_N) \bigr\rvert \le C h^{\beta}
$$

defines weak order $\beta$ when its bound holds.
Strong order measures pathwise closeness (simulation with identical noise); weak order measures distribution or expectation closeness (Monte Carlo bias).

For Lipschitz coefficients satisfying linear-growth and smoothness conditions, Euler–Maruyama has strong order 1/2 and weak order 1.
Stochastic Heun has weak order 1 and strong order 1 for scalar ($m = 1$) or commutative noise, otherwise 1/2.
Commutative noise means diffusion columns $b_j$ satisfy $(\partial b_j/\partial X)\, b_l = (\partial b_l/\partial X)\, b_j$ for all $j, l$.
Even diagonal diffusion need not be commutative if components depend on other components.
This is a coefficient property, unchecked by the solver.

Measuring strong order requires solutions on the same Wiener path with only step size changed.
Generating fresh increments for each step count compares different paths, adding path differences to the measured discrepancy.
Measuring weak order requires expectations estimated over many paths, distinguishing bias from sampling error.
Sample-mean standard errors decrease only as $1/\sqrt M$ in path count $M$, requiring many paths to resolve small bias.

## Supplying Wiener increments

The problem `SdeProblem::new(system, times, increments)` pairs time grid $t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_N$ with an increment matrix.
Row $i$ contains $W(t_{i+1}) - W(t_i)$ over interval $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$; column $j$ is Wiener component $j$, giving shape $N \times m$.
Strided `MatrixView` is also accepted.
Solvers generate no random numbers and store no paths.
They check only shapes and finite values, not independent normal increments of variance $t_{i+1} - t_i$.
Generate $N(0, h)$ values with scioxide-random's `Normal`, or construct paths with `BrownianBridgePlan` and take differences.

The method `with_step_boundaries(boundaries)` specifies step boundaries as a sequence of grid indices.
Indices must start at 0, end at $N$, and increase strictly.
Gaps between indices make a step use the sum of increments over intervening intervals.
This constructs coarse-step solutions from one fine-grid path, permitting same-path comparisons with only step size changed.
Conversely, refine a coarse path by generating intermediate values with Brownian bridges conditional on endpoints.

The setting `SdeOptions::with_refinement(tolerance)` enables stochastic-Heun step refinement.
Solve each step once and as two halves split at a grid midpoint; if the normalized difference exceeds 1, refine both halves again; otherwise accept the two-step result.
If refinement reaches an unsplittable step (one grid interval), integration stops with `StepSizeLimit`.
The difference is a local guide, not a strong- or weak-error bound.
Deciding refinement from that step's increments makes accepted steps path-dependent and biases quadratic-variation sampling.
This bias changes Itô limits, so configuring Euler–Maruyama refinement gives `InvalidInput`.
Refined stochastic Heun still converges to the Stratonovich solution (Gaines and Lyons 1997).

## Plans, workspaces, and reports

The values of `SdeMethod` (`EulerMaruyama`, `StochasticHeun`) provide `workspace(problem, options)`, `advance(problem, options, state, sink, workspace, scope)`, and `solve(problem, options, initial)`.
The time in `advance` state `OdeState` must equal the grid's first time.
Every accepted step updates state and emits it to the sink; the initial state is not emitted.
The method `solve` creates workspace and executes sequentially, returning `SdeSolution` (final state and report).

The one-shot `solve_sde(drift, diffusion_product, times, increments, y0)` uses Euler–Maruyama, returning the full-path matrix including initial values ($(N + 1) \times n$).
Unsuccessful termination gives an error with progress `SdeReport` in `PartialFailure`.

The settings `SdeOptions` provide refinement, workspace-byte limit (`with_max_workspace_bytes`), attempted-step limit (`with_max_steps`), and drift/diffusion evaluation limit (`with_max_evaluations`).
The `SdeRequirements` returned by `SdeMethod::workspace_requirements` holds state and noise dimensions, workspace bytes (`required_bytes`), and limit (`byte_limit`).
Workspace `SdeWorkspace` stores only step and refinement memory, without random state or paths.
Reuse it across problems with the same scalar type, retaining maximum allocated capacity.
With sufficient workspace and nonallocating callbacks and sinks, `advance` does not allocate.

Only `ReachedEnd` of `SdeTermination` is successful.
The status `BudgetLimit` indicates work limits, `StepSizeLimit` missing finer-grid increments for refinement, and `ExecutionStopped` cancellation or deadline.
The status `TerminalEvent` never occurs because SDEs have no events.
The report `SdeReport` holds termination status, last accepted time and grid index (`grid_index`), accepted and rejected steps, evaluations, and the final refinement-test difference (`last_step_error_estimate`).
The value `trust` describes treatment of the accepted state, not proof of accuracy.

## Expectation estimation and the two interpretations

Estimate expectations over many paths by solving the same SDE with independent increments per path.
The function `solve_ensemble_into` executes paths under one solver and retains each termination status ([Forward sensitivities and ensembles](../forward-sensitivities-and-ensembles/)).

For example, consider mean-reverting drift with proportional noise,

$$
dX = (\alpha - \beta X)\,dt + \sigma X\,dW, \qquad X(0) = x_0
$$

the same form as the Brennan–Schwartz interest-rate model.
Under Itô, expectation $m(t) = \mathbb E X(t)$ satisfies $m' = \alpha - \beta m$, giving

$$
m_{\mathrm I}(T) = \frac{\alpha}{\beta} + \left(x_0 - \frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right) e^{-\beta T}
$$


Under Stratonovich, drift correction $+\tfrac12\sigma^2 X$ replaces it with $\kappa = \beta - \sigma^2/2$, giving

$$
m_{\mathrm S}(T) = \frac{\alpha}{\kappa} + \left(x_0 - \frac{\alpha}{\kappa}\right) e^{-\kappa T}
$$


Discretization bias also has a closed form.
Euler–Maruyama has $\mathbb E[\sigma X_k \Delta W_k] = 0$, so its mean follows $m_{k+1} = m_k + h(\alpha - \beta m_k)$.
For stochastic Heun, $\mathbb E[\tilde X \Delta W_k] = \sigma m_k h$ gives the mean recurrence

$$
m_{k+1} = m_k + h\left(1 - \frac{\beta h}{2}\right)(\alpha - \beta m_k) + \frac{\sigma^2 h}{2}\, m_k
$$


The discrete mean minus continuous expectation is weak error (bias); sample mean minus discrete mean is sampling error.

The following cell sets $\alpha = 1$, $\beta = 1$, $x_0 = 0.5$, and $T = 2$, generating increments for $M$ paths together with scioxide-random's `Normal` and `SplitMix64`.
Both solvers receive the same increments and shared parameters $(\alpha, \beta, \sigma)$, solving each path through `solve_ensemble_into`.
Members hold per-path workspace and terminal values; each path solver receives nested context `cx` passed to `run`.
The table shows ensemble reports (executed paths, successful paths, evaluations), passing each solver's sample mean and standard error (SE) to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: ode-sde-ensemble-run
//| ref: ode-sde-ensemble-run
//| title: "Solve an ensemble of paths with the same increments using two methods"
//| run: button
//| cache: inputs
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-random, scioxide-ode]
//| exports: [noise, steps, means, standard_errors]
//| inputs:
//|   noise: {"type":"range","label":"Noise strength σ [-]","description":"Both interpretations coincide at σ = 0.","min":0,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.8}
//|   steps: {"type":"range","label":"Step count N [-]","description":"Step size h = T/N. Bias is proportional to h.","min":4,"max":128,"step":4,"value":32}
//|   paths: {"type":"range","label":"Path count M [paths]","description":"Standard error shrinks as 1/√M.","min":200,"max":4000,"step":200,"value":2000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":42}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"additive-limit","label":"σ = 0","description":"Without noise, both solver means approach the same ODE solution.","inputs":{"noise":0}}
//|   - {"id":"coarse","label":"N = 4","description":"Coarse steps make bias larger than standard error.","inputs":{"steps":4}}
//|   - {"id":"other-seed","label":"Seed 7","description":"Sample mean from another path ensemble.","inputs":{"seed":7}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"ensemble","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":2000}
//|       - {"artifact":"ensemble","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":128000}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::{
    EvaluationRole, ExecutionContext, ExecutionPolicy, SciOxideError, RealScalar, SolveContext,
};
use scioxide_linalg::MatrixView;
use scioxide_ode::{
    EnsembleParameters, EnsembleWorkspace, OdeState, SdeFn, SdeMethod, SdeOptions, SdeProblem,
    SdeReport, SdeWorkspace, solve_ensemble_into,
};
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let (n, m, horizon) = (steps.round() as usize, paths.round() as usize, 2.0_f64);
let h = horizon / n as f64;
let times: Vec<f64> = (0..=n).map(|k| k as f64 * h).collect();
// Path i uses increments[i n .. (i + 1) n]. Generate all paths sequentially from one seed.
let normal = Normal::new(0.0, RealScalar::sqrt(h)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let increments: Vec<f64> = (0..n * m)
    .map(|_| normal.sample(&mut rng))
    .collect::<Result<_, SciOxideError>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Shared parameters p = (α, β, σ). dX = (α − βX) dt + σX dW, X(0) = 0.5.
let parameters = [1.0, 1.0, noise];
let options = SdeOptions::new();
type Member = (SdeWorkspace<f64>, f64);
let (mut rows, mut means, mut errors) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for method in [SdeMethod::EulerMaruyama, SdeMethod::StochasticHeun] {
    let run = |i: usize, p: &[f64], member: &mut Member, cx: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>| {
        let (a, b, s) = (p[0], p[1], p[2]);
        let drift = move |_: f64, x: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
            out[0] = a - b * x[0];
            Ok(())
        };
        let diffusion = move |_: f64, x: &[f64], dw: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
            out[0] = s * x[0] * dw[0];
            Ok(())
        };
        let system = SdeFn::<f64, _, _>::new(1, 1, drift, diffusion);
        let view = MatrixView::from_slice(n, 1, &increments[i * n..(i + 1) * n])?;
        let problem = SdeProblem::new(&system, &times, view);
        let mut state = OdeState::from_slice(0.0, &[0.5])?;
        let report =
            method.advance(&problem, &options, &mut state, &mut (), &mut member.0, cx)?;
        member.1 = state.values()[0];
        Ok(report)
    };
    let mut members: Vec<Member> = (0..m).map(|_| (SdeWorkspace::new(), 0.0)).collect();
    let mut outcomes: Vec<Option<Result<SdeReport<f64>, SciOxideError>>> =
        (0..m).map(|_| None).collect();
    let mut workspace = EnsembleWorkspace::new();
    let shared = EnsembleParameters::Shared(&parameters);
    let policy = ExecutionPolicy::Sequential;
    let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
    let report = solve_ensemble_into(
        shared,
        &run,
        policy,
        &mut members,
        &mut outcomes,
        &mut workspace,
        cx,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mean = members.iter().map(|x| x.1).sum::<f64>() / m as f64;
    let variance = members.iter().map(|x| (x.1 - mean).powi(2)).sum::<f64>() / (m - 1) as f64;
    means.push(mean);
    errors.push(RealScalar::sqrt(variance / m as f64));
    let work = report.accounting().nested();
    rows.push((
        format!("{method:?}"),
        report.completed(),
        report.successful(),
        work.provider(EvaluationRole::RightHandSide),
        work.provider(EvaluationRole::LinearOperator),
    ));
}
export!(noise, noise);
export!(steps, n);
export!(means, &means);
export!(standard_errors, &errors);
let successful = rows.iter().all(|r| r.2 == m);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Solver" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Executed paths [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Successful paths [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Drift evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Diffusion evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ensemble" })
);
let (outcome, status) = if successful {
    (Outcome::satisfied("All paths reached the endpoint"), "ReachedEnd")
} else {
    (
        Outcome::partial("Some paths did not reach the endpoint"),
        "Partially incomplete",
    )
};
emit_outcome!(outcome.status(status).diagnostic("Path count", m as f64, None));
```

With defaults, both solvers take all 2000 paths to `ReachedEnd`.
Euler–Maruyama evaluates drift and diffusion once each per step, giving $32 \times 2000 = 64000$ evaluations each; stochastic Heun doubles this to 128000 each.

The next cell compares sample means and SE above with closed-form continuous expectations and discrete means.
The table gives sample mean, SE, discrete mean, continuous expectation, bias (discrete mean minus continuous expectation), and $z$, the sample-to-discrete-mean difference divided by SE.
Correctly generated increments make $z$ approximately standard normal; $|z| > 3$ suggests checking increments or insufficient path counts.
The plot shows sample-mean 95% confidence intervals (±1.96 SE) and expectation lines for both interpretations.

```rust
//| id: ode-sde-ensemble-mean
//| ref: ode-sde-ensemble-mean
//| title: "Compare SDE expectations for identical coefficients with theoretical values under two interpretations"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-ode]
//| dependsOn:
//|   ensemble: ode-sde-ensemble-run
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":0.932332,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":1.221476,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/0/7","range":{"min":-3,"max":3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/1/7","range":{"min":-3,"max":3}}
use scioxide_ode::SdeMethod;

let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let read = |key: &str| serde_json::from_value::<Vec<f64>>(ensemble[key].clone());
let means = read("means").map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let errors = read("standard_errors").map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let noise = ensemble["noise"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing σ")?;
let n = ensemble["steps"].as_u64().ok_or("Missing step count")? as usize;
let (alpha, beta, start, horizon) = (1.0_f64, 1.0_f64, 0.5_f64, 2.0_f64);
let h = horizon / n as f64;
let kappa = beta - noise * noise / 2.0;
// Continuous expectations (closed form): Itô m' = α − βm, Stratonovich m' = α − κm.
let mean_of = |rate: f64| alpha / rate + (start - alpha / rate) * (-rate * horizon).exp();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut bars = Vec::new();
for (k, method) in [SdeMethod::EulerMaruyama, SdeMethod::StochasticHeun]
    .into_iter()
    .enumerate()
{
    // Iterate the discrete mean recurrence (closed-form mean of one step) N times.
    let mut scheme = start;
    for _ in 0..n {
        let drift = alpha - beta * scheme;
        scheme += match method {
            SdeMethod::EulerMaruyama => h * drift,
            _ => h * (1.0 - beta * h / 2.0) * drift + noise * noise * h / 2.0 * scheme,
        };
    }
    let theory = mean_of(if k == 0 { beta } else { kappa });
    let (mean, se) = (means[k], errors[k]);
    // At σ = 0, every path is identical and SE is rounding-sized, making z meaningless.
    let z = (se > 1e-12).then(|| (mean - scheme) / se);
    rows.push((
        format!("{method:?}"),
        format!("{:?}", method.interpretation()),
        mean,
        se,
        scheme,
        theory,
        scheme - theory,
        z,
    ));
    bars.push([(k + 1) as f64, mean, mean - 1.96 * se, mean + 1.96 * se]);
}
let ito = serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "line",
    "axis": "y",
    "value": mean_of(beta),
    "label": "Itô E[X(T)]"
});
let stratonovich = serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "line",
    "axis": "y",
    "value": mean_of(kappa),
    "label": "Stratonovich E[X(T)]",
    "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
});
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "errorbar",
    "xLabel": "Solver (1: Euler–Maruyama, 2: stochastic Heun) [-]",
    "yLabel": "Estimated E[X(T)] [-]",
    "xMin": 0.5,
    "xMax": 2.5,
    "series": [{ "name": "Sample mean ±1.96 SE", "points": bars }],
    "annotations": [ito, stratonovich]
}));
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Solver" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Interpretation" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Sample mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Discrete mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Continuous expectation [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Bias [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "z [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2, "nullText": "SE = 0" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "means" })

```

With defaults ($\sigma = 0.8$, $N = 32$, $M = 2000$, seed 42), Euler–Maruyama's sample interval $0.913 \pm 0.024$ (±1.96 SE) contains Itô expectation 0.9323, while stochastic Heun's $1.196 \pm 0.033$ contains Stratonovich expectation 1.2215.
The expectation difference 0.29 is about 20 SE, verifying through sample means that identical coefficients define different equations under different interpretations.
Biases are $4.3 \times 10^{-3}$ for Euler–Maruyama and $7.6 \times 10^{-3}$ for stochastic Heun, both below SE (0.012 and 0.017).
Both $z$ values lie near $-1.94$ because shared increments strongly correlate sampling errors.
At $N = 4$, biases grow to 0.036 and 0.050, two to three SE; at $N = 128$ they fall to 0.0011 and 0.0019.
Reducing $h$ by a factor of 32 reduces biases to one-thirty-fourth and one-twenty-sixth, showing weak order 1 for both solvers.
At $\sigma = 0$, every path solves the same ODE; rounding-sized SE makes $z$ undisplayed.
Stochastic-Heun bias $-9.2 \times 10^{-5}$ is then far below Euler–Maruyama's $4.3 \times 10^{-3}$, exposing deterministic Heun's second-order accuracy.

## Summary

SDE solutions depend on Itô or Stratonovich interpretation; in `scioxide-ode` the solver fixes interpretation (`EulerMaruyama` Itô, `StochasticHeun` Stratonovich).
Solvers do not convert drift; changing interpretation requires adding or subtracting correction $\tfrac12 \sum B\,\partial B$.
Callers supply Wiener increments through `SdeProblem`; `with_step_boundaries` constructs coarse solutions from the same path.
Measure strong order by same-path step comparisons and weak order through many-path averages; separate expectation bias from sampling error (SE).
Refinement supports only stochastic Heun, and differences at accepted steps are not error bounds.
