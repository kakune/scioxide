A smooth-looking numerical trajectory is not necessarily correct.
Artificial energy damping, negative concentrations, phase shifts, and missed events become apparent only through comparison with analytical solutions, conserved quantities, or computations using different tolerances or methods.
Each example in this chapter includes at least one independent check.

## Summary

Examples check numerical solutions using analytical solutions, conserved quantities, or independent computations.
For event times, see impact events, the SIR model, and bouncing balls; for invariant drift, see predator–prey systems, oscillators, and the Kepler problem.
Reversible reactions, Van der Pol oscillators, and Robertson kinetics demonstrate method differences under stiffness; geometric Brownian motion and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes demonstrate stochastic differential equation convergence orders.
Lorenz systems and Mackey–Glass equations examine what can and cannot be predicted in chaotic systems.
