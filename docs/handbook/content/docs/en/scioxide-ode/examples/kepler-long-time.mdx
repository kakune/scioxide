Over hundreds of planetary or satellite revolutions, even small per-orbit errors can accumulate in energy and change orbital size or period.
This example solves the gravitational two-body Kepler problem with symplectic Störmer–Verlet (`StormerVerlet`), classical RK4 at the same fixed step (`ClassicalRk4`), and adaptive Dormand–Prince 8(5,3) (`DormandPrince853`), checking exact energy and angular momentum.
Root finding for Kepler equation $M = E - e\sin E$ to obtain orbital position from time is separate, covered in [Kepler's equation and eccentricity](../../../scioxide-rootfind/examples/kepler-equation/).
[Events and invariants in the restricted three-body problem](../../../applications/orbital-mechanics/) integrates rotating-frame orbits adaptively and locates closest approach as an event.

## Two-body problem and invariants

When the Sun greatly outweighs the planet, planetary position $q \in \mathbb{R}^2$ satisfies

$$
q'' = -\frac{\mu\, q}{|q|^3}
$$


Here $\mu = GM$ is gravitational constant times solar mass.
Using semimajor axis $a$ for length and $\sqrt{a^3/\mu}$ for time gives $\mu = 1$ and $a = 1$, with period $2\pi$ (for Earth, length unit 1 au and time unit $1/(2\pi)$ of a year).
Use these dimensionless quantities below.

With momentum $p = q'$, the system has separable Hamiltonian

$$
H(q, p) = \frac{|p|^2}{2} - \frac{1}{|q|}
$$

with $T(p) = |p|^2/2$ and $V(q) = -1/|q|$.
Energy $H$ and angular momentum $L = q_1 p_2 - q_2 p_1$ are conserved; an ellipse of eccentricity $e$ has

$$
H = -\frac{1}{2a} = -\frac{1}{2}, \qquad L = \sqrt{a(1 - e^2)} = \sqrt{1 - e^2}
$$


Starting at perihelion, the closest point to the Sun, gives initial values

$$
q_0 = (1 - e,\; 0), \qquad p_0 = \Bigl(0,\; \sqrt{\tfrac{1 + e}{1 - e}}\Bigr)
$$

whose exact solution returns to $q_0$ every period $2\pi$.
Position offset $|q(2\pi N) - q_0|$ after $N$ revolutions therefore directly measures phase and orbital-shape error.

Perihelion speed is $\sqrt{(1 + e)/(1 - e)}$ and distance $1 - e$, with high curvature.
Fixed-step methods need fine resolution there, requiring more steps per orbit as eccentricity increases.

## Three methods and cost

Störmer–Verlet supplies $\nabla T(p) = p$ and $\nabla V(q) = q/|q|^3$ through `HamiltonianFn`, advancing $Nm$ steps of size $h = 2\pi/m$ ($m$ per orbit).
For only final state, `solve_hamiltonian` suffices; to inspect each step's energy, use `StormerVerlet::advance` and a sink.
Each step evaluates $\nabla T$ and $\nabla V$ once each.
Classical RK4 solves the four-component $y = (q, p)$ ODE at the same $h$, evaluating the right-hand side four times per step.
Dormand–Prince 8(5,3) selects steps at absolute and relative tolerance $\varepsilon$, using 12 right-hand-side evaluations per step, also counting rejected-step work.

Symplectic Störmer–Verlet has energy errors oscillating at size $O(h^2)$ without continual growth ([Modified Hamiltonians and long-term energy error](oxref:ode-modified-hamiltonian)).
Angular momentum is a quadratic invariant of form $p^{\mathsf T} D q$, preserved by Störmer–Verlet to rounding.
Nonsymplectic RK4 and Dormand–Prince accumulate errors in nearly the same direction each orbit.
Tight tolerances reduce Dormand–Prince error but do not remove its continual growth.

## Hundreds of orbital revolutions

The next cell selects eccentricity $e$, revolutions $N$, steps per orbit $m$, and Dormand–Prince tolerance $\varepsilon = 10^{k}$, integrating all methods through $t = 2\pi N$.
Linked log–log plots show within-orbit maximum $|H - (-1/2)|$ above and $|L - \sqrt{1 - e^2}|$ below against orbit number.
Omit zero errors from logarithmic axes.
The table gives overall maximum energy/angular-momentum errors, position offset $|q_N - q_0|$ after $N$ orbits, accepted steps, right-hand-side evaluations ($\nabla T$ plus $\nabla V$ for Störmer–Verlet), and termination status.
The computation is heavy, so run it by button.

```rust
//| id: ode-kepler-long-time
//| ref: ode-kepler-long-time
//| title: "Track Kepler energy and angular momentum for hundreds of revolutions with three methods"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   eccentricity: {"type":"range","label":"Eccentricity e","description":"Zero gives a circle. Larger values increase speed and curvature near perihelion.","min":0,"max":0.8,"step":0.05,"value":0.5}
//|   orbits: {"type":"range","label":"Revolutions N","description":"Number of orbital revolutions to integrate.","min":10,"max":500,"step":10,"value":500}
//|   steps_per_orbit: {"type":"range","label":"Steps per orbit m","description":"Fixed step h = 2π/m for Störmer–Verlet and RK4.","min":50,"max":500,"step":50,"value":100}
//|   tolerance_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Exponent k of Dormand–Prince tolerance ε = 10^k","description":"Vary absolute and relative tolerances together.","min":-13,"max":-6,"step":1,"value":-10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"eccentric","label":"e = 0.8","description":"Closer perihelion increases both fixed-step methods' errors.","inputs":{"eccentricity":0.8}}
//|   - {"id":"circular","label":"e = 0","description":"For circular orbits, step effects are uniform along the orbit.","inputs":{"eccentricity":0}}
//|   - {"id":"loose","label":"ε = 10⁻⁶","description":"Looser tolerance accelerates Dormand–Prince energy growth.","inputs":{"tolerance_exponent":-6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"kepler","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kepler","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":6e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kepler","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kepler","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kepler","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|   - case: eccentric
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"kepler","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-11}}
use scioxide_core::{
    ErrorTolerance, EvaluationRole, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, NormSelection, RealScalar,
};
use scioxide_linalg::Vector;
use scioxide_ode::{
    ClassicalRk4, DormandPrince853, HamiltonianFn, MechanicalOptions, OdeFn, OdeMethod,
    OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule, OdeProblem, OdeState, PhaseSpace, StormerVerlet,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let e = eccentricity;
let (n_orbits, m) = (orbits as usize, steps_per_orbit as usize);
let tau = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI;
let h = tau / m as f64;
let end = tau * n_orbits as f64;
let q0 = [1.0 - e, 0.0];
let p0 = [0.0, RealScalar::sqrt((1.0 + e) / (1.0 - e))];
// Exact invariants H = −1/2, L = √(1 − e²) (for verification).
let angular0 = RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 - e * e);
let deviations = move |q: &[f64], p: &[f64]| {
    let energy = 0.5 * (p[0] * p[0] + p[1] * p[1]) - 1.0 / RealScalar::hypot(q[0], q[1]);
    let angular = q[0] * p[1] - q[1] * p[0];
    ((energy + 0.5).abs(), (angular - angular0).abs())
};
// Maximum |ΔH| and |ΔL| within each orbit.
let record = |table: &mut Vec<[f64; 2]>, orbit: usize, d: (f64, f64)| {
    let row = &mut table[orbit.min(n_orbits - 1)];
    row[0] = row[0].max(d.0);
    row[1] = row[1].max(d.1);
};
let mut per_orbit = vec![vec![[0.0_f64; 2]; n_orbits]; 3];
let mut rows = Vec::new();

// Störmer–Verlet: ∇T(p) = p, ∇V(q) = q/|q|³.
let hamiltonian = HamiltonianFn::new(
    2,
    |p: &[f64], velocity: &mut [f64]| {
        velocity.copy_from_slice(p);
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    },
    |q: &[f64], gradient: &mut [f64]| {
        let r3 = RealScalar::powi(RealScalar::hypot(q[0], q[1]), 3);
        gradient[0] = q[0] / r3;
        gradient[1] = q[1] / r3;
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    },
);
let mut state = PhaseSpace::new(q0.to_vec(), p0.to_vec()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = StormerVerlet
    .workspace(&hamiltonian)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let report = StormerVerlet
    .advance(
        &hamiltonian,
        h,
        n_orbits * m,
        &MechanicalOptions::new(),
        &mut state,
        &mut |n: usize, q: &[f64], p: &[f64]| {
            record(&mut per_orbit[0], (n - 1) / m, deviations(q, p));
            Ok(())
        },
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let counts = report.counts();
rows.push((
    "Störmer–Verlet",
    state.position().to_vec(),
    report.accepted_steps(),
    counts.provider(EvaluationRole::Gradient) + counts.provider(EvaluationRole::RightHandSide),
    report
        .termination()
        .map_or_else(|| "None".to_string(), |t| format!("{t:?}")),
));

// RK4 and Dormand–Prince 8(5,3): y = (q₁, q₂, p₁, p₂).
let system = OdeFn::new(4, |_: f64, y: &[f64], dydt: &mut [f64]| {
    let r3 = RealScalar::powi(RealScalar::hypot(y[0], y[1]), 3);
    dydt[0] = y[2];
    dydt[1] = y[3];
    dydt[2] = -y[0] / r3;
    dydt[3] = -y[1] / r3;
    Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
})
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = OdeProblem::new(&system, end);
let epsilon = 10.0_f64.powf(tolerance_exponent);
let adaptive_tolerance =
    ErrorTolerance::scalar(epsilon, epsilon, NormSelection::RootMeanSquare)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
for (k, name) in [(1, "Classical RK4"), (2, "Dormand–Prince 8(5,3)")] {
    let mut state = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![q0[0], q0[1], p0[0], p0[1]]));
    let table = &mut per_orbit[k];
    let mut sink = |t: f64, y: &[f64]| {
        // Include endpoint t = 2πN in the last orbit.
        record(table, (t / tau) as usize, deviations(&y[..2], &y[2..]));
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    };
    let report = if k == 1 {
        let options = OdeOptions::new()
            .with_initial_step(h)
            .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::AcceptedSteps);
        let method = ClassicalRk4::new();
        let mut workspace = method
            .workspace(&problem, &options)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        method.advance(
            &problem,
            &options,
            &mut state,
            &mut sink,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
    } else {
        let options = OdeOptions::new()
            .with_error_tolerance(adaptive_tolerance.clone())
            .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::AcceptedSteps);
        let method = DormandPrince853::new();
        let mut workspace = method
            .workspace(&problem, &options)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        method.advance(
            &problem,
            &options,
            &mut state,
            &mut sink,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
    }
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        name,
        state.values()[..2].to_vec(),
        report.accepted_steps(),
        report.counts().provider(EvaluationRole::RightHandSide),
        report
            .termination()
            .map_or_else(|| "None".to_string(), |t| t.to_string()),
    ));
}

let table: Vec<_> = rows
    .into_iter()
    .zip(&per_orbit)
    .map(|((name, q, steps, evaluations, termination), orbit)| {
        let worst = orbit
            .iter()
            .fold([0.0_f64; 2], |w, d| [w[0].max(d[0]), w[1].max(d[1])]);
        let distance = RealScalar::hypot(q[0] - q0[0], q[1] - q0[1]);
        (
            name,
            worst[0],
            worst[1],
            distance,
            steps,
            evaluations,
            termination,
        )
    })
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum |H + 1/2| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum |L − √(1 − e²)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "|q_N − q₀| after N orbits [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Accepted steps", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination status" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    table,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "kepler" })
);

let names = ["Störmer–Verlet", "Classical RK4", "Dormand–Prince 8(5,3)"];
for (k, label) in [
    (0, "Per-orbit maximum |H + 1/2| [-]"),
    (1, "Per-orbit maximum |L − √(1 − e²)| [-]"),
] {
    // Omit zero errors from logarithmic axes.
    let series: Vec<_> = (0..3)
.map(|method| {
let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = per_orbit[method]
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .filter(|(_, d)| d[k] > 0.0)
    .map(|(orbit, d)| [(orbit + 1) as f64, d[k]])
    .collect();
serde_json::json!({ "name": names[method], "points": points, "style": { "showSymbol": false } })
})
.collect();
    emit_line_chart!(
        &series,
        options = &serde_json::json!({
            "link": { "group": "kepler-orbits" },
            "xLabel": "Orbit number [-]",
            "yLabel": label,
            "xType": "log",
            "yType": "log"
        })
    );
}
```

With defaults $e = 0.5$, $N = 500$ orbits, and $m = 100$, Störmer–Verlet per-orbit maximum $|H + 1/2|$ stays at $5.42 \times 10^{-3}$ from orbit 1 through 500, unchanged to four digits.
RK4 error grows in proportion to the orbit count from first-orbit $1.7 \times 10^{-5}$, exceeding Störmer–Verlet near orbit 365 and reaching $7.4 \times 10^{-3}$ at orbit 500.
The log–log plot shows approximately slope-1 lines for RK4 and Dormand–Prince and a horizontal Störmer–Verlet line.
Dormand–Prince ($\varepsilon = 10^{-10}$) has small 500-orbit error $1.8 \times 10^{-8}$, still growing proportionally with revolutions.

Störmer–Verlet angular-momentum error stays at rounding magnitude, at most $2.5 \times 10^{-14}$; RK4 grows to $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ and Dormand–Prince to $1.1 \times 10^{-8}$.
After 500 orbits, position offsets are $0.76$ for Störmer–Verlet, $1.94$ for RK4, and $1.4 \times 10^{-4}$ for Dormand–Prince.
Störmer–Verlet preserves invariants, but phase error accumulates per orbit, producing an orbital-scale position offset after 500 revolutions.

Evaluation counts are 100001 for Störmer–Verlet, 200004 for RK4, and 329954 for Dormand–Prince.
At equal evaluation counts, Störmer–Verlet can take twice as many steps as RK4.
At $e = 0.8$, perihelion distance is 0.2, giving larger errors for both fixed-step methods at 100 steps per orbit.
Störmer–Verlet energy error grows to $0.37$ while angular-momentum error remains at $9.7 \times 10^{-15}$; RK4's $|\Delta H| = 13.6$ fails to preserve the orbit.
Dormand–Prince shrinks steps near perihelion, maintaining $|\Delta H| \le 9.1 \times 10^{-8}$ in 29501 steps.
Loosening tolerance to $10^{-6}$ increases Dormand–Prince error to $7.8 \times 10^{-4}$ over 500 orbits.

## Summary

Störmer–Verlet Kepler energy errors remain within a revolution-independent range, while angular momentum is preserved to rounding.
Same-step RK4 and tolerance-adaptive Dormand–Prince 8(5,3) accumulate energy errors each revolution.
Even preserved invariants allow growing phase error, giving large Störmer–Verlet position offsets after $N$ orbits.
High eccentricity leaves fixed steps too coarse at perihelion; increase steps per orbit or tighten adaptive tolerances.
Choose Störmer–Verlet for long-term statistical properties such as energy distributions or orbit shape; choose tightly controlled adaptive methods for accurate positions at specific times.
