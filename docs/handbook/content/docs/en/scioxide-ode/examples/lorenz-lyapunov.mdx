Lorenz truncates atmospheric thermal convection to three modes and illustrates deterministic solutions exponentially magnifying small initial differences.
Lyapunov exponents describe how fast initial differences spread; positive exponents prevent long-term state prediction.
This example integrates variational (tangent) equations with the solution through forward sensitivities, reorthogonalizing tangents by QR at fixed intervals to obtain three exponents.
Compare against the exact Jacobian-trace sum, literature, and the largest exponent from nearby-trajectory separation.

## Lorenz system

For state $y = (x, y_2, z)$, the Lorenz system is

$$
x' = \sigma (y_2 - x), \qquad
y_2' = x (\rho - z) - y_2, \qquad
z' = x y_2 - \beta z
$$


Here $\sigma$ is the Prandtl number, $\rho$ the Rayleigh number divided by its critical value, and $\beta$ a constant determined by the convection-cell aspect ratio; all are dimensionless.
Time is dimensionless too; omit units below.
Fix Lorenz's (1963) values $\sigma = 10$ and $\beta = 8/3$, taking $\rho$ as input.

For $\rho > 1$, two equilibria $C_\pm = (\pm\sqrt{\beta(\rho - 1)}, \pm\sqrt{\beta(\rho - 1)}, \rho - 1)$ appear.
The equilibria $C_\pm$ are linearly stable at $\rho < \rho_H = \sigma(\sigma + \beta + 3)/(\sigma - \beta - 1) \approx 24.74$ and unstable at $\rho = 28$, where solutions approach a chaotic orbit switching irregularly around both (the Lorenz attractor).

## Variational equations and exponent sum

An infinitesimal perturbation $v(t)$ along solution $y(t)$ satisfies the linearized variational equation

$$
v' = J(y(t))\,v, \qquad
J(y) = \begin{pmatrix} -\sigma & \sigma & 0 \\ \rho - z & -1 & -x \\ y_2 & x & -\beta \end{pmatrix}
$$


The $3 \times 3$ matrix $\Phi(t)$ mapping initial perturbation $v(0)$ to $v(t) = \Phi(t)\,v(0)$ is the fundamental matrix, with each column an initial-component sensitivity $\partial y(t)/\partial y(0)$.
Oseledets' multiplicative ergodic theorem gives logarithmic singular-value growth rates for $s_1(t) \ge s_2(t) \ge s_3(t)$ of $\Phi(t)$,

$$
\lambda_i = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{1}{t} \ln s_i(t)
$$

existing for almost every initial value and independent of trajectory choice.
These are Lyapunov exponents; for $\lambda_1 > 0$, nearby trajectories separate on average as $e^{\lambda_1 t}$.
For bounded orbits that do not converge to equilibria, tangential perturbations neither grow nor decay, so one exponent is zero.

Lorenz $J$ has constant trace $\operatorname{tr} J = -(\sigma + 1 + \beta)$.
Liouville formula $\det \Phi(t) = \exp\left(\int_0^t \operatorname{tr} J\,ds\right)$ and $\det \Phi = s_1 s_2 s_3$ give exponent sum

$$
\lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = -(\sigma + 1 + \beta) = -\frac{41}{3} \approx -13.667
$$


This is independent of trajectory duration and statistical fluctuations, providing an accuracy check.
Individual $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_3$ lack analytical expressions; Sprott (*Chaos and Time-Series Analysis*, Oxford University Press, 2003) reports $(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3) \approx (0.9056, 0, -14.5723)$ at $\rho = 28$.

## Reorthogonalizing tangent vectors

Directly integrating $\Phi(t)$ aligns all three columns with the fastest-growing direction, hiding components contracting by ratio $e^{(\lambda_1 - \lambda_3)t}$ in rounding.
Benettin, Galgani, Giorgilli, and Strelcyn (*Meccanica* 15, 1980) and Shimada and Nagashima (*Prog. Theor. Phys.* 61, 1979) avoid this by reorthogonalizing every $\Delta$ time units.
Integrate variational equations from orthonormal frame $Q_k$ at $t_k = k\Delta$ to the interval end, then factor by QR:

$$
\Phi(t_{k+1}, t_k)\,Q_k = Q_{k+1} R_{k+1}, \qquad
\lambda_i \approx \frac{1}{t_K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \ln \left| (R_k)_{ii} \right|
$$


The factor $Q_{k+1}$ initializes the next tangents.
Since $\Phi(t_K, 0) = Q_K R_K \cdots R_1$, diagonals of the upper-triangular product represent directional stretching.
Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization gives the same values instead of QR.

The fastest-growing/contracting column ratio over $\Delta$ is approximately $e^{(\lambda_1 - \lambda_3)\Delta}$.
Beyond double-precision $1/\varepsilon \approx 4.5 \times 10^{15}$, QR constructs its third column from rounding error and loses $\lambda_3$.
For Lorenz, $\lambda_1 - \lambda_3 \approx 15.5$, making $\Delta < \ln(1/\varepsilon)/15.5 \approx 2.3$ a practical guide.
Smaller $\Delta$ increases QR work without changing exponent estimates.

## Propagating tangents with forward sensitivities

The type `ForwardSensitivitySystem` assembles state $y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $k$ sensitivities $s_1, \ldots, s_k$ into the dimension-$n(k + 1)$ system

$$
y' = f(t, y, p), \qquad
s_j' = \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} s_j + \frac{\partial f}{\partial p} d_j \quad (j = 1, \ldots, k)
$$

as an ordinary `OdeSystem`.
Here $p \in \mathbb{R}^m$ is a real parameter vector and $d_j$ a parameter-space direction.
A type implementing `OdeSensitivitySystem` supplies right-hand side and directional derivative through `rhs` and `tangent_rhs`; `tangent_rhs` writes $(\partial f/\partial y)\,s + (\partial f/\partial p)\,d$.
The `parameter_directions` of `ForwardSensitivitySystem::bind` is a $k \times m$ matrix with row $j$ equal to $d_j$.

Setting all rows to zero removes parameter terms, leaving only $s_j' = J s_j$ and initial-value sensitivities.
Supply `initialize_into` with a $n \times k$ matrix whose columns are initial tangents, constructing augmented state $[y, s_1, \ldots, s_k]$.
A unit-vector column $e_i$ gives sensitivity to initial component $y_i(0)$.
Thus no separate tangent-equation implementation as `OdeSystem` is needed here.
With $n = 3$ and $k = 3$, augmentation has dimension 12; each right-hand-side evaluation calls `rhs` once and `tangent_rhs` three times.
Calls to `tangent_rhs` count as `LinearOperator` evaluations in solver records.

At interval ends, `extract_into` splits augmented state into $y$ and $3 \times 3$ matrix $[s_1, s_2, s_3]$, factorized by `HouseholderDecomposition::factor`.
Supply resulting $Q$ to `initialize_into` and replace state through `OdeState::reset` for the next interval.
Each `OdeMethod::advance` restarts the method from state, without carrying previous step size.
Initial-step estimation therefore repeats per interval; shorter intervals increase right-hand-side evaluations.

Construct tolerances through `ForwardSensitivitySystem::error_tolerance`.
States and sensitivities can have separate absolute tolerances, sharing relative tolerance.
Explicit `DormandPrince5` uses no Jacobian, avoiding augmented finite-difference Jacobian costs of implicit methods.

The following block supplies shared definitions for three cells.
The type `Lorenz` implements `OdeSensitivitySystem` with parameters $p = (\sigma, \rho, \beta)$; `tangent_rhs` writes products with $J$ plus parameter-direction terms.
The function `lorenz_rhs` also supplies the right-hand side for the two-trajectory cell.
The function `batch_means_2se` estimates correlated-sequence mean standard errors by batch means, returning twice that value.
Divide the sequence into $B$ equal-length blocks, then divide sample standard deviation of block means $\bar{\ell}_b$ by $\sqrt{B}$.
Blocks much longer than correlation length have approximately independent means, providing a finite-time-average fluctuation guide.

```rust
//| id: ode-lorenz-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Lorenz right-hand side and directional derivatives"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar, SolveContext};
use scioxide_ode::OdeSensitivitySystem;

/// Prandtl σ and geometric β (Lorenz 1963 values).
pub(crate) const SIGMA: f64 = 10.0;
pub(crate) const BETA: f64 = 8.0 / 3.0;

/// Lorenz right-hand side f(y; σ, ρ, β).
pub(crate) fn lorenz_rhs(y: &[f64], p: [f64; 3], out: &mut [f64]) {
    let [sigma, rho, beta] = p;
    out[0] = sigma * (y[1] - y[0]);
    out[1] = y[0] * (rho - y[2]) - y[1];
    out[2] = y[0] * y[1] - beta * y[2];
}

/// Lorenz system with parameters p = (σ, ρ, β).
pub(crate) struct Lorenz;

impl OdeSensitivitySystem<f64> for Lorenz {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        3
    }
    fn parameter_dimension(&self) -> usize {
        3
    }
    fn rhs(
        &self,
        _time: f64,
        state: &[f64],
        parameters: &[f64],
        output: &mut [f64],
        _scratch: &mut [f64],
        _cx: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        lorenz_rhs(state, [parameters[0], parameters[1], parameters[2]], output);
        Ok(())
    }
    /// Write (∂f/∂y) v + (∂f/∂p) d. ∂f/∂y is J in the text.
    fn tangent_rhs(
        &self,
        _time: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        p: &[f64],
        v: &[f64],
        d: &[f64],
        output: &mut [f64],
        _scratch: &mut [f64],
        _cx: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        output[0] = p[0] * (v[1] - v[0]) + d[0] * (y[1] - y[0]);
        output[1] = (p[1] - y[2]) * v[0] - v[1] - y[0] * v[2] + d[1] * y[0];
        output[2] = y[1] * v[0] + y[0] * v[1] - p[2] * v[2] - d[2] * y[2];
        Ok(())
    }
}

/// Split the sequence into batches blocks and return twice the standard error of block means.
/// None if blocks contain fewer than two values.
pub(crate) fn batch_means_2se(values: &[f64], batches: usize) -> Option<f64> {
    let size = values.len() / batches;
    if size < 2 {
        return None;
    }
    let means: Vec<f64> = values
        .chunks_exact(size)
        .take(batches)
        .map(|chunk| chunk.iter().sum::<f64>() / size as f64)
        .collect();
    let mean = means.iter().sum::<f64>() / batches as f64;
    let variance = means.iter().map(|m| (m - mean).powi(2)).sum::<f64>() / (batches - 1) as f64;
    Some(2.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(variance / batches as f64))
}
```

## Computing the Lyapunov spectrum

The next cell selects $\rho$, reorthogonalization interval $\Delta$, integration duration $T$, and tolerance $\varepsilon$ (shared absolute and relative state/sensitivity values), computing three exponents with `DormandPrince5`.
Start from $(1, 1, 1)$ at $t = -20$, discard the transient through $t = 0$, reset tangents to identity, and accumulate exponents over $[0, T]$.
The cell outcome indicates whether all intervals reached their endpoints; diagnostics show three exponents, sum difference from $-41/3$, and accepted steps.
The next cell draws exponent tables and running estimates.
This cell passes $\rho$, $\Delta$, $\varepsilon$, points of $t = 0$, exponents and 2SE, and running estimates to later cells.

```rust
//| id: ode-lorenz-lyapunov-spectrum
//| ref: ode-lorenz-lyapunov-spectrum
//| title: "Lorenz Lyapunov spectrum with QR reorthogonalization"
//| run: button
//| cache: inputs
//| uses: [ode-lorenz-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| exports: [rho, interval, tolerance, start, exponents, two_se, running]
//| inputs:
//|   rho: {"type":"range","label":"ρ [-]","description":"Rayleigh-number ratio. Below 24.74, equilibria C± are stable.","min":10,"max":60,"step":0.5,"value":28}
//|   interval: {"type":"range","label":"Reorthogonalization interval Δ [-]","description":"Time between QR tangent reorthogonalizations.","min":0.05,"max":5,"step":0.05,"value":1}
//|   horizon: {"type":"range","label":"Integration duration T [-]","description":"Duration over which to accumulate exponents.","min":100,"max":1000,"step":50,"value":500}
//|   tolerance_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Tolerance exponent p (ε = 10^p)","description":"Vary absolute and relative tolerances together for solution and sensitivities.","min":-10,"max":-5,"step":1,"value":-8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"stable-equilibrium","label":"ρ = 10","description":"Stable equilibria make exponents approach real parts of their Jacobian eigenvalues.","inputs":{"rho":10}}
//|   - {"id":"long-interval","label":"Δ = 5","description":"Interval stretching ratio exceeds 1/ε, losing λ₃ and the exponent sum.","inputs":{"interval":5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/0/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/0/diagnostics/0/value","approx":{"value":0.9056,"atol":0.03}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/0/diagnostics/1/value","range":{"min":-0.02,"max":0.02}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/0/diagnostics/2/value","approx":{"value":-14.5723,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/0/diagnostics/3/value","range":{"max":1e-5}}
//|   - case: stable-equilibrium
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/0/diagnostics/0/value","approx":{"value":-0.5955,"atol":0.02}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/0/diagnostics/2/value","approx":{"value":-12.4757,"atol":0.02}}
//|   - case: long-interval
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/0/diagnostics/3/value","range":{"min":1}}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::{HouseholderDecomposition, HouseholderOrientation, HouseholderPivoting};
use scioxide_linalg::{Matrix, MatrixView};
use scioxide_ode::{DormandPrince5, ForwardSensitivitySystem, ForwardSensitivityWorkspace};
use scioxide_ode::{OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeProblem, OdeState};

let parameters = [SIGMA, rho, BETA];
// Set all parameter-direction rows to zero, propagating only initial-value sensitivities.
let directions = MatrixView::from_slice(3, 3, &[0.0; 9]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut binding = ForwardSensitivityWorkspace::new();
let system = ForwardSensitivitySystem::bind(&Lorenz, &parameters, directions, &mut binding)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let epsilon = 10.0_f64.powf(tolerance_exponent);
let tolerance = system
    .error_tolerance(epsilon, epsilon, epsilon, NormSelection::Infinity)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = OdeOptions::new().with_error_tolerance(tolerance);
let method = DormandPrince5::new();
// Discard t = −20 to 0, resetting tangents to identity at t = 0.
let mut frame = Matrix::identity(3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut augmented = vec![0.0; system.augmented_dimension()];
system
    .initialize_into(&[1.0, 1.0, 1.0], frame.view(), &mut augmented)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut state = OdeState::from_slice(-20.0, &augmented).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut ws = method
    .workspace(&OdeProblem::new(&system, 0.0), &options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let intervals = (horizon / interval).round() as usize;
let stride = (intervals / 600).max(1);
let (mut y, mut start) = ([0.0; 3], [0.0; 3]);
let mut tangents = Matrix::zeros(3, 3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut log_sums, mut rates) = ([0.0_f64; 3], vec![Vec::new(); 3]);
let (mut running, mut reached, mut accepted, mut status) = (Vec::new(), 0.0, 0, None);
for k in 0..=intervals {
    let end = k as f64 * interval;
    let (problem, cx) = (
        OdeProblem::new(&system, end),
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    );
    let report = method
        .advance(&problem, &options, &mut state, &mut (), &mut ws, cx)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    accepted += report.accepted_steps();
    status = report.termination();
    if !report.is_success() {
        break;
    }
    system
        .extract_into(state.values(), &mut y, tangents.view_mut())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if k == 0 {
        start = y;
    } else {
        // Φ(t_k, t_{k−1}) Q_{k−1} = Q_k R_k. Accumulate logarithms of |(R_k)_ii|.
        let qr = HouseholderDecomposition::factor(
            &tangents,
            HouseholderOrientation::Qr,
            HouseholderPivoting::None,
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        for (i, r) in qr.triangular_diagonal().iter().enumerate() {
            let growth = RealScalar::ln(r.abs());
            if !growth.is_finite() {
                return Err(format!("At t = {end}, R diagonal entry {i} became zero"));
            }
            log_sums[i] += growth;
            rates[i].push(growth / interval);
        }
        frame = qr.to_q_matrix().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        reached = end;
        if k % stride == 0 || k == intervals {
            running.push([end, log_sums[0] / end, log_sums[1] / end, log_sums[2] / end]);
        }
    }
    system
        .initialize_into(&y, frame.view(), &mut augmented)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    state.reset(end, &augmented).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
if reached == 0.0 {
    return Err(format!("First-interval integration stopped with {status:?}"));
}
let exponents = log_sums.map(|s| s / reached);
let sum_error = (exponents.iter().sum::<f64>() + SIGMA + 1.0 + BETA).abs();
export!(rho, rho);
export!(interval, interval);
export!(tolerance, epsilon);
export!(start, start);
export!(exponents, exponents);
export!(
    two_se,
    rates
        .iter()
        .map(|r| batch_means_2se(r, 10))
        .collect::<Vec<_>>()
);
export!(running, running);

let outcome = if status.is_some_and(|t| t.is_success()) {
    Outcome::satisfied("Every interval reached its endpoint")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Integration stopped early; exponents are estimates through the stopping time")
        .explanation("Reconsider tolerance or interval Δ.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(status.map_or("None".to_string(), |t| t.to_string()))
        .diagnostic("λ₁", exponents[0], None)
        .diagnostic("λ₂", exponents[1], None)
        .diagnostic("λ₃", exponents[2], None)
        .diagnostic("Sum difference from −41/3", sum_error, None)
        .diagnostic("Accepted steps", accepted as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Exponent accumulation time", reached, None)
);
```

With defaults ($\rho = 28$, $\Delta = 1$, $T = 500$, $\varepsilon = 10^{-8}$), all 500 intervals end with `ReachedEnd`, totaling 71518 accepted steps.
Exponents are $\lambda_1 = 0.9092$, $\lambda_2 = -0.0020$, and $\lambda_3 = -14.5738$; sum $-13.66666609$ differs from exact $-41/3$ by $5.7 \times 10^{-7}$.
Tolerance $10^{-10}$ gives sum difference $7.5 \times 10^{-9}$, while $10^{-5}$ gives $1.1 \times 10^{-3}$, linking sum drift to integration accuracy.

At $\rho = 10$, equilibrium $C_\pm$ is stable, and trajectories approach $C_\pm$.
Exponents then approach real parts of $J$ eigenvalues at $C_\pm$; characteristic polynomial $\mu^3 + (\sigma + \beta + 1)\mu^2 + \beta(\sigma + \rho)\mu + 2\sigma\beta(\rho - 1)$ has conjugate-pair real parts $-0.5955$ and real root $-12.4757$.
Computed exponents $-0.5978$, $-0.5952$, and $-12.4737$ differ from these real parts by at most 0.003.
The remainder likely reflects stretching during approach from the $t = 0$ point to equilibrium, averaged with weight $1/T$.

At $\Delta = 5$, $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ stay within 2SE, but $\lambda_3$ becomes $-6.689$ and the sum $-5.774$, raising sum error to 7.89.
Interval stretching ratio $e^{(\lambda_1 - \lambda_3) \cdot 5} \approx e^{77}$ greatly exceeds $1/\varepsilon$, constructing the third column from rounding error.
At $\Delta = 0.05$, $\lambda_1 = 0.9049$, with sum difference $4.5 \times 10^{-8}$.
Exponents agree with the $\Delta = 1$ case within 2SE, but accepted steps rise to 89567.
Each `advance` reestimates initial step size, making short intervals start with smaller steps.

## Growth of initial differences

Nearby trajectory distances provide a direct view without tangent equations.
The next cell receives $\rho$, $\varepsilon$, and point $y(0)$ of $t = 0$, integrating two trajectories with $\tilde{y}(0) = y(0) + (d_0, 0, 0)$ as one six-dimensional system through $t \in [0, 40]$ using `DormandPrince5`.
The logarithmic-distance plot shows $|\tilde{y}(t) - y(t)|$ every 0.1, overlaying dashed $d_0 e^{\lambda_1 t}$ based on preceding $\lambda_1$.
Small distances grow on average with that slope, saturating near attractor size, whose coordinate range is tens.

```rust
//| id: ode-lorenz-separation
//| ref: ode-lorenz-separation
//| title: "Distance between nearby trajectories"
//| run: button
//| uses: [ode-lorenz-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-ode]
//| dependsOn:
//|   spectrum: ode-lorenz-lyapunov-spectrum
//| inputs:
//|   separation_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Initial-distance exponent q (d₀ = 10^q)","description":"Initial separation of two trajectories.","min":-12,"max":-2,"step":1,"value":-8}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_ode::OdeState;
use scioxide_ode::{DormandPrince5, OdeFn, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule, OdeProblem};

let rho = spectrum["rho"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing ρ")?;
let epsilon = spectrum["tolerance"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing tolerance")?;
let lambda1 = spectrum["exponents"][0].as_f64().ok_or("Missing λ₁")?;
let y0: [f64; 3] =
    serde_json::from_value(spectrum["start"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let d0 = 10.0_f64.powf(separation_exponent);
let rhs = OdeFn::new(6, move |_t: f64, y: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
    lorenz_rhs(&y[..3], [SIGMA, rho, BETA], &mut out[..3]);
    lorenz_rhs(&y[3..], [SIGMA, rho, BETA], &mut out[3..]);
    Ok(())
})
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(epsilon, epsilon, NormSelection::Infinity)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let times: Vec<f64> = (1..=400).map(|i| 0.1 * i as f64).collect();
let options = OdeOptions::new()
    .with_error_tolerance(tolerance)
    .with_output_schedule(OdeOutputSchedule::times(times));
let initial = OdeState::from_slice(0.0, &[y0[0], y0[1], y0[2], y0[0] + d0, y0[1], y0[2]])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solution = DormandPrince5::new()
    .solve(&OdeProblem::new(&rhs, 40.0), &options, initial)
    .map_err(|e| e.error().to_string())?;
let trajectory = solution.trajectory();
let separation: Vec<[f64; 2]> = trajectory
    .times()
    .iter()
    .zip(trajectory.states())
    .map(|(&t, s)| {
        let y = s.as_slice();
        [
            t,
            RealScalar::sqrt((0..3).map(|i| (y[i + 3] - y[i]).powi(2)).sum::<f64>()),
        ]
    })
    .collect();
// Reference d₀ e^{λ₁ t} (f64 standard function). Omit portions above distance 100.
let reference: Vec<[f64; 2]> = separation
    .iter()
    .map(|p| [p[0], d0 * (lambda1 * p[0]).exp()])
    .take_while(|p| p[1] < 100.0)
    .collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "xLabel": "Time t [-]",
    "yLabel": "Two-trajectory distance [-]",
    "yType": "log",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "layers": [
        { "id": "distance", "kind": "line", "name": "|ỹ(t) − y(t)|", "points": separation },
        {
            "id": "growth",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "d₀ e^(λ₁ t) (QR λ₁)",
            "points": reference,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
        }
    ]
}));
```

At default $d_0 = 10^{-8}$, distance exceeds $10^{-6}$ at $t = 4.3$, $10^{-4}$ at $t = 9.0$, and $10^{-2}$ at $t = 10.9$, reaches 1 at $t = 18.8$ and 10 at $t = 21.8$.
It then saturates near attractor size, with maximum 44.6.
Growth varies along the attractor, but averages along dashed slope $\lambda_1$.
At $d_0 = 10^{-12}$, the whole curve starts four orders lower, reaching distance 1 at $t = 29.4$.
This is a delay of 10.6 relative to $d_0 = 10^{-8}$, close to $\ln(10^{4})/\lambda_1 \approx 10.1$.
Reducing initial error by one order extends predictability by only $\ln 10/\lambda_1 \approx 2.5$.

## Independent estimate from two-trajectory separation

Benettin et al.'s original paper measures separation every $\Delta$ to estimate $\lambda_1$.
After measuring distance $d_k = |\tilde{y}(t_k) - y(t_k)|$, reset $\tilde{y}$ to distance $d_0$ from $y$ in the same direction (renormalization),

$$
\lambda_1 \approx \frac{1}{K\Delta} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \ln\frac{d_k}{d_0}
$$


For small $d_0$, difference $\tilde{y} - y$ approximates the variational solution and aligns with the fastest-growing direction.
Resetting before nonlinear effects become significant prevents the saturation shown above.

The next cell repeats renormalization using the same $d_0$ and six-dimensional system, over the preceding exponent-accumulation duration $T$ and intervals $\Delta$.
The table compares two-trajectory $\lambda_1$ with tangent/QR $\lambda_1$.
Both start at the same point, but error amplification $e^{\lambda_1 t}$ makes their long-time averages follow different trajectories.
Compare differences with combined uncertainty $\sqrt{(2\mathrm{SE}_1)^2 + (2\mathrm{SE}_2)^2}$ from both estimates' 2SE.

```rust
//| id: ode-lorenz-twin-trajectories
//| ref: ode-lorenz-twin-trajectories
//| title: "Largest Lyapunov exponent from two-trajectory separation"
//| run: button
//| uses: [ode-lorenz-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-ode]
//| dependsOn:
//|   spectrum: ode-lorenz-lyapunov-spectrum
//| inputs:
//|   separation_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Initial-distance exponent q (d₀ = 10^q)","description":"Initial separation of two trajectories.","min":-12,"max":-2,"step":1,"value":-8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"tiny-separation","label":"d₀ = 10⁻¹²","description":"Initial separation below tolerance still gives estimates unchanged within 2SE.","inputs":{"separation_exponent":-12}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.9,"atol":0.05}}
//|   - case: tiny-separation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.9,"atol":0.05}}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_ode::{DormandPrince5, OdeFn, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeProblem, OdeState};

let read = |key: &str| spectrum[key].as_f64().ok_or(format!("Missing {key}"));
let (rho, interval, epsilon) = (read("rho")?, read("interval")?, read("tolerance")?);
let y0: [f64; 3] =
    serde_json::from_value(spectrum["start"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let horizon = spectrum["running"]
    .as_array()
    .and_then(|r| r.last())
    .and_then(|r| r[0].as_f64());
let horizon = horizon.ok_or("Missing exponent-accumulation duration")?;
let d0 = 10.0_f64.powf(separation_exponent);
let rhs = OdeFn::new(6, move |_t: f64, y: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
    lorenz_rhs(&y[..3], [SIGMA, rho, BETA], &mut out[..3]);
    lorenz_rhs(&y[3..], [SIGMA, rho, BETA], &mut out[3..]);
    Ok(())
})
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(epsilon, epsilon, NormSelection::Infinity)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = OdeOptions::new().with_error_tolerance(tolerance);
let method = DormandPrince5::new();
let mut y = [y0[0], y0[1], y0[2], y0[0] + d0, y0[1], y0[2]];
let mut state = OdeState::from_slice(0.0, &y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut ws = method
    .workspace(&OdeProblem::new(&rhs, interval), &options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rates = Vec::new();
for k in 1..=(horizon / interval).round() as usize {
    let end = k as f64 * interval;
    let (problem, cx) = (OdeProblem::new(&rhs, end), ExecutionContext::sequential());
    let report = method
        .advance(&problem, &options, &mut state, &mut (), &mut ws, cx)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if !report.is_success() {
        return Err(format!(
            "Integration through t = {end} stopped with {:?}",
            report.termination()
        ));
    }
    // Measure d_k, then reset the second trajectory to distance d₀ from the first in the same direction.
    y.copy_from_slice(state.values());
    let d = RealScalar::sqrt((0..3).map(|i| (y[i + 3] - y[i]).powi(2)).sum::<f64>());
    rates.push(RealScalar::ln(d / d0) / interval);
    for i in 0..3 {
        y[i + 3] = y[i] + (y[i + 3] - y[i]) * d0 / d;
    }
    state.reset(end, &y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
let twin = rates.iter().sum::<f64>() / rates.len() as f64;
let twin_2se = batch_means_2se(&rates, 10);
let qr = spectrum["exponents"][0].as_f64();
let qr_2se = spectrum["two_se"][0].as_f64();
let combined = qr_2se.zip(twin_2se).map(|(a, b)| RealScalar::hypot(a, b));
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Estimation method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "λ₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "2SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "None" }
]);
let rows = vec![
    ("Tangent equations and QR", qr, qr_2se),
    ("Two-trajectory separation", Some(twin), twin_2se),
    (
        "Absolute difference and combined 2SE",
        qr.map(|a| (a - twin).abs()),
        combined,
    ),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "comparison" })
);
```

At default $d_0 = 10^{-8}$, the two-trajectory estimate is $\lambda_1 = 0.9185$ (2SE 0.020); difference 0.0093 from QR 0.9092 is below combined 2SE 0.032.
At $d_0 = 10^{-12}$ and $d_0 = 10^{-2}$, values 0.9050 and 0.9128 both agree with QR within combined 2SE.
The separation $d_0 = 10^{-12}$ is below local errors from tolerance $10^{-8}$, yet estimates remained unchanged.
The estimate difference is consistent with fluctuations of interval growth-rate time averages.

## Summary

Lyapunov exponents measure fundamental-matrix growth; zero parameter directions in `ForwardSensitivitySystem` and initial tangents supplied to `initialize_into` integrate tangent equations alongside state as initial-value sensitivities.
Uncontrolled tangents align with the fastest-growing direction; reorthogonalize by QR every $\Delta$ and accumulate logarithms of $R$ diagonal entries.
Choose $\Delta$ so stretching ratio $e^{(\lambda_1 - \lambda_3)\Delta}$ does not exceed $1/\varepsilon$ (up to about 2.3 for Lorenz).
Jacobian trace fixes the exponent sum at $-41/3$, providing an accuracy check independent of trajectory fluctuations; assess individual time-average variability with batch-means 2SE against literature or independent estimates.
Two-trajectory separation independently checks tangent equations; here varying $d_0$ from $10^{-12}$ to $10^{-2}$ retained agreement with QR within 2SE.
