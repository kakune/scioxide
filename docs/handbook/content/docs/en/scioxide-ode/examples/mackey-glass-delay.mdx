Delay differential equations describe systems whose rate of change depends on a past state.
Mackey and Glass (*Science* 197, 1977) proposed a model incorporating the delay between blood cell production in the bone marrow and the release of mature cells into the bloodstream, and showed that increasing the delay makes blood cell counts fluctuate periodically and then irregularly.
This example solves the Mackey–Glass equation with `DdePlan` and compares the critical delay and period obtained by linearizing the equilibrium with the onset of oscillations in the numerical solution.
Finally, it examines how a chaotic solution amplifies small differences in the initial history.

## The Mackey–Glass equation

Let $x(t)$ denote the blood cell concentration nondimensionalized by its equilibrium value, with time measured in days.
The Mackey–Glass equation is

$$
x'(t) = \frac{\beta\, x(t - \tau)}{1 + x(t - \tau)^n} - \gamma\, x(t)
$$


The first term on the right represents production in response to the concentration at time $t - \tau$, and the second represents removal from the bloodstream.
The production rate $\beta = 0.2$ [1/day], removal rate $\gamma = 0.1$ [1/day], and Hill coefficient $n = 10$ are the values Mackey and Glass used in their numerical examples; the delay $\tau$ [days] is an input.
The production term increases proportionally when $x(t - \tau)$ is small and decreases through negative feedback when it is large.

Determining a solution of a delay equation requires a history $\varphi(t)$ over the interval $[-\tau, 0]$, in addition to the value at the initial time $t_0 = 0$.
This example uses the constant history $\varphi(t) = x_0$.
Since the state is a function on an interval, a delay equation is an infinite-dimensional dynamical system.

## Integration by the method of steps

`DdePlan` integrates equations with constant delays using the method of steps.
Because the step size is bounded by the smallest delay, the delayed value $x(t - \tau)$ comes from either the initial history or the dense output of an already accepted step (fourth-order interpolation for Dormand–Prince 5(4)), so no iteration within a step is needed.
The derivative of the solution generally jumps at $t_0$ ($\varphi'(0) = 0$, but $x'(0)$ is determined by the right-hand side), and this jump propagates to the second derivative at $t_0 + \tau$ and the third derivative at $t_0 + 2\tau$.
`DdePlan::new` prepares a table of these discontinuities through order five in advance and aligns step boundaries with them.
Error control uses the tolerances in the `OdeOptions` passed to `DdeOptions::with_ode_options`.
The local error estimate does not include error in the interpolated history.
[Delay differential equations and the method of steps](../../algorithms/delay-equations/) discusses the propagation of discontinuities and the role of dense output in detail.

## Equilibrium linearization and the characteristic equation

When $x(t) = x^*$ is constant, $\beta/(1 + x^{*n}) = \gamma$, so the positive equilibrium is $x^{*n} = \beta/\gamma - 1 = 1$, or $x^* = 1$.
The derivative of the production function $g(u) = \beta u/(1 + u^n)$ is

$$
g'(u) = \beta\,\frac{1 + (1 - n)u^n}{(1 + u^n)^2}
$$

which gives $a = g'(1) = \beta(2 - n)/4 = -0.4$ [1/day] at $x^* = 1$.
A small perturbation $u(t) = x(t) - 1$ satisfies the linear delay equation

$$
u'(t) = -\gamma\, u(t) + a\, u(t - \tau)
$$


Substituting $u = e^{\lambda t}$ gives the characteristic equation

$$
\lambda = -\gamma + a\, e^{-\lambda \tau}
$$


Unlike an ordinary differential equation without delay, this equation has infinitely many roots, and the equilibrium is asymptotically stable if every root has a negative real part.
At $\tau = 0$, the only root is $\lambda = -\gamma + a = -0.5$, and the equilibrium is stable.

## Critical delay and period at onset

As $\tau$ increases, a pair of complex conjugate roots crosses the imaginary axis at some value $\tau_c$.
Substituting $\lambda = i\omega$ ($\omega > 0$) into the characteristic equation and separating real and imaginary parts gives

$$
0 = -\gamma + a\cos(\omega\tau), \qquad
\omega = -a\sin(\omega\tau)
$$


Squaring and adding the two equations gives $\omega^2 = a^2 - \gamma^2$, hence

$$
\omega_c = \sqrt{0.16 - 0.01} = \sqrt{0.15} \approx 0.3873 \ \text{[rad/day]}
$$


Since $\cos(\omega\tau) = \gamma/a = -1/4$ and $\sin(\omega\tau) = \omega/|a| > 0$, $\omega\tau$ lies in the second quadrant, and

$$
\tau_c = \frac{\arccos(-1/4)}{\omega_c} = \frac{1.8235}{0.3873} \approx 4.708 \ \text{[days]}, \qquad
P_c = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_c} \approx 16.22 \ \text{[days]}
$$


When $\tau$ exceeds $\tau_c$, the real parts of this pair of roots become positive, the equilibrium becomes unstable, and oscillations with a period near $P_c$ begin (a Hopf bifurcation).
Further increases in $\tau$ lead through period-doubling bifurcations to aperiodic chaotic oscillations near $\tau = 17$.

The rightmost root at $\tau \ne \tau_c$ has no closed-form expression.
The next cell starts at the root $i\omega_c$ for $\tau_c$ and tracks it by applying Newton's method to the characteristic equation while changing $\tau$ in small increments (continuation).
This provides a check independent of the numerical solution; root tracking uses `Complex64` arithmetic.

The following block supplies shared definitions for three cells.
`production` is $g(u)$; `integrate` is the `DdePlan` solve that outputs the solution from a constant history $x_0$ at times spaced by `spacing`; and `rightmost_root` returns the root of the characteristic equation found by the continuation procedure above.

```rust
//| id: ode-mackey-glass-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "The Mackey–Glass equation and roots of its characteristic equation"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{Complex64, ErrorTolerance, SciOxideError, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::MatrixView;
use scioxide_ode::{DdeOptions, DdePlan, DdeProblem, DdeReport, OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule};

/// Production rate β [1/day], removal rate γ [1/day], and Hill coefficient n (Mackey and Glass's values).
pub(crate) const BETA: f64 = 0.2;
pub(crate) const GAMMA: f64 = 0.1;
pub(crate) const HILL: i32 = 10;
/// The value a = β(2 − n)/4 of g′ at the equilibrium x* = 1.
pub(crate) const SLOPE: f64 = BETA * (2 - HILL) as f64 / 4.0;

/// Production function g(u) = βu/(1 + uⁿ).
pub(crate) fn production(u: f64) -> f64 {
    BETA * u / (1.0 + RealScalar::powi(u, HILL))
}

/// Solution from a constant history x₀. times contains times starting at 0 with interval spacing.
pub(crate) struct Path {
    pub(crate) times: Vec<f64>,
    pub(crate) values: Vec<f64>,
    pub(crate) report: DdeReport<f64>,
}

pub(crate) fn integrate(
    tau: f64,
    horizon: f64,
    history: f64,
    tolerance: f64,
    spacing: f64,
) -> Result<Path, String> {
    let rhs = |_t: f64, x: &[f64], delayed: MatrixView<'_, f64>, out: &mut [f64]| {
        out[0] = production(delayed.get(0, 0)?) - GAMMA * x[0];
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    };
    let constant = move |_t: f64, out: &mut [f64]| {
        out[0] = history;
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    };
    let samples = (horizon / spacing).floor() as usize;
    let times = (1..=samples).map(|i| i as f64 * spacing).collect();
    let schedule = OdeOutputSchedule::Times {
        times,
        initial: true,
        final_state: false,
    };
    let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(tolerance, tolerance, NormSelection::Infinity)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let ode = OdeOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(tolerance)
        .with_output_schedule(schedule);
    let options = DdeOptions::new().with_ode_options(ode);
    let plan = DdePlan::new(1, (0.0, horizon), &[tau], &[], options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let solution = plan
        .solve(&DdeProblem::new(&rhs, &constant))
        .map_err(|e| e.error().to_string())?;
    let (_, report, trajectory) = solution.into_parts();
    let (times, states, _) = trajectory.into_parts();
    let values = states.iter().map(|s| s.as_slice()[0]).collect();
    Ok(Path {
        times,
        values,
        report,
    })
}

/// Critical delay τ_c = arccos(γ/a)/ω_c and ω_c = √(a² − γ²) (reference values using f64 standard functions).
pub(crate) fn critical() -> (f64, f64) {
    let omega = (SLOPE * SLOPE - GAMMA * GAMMA).sqrt();
    ((GAMMA / SLOPE).acos() / omega, omega)
}

/// The rightmost root of λ = −γ + a e^{−λτ}. Track it from the root iω_c at τ_c to τ by continuation (for checking).
pub(crate) fn rightmost_root(tau: f64) -> Complex64 {
    let (tau_c, omega_c) = critical();
    let mut lambda = Complex64::new(0.0, omega_c);
    for step in 1..=200 {
        let delay = tau_c + (tau - tau_c) * step as f64 / 200.0;
        for _ in 0..6 {
            let decay = (lambda * Complex64::new(-delay, 0.0)).exp() * SLOPE;
            let residual = lambda + Complex64::new(GAMMA, 0.0) - decay;
            lambda = lambda - residual / (Complex64::new(1.0, 0.0) + decay * delay);
        }
    }
    lambda
}
```

## Onset of oscillations and delay embedding

The next cell selects the delay $\tau$, integration time $T$, and history value $x_0$, and solves with tolerance $10^{-8}$.
The upper plot shows $x(t)$, with the equilibrium $x^* = 1$ drawn as a dashed line.
The lower plot is a delay embedding of the points $(x(t), x(t - \tau))$ from the second half $t \ge T/2$: convergence to equilibrium gives one point, a periodic solution gives a closed curve, and a chaotic solution gives a band-shaped set.
The output spacing is an integer subdivision of $\tau$, so $x(t - \tau)$ is obtained by shifting indices in the output of the same solution.
The table lists $\tau_c$, $\omega_c$, and $P_c$ from the linearization, the rightmost root at this $\tau$, and the amplitude and mean period measured from the second half of the numerical solution.
The amplitude is $(\max x - \min x)/2$ over the second half, and the mean period is the mean interval between upward crossings of $x = 1$.

let late = &values[half..];

The first 3 rows of the table are independent of the inputs: $\omega_c = 0.3873$ rad/day, $\tau_c = 4.7082$ days, and $P_c = 16.2231$ days, matching the derivation above.
With the defaults $\tau = 17$ and $x_0 = 0.5$, integration finishes at `ReachedEnd` with 611 accepted and 122 rejected steps.
In the second half, the solution continues to oscillate irregularly with amplitude 0.44, and the mean interval between upward crossings of $x = 1$ is 42.1 days.
The rightmost root gives $\operatorname{Re}\lambda = 0.0413$ /day and $2\pi/\operatorname{Im}\lambda = 45.2$ days: the linearization indicates that the equilibrium is unstable and gives the oscillation time scale, but nonlinear terms determine the amplitude and waveform.
The delay embedding points form an overlapping band rather than a closed curve.

For the case $\tau = 4$ and $x_0 = 1.2$, $\operatorname{Re}\lambda = -0.0249$ /day, the amplitude in the second half decreases to $5.0 \times 10^{-5}$, and the embedding points gather at $(1, 1)$.
The mean period of 14.431 days in the second half agrees with the linearized value $2\pi/\operatorname{Im}\lambda = 14.430$ days to two decimal places.
At $\tau = 6$, the equilibrium is unstable, the solution settles into a periodic solution of amplitude 0.157, and the embedding points form a single closed curve.
The period of 20.08 days is longer than the linearized value of 19.45 days; the period of nonlinear oscillations of finite amplitude also departs from the linearization.
At $\tau = 2$, the root is $-0.267 \pm 0.662i$ and decay is fast; there are no crossings of $x = 1$ in the second half, so the mean period cannot be determined.

## Comparison with the linearized growth rate

To compare the onset of oscillations quantitatively with the linearization, measure how the amplitude of a solution starting very close to equilibrium grows or decays.
The next cell takes 9 values of $\tau$ around the critical delay and solves with history $x_0 = 1 + \varepsilon$ and tolerance $10^{-10}$ up to $T = 400$ days.
Among the amplitudes $A_j = x(t_j) - 1$ at the local maxima $t_j$ of each solution, those satisfying $t_j \ge 50$ days and $10^{-4}\varepsilon < A_j < 0.02$ are regarded as lying in the linear regime; the slope of a least-squares fit of $\ln A_j$ against $t_j$ is the observed growth rate.
The mean interval between local maxima is the observed period.
The plot draws $\operatorname{Re}\lambda(\tau)$ obtained by continuation as a dashed line and overlays points for the observed growth rates.
Increasing $\varepsilon$ puts the amplitude outside the linear regime from the start and reduces the number of local maxima available for fitting.

    let path = integrate(tau, 400.0, 1.0 + epsilon, 1e-10, 0.05)?;
    let (t, x) = (&path.times, &path.values);
    let peaks: Vec<(f64, f64)> = (1..x.len() - 1)
        .filter(|&i| x[i] > x[i - 1] && x[i] >= x[i + 1])
        .map(|i| (t[i], x[i] - 1.0))
        .collect();
    let linear: Vec<(f64, f64)> = peaks
        .iter()
        .filter(|&&(time, amplitude)| {
            time >= 50.0 && amplitude > 1e-4 * epsilon && amplitude < 0.02
        })
        .map(|&(time, amplitude)| (time, RealScalar::ln(amplitude)))
        .collect();
    // Least-squares slope r of ln A = c + r t.
    let rate = (linear.len() >= 3).then(|| {
        let m = linear.len() as f64;
        let (st, sy) = linear
            .iter()
            .fold((0.0, 0.0), |(a, b), &(t, y)| (a + t, b + y));
        let (mt, my) = (st / m, sy / m);
        let sxy: f64 = linear.iter().map(|&(t, y)| (t - mt) * (y - my)).sum();
        let sxx: f64 = linear.iter().map(|&(t, _)| (t - mt).powi(2)).sum();

    });
    let period = (linear.len() >= 2)
        .then(|| (linear[linear.len() - 1].0 - linear[0].0) / (linear.len() - 1) as f64);
    let root = rightmost_root(tau);
    let theory_period = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI / root.im;

        .report
        .termination()
        .map_or("None".to_string(), |s| format!("{s:?}"));
    if let Some(r) = rate {
        observed.push([tau, r]);
    }
    rows.push((
        tau,
        root.re,
        rate,
        theory_period,
        period,
        linear.len(),
        status,
    ));

    .map(|tau| [tau, rightmost_root(tau).re])
    .collect();

With the default $\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$, the sign and magnitude of the observed growth rate agree with the linearized $\operatorname{Re}\lambda$ for all 9 values of $\tau$.
For $\tau \le 4.6$, the relative difference is at most $2 \times 10^{-4}$; for example, at $\tau = 4.5$, the values are $-6.1017 \times 10^{-3}$ and $-6.1022 \times 10^{-3}$ /day.
At $\tau = 4.7$, $\operatorname{Re}\lambda = -2.25 \times 10^{-4}$ /day is nearly 0, and the observed value $-2.26 \times 10^{-4}$ /day is also negative, indicating that the critical delay has not yet been reached.
At $\tau = 5$, 5.5, and 6, the growth rate becomes positive; only local maxima before the amplitude exceeds 0.02 are used, reducing the available counts to 20, 7, and 4.
The observed values are then 0.6% to 1.1% smaller than $\operatorname{Re}\lambda$.
Since the fit uses local maxima with amplitudes up to nearly 0.02, it appears to be influenced by nonlinear terms that suppress amplitude growth.
The observed period at $\tau = 4.7$ is 16.2025 days, close to $P_c = 16.2231$ days, and agrees with $2\pi/\operatorname{Im}\lambda$ to one decimal place up to the 19.45 days at $\tau = 6$.

At $\varepsilon = 10^{-1}$, the amplitude is outside the linear regime from the start.
At $\tau \ge 4.7$, no local maximum is smaller than 0.02, so the growth rate cannot be determined.
At $\tau = 4.6$, the observed value $-3.40 \times 10^{-3}$ /day differs by 10% from the linearized value $-3.07 \times 10^{-3}$ /day.
The amplitude lower bound $10^{-4}\varepsilon$ excludes from the fit local maxima that have decayed close to the level of rounding error.

## Amplification of small differences in history

The solution at $\tau = 17$ is chaotic, and small differences in the initial history grow exponentially with time.
The next cell computes a solution $x_A$ with $\tau = 17$ and history $x_0 = 0.5$, and a solution $x_B$ with history $0.5 + \delta$, using the same tolerance $10^{p}$.
It also computes a solution $x_C$ with the same history as $x_A$ but tolerance $10^{p-2}$, using $|x_C - x_A|$ as an indication of the numerical error in $x_A$.
The plot shows the maxima of $|x_B - x_A|$ and $|x_C - x_A|$ over each 5-day interval on a logarithmic vertical axis.
The table shows the first time $|x_B - x_A|$ exceeds 0.1 and the growth rate fitted to $\ln$ over intervals whose 5-day maxima lie between $10\delta$ and $10^{-2}$.
This growth rate gives an indication of the largest Lyapunov exponent of this solution.

```rust
//| id: ode-mackey-glass-sensitivity
//| ref: ode-mackey-glass-sensitivity
//| title: "Growth of a history difference at τ = 17"
//| run: button
//| uses: [ode-mackey-glass-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   offset_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"History difference exponent q (δ = 10^q)","description":"Difference between the histories of the two solutions.","min":-10,"max":-4,"step":1,"value":-8}
//|   horizon: {"type":"range","label":"Integration time T [days]","description":"Time interval over which to solve.","min":1000,"max":6000,"step":250,"value":4000}
//|   tolerance_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Tolerance exponent p (ε = 10^p)","description":"Absolute and relative tolerances for x_A and x_B. x_C uses 10^(p−2).","min":-11,"max":-6,"step":1,"value":-10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"loose-tolerance","label":"ε = 10⁻⁶","description":"Numerical error exceeds the history difference of 10⁻⁸, making the two curves indistinguishable.","inputs":{"tolerance_exponent":-6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"separation","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":2000,"max":4000}}
//|       - {"artifact":"separation","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0.003,"max":0.012}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

let delta = 10.0_f64.powf(offset_exponent);
let tolerance = 10.0_f64.powf(tolerance_exponent);
let a = integrate(17.0, horizon, 0.5, tolerance, 0.5)?;
let b = integrate(17.0, horizon, 0.5 + delta, tolerance, 0.5)?;
let c = integrate(17.0, horizon, 0.5, tolerance / 100.0, 0.5)?;
let difference = |other: &Path| -> Vec<f64> {
    a.values
        .iter()
        .zip(&other.values)
        .map(|(x, y)| (x - y).abs())
        .collect()
};
let (perturbed, numerical) = (difference(&b), difference(&c));
// Maxima over each 5-day interval (10 points). Zeros are omitted because they cannot be plotted on the logarithmic vertical axis.
let envelope = |values: &[f64]| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    values
        .chunks(10)
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(j, chunk)| [a.times[10 * j], chunk.iter().copied().fold(0.0, f64::max)])
        .filter(|p| p[1] > 0.0)
        .collect()
};
let (upper, noise) = (envelope(&perturbed), envelope(&numerical));
let separated = perturbed.iter().position(|&d| d > 0.1).map(|i| a.times[i]);
let growth: Vec<(f64, f64)> = upper
    .iter()
    .filter(|p| p[1] > 10.0 * delta && p[1] < 1e-2)
    .map(|p| (p[0], RealScalar::ln(p[1])))
    .collect();
let rate = (growth.len() >= 3).then(|| {
    let m = growth.len() as f64;
    let (mt, my) = growth
        .iter()
        .fold((0.0, 0.0), |(s, r), &(t, y)| (s + t / m, r + y / m));
    let sxy: f64 = growth.iter().map(|&(t, y)| (t - mt) * (y - my)).sum();
    let sxx: f64 = growth.iter().map(|&(t, _)| (t - mt).powi(2)).sum();
    sxy / sxx
});
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "xLabel": "Time t [days]",
    "yLabel": "5-day maxima of the solution difference [-]",
    "yType": "log",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "layers": [
        { "id": "perturbed", "kind": "line", "name": "|x_B − x_A| (history difference δ)", "points": upper },
        { "id": "numerical", "kind": "line", "name": "|x_C − x_A| (tolerance difference)", "points": noise }
    ],
    "annotations": [{
        "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": delta, "label": "δ", "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }]
}));
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "History difference δ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    {
        "key": "1",
        "label": "Time when the difference exceeded 0.1 [days]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": fixed1,
        "nullText": "Not exceeded"
    },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Growth rate [1/day]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Insufficient points" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum |x_C − x_A| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
let worst = numerical.iter().copied().fold(0.0, f64::max);
let rows = vec![(delta, separated, rate, worst)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "separation" })
);
let complete = [&a, &b, &c].iter().all(|p| p.report.is_success());
let outcome = if complete {
    Outcome::satisfied("All 3 solutions reached the endpoint")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("At least one solution stopped before the endpoint")
};
let status = a
    .report
    .termination()
    .map_or("None".to_string(), |t| format!("{t:?}"));
emit_outcome!(outcome.status(status).diagnostic(
    "Accepted steps for x_A",
    a.report.accepted_steps() as f64,
    None
));
```

With the defaults ($\delta = 10^{-8}$, $T = 4000$ days, $\varepsilon = 10^{-10}$), the 5-day maximum of $|x_B - x_A|$ increases to $3.8 \times 10^{-6}$ at $t = 1000$ days and $6.0 \times 10^{-3}$ at 2500 days, first exceeding 0.1 at 3013 days.
The growth rate is $4.4 \times 10^{-3}$ /day, taking about 230 days for the difference to grow by a factor of $e$.
Setting $\delta$ to $10^{-4}$ makes the difference exceed 0.1 at 1006.5 days, and setting it to $10^{-10}$ does so at 3741.5 days; each tenfold decrease in $\delta$ delays this time by about 460 days on average.

The numerical error indicator $|x_C - x_A|$ grows at the same rate, reaching $2.4 \times 10^{-5}$ at 2500 days and $6.4 \times 10^{-3}$ at 3500 days, with a maximum of 0.14 by 4000 days.
Numerical error alone makes the solution with tolerance $10^{-10}$ differ by about 0.1 between 3500 and 4000 days.
Until 3000 days, $|x_B - x_A|$ is several tens to about 300 times $|x_C - x_A|$, so the separation up to that point can be attributed to the history difference.
In the case with tolerance $10^{-6}$, $|x_C - x_A|$ is $4 \times 10^{-7}$ from the start, larger than $\delta$, reaching 0.4 at 2000 days.
In this case, $|x_B - x_A|$ reflects differences in numerical error rather than in history, and the time of 2129 days when it exceeds 0.1 does not reflect the effect of $\delta$.
Because chaotic solutions amplify local error just as they amplify history differences, the tolerance determines how long the details of a single numerical solution can be trusted.

## Summary

`DdePlan` solves equations with constant delays using the method of steps, aligning step boundaries with derivative discontinuities that propagate from the initial time.
Linearizing the Mackey–Glass equation at the equilibrium $x^* = 1$ gives the characteristic equation $\lambda = -\gamma + a e^{-\lambda\tau}$ ($a = -0.4$), yielding a closed-form critical delay $\tau_c \approx 4.708$ days and onset period $2\pi/\sqrt{0.15} \approx 16.22$ days.
The amplitude growth rate and period of a numerical solution starting near equilibrium can be compared with the rightmost root tracked by continuation.
In the chaotic solution at $\tau = 17$, history differences grow exponentially, so the details of a single numerical solution change with the tolerance after a long time; quantities that can be compared over long times include the shape of the delay embedding and statistical quantities.
