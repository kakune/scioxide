There are two ways to view errors in numerical solutions of stochastic differential equations: strong convergence measures closeness for individual paths, while weak convergence measures closeness of distributional quantities such as expectations.
Estimating an expectation by Monte Carlo adds sampling error from the finite number of paths to the discretization bias.
This example solves the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with Euler–Maruyama and shows the expectation bias from a closed form separately from sampling error measured by the standard error.
It verifies that halving the step size halves the bias (weak order 1), and that sampling error exceeds the bias at fine step sizes.

## The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process is defined by

$$
dX_t = -\theta X_t\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t, \qquad X_0 = x_0
$$


Here $W_t$ is a standard Wiener process, $\theta > 0$ [1/s] is the rate of mean reversion, and $\sigma$ [$\mathrm{s}^{-1/2}$] is the noise intensity.
The velocity of a particle in a viscous fluid (the Langevin equation) and the Vasicek interest rate model have this form.
This example fixes $x_0 = 1$, $\sigma = 0.5\ \mathrm{s}^{-1/2}$, and the endpoint $T = 1$ s, with $\theta$ as an input.

Since the diffusion coefficient $\sigma$ is independent of the state (additive noise), the correction term $\tfrac12 \sigma\,\partial\sigma/\partial x$ that distinguishes the Itô and Stratonovich integrals is 0, and both interpretations describe the same process.
Applying Itô's formula to the change of variables $e^{\theta t}X_t$ gives

$$
X_T = x_0 e^{-\theta T} + \sigma \int_0^T e^{-\theta(T - s)}\,dW_s
$$


The stochastic integral on the right is normally distributed with mean 0, and its variance follows from the Itô isometry, giving

$$
\mathbb{E}[X_T] = x_0 e^{-\theta T}, \qquad
\operatorname{Var}[X_T] = \frac{\sigma^2}{2\theta}\left(1 - e^{-2\theta T}\right)
$$


For $\theta = 1$ s⁻¹, $\mathbb{E}[X_1] = e^{-1} \approx 0.36788$ and $\operatorname{Var}[X_1] = 0.125(1 - e^{-2}) \approx 0.10808$.

## Closed forms for the Euler–Maruyama mean and variance

Euler–Maruyama with step size $h = T/N$ advances using the Wiener increment $\Delta W_k \sim N(0, h)$ as

$$
X_{k+1} = X_k - \theta h X_k + \sigma\,\Delta W_k = q X_k + \sigma\,\Delta W_k, \qquad q = 1 - \theta h
$$


The increments are mutually independent and independent of $X_k$, so the mean and variance of the numerical solution $X_N$ have closed forms given by geometric sums.

$$
\mathbb{E}[X_N] = x_0 q^N, \qquad
\operatorname{Var}[X_N] = \sigma^2 h \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} q^{2k} = \sigma^2 h\,\frac{1 - q^{2N}}{1 - q^2}
$$

For $q^2 = 1$, the variance is $\sigma^2 h N$.
The mean bias $b(h) = x_0 q^N - x_0 e^{-\theta T}$ follows from $N \ln(1 - \theta h) = -\theta T - \theta^2 T h/2 + O(h^2)$ as

$$
b(h) = -\frac{\theta^2 T}{2}\, x_0 e^{-\theta T}\, h + O(h^2)
$$

and is proportional to the step size.
This is the weak order 1 of Euler–Maruyama.
For general SDEs, under smoothness and growth conditions on the coefficients, $|\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_N)] - \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_T)]| = O(h)$ holds for smooth functions $\varphi$ (Kloeden and Platen, *Numerical Solution of Stochastic Differential Equations*, Springer, 1992, §14.1).
For $\theta h > 1$, $q < 0$ and the numerical mean decays with alternating signs; for $\theta h > 2$, $|q| > 1$ and both the mean and variance grow.

The following definitions are shared by both cells.
`X0`, `SIGMA`, and `END` correspond to $x_0$, $\sigma$, and $T$; `exact_mean` corresponds to $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = x_0 e^{-\theta t}$; and `em_moments` corresponds to the mean $x_0 q^k$ and variance $\sigma^2 h (1 - q^{2k})/(1 - q^2)$ of Euler–Maruyama after $k$ steps.

```rust
//| id: ode-ornstein-uhlenbeck-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process and the Euler–Maruyama mean and variance"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

/// Initial value x₀ [-], noise intensity σ [s^(−1/2)], and endpoint T [s].
pub(crate) const X0: f64 = 1.0;
pub(crate) const SIGMA: f64 = 0.5;
pub(crate) const END: f64 = 1.0;

/// Exact mean E[X_t] = x₀ e^{−θt}.
pub(crate) fn exact_mean(theta: f64, time: f64) -> f64 {
    X0 * RealScalar::exp(-theta * time)
}

/// Euler–Maruyama mean x₀qᵏ and variance σ²h(1 − q²ᵏ)/(1 − q²) after k steps of size h.
/// q = 1 − θh. When q² = 1, the variance is σ²hk.
pub(crate) fn em_moments(theta: f64, h: f64, steps: usize) -> (f64, f64) {
    let q = 1.0 - theta * h;
    let mean = X0 * RealScalar::powi(q, steps as i32);
    let variance = if (1.0 - q * q).abs() < 1e-12 {
        SIGMA * SIGMA * h * steps as f64
    } else {
        SIGMA * SIGMA * h * (1.0 - RealScalar::powi(q, 2 * steps as i32)) / (1.0 - q * q)
    };
    (mean, variance)
}
```

## Separating bias and sampling error

When the expectation is estimated by the sample mean $\bar{X}_N$ of the terminal values $X_N^{(1)}, \ldots, X_N^{(M)}$ of $M$ independent paths, the error splits as

$$
\bar{X}_N - \mathbb{E}[X_T] =
\underbrace{\left(\mathbb{E}[X_N] - \mathbb{E}[X_T]\right)}_{\text{bias } b(h)} +
\underbrace{\left(\bar{X}_N - \mathbb{E}[X_N]\right)}_{\text{sampling error}}
$$


The bias is a deterministic quantity determined only by $h$ and is known exactly from the closed form above.
The sampling error is a random variable with mean 0 and standard deviation $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}[X_N]/M}$; compute the standard error $\mathrm{SE} = s/\sqrt{M}$ from the sample variance $s^2$ and use $\pm 2\,\mathrm{SE}$ as an indication of an approximately 95% range.
The bias decreases proportionally to $h$, but sampling error decreases only as $M^{-1/2}$; as $h$ decreases, sampling error eventually determines the observed error and obscures the decreasing bias.
Reducing the bias to about $\varepsilon$ requires $h \propto \varepsilon$, and reducing sampling error to the same level requires $M \propto \varepsilon^{-2}$, so the computational cost $MN$ is proportional to $\varepsilon^{-3}$.

## Integration with paths arranged as a single system

`solve_sde` takes closures returning the drift $a(t, X)$ and diffusion product $B(t, X)\,\Delta W$, a time grid, a matrix of Wiener increments, and initial values, and returns the values of all paths as a matrix using Euler–Maruyama.
In the increment matrix, row $i$ contains increments for the interval $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$, and column $j$ corresponds to Wiener process component $j$.
The solver does not generate random numbers, so the caller constructs the increments with scioxide-random.
`solve_sde` internally calls `advance` on `SdeMethod::EulerMaruyama` and returns `PartialFailure` if the termination status is not successful.

This example arranges $M$ paths as components of an $M$-dimensional state and uses $M$ Wiener process components as well.
The drift $-\theta X_j$ and diffusion $\sigma\,\Delta W_j$ act independently on each component, so one Euler–Maruyama step performs the same computation as advancing each of the $M$ paths by one step separately.
To solve each path as a separate problem, use `solve_ensemble_into`.
This function passes separate parameters and workspaces to each member and performs independent solves sequentially or in parallel; for a system with independent components as in this example, a single call suffices.

Wiener increments are generated using the random number generator in `Philox4x64`.
A root generator created from seed $s$ is split with `SplittableRng::split` into per-path streams $j = 0, \ldots, M - 1$; stream $j$ supplies $N_{\max}$ increments at the finest step size $h_{\min} = T/N_{\max}$ for path $j$.
Each stream depends only on the path index, so increasing $M$ leaves existing paths unchanged.
Increments for a coarse step $h = 2^l h_{\min}$ are constructed by summing $2^l$ fine increments at a time.
As sums of independent normal random variables, the coarse increments also follow $N(0, h)$ exactly, and all step sizes use the same Brownian paths.

## Estimating expectations at different step sizes

The next cell selects the number of paths $M$, finest step count $N_{\max} = 2^K$, $\theta$, and random seed, and computes the sample mean and sample variance of terminal values for each $N = 1, 2, 4, \ldots, N_{\max}$.
For each $N$, the table lists the closed-form mean $\mathbb{E}[X_N]$, sample mean, 2SE, bias $b(h)$, sampling error $\bar{X}_N - \mathbb{E}[X_N]$, and closed-form and sample variances.
The “Bias order” column is the bias ratio $\log_2\left(b(2h)/b(h)\right)$ relative to the next coarser step size; it approaches 1 for weak order 1.
At step sizes where the bias changes sign, this ratio is negative and the order is undefined.
This cell passes $\theta$, the error and 2SE for each step size, and the sample mean and 2SE at each time on the finest grid to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: ode-ornstein-uhlenbeck-levels
//| ref: ode-ornstein-uhlenbeck-levels
//| title: "Expectation bias and sampling error at each step size"
//| run: button
//| cache: inputs
//| uses: [ode-ornstein-uhlenbeck-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode, scioxide-random]
//| exports: [theta, levels, mean_path]
//| inputs:
//|   paths: {"type":"range","label":"Number of paths M [count]","description":"Number of independent paths used for the sample mean.","min":1000,"max":20000,"step":1000,"value":10000}
//|   finest_level: {"type":"range","label":"Finest step count exponent K (N_max = 2^K)","description":"Double the step count successively from 1 to 2^K.","min":3,"max":6,"step":1,"value":6}
//|   theta: {"type":"range","label":"Mean reversion rate θ [1/s]","description":"Rate of return to the mean. For θh > 1, the numerical mean oscillates in sign.","min":0.25,"max":4,"step":0.25,"value":1}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed s [-]","description":"Seed for the root generator of Wiener increments.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"few-paths","label":"M = 1000","description":"Sampling error increases, and the step size where the bias falls below 2SE shifts toward coarser steps.","inputs":{"paths":1000}}
//|   - {"id":"fast-reversion","label":"θ = 4 s⁻¹","description":"Coarse steps give θh > 2, and the numerical mean grows with alternating signs.","inputs":{"theta":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/6/4","approx":{"value":-2.892917e-3,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/6/5","range":{"min":-6.6e-3,"max":6.6e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/6/6","approx":{"value":1.0095,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/6/7","approx":{"value":0.1092012,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: fast-reversion
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-3.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/1/6","equals":null}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::MatrixView;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, Philox4x64, SeedableRng, SplittableRng};

let (m, finest) = (paths as usize, 1_usize << (finest_level as u32));
// Draw the fine increments for path j from stream j and place them in row k, column j.
let normal =
    Normal::new(0.0, RealScalar::sqrt(END / finest as f64)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let root = Philox4x64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let mut fine = vec![0.0; finest * m];
for j in 0..m {
    let mut stream = root.split(j as u64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    for k in 0..finest {
        fine[k * m + j] = normal.sample(&mut stream).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
}
let exact = exact_mean(theta, END);
let (mut rows, mut levels, mut mean_path) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut previous_bias: Option<f64> = None;
for level in 0..=finest_level as u32 {
    let (steps, ratio) = (1_usize << level, finest >> level);
    let h = END / steps as f64;
    // Coarse increments sum ratio fine increments at a time (the same Brownian path).
    let mut increments = vec![0.0; steps * m];
    for (k, row) in increments.chunks_exact_mut(m).enumerate() {
        for fine_row in fine[k * ratio * m..(k + 1) * ratio * m].chunks_exact(m) {
            row.iter_mut().zip(fine_row).for_each(|(w, dw)| *w += dw);
        }
    }
    let times: Vec<f64> = (0..=steps).map(|k| k as f64 * h).collect();
    let view = MatrixView::from_slice(steps, m, &increments).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Path j is state component j. The drift −θX_j and diffusion σΔW_j act independently on each component.
    let drift = |_t: f64, x: &[f64], a: &mut [f64]| {
        a.iter_mut().zip(x).for_each(|(a, &x)| *a = -theta * x);
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    };
    let diffusion = |_t: f64, _x: &[f64], dw: &[f64], b: &mut [f64]| {
        b.iter_mut().zip(dw).for_each(|(b, &w)| *b = SIGMA * w);
        Ok::<(), SciOxideError>(())
    };
    let path = scioxide_ode::solve_sde(drift, diffusion, &times, view, &vec![X0; m])
        .map_err(|e| e.error().to_string())?;
    // Sample mean and 2SE at each time (divide by M − 1 for the sample variance).
    let moments: Vec<(f64, f64, f64)> = path
        .as_slice()
        .chunks_exact(m)
        .map(|values| {
            let mean = values.iter().sum::<f64>() / m as f64;
            let variance =
                values.iter().map(|x| (x - mean).powi(2)).sum::<f64>() / (m - 1) as f64;
            (mean, variance, 2.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(variance / m as f64))
        })
        .collect();
    let (sample_mean, sample_variance, two_se) = moments[steps];
    let (em_mean, em_variance) = em_moments(theta, h, steps);
    let bias = em_mean - exact;
    let order = previous_bias
        .map(|coarse| coarse / bias)
        .filter(|&ratio| ratio > 0.0)
        .map(RealScalar::log2);
    previous_bias = Some(bias);
    let sampling = sample_mean - em_mean;
    rows.push((
        steps,
        em_mean,
        sample_mean,
        two_se,
        bias,
        sampling,
        order,
        em_variance,
        sample_variance,
    ));
    levels.push([h, bias.abs(), (sample_mean - exact).abs(), two_se]);
    if steps == finest {
        mean_path = times
            .iter()
            .zip(&moments)
            .map(|(&t, &(mean, _, se))| [t, mean, se])
            .collect();
    }
}

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed5 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 5 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Step count N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "E[X_N] (closed form) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Sample mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "2SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Bias b(h) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Sampling error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Bias order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Var[X_N] (closed form) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Sample variance [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "levels" })
);
export!(theta, theta);
export!(levels, levels);
export!(mean_path, mean_path);
```

With the defaults ($M = 10000$, $N_{\max} = 64$, $\theta = 1$ s⁻¹, seed 1), the exact mean is $\mathbb{E}[X_1] = 0.36788$.
The bias approximately halves each time the step size is halved, from $-0.368$ at $N = 1$ to $-2.89 \times 10^{-3}$ at $N = 64$, and its order approaches 1 through 1.642, 1.195, 1.085, 1.040, 1.019, and 1.0095.
The order exceeds 1 at coarse step sizes because the $O(h^2)$ term in the bias expansion remains significant.
For $N \ge 2$, 2SE lies between $6.6 \times 10^{-3}$ and $7.9 \times 10^{-3}$; the bias exceeds 2SE only for step counts $N \le 16$.
For $N = 32$ and $N = 64$, the bias is smaller than 2SE and cannot be verified from the sample mean alone.

For $N \ge 2$, the sampling error $\bar{X}_N - \mathbb{E}[X_N]$ always lies between $1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ and $1.8 \times 10^{-3}$, giving nearly the same value at every step size.
This is because all step sizes use the same Brownian paths and therefore share most of the sampling error.
Drawing independent increments for each step size gives unrelated sampling errors at different step sizes, making differences in bias harder to distinguish within 2SE.
For the variance, the difference between the closed form and sample variance is at most $4.1 \times 10^{-4}$ for $N \ge 4$.
The standard error of the sample variance is approximately $\sqrt{2/M}\,s^2 \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-3}$, so this difference is within the sampling variation.
The variance 0.10920 at $N = 64$ exceeds the exact $\operatorname{Var}[X_1] = 0.10808$ by $1.1 \times 10^{-3}$, showing that the variance also has a bias proportional to the step size.

Setting $M = 1000$ increases 2SE to the range from $2.1 \times 10^{-2}$ to $3.2 \times 10^{-2}$, leaving only $N \le 8$ with bias exceeding 2SE.
For $\theta = 4$ s⁻¹, $N = 1$ gives $q = -3$, $N = 2$ gives $q = -1$, and $N = 4$ gives $q = 0$, with closed-form means $-3$, 1, and 0 changing sign.
The order is undefined at these step sizes where the bias changes sign, and from $N = 8$ onward it approaches 1 more slowly, through 0.346, 0.797, 0.922, and 0.966.
This is because the leading term of the bias expansion does not dominate until $\theta h$ becomes small.

## Bias decay and evolution of the sample mean

The next cell plots the results of the previous cell in two charts.
The upper plot uses logarithmic axes to show the closed-form bias $|b(h)|$ (dashed), observed error $|\bar{X}_N - \mathbb{E}[X_T]|$ (symbols), and 2SE (dotted) against the step size $h$.
A bias line with slope 1 indicates weak order 1; at step sizes where the observed error lies below the 2SE line, sampling error obscures the bias.
The lower plot shows the sample mean $\bar{X}(t_k)$ on the finest grid and a band of $\pm 2\,\mathrm{SE}$, with the exact mean $x_0 e^{-\theta t}$ overlaid as a dashed line and the Euler–Maruyama mean $x_0 q^k$ at the same step size as points.

## Summary

The expectation error of Euler–Maruyama is the sum of a bias proportional to the step size and sampling error inversely proportional to the square root of the number of paths.
For the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, the mean $x_0(1 - \theta h)^N$ and variance of the numerical solution have closed forms, so the bias can be shown exactly without mixing it with sampling error.
`solve_sde` takes a matrix of Wiener increments without generating random numbers; creating per-path streams with scioxide-random's `SplittableRng::split` makes results depend on the seed and path index.
Constructing coarse increments by summing fine ones lets all step sizes use the same Brownian paths, making differences due to step size less easily obscured by sampling variation.
When measuring weak order, read the slope only over step sizes where the bias is sufficiently larger than 2SE, and increase the number of paths at finer step sizes.
