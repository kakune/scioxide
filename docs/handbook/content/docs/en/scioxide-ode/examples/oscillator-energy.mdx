Harmonic oscillators approximate many vibrations, including small spring and pendulum oscillations, LC circuits, and molecular bond stretching.
Exact energy is constant, and phase-plane trajectories are closed ellipses.
After hundreds of periods, numerical methods show gradual energy decay, preservation within rounding, or oscillation within a fixed range.
This example compares classical RK4 (`ClassicalRk4`), two-stage fourth-order Gauss (`GaussRk`), and Störmer–Verlet (`StormerVerlet`) at the same fixed step, explaining differences through one-step eigenvalues.
[Symplectic integration and constrained dynamical systems](../../algorithms/geometric-integrators/) covers general symplecticity and modified Hamiltonians.

## Harmonic oscillator and energy

For mass 1 kg and angular frequency $\omega$ [rad/s], displacement $q$ [m] and momentum $p$ [kg·m/s] obey

$$
q' = p, \qquad p' = -\omega^2 q
$$


Energy

$$
E(q, p) = \frac{p^2}{2} + \frac{\omega^2 q^2}{2}
$$

satisfies $\frac{dE}{dt} = p\,p' + \omega^2 q\,q' = 0$ and is constant.
Starting from $(q, p) = (1, 0)$ gives exact $q = \cos\omega t$ and $p = -\omega\sin\omega t$, tracing a unit circle at constant speed in normalized phase plane $(q, p/\omega)$.
The period is $P = 2\pi/\omega$.

Setting $w = q - i p/\omega$ gives $w' = i\omega w$, corresponding to test equation $y' = \lambda y$ with purely imaginary $\lambda = i\omega$.
For linear methods, the step map is $2 \times 2$ matrix $\Phi$ depending only on $\xi = h\omega$; after $K$ periods ($Km$ steps if $m$ per period), state is multiplied by $\Phi^{Km}$.

The following block defines the model used by two cells.
The type `Oscillator` implements $f(t, y) = (p, -\omega^2 q)$ as `OdeSystem`, supplying a constant Jacobian for `GaussRk` Newton iteration.

```rust
//| id: ode-oscillator-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Harmonic-oscillator right-hand side and constant Jacobian"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SolveContext};
use scioxide_linalg::MatrixViewMut;
use scioxide_ode::OdeSystem;

/// q′ = p, p′ = −ω² q. Field ω [rad/s].
pub(crate) struct Oscillator(pub(crate) f64);

impl OdeSystem<f64> for Oscillator {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn rhs(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        dydt: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        dydt[0] = y[1];
        dydt[1] = -self.0 * self.0 * y[0];
        Ok(())
    }
    fn jacobian(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        _: &[f64],
        jac: &mut MatrixViewMut<'_, f64>,
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<Option<()>, SciOxideError> {
        jac.set(0, 0, 0.0)?;
        jac.set(0, 1, 1.0)?;
        jac.set(1, 0, -self.0 * self.0)?;
        jac.set(1, 1, 0.0)?;
        Ok(Some(()))
    }
    fn jacobian_is_constant(&self) -> bool {
        true
    }
}
```

## Long-term energy and trajectories

The next cell selects angular frequency $\omega$, period count $K$, and steps per period $m$, integrating $K$ periods at step $h = P/m$ with three methods.
The methods `ClassicalRk4` and `GaussRk` use `advance` of `OdeMethod`, retaining accepted states in `OdeTrajectory`.
The stages of `GaussRk` are solved to tolerance $10^{-12}$.
Störmer–Verlet supplies $\nabla T(p) = p$ and $\nabla V(q) = \omega^2 q$ through `HamiltonianFn`, collecting each step's state with the sink passed to `StormerVerlet::advance`.
The upper plot shows normalized phase plane $(q, p/\omega)$; the lower plots relative energy error $(E_n - E_0)/E_0$ against elapsed periods $t/P$.
The table gives maximum relative energy error, next-section theory, terminal analytical difference $\sqrt{(q - q_{\mathrm{exact}})^2 + ((p - p_{\mathrm{exact}})/\omega)^2}$, steps, evaluations, and termination status.

With defaults $m = 16$ ($\xi = 0.393$) and $K = 80$ periods, RK4 maximum relative energy error $6.2 \times 10^{-2}$ agrees with next-section formula $1 - |R(i\xi)|^{2Km}$ to over 10 digits.
RK4 phase trajectories spiral gradually inward.
Two-stage Gauss error $2.7 \times 10^{-15}$ is of rounding magnitude.
Störmer–Verlet maximum error is $3.9 \times 10^{-2}$; the plot oscillates between zero and that maximum throughout, without growth or decay.
Formula value $4.0 \times 10^{-2}$ concerns the instant $p = 0$; sampled steps do not coincide exactly, making the measured maximum slightly smaller.
Terminal analytical differences are $0.098$ for RK4, $0.016$ for Gauss, and $1.99$ for Störmer–Verlet.
Second-order Störmer–Verlet has larger phase error, placing it nearly opposite on the orbit after 80 periods.
Energy preservation and correct orbital position are distinct properties.
Even at $\omega = 3$ rad/s, relative energy errors are unchanged if $m$ is unchanged.

## One-step maps and phase error

Runge–Kutta applied to $w' = i\omega w$ gives step $w_{n+1} = R(i\xi)\,w_n$, where $R$ is the stability function.
Map eigenvalues $R(\pm i\xi)$ determine one-step amplitude change by magnitude and phase change by argument.
Classical RK4 has stability function

$$
R_{\mathrm{RK4}}(i\xi) = 1 + i\xi - \frac{\xi^2}{2} - i\frac{\xi^3}{6} + \frac{\xi^4}{24}, \qquad
|R_{\mathrm{RK4}}(i\xi)|^2 = 1 - \frac{\xi^6}{72} + \frac{\xi^8}{576}
$$

with $|R| < 1$ at $0 < \xi < 2\sqrt 2$, shrinking amplitude each step.
After $K$ periods, energy is multiplied by $|R|^{2Km}$.
Its argument is $\arg R_{\mathrm{RK4}}(i\xi) = \xi - \xi^5/120 + O(\xi^7)$, giving phase lag $\xi^5/120$ per step.

Two-stage Gauss has stability function equal to the exponential's $[2/2]$ Padé approximant,

$$
R_{\mathrm{G}}(i\xi) = \frac{1 + i\xi/2 - \xi^2/12}{1 - i\xi/2 - \xi^2/12}
$$

whose numerator and denominator are conjugates, so $|R_{\mathrm{G}}(i\xi)| = 1$ for every real $\xi$.
Its argument $2\arctan\bigl((\xi/2)/(1 - \xi^2/12)\bigr) = \xi - \xi^5/720 + O(\xi^7)$ gives about one-sixth of RK4's phase lag.

Störmer–Verlet has one-step map

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{SV}} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - \xi^2/2 & h \\ -\omega\,\xi\,(1 - \xi^2/4) & 1 - \xi^2/2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

with $\det \Phi_{\mathrm{SV}} = 1$.
For $\xi < 2$, eigenvalues are $e^{\pm i\theta}$ and $\cos\theta = 1 - \xi^2/2$; since $\theta = \xi + \xi^3/24 + O(\xi^5)$, phase advances by $\xi^3/24$ per step (second-order).
The map is not a rotation, so $E$ is not constant; however, the quadratic form

$$
\tilde E = \frac{p^2}{2} + \frac{\omega^2 q^2}{2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{\xi^2}{4}\Bigr)
$$

is preserved to within rounding error.
At $q = 0$, $E = \tilde E$, and at $p = 0$, $E = \tilde E/(1 - \xi^2/4)$, so $E$ oscillates over relative width $(\xi^2/4)/(1 - \xi^2/4)$ without continual growth.
This is the harmonic oscillator's modified Hamiltonian.

For a general $2 \times 2$ map $\Phi$, eigenvalues follow from $\det\Phi$ and $\operatorname{tr}\Phi$ as $|\mu| = \sqrt{\det\Phi}$ and $\cos\theta = \operatorname{tr}\Phi/(2\sqrt{\det\Phi})$.
The next cell takes one step from $(1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$ with each method at $\omega = 1$, measuring both columns of $\Phi$ and obtaining eigenvalue magnitudes and arguments $\theta$.
The methods `ClassicalRk4` and `GaussRk` use `OdeMethod::solve`; Störmer–Verlet advances one step with `solve_hamiltonian`.
For this linear system, phase error after $K$ periods ($Km$ steps) is $Km(\xi - \theta)$, positive for lag and negative for advance.
The table compares measured values at $m = 8, 16, 32, 60$ with formula values; the log–log plot shows phase-error magnitudes for $m = 4, \ldots, 60$.

//|   - case: default

//|       - {"artifact":"step-map","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.999975,"atol":2e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"step-map","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.118,"rtol":0.02}}

//|       - {"artifact":"step-map","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":-4.109,"rtol":0.01}}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, SciOxideError, NormSelection, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::Vector;
use scioxide_ode::{
    ClassicalRk4, GaussRk, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeProblem, OdeState, solve_hamiltonian,
};

/// From columns obtained by one step ξ from (1, 0) and (0, 1), return eigenvalue magnitudes and arguments.
fn eigen<F: Fn([f64; 2]) -> Result<[f64; 2], String>>(step: F) -> Result<(f64, f64), String> {
    let (a, b) = (step([1.0, 0.0])?, step([0.0, 1.0])?);
    let det = a[0] * b[1] - b[0] * a[1];
    let half_trace = 0.5 * (a[0] + b[1]);
    let modulus = RealScalar::sqrt(det);
    let imaginary = RealScalar::sqrt((det - half_trace * half_trace).max(0.0));
    Ok((modulus, RealScalar::atan2(imaginary, half_trace)))
}
/// Advance one step ξ with OdeMethod.
fn one_step<M: OdeMethod<f64> + Clone>(
    method: M,
    xi: f64,
    y0: [f64; 2],
) -> Result<[f64; 2], String> {
    // Set tolerance 1e-14 to solve stage equations to rounding accuracy.
    let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::scalar(1e-14, 1e-14, NormSelection::RootMeanSquare)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let options = OdeOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(tolerance)
        .with_initial_step(xi);
    let system = Oscillator(1.0);
    let initial = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(y0.to_vec()));
    let solution = method
        .solve(&OdeProblem::new(&system, xi), &options, initial)
        .map_err(|e| SciOxideError::from(e).to_string())?;
    let y = solution.state().values();
    Ok([y[0], y[1]])
}

Measured RK4 $|\mu|$ agrees with formula $\sqrt{1 - \xi^6/72 + \xi^8/576}$ to all 10 displayed digits.
At $m = 16$, $|\mu| = 0.9999750$, predicting energy multiplied by $0.9999750^{2 \cdot 1280} = 0.938$ after $K = 80$ periods, a 6.2% reduction matching the preceding long-integration measurement.
Gauss and Störmer–Verlet have $|\mu|$ equal to 1 in double precision.

## Summary

For harmonic oscillators, RK4 amplitude shrinks by factor $|R(i\xi)| < 1$ per step, with energy loss proportional to period count.
Two-stage Gauss has $|R(i\xi)| = 1$ and preserves energy to rounding; Störmer–Verlet confines energy to oscillations of width $(\xi^2/4)/(1 - \xi^2/4)$.
Every method's phase error grows proportionally to periods: per step, RK4 lags by $\xi^5/120$, Gauss by $\xi^5/720$, and Störmer–Verlet advances by $\xi^3/24$.
Preserved energy does not guarantee correct orbital position; check amplitude and phase separately over long integrations.
For nonlinear Hamiltonians, even Gauss energy is not exactly constant but oscillates within bounds.
