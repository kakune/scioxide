The Van der Pol oscillator is nonlinear, originating as a vacuum-tube oscillator circuit model.
Increasing damping coefficient $\mu$ produces relaxation oscillations alternating slow motion with rapid jumps, making the equations stiff.
This page varies $\mu = 1$ through 1000, comparing steps and evaluations of explicit `DormandPrince5` and stiff methods.
Check solutions by comparing event-measured periods with small-$\mu$ and large-$\mu$ approximations and terminal states across methods.

## Van der Pol equation

With dimensionless time $t$ and displacement $x$, the equation is

$$
x'' - \mu (1 - x^2) x' + x = 0, \qquad \mu > 0
$$


For $\lvert x \rvert < 1$, negative damping supplies energy; for $\lvert x \rvert > 1$, positive damping removes it.
Thus trajectories from any initial values approach one periodic orbit (limit cycle) of amplitude about 2.
Setting $v = x'$ gives the first-order system

$$
x' = v, \qquad v' = \mu (1 - x^2) v - x
$$


Component order is $(x, v)$, with Jacobian

$$
J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -2\mu x v - 1 & \mu (1 - x^2) \end{pmatrix}
$$


At large $\mu$, the periodic orbit separates into two regimes.
While $\lvert x \rvert$ slowly decreases from 2 to 1, $v$ is small; one eigenvalue of $J$ is about $\mu(1 - x^2)$, reaching $-3\mu$ at $x = 2$.
Upon reaching $\lvert x \rvert = 1$, $x$ jumps over time $O(1/\mu)$ toward the opposite side near $\mp 2$.
Slow-regime solutions change over time $O(\mu)$, while $J$ has a negative eigenvalue of magnitude $3\mu$.
A component decaying much faster than the solution changes makes the equation stiff.

Explicit step $h$ is restricted to keep $h\lambda$ within the method's stability region.
The `linear_stability` of `DormandPrince5` reports stable negative-real interval $[-3.3065, 0]$.
At $\lambda \approx -3\mu$, steps are limited to $h \le 3.3065/(3\mu) \approx 1.1/\mu$, far smaller than accuracy requires.
The slow regime lasts $O(\mu)$, so steps per period grow proportionally to $\mu^2$.
Stiff methods ([Rosenbrock-W methods and automatic stiffness switching](../../algorithms/rosenbrock-and-stiffness-switching/), [Multistep methods](../../algorithms/multistep-methods/)) are stable on the whole negative real axis, leaving accuracy to determine steps.

## Period approximations

For small $\mu$, Lindstedt–Poincaré expands frequency in powers of $\mu$.
Frequency is $\omega = 1 - \mu^2/16 + O(\mu^4)$, giving period

$$
T \approx 2\pi \left( 1 + \frac{\mu^2}{16} \right)
$$


For large $\mu$, slow-regime duration dominates.
Neglecting $x''$ there gives $dt = \mu (1 - x^2)/x \, dx$ from $\mu(1 - x^2)x' \approx x$.
Doubling the time for $x$ to move from 2 to 1 gives leading term

$$
T_0 = 2\mu \int_1^2 \frac{x^2 - 1}{x} \, dx = (3 - 2\ln 2)\mu \approx 1.6137\mu
$$


Treating the boundary layer at jump onset using Airy functions adds the next term,

$$
T \approx (3 - 2\ln 2)\mu + 3\alpha \mu^{-1/3}
$$

(Dorodnitsyn's formula).
Here $\alpha \approx 2.338107$ is the first zero of Airy function $\mathrm{Ai}(-z)$.
Remaining terms are $O(\mu^{-1}\ln\mu)$, so relative approximation error decreases as $\mu$ grows.
Both formulas lose accuracy at intermediate $\mu$, around 3.

The following block supplies shared definitions for this page's cells.
The type `VanDerPol` supplies the right-hand side and analytical Jacobian; because the system is autonomous, it returns 0 for the time derivative $\partial f/\partial t$.
Returning 0 lets `RosenbrockW` skip difference evaluations for $\partial f/\partial t$.
The event `upward_crossing` detects $x$ crossing zero upward and is nonterminal.
The function `integrate` solves from $(x, v) = (2, 0)$ through `horizon` at absolute and relative tolerance $\varepsilon$ (RMS norm), with at most 20000 attempted steps.
The quantity `period` is the difference between the last two event times.

```rust
//| id: ode-van-der-pol-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Van der Pol right-hand side, Jacobian, and period event"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
use scioxide_core::{Budget, ErrorTolerance, SciOxideError, NormSelection, SolveContext};
use scioxide_linalg::{MatrixViewMut, Vector};
use scioxide_ode::{
    EventDirection, EventFn, EventFunction, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule,
    OdeProblem, OdeSolveReport, OdeState, OdeSystem, OdeTrajectory,
};

/// x′ = v, v′ = μ(1 − x²)v − x. Component order (x, v).
pub(crate) struct VanDerPol {
    pub(crate) mu: f64,
}

impl OdeSystem<f64> for VanDerPol {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }

    fn rhs(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        out[0] = y[1];
        out[1] = self.mu * (1.0 - y[0] * y[0]) * y[1] - y[0];
        Ok(())
    }

    fn jacobian(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        y: &[f64],
        j: &mut MatrixViewMut<'_, f64>,
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<Option<()>, SciOxideError> {
        j.set(0, 0, 0.0)?;
        j.set(0, 1, 1.0)?;
        j.set(1, 0, -2.0 * self.mu * y[0] * y[1] - 1.0)?;
        j.set(1, 1, self.mu * (1.0 - y[0] * y[0]))?;
        Ok(Some(()))
    }

    fn time_derivative(
        &self,
        _: f64,
        _: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<Option<()>, SciOxideError> {
        out.fill(0.0);
        Ok(Some(()))
    }
}

/// Upward zero crossing of x. Nonterminal.
pub(crate) fn upward_crossing() -> impl EventFunction<f64> {
    EventFn::new(|_: f64, y: &[f64]| Ok(y[0])).with_direction(EventDirection::Rising)
}

/// End at three times the approximate period, including three upward crossings; the final period has lost initial-value effects.
pub(crate) fn horizon(mu: f64) -> f64 {
    3.0 * (2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI + (3.0 - 2.0 * std::f64::consts::LN_2) * mu)
}

/// Solve from (x, v) = (2, 0) through horizon, with tolerance ε and at most 20000 attempted steps.
pub(crate) fn integrate<M: OdeMethod<f64> + Clone>(
    method: &M,
    mu: f64,
    eps: f64,
    schedule: OdeOutputSchedule<f64>,
) -> Result<(OdeTrajectory<f64>, OdeSolveReport<f64>), SciOxideError> {
    let system = VanDerPol { mu };
    let crossing = upward_crossing();
    let events: [&dyn EventFunction<f64>; 1] = [&crossing];
    let problem = OdeProblem::new(&system, horizon(mu)).with_events(&events);
    let options = OdeOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(ErrorTolerance::scalar(
            eps,
            eps,
            NormSelection::RootMeanSquare,
        )?)
        .with_max_steps(Budget::try_from(20_000)?)
        .with_output_schedule(schedule);
    let initial = OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(vec![2.0, 0.0]));
    let solution = method
        .solve(&problem, &options, initial)
        .map_err(SciOxideError::from)?;
    let (_, report, path) = solution.into_parts();
    Ok((path, report))
}

/// Difference between the last two upward-crossing times. None if fewer than two.
pub(crate) fn period(path: &OdeTrajectory<f64>) -> Option<f64> {
    match path.events() {
        [.., previous, last] => Some(last.time() - previous.time()),
        _ => None,
    }
}
```

## Comparing explicit and stiff methods

The next cell selects $\mu$ and tolerance $\varepsilon$, solving the same problem with four methods.

- `DormandPrince5`: explicit Runge–Kutta 5(4).
- `RosenbrockW` (`RosenbrockMethod::Ros34Pw2`): third-order linearly implicit.
  Factor $I - h\gamma J$ by LU per step, without Newton iteration.
- `Bdf` (`MultistepOrder::Automatic(5)`): BDF with automatic orders 1–5.
  Since `Bdf::new(5)` fixes order and step size, use `with_order_policy` for adaptation.
- `AdamsBdf`: `AutoStiff` pairing starting with Adams and switching to `Bdf` when stiffness indicator $\lvert h \rvert \rho(J)$ exceeds threshold.

Every method stops with `BudgetLimit(Proposal)` at 20000 attempted steps, returning the last accepted state.
The table gives termination status, steps, right-hand-side/Jacobian/LU counts, event-measured periods, and terminal states for methods reaching the endpoint.
The method `RosenbrockW` also counts calls to $\partial f/\partial t$ as Jacobian evaluations.
A second table shows `AdamsBdf` switch counts and the latest record (`OdeMethodSwitch`).
The cell passes `Bdf` solution $x(t)$ and each method's accepted step sizes to the next cell.

With defaults ($\mu = 100$, $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$), the endpoint is $t = 503.0$ and period about 163.
The method `DormandPrince5` accepts 16703 and rejects 3297 of 20000 attempts, stopping with `BudgetLimit(Proposal)` after reaching only $t = 291.0$.
All three stiff methods reach the endpoint.
The method `Bdf` takes 2105 steps (7437 right-hand-side evaluations, 801 LU factorizations); third-order `RosenbrockW` needs 5342 steps.
The method `AdamsBdf` switches from `AdamsMoulton` to `Bdf` at $t = 0.038$ when indicator $\lvert h \rvert \rho(J) = 1.10$ exceeds 1 (reason `Stiffness`), never switching back.
Returning requires six consecutive accepted indicators below 1/4; the $h$ in the indicator uses the largest step taken by `Bdf` since switching.
On the periodic orbit, $\rho(J)$ does not fall below 1 ($\lvert \det J \rvert \ge 5/3$ in slow regimes, large $\lvert \operatorname{tr} J \rvert$ during jumps), so its product with the maximum step near 5 does not fall below 1/4.

Tolerance magnitude determines agreement among methods.
At $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$, periods are 162.83696 for `RosenbrockW` and 162.82850 for `Bdf`, with relative difference $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and terminal $x$ difference $1.9 \times 10^{-4}$.
At $\varepsilon = 10^{-8}$, terminal $x$ from `Bdf` and `AdamsBdf` agrees to eight digits at 1.8994446; period 162.83689 agrees with the next cell's `RadauIia` value 162.83708 to relative difference $1.2 \times 10^{-6}$.
At this tolerance, `RosenbrockW` also reaches 20000 attempts and stops with $t = 406.9$.

At $\mu = 1$ the problem is nonstiff: `DormandPrince5` takes the fewest steps, 169; `AdamsBdf` stays with Adams without switching and finishes in 497 steps.
At $\mu = 30$, `DormandPrince5` reaches the endpoint but needs 3124 steps and 658 rejections, more than `Bdf` with 1659 steps.
At $\mu = 1000$, `DormandPrince5` reaches only $t = 18.3$, before the first jump.
From $\mu = 100$ to 1000, `Bdf` step counts increase only from 2105 to 2596, unlike explicit counts proportional to $\mu^2$.

## Comparing periods with approximations

The next cell varies $\mu$ over seven choices from 1 to 1000, measuring periods using `RadauIia`.
The method `RadauIia` is fifth-order L-stable implicit Runge–Kutta, providing an independent method check.
The table gives measured period $T$, Lindstedt $2\pi(1 + \mu^2/16)$, large-$\mu$ asymptotic $(3 - 2\ln 2)\mu + 3\alpha\mu^{-1/3}$, and their relative differences.
The upper log–log plot shows periods with dashed approximations.
The lower plot shows relative differences on a linked horizontal axis.
Comparing tolerances $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-8}$ reveals numerical error through period changes.

```rust

//| title: "Measure periods for seven μ values and compare approximations"
//| run: button
//| uses: [ode-van-der-pol-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-ode]
//| inputs:
//|   period_tolerance: {"type":"select","label":"Tolerance ε [-]","description":"Absolute and relative tolerances.","options":["1e-6","1e-8"],"value":"1e-8"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"loose","label":"ε = 10⁻⁶","description":"Looser tolerance shifts the μ = 1000 period by order 10⁻².","inputs":{"period_tolerance":"1e-6"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"periods","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":6.6632868549,"rtol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"periods","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":1614.4011,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"periods","path":"/rows/6/5","range":{"max":1e-5}}

let asymptotic =
    |mu: f64| (3.0 - 2.0 * std::f64::consts::LN_2) * mu + 3.0 * airy_zero * mu.powf(-1.0 / 3.0);
let method = RadauIia::new();
let (mut rows, mut measured, mut small_gap, mut large_gap) =
    (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for mu in [1.0, 3.0, 10.0, 30.0, 100.0, 300.0, 1000.0] {
    let (path, report) =
        integrate(&method, mu, eps, OdeOutputSchedule::FinalOnly).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let termination = report.termination().ok_or("No termination status")?;
    if !termination.is_success() {
        return Err(format!("Stopped at μ = {mu} with {termination}"));
    }
    let t = period(&path).ok_or("Fewer than two crossings")?;
    let (small, large) = (lindstedt(mu), asymptotic(mu));
```

With default $\varepsilon = 10^{-8}$, period at $\mu = 1$ is 6.6632869, with Lindstedt relative difference $1.9 \times 10^{-3}$.
This is the magnitude of omitted $O(\mu^4)$ terms; at $\mu = 3$ the relative difference rises to 0.11, and at $\mu = 10$ the expansion is unusable.
Relative differences from large-$\mu$ asymptotics decrease from $1.6 \times 10^{-2}$ at $\mu = 10$ to $2.7 \times 10^{-4}$ at 100 and $3.6 \times 10^{-6}$ at 1000.
A tenfold increase in $\mu$ reduces relative differences to about 1/77, consistent with relative remainder $O(\mu^{-2}\ln\mu)$.
The $\mu = 1000$ period is 1614.4013; difference 0.0058 from asymptotic 1614.4071 matches remainder magnitude $-(2/3)\mu^{-1}\ln\mu \approx -0.0046$.
At $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$, the $\mu = 1000$ period shifts to 1614.4082; numerical error 0.0069 exceeds the asymptotic remainder, requiring $10^{-8}$ for verification.
At intermediate values such as $\mu = 3$, both approximations differ by about 10%, leaving numerical solutions to supply periods.

## Summary

Large $\mu$ makes Van der Pol stiff, with eigenvalues of order $-3\mu$ during slow motion.
Explicit `DormandPrince5` step counts grow with $\mu^2$ under stability bound $3.3065/(3\mu)$, reaching the step limit at $\mu \ge 100$.
At limits, inspect `BudgetLimit` reports and last accepted times; do not treat values as endpoint solutions.
Step counts for stiff `Bdf`, `AdamsBdf`, and `RosenbrockW` grow only four- to fivefold as $\mu$ increases from 1 to 1000; inspect `AdamsBdf` switches through `last_method_switch`.
Check solutions by comparing event-measured periods with Lindstedt and large-$\mu$ asymptotics, varying tolerances and using another method (`RadauIia`).
