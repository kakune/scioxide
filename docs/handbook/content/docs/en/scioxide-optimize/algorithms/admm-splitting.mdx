[Proximal gradient](../composite-and-splitting/) assumes that the proximal map of nonsmooth term $g$ can be computed in closed form.
Inserting a linear map as in $g(Dx)$ generally removes the closed form.
The alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) splits the problem into two blocks using a new variable such as $z=Dx$ and solves the subproblems alternately.
This page assumes $f$ and $g$ are proper closed convex functions; convergence discussions use that assumption.

## ADMM variable splitting

For $\min f(x)+g(z)$ and $Ax+Bz=c$, the augmented Lagrangian is

$$
L_\rho(x,z,y)=f(x)+g(z)+y^T(Ax+Bz-c)+\frac\rho2\|Ax+Bz-c\|^2
$$

$y$ is the equality-constraint Lagrange multiplier and $\rho>0$ the penalty weight.
Completing the square with scaled dual variable $u=y/\rho$ combines linear and quadratic terms into $\frac\rho2\|Ax+Bz-c+u\|^2-\frac\rho2\|u\|^2$.
Alternately minimizing $x$ and $z$ and updating the dual variable by ascent with step $\rho$ gives the iteration

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{k+1}&=\arg\min_x f(x)+\tfrac\rho2\|Ax+Bz^k-c+u^k\|^2,\\
z^{k+1}&=\arg\min_z g(z)+\tfrac\rho2\|Ax^{k+1}+Bz-c+u^k\|^2,\\
u^{k+1}&=u^k+Ax^{k+1}+Bz^{k+1}-c
\end{aligned}
$$

The primal residual $r=Ax+Bz-c$ measures coupling-constraint violation; the dual residual $s=\rho A^TB(z^{k+1}-z^k)$ measures the stationarity defect introduced by splitting.
Substituting $u^{k+1}$ into the $x$ subproblem's optimality condition $0\in\partial f(x^{k+1})+\rho A^T(Ax^{k+1}+Bz^k-c+u^k)$ gives $0\in\partial f(x^{k+1})+A^Ty^{k+1}-\rho A^TB(z^{k+1}-z^k)$.
The last term is $-s$; when $s=0$, the original optimality condition $0\in\partial f(x)+A^Ty$ holds for $x^{k+1}$.
Condition $0\in\partial g(z^{k+1})+B^Ty^{k+1}$ for $z$ holds at every iteration from the $z$ subproblem's optimality condition and the definition of $u^{k+1}$.
The remaining condition is coupling constraint $r=0$.
Thus smallness of only one of $r$ and $s$ is insufficient.
If $f,g$ are proper closed convex and $L_0$ has a saddle point, $r\to0$, objective convergence, and dual-variable convergence hold (Boyd et al., 2011, §3.2).
Convergence is often at best linear and strongly depends on $\rho$.
This iteration has no general nonconvex convergence guarantee, so check convexity assumptions for the model.

## Block-update API

`AdmmProblem::new(a, b, c, x_update, z_update)` borrows operators $A$ (`AdjointOperator` with an adjoint) and $B$ (`LinearOperator`), right-hand side $c$, and two `AdmmBlockUpdate` values.
Different row counts for $A$, $B$, and $c$ give `DimensionMismatch`; nonfinite $c$ gives `NonFinite`.
$f$ and $g$ appear only through subproblem solvers, so both may be nonsmooth.
The update reads `AdmmUpdateInput` accessors `block()` (`AdmmBlock::X` or `Z`), `current()`, the latest other-block value `other()`, `scaled_dual()`, `rhs()`, `operator()`, `other_operator()`, `rho()`, and writes the subproblem solution to output.
Construct the return value with `AdmmUpdateReport::new(converged, stationarity_norm)`, reporting whether the subproblem solver finished and the magnitude of its stationarity violation.
The solver trusts this report and does not recompute the violation.
If the report is unconverged or its violation exceeds subproblem tolerance (`with_subproblem_tolerance`, default absolute and relative $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, scale 1), ADMM stops with error `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`), not a termination reason.
Updates receive solver `SolveContext`; nested solves performed there count against ADMM's limits.

`AdmmStart::new(x, z, u)` borrows initial $x,z,u$; shape mismatch with the problem gives `DimensionMismatch`, nonfinite values `NonFinite`.
`AdmmOptions::new(rho)` holds $\rho$, residual and subproblem tolerances, and an iteration limit; `Admm::new` returns `InvalidInput` unless $\rho$ is positive and finite.
`solve(&problem, start)` allocates workspace per call; pass `AdmmWorkspace` and `AdmmResult::empty()` to `solve_into` for reuse.
`workspace_requirements(&problem)` returns requirements as `AdmmWorkspaceRequirements`, exposing dimensions of $x$ and $z$, constraint count, and bytes `bytes()` including operator workspace.
`AdmmResult` returns `x()`, `z()`, `scaled_dual()` and residual vectors `primal_residual()` and `dual_residual()`.
Each iteration uses two subproblems, one $A$ product, two $B$ products, and two adjoint $A^T$ products.

## ADMM stopping and penalty adjustment

Each iteration computes $r$ and $s$ in original units; using Euclidean norms,

$$
\|r\|_2\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}\max(\|Ax\|_2,\|Bz\|_2,\|c\|_2),\qquad
\|s\|_2\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}\|\rho A^Tu\|_2
$$

both conditions give termination `Converged`.
Each component of $r$ adds $Ax$, $Bz$, and $-c$ before rounding, reducing intermediate overflow risk even with large cancelling terms.
`AdmmOptions::new(rho)` defaults to $\epsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}=\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ (change with `with_residual_tolerance`) and 1000 iterations (`with_max_iterations`).
Reaching the limit stops with `IterationLimit` and returns the last accepted $x,z,u$.
`primal_norm()` and `dual_norm()` of `AdmmResult` are the final iteration's $\|r\|_2$ and $\|s\|_2$; before the first iteration finishes they are `None`.
`rho()` is the final $\rho$, `iterations()` the iteration count, and `is_success()` true only for `Converged`.

Large $\rho$ strongly enforces coupling and reduces $r$, but multiplies changes in $z$ by $\rho$, tending to enlarge $s$.
Small $\rho$ instead makes $s$ small while $r$ remains.
The default fixes $\rho$ without residual balancing.
With `with_residual_balancing(ratio, factor)` specified, after an unconverged iteration, update $\rho$ by multiplying by `factor` if $\|r\|>\text{ratio}\cdot\|s\|$ and dividing by `factor` if $\|s\|>\text{ratio}\cdot\|r\|$ (both parameters are finite and greater than 1).
Changing $\rho$ multiplies $u$ by $\rho_{\text{old}}/\rho_{\text{new}}$ to preserve $y=\rho u$ and hence the meaning of the scaled dual variable.
Boyd et al. give ratio = 10 and factor = 2 as typical values.

## Box-projection model

Solve projection of point $a\in\mathbb R^8$ ($a_i=-1+3i/7$, $i=0,\dots,7$) onto box $[0,1]^8$ with ADMM by splitting into $f(x)=\tfrac12\|x-a\|^2$, box indicator $g$, and $x-z=0$ ($A=I$, $B=-I$, $c=0$).
The analytical solution is componentwise clipping $\operatorname{clamp}(a_i,0,1)$.
The $x$ subproblem minimizes $\tfrac12\|x-a\|^2+\tfrac\rho2\|x-z+u\|^2$ with closed form $x=(a+\rho(z-u))/(1+\rho)$.
The $z$ subproblem projects $x+u$ onto the box.
$f$ has strong convexity constant 1, satisfying convexity assumptions and giving a unique minimizer.

The following definitions share the two subproblems, operators, settings, and checking helpers between the next two cells.
`Fit` updates $x$ and computes and returns violation of stationarity condition $(x-a)+\rho(x-z+u)=0$.
`UnitBox` updates $z$ and returns zero violation because projection is exact.
Construct $A$ and $B$ by passing functions `identity` and `negation` to scioxide-core's `ClosureOperator`.

```rust
//| id: optimize-admm-box-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Two box-projection subproblems and operators"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SolveContext};
use scioxide_optimize::{AdmmBlockUpdate, AdmmOptions, AdmmUpdateInput, AdmmUpdateReport};

/// Point to project aᵢ = −1 + 3i/7 (i = 0, …, 7).
pub(crate) fn box_target() -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..8).map(|i| -1.0 + 3.0 * i as f64 / 7.0).collect()
}

/// Product with A = I. The same function represents its adjoint.
pub(crate) fn identity(v: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
    out.copy_from_slice(v);
    Ok(())
}

/// Product with B = −I.
pub(crate) fn negation(v: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
    for (o, &x) in out.iter_mut().zip(v) {
        *o = -x;
    }
    Ok(())
}

/// x subproblem argmin ½‖x − a‖² + (ρ/2)‖x − z + u‖² = (a + ρ(z − u)) / (1 + ρ).
pub(crate) struct Fit(pub(crate) Vec<f64>);

impl AdmmBlockUpdate<f64> for Fit {
    fn update_into(
        &self,
        input: AdmmUpdateInput<'_, f64>,
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<AdmmUpdateReport<f64>, SciOxideError> {
        let rho = input.rho();
        let mut defect = 0.0_f64;
        let pairs = input.other().iter().zip(input.scaled_dual());
        for ((x, &a), (&z, &u)) in out.iter_mut().zip(&self.0).zip(pairs) {
            *x = (a + rho * (z - u)) / (1.0 + rho);
            // Violation of stationarity (x − a) + ρ(x − z + u) = 0.
            defect = defect.max(((*x - a) + rho * (*x - z + u)).abs());
        }
        Ok(AdmmUpdateReport::new(true, defect))
    }
}

/// z subproblem: project x + u onto box [0, 1]ⁿ.
pub(crate) struct UnitBox;

impl AdmmBlockUpdate<f64> for UnitBox {
    fn update_into(
        &self,
        input: AdmmUpdateInput<'_, f64>,
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<AdmmUpdateReport<f64>, SciOxideError> {
        let pairs = input.other().iter().zip(input.scaled_dual());
        for (z, (&x, &u)) in out.iter_mut().zip(pairs) {
            *z = (x + u).clamp(0.0, 1.0);
        }
        Ok(AdmmUpdateReport::new(true, 0.0))
    }
}

/// Construct settings from initial penalty ρ and residual balancing (ratio = 10, factor = 2).
pub(crate) fn admm_options(rho: f64, balanced: bool) -> AdmmOptions<f64> {
    let options = AdmmOptions::new(rho);
    if balanced {
        options.with_residual_balancing(10.0, 2.0)
    } else {
        options
    }
}

/// Independent check: maximum difference from projection written with f64 clamp (reference values).
pub(crate) fn projection_error(z: &[f64], target: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    z.iter()
        .zip(target)
        .map(|(&z, &a)| (z - a.clamp(0.0, 1.0)).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max)
}
```

## Penalty and iteration count

The following cell solves 10 combinations of $\rho\in\{0.01,0.1,1,10,100\}$ and balancing on/off, starting from zero.
The table lists initial $\rho$, balancing choice, iterations, termination reason, final $\|r\|_2$ and $\|s\|_2$, final $\rho$, and analytical-solution difference $\max_i|z_i-\operatorname{clamp}(a_i,0,1)|$.
There are no inputs.

```rust
//| id: optimize-admm-rho-sweep
//| ref: optimize-admm-rho-sweep
//| title: "Compare ADMM iterations by penalty ρ and residual balancing"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [optimize-admm-box-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rho-sweep","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"min":1,"max":1000}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rho-sweep","path":"/rows/2/3","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"rho-sweep","path":"/rows/2/7","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
use scioxide_core::ClosureOperator;
use scioxide_optimize::{Admm, AdmmProblem, AdmmStart};
let target = box_target();
let n = target.len();
let a = ClosureOperator::new(n, n, identity).with_adjoint(identity);
let b = ClosureOperator::new(n, n, negation);
let zero = vec![0.0; n];
let fit = Fit(target.clone());
let problem = AdmmProblem::new(&a, &b, &zero, &fit, &UnitBox).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for balanced in [false, true] {
    for exponent in -2..=2 {
        let rho = 10.0_f64.powi(exponent);
        let result = Admm::new(admm_options(rho, balanced))
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            .solve(&problem, AdmmStart::new(&zero, &zero, &zero))
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        rows.push((
            rho,
            if balanced { "On" } else { "None" },
            result.iterations(),
            result.termination().to_string(),
            result.primal_norm(),
            result.dual_norm(),
            result.rho(),
            projection_error(result.z(), &target),
        ));
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let general3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Initial ρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Balancing" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Primal residual ‖r‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Not computed" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Dual residual ‖s‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Not computed" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Final ρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Analytical-solution difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rho-sweep" })
);
```

With fixed $\rho$, $\rho=1$ takes the fewest iterations, 27, while $\rho=0.1$ and $\rho=10$ require 184 and 177.
$\rho=0.01$ and $\rho=100$ reach the 1000-iteration limit with `IterationLimit`.
However, their stopping behavior is opposite.
At $\rho=0.01$, $z$ matches the analytical solution after 8 iterations and stops moving, making $s=\rho(z^{k+1}-z^k)$ zero, while $x$ is pulled toward $a$ and $\|r\|_2=7.8\times10^{-5}$ remains.
At $\rho=100$, $x=z$ gives $\|r\|_2=0$, but changes in $z$ are multiplied by 100, leaving $\|s\|_2=3.7\times10^{-5}$, and $z$ differs from the analytical solution by $3.4\times10^{-5}$.
At $\rho=0.01$, $z$ matches the analytical solution even under `IterationLimit`, so termination reasons alone do not give accuracy; read both residuals and independent checks together.

With balancing, every initial value reaches `Converged` in 14–32 iterations.
Starting at $\rho=0.01$ doubles $\rho$ whenever the primal residual exceeds 10 times the dual residual, ending with $\rho$ equal to $0.01\times2^{13}=81.92$.
Starting at $\rho=100$ repeatedly halves $\rho$, ending at 0.78.
At $\rho=1$ the residual ratio never exceeds 10, so $\rho$ stays unchanged and the solve takes the same 27 iterations as the fixed case.

## Residual history

The following cell plots $\|r_k\|_2$ and $\|s_k\|_2$ and their stopping thresholds (dashed) by iteration for selected initial $\rho$ and balancing choice.
Record history by setting `solve_into` to one iteration and restarting from the previous result's $x,z,u,\rho$ one step at a time.
Reuse workspace `AdmmWorkspace` and result `AdmmResult` between calls.
Compare iterations and $z$ against a single solve with the same settings to check that one-step restarts give identical iterations.
Finally, show the single solve's termination status as the outcome.

```rust
//| id: optimize-admm-residual-history
//| ref: optimize-admm-residual-history
//| title: "Plot ADMM primal and dual residual histories by penalty ρ"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [optimize-admm-box-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   log_rho: {"type":"range","label":"Initial penalty log₁₀ ρ [-]","min":-2,"max":2,"step":0.5,"value":0}
//|   balancing: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Residual balancing (ratio = 10, factor = 2)","value":false}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"small-rho","label":"ρ = 0.01","description":"The dual residual stays small while the primal residual decreases slowly.","inputs":{"log_rho":-2}}
//|   - {"id":"large-rho","label":"ρ = 100","description":"The primal residual becomes small quickly while the dual residual decreases slowly.","inputs":{"log_rho":2}}
//|   - {"id":"balanced-large-rho","label":"ρ = 100 with balancing","description":"Halve ρ whenever the dual residual exceeds 10 times the primal residual.","inputs":{"log_rho":2,"balancing":true}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/diagnostics/0/value","equals":27}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/diagnostics/4/value","equals":0}
//|   - case: small-rho
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: large-rho
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: balanced-large-rho
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, ClosureOperator, ExecutionContext, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{Admm, AdmmProblem, AdmmResult, AdmmStart, AdmmWorkspace};
let target = box_target();
let n = target.len();
let a = ClosureOperator::new(n, n, identity).with_adjoint(identity);
let b = ClosureOperator::new(n, n, negation);
let zero = vec![0.0; n];
let fit = Fit(target.clone());
let problem = AdmmProblem::new(&a, &b, &zero, &fit, &UnitBox).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rho = RealScalar::powf(10.0, log_rho);
let full = Admm::new(admm_options(rho, balancing))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .solve(&problem, AdmmStart::new(&zero, &zero, &zero))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = AdmmWorkspace::new();
let mut step = AdmmResult::empty();
let (mut x, mut z, mut u, mut current) = (zero.clone(), zero.clone(), zero.clone(), rho);
let (mut primal, mut dual, mut primal_limit, mut dual_limit) =
    (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let norm = |v: &[f64]| RealScalar::sqrt(v.iter().map(|t| t * t).sum::<f64>());
let tolerance = RealScalar::sqrt(f64::EPSILON);
for k in 1..=full.iterations() {
    // Set the iteration limit to 1 and take one step from the previous result's x, z, u, ρ.
    Admm::new(admm_options(current, balancing).with_max_iterations(Budget::ONE))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .solve_into(
            &problem,
            AdmmStart::new(&x, &z, &u),
            &mut step,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential_native(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let k = k as f64;
    primal.push([k, step.primal_norm().ok_or("Missing primal residual")?]);
    dual.push([k, step.dual_norm().ok_or("Missing dual residual")?]);
    // Thresholds ε_abs + ε_rel·max(‖x‖, ‖z‖) and ε_abs + ε_rel·‖ρu‖ (balancing leaves ρu unchanged).
    primal_limit.push([
        k,
        tolerance + tolerance * norm(step.x()).max(norm(step.z())),
    ]);
    dual_limit.push([
        k,
        tolerance + tolerance * step.rho() * norm(step.scaled_dual()),
    ]);
    x.copy_from_slice(step.x());
    z.copy_from_slice(step.z());
    u.copy_from_slice(step.scaled_dual());
    current = step.rho();
}
// Omit zero residuals from logarithmic axes and show the count in the outcome.
let removed = primal.iter().chain(&dual).filter(|p| p[1] <= 0.0).count();
primal.retain(|p| p[1] > 0.0);
dual.retain(|p| p[1] > 0.0);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Primal residual ‖r‖₂", "points": primal, "style": { "showSymbol": true } }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Dual residual ‖s‖₂", "points": dual, "style": { "showSymbol": true } }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Primal residual threshold",
            "points": primal_limit,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Dual residual threshold",
            "points": dual_limit,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Iteration k [count]",
        "yLabel": "Residual norm [-]",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let chain_gap = z
    .iter()
    .zip(full.z())
    .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let primal_norm = full.primal_norm().ok_or("Missing primal residual")?;
let dual_norm = full.dual_norm().ok_or("Missing dual residual")?;
let outcome = if full.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Both primal and dual residuals fell below their thresholds")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Iteration limit reached before residuals fell below thresholds")
        .explanation("Returned x and z are the last accepted iterate; treat them as projection estimates.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(full.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Iterations", full.iterations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Primal residual", primal_norm, None)
        .diagnostic("Dual residual", dual_norm, None)
        .diagnostic("Analytical-solution difference", projection_error(full.z(), &target), None)
        .diagnostic("Difference from one-step z", chain_gap, None)
        .diagnostic("Points omitted from logarithmic axes", removed as f64, None)
);
```

At the default $\rho=1$, both residuals converge linearly at nearly the same rate, falling below thresholds in 27 iterations with outcome satisfied.
$z$ from one-step restarts exactly matches $z$ from the single solve.
At $\rho=0.01$, the dual residual is zero from iteration 9, so only its first 8 points appear on the log axis while the primal residual decreases slowly above threshold.
At $\rho=100$, the primal residual instead becomes zero from iteration 36 and the dual residual remains above threshold.
The omitted-point count appears as “Points omitted from logarithmic axes” in the outcome.
With balancing enabled and a start at $\rho=100$, $\rho$ halves whenever the dual residual exceeds 10 times the primal residual; both fall below thresholds once balanced.

## Summary

Splitting variables as in $z=Dx$ handles terms without a closed-form proximal map by alternating minimization of two subproblems.
Stopping uses both primal and dual residuals; one small residual alone is not convergence.
Too small a penalty $\rho$ leaves primal residuals, while too large a penalty leaves dual residuals; with many iterations, examine their ratio and change $\rho$ or use `with_residual_balancing`.
Subproblem solvers must honestly report stationarity violations through `AdmmUpdateReport`; ADMM stops with an error when tolerance is exceeded.
Even under `IterationLimit`, the last $x,z,u$ is returned; check residuals and independent accuracy before using it.
