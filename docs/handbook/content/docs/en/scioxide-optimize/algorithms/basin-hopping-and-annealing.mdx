Basin hopping and dual annealing perform global search through a chain that proposes a next candidate from the current point and decides acceptance probabilistically.
Both accept worsening candidates with some probability, allowing escape from shallow valleys.
Their acceptance rules originate in the statistical-mechanics Metropolis method; a parameter called temperature controls willingness to accept worsening.
This page derives both procedures and the meaning of temperature, then examines SciOxide's `BasinHopping` and `DualAnnealing` settings and results on two-dimensional Rastrigin.
Neither claims global optimality; exhausting chain steps gives unsuccessful `SearchBudgetCompleted`.

## basin hopping

Basin hopping (Wales and Doye, 1997) replaces objective function $f$ with the locally minimized value

$$
\tilde f(\mathbf{x}) = f\bigl(\operatorname{LocalMin}(\mathbf{x})\bigr)
$$

and advances the chain over this value.
$\tilde f$ becomes a step function constant within each basin, removing small-scale variation inside basins.
One step proceeds as follows.

1. Displace current local solution $\mathbf{x}_k$ to create candidate starting point $\mathbf{y}$.
2. Minimize from $\mathbf{y}$ with a local solver, obtaining local solution $\mathbf{x}'$ and value $f' = f(\mathbf{x}')$.
3. Setting $\Delta = f' - f(\mathbf{x}_k)$, set $\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}'$ with probability $\min(1, e^{-\Delta/T})$; otherwise set $\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k$.

Step 3 uses the Metropolis rule; temperature $T$ has the same units as the objective.
Candidates with $\Delta \le 0$ are always accepted; worsening candidates are rarely accepted when $\Delta$ exceeds a few times $T$.
At $T = 0$, basin hopping is monotone and rejects all worsening candidates.
Choose $T$ to match typical objective differences between local solutions.
Too small a temperature traps the chain near its first valley; too large a temperature prevents it staying in deep valleys and approaches random search.

SciOxide's `BasinHopping` locally minimizes from `x0` at step 0 and uses the result as the initial chain point.
From step 1, `BasinProposal` displaces the current point to create a start, locally minimizes it, and decides acceptance with Metropolis.
A step whose local solve errors has no candidate and does not replace the current point.
The chain is sequential, so `BasinHopping::new` rejects nonsequential execution policies with `InvalidInput`.
Each step costs one local solve.

`UniformBasinProposal::new(step)` moves each coordinate independently by a uniform variate on $[-\mathrm{step}, \mathrm{step}]$ times box width, reflecting any excursion at the boundary.
NaN, infinite, or nonpositive `step` gives `InvalidInput`.
Supply another displacement by implementing `BasinProposal`.
Implementations must draw randomness only from supplied `SearchRandom` and write an in-box point; returning an out-of-box point stops search with `InvalidInput`.

## dual annealing

Simulated annealing accepts candidates by Metropolis while gradually lowering temperature.
At high temperature it accepts worsening moves and explores widely; at low temperature it remains near valley floors.
Dual annealing (Xiang et al., 1997) combines Tsallis and Stariolo's generalized simulated annealing with local optimization.

### Visiting distribution and cooling

Normalize box coordinates to $[0, 1)$ and draw displacements from a visiting distribution.
The generalized-annealing visiting distribution is a Tsallis $q$-Gaussian with exponent $q_v \in (1, 3)$; its one-coordinate density is

$$
g(\Delta) \propto \left[1 + (q_v - 1)\frac{\Delta^2}{T^{2/(3-q_v)}}\right]^{-1/(q_v-1)}
$$

This equals a Student $t$ distribution with $\nu = (3 - q_v)/(q_v - 1)$ degrees of freedom stretched by scale $T^{1/(3-q_v)}/\sqrt{3 - q_v}$; SciOxide draws displacements from this $t$ distribution.
Smaller $\nu$ gives heavier tails and occasional large jumps to distant valleys.
Default $q_v = 2.62$ gives $\nu \approx 0.23$.
Coordinates displaced outside $[0, 1)$ wrap periodically from the opposite side.

Visiting temperature decreases with sweep index $k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ as

$$
T(k) = T_0 \, \frac{2^{q_v - 1} - 1}{(k + 2)^{q_v - 1} - 1}
$$

the schedule above.
$T(0) = T_0$; for large $k$, it decays as the power $T(k) \approx T_0 (2^{q_v-1} - 1) k^{-(q_v-1)}$.
A sweep has $2n$ steps for $n$ variables: the first $n$ move all coordinates, the last $n$ move one coordinate at a time.

### Generalized Metropolis rule and reheating

Worsening candidates ($\Delta > 0$) are accepted with generalized Metropolis probability of acceptance exponent $q_a < 1$,

$$
p = \left[1 - (1 - q_a)\frac{\Delta}{T_a}\right]^{1/(1 - q_a)}, \qquad T_a = \frac{T(k)}{k + 1}
$$

and rejected if $1 - (1-q_a)\Delta/T_a \le 0$.
The limit $q_a \to 1$ recovers ordinary $e^{-\Delta/T_a}$.
At $q_a < 1$ the acceptance probability becomes zero at finite $\Delta$, always rejecting sufficiently large worsening.
Acceptance temperature $T_a$ divides visiting temperature by a further $k + 1$ and cools faster.

When $T(k)$ reaches or falls below $T_0$ times reheating ratio $r$, restart the chain from a uniform in-box point at temperature $T_0$ (reannealing).
The reheating point becomes the current chain point without an acceptance test.
Default $q_v = 2.62$ and $r = 2 \times 10^{-5}$ reheat after $k \approx 1245$ sweeps from $(k + 2)^{1.62} \ge (2^{1.62} - 1)/r$, approximately 5000 steps for 2 variables.

### Local optimization

With local optimization enabled, locally minimize the first point, points improving the best value so far, and reheating points.
Use the local solution as candidate if its value is no larger than the annealing point's.
If the local solve fails or has a higher value, retain the annealing point as candidate.
Each step costs one objective evaluation and the local-optimization solve.

## Basin-hopping and dual-annealing settings and results

`BasinHoppingOptions::new(multistart, temperature)` accepts `GlobalSearchOptions` and a Metropolis temperature, returning `InvalidInput` for NaN, infinite, or negative temperature.
`DualAnnealingOptions::new(multistart)` and `default()` (constructed from `GlobalSearchOptions::default()`) use $T_0 = 5230$, $q_v = 2.62$, $q_a = -5$, $r = 2 \times 10^{-5}$, with local optimization enabled.
`with_schedule(T₀, q_v, q_a, r)` stores values without checking; `DualAnnealing::new(options, factory)` rejects nonfinite values, $T_0 \le 0$, $q_v$ outside $(1, 3)$, $q_a \ge 1$, and $r$ outside $(0, 1)$ with `InvalidInput`.
Disable local optimization with `with_local_search(false)`.

For both methods, `GlobalSearchOptions::with_starts` determines chain length including step 0.
The default is 10 steps; a 2-variable dual-annealing sweep has 4 steps, so the 9 after step 0 amount to only a little over 2 sweeps.
Therefore the step count is usually specified explicitly.
`BasinHopping::new(options, proposal, factory)` and `DualAnnealing::new(options, factory)` reject a nonfinite or nonpositive local-minimum merging tolerance in `GlobalSearchOptions` and nonsequential execution policies with `InvalidInput`.
Local-solver factories use the same `LocalSolverFactory` as [Multistart methods](oxref:optimize-local-solver-injection).

`minimize_into(problem, x0, output, workspace, scope)` and `minimize(problem, x0)` run the chain from `x0`.
`x0` must be finite and inside the box; otherwise `InvalidInput`.
In dual annealing, a NaN or infinite objective at a visited point gives `NonFinite`; visiting-displacement overflow gives `NumericalInstability`.

The result is the same `GlobalSearchResult` as multistart, with `SearchStartReport` for each step.
`accepted()` reports whether that step's candidate became current, `temperature()` its temperature (constant $T$ for basin hopping, visiting temperature $T(k)$ for dual annealing), and `reannealed()` whether reheating occurred.
In dual annealing, `candidate()` is the lower-valued annealing point or local solution, and `local_result()` is the local-solve result.
`best()` is the best candidate over all steps; exhausting chain steps gives `SearchBudgetCompleted`.

## Rastrigin function and injected local solver

The two-dimensional Rastrigin function

$$
f(x, y) = 2A + \bigl(x^2 - A\cos 2\pi x\bigr) + \bigl(y^2 - A\cos 2\pi y\bigr), \qquad (x, y) \in [-5.12, 5.12]^2
$$

has global minimum value $f = 0$ at the origin when $A = 10$, with local solutions near integer grid points.
A local solution near grid point $(i, j)$ has value approximately $i^2 + j^2$, so neighboring local values differ by roughly 1 to 9.
The x component of the gradient is $\partial f/\partial x = 2x + 2\pi A \sin 2\pi x$, with the $y$ component of the same form.

The following definitions give this function and analytical gradient and implement an L-BFGS-B local-solver factory in the same form as [Multistart methods](../multistart/).
Both cells set `x0` to local solution $\mathbf{x}_0 = (3.97978, -2.98486)$ ($f = 24.87$) near grid point $(4, -3)$.
This point was obtained by Newton solution of $\partial f/\partial x = \partial f/\partial y = 0$, and the local solver does not move from it; the chain can progress only by moving to another valley through displacement or visiting.
The table’s “First step reaching the minimum” is the first step at which a candidate objective falls below $10^{-8}$, checking entry into the global-minimum basin against the known minimum 0.

```rust
//| id: optimize-chain-rastrigin-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Two-dimensional Rastrigin and an injected L-BFGS-B local-solver factory"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar, SolveContext};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    BoundedObjective, GlobalSearchResult, LbfgsB, LbfgsBWorkspace, LocalSolverFactory,
    ObjectiveFunction, OptimizeResult, SearchLocalSolver,
};

/// Variation strength A.
pub(crate) const AMPLITUDE: f64 = 10.0;

/// Local solution near grid point (4, −3). Found by Newton solution of zero gradient, with f = 24.87.
pub(crate) const START: [f64; 2] = [3.9797838603007536, -2.984855701039481];

/// First step whose candidate objective is below 1e-8. Checks whether the global minimum 0 was reached.
pub(crate) fn first_hit(result: &GlobalSearchResult<f64>) -> Option<usize> {
    result
        .starts()
        .iter()
        .position(|r| r.candidate().is_some_and(|c| c.fun() < 1e-8))
}

/// Rastrigin f = 2A + Σ (x_j² − A cos 2πx_j) and its analytical gradient.
pub(crate) struct Rastrigin;
impl ObjectiveFunction<f64> for Rastrigin {
    fn dim(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn eval(&self, x: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        let tau = std::f64::consts::TAU;
        Ok(x.iter()
            .map(|&v| AMPLITUDE + v * v - AMPLITUDE * RealScalar::cos(tau * v))
            .sum())
    }
    fn gradient_into(&self, x: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<bool, SciOxideError> {
        let tau = std::f64::consts::TAU;
        for (g, &v) in out.iter_mut().zip(x) {
            *g = 2.0 * v + tau * AMPLITUDE * RealScalar::sin(tau * v);
        }
        Ok(true)
    }
}

/// Injected local-solver factory. Search uses the solver prepared for each step.
#[derive(Debug)]
pub(crate) struct LbfgsBFactory;
pub(crate) struct LbfgsBLocal {
    solver: LbfgsB<f64>,
    work: LbfgsBWorkspace<f64>,
}
impl LocalSolverFactory<f64> for LbfgsBFactory {
    type Solver = LbfgsBLocal;
    fn prepare(&self, dimension: usize) -> Result<LbfgsBLocal, SciOxideError> {
        let solver = LbfgsB::default();
        let work = solver.workspace(dimension)?;
        Ok(LbfgsBLocal { solver, work })
    }
}
impl SearchLocalSolver<f64> for LbfgsBLocal {
    fn minimize_into(
        &mut self,
        problem: &BoundedObjective<'_, f64>,
        start: &[f64],
        _: u64,
        output: &mut OptimizeResult<f64>,
        context: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        self.solver
            .minimize_into(problem, start, output, &mut self.work, context)
    }
}
```

At default $T = 2$, step size 0.1 ($\pm 1.02$ per coordinate), and seed 42, 25 of 59 candidates are accepted, including 7 worsening moves.
The chain enters the minimum-0 valley at step 17, and the best point is distance $9.3 \times 10^{-10}$ from the origin.
At $T = 0$ no worsening is accepted; accepted steps fall to 8, but this seed reaches the minimum at the same step 17.
At $T = 20$, 51 candidates are accepted, including 20 worsening moves, and the chain wanders among valleys.
It fails to reach the minimum in 60 steps; its best value is 0.995 at the local solution next to the origin.
When local-solution values differ by roughly 1 to 9, taking $T$ much larger makes acceptance indiscriminate.

Seed 100 reaches the minimum at step 6, while seed 1 misses it within 60 steps.
A single chain depends strongly on its random sequence.

### Dual-annealing temperature schedule and reheating

## Summary

Basin hopping displaces a local solution, locally minimizes, and accepts through Metropolis comparison of objective differences with temperature $T$; choose $T$ to match differences between local values.
Dual annealing makes distant jumps with heavy-tailed visiting, cools by a power law, reheats at a threshold, and locally optimizes initial and best points.
Both are sequential chains whose step count comes from `GlobalSearchOptions::with_starts`; the default 10 steps are often insufficient.
From `accepted()`, `temperature()`, and `reannealed()` in each step's `SearchStartReport`, check movement between valleys and temperature schedules.
`SearchBudgetCompleted` is unsuccessful; treat the best point as a candidate to check against known values, other seeds, or other methods.
