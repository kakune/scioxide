In box-constrained minimization,

$$
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^n} f(\mathbf{x}) \quad \text{subject to} \quad l_i\le x_i\le u_i \quad (i=1,\dots,n)
$$

outward movement from a boundary is not permitted.
Assume $f$ is continuously differentiable; bounds may be one-sided or fixed at $l_i=u_i$ ($l_i=-\infty$ and $u_i=+\infty$ denote no bound).
The ordinary gradient need not vanish at a minimizer, so examine whether feasible improvement directions remain.
This page explains first-order optimality conditions, projected-gradient stopping, one iteration of SciOxide's `LbfgsB`, settings, and workspace in that order.
Classical L-BFGS-B generalized Cauchy points and changes in active bounds are discussed in [Generalized Cauchy point and active-set identification](../lbfgsb-cauchy-point-and-active-set/).

## First-order conditions at bounds

Attaching multipliers $\lambda_i,\mu_i\ge0$ to inequalities $x_i-l_i\ge0$ and $u_i-x_i\ge0$ gives KKT conditions

$$
\nabla f(\mathbf{x}) = \boldsymbol{\lambda} - \boldsymbol{\mu}, \qquad \lambda_i (x_i-l_i)=0, \qquad \mu_i (u_i-x_i)=0, \qquad \boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}\ge\mathbf{0}
$$

All box constraints are linear, so a constraint qualification holds and local minimizers satisfy these conditions.
For components with $l_i=u_i$, the two constraint gradients $\pm\mathbf{e}_i$ are linearly dependent, but this is no obstruction for linear constraints.
Componentwise, require $g_i=0$ in the interior, $g_i=\lambda_i\ge0$ at a lower bound, and $g_i=-\mu_i\le0$ at an upper bound ($g_i=\partial f/\partial x_i$).
A positive gradient at a lower bound means the objective rises in the permitted increasing direction, so that component need not move.
A component with equal lower and upper bounds has no free direction, regardless of its gradient sign.

The projected gradient removes negative-gradient components pointing outside bounds.

$$
(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{g})_i = \begin{cases}
0 & (l_i=u_i,\ \text{or}\ x_i=l_i \text{ and } g_i\ge0,\ \text{or}\ x_i=u_i \text{ and } g_i\le0) \\
g_i & (\text{otherwise})
\end{cases}
$$

At a feasible point, first-order optimality is equivalent to $\mathbf{P}\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{0}$.
Writing box projection as $P_{[\mathbf{l},\mathbf{u}]}$, this is also equivalent to fixed-point condition $\mathbf{x}=P_{[\mathbf{l},\mathbf{u}]}(\mathbf{x}-\alpha\mathbf{g})$ for any $\alpha>0$.
For convex objectives this also suffices for global minimality; for nonconvex objectives it establishes only stationarity.

### Projected-gradient stopping

Before each iteration, `LbfgsB` checks the largest projected-gradient component and stops when

$$
\|\mathbf{P}\mathbf{g}\|_\infty \le \tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\max\!\left(1, \frac{|f(\mathbf{x})|}{\max(1,\|\mathbf{x}\|_\infty)}\right)
$$

holds, returning `GradientSmall`.
Default `GradientTolerance` is $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}=\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}\approx1.49\times10^{-8}$.
Result diagnostic `diagnostics().stationarity()` returns the same projected gradient's 2-norm; `bound_violation()` returns maximum box violation.
The stopping quantity ($\infty$-norm) and diagnostic quantity (2-norm) use different norms.
Objective changes and small steps do not stop the method; this criterion alone determines success.

## One projected L-BFGS iteration

`LbfgsB` applies an inverse L-BFGS model to the projected gradient, then projects the result onto the same free components.
Let $F$ contain free components excluding fixed components and those pressed against bounds by the gradient; with diagonal 0/1 matrix $\mathbf{P}_F$ retaining only $F$ components, the projected gradient is $\mathbf{P}\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{g}$.
Using current point $\mathbf{x}_k$, gradient $\mathbf{g}_k$, and inverse-Hessian approximation $\mathbf{H}_k$ from the latest $m$ difference pairs (only its product is computed by the [two-loop recursion](../first-order-and-quasi-newton/)), one iteration proceeds as follows.

1. Form projected gradient $\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{g}_k$ (zero fixed components and those pushing outside the bounds).
2. Compute model direction $\mathbf{d}=-\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{q}$ with the two-loop recursion.
   If recursion breaks down numerically, discard history and set $\mathbf{d}=-\mathbf{g}_k$.
3. Zero components outside $F$ and boundary components where $\mathbf{d}$ points outward ($x_i=l_i$ and $d_i<0$, or $x_i=u_i$ and $d_i>0$).
   Before zeroing outward components, the direction is $\mathbf{d}=-\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{g}_k$.
4. Unless $\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}<0$, discard history, set $\mathbf{d}=-\mathbf{g}_k$, and repeat from step 3 (projected steepest descent).
5. Project the unit-step endpoint onto the box and replace the direction with $\mathbf{d}\leftarrow P_{[\mathbf{l},\mathbf{u}]}(\mathbf{x}_k+\mathbf{d})-\mathbf{x}_k$.
   Recompute slope $\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}$ for the replacement direction; if nonnegative, switch to step 4's projected steepest descent.
   If even projected steepest descent gives no negative slope, stop with `Stagnation`.
6. Choose step $\alpha$ on segment $\mathbf{x}_k+\alpha\mathbf{d}$ ($0<\alpha\le1$) by Armijo backtracking ($c_1=10^{-4}$, contraction 0.5, at most 40 trials).
   The box is convex, so every point on this segment is feasible.
   If no acceptable step exists, stop with `LineSearchFailed`.
7. At the accepted point, form $\mathbf{s}_k=\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_k$ and $\mathbf{y}_k=\mathbf{g}_{k+1}-\mathbf{g}_k$, check curvature, and add them to history.

### Why the direction is descending

Since $\mathbf{P}_F$ is symmetric, the direction's slope before removing outward components in step 3 is $\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}=-(\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{g}_k)^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}_k(\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{g}_k)$, negative when $\mathbf{H}_k$ is positive definite and $\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{g}_k\ne\mathbf{0}$.
Without output-side $\mathbf{P}_F$, off-diagonal entries of $\mathbf{H}_k$ can move gradient-constrained components inward and produce a positive slope.
The guarantee no longer applies after zeroing outward components or step 5's replacement, so recompute the slope and verify negativity before searching.
Armijo comparisons allow rounding slack of $8\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ relative to the compared magnitudes, but only the projected-gradient criterion above determines stopping.

Any number of active bounds may change in one iteration.
Step 5 can project many components onto bounds simultaneously; steps 1 and 3 hold components only by gradient and direction signs, so a component leaves its bound at the next iteration if its gradient turns inward.

### Curvature checks and damping

Step 7 checks curvature in Hessian-approximation $\mathbf{B}_k=\mathbf{H}_k^{-1}$ form; if $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k$ falls below $\eta\,\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k$ (default $\eta=0.2$), Powell-damp $\mathbf{y}_k$.
Compute $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k$ through LU of the $2m\times2m$ coefficient matrix in compact representation $\mathbf{B}_k=\theta\mathbf{I}-\theta\mathbf{W}\mathbf{K}^{-1}\mathbf{W}^\mathsf{T}$ ($\mathbf{W}=[\mathbf{S},\mathbf{Y}]$), storing normalized history inner products.
Damped pairs and pairs unresolved by coefficient-space computation are reevaluated by reconstructing normalized length-$n$ vectors.
Damping preserves $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k>0$, so updates keep $\mathbf{H}_k$ positive definite.

This method does not use the generalized Cauchy point or compact free-subspace linear system of classical BLNZ (Byrd–Lu–Nocedal–Zhu) L-BFGS-B.
Do not read descriptions of the classical algorithm as iteration steps of this implementation.
Compare the two in [Differences from classical L-BFGS-B](oxref:optimize-lbfgsb-vs-classical).

## Problem and workspace

Bundle objective and bounds in `BoundedObjective`; configure history length and tolerances with `LbfgsBOptions`.
The start must lie inside the box; an outside start gives `InvalidInput`.
A starting-point length different from the dimension gives `DimensionMismatch`; NaN or infinity in the start, initial objective, or initial gradient gives `NonFinite`.
Defaults for `LbfgsBOptions` are as follows.

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `with_memory` | 10 pairs | Number $m$ of retained difference pairs $(\mathbf{s}_i,\mathbf{y}_i)$ |
| `with_max_iterations` | 1000 iterations | Outer-iteration limit (gives `IterationLimit` when reached) |
| `with_gradient_tolerance` | $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$ (absolute/relative) | Projected-gradient stopping |
| `with_curvature_policy` | Powell damping, $\eta=0.2$ | Treatment of insufficient-curvature pairs |
| `with_derivative_strategy` | `AnalyticOrForward` (forward differences without analytical gradient) | Gradient source (numerical differences evaluate inside the box) |
| `with_time_limit` | None | Time limit checked at computation boundaries |

Line search is fixed to Armijo backtracking and cannot be configured to strong Wolfe.

Termination reason is returned as `OptimizeTermination`.
Only `GradientSmall` is successful, indicating the projected-gradient criterion was met.
`IterationLimit`, time limits, and `Stagnation` return the last accepted point as an estimate.
`LineSearchFailed` means no acceptable step was found in that iteration.

`LbfgsBWorkspaceRequirements` describes required capacity; `LbfgsBWorkspace` holds iteration memory.
Pass workspace created by `LbfgsB::workspace(n)` to `minimize_into` to resolve same-sized problems without reallocating.
Memory is $O(nm+m^2)$ for $n$ variables and history length $m$.
Each iteration costs $O(nm)$ in vector operations, $O(m^3)$ for coefficient-space curvature evaluation, and $O(nm^2)$ for exceptional reevaluation.
Balance benefits of longer history against memory and curvature-evaluation work.

One-shot `minimize_bounded(f, &x0, &bounds)` constructs `BoundedObjective` from an objective closure and `scioxide_core::Bounds` and solves with default `LbfgsB`.
Gradients use forward differences; unsuccessful terminations return `Err`.

Rescaling variables also rescales bounds and gradients.
State which coordinates' projected gradients receive tolerances, alongside objective magnitude.

## A quadratic reaching the boundary

Minimize $f(x)=\tfrac12[(x_0-a)^2+(x_1+1)^2]$ over $0\le x_i\le1$.
The input is the first component $a$ of the unconstrained minimizer; the start is $(0.5, 0.5)$.
Output tables give component solutions, original and projected gradients, bound status, analytical-solution differences, termination reasons, iterations, and evaluations.
The independent analytical solution is $(\min(1,\max(0,a)),0)$.
Finally, show termination as the computation outcome.

```rust
//| id: optimize-lbfgsb-active-set
//| ref: optimize-lbfgsb-active-set
//| title: "Compare gradient signs in the box interior and at bounds"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   target: {"type":"range","label":"Unconstrained minimizer a [-]","min":-1,"max":2,"step":0.25,"value":1.5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"interior","label":"a = 0.5 (interior)","description":"The first component is interior at x₀ = 0.5 with zero gradient; only the second remains at the lower bound.","inputs":{"target":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"lower","label":"a = −1 (lower bound)","description":"Both components are at lower bound 0; their original gradients are +1 but projected gradients vanish.","inputs":{"target":-1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-0.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":"Upper bound"}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":"Lower bound"}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: interior
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":"Interior"}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-8,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: lower
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":"Lower bound"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::Bounds;
use scioxide_optimize::{BoundedObjective, LbfgsB, ObjectiveFn, OptimizeTermination};

let a = target;
let f = ObjectiveFn::new(2, move |x: &[f64]| {
    Ok(0.5 * ((x[0] - a) * (x[0] - a) + (x[1] + 1.0) * (x[1] + 1.0)))
})
.with_gradient(move |x: &[f64], g: &mut [f64]| {
    g.copy_from_slice(&[x[0] - a, x[1] + 1.0]);
    Ok(())
});
let bounds = Bounds::new(vec![(Some(0.0), Some(1.0)); 2]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = BoundedObjective::new(&f, &bounds).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let result = LbfgsB::default()
    .minimize(&problem, &[0.5, 0.5])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let x = result.x();
// Independent check: project the vertices of two separable parabolas onto [0, 1].
let exact = [a.clamp(0.0, 1.0), 0.0];
let gradient = [x[0] - a, x[1] + 1.0];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for i in 0..2 {
    // Components with g ≥ 0 at lower bounds or g ≤ 0 at upper bounds have zero projected gradient.
    let (state, projected) = if x[i] == 0.0 && gradient[i] >= 0.0 {
        ("Lower bound", 0.0)
    } else if x[i] == 1.0 && gradient[i] <= 0.0 {
        ("Upper bound", 0.0)
    } else {
        ("Interior", gradient[i])
    };
    rows.push((
        i,
        x[i],
        gradient[i],
        projected,
        state,
        (x[i] - exact[i]).abs(),
    ));
}
let general = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Component i", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Solution xᵢ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Original gradient gᵢ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Projected gradient (Pg)ᵢ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Status" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Analytical-solution difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "components" })
);
let summary_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let termination = result.termination();
emit_table_with_columns!(
    summary_columns,
    vec![(
        termination.to_string(),
        result.iterations(),
        result.fun_evals()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Projected gradient fell below the stopping threshold")
} else if matches!(termination, OptimizeTermination::LineSearchFailed(_)) {
    Outcome::failed("Line search could not find an acceptable step")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped before meeting the projected-gradient criterion")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(termination.to_string())
        .diagnostic("Iterations", result.iterations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Objective evaluations", result.fun_evals() as f64, None)
);
```

At $a=1.5$ the first component is at its upper bound and second at its lower bound; the original gradient is $(-0.5, 1)$ but the projected gradient is $(0,0)$, giving `GradientSmall` after 1 iteration (2 objective evaluations).
The gradient at start $(0.5,0.5)$ is $(-1,1.5)$, so projecting endpoint $(1.5,-1)$ of initial direction $-\mathbf{g}=(1,-1.5)$ onto the box immediately gives solution $(1,0)$.
At $a=0.5$ the first component enters the interior and its gradient vanishes.
At $a=-1$ both components lie at lower bounds with original gradients $+1$; the permitted increasing direction raises the objective, so projected gradients vanish.
Original gradient magnitudes remain of order 1 depending on $a$, so unconstrained-gradient-norm stopping would never declare success in these cases.

## Summary

At a box optimum, $g_i=0$ in the interior, $g_i\ge0$ at lower bounds, and $g_i\le0$ at upper bounds combine into projected-gradient condition $\mathbf{P}\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{0}$.
`LbfgsB` returns `GradientSmall` only when $\|\mathbf{P}\mathbf{g}\|_\infty$ falls below a threshold based on $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$; original gradient magnitude or small steps do not give success.
Each iteration applies an inverse L-BFGS model to the projected gradient and chooses an Armijo step along the segment to the projected unit-step endpoint, keeping trial points inside the box.
The direction is $-\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{P}_F\mathbf{g}$, descending if $\mathbf{H}_k$ is positive definite; history is discarded for projected steepest descent only on numerical breakdown or loss of descent.
Read termination, projected gradients, and bound status together; treat points from `IterationLimit` or `Stagnation` as estimates.
