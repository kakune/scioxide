Convex quadratic programs constrained only by variable bounds $\mathbf{l}\le\mathbf{x}\le\mathbf{u}$ arise as subproblems of nonnegative least squares and box-constrained regression.
Their feasible-set projection is componentwise, allowing methods without matrix factorization.
This page derives projected gradients and Barzilai–Borwein (BB) steps and explains the `QpMethod::Automatic` implementation and differences from active-set methods.
KKT conditions, active sets, and convexity checks are in [Convex quadratic programming and KKT systems](../quadratic-programming/).

## Projected gradients and Barzilai–Borwein steps

Projection onto box $\{\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{l}\le\mathbf{x}\le\mathbf{u}\}$ is

$$
P(\mathbf{x})=\min(\max(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{l}),\mathbf{u})
$$

computed componentwise (unbounded components remain unchanged).
Projected gradient iterates as

$$
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}=P\big(\mathbf{x}_k-\alpha_k\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)\big)
$$

without requiring matrix factorization.
For convex $f$, condition $\mathbf{x}^*=P(\mathbf{x}^*-\alpha\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^*))$ for some $\alpha>0$ is equivalent to $\mathbf{x}^*$ being a minimizer.
Componentwise, $\partial_j f=0$ in the interior, $\partial_j f\ge0$ at lower bounds, and $\partial_j f\le0$ at upper bounds are exactly the bound KKT conditions.
Measure their violation by the norm of the gradient after zeroing outward boundary components (projected-gradient norm).

The step size uses the Barzilai–Borwein formula

$$
\alpha_{k+1}=\frac{\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{s}_k}{\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k},\qquad \mathbf{s}_k=\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_k,\quad \mathbf{y}_k=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{k+1})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{s}_k
$$

(Barzilai and Borwein, 1988).
This chooses scalar matrix $\alpha^{-1}\mathbf{I}$ to satisfy secant condition $\alpha^{-1}\mathbf{s}_k\approx\mathbf{y}_k$ in least squares; $\alpha^{-1}=\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{s}_k/\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{s}_k$ is a Rayleigh quotient of $\mathbf{Q}$ and lies between its smallest and largest eigenvalues.
Capturing curvature in one scalar often needs fewer iterations than fixed-step gradient descent, but pure BB steps need not decrease the objective monotonically.
Each iteration computes a $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}$ product, costing $O(n^2)$ for dense $\mathbf{Q}$.
Large condition numbers of $\mathbf{Q}$ increase iteration counts.

## Automatic projected BB method

`QpMethod::Automatic` uses this method for bound-only problems without general row constraints.

- Start from the point in `QpWarmStart` or zero projected onto the bounds.
- Set the initial step size to $1/\max_iQ_{ii}$.
- If the trial objective exceeds the preceding value, halve the step and project again (at most 64 times).
  If no decrease occurs in 64 trials, return the previous point and stop with `Stagnation`.
  This backtracking makes implementation iterates nonincreasing in objective.
- If the normalized inner product (angle cosine) of $\mathbf{s}_k$ and $\mathbf{y}_k$ is at most $\sqrt{\epsilon}$, skip the BB update and retain the previous step size.
- When the projected-gradient norm is at most the gradient tolerance, construct and check original-problem KKT certificates.
  Passing verification stops with `Converged`.
  Otherwise continue; if the point has stopped moving, stop with `Stagnation`.
- Reaching the iteration limit (default 1000) gives `IterationLimit` with the final point and certificates.

Specify `QpMethod::ActiveSet` to solve the same problem by active sets.
The active-set method updates a Cholesky factorization with active coordinates eliminated and places boundary components exactly at their bounds.
Both methods check positive semidefiniteness of $\mathbf{Q}$ before solving and reject failures with `InvalidInput`.

## Comparing projected BB and active sets

Solve a bound-only QP with `Automatic` (projected BB) and `ActiveSet`.
The objective is $\tfrac12\mathbf{x}^\mathsf{T}\begin{bmatrix}2&q\\q&2\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}-4x_1+x_2$, the box $0\le x_i\le1$, and the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{Q}$ are $2\pm q$.
For $|q|\le2$, $\partial_1f=qx_2-2\le0$ when $x_1=1$, so $x_1$ stays at upper bound 1 and $x_2$ is $\max(0,-(q+1)/2)$, the solution of $\partial_2f=q+2x_2+1=0$ projected onto $[0,1]$.
For $|q|>2$, $\mathbf{Q}$ is indefinite and the solver rejects the problem without solving.
Input is off-diagonal entry $q$; the table shows termination reason (error classification on rejection), solution, analytical-solution difference, and iterations.

```rust

//| ref: optimize-qp-projected-bb
//| title: "Solve a bound-only QP with projected BB and active sets and reject nonconvex Q"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   coupling: {"type":"range","label":"Q off-diagonal entry q [-]","description":"Q becomes indefinite for |q| > 2.","min":-3,"max":3,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"interior-second","label":"q = −1.5","description":"x₂ leaves its lower bound and the solution is (1, 0.25). Both methods match the analytical solution.","inputs":{"coupling":-1.5}}
//|   - {"id":"singular","label":"q = 2 (positive semidefinite)","description":"Q with minimum eigenvalue 0 is also accepted, giving solution (1, 0).","inputs":{"coupling":2}}
//|   - {"id":"indefinite","label":"q = 2.5 (indefinite)","description":"The solver rejects Q with minimum eigenvalue −0.5 as InvalidInput; the table gives the rejection classification.","inputs":{"coupling":2.5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: interior-second
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.25,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.25,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: singular
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: indefinite
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"InvalidInput"}

//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":null}
use scioxide_core::Bounds;
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{QpMethod, QpProblem, QpSolver};
let general = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
// Objective ½ xᵀ [[2, q], [q, 2]] x + (−4, 1)ᵀ x, box 0 ≤ x ≤ 1. Q eigenvalues are 2 ± q.
let q = coupling;
let h = Matrix::from_vec(2, 2, vec![2.0_f64, q, q, 2.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let bounds = Bounds::new(vec![(Some(0.0), Some(1.0)); 2]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let p = QpProblem::new(h, vec![-4.0, 1.0])
    .and_then(|p| p.with_bounds(bounds))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent analytical solution (|q| ≤ 2): x₁ at upper bound 1; x₂ solves gradient q + 2x₂ + 1 = 0, projected onto [0, 1].
let analytic = [1.0, (-(q + 1.0) / 2.0).clamp(0.0, 1.0)];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, method) in [
    ("Automatic (projected BB)", QpMethod::Automatic),
    ("ActiveSet", QpMethod::ActiveSet),
] {
    match QpSolver::default().with_method(method).solve(&p) {
        Ok(r) => {
            let x = r.x();
            rows.push((
                name,
                r.termination().to_string(),
                Some(x[0]),
                Some(x[1]),
                Some((x[0] - analytic[0]).abs().max((x[1] - analytic[1]).abs())),
                Some(r.iterations()),
            ));
        }
        // Reject nonconvex Q without solving. Keep the classification in the table.
        Err(e) => rows.push((name, format!("{:?}", e.kind()), None, None, None, None)),
    }
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Termination reason or rejection classification" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "x₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "x₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Analytical-solution difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })
);
```

At default $q=1$, the solution is $(1,0)$; both methods give `Converged` and match the analytical solution without rounding error.
At $q=-1.5$, $x_2$ leaves its lower bound and the solution is $(1,0.25)$.
At $q=2$, $\mathbf{Q}$ is positive semidefinite with minimum eigenvalue 0, but both methods accept it and return $(1,0)$.
At $|q|\ge2.5$, the minimum eigenvalue is negative; both methods reject with `InvalidInput` and return neither a point nor iterations.
In this small example, projected BB stops in 1–2 iterations and active sets in 3–5.
Per-iteration work and iteration counting differ by method, so counts alone do not compare speed.

## Summary

For bound-only convex QP, componentwise projection lets projected gradients advance using only $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}$ products without matrix factorization.
Optimality is determined by a zero projected gradient, equivalent to the bound KKT conditions.
BB uses a Rayleigh quotient of $\mathbf{Q}$ for step size; the `Automatic` implementation halves steps in backtracking to avoid objective increases and returns `Converged` only after checking original-problem KKT certificates.
Specify `QpMethod::ActiveSet` to place boundary components exactly at bounds or when ill-conditioned $\mathbf{Q}$ increases iteration counts.
Both methods reject non-positive-semidefinite $\mathbf{Q}$ with `InvalidInput`.
