CMA-ES (covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy; Hansen and Ostermeier, 2001) draws candidates from a multivariate normal distribution and updates its mean, covariance, and overall scale from good candidates' displacements.
Distribution updates use only objective ranks, so strictly increasing transformations of the objective leave them unchanged (stopping can change because it uses objective spread).
For continuous black-box functions with strong variable correlations, the search distribution learns elongated, rotated valley directions.
This page derives one-generation CMA-ES updates, explains SciOxide's `CmaEs` settings, stopping, and restarts, and examines covariance learning of conditioning in a rotated elliptical valley.
`CmaEs` does not claim global optimality; none of its termination reasons is successful.

## CMA-ES covariance updates

SciOxide works in coordinates normalizing the box to unit box $[0, 1]^d$.
The search distribution is normal $N(m, \sigma^2 C)$ with mean $m$, overall scale (step size) $\sigma$, and covariance $C$.
Each generation constructs $\lambda$ offspring

$$
x_i = m + \sigma y_i, \qquad y_i \sim N(0, C), \qquad i = 1, \dots, \lambda
$$

Using the eigendecomposition of $C = B D^2 B^T$, draw standard normal $z_i$ and form $y_i = B D z_i$.
Reflect coordinates leaving the unit box at its boundaries and recompute $y_i$ from the reflected points.

Rank offspring by objective, writing the $i$th-best displacement as $y_{i:\lambda}$.
Give the top $\mu = \lfloor \lambda/2 \rfloor$ positive weights $w_i$ proportional to $\ln(\mu + \frac12) - \ln i$ and summing to 1, setting

$$
\langle y \rangle_w = \sum_{i=1}^{\mu} w_i y_{i:\lambda}, \qquad \mu_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{1}{\sum_i w_i^2}
$$

$\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the effective selection count, equal to $\mu$ for equal weights.
The updates are

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &\leftarrow m + \sigma \langle y \rangle_w, \\
p_\sigma &\leftarrow (1 - c_\sigma) p_\sigma + \sqrt{c_\sigma (2 - c_\sigma) \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}\, C^{-1/2} \langle y \rangle_w, \\
p_c &\leftarrow (1 - c_c) p_c + h_\sigma \sqrt{c_c (2 - c_c) \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}\, \langle y \rangle_w, \\
C &\leftarrow (1 - c_1 - c_\mu) C + c_1 p_c p_c^T + c_\mu \textstyle\sum_i w_i y_{i:\lambda} y_{i:\lambda}^T, \\
\sigma &\leftarrow \sigma \exp\!\Bigl(\frac{c_\sigma}{d_\sigma}\Bigl(\frac{\|p_\sigma\|}{\mathrm{E}\|N(0, I)\|} - 1\Bigr)\Bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

$p_\sigma$ and $p_c$ are evolution paths, exponential moving averages of mean movement.
The second term updating $C$ is a rank-one update from $p_c$, and the third is a rank-$\mu$ update from selected displacements.
$h_\sigma$ is 1 when $\|p_\sigma\|/\sqrt{1 - (1 - c_\sigma)^{2g}}$ ($g$ is generation count) is below $(1.4 + 2/(d + 1))\,\mathrm{E}\|N(0, I)\|$ and 0 otherwise; when 0, add $c_1 c_c (2 - c_c)$ to the coefficient of $C$.
Clip the updated mean into the unit box.

Coefficients use the standard values in Hansen's tutorial (2016), determined from dimension $d$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as

$$
\begin{gathered}
c_\sigma = \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} + 2}{d + \mu_{\mathrm{eff}} + 5}, \qquad
d_\sigma = 1 + 2\max\Bigl(0, \sqrt{\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1}{d + 1}} - 1\Bigr) + c_\sigma, \qquad
c_c = \frac{4 + \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}/d}{d + 4 + 2\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}/d}, \\
c_1 = \frac{2}{(d + 1.3)^2 + \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}, \qquad
c_\mu = \min\Bigl(1 - c_1, \frac{2(\mu_{\mathrm{eff}} - 2 + 1/\mu_{\mathrm{eff}})}{(d + 2)^2 + \mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}\Bigr)
\end{gathered}
$$

$\mathrm{E}\|N(0, I)\| \approx \sqrt{d}\,(1 - \frac{1}{4d} + \frac{1}{21 d^2})$ is the expected length of an $d$-dimensional standard normal vector.

The step-size update is called cumulative step-size adaptation.
Evolution path $p_\sigma$ whitened by $C^{-1/2}$ has the length distribution of a standard normal vector under random selection.
A longer path means persistent movement in one direction, so increase $\sigma$; a shorter path indicates back-and-forth movement, so decrease it.
For convex quadratic $f(x) = \frac12 (x - x^\star)^T H (x - x^\star)$, $C$ is known to approach a shape proportional to the inverse Hessian $H^{-1}$.
This results from $C$ learning the distribution of good displacements, rather than directly estimating the objective Hessian.

Each generation costs $\lambda$ objective evaluations, $O(d^3)$ for dense eigendecomposition of $C$, and $O(\lambda d^2)$ for sampling; covariance storage uses $O(d^2)$ memory.

## Stopping and restarts

One run ends for any of the following reasons.
The first two are checked after each generation update, the third at eigendecomposition of $C$ at the next generation's start.

- Generation objective spread $f_{\max} - f_{\min}$ is at most `ResidualTolerance` tolerance (default absolute and relative $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, scale $\max(|f_{\min}|, |f_{\max}|)$).
- Effective step size $\sigma \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(C)}$ is at most `StepTolerance` tolerance (scale 1 in normalized-box units; default absolute and relative $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$).
- Numerical rank of $C$ falls below $d$ or $\sigma$ becomes 0.

With `CmaEsRestartPolicy::IncreasingPopulation { max_restarts }`, each completed run doubles offspring count and restarts from a design point up to `max_restarts` times (IPOP-CMA-ES; Auger and Hansen, 2005).
The first run starts at `x0`; restart $k$ begins at design point $k - 1$.
The default design is uniform-random `UniformStartDesign`, replaceable with `CmaEs::with_design`.
Each run evaluates its starting point once before generations begin.

Completion of the final run gives `PopulationStagnation`; reaching the generation limit across all runs gives `SearchBudgetCompleted`; neither is successful.
Evaluation limits, cancellation, or deadlines after the first evaluation also return the best point so far.
Supplying a local-solver factory with `CmaEs::with_local` locally optimizes the final best point, replacing it only if its value decreases.
Failure of local optimization does not fail the search; read its error through `CmaEsWorkspace::local_error()`.

## CmaEs settings and diagnostics

`CmaEsOptions::default()` uses initial step size 0.3 (normalized-box units), offspring count $4 + \lfloor 3 \ln d \rfloor$, a 1000-generation limit across restarts, no restarts, seed 42, no local-optimization limit, and sequential execution.
Change these with `with_initial_step`, `with_population_size`, `with_max_generations`, `with_fitness_tolerance`, `with_step_tolerance`, `with_restart`, `with_seed`, `with_local_budgets`, `with_execution`.
`CmaEs::new(options)` returns `InvalidInput` for NaN, infinite, or nonpositive initial step size or fewer than 4 offspring.
Nonsequential policies evaluate offspring in parallel and require the problem to be created with `BoundedObjective::new_sync`.
Offspring are created and ranked in index order, so deterministic objectives give results independent of worker count.

`workspace(dimension)` prepares `CmaEsWorkspace` for the largest restart population; query requirements with `CmaEsWorkspaceRequirements`.
Solve with `minimize_into(problem, x0, output, workspace, scope)` and `minimize(problem, x0)`.
A wrong `x0` length gives `DimensionMismatch`; NaN/infinite entries or objective values give `NonFinite`; out-of-box input gives `InvalidInput`; sample or step-size overflow gives `NumericalInstability`.

`CmaEsWorkspace::generations()` returns `CmaEsGeneration` for each completed generation.
Generation reports hold generation index `generation()` (across restarts), run index `restart()`, best/worst generation objectives, the numerical rank `covariance_rank()` and log positive-eigenvalue ratio $\ln(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min})$ `covariance_log_condition()` of $C$ at the start of the generation, `step_size()` of updated $\sigma$, and reflected-coordinate count `repaired_coordinates()`.
`restarts()` returns restart count.
Population contraction shows search progress but does not prove attainment of the global minimizer.

## Covariance learning in an elongated valley

Examine CMA-ES learning valley orientation and elongation on a rotated elliptical quadratic.
The objective is $f(x) = u^2 + \kappa v^2$, with $(u, v)$ obtained by rotating $x - x^\star$ by angle $\theta$, $x^\star = (0.2, -0.1)$, and box $[-1, 1]^2$.
The Hessian condition number is $\kappa$, so if $C$ learns the shape of $H^{-1}$, its log eigenvalue ratio $\ln(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min})$ approaches $\ln \kappa$.
Normalized coordinates scale uniformly by box width 2, leaving the eigenvalue ratio unchanged.
Inputs are $\log_{10} \kappa$, rotation angle $\theta$, and random seed.
Outputs are linked charts of generation-best objectives and log eigenvalue ratios of $C$, a table of final-point error, evaluations, and final-generation log eigenvalue ratio, and an outcome.
Show $\ln \kappa$ as a reference line.

```rust
//| id: optimize-cma-es-ellipsoid
//| ref: optimize-cma-es-ellipsoid
//| title: "Observe CMA-ES covariance learning the condition number of an elliptical valley"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   condition_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Condition number log₁₀κ [-]","min":0,"max":4,"step":0.5,"value":3}
//|   angle: {"type":"range","label":"Rotation angle θ [degrees]","min":0,"max":90,"step":15,"value":30}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":42}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"isotropic","label":"κ = 1 (isotropic)","description":"The valley is circular and stops after 48 generations and 289 evaluations. C's log eigenvalue ratio is 1.05; its difference from ln κ = 0 is fluctuation from updating C with 3 selected displacements per generation.","inputs":{"condition_exponent":0}}
//|   - {"id":"steep-valley","label":"κ = 10⁴","description":"Stops after 93 generations and 559 evaluations; C's log eigenvalue ratio 8.72 approaches ln κ = 9.21.","inputs":{"condition_exponent":4}}
//|   - {"id":"seed-100","label":"Seed 100","description":"At the same κ = 10³, stops after 81 generations and 487 evaluations, with final log ratio 7.40 above ln κ = 6.91.","inputs":{"seed":100}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"PopulationStagnation"}
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":50,"max":85}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":2e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":5.5,"max":7.6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":6.907755,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: isotropic
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":2e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0,"max":2}}
//|   - case: steep-valley
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":2e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":7.5,"max":10}}
//|   - case: seed-100
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":2e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cma","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":5.5,"max":8}}
use scioxide_core::{Bounds, ExecutionContext, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{BoundedObjective, CmaEs, CmaEsOptions, ObjectiveFn, OptimizeResult};

let kappa = RealScalar::powf(10.0, condition_exponent);
let theta = angle * std::f64::consts::PI / 180.0;
let (cos, sin) = (RealScalar::cos(theta), RealScalar::sin(theta));
let center = [0.2, -0.1];
// f = u² + κ v²; (u, v) are x − x* rotated by θ.
let ellipsoid = ObjectiveFn::new(2, |x: &[f64]| {
    let (dx, dy) = (x[0] - center[0], x[1] - center[1]);
    let u = cos * dx + sin * dy;
    let v = -sin * dx + cos * dy;
    Ok(u * u + kappa * v * v)
});
let bounds = Bounds::new(vec![(Some(-1.0), Some(1.0)); 2]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = BoundedObjective::new(&ellipsoid, &bounds).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solver = CmaEs::new(CmaEsOptions::default().with_seed(seed.round() as u64))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = solver.workspace(2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut output = OptimizeResult::empty();
solver
    .minimize_into(
        &problem,
        &[-0.8, 0.8],
        &mut output,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let generations = workspace.generations();
// Omit generations with best values ≤ 0 from logarithmic axes and show their count in the table.
let best: Vec<[f64; 2]> = generations
    .iter()
    .filter(|g| g.best_fitness() > 0.0)
    .map(|g| [g.generation() as f64, g.best_fitness()])
    .collect();
let condition: Vec<[f64; 2]> = generations
    .iter()
    .map(|g| [g.generation() as f64, g.covariance_log_condition()])
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Generation best value", "points": best })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Generation [-]",
        "yLabel": "Objective [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "link": { "group": "cma-generation" }
    })
);
let log_kappa = RealScalar::ln(kappa);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "ln(λmax/λmin)", "points": condition })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Generation [-]",
        "yLabel": "Log eigenvalue ratio of C [-]",
        "link": { "group": "cma-generation" },
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": log_kappa, "label": "ln κ" }]
    })
);
// Independent check: distance from final point to known minimizer x*.
let x = output.x();
let error = RealScalar::hypot(x[0] - center[0], x[1] - center[1]);
let last = generations.last().map(|g| g.covariance_log_condition());
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let na = "None";
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Termination reason" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Generation [-]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Final objective [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Distance to x* [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Log eigenvalue ratio [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2, "nullText": na },
        { "key": "6", "label": "ln κ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
        { "key": "7", "label": "Generations omitted from log axis [count]", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    vec![(
        output.termination().to_string(),
        generations.len(),
        output.fun_evals(),
        output.fun(),
        error,
        last,
        log_kappa,
        generations.len() - best.len()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "cma" })
);
emit_outcome!(
    Outcome::partial("Population objective spread fell below tolerance. Does not prove attainment of a minimizer")
        .status(output.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Final objective", output.fun(), None)
        .diagnostic("Distance to x*", error, None)
        .diagnostic("Generations", generations.len() as f64, None)
);
```

At default $\kappa = 10^3$ and $\theta = 30°$, offspring count is $\lambda = 4 + \lfloor 3 \ln 2 \rfloor = 6$ and `PopulationStagnation` occurs after 65 generations and 391 evaluations.
Evaluations comprise 1 starting-point evaluation and 390 for 6 offspring in each of 65 generations.
Final objective is $9.9 \times 10^{-10}$ and distance to $x^\star$ is $1.2 \times 10^{-6}$.
The log eigenvalue ratio rises from 0 in the first generation to 6.52 at the end, close to $\ln \kappa = 6.91$.
In the objective chart, values fall from 550 to about 0.1 in roughly the first 13 generations, entering the valley.
Until around generation 25, the objective barely falls while the log eigenvalue ratio of $C$ grows from about 1 to 4; afterward it falls with an approximately constant slope and fluctuations.

$\kappa = 1$ stops after 48 generations and $\kappa = 10^4$ after 93; a ten-thousandfold condition-number increase only roughly doubles generations.
At $\kappa = 10^4$ the final log ratio is 8.72, approaching $\ln \kappa = 9.21$.
Rotation angles 0° and 90° both take 79 generations, with log ratios 6.71 and 6.13; $C$ learns the unaligned 30° valley direction comparably well.
Each generation updates $C$ incrementally using only 3 selected displacements and evolution paths, so the log ratio fluctuates randomly.
It is 1.05 even at $\kappa = 1$, and seed 100 gives 7.40, above $\ln \kappa$.
Termination means population objective spread fell below tolerance, not proof of reaching a minimizer.
Closeness is checked by distance to known $x^\star$.

## Summary

CMA-ES draws normal offspring, moves the mean to their top weighted mean, updates covariance through evolution paths and selected displacements, and updates overall scale through cumulative step-size adaptation.
On convex quadratics, covariance learns the inverse-Hessian shape, so generations grow much less than conditioning even in rotated elongated valleys.
A run ends by objective spread, effective step size, or covariance rank; `IncreasingPopulation` doubles offspring count and restarts.
Neither `PopulationStagnation` nor `SearchBudgetCompleted` is successful; check final points through known properties, local optimization with `with_local`, and other seeds.
Read per-generation rank, eigenvalue ratio, and step size of $C$ through `CmaEsWorkspace::generations()`.
