Even when only objective and constraint values are available, nearby samples can form a local model.
COBYLA uses linear interpolation and COBYQA quadratic interpolation, limiting model use to a trust region.
Both call only function values and never evaluate original derivatives.
They target expensive problems with up to a few dozen variables, such as objectives from simulations or low-noise experiments.
Constrained-problem notation and KKT conditions follow [Nonlinear constraints and KKT conditions](../nonlinear-constraints/).

## Interpolation models and geometry

Near point $\mathbf{x}$, COBYLA forms linear objective model $m_f(\mathbf{s})=a_f+\mathbf{b}_f^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{s}$ and linear models for constraint rows.
Variables with equal bounds are fixed and removed from the model, so $n$ below counts unfixed variables.
An $n$-dimensional linear model needs $n+1$ points not lying in one hyperplane.
From center $\mathbf{y}_0$ and remaining points $\mathbf{y}_1,\dots,\mathbf{y}_n$, coefficients $\mathbf{b}_f$ are determined by solving

$$
\begin{bmatrix}(\mathbf{y}_1-\mathbf{y}_0)^\mathsf{T}\\ \vdots\\ (\mathbf{y}_n-\mathbf{y}_0)^\mathsf{T}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{b}_f
=\begin{bmatrix}f(\mathbf{y}_1)-f(\mathbf{y}_0)\\ \vdots\\ f(\mathbf{y}_n)-f(\mathbf{y}_0)\end{bmatrix}
$$

and each constraint uses the same matrix.
The matrix contains simplex edges formed by the $n+1$ points; nearly coplanar points make it nearly singular, allowing small evaluation errors to produce large gradient estimates.
Simplex geometry repair controls this interpolation ill-conditioning.

Before model construction, `Cobyla` checks that each edge exceeds $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$ times the radius (absolute rank threshold) and that its component orthogonal to previous edges exceeds 0.05 times its length.
If these fail, rebuild interpolation points one radius from the center along coordinate axes.
During iteration, inserting a point replaces a selected point far from the center with a large effect on simplex volume.
When model improvement stalls, try a geometry step replacing points farther than $2.1\Delta$ from the center and similar points by trust-region points maximizing the absolute interpolation Lagrange basis polynomial (volume-change ratio).
For smooth functions, linear-model gradient error is proportional to radius $\Delta$ ($O(\Delta)$).

COBYQA uses quadratic model $m_f(\mathbf{s})=a_f+\mathbf{b}_f^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{s}+\tfrac12\mathbf{s}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}_f\mathbf{s}$.
A quadratic has $(n+1)(n+2)/2$ coefficients, but `Cobyqa` retains $2n+1$ interpolation points, insufficient to determine all coefficients when $n\ge2$.
Among interpolating quadratics, choose the smallest Hessian change $\|\mathbf{H}_f-\mathbf{H}_f^{\mathrm{old}}\|_F$ (Frobenius norm) from the preceding Hessian.
Measure this change in coordinates scaled per axis by the initial interpolation-point spread.
Construct the first model from $\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{old}}=\mathbf{0}$; initial interpolation points are $2n$ points one radius from the center in positive and negative coordinate directions.
Thus the first model determines only diagonal curvature; cross terms unresolved by point geometry become 0.
Solve this interpolation for the objective and each constraint, accumulating curvature information gradually across iterations.
A model with finitely many interpolation points need not match the original Hessian.

## Trust-region subproblems and merit functions

Direct minimization of constrained models can be unbounded for linear models or move beyond reliable model regions for quadratic models.
Use the model only in a ball of radius $\Delta$ and assess trial points with merit function

$$
\phi_\sigma(\mathbf{x})=\frac{f(\mathbf{x})}{f_s}+\sigma\max\Big(0,\ \max_i v_i(\mathbf{x})\Big)
$$

combining objective and constraint violations.
$v_i$ is $|c_i|$ for equalities or $g_j$ for inequalities divided by per-row scales from the first model's gradients; $f_s$ is likewise the scale for the objective.
Penalty coefficient $\sigma$ starts at 1.
For model-predicted violation decrease with objective increase, raise $\sigma$ so predicted merit decrease is at least $\sigma/2$ times the violation decrease.
$\sigma$ never decreases, so an increased penalty remains until the iterations end.

COBYLA's subproblem is linear programming within ball $\|\mathbf{d}\|_2\le\Delta$ and the variable box.
The first stage minimizes maximum linearized violation; the second minimizes the linearized objective without increasing that violation.
Even from an infeasible start, this first stage reduces violation first, so no separate restoration stage is used.

COBYQA uses a two-stage Byrd–Omojokun step.
Normal step $\mathbf{n}$ reduces squared linearized violations within radius $0.8\Delta$.
Tangential step $\mathbf{t}$ holds linearized violations at the $\mathbf{n}$ values while minimizing the model Lagrangian's quadratic model in the remaining trust region.
The normal-step radius is smaller than $\Delta$ to leave room for tangential objective decrease.
Also solve COBYLA's linear-programming step at radius $\rho$ (next section's resolution); use it if its predicted decrease (after repeated halving if needed) exceeds this step's by a factor of 10.

## Radius and resolution updates

After evaluating a trial, update the radius using ratio $r$ of actual merit decrease to model-predicted decrease.
With step length $\|\mathbf{d}\|$ and resolution $\rho$, the new radius is as follows, never below $\rho$.

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{new}} =
\begin{cases}
\min\big(\Delta/2,\ \max(\|\mathbf{d}\|,\ \rho/2)\big) & (r\le0.1) \\
\max(\Delta/2,\ \|\mathbf{d}\|) & (0.1<r\le0.7) \\
\max(\Delta/2,\ 2\|\mathbf{d}\|) & (r>0.7)
\end{cases}
$$

Resolution $\rho$ is a radius lower bound starting at initial radius $\rho_{\mathrm{beg}}$.
When model improvement stalls and radius is at most $1.5\rho$, reduce resolution to $\rho/10$ if $\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{end}}>250$, $\sqrt{\rho\,\rho_{\mathrm{end}}}$ if $>16$, or final radius $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ otherwise, and set radius to $\rho$.
This rule has the same form as Powell's COBYLA, refining neighborhoods while rebuilding models until resolution reaches the final radius.

Each COBYLA iteration costs roughly $O(n^3)$ for LU of the $n\times n$ interpolation matrix and linear programming.
COBYQA stores an $n\times n$ Hessian for the objective and each of $m$ constraints, using $O((m+1)n^2)$ memory, and uses SVD for interpolation updates.
Both target problems dominated by evaluation cost.
With cheap evaluations and available derivatives, [SQP](../sqp-and-interior-point/) or [nonlinear interior point](../nonlinear-interior-point/) achieves high accuracy with fewer evaluations.

## Feasibility and termination

Both receive equalities, inequalities, and boxes from `ConstrainedProblem`.
Starts and trials are projected onto the box, so all evaluations are in-box, although intermediate trials may violate nonlinear constraints.
Nonlinear feasibility is assessed at the accepted center.
Trials with nonfinite objectives or constraints are rejected.
Nonfinite values at the initial point or coordinate-axis points creating or rebuilding interpolation give error `NonFinite`.

With noisy values, excessively small radii make noise larger than model variation.
For noise standard deviation $\epsilon$, gradient error from difference $f(\mathbf{y}_k)-f(\mathbf{y}_0)$ is roughly $\epsilon/\Delta$, so do not set $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ below a noise-determined scale.

`Converged` occurs only if all conditions below hold.

- Resolution has reached final radius and radius is at most 1.5 times resolution.
- The center's normalized violation is within the feasibility threshold at reference scale $\max(1,\|\mathbf{x}\|_\infty)$.
- Interpolation-point geometry passes the rank check.
- Model-predicted decrease divided by radius is small to the order of resolution.
  For COBYLA, a linear-model step shorter than half the resolution also suffices.

More than $2n+8$ unimproving trials at final resolution, or reaching final radius without constructing an interpolation model, gives `Stagnation`.
Reaching the iteration limit gives `IterationLimit`, and the objective-evaluation limit `EvaluationLimit`; none is successful.

Configure radii and iteration limits with `CobylaOptions` and `CobyqaOptions` and prepare `CobylaWorkspace` and `CobyqaWorkspace`.
Specify radii in coordinate units with `with_radii(initial, final)`; defaults are initial 1 and final $10^{-4}$.
`with_radii` is an unchecked `const` builder; `Cobyla::new` and `Cobyqa::new` check $0<\text{final}\le\text{initial}$ and finiteness, returning `InvalidInput` or `NonFinite` on violations.
Other settings include normalized-violation tolerance (`with_feasibility_tolerance`), interpolation-rank threshold (`with_rank_threshold`), iteration limit (`with_max_iterations`, default 1000), objective-evaluation limit including interpolation and repair points (`with_max_evaluations`, default 1000), and time limits.
Check capacity with `CobylaWorkspaceRequirements` and `CobyqaWorkspaceRequirements`; `free_variables()` returns unfixed-variable count.

Read geometry and model information from `OptimizeDiagnostics::Cobyla` and `OptimizeDiagnostics::Cobyqa` returned by `OptimizeResult::method_diagnostics()` through `CobylaDiagnostics` and `CobyqaDiagnostics` accessors.

| Accessor | Content |
|---|---|
| `radius()` | Final radius $\Delta$ (coordinate units) |
| `resolution()` | Final resolution $\rho$ (coordinate units) |
| `penalty()` | Merit penalty coefficient $\sigma$ |
| `model_error()` | Maximum model/actual difference at the final trial (normalized units); `None` if no trial |
| `model_stationarity()` | Predicted merit decrease from the linear-programming step divided by its radius ($\Delta$ for COBYLA, $\rho$ for COBYQA) |
| `geometry_repairs()` | Geometry repair count (initial interpolation-point construction, complete rebuilds, and single-point geometry replacements) |
| `poised()` | Whether interpolation geometry passed rank checks |

Without original derivatives, `OptimizeResult::stationarity()` returns `None` and does not treat model gradients as certificates of original KKT residuals.
`primal_feasibility()` returns constraint violations in original units.
At termination, report violation, radius, model diagnostics, and reason together.

## Projection onto a disk

Minimize squared distance from $(2,0)$ over the unit disk.
The analytical solution is $(1,0)$, with constraint $x^2+y^2\le1$ active there.
Supply neither objective nor constraint derivatives and check against the geometric analytical solution.

The following cell solves with `Cobyla` and `Cobyqa` from the same initial point $(0, h)$ and initial radius 1.
Inputs are starting height $h$ and final radius $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}=10^{q}$; outputs give each method's optimum, original-expression disk violation, analytical-solution difference, objective evaluations, and model diagnostics.

```rust
//| id: optimize-cobyla-feasibility
//| ref: optimize-cobyla-feasibility
//| title: "Find a disk-constrained optimum using function values only"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   height: {"type":"range","label":"Starting height h [-]","min":-1,"max":1,"step":0.25,"value":0.5}
//|   final_radius_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Final-radius exponent q (ρ_end = 10^q) [-]","min":-8,"max":-2,"step":1,"value":-4}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fine-radius","label":"ρ_end = 10⁻⁸","description":"COBYLA's analytical-solution difference falls to 6.4×10⁻⁹, but objective evaluations rise from 35 to 62. COBYQA remains at about 10⁻¹³.","inputs":{"final_radius_exponent":-8}}
//|   - {"id":"coarse-radius","label":"ρ_end = 10⁻²","description":"COBYLA stops after 24 evaluations with analytical-solution difference only 9.2×10⁻⁴.","inputs":{"final_radius_exponent":-2}}
//|   - {"id":"top-start","label":"Starting height 1","description":"COBYLA's penalty rises to 1.67, with original-expression violation 6.0×10⁻⁹ remaining.","inputs":{"height":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":1e-6,"max":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":25,"max":50}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/6","range":{"min":12,"max":30}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/8","approx":{"value":1e-4,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: fine-radius
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|   - case: coarse-radius
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":1e-4,"max":1e-2}}
//|   - case: top-start
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/9","range":{"min":1,"max":3}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    Cobyla, CobylaOptions, Cobyqa, CobyqaOptions, ConstrainedProblem, ConstraintFn,
    Constraints, ObjectiveFn, OptimizeDiagnostics,
};
// Objective (x − 2)² + y² and inequality x² + y² − 1 ≤ 0. No supplied derivatives.
let f = ObjectiveFn::new(2, |x: &[f64]| Ok((x[0] - 2.0) * (x[0] - 2.0) + x[1] * x[1]));
let c = ConstraintFn::new(2, |x: &[f64]| Ok(x[0] * x[0] + x[1] * x[1] - 1.0));
let constraints = Constraints::new(2)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .inequality(c)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = ConstrainedProblem::new(&f, &constraints).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// The builder does not check the radius pair; new checks 0 < final ≤ initial.
let final_radius = RealScalar::powf(10.0, final_radius_exponent);
let cobyla = Cobyla::new(CobylaOptions::default().with_radii(1.0, final_radius))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let cobyqa = Cobyqa::new(CobyqaOptions::default().with_radii(1.0, final_radius))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let start = [0.0, height];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut all_converged = true;
for (name, result) in [
    ("COBYLA", cobyla.minimize(&problem, &start)),
    ("COBYQA", cobyqa.minimize(&problem, &start)),
] {
    let r = result.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (radius, resolution, penalty, repairs) = match r.method_diagnostics() {
        OptimizeDiagnostics::Cobyla(d) => (
            d.radius(),
            d.resolution(),
            d.penalty(),
            d.geometry_repairs(),
        ),
        OptimizeDiagnostics::Cobyqa(d) => (
            d.radius(),
            d.resolution(),
            d.penalty(),
            d.geometry_repairs(),
        ),
        _ => return Err("Missing interpolation-method diagnostics".to_string()),
    };
    all_converged &= r.is_success();
    let x = r.x();
    // Independent check: distance to analytical disk projection (1, 0) and original-expression violation.
    rows.push((
        name,
        r.termination().to_string(),
        x[0],
        x[1],
        (x[0] * x[0] + x[1] * x[1] - 1.0).max(0.0),
        RealScalar::sqrt((x[0] - 1.0) * (x[0] - 1.0) + x[1] * x[1]),
        r.fun_evals(),
        radius,
        resolution,
        penalty,
        repairs,
    ));
}
let status = format!("{} / {}", rows[0].1, rows[1].1);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed8 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 8 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Termination reason" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "x [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed8 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "y [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Constraint violation [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Analytical-solution difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
        { "key": "6", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "7", "label": "Final radius [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
        { "key": "8", "label": "Resolution ρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
        { "key": "9", "label": "Penalty coefficient σ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
        { "key": "10", "label": "Geometry repairs [count]", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })
);
let outcome = if all_converged {
    Outcome::satisfied("Both methods met resolution and model criteria")
        .explanation("Without original-derivative KKT residuals, check accuracy by analytical-solution differences.")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("One method stopped before meeting the criteria")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(status)
        .diagnostic("Final radius", final_radius, None)
);
```

At default $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}=10^{-4}$, both methods terminate with `Converged`.
COBYLA reaches analytical-solution difference $2.0\times10^{-5}$ in 35 evaluations and COBYQA $8.6\times10^{-14}$ in 19; both have zero original-expression violation.
The start lies inside the disk but the optimum lies on its boundary.
COBYLA's penalty increases from 1 to 1.48, indicating objective increase during violation-reducing steps.
COBYLA's difference remains about 0.2 times final radius because its linear model cannot represent constraint curvature or resolve locations finer than $\rho$.
At $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}=10^{-8}$, COBYLA's difference falls to $6.4\times10^{-9}$ with 62 evaluations; at $10^{-2}$ it takes 24 with difference $9.2\times10^{-4}$.
COBYQA's difference stays of order $10^{-13}$ independently of $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$.
This objective and constraint are quadratics without cross terms, so the initial 5 axis points and minimum-Frobenius interpolation reproduce the original functions.
This equality does not occur for nonquadratic problems; COBYQA accuracy then also depends on $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ and model error.
Analytical-solution distance and constraint violation differ, so check both.

## Summary

COBYLA uses $n+1$-point linear models and linear programming in a ball; COBYQA uses $2n+1$-point quadratics and Byrd–Omojokun decomposition.
Both form trust-region steps solely from values.
Attainable accuracy is roughly set by final radius $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ (default $10^{-4}$); linear-model COBYLA retains solution errors of order at most $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ that do not decrease until $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ is reduced.
Radius pairs are checked by `Cobyla::new` and `Cobyqa::new`, so follow `with_radii` with `?`.
Even `Converged` provides no original-derivative KKT residuals; report reason, original-unit violations, `resolution()`, and `model_stationarity()` together, checking against analytical solutions or another method where possible.
For noisy functions, do not set $\rho_{\mathrm{end}}$ below the noise-determined scale.
