Global-search methods differ in how finite observations determine the next evaluation location.
Both methods on this page use observation geometry.
DIRECT partitions the box into rectangles and selects subdivisions from rectangle sizes and center values.
SHGO triangulates samples by Delaunay, choosing points below neighboring samples as local-minimum candidates and refining them with local solvers.
DIRECT uses no randomness, and SHGO can choose deterministic starting-point designs, making both easy to reproduce.

Like `MultiStart`, both receive a box from `BoundedObjective` per call and require finite bounds for every coordinate.
Neither returns a global-optimality certificate or treats computation-limit termination as success.
General limitations are in [Global-search limits and basin-hit probabilities](../global-search/).

## DIRECT domain partitioning

DIRECT (dividing rectangles; Jones et al., 1993) normalizes the box to unit box $[0, 1]^d$ and evaluates rectangle centers.
If the objective has unknown Lipschitz constant $K$, a rectangle with center value $f_i$ and farthest-point distance $d_i$ satisfies $f \ge f_i - K d_i$.
Knowing $K$ would allow subdivision of the lowest-bound rectangle, but $K$ is unknown.
DIRECT subdivides every rectangle potentially having the lowest bound for some $K > 0$, examining both low-objective neighborhoods and still-coarse regions.

### Potentially optimal rectangles

Rectangle $j$ is potentially optimal if, for some $K > 0$,

$$
f_j - K d_j \le f_i - K d_i \quad (\text{for all } i), \qquad
f_j - K d_j \le f_{\min} - \epsilon |f_{\min}|
$$

the conditions above hold.
$f_{\min}$ is the best value so far.
The first condition puts point $(d_j, f_j)$ on the lower-right part of the lower convex hull of all points in the $(d, f)$ plane.
Permitted $K$ values are at least the maximum slope $(f_j - f_i)/(d_j - d_i)$ from $d_i < d_j$ points and at most the minimum slope $(f_i - f_j)/(d_i - d_j)$ to $d_i > d_j$ points.
The second condition excludes small rectangles with no prospect of relative improvement of at least $\epsilon$ (default $10^{-4}$) over the best value.

The lowest-center-value rectangle among the largest is selected every sweep because it has no comparison point to its right.
Thus more evaluations eventually subdivide every rectangle and make samples dense in the box.
For continuous objectives, the best value approaches the global minimum, but finite truncation gives no bound on the remaining gap.

`DirectVariant::Original` defines size $d_i$ as center-to-vertex distance (Euclidean norm of normalized half-side lengths) and selects all rectangles sharing size and value.
`DirectVariant::LocallyBiased` (DIRECT-L; Gablonsky and Kelley, 2001) uses the longest half-side as size, selects only one per size, and uses $\epsilon = 0$.
Fewer size classes and subdivisions per sweep allocate more evaluations to local improvement.

### Rectangle trisection

Let $c$ be the selected center and $h$ its longest half-side length.
For each longest-side direction $e_k$, evaluate two points $c \pm \frac{2h}{3} e_k$ and set $w_k = \min\bigl(f(c + \frac{2h}{3} e_k), f(c - \frac{2h}{3} e_k)\bigr)$.
Trisect directions by increasing $w_k$, leaving offspring in the best-value direction as the largest rectangles.
Subdividing a rectangle with $m$ longest sides requires $2m$ evaluations.
Previously evaluated points reuse recorded values.
Rectangles unable to generate new points due to rounding count as stagnant and are not subdivided further.
Variables with equal bounds remain fixed and are not subdivided.

### DIRECT API and termination

`DirectOptions::new(max_evaluations, max_rectangles, max_storage_bytes)` infallibly accepts limits on objective evaluations, simultaneously held rectangles, and allocated workspace bytes.
Defaults are `Original`, sweep limit equal to evaluation limit, $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$, and sequential execution; change with `with_variant`, `with_max_sweeps`, `with_epsilon`.
`Direct::new` rejects negative or nonfinite $\epsilon$ and nonsequential policies with `InvalidInput`.
Each sweep depends on every preceding sample, so DIRECT runs only sequentially.
`Direct::workspace(dimension)` prepares workspace `DirectWorkspace`, returning `ResourceLimit` if required memory exceeds `max_storage_bytes`.
Query requirements through `DirectWorkspaceRequirements` returned by `workspace_requirements(dimension)`.
Passing empty `DirectWorkspace::new()` to `minimize_into` also prepares it on the first call.
`minimize(&problem)` internally prepares workspace and results and solves sequentially.
Memory is $O(rd)$ for $r$ rectangles.

Stop with `EvaluationLimit` if the next subdivision exceeds the evaluation limit, `MemoryLimit` if it exceeds the rectangle limit, `SearchBudgetCompleted` at the sweep limit, or `Stagnation` when no rectangle can be subdivided; none is successful.
Evaluation limits are respected by whole subdivisions, so actual counts can be slightly below the limit.
`DirectResult` returns best sample `best_sample()`, evaluations `evaluations()`, sweeps `sweeps()`, rectangles `rectangles()`, stagnant rectangles `stalled_rectangles()`, and maximum half-side length `max_live_half_side()`.
Maximum half-side length measures box coverage resolution, not an objective-error bound.

## Partitioning the Branin function

The Branin function

$$
f(x, y) = \Bigl(y - \frac{5.1}{4\pi^2} x^2 + \frac{5}{\pi} x - 6\Bigr)^2 + 10\Bigl(1 - \frac{1}{8\pi}\Bigr)\cos x + 10
$$

is a standard test problem with three global minimizers $(-\pi, 12.275)$, $(\pi, 2.275)$, and $(3\pi, 2.475)$ in box $x \in [-5, 10]$, $y \in [0, 15]$.
The minimum is $5/(4\pi) \approx 0.397887$, attained where the squared term vanishes and $\cos x = -1$.
This cell takes evaluation limit and selection method and reports best sample, known-minimum gap, nearest-minimizer distance, actual evaluations, sweep and rectangle counts, and maximum half-side length.
All three minimizers have the same objective, so measure distance to the nearest.

The best sample is $(9.42501, 2.47485)$ near $(3\pi, 2.475)$, with known-minimum gap $3.8 \times 10^{-7}$ and minimizer distance $2.8 \times 10^{-4}$.

Because three minimizers have equal values, which one is nearest the best sample changes as evaluations proceed.
All terminations are limit-driven; proximity to a global minimizer is checked by comparison with known values.

## SHGO sampling and local optimization

SHGO (simplicial homology global optimization; Endres et al., 2018) selects locally low points from a sampled simplicial complex and refines them with local solvers.
SciOxide sequentially evaluates the box's $2^d$ vertices and `samples` points from `StartDesign`.
These points are partitioned into simplices by [Delaunay triangulation](../../../scioxide-spatial/algorithms/delaunay-voronoi-and-hulls/), with simplex-edge connections defining adjacency.
Points lower than every neighbor become sampled local-minimum candidates.
Equal-valued neighbors form plateaus; select one candidate from a plateau if no point in it has a lower neighbor.
Order candidates by increasing value and refine with local solvers created by `LocalSolverFactory`.

Sufficiently dense samples enter each local minimum's basin, making the candidate list approach the local-minimum list.
However, low points in finite samples are not all minima, including those in unknown regions.
Work comprises $2^d$ plus sample-count evaluations, Delaunay triangulation, and a local solve per candidate.
Vertex count $2^d$ and the number of Delaunay simplices grow rapidly with dimension, so SHGO suits low dimensions.

`Shgo::new(options, design, factory)` is infallible.
`ShgoOptions::new(samples, geometry)` is also infallible; `geometry` is scioxide-spatial's `GeometryOptions`, limiting triangulation point count, dimension (default maximum 8), memory, and work.
Defaults locally optimize all candidates, with seed 42, no per-local-solve limit, and sequential execution.
`with_max_refinements` sets how many candidates to refine, `with_seed` design and local-solve seeds, `with_local_budgets` per-local-solve limits, and `with_execution` local-optimization execution policy.
Sampling and triangulation remain sequential; nonsequential policies require a problem built with `BoundedObjective::new_sync`.

Prepare `Shgo::workspace(dimension)` or empty `ShgoWorkspace::new()`.
If vertices plus samples exceed the geometry point limit or dimension exceeds its limit, `workspace_requirements(dimension)` returns `ResourceLimit`.
Success returns requirements `ShgoWorkspaceRequirements` exposing dimension `dimension()`, total vertices and samples `sites()`, and prepared local-solver count `refinements()`.
Solve with `minimize_into(problem, output, workspace, scope)` or `minimize(problem)`; out-of-box samples give `InvalidInput`, NaN/infinite objectives `NonFinite`.
Refining every candidate ends with `SearchBudgetCompleted`, which is unsuccessful.

`ShgoResult` returns sample points `points()` and values `values()`, candidate sample indices `candidates()`, refinement records `refinements()`, and simplex count `simplex_count()`.
`best_sample()` is the best sample; `best_observed()` the best among samples and local solutions; `best_refined()` the best local refinement whose solve succeeded.
`ShgoRefinement` holds candidate sample index `vertex()`, local solution `result()`, error `error()`, and evaluations `counts()`.

## Listing Himmelblau minima

Himmelblau $f(x, y) = (x^2 + y - 11)^2 + (x + y^2 - 7)^2$ has 4 local minima (all $f = 0$) in box $[-5, 5]^2$, the same function as [Multistart methods](oxref:optimize-multistart-designs).
This cell uses `Shgo`, varies Sobol sample count, and lists local candidates and refined solutions.
Inject a local-solver factory running L-BFGS-B with analytical gradients (contract in [Replacing local solvers](oxref:optimize-local-solver-injection)).
Outputs include a scatter plot of samples, candidates, and local solutions, per-refinement tables, a summary, and an outcome.
Distances to the 4 known minima provide independent checks.

```rust

//| ref: optimize-shgo-himmelblau
//| title: "Observe Himmelblau minima found by SHGO with varying samples"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize, scioxide-random, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   samples: {"type":"range","label":"Design samples [points]","description":"Sobol points added to the box's 4 vertices.","min":4,"max":128,"step":4,"value":16}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"few-samples","label":"4 samples","description":"Coarse samples give 2 candidates and find only 2 minima.","inputs":{"samples":4}}
//|   - {"id":"many-samples","label":"128 samples","description":"Gives 4 candidates and finds all 4 minima.","inputs":{"samples":128}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shgo","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"SearchBudgetCompleted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"shgo","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"shgo","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"refinements","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-4}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: few-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shgo","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":2}
//|   - case: many-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shgo","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":4}
use scioxide_core::{Bounds, Budget, EvaluationRole, SciOxideError, SolveContext};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    BoundedObjective, LbfgsB, LbfgsBWorkspace, LocalSolverFactory, ObjectiveFunction,
    OptimizeResult, SearchLocalSolver, Shgo, ShgoOptions,
};
use scioxide_random::QmcPlan;
use scioxide_spatial::GeometryOptions;

// Himmelblau and its analytical gradient.
struct Himmelblau;
impl ObjectiveFunction<f64> for Himmelblau {
    fn dim(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn eval(&self, x: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        let (a, b) = (x[0] * x[0] + x[1] - 11.0, x[0] + x[1] * x[1] - 7.0);
        Ok(a * a + b * b)
    }
    fn gradient_into(&self, x: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<bool, SciOxideError> {
        let (a, b) = (x[0] * x[0] + x[1] - 11.0, x[0] + x[1] * x[1] - 7.0);
        out[0] = 4.0 * x[0] * a + 2.0 * b;
        out[1] = 2.0 * a + 4.0 * x[1] * b;
        Ok(true)
    }
}
// Local-solver factory preparing L-BFGS-B and workspace per candidate.
#[derive(Debug)]
struct LbfgsBFactory;
struct LbfgsBLocal(LbfgsB<f64>, LbfgsBWorkspace<f64>);
impl LocalSolverFactory<f64> for LbfgsBFactory {
    type Solver = LbfgsBLocal;
    fn prepare(&self, dimension: usize) -> Result<LbfgsBLocal, SciOxideError> {
        let solver = LbfgsB::default();
        let work = solver.workspace(dimension)?;
        Ok(LbfgsBLocal(solver, work))
    }
}
impl SearchLocalSolver<f64> for LbfgsBLocal {
    fn minimize_into(
        &mut self,
        problem: &BoundedObjective<'_, f64>,
        start: &[f64],
        _: u64,
        output: &mut OptimizeResult<f64>,
        context: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        self.0
            .minimize_into(problem, start, output, &mut self.1, context)
    }
}

let bounds = Bounds::new(vec![(Some(-5.0), Some(5.0)); 2]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = BoundedObjective::new(&Himmelblau, &bounds).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let count = Budget::try_from(samples.round() as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = ShgoOptions::new(count, GeometryOptions::new());
let plan = QmcPlan::sobol(2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let result = Shgo::new(options, plan, LbfgsBFactory)
    .minimize(&problem)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Known minima for independent checks (all f = 0). Except (3, 2), values were obtained to double precision by Newton iteration.
let minima = [
    (3.0, 2.0),
    (-2.805118086952745, 3.131312518250573),
    (-3.779310253377747, -3.283185991286170),
    (3.584428340330492, -1.848126526964404),
];
let nearest = |x: &[f64]| {
    minima
        .iter()
        .map(|&(a, b)| (x[0] - a).hypot(x[1] - b))
        .fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min)
};
let pairs = |rows: &[f64]| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> { rows.chunks(2).map(|p| [p[0], p[1]]).collect() };
let sampled = pairs(result.points());
let chosen: Vec<[f64; 2]> = result.candidates().iter().map(|&i| sampled[i]).collect();
let refined: Vec<[f64; 2]> = result
    .refinements()
    .iter()
    .filter_map(|r| r.result())
    .map(|r| [r.x()[0], r.x()[1]])
    .collect();
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Samples", "points": sampled, "style": { "symbol": "circle" } }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Local-minimum candidates", "points": chosen, "style": { "symbol": "rect" } }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Locally refined solutions",
            "points": refined,
            "style": { "symbol": "diamond" }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "x [-]",
        "yLabel": "y [-]",
        "xMin": -5.0,
        "xMax": 5.0,
        "yMin": -5.0,
        "yMax": 5.0
    })
);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for refinement in result.refinements() {
    let vertex = refinement.vertex();
    let local = refinement.result();
    rows.push((
        vertex,
        result.values()[vertex],
        local.map(|r| r.x()[0]),
        local.map(|r| r.x()[1]),
        local.map(|r| r.fun()),
        local.map_or("Error".to_string(), |r| r.termination().to_string()),
        local.map(|r| nearest(r.x())),
    ));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Candidate sample index", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Sample value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Local solution x [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "None" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Local solution y [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "None" },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Local objective [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Local-solve termination" },
        { "key": "6", "label": "Distance to local minimum [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "refinements" })
);
let found = minima
    .iter()
    .filter(|&&(a, b)| refined.iter().any(|p| (p[0] - a).hypot(p[1] - b) <= 1e-4))
    .count();
let evaluations = result
    .counts()
    .total()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .provider(EvaluationRole::Objective);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Termination reason" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Samples [points]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Simplices [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Candidates [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Known minima found [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    vec![(
        result.termination().to_string(),
        result.values().len(),
        result.simplex_count(),
        result.candidates().len(),
        found,
        evaluations
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "shgo" })
);
emit_outcome!(
    Outcome::partial("All candidates locally refined. The list contains minima seen in the samples")
        .status(result.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Candidates", result.candidates().len() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Known minima found", found as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Objective evaluations", evaluations as f64, None)
```

All 3 candidates reach `GradientSmall` with L-BFGS-B and refine to minima $(3, 2)$, $(-2.8051, 3.1313)$, and $(-3.7793, -3.2832)$.
Near $(3.5844, -1.8481)$, no sample was lower than its neighbors.
Objective evaluations total 59: 20 samples and 39 local-solve evaluations.
With 128 samples there are 256 simplices and 4 candidates, finding all 4 minima.

## Summary

DIRECT uses no randomness and allocates global/local evaluations through potentially optimal rectangles, suiting reproducible low-dimensional search.
`Original` and `LocallyBiased` differ in how many rectangles they select; the latter favors local improvement at the same evaluation count.
SHGO is limited to low dimensions by $2^d$ vertices and Delaunay triangulation, but returns multiple local candidates and can form a list of minima through refinement.
For both, limit rectangle counts, samples, and geometry memory as well as evaluations.
No termination is successful, so check best samples and local solutions against known values, local-solver criteria, or other methods.
