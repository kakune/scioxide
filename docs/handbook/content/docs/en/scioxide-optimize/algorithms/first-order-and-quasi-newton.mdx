Methods minimizing multivariable $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ using gradient $\nabla f$ divide into steepest descent, nonlinear conjugate gradients, and quasi-Newton methods according to memory and curvature approximation.
This page explains derivations, convergence, and numerical safeguards, relating them to defaults and termination of SciOxide's `GradientDescent`, `NonlinearCg`, `Bfgs`, and `Lbfgs`.
Step-size selection is in [Coordinate scaling, descent directions, and line search](../line-search-and-scaling/).

Below, $\mathbf{g}_k=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)$ denotes the gradient and $\nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}_k)$ the true Hessian.
Following Nocedal–Wright and SciOxide's `HessianForm`, quasi-Newton notation uses $\mathbf{B}_k$ for Hessian approximations (secant condition $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{y}$, `HessianForm::Hessian`) and $\mathbf{H}_k\approx(\nabla^2 f)^{-1}$ for inverse-Hessian approximations ($\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{s}$, `HessianForm::Inverse`).
$\mathbf{H}$ in [Symbols and terminology](../../../reference/) is the true Hessian; $\mathbf{H}_k$ here is an inverse-Hessian approximation.

## Stopping criteria

All four methods test the gradient 2-norm condition

$$
\lVert\mathbf{g}_k\rVert_2 \le \mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}\,\max\!\left(1, \frac{|f(\mathbf{x}_k)|}{\max(1, \lVert\mathbf{x}_k\rVert_\infty)}\right)
$$

and stop with `GradientSmall` when it holds.
Tolerance uses `GradientTolerance` with default absolute and relative $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$; the default iteration limit is 1000.
The relative part scales with objective magnitude divided by coordinate magnitude, helping preserve criterion meaning when objective units change.
Rejected line search gives `LineSearchFailed(reason)` and iteration limits `IterationLimit`; both return the last accepted point but are unsuccessful (`is_success()`).
`GradientSmall` indicates proximity to stationarity, not a local or global minimum.

## Steepest descent and ill-conditioning

For continuously differentiable $f$, the Taylor expansion for a small displacement $\mathbf{p}$ from point $\mathbf{x}$ is

$$
f(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{p}) = f(\mathbf{x}) + \nabla f(\mathbf{x})^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p} + \mathcal{O}(\|\mathbf{p}\|^2)
$$


Among unit directions in Euclidean norm $\|\mathbf{p}\|_2 = 1$, Cauchy–Schwarz makes the first-order decrease $-\nabla f(\mathbf{x})^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}$ largest in the negative-gradient direction

$$
\mathbf{p} = -\frac{\nabla f(\mathbf{x})}{\|\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\|_2}
$$


Moving in this direction gives steepest descent.

For the convex quadratic form with symmetric positive definite $\mathbf{A} \succ 0$,

$$
f(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}
$$

exact line search along negative gradient $\mathbf{p}_k = -\mathbf{g}_k = -(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{b})$ gives optimal step $\alpha_k$ as

$$
\alpha_k = \frac{\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k}{\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}_k}
$$


The inner product of updated gradient $\mathbf{g}_{k+1} = \mathbf{g}_k + \alpha_k \mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_k$ with the preceding search direction is

$$
\mathbf{g}_{k+1}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}_k = -\mathbf{g}_{k+1}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k = -\left(\mathbf{g}_k - \frac{\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k}{\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}_k} \mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}_k\right)^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k = -\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k + \mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k = 0
$$

so consecutive search directions (gradients) are orthogonal.

With spherical contours (condition number $\kappa(\mathbf{A}) = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min} = 1$), steepest descent reaches the true minimizer in 1 iteration.
But in elongated valleys with $\kappa(\mathbf{A}) \gg 1$, orthogonality creates a zigzag alternating between valley walls.
For minimizer $\mathbf{x}^*$, $f(\mathbf{x}) - f(\mathbf{x}^*) = \frac12\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^*\|_{\mathbf{A}}^2$ is half the squared energy-norm error; Kantorovich's inequality bounds its worst one-iteration contraction by

$$
\frac{f(\mathbf{x}_{k+1}) - f(\mathbf{x}^*)}{f(\mathbf{x}_k) - f(\mathbf{x}^*)} \le r = \left(\frac{\kappa(\mathbf{A}) - 1}{\kappa(\mathbf{A}) + 1}\right)^2
$$


At $\kappa = 100$, $r \approx 0.9608$, requiring $\ln 10^{-8}/\ln r \approx 460$ iterations to multiply $f-f^*$ (squared energy norm) by $10^{-8}$.
Reducing the energy norm itself by $10^{-8}$ requires twice as many, roughly 920 iterations.

`GradientDescent` does not use exact line search; its default acceptance is the Armijo condition in `BacktrackingLineSearch`.
The first iteration backtracks from $\alpha_0 = 1$; subsequent iterations use the second Barzilai–Borwein step from the preceding difference pair $\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_k$, $\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{g}_{k+1}-\mathbf{g}_k$ as their initial trial,

$$
\alpha^{\mathrm{BB}} = \frac{\mathbf{s}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}}{\mathbf{y}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}} = \arg\min_{\alpha} \lVert\mathbf{s} - \alpha\mathbf{y}\rVert_2
$$


This satisfies secant condition $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{s}$ in least squares when approximating the inverse Hessian by $\alpha\mathbf{I}$; for quadratics it lies within the reciprocal-eigenvalue range of $\mathbf{A}$.
If uncomputable, for example when $\mathbf{s}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y} \le 0$, use the last accepted step (halved if that search rejected a trial).
Backtracking confines the initial trial to $[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}, 1]$, so a BB step above 1 still starts at 1.
BB initial trials often progress faster than the exact-line-search bound above, but iterations still grow with conditioning.
Per-iteration work is $O(n)$ plus line-search evaluations; memory is several vectors ($O(n)$).
`GradientDescentOptions` configures gradient tolerance (`with_gradient_tolerance`), iteration limit (`with_max_iterations`), derivatives (`with_derivative_strategy`, default `AnalyticOrCentral`), time limit (`with_time_limit`, default none), and line search (`with_line_search`, default `BacktrackingLineSearch::default()`).

For sample-dependent fluctuating gradients, use `StochasticGradientDescent` in [Stochastic gradient descent](oxref:optimize-sgd).
`DeterministicGradient` wraps a deterministic objective implementing `SmoothGradient` as a sampled gradient ignoring sample indices, for comparing stochastic step rules against `GradientDescent` on noiseless problems.

## Nonlinear conjugate gradients

Linear conjugate gradients for quadratics builds directions conjugate with respect to $\mathbf{A}$ ($\mathbf{p}_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_j = 0, \; i \ne j$), converging in at most $n$ iterations in exact arithmetic.
Energy-norm error contracts by at most $2\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)^k$, making iterations proportional to $\sqrt{\kappa}$ rather than steepest descent's $\kappa$.
Nonlinear CG extends conjugate directions to general nonlinear $f(\mathbf{x})$.

Its search-direction update is

$$
\mathbf{p}_0 = -\mathbf{g}_0, \qquad \mathbf{p}_{k+1} = -\mathbf{g}_{k+1} + \beta_k \mathbf{p}_k
$$


Several standard formulas exist for conjugacy coefficient $\beta_k$.

- Fletcher–Reeves (FR) formula
  $$
  \beta_k^{\mathrm{FR}} = \frac{\mathbf{g}_{k+1}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_{k+1}}{\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k}
  $$
- Polak–Ribière (PR) formula
  $$
  \beta_k^{\mathrm{PR}} = \frac{\mathbf{g}_{k+1}^\mathsf{T}(\mathbf{g}_{k+1} - \mathbf{g}_k)}{\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k}
  $$

They coincide on quadratics with exact line search because gradients are mutually orthogonal.
For nonlinear functions, stagnation with almost unchanged successive gradients ($\mathbf{g}_{k+1} \approx \mathbf{g}_k$) makes the PR numerator approach 0, returning direction $\mathbf{p}_{k+1} \approx -\mathbf{g}_{k+1}$ to steepest descent.
Thus PR often escapes stagnation more easily.

`NonlinearCg` uses Polak–Ribière+ (PR+), clipping negative coefficients to 0,

$$
\beta_k^{\mathrm{PR+}} = \max\left(0, \; \beta_k^{\mathrm{PR}}\right)
$$


In the cases below, set $\beta_k = 0$ and restart from steepest descent; record counts and the last reason in `restarts()` and `last_restart()` (`CgRestartReason`) of `GlobalizationDiagnostics`.

- `Periodic`: iteration count is a multiple of dimension $n$.
- `NegativeBeta`: the numerator of $\beta^{\mathrm{PR}}$ is negative and PR+ sets the coefficient to zero.
- `Unrepresentable`: the coefficient or new direction is unrepresentable in floating point.
- `DescentLoss`: the new direction is not descending ($\mathbf{g}_{k+1}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}_{k+1} \ge 0$), or the preceding gradient is zero and cannot form the coefficient.

`beta()` in `GlobalizationDiagnostics` is the final coefficient; `gradient_orthogonality()` is $|\cos|$ of the angle between successive gradients, zero for quadratic exact line search.
Combined with sufficient descent and strong Wolfe line search, PR+ is globally convergent in the sense $\liminf_k \lVert\mathbf{g}_k\rVert = 0$ (Gilbert–Nocedal, 1992).
`NonlinearCg` checks descent each iteration and restarts by these rules if lost.
The default is strong Wolfe with $c_2 = 0.1$ (`StrongWolfeLineSearch::for_nonlinear_cg`).
Small $c_2$ seeks steps near line minima, preserving conjugacy more readily at the cost of more evaluations per iteration.
`NonlinearCgOptions` configures gradient tolerance, iteration limits, differentiation methods, time limits, and line search; the method uses $O(n)$ memory.

## Secant conditions and quasi-Newton methods

Pure Newton uses the Hessian for direction $\mathbf{p}_k = -(\nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}_k))^{-1}\mathbf{g}_k$, but Hessian evaluation and dense linear solves ($O(n^3)$) are expensive for many variables.

Quasi-Newton methods learn curvature from observed position differences $\mathbf{s}_k$ and gradient differences $\mathbf{y}_k$,

$$
\mathbf{s}_k = \mathbf{x}_{k+1} - \mathbf{x}_k, \qquad \mathbf{y}_k = \mathbf{g}_{k+1} - \mathbf{g}_k
$$


The mean-value theorem gives $\mathbf{y}_k = \left(\int_0^1 \nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}_k + t\mathbf{s}_k)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathbf{s}_k$, motivating the secant condition on the new approximation,

$$
\mathbf{B}_{k+1}\mathbf{s}_k = \mathbf{y}_k \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathbf{H}_{k+1}\mathbf{y}_k = \mathbf{s}_k \qquad (\mathbf{H}_{k+1}=\mathbf{B}_{k+1}^{-1})
$$

and updating that approximation each iteration.
The direction is $\mathbf{p}_k=-\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{g}_k$; retaining inverse approximation $\mathbf{H}_k$ computes it by matrix-vector products ($O(n^2)$) without linear solves.

## BFGS updates and positive definiteness

Among symmetric rank-two secant updates, BFGS (Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno) is widely used.
Its inverse-Hessian approximation $\mathbf{H}_k$ updates as follows.

$$
\mathbf{H}_{k+1} = \left(\mathbf{I} - \rho_k \mathbf{s}_k \mathbf{y}_k^\mathsf{T}\right) \mathbf{H}_k \left(\mathbf{I} - \rho_k \mathbf{y}_k \mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\right) + \rho_k \mathbf{s}_k \mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}, \qquad \rho_k = \frac{1}{\mathbf{y}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{s}_k}
$$

Among symmetric matrices satisfying secant condition $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}_k=\mathbf{s}_k$, this is closest to $\mathbf{H}_k$ in a weighted Frobenius norm.
In Hessian-approximation form the same update is $\mathbf{B}_{k+1} = \mathbf{B}_k - \frac{\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k}{\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k} + \frac{\mathbf{y}_k\mathbf{y}_k^\mathsf{T}}{\mathbf{y}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{s}_k}$.

### Positive-definiteness preservation

If initial $\mathbf{H}_0 \succ 0$ is positive definite and every step satisfies curvature condition

$$
\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k > 0
$$

then BFGS-updated $\mathbf{H}_{k+1}$ is also positive definite ($\mathbf{H}_{k+1} \succ 0$), an exact-arithmetic theorem.
For any $\mathbf{z}\ne\mathbf{0}$, setting $\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{z}-\rho_k(\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{z})\mathbf{y}_k$ gives $\mathbf{z}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}_{k+1}\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{w}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{w}+\rho_k(\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{z})^2$; both right-hand terms are nonnegative and cannot vanish together, proving the claim.

Then direction $\mathbf{p}_{k+1} = -\mathbf{H}_{k+1}\mathbf{g}_{k+1}$ is descending ($\mathbf{g}_{k+1}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}_{k+1} < 0$).
Steps satisfying [Wolfe conditions](oxref:optimize-wolfe-conditions) satisfy curvature $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k > 0$, so `Bfgs` defaults to strong Wolfe (`StrongWolfeLineSearch::default()`, $c_1 = 10^{-4}$, $c_2 = 0.9$).

`Bfgs` starts at $\mathbf{H}_0 = \mathbf{I}$ and rescales to $\mathbf{H}_0 = \gamma\mathbf{I}$, $\gamma = \mathbf{s}_0^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_0/\mathbf{y}_0^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_0$ just before the first update (Nocedal–Wright, Eq. 6.20).
$\gamma$ estimates inverse-Hessian scale from the first difference pair; retaining the identity would make the second iteration's step size mismatch the problem scale.
Thereafter the dense lower triangle of $\mathbf{H}_k$ is updated with $O(n^2)$ operations per iteration, using $O(n^2)$ memory.
If rounding or similar effects make $-\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{g}_k$ non-descending, reset $\mathbf{H}_k$ to identity and continue from steepest descent.
Updates pass `CurvaturePolicy` checks and verify positive updated diagonal entries before acceptance.

### Damped BFGS

For nonconvex objectives or constrained optimization (SQP), $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k$ can be small or negative.
`CurvaturePolicy` defaults to Powell damping (`CurvatureAction::Damp`, threshold $\eta = 0.2$).
In Hessian form (`HessianForm::Hessian`), when $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k < \eta\, \mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k$, update with the modified difference vector

$$
\bar{\mathbf{y}}_k = \theta \mathbf{y}_k + (1 - \theta)\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k, \qquad \theta = \frac{(1 - \eta)\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k}{\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k - \mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k}
$$


The modified pair satisfies $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\bar{\mathbf{y}}_k = \eta\,\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k > 0$.
In inverse form (`HessianForm::Inverse`) used by `Bfgs`, exchange $\mathbf{s}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ roles and use $\bar{\mathbf{s}}_k = \theta\mathbf{s}_k + (1-\theta)\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{y}_k$ when $\mathbf{y}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{s}_k < \eta\,\mathbf{y}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{y}_k$.
Selecting `CurvatureAction::Skip` drops insufficient-curvature pairs and retains the preceding approximation.
Read accepted, damped, and skipped update counts through `updates()`, `damped_updates()`, and `skipped_updates()` of `GlobalizationDiagnostics`.
[Curvature-update safeguards and SR1](../quasi-newton-updates/) explains dropping conditions and reasons, direct-matrix `HessianUpdateWorkspace` updates, and SR1 without positive-definiteness preservation.

## L-BFGS and the two-loop recursion

For many variables $n$, retaining and updating dense $n \times n$ matrix $\mathbf{H}_k$ ($O(n^2)$ memory) is expensive.
L-BFGS (limited-memory BFGS) stores only the latest $m$ difference pairs $\{(\mathbf{s}_i, \mathbf{y}_i)\}_{i=k-m}^{k-1}$ in a circular buffer and computes inverse-Hessian/gradient product $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{g}_k$ without forming the matrix.

The following two-loop recursion is central.
Expanding inverse BFGS update $\mathbf{H}_{i+1}=\mathbf{V}_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}_i\mathbf{V}_i+\rho_i\mathbf{s}_i\mathbf{s}_i^\mathsf{T}$ ($\mathbf{V}_i=\mathbf{I}-\rho_i\mathbf{y}_i\mathbf{s}_i^\mathsf{T}$) $m$ times and multiplying $\mathbf{g}_k$ from the right gives this procedure.

```text
// L-BFGS two-loop recursion (ρ_i = 1 / (y_iᵀ s_i))
1. q = g_k
2. for i = k-1 down to k-m:
       α_i = ρ_i * (s_iᵀ q)
       q = q - α_i * y_i
3. γ_k = (s_{k-1}ᵀ y_{k-1}) / (y_{k-1}ᵀ y_{k-1})
   r = γ_k * q   // Apply initial matrix H_k⁰ = γ_k I
4. for i = k-m up to k-1:
       β = ρ_i * (y_iᵀ r)
       r = r + s_i * (α_i - β)
5. Search direction p_k = -r
```

Memory is $O(mn)$; per-iteration work is roughly $4mn$ multiply-adds and $O(n)$.
Initial-matrix $\gamma_k$ is recomputed each iteration from the latest pair, unlike dense BFGS's one-time $\gamma$.
Discarding old curvature can make dense BFGS require fewer iterations for few variables.

`Lbfgs` sets $m$ through `with_memory` of `LbfgsOptions` (default 10); default line search is the same strong Wolfe as `Bfgs`.
$\mathbf{H}$ in $\mathbf{y}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}$ used to assess `CurvaturePolicy` is the matrix to be updated by the new pair: apply retained-pair updates to that pair's $\gamma\mathbf{I}$ (Nocedal–Wright, Eqs. 7.19–7.20).
Store damped $\bar{\mathbf{s}}$ for damped pairs and nothing for skipped pairs.
If two-loop directions are uncomputable or not descending, clear all stored pairs and continue from steepest descent.

For variable bounds $\mathbf{l} \le \mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{u}$ alone, use `LbfgsB`; [Box constraints and projected L-BFGS](../bound-constrained-lbfgsb/) explains projected-gradient stopping and active bounds.
For general nonlinear constraints, use methods in [Nonlinear constraints and KKT conditions](../nonlinear-constraints/).
[Conditioning and convergence comparison](../first-order-convergence/) examines how the four methods' iterations and evaluations vary with conditioning.

## Workspace and resumption

`Bfgs::default().minimize(&f, &x0)` internally allocates workspace and results and solves sequentially.
`Bfgs`, `Lbfgs`, `GradientDescent`, and `NonlinearCg` default to derivative policy `AnalyticOrCentral`, using central differences without gradients ($2n$ objective evaluations per gradient).
One-shot `minimize(f, &x0)` uses these central differences with default `Bfgs`, returning unsuccessful termination as `Err`.

For repeated same-dimension solves, prepare workspace once and pass it to `minimize_into`.
`GradientDescentWorkspace`, `NonlinearCgWorkspace`, `LbfgsWorkspace`, and `BfgsWorkspace` are method workspaces; `workspace(n)` allocates for dimension $n$, and `new()` does not allocate.
`workspace_requirements(n)` returns required shapes as `GradientDescentWorkspaceRequirements`, `NonlinearCgWorkspaceRequirements`, `LbfgsWorkspaceRequirements`, `BfgsWorkspaceRequirements`; workspace `check` checks sufficiency and `try_reserve` allocates.
`BfgsWorkspaceRequirements` reports the size of roughly $10n^2 + 40n$ values through `bytes()` and returns `ResourceLimit` beyond `BfgsOptions::with_workspace_byte_limit`, rejecting excessive $n$ before dense allocation.
Errors from `minimize_into` leave output unchanged from before the call.

Workspace `last_attempt()` records the latest call; `last_accepted()` borrows that call's last accepted point as `MinimizerAccepted`.
`point()` and `gradient()` of `MinimizerAccepted` return point and gradient; `progress()` returns `MinimizerProgress` (objective, iterations, gradient 2-norm, confidence).
Calls erroring before acceptance have `last_accepted()` equal to `None`; distinguish this from output retaining preceding values.
`BfgsWorkspace::checkpoint()` copies the accepted point, gradient, objective, inverse-Hessian approximation $\mathbf{H}$, and iteration count into an owned `BfgsCheckpoint`; `Bfgs::resume_into` checks matching method, settings, problem shape, and state before objective calls, then continues.
Iteration limits count from the original solve's start; resumption advances at most (iteration limit) − (checkpoint iterations), and returned iterations include checkpoint work.
For cancellation and resumption, see the [API resumption experiment](oxref:optimize-api-bfgs-lifecycle).

## Summary

Choose gradient-only methods by variable count and available memory: `GradientDescent` and `NonlinearCg` with $O(n)$ memory, `Lbfgs` with $O(mn)$, or `Bfgs` with $O(n^2)$.
Steepest-descent iterations grow with condition number $\kappa$, CG with $\sqrt{\kappa}$, and quasi-Newton curvature learning weakens this dependence; first scale ill-conditioned variables, then change methods.
BFGS positive definiteness depends on $\mathbf{s}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}>0$; failing pairs are Powell-damped by default or dropped under `CurvatureAction::Skip`, so inspect update counts in `GlobalizationDiagnostics`.
For every method, judge success through `GradientSmall` termination, not points stopped by iteration limits or failed line search.
Reuse workspace for same-dimension solves; long solves can be split and continued with `BfgsCheckpoint`.
