Optimization searches a set of permitted points for a point minimizing an objective function.
First organizing the objective expression, constraints, derivative availability, and required result narrows the choice of solver.
This chapter distinguishes local stopping, optimality for convex problems, finite global search, and statistical estimation.
This page collects chapter-wide types, notation, termination reasons, and call forms and leads to individual algorithms.

## Problem types

`ObjectiveFunction` returns a real-valued objective, and `LeastSquaresProblem` a residual vector.
`ObjectiveFn` and `LeastSquaresFn` connect closures to these contracts.
Collect box constraints in `BoundedObjective` and general equalities/inequalities in `ConstrainedProblem`.
`ConstraintFunction` and `ConstraintFn` supply constraint values; register equality or inequality status in `Constraints`.
LP, QP, and MIP use coefficient-owning `LpProblem`, `QpProblem`, and `MipProblem`.

Inequalities use direction $g(x)\le0$.
For example, write $x\ge a$ as $a-x\le0$.
Keep matrix rows and columns, parameter component order, and residual units consistent between expressions and callbacks.
`ConstraintsWorkspace` can be reused for constraint-value and Jacobian evaluation.
`ConstraintProfile` describes constraint composition; `ConstraintValues` and `ConstraintJacobianStats` describe evaluation results and information.

## Notation

This chapter writes iterates as $\mathbf{x}_k$, gradients as $\mathbf{g}_k=\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)$, and Hessians as $\nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}_k)$.
[Newton and trust-region methods](newton-and-trust-region/) writes the Hessian as $\mathbf{H}_k=\nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}_k)$, following [Symbols and terminology](../../reference/).
Quasi-Newton pages follow Nocedal–Wright notation: $\mathbf{B}_k$ for Hessian approximations and $\mathbf{H}_k$ for inverse-Hessian approximations.
These correspond to `HessianForm::Hessian` (secant condition $\mathbf{B}\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{y}$) and `HessianForm::Inverse` ($\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{s}$).
Least-squares pages write residuals as $\mathbf{r}$ and their Jacobian as $\mathbf{J}$.
$\varepsilon$ is the scalar type's machine epsilon, equal to $2^{-52} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ for `f64`.

## Derivatives and scaling

Analytical gradients, automatic differentiation, and finite differences differ in computational work and error characteristics.
Supply available derivatives through trait methods or adapter closures; settings `DerivativeStrategy` determine treatment of unsupplied derivatives.
Defaults are `AnalyticOrCentral` (central differences without analytical derivatives) for line-search local methods (`Bfgs`, `Lbfgs`, `GradientDescent`, `NonlinearCg`, `NewtonOptimizer`), and `AnalyticOrForward` (forward differences when unavailable) for other methods supplementing derivatives with differences.
Central differences use $2n$ objective evaluations per gradient with error of order $O(\varepsilon^{2/3})$; forward differences use $n$ with error of order $O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})$.
`DerivativeWorkspace` prepares memory for obtaining derivatives.
`NelderMead`, `Powell`, `Cobyla`, and `Cobyqa` use only values and have no such setting.
Choose a derivative source in the [Differentiation chapter](../../scioxide-differentiate/) and [Automatic differentiation chapter](../../scioxide-ad/).

Scaling variables by $\mathbf{x}=D\mathbf{z}$ gives $\nabla_{\mathbf{z}} f=D^\mathsf{T}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f$.
The meaning of a small gradient depends on coordinate and objective scales.
Stopping thresholds follow the `scioxide-core` tolerance form $a + r \cdot s$ ($a$ absolute tolerance, $r$ relative tolerance, $s$ problem scale); smooth local methods use $s = \max\bigl(1, |f(\mathbf{x})| / \max(1, \lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_\infty)\bigr)$.
`OptimalityTolerances` separately configures feasibility, gradient, complementarity, numerical rank, dual feasibility, pivots, reduced costs, and dual gaps; constrained methods, mathematical programming, and verification use these settings.
Step-size tolerance is not part of `OptimalityTolerances`; supply it through method-specific settings (such as `StepTolerance`).
[Coordinate scaling, descent directions, and line search](line-search-and-scaling/) discusses effects on search directions.

## Termination reasons and diagnostics

`OptimizeTermination` returned by result `termination()` is the sole termination criterion; decide success with `is_success()`.
The result type's direct `is_success()` also derives from this reason.
`Ok` means a result was received, not necessarily convergence.
Do not reinterpret partial results stopped by small steps, small objective changes, stagnation, or limits as success.
Exceptions are line-search `LineSearchResult` (`LineSearchTermination`) and LP/QP verification `VerificationReport` (`VerificationStatus` and `is_accepted()`), each with its own status.

`OptimizeResult` holds a point, objective, gradient, termination reason, work counts, and two kinds of diagnostics.
`OptimalityDiagnostics` contains original-problem residuals (primal feasibility, stationarity, dual feasibility, complementarity, integrality, bound violations, optimality gaps); `OptimizeDiagnostics` contains method-specific observations.
`None` for an uncomputed diagnostic does not mean a zero residual.
`trust()` indicates how far the returned point has been checked through `PartialResultTrust`, distinguishing `Admissible` using only the method's acceptance rules, `ResidualChecked` with independently checked residuals, and `CertificateVerified` with verified certificates.
Interpret LP/QP `ProgrammingResult` and `MipResult` with integer solutions, bounds, and gaps according to their use.

### Termination reason classification

The table groups unsuccessful reasons by meaning and shows the errors produced by one-shot functions and `require_success()`.
Parentheses in the error column contain the `ConvergenceFailure` reason (`ConvergenceReason`).

| Class | Termination reasons | Point in the result | One-shot functions and `require_success()` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success | `Converged`, `GradientSmall` | Point satisfying stopping criteria | No error |
| No further progress | `StepSmall`, `FunctionChangeSmall`, `Stagnation` | Last accepted point | `ConvergenceFailure` (`StepSizeLimit`, `Stagnation`) |
| Line-search failure | `LineSearchFailed(_)` | Last accepted point | `ConvergenceFailure` (`NestedSolver`) |
| Count and memory limits | `IterationLimit`, `EvaluationLimit`, `ProposalLimit`, `SampleLimit`, `NodeLimit`, `DepthLimit`, `MemoryLimit` | Last accepted point | `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`, `EvaluationLimit`, `ResourceLimit`) |
| Time limit and cancellation | `TimeLimit`, `Cancelled` | Last accepted point | `ExecutionStopped` |
| End of global search | `SearchBudgetCompleted`, `PopulationStagnation` | Best observed point | `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`, `Stagnation`). `minimize_global` returns a result |
| Sampled objective | `EstimatedStationarity`, `NoiseLimit` | Last accepted point | `ConvergenceFailure` (`Stagnation`) |
| Certified infeasibility/unboundedness | `Infeasible`, `Unbounded` | No optimum; contains a certificate | `DomainError` |
| Uncertified infeasibility/unboundedness | `SuspectedInfeasible`, `SuspectedUnbounded` | Last iterate | `ConvergenceFailure` (`Stagnation`, `Divergence`) |

Even a limit or cancellation stop gives an error without a result if no first point has been accepted.
`NotStarted` is the initial value in empty results such as `OptimizeResult::empty()`, not a post-solve reason.
Global-search methods return no successful status; completing the planned search is also `SearchBudgetCompleted`.
For sampled objectives, meeting the gradient criterion on noiseless samples gives `GradientSmall`, whereas noisy samples give estimate-based `EstimatedStationarity`.

| Quantity checked in results | What it shows | Additional checks needed |
|---|---|---|
| Gradient/projected gradient | Remaining first-order improvement directions | Curvature, locality, variable scales |
| Constraint violation | Departure from original constraints | Stationarity, dual feasibility, complementarity |
| Primal-dual numerical verification | Consistency of supplied point and certificates | Convexity assumptions, rounding, tolerances |
| MIP bound and gap | Separation between integer incumbent and search lower bound | Integrality, original constraints, search limits |
| Best global-search sample | Best observed candidate | Unexplored regions, seed variability |
| Sample mean and standard error | Observed estimate uncertainty | Sampling assumptions, independent replicate count |

## Calling forms

The same optimization can be called in several forms according to the control needed.
This chapter names argument/result forms as follows.

| Form | Examples | Input | Output | Execution context |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-shot function | `minimize(f, &x0)`, `minimize_bounded(f, &x0, &bounds)`, `minimize_constrained(f, &x0, &constraints)`, `minimize_global(f, &bounds, seed)`, `minimize_scalar(f, a, b)`, `least_squares(r, &x0, m)`, `curve_fit(model, &xdata, &ydata, &p0)`, `nnls(&a, &b)`, `bvls(&a, &b, &bounds)`, `quadratic_assignment(a, b, direction)`, `LpProblem::solve`, `QpProblem::solve`, `MipProblem::solve` | Problem data only | Successful result | Sequential, no clock |
| Standard method-object form | `Bfgs::default().minimize(&f, &x0)`, `LeastSquares::new(options)?.solve(&problem, &x0)`, `DifferentialEvolution::new(options)?.minimize(&problem, &x0)`, `Direct::new(options)?.minimize(&problem)` | Value with validated settings and problem | Newly allocated result (also returned when unsuccessful) | Sequential, no clock |
| Plan | `solve(direction, initial)` of `QapPlan` built by `QapProblemView::prepare()`; options form `solve_with_options(direction, initial, &options)` | Preprocessed problem | Newly allocated result | Sequential, no clock |
| Output-buffer API | `solver.minimize_into(&f, &x0, &mut result, &mut workspace, context)`, `solve_into`, `verify_into` | Input, output, workspace, context last | Writes output | Supplied context |

One-shot functions are free functions constructing a fixed method object with defaults for one solve (methods on problem types for linear, quadratic, and integer programming).
`minimize` uses BFGS, `minimize_bounded` L-BFGS-B, `minimize_constrained` SQP, `minimize_global` differential evolution, `minimize_scalar` interval Brent, and `least_squares` and `curve_fit` Levenberg–Marquardt.
Supply the objective as a value-returning closure (`Fn(&[S]) -> Result<S, SciOxideError>` for multiple variables, or `Fn(&[S], &mut [S])` writing residuals for `least_squares`); methods using derivatives approximate them by differences.
`minimize` uses central differences; `minimize_bounded`, `minimize_constrained`, `least_squares`, and `curve_fit` use forward differences.
`minimize`, `minimize_bounded`, `minimize_constrained`, `minimize_scalar`, `least_squares`, and `curve_fit` have iteration limit 1000 and stopping tolerance $a = r = \sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
`LpProblem::solve` uses revised simplex, `QpProblem::solve` automatic selection by problem shape, and `MipProblem::solve` branch and bound (1000 search nodes).

One-shot functions return unsuccessful termination as `Err`, without partial results carrying a point.
Error mappings follow the [Termination reason classification](#termination-reason-classification) table.
The exception is `minimize_global`, which returns results for differential evolution exhausting planned generations, `SearchBudgetCompleted`, and population stagnation, `PopulationStagnation`.
These terminations do not claim convergence, so read the result's reason.

Method objects (such as `Bfgs`, `LeastSquares`, `Sqp`, `Direct`, `BasinHopping`) hold validated settings and can solve repeatedly.
Standard `minimize`, `solve`, and `verify` allocate workspace and results, solve sequentially, and return the best accepted point even on unsuccessful termination.
To accept only success, use result `require_success()` to map termination reasons to one-shot errors.
The `Optimizer` trait selects local methods for the same problem type at runtime as `dyn Optimizer`.
The only Plan with preprocessing is quadratic-assignment `QapPlan`, which constructs matrix copies and unfixed-facility blocks with $O(n^2)$ work and memory.

Workspace records the latest attempt and state accepted during that attempt.
`MinimizerProgress` and `MinimizerAccepted`, and Plan-method `ProgrammingProgress` and `ProgrammingAccepted`, read this correspondence.
Preserving caller output on an error is separate from success of the latest attempt.
Owned restart snapshots include BFGS, NSGA-II, and stochastic first-order checkpoints (`BfgsCheckpoint`, `Nsga2Checkpoint`, `StochasticCheckpoint`), and simplex `SimplexWarmStart`.

## Evaluation counts and execution environment

One evaluation of an objective, residual, gradient, or Jacobian has different computational work.
Result `counts()` returns `SolveCounts` of `scioxide-core`, counting actually permitted calls by role (`EvaluationRole`), such as objective, gradient, Hessian, constraints, and residuals.
Own work is in `own()` and nested-solve work in `nested()`; obtain totals with overflow-checking `total()`.
Numerical differentiation requires several objective evaluations per gradient; failed derivative attempts also count.
Local solves called by global search also deduct from the whole search's limit.

Browser cells run sequentially on Wasm.
Use iteration, evaluation, and sample limits instead of thread execution or clock-dependent time limits in cells.
Allocation-free execution requires preallocated workspace and output plus callbacks and providers that also allocate nothing.

## Summary

Objective smoothness and derivatives, box versus general equality/inequality constraints, and residual structure determine which mathematical pages to read.
For one solve, one-shot functions return unsuccessful termination as errors; for settings or workspace reuse, use method objects and output-buffer APIs.
Judge method-object success with `termination().is_success()` or `require_success()`; receiving `Ok` or a small objective is not convergence.
On unsuccessful termination, use the classification to distinguish the last accepted point from the best observed point.
`OptimalityTolerances` separates gradient, feasibility, and complementarity tolerances by role; interpret which residual is small at which scale alongside the point and objective.
Read evaluation counts by role from `counts()`, accounting for numerical-derivative attempts and local solves inside global search also counting toward limits.
Check suitability for the application through analytical solutions, substitution into original equations, accuracy refinement, or another method.
