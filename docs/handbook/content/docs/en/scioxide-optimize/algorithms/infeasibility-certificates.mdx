A solver's claims of infeasibility or unboundedness can be checked with certificates, just like an optimal solution.
This page explains the mathematical basis of both certificates, the checks performed by `ProgrammingVerifier`, conversion of external solver output to SciOxide results for checking, and workspace reuse for repeated checks.
Multiplier sign conventions and optimality certificates are covered in [Certificates and verification for linear and quadratic programming](../programming-certificates/).

## Farkas' lemma

The standard-form constraints $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{x}\ge\mathbf{0}$ having no solution is equivalent to the existence of a vector satisfying

$$
\mathbf{A}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}\le\mathbf{0},\qquad \mathbf{b}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}>0
$$

where the vector is $\mathbf{y}$ (Farkas' lemma).
One implication follows in a single line.
A feasible $\mathbf{x}$ would give $0<\mathbf{b}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{A}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}\le0$, a contradiction.
The converse follows from the separating hyperplane theorem, so every infeasible LP has such a $\mathbf{y}$.
The certificate means that weighting the constraints by $\mathbf{y}$ and summing them yields an inequality no $\mathbf{x}$ can satisfy.

SciOxide expresses the same argument in the original problem form ($\le$, $=$ and $\ge$ rows and variable bounds), using multipliers $\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}$ satisfying the sign conventions.
If stationarity for a zero objective holds:

$$
\mathbf{A}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}=\mathbf{0}
$$

then the [optimality certificate lower bound](../programming-certificates/) states that the optimal value of the zero-objective problem is at least $-\mathbf{b}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{l}^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}-\mathbf{u}^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}$.
The zero-objective problem has value 0, so a positive bound implies there is no feasible point.
The `ProgrammingCertificate::Infeasibility(dual)` check verifies multiplier signs without tolerance, this stationarity condition, and that the value (margin, `witness_margin()`) is positive and exceeds the gap tolerance.
Before checking, multipliers are scaled by dividing by a power of two so that their largest absolute component lies in $[1,2)$.
Multiplying a multiplier set by a power of two therefore leaves the margin unchanged, while other constant factors can change it by a factor of less than two.
Different multiplier sets have different margins.

## Unboundedness certificates

If a feasible point $\mathbf{x}_0$ and direction $\mathbf{d}$ (a recession direction) keep $\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{d}$ feasible for all $t\ge0$, and $\mathbf{c}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}<0$, the objective decreases to $-\infty$ as $t\to\infty$.
Recession conditions are $\mathbf{a}_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}\le0$ for $\le$ rows, $\mathbf{a}_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}=0$ for $=$ rows, $\mathbf{a}_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}\ge0$ for $\ge$ rows, $d_j\ge0$ for components with finite lower bounds, and $d_j\le0$ for components with finite upper bounds.
The `ProgrammingCertificate::Unboundedness { point, direction }` check verifies point feasibility, the direction's recession conditions, and objective decrease $-\mathbf{c}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}>0$ (`witness_margin()`).
The direction is scaled before checking.
Point feasibility is tested within tolerance, but recession conditions are tested exactly without tolerance (zero violation).
For a convex QP, the objective along $\mathbf{d}$ is $\tfrac12t^2\mathbf{d}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{d}+t(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}_0+\mathbf{c})^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}+f(\mathbf{x}_0)$, so it is bounded below if $\mathbf{d}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{d}>0$.
A QP unboundedness certificate therefore additionally requires $(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{Q}^\mathsf{T})\mathbf{d}/2$ to equal $\mathbf{0}$ exactly.
For a convex QP, $\mathbf{d}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{d}=0$ and $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{0}$ are equivalent, so the objective then becomes the linear function $t\,\mathbf{c}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{d}+f(\mathbf{x}_0)$ of $t$.

Even if a solver detects signs of infeasibility or unboundedness, a failed certificate check leaves termination as `SuspectedInfeasible` or `SuspectedUnbounded` rather than `Infeasible` or `Unbounded`.

## External solver results

Points, objective values, termination reasons and certificates from other solvers can be converted to `ProgrammingResult` with `ProgrammingResult::from_candidate(x, fun, termination, iterations, evidence)`.
The `evidence` is a `OwnedProgrammingCertificate` (`Optimality`, `Infeasibility` or `Unboundedness`), and multipliers are constructed with `OwnedDualCertificate::new(rows, lower, upper)`.
The `from_candidate` constructor checks only that the point is nonempty and its components and objective value are finite; the constructed result is unverified.
Passing it to `ProgrammingVerifier::verify(&problem, &result)` checks the certificate, agreement between the termination reason and certificate kind, and agreement between the returned objective and its recomputed value.
Results without a certificate are rejected.

## Reusing workspace

The `ProgrammingVerifier::workspace(&problem)` method allocates `VerificationWorkspace` for one problem, including memory for dense Cholesky factorization and eigenvalues used in QP convexity checking.
Calling `verify_into(&problem, claim, &mut report, &mut workspace, scope)` with this workspace performs no allocation, even for the first check.
An incorrectly sized workspace (created for a differently sized problem), a certificate with incorrect lengths, or a certificate with nonfinite components is rejected before any operator application with `InvalidInput`, `DimensionMismatch` or `NonFinite` errors, respectively.
Pass an execution context to `scope`; a check stopped by the `VerificationOptions::with_max_evaluations` limit, cancellation or deadline yields `Incomplete`.
The `VerificationReport::empty()` value is an empty report used for the output of `verify_into`.
Read the most recent check record with `VerificationWorkspace::last_attempt()`.

## Inventory and delivery commitments

Suppose only 1 t of raw material is available, yet delivery of at least $r$ t in total of two products $x_1,x_2$ [t] has been promised.
The constraints are $x_1+x_2\le1$, $x_1+x_2\ge r$ and $x_1,x_2\ge0$, and the objective minimizes computational cost $x_1+2x_2$; the problem is infeasible if $r>1$.
Multiply the $\le$ row by $y_1=1$ and the $\ge$ row by $y_2=-1$, orient both as $\le$ and add them; the left sides cancel to give $0\le1-r$, a contradiction when $r>1$.
In the formula in the Farkas' lemma section, the margin is $-(1\cdot1+(-1)\cdot r)=r-1$.
This cell verifies the results of `SimplexSolver` and `InteriorPointLp`, then uses `ProgrammingResult::from_candidate` to construct a result claiming these hand-derived multipliers as external solver output and passes it to `verify_into` with reusable `VerificationWorkspace`.
The input is the promised delivery $r$; “Analytical margin” in the table is $r-1$.
The final outcome shows whether the `SimplexSolver` claim (optimal or infeasible) passes verification.

```rust
//| id: optimize-lp-farkas-certificate
//| ref: optimize-lp-farkas-certificate
//| title: "Verify infeasibility certificates from solvers and hand-derived multipliers"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   promised: {"type":"range","label":"Promised delivery r [t]","description":"Exceeding the 1 t inventory makes the problem infeasible.","min":0,"max":4,"step":0.25,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"feasible","label":"r = 0.5 t (feasible)","description":"The solver returns Converged and an optimality certificate, while the hand-derived Farkas multipliers have margin −0.5 and are Rejected.","inputs":{"promised":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"boundary","label":"r = 1 t (boundary)","description":"The problem is feasible; the hand-derived multipliers have zero margin and are Rejected because it is not positive.","inputs":{"promised":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Infeasible"}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"Infeasible"}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/2/3","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/2/4","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: feasible
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":"Optimality"}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/2/3","equals":"Rejected"}
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/2/4","approx":{"value":-0.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: boundary
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"claims","path":"/rows/2/3","equals":"Rejected"}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    InteriorPointLp, LinearConstraintSense, LinearConstraints, LpProblem, OptimizeTermination,
    OwnedDualCertificate, OwnedProgrammingCertificate, ProgrammingCertificate,
    ProgrammingResult, ProgrammingVerifier, SimplexSolver, VerificationReport,
};
let general = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
// Row 0: inventory x₁ + x₂ ≤ 1; row 1: delivery x₁ + x₂ ≥ r. LpProblem without explicit bounds uses x ≥ 0.
let rows = LinearConstraints::new(
    Matrix::from_vec(2, 2, vec![1.0_f64, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    vec![1.0, promised],
    vec![
        LinearConstraintSense::LessEqual,
        LinearConstraintSense::GreaterEqual,
    ],
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lp = LpProblem::new(vec![1.0, 2.0])
    .and_then(|p| p.with_constraints(rows))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let verifier = ProgrammingVerifier::default();
let simplex = SimplexSolver::default()
    .solve(&lp)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let interior = InteriorPointLp::default()
    .solve(&lp)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Hand-derived Farkas multipliers: 1 for the ≤ row and −1 for the ≥ row. Construct a result claiming them as external solver output.
let farkas = OwnedDualCertificate::new(vec![1.0, -1.0], vec![0.0; 2], vec![0.0; 2]);
let external = ProgrammingResult::from_candidate(
    vec![0.0, 0.0],
    0.0,
    OptimizeTermination::Infeasible,
    0,
    Some(OwnedProgrammingCertificate::Infeasibility(farkas)),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Allocate workspace once and check all three claims using the same workspace.
let mut workspace = verifier.workspace(&lp).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut reports = Vec::new();
for result in [&simplex, &interior, &external] {
    let mut report = VerificationReport::empty();
    verifier
        .verify_into(
            &lp,
            result,
            &mut report,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    reports.push(report);
}
let kind = |result: &ProgrammingResult<f64>| match result.certificate() {
    Some(ProgrammingCertificate::Optimality { .. }) => "Optimality",
    Some(ProgrammingCertificate::Infeasibility(_)) => "Infeasibility",
    Some(ProgrammingCertificate::Unboundedness { .. }) => "Unbounded",
    None => "None",
};
let table: Vec<_> = [
    ("SimplexSolver", &simplex),
    ("InteriorPointLp", &interior),
    ("Hand-derived multipliers", &external),
]
.into_iter()
.zip(&reports)
.map(|((name, result), report)| {
    (
        name,
        result.termination().to_string(),
        kind(result),
        format!("{:?}", report.status()),
        report.witness_margin(),
        promised - 1.0,
        report
            .primal_objective()
            .filter(|_| result.termination() == OptimizeTermination::Converged),
    )
})
.collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Claim" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Certificate kind" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Verification status" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Margin [-]", "type": "number", "format": general, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Analytical margin r − 1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Optimal computational cost [-]", "type": "number", "format": general, "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    table,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "claims" })
);
// Conclusion: whether the SimplexSolver claim passes checking against the original problem.
let check = &reports[0];
let outcome = if check.is_accepted() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The SimplexSolver claim passed checking against the original problem")
} else {
    Outcome::failed("The SimplexSolver claim failed checking against the original problem")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(simplex.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Stationarity residual", check.stationarity(), None)
        .diagnostic("Multiplier sign violation", check.dual_violation(), None)
);
```

At the default $r=2$ t, both `SimplexSolver` and `InteriorPointLp` return `Infeasible`, and their infeasibility certificates are `Accepted`.
Both the `SimplexSolver` multipliers and the hand-derived multipliers have margin 1, matching the analytical value $r-1$.
The `InteriorPointLp` certificate uses a different multiplier set, with margin 0.42.
The proof requires a positive margin exceeding the tolerance; its magnitude depends on the multiplier set.

At $r=0.5$ t, both solvers return an optimum with computational cost 0.5 and an optimality certificate, while the hand-derived multipliers have margin $-0.5$ and are `Rejected`.
At the boundary $r=1$ t, the margin is exactly 0 and therefore not positive, yielding `Rejected`.
Even when a result constructed with `from_candidate` claims termination `Infeasible`, it is not accepted unless it passes checking.

## Summary

Infeasibility can be established by multipliers satisfying the sign conventions and zero-objective stationarity with a positive margin.
Unboundedness can be established by a feasible point and a recession direction that preserves every constraint while decreasing the objective; a QP additionally requires $\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{0}$.
Margin magnitudes differ between multiplier sets, so interpret only positivity and whether the margin exceeds tolerance.
Convert external solver output into a result with `from_candidate` before verification; claims whose termination reason conflicts with their certificate kind are not accepted.
For repeated checks, allocate `VerificationWorkspace` once and check without allocation using `verify_into`.
