Gradient-based local methods choose a search direction $\mathbf{p}_k$ at the current point $\mathbf{x}_k$ and advance by step length $\alpha_k > 0$, giving iterations of the form

$$
\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k + \alpha_k \mathbf{p}_k
$$

.
Direction construction is covered in [First-order methods, nonlinear conjugate gradients and quasi-Newton methods](../first-order-and-quasi-newton/); this page covers line search for choosing $\alpha_k$ and variable scales, which affect both directions and step lengths.
Methods for finding a univariate minimum within an interval without derivatives are covered in [Scalar minimization](../scalar-minimization/).

## Coordinate scaling and nondimensionalization

Design variables $x_i$ in physics and engineering have units with different orders of magnitude, such as length (m), temperature (K) and pressure (Pa).
When optimizing directly, the choice of units alone (for example, changing m to μm) changes the gradient direction $\nabla f$ and the meaning of stopping tests.

Choose a reference point $\mathbf{x}^{(0)}$ and collect representative variation scales $d_i > 0$ in a diagonal matrix $\mathbf{D} = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \dots, d_n)$, then introduce dimensionless coordinates $\mathbf{z}$ by

$$
\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}^{(0)} + \mathbf{D}\mathbf{z} \iff \mathbf{z} = \mathbf{D}^{-1}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^{(0)})
$$

.
By the chain rule, the gradient and Hessian in $\mathbf{z}$ are

$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{z}} f = \mathbf{D} \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f, \qquad \nabla_{\mathbf{z}}^2 f = \mathbf{D} (\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}^2 f) \mathbf{D}
$$

.
Choosing $d_i$ near the inverse square roots $d_i \approx (\partial^2 f / \partial x_i^2)^{-1/2}$ of Hessian diagonal entries makes the diagonal entries of $\nabla_{\mathbf{z}}^2 f$ roughly 1 and often reduces condition number $\kappa = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$.
This aligns only the diagonal, leaving strong correlations between variables.

The steepest descent direction $-\nabla f$ depends on coordinates, while Newton iterations are unchanged under invertible linear transformations of $\mathbf{x}$ (affine invariance).
BFGS also produces equivalent iterations if its initial approximation is transformed with the coordinates, but choosing a scalar multiple of the identity makes its iterations coordinate-dependent.
Gradient-norm stopping tests also depend on coordinates.
SciOxide's gradient-based local methods do not automatically align variable scales; supply an objective written as a function of $\mathbf{z}$.

## Descent directions and directional derivatives

Fix point $\mathbf{x}_k$ and search direction $\mathbf{p}_k$, and consider the univariate function of step length $\alpha \ge 0$:

$$
\phi(\alpha) = f(\mathbf{x}_k + \alpha \mathbf{p}_k)
$$


By the chain rule, its directional derivative at $\alpha = 0$ is

$$
\phi'(0) = \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}_k
$$

.
A direction $\mathbf{p}_k$ satisfying $\phi'(0) < 0$ is a descent direction; then $\phi(\alpha) < \phi(0)$ for sufficiently small $\alpha > 0$.
If $\phi'(0) > 0$, the objective increases for small $\alpha$; if $\phi'(0) = 0$, there is no first-order decrease, so reconstruct the direction before line search.
If $\theta_k$ is the angle between direction $\mathbf{p}_k$ and negative gradient $-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)$, then $\cos\theta_k = -\phi'(0)/(\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)\rVert_2\lVert\mathbf{p}_k\rVert_2)$.

## Armijo condition and backtracking

An exact line search that accurately minimizes $\phi$ requires many evaluations.
In practice, an inexact line search chooses a step satisfying the following conditions with few evaluations.

The Armijo condition (sufficient decrease condition) requires, for constant $c_1 \in (0, 1)$,

$$
\phi(\alpha) \le \phi(0) + c_1 \alpha \phi'(0)
$$

.
The right side is a tangent line with slope reduced by factor $c_1$, and the objective must lie below this line.
If $\phi$ is differentiable and $\phi'(0) < 0$, then $\phi(\alpha) = \phi(0) + \alpha\phi'(0) + o(\alpha)$, so the condition holds for all $\alpha$ below some $\bar\alpha > 0$.

Backtracking starts from initial step length $\alpha_0$ and repeatedly multiplies by reduction factor $\rho \in (0, 1)$, giving $\alpha \leftarrow \rho\alpha$, until the condition holds.
The existence of $\bar\alpha$ above means backtracking terminates finitely and accepts $\alpha$ at least $\min(\alpha_0, \rho\bar\alpha)$.
For the quadratic function $\phi(\alpha) = \phi(0) + \alpha\phi'(0) + \tfrac12\alpha^2\phi''$ ($\phi'' > 0$), use its line minimizer $\alpha^* = -\phi'(0)/\phi''$ to express the exact Armijo acceptance interval as

$$
0 < \alpha \le 2(1 - c_1)\,\alpha^*
$$

.
At $c_1 = 10^{-4}$, this upper endpoint is nearly twice $\alpha^*$, and $\alpha$ near the endpoint barely decreases the objective.

The `BacktrackingLineSearch` method implements this backtracking.
Defaults are $\alpha_0 = 1$ (the natural step for Newton and quasi-Newton methods), $c_1 = 10^{-4}$, $\rho = 0.5$, minimum step $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ and 40 trials; change them with `new(alpha_init, alpha_min, c1, rho, max_iterations)`.
Each trial evaluates the objective once and does not compute a trial-point gradient.
If a trial point is unrepresentable, outside the objective domain or has a nonfinite objective, reject the trial and reduce $\alpha$.
Return an unaccepted result with `StepSizeLimit` if the next $\alpha$ is below the minimum step, or `IterationLimit` if the trial limit is reached.

## Wolfe and strong Wolfe conditions

The Armijo condition alone accepts very small $\alpha$, so iterations can make little progress.
Also, preserving positive definiteness in quasi-Newton BFGS updates requires position difference $\mathbf{s}_k = \alpha_k\mathbf{p}_k$ and gradient difference $\mathbf{y}_k = \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_{k+1}) - \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)$ to satisfy $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k > 0$.

Therefore add the curvature condition for constant $c_2 \in (c_1, 1)$:

$$
\phi'(\alpha) \ge c_2 \phi'(0)
$$


Together, the Armijo and curvature conditions are the Wolfe conditions.
The curvature condition seeks a point where the slope has become sufficiently shallower than its initial value.
The strong Wolfe conditions additionally bound the absolute slope, excluding points that have crossed the valley floor and climbed the opposite side.

$$
\phi(\alpha) \le \phi(0) + c_1 \alpha \phi'(0), \qquad |\phi'(\alpha)| \le c_2 |\phi'(0)|
$$

If $\phi$ is bounded below and $0 < c_1 < c_2 < 1$, an interval of $\alpha$ satisfying the strong Wolfe conditions exists (Nocedal–Wright, Lemma 3.1).
Under either curvature condition,

$$
\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k = \alpha_k (\phi'(\alpha_k) - \phi'(0)) \ge \alpha_k (c_2 - 1) \phi'(0) > 0
$$

holds.

The Wolfe conditions also imply the following global convergence result for the iteration.
If $f$ is bounded below and $\nabla f$ is Lipschitz continuous on an open set containing the level set $\{\mathbf{x} : f(\mathbf{x}) \le f(\mathbf{x}_0)\}$, iterations using descent directions and Wolfe steps satisfy the Zoutendijk condition:

$$
\sum_{k \ge 0} \cos^2\theta_k \, \lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)\rVert_2^2 < \infty
$$

(Nocedal–Wright, Theorem 3.2).
For directions with $\cos\theta_k$ bounded below by a positive constant (steepest descent, or directions from positive-definite matrices with bounded condition numbers), $\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)\rVert_2 \to 0$.
This establishes convergence to stationarity, not convergence to a minimum.

The `StrongWolfeLineSearch` method finds $\alpha$ satisfying the strong Wolfe conditions through bracketing and a zoom phase that shrinks the bracket.
Defaults are initial step 1, $c_1 = 10^{-4}$, $c_2 = 0.9$ and 40 trials; `for_nonlinear_cg` uses $c_2 = 0.1$.
Each trial evaluates the objective and gradient once each.
The procedure, safeguarded interpolation and failure reasons are explained in [Strong Wolfe bracketing and zoom](../strong-wolfe-zoom/).

## Line search API

The `search_into` method of trait `LineSearch` takes the objective, starting point $\mathbf{x}$, $f(\mathbf{x})$, direction $\mathbf{p}$, directional derivative $\phi'(0)$ and differentiation mode (`DerivativeStrategy`).
The accepted point is written to the caller's trial-point array; its gradient is written to the gradient array only if computed.
The candidate point and gradient arrays and finite-difference workspace `DerivativeWorkspace` store intermediate search values.
The final argument proposes the first trial step; built-in searches clamp it between minimum and initial step lengths, using the configured initial step when it is `None`.

The `LineSearchScope` type connects search counts to the enclosing solve.
In a scope converted from `ExecutionContext`, a search runs independently under that context's cancellation and deadline, and counts include only that search.
Within an optimization method, every trial counts toward its evaluation and iteration limits.
When implementing a custom `LineSearch`, call `propose` before constructing a trial point and `reject` after a rejected trial.

The `termination()` method of `LineSearchResult` returns `LineSearchTermination`, either `Accepted` or `Unsuccessful(LineSearchFailure)`.
The `LineSearchFailure` type distinguishes `IterationLimit`, `EvaluationLimit`, `StepSizeLimit` and `Stagnation`.
The `Display` method of `LineSearchTermination` returns the string `Accepted` or `Unsuccessful(reason)`; the reason is the `Display` representation of `LineSearchFailure` (for example `step-size limit reached`).
Even without acceptance, `has_trial()` is true if a best finite-objective trial exists, and `step()` and `value()` return that trial.
The `fun_evals()`, `grad_evals()` and `rejected_trials()` counts cover search evaluations and rejections, excluding the starting value and gradient supplied by the caller.

Directional derivative $\phi'(0) \ge 0$ (no descent) yields `InvalidInput`; nonfinite $f(\mathbf{x})$ or a nonfinite trial gradient yields a `NonFinite` error.
A nonfinite trial objective is treated as a rejected trial rather than an error.
If a search within an optimizer ends without acceptance, the optimizer stops with `OptimizeTermination::LineSearchFailed(reason)` and returns the last accepted point.
Line search acceptance alone does not mean the outer optimization has converged.

## Experiment in a narrow valley

For the narrow-valley quadratic $f(x, y) = \tfrac12(x^2 + \kappa y^2)$, choose a step from $(1, 1)$ along steepest descent direction $\mathbf{p} = -\nabla f = -(1, \kappa)$ using `BacktrackingLineSearch` ($c_1 = 10^{-4}$, $\rho = 0.5$ and 40 trials).
Along the direction, the function is $\phi(\alpha) = \tfrac12\{(1 - \alpha)^2 + \kappa(1 - \kappa\alpha)^2\}$ with $\phi'(0) = -(1 + \kappa^2)$, and its minimizer is $\alpha^* = \mathbf{g}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}/\mathbf{g}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g} = (1 + \kappa^2)/(1 + \kappa^3)$ ($\mathbf{A} = \operatorname{diag}(1, \kappa)$).
The values $\phi$, $\alpha^*$ and acceptance interval upper endpoint $2(1 - c_1)\alpha^*$ are independent comparisons computed from formulas.
Inputs vary curvature ratio $\kappa$ and initial step $\alpha_0$.
The plot shows $\phi(\alpha)$ and the Armijo upper bound; vertical lines show accepted $\alpha$, $\alpha^*$ and the acceptance interval upper endpoint.
The table gives termination reason, ratio of accepted $\alpha$ to $\alpha^*$, $\phi(\alpha)$, line minimum $\phi(\alpha^*)$, and rejected trial and objective evaluation counts.

```rust
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize, scioxide-differentiate]

//|       - {"artifact":"armijo","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":9}

//|       - {"artifact":"armijo","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"armijo","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-15}}

use scioxide_core::{Budget, ExecutionContext};
use scioxide_differentiate::{DerivativeOperation, DerivativeStrategy};
use scioxide_optimize::{BacktrackingLineSearch, DerivativeWorkspace, LineSearch, ObjectiveFn};

let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let kappa = condition_number;
let objective = ObjectiveFn::new(2, move |v: &[f64]| {
    Ok(0.5 * (v[0] * v[0] + kappa * v[1] * v[1]))
});
// Formula for comparison: closed form of φ(α), line minimizer α*, and acceptance interval upper endpoint 2(1 − c₁)α*.
let phi = |a: f64| {
    let (u, v) = (1.0 - a, 1.0 - kappa * a);
    0.5 * (u * u + kappa * v * v)
};
let exact = (1.0 + kappa * kappa) / (1.0 + kappa * kappa * kappa);
let upper = 2.0 * (1.0 - 1.0e-4) * exact;
// Starting point (1, 1), steepest descent direction p = −∇f = −(1, κ), φ(0) and φ′(0) = −(1 + κ²).
let (x, p) = ([1.0_f64, 1.0], [-1.0, -kappa]);
let (f0, slope) = (phi(0.0), -(1.0 + kappa * kappa));
let trials = Budget::try_from(40).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let search = BacktrackingLineSearch::new(initial_step, f64::EPSILON, 1.0e-4, 0.5, trials)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut derivatives =
    DerivativeWorkspace::new(DerivativeOperation::Gradient, 2, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut buffers = [[0.0_f64; 2]; 4];
let [trial, gradient, candidate, candidate_gradient] = &mut buffers;
let result = search
    .search_into(
        &objective,
        &x,
        f0,
        &p,
        slope,
        DerivativeStrategy::default(),
        trial,
        gradient,
        candidate,
        candidate_gradient,
        &mut derivatives,
        ExecutionContext::sequential().into(),
        None,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let step = result.has_trial().then(|| result.step());
let span = 1.25 * upper.max(step.unwrap_or(0.0));

    (0..=200)
        .map(|i| [span * i as f64 / 200.0, g(span * i as f64 / 200.0)])
        .collect()
};

        "yLabel": "Objective [-]",

    { "key": "1", "label": "Accepted α [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "α/α* [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "φ(α) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "φ(α*) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Rejected trials [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let row = (
    result.termination().to_string(),
    step,
    step.map(|a| a / exact),
    step.map(|_| result.value()),
    phi(exact),
    result.rejected_trials(),
    result.fun_evals(),

emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "armijo" })
);
let outcome = if result.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Accepted a step satisfying the Armijo condition")
} else if result.has_trial() {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped without acceptance and returned only the best trial")
} else {
    Outcome::failed("No trial with a finite objective")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(result.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Rejected trials", result.rejected_trials() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Acceptance interval upper endpoint", upper, None)

```

At the default $\kappa = 100$ and $\alpha_0 = 1$, six trials are rejected before accepting $\alpha = 2^{-6} = 0.015625$ (seven objective evaluations).
The line minimizer is $\alpha^* = 0.01000$; accepted $\alpha$ is $1.56\alpha^*$ and lies below the acceptance interval upper endpoint $0.02000$.
At $\kappa = 1000$, nine rejections precede acceptance of $\alpha = 2^{-9} \approx 1.95\alpha^*$, and $\phi$ falls only from 500.5 to 454.7.
Because $\phi(t\alpha^*) = \phi(0) - (2t - t^2)(\phi(0) - \phi(\alpha^*))$, $t \approx 2$ near the upper endpoint achieves less than one tenth of the decrease to the line minimum.
At $\kappa = 1$, the first $\alpha = 1$ is already the minimizer.
Starting from $\alpha_0 = 0.05$ accepts $\alpha = 0.0125$ after two rejections, giving $\phi(\alpha) = 3.61$ below the default 16.3.
Backtracking simply chooses the first point of the form $\alpha_0\rho^j$ inside the acceptance interval, so the accepted point depends on the initial step.
Transforming variables as $z_y = \sqrt{\kappa}\,y$ makes contours circular, and $\alpha = 1$ becomes the line minimizer as in the $\kappa = 1$ case.

## Summary

Choosing a step length is a univariate problem assuming descent direction $\nabla f^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p} < 0$.
The `BacktrackingLineSearch` method imposes only Armijo, halves $\alpha_0 = 1$ in single-evaluation trials and accepts the first satisfying point; avoiding gradient evaluations reduces computation, but points nearly twice as far as the minimizer can be accepted.
The `StrongWolfeLineSearch` method adds curvature conditions to preserve $\mathbf{s}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y} > 0$ and the Zoutendijk condition, evaluating the objective and gradient at each trial.
If accepted step lengths shrink to $O(1/\kappa)$, align variable scales before changing methods.
Read line search failure through the `LineSearchTermination` reason; inside an optimizer it is returned as `LineSearchFailed(reason)` with the last accepted point.
