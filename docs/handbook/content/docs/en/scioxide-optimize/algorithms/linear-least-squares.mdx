For observations $b\in\mathbb R^m$ and design matrix $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$, minimize $f(x)=\tfrac12\|Ax-b\|_2^2$.
The gradient is $g=A^T(Ax-b)$, and unconstrained stationarity gives the normal equations $A^TAx=A^Tb$.
This page covers unconstrained solution methods and conditioning, first-order conditions under nonnegative and box constraints, Lawson–Hanson NNLS and BVLS active set methods, and interpretation of SciOxide results.

## Conditioning of normal equations and QR

Even with full column rank, $\kappa_2(A^TA)=\kappa_2(A)^2$; forming normal equations and solving by Cholesky can therefore give relative solution error of order $\kappa_2(A)^2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$.
For $\kappa_2(A)=10^6$, errors around $10^{-4}$ are possible, easily losing information along small singular-value directions.
QR factorization $A=QR$ works directly with $A$ and solves $Rx=Q^Tb$ by a backward-stable method.
With $\theta$ the angle between $b$ and the column space of $A$, the relative sensitivity of the least squares solution itself is approximately proportional to $\kappa_2(A)+\kappa_2(A)^2\tan\theta$ (Golub–Van Loan, Section 5.3).
When residual $\|Ax^*-b\|$ is small and $\theta\approx0$, sensitivity is of order $\kappa_2(A)$; QR preserves this accuracy, while normal equations introduce error $\kappa_2(A)^2$.
For large-residual problems, the problem's own sensitivity contains $\kappa_2(A)^2\tan\theta$, narrowing the difference between the methods.
For rank deficiency, SVD $A=\sum_i\sigma_iu_iv_i^T$ separates directions determined by observations ($\sigma_i>\tau$) from undetermined directions, choosing the minimum-norm solution by setting undetermined components to zero.
Householder QR of a dense rectangular matrix costs about $2mn^2-\tfrac23n^3$ flops when $m\ge n$.

## Active sets and sign conditions

Under bounds $l\le x\le u$, the KKT conditions are $g=\lambda-\mu$, $\lambda,\mu\ge0$, $\lambda_i(x_i-l_i)=0$ and $\mu_i(u_i-x_i)=0$ (the same form as [First-order conditions for box constraints](../bound-constrained-lbfgsb/)).
At a lower bound $x_i=l_i$, only increases are allowed, so the optimal gradient must satisfy $g_i=\lambda_i\ge0$.
At an upper bound $x_i=u_i$, $g_i=-\mu_i\le0$; in the interior, $g_i=0$.
Because the objective is convex, these conditions are necessary and sufficient for a global minimum.
Clipping a solution alone ignores changes in optimal free components caused by correlated columns.

The set of components away from bounds is the passive set $\mathcal{P}$; components fixed at bounds form the active set $\mathcal{Z}$.
Fixing components $\mathcal{Z}$ at bound values leaves the unconstrained least squares problem $\min\|A_{\mathcal{P}}x_{\mathcal{P}}-(b-A_{\mathcal{Z}}x_{\mathcal{Z}})\|$ using only columns $\mathcal{P}$.
Once the correct $\mathcal{Z}$ is known, a single least squares solve suffices, so active set methods search for this set.

### Lawson–Hanson NNLS

For nonnegative constraints $x\ge0$, Lawson–Hanson starts from $x=0$ and $\mathcal{P}=\emptyset$ and repeats:

1. Compute dual variable $w=-g=A^T(b-Ax)$.
   Stop if $w_j\le0$ (that is, $g_j\ge0$) for every $j$ in $\mathcal{Z}$, since the KKT conditions then hold.
2. Move the $j\in\mathcal{Z}$ with largest $w_j$ into $\mathcal{P}$ (increasing this component decreases the objective fastest).
3. Solve unconstrained least squares on columns $\mathcal{P}$ to obtain $z$.
   If $z_{\mathcal{P}}>0$, set $x\leftarrow z$ and return to step 1.
4. Otherwise, find the fraction $\alpha=\min_{j\in\mathcal{P},\,z_j\le0}x_j/(x_j-z_j)$ along the segment from $x$ to $z$ at which a component first reaches zero, and set $x\leftarrow x+\alpha(z-x)$.
   Return zero components to $\mathcal{Z}$ and repeat step 3.

Each outer iteration strictly decreases the objective, and no passive set recurs, so termination is finite in exact arithmetic.
At termination, multipliers $\lambda_j=g_j\ge0$ of components $\mathcal{Z}$ are exactly the KKT multipliers.

### BVLS (lower and upper bounds)

Stark–Parker BVLS extends the same idea to $l\le x\le u$.
Active components are fixed at either lower or upper bounds; candidates for the passive set have $-g_j>0$ at lower bounds (increasing decreases the objective), or $g_j>0$ at upper bounds (decreasing decreases it).
The fraction in step 4 now ends when a passive component first reaches either its lower or upper bound.
Components with equal lower and upper bounds are fixed from the start, with no multiplier sign requirement.

Both `Nnls` and `BoundedLinearLeastSquares` take zero projected onto the bounds as the first accepted point, then start from the unconstrained solution clipped to the box.
This initial point adds many components to the passive set at once, avoiding one-column-at-a-time additions for problems with many positive components.
Subsequent iterations release the component with the largest dual feasibility violation (step 2) and resolve the passive subproblem.
Components hitting bounds during this solve return to the active set; when passive gradients are below threshold, return to component selection (step 1), even if the point did not move.
Components placed on bounds by initial clipping remain eligible for later release.
Only components that return to bounds immediately after release without moving the point are excluded until the point next moves.
Dense problems update QR factors (Householder for column addition, Givens for removal, at $O(mn)$ per column change).
If numerical rank is unresolved or the system is wide, switch to column-pivoted QR or SVD.
Success requires KKT residuals recomputed in original coordinates to meet tolerances; if no component can be added and KKT conditions fail, stop with `Stagnation`.

## Inputs and results

The `LinearLeastSquaresProblem` type borrows the coefficient matrix, right-hand side and bound information.
Both `from_dense` and `from_view` accept dense matrices and select direct QR/SVD methods; `new` accepts a `AdjointOperator` (an operator providing only $Av$ and $A^Tw$ products, including sparse matrices) and solves passive subproblems with LSMR.
Attach bounds with `with_bounds`; unspecified endpoints are unbounded.
The `Nnls` solver imposes $x\ge0$ implicitly, so problems with explicit bounds produce an input error.
For a single solve, one-shot functions `nnls(&a, &b)` and `bvls(&a, &b, &bounds)` construct dense problems, solve using default `Nnls` and `BoundedLinearLeastSquares`, and return unsuccessful termination as `Err`.

The `LinearLeastSquaresOptions` type configures:

- `with_tolerances`: stationarity, feasibility and complementarity tolerances.
  All default absolute and relative tolerances are $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$; relative scales are $\max(1,\max_i|x_i|)$ for feasibility, $\max(1,\ |f|/\max(1,\max_i|x_i|),\ \max_i|g_i|,\ \max_i\lambda_i,\ \max_i\mu_i)$ for stationarity and $\max(1,|f|)$ for complementarity.
- `with_rank_policy`: the numerical rank threshold for dense subproblems.
  The default `DimensionScaledEpsilon` threshold is the largest R diagonal magnitude or singular value multiplied by $\min(\max(m,n)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}},1/2)$.
- `with_max_iterations`: outer iteration limit (default 1000).
- `with_max_inner_iterations`: LSMR iteration limit per subproblem for operator problems (default 1000).

The `solve(&problem)` method returns a result in `Ok` even after unsuccessful termination; `x()` is then the last accepted feasible point.
Read success through `is_success()` (true only for `Converged`); `require_success()` converts unsuccessful termination to a structured error.
For repeated problems of the same shape, prepare `NnlsWorkspace` or `BoundedLinearLeastSquaresWorkspace` with `Nnls::workspace(&problem)` or `BoundedLinearLeastSquares::workspace(&problem)` and pass it to `solve_into`.
Workspace allocates all memory for one problem shape and operation kind (dense or operator) and does not grow; combined with preallocated output, the solve performs no allocation.
A differently shaped problem returns `DimensionMismatch`; a problem with a different solver or operation kind returns `InvalidInput`.

The main `LinearLeastSquaresResult` quantities are:

| Quantity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `x()`, `residual()`, `gradient()` | Solution, original residual $Ax-b$ and original gradient $A^T(Ax-b)$ |
| `activity()` | Each component's `LinearLeastSquaresActivity` (`Free`, `Lower`, `Upper` or `Fixed`). A component is on a bound if its endpoint distance is within the feasibility tolerance |
| `lower_multipliers()`, `upper_multipliers()` | Nonnegative multipliers formed as $\lambda_i=\max(g_i,0)$ and $\mu_i=\max(-g_i,0)$ for bound components |
| `kkt()` | Observed values of the four $\infty$ norms in `LinearLeastSquaresKkt` |
| `rank()` | Numerical rank of final passive columns (`None` for operator problems or termination at the initial projected zero) |
| `objective()`, `residual_norm()` | $\tfrac12\|Ax-b\|^2$ and $\|Ax-b\|$ |

The four `LinearLeastSquaresKkt` quantities are: `primal_feasibility`, maximum bound violation; `stationarity`, maximum $|g_i|$ among components without positive multipliers; `dual_feasibility`, largest negative multiplier magnitude (always zero with the construction above); and `complementarity`, largest product of multiplier and endpoint distance.
A bound component with the wrong gradient sign has zero multiplier, so its $|g_i|$ appears in the stationarity residual.
These are observed values computed in original coordinates, not interval-arithmetic proofs.
Residual $\|Ax-b\|$ measures fit to observations and is separate from optimality residuals.

## Nonnegative and bounded least squares

Adding $x\ge0$ or box constraints to $\min\|Ax-b\|^2/2$ means simply clipping the unconstrained solution afterward may not give an optimum.
The `Nnls` and `BoundedLinearLeastSquares` solvers handle free and bound components and check KKT conditions.

The next cell solves nonnegative least squares with $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $b = (-1, 2, 1)$ using `Nnls`.
The unconstrained solution is $x = (-1, 2)$ with $x_0 < 0$, so its clipped value $(0, 2)$ is not optimal.
Fixing $x_0 = 0$ at its bound and solving for the remainder gives $x_1 = (2 + 1)/2 = 1.5$, used as an independent check.
Output consists of a table of termination reason, solution, difference from the known solution, the gradient component for $x_0$ recomputed from $A$ and $b$, and state of $x_0$, plus a table of four KKT quantities, multipliers and iteration count.
The cell ends with an outcome determined from `is_success()`.

```rust
//| id: optimize-nonnegative-kkt
//| ref: optimize-nonnegative-kkt
//| title: "Check nonnegative least squares constraints and optimality"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solution","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solution","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solution","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.5,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solution","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.5,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solution","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"Lower"}
//|       - {"artifact":"kkt","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kkt","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kkt","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.5,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{LinearLeastSquaresProblem, Nnls};

let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let a = Matrix::from_vec(3, 2, vec![1.0_f64, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let b = [-1.0, 2.0, 1.0];
let problem = LinearLeastSquaresProblem::from_dense(&a, &b).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let result = Nnls::<f64>::default()
    .solve(&problem)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let x = result.x();
// Independent check: minimizing over x₁ with x₀ = 0 fixed gives x₁ = (2 + 1)/2 = 1.5.
// Recompute gradient component (Aᵀ(Ax − b))₀ = r₀ + r₂ from A and b (the bound multiplier).
let residual: Vec<f64> = (0..3)
    .map(|i| a.as_slice()[2 * i] * x[0] + a.as_slice()[2 * i + 1] * x[1] - b[i])
    .collect();
let gradient0 = residual[0] + residual[2];
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "x₀ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "x₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference from known solution [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "x₀ gradient component (check) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "x₀ state" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        result.termination().to_string(),
        x[0],
        x[1],
        x[0].abs().max((x[1] - 1.5).abs()),
        gradient0,
        format!("{:?}", result.activity()[0])
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "solution" })
);
let kkt = result.kkt();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Stationarity [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Primal feasibility [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Dual feasibility [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Complementarity [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Lower bound multiplier λ₀ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        kkt.stationarity(),
        kkt.primal_feasibility(),
        kkt.dual_feasibility(),
        kkt.complementarity(),
        result.lower_multipliers()[0],
        result.iterations()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "kkt" })
);
let outcome = if result.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("KKT conditions recomputed in original coordinates met tolerances")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped before satisfying KKT conditions")
        .explanation("The returned point is the last accepted feasible point; residuals show it does not satisfy stationarity.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(result.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Stationarity", kkt.stationarity(), None)
        .diagnostic("Iterations", result.iterations() as f64, None)
);
```

The `Nnls` method reaches `Converged` in two iterations and returns $x = (0, 1.5)$ with zero difference from the known solution.
The gradient component of bound variable $x_0$ is positive at 1.5, so increasing $x_0$ increases the objective and staying on the bound is optimal.
This value is the lower bound multiplier $\lambda_0=1.5$; stationarity, primal feasibility, dual feasibility and complementarity residuals are all zero.

## Mixture proportions from absorption spectra

Consider matrix $A$ of four Gaussian absorption bands (centers 430, 470, 520 and 580 nm; widths 25–35 nm) at 40 wavelengths from 400 to 634 nm, and $b$ formed by adding measurement perturbations of amplitude 0.02 to the absorbance of mixture $x_{\mathrm{true}}=(0.8,0,0.5,1.2)$, which contains no second component.
Concentrations cannot be negative; input $u$ is a common upper bound for every component ($0\le x\le u$).
Solve the same $A$ and $b$ without bounds (QR least squares when `BoundedLinearLeastSquares` has no bounds), with nonnegativity alone using `Nnls`, and with a box using `BoundedLinearLeastSquares`.
The first table gives three solutions for each component, BVLS states, gradients and multipliers; the second gives BVLS termination, iterations, residual, KKT quantities, maximum sign-condition violation recomputed from $A$ and $b$, and numerical rank of free columns.
The cell ends with an outcome determined from BVLS `is_success()`.

At the default $u=1$, the unconstrained solution has negative second component $-0.0009$; `Nnls` sets it to zero and returns $(0.801, 0, 0.500, 1.200)$.
BVLS fixes component 4 at upper bound 1 and component 2 at lower bound 0, causing free component 3 to rise to 0.587.
The second component's gradient is $+0.187$ (its lower bound multiplier), and the fourth's is $-1.765$ (upper bound multiplier 1.765); both satisfy sign conditions, reaching `Converged` in two iterations.
Free-component gradients are of order $10^{-15}$, as is the independently checked sign-condition violation.
At $u=2$, no components touch upper bounds and BVLS returns the same solution as `Nnls`.
Both share an active set implementation, so this agreement is not independent; the independent check uses sign conditions recomputed from $A$ and $b$.

At $u=0.7$ and $u=0.6$, components 1, 3 and 4 all stop at upper bounds, leaving only component 2, absent from the true mixture, free.
Component 2 takes positive values 0.0048 at $u=0.7$ and 0.104 at $u=0.6$, accounting for part of the absorbance of the three capped components.
At $u=0.6$, their gradients $-0.929$, $-0.878$ and $-5.763$ satisfy upper bound sign conditions; BVLS reaches `Converged` in four iterations and the residual grows to 1.888.
The checked sign-condition violation is also of order $10^{-15}$, so this point is optimal under the box constraints.
However, component 2 becomes positive because of the imposed upper bounds; it does not establish its presence in the sample.
Upper bound multipliers describe the objective decrease rate when a bound is relaxed, so component 4's upper bound, with the largest multiplier (5.763), restricts the fit most strongly.

## Summary

Solve unconstrained linear least squares by QR without forming normal equations, and select an SVD minimum-norm solution for rank deficiency; even with small residuals, accuracy depends on problem conditioning rather than $\kappa_2(A)^2$.
Under nonnegative or box constraints, sign conditions $g_i\ge0$ at lower bounds, $g_i\le0$ at upper bounds and $g_i=0$ in the interior are necessary and sufficient for optimality; clipping the unconstrained solution may violate them.
The active set methods `Nnls` (Lawson–Hanson) and `BoundedLinearLeastSquares` (BVLS) change passive components one at a time, recomputing multipliers `lower_multipliers()` and `upper_multipliers()` and the four `kkt()` quantities in original coordinates before returning `Converged`.
Judge success with `is_success()`; interpret results stopped by `Stagnation` or `IterationLimit` as the last accepted feasible point and use `activity()` and stationarity residuals to identify violating components.
For sparse matrices or operator-only problems, select the LSMR path with `LinearLeastSquaresProblem::new`; for repeated solves, pass workspace to `solve_into`.
