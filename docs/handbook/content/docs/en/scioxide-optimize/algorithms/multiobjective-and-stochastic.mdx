When minimizing multiple objectives $f(x)=(f_1(x),\dots,f_M(x))$ simultaneously, generally no point minimizes every objective at once.
Improving one objective worsens another, creating a trade-off; results are presented as a set of candidates rather than a single point.
This page explains candidate comparison (Pareto dominance), approximation quality (hypervolume) and population-based front approximation with NSGA-II.
Sampling observations for noisy objective evaluations and stochastic first-order methods for a single noisy objective are covered in [Stochastic gradient descent and stochastic trust-region methods](../stochastic-gradient-and-trust-region/).

## Pareto dominance

For objective vectors to minimize, $x$ dominates $y$ if $f_i(x)\le f_i(y)$ in every component and $f_i(x)<f_i(y)$ in at least one.
Mutually nondominating candidates cannot be ranked without deciding the importance of objectives.
A point dominated by no other point is Pareto optimal; such points form the Pareto set, whose image in objective space is the Pareto front.
The `pareto_dominance(a, b)` function compares two objective vectors without tolerance and returns one of the `ParetoDominance` values `Dominates` ($a$ dominates), `Dominated` ($b$ dominates), `Equal` (all components equal, with equal $-0$ and $+0$), or `Incomparable` (mutually nondominating).
Empty vectors, unequal lengths and nonfinite components yield `InvalidInput`.

Minimizing the weighted sum $\sum_iw_if_i$ as a single objective can obtain only points on the front's convex hull.
Population-based front approximation shows the full trade-off even when the front has concave portions.
Measure approximation quality by the area dominated by candidates up to reference point $r$ (two-objective hypervolume).
Any point dominated by a candidate is also dominated by some Pareto-front point, so the true front's hypervolume is an upper bound; hypervolume grows with both proximity to the front and coverage.

The `ParetoArchive::new(dimension, objectives, capacity)` type is an archive retaining at most `capacity` nondominated feasible candidates.
The `insert` method retains only feasible candidates neither dominated by nor equal to stored points, removing points dominated by the new candidate.
On exceeding capacity, discard the candidate with smallest crowding distance (next section); read the discard count with `pruned()`.
The `ParetoArchive::hypervolume_2d(reference)` method computes two-objective archive hypervolume in $O(n^2)$ ($n$ stored points), returning `InvalidInput` if any point has a component worse than the reference point.
The `verify()` method independently checks that retained candidates are feasible and mutually nondominating.
The `entries()` method returns stored points as `ParetoEntry` (borrowing points, objectives, constraint values, violations, replicate counts and standard errors).
A capacity-limited archive does not enumerate the entire Pareto set.

## NSGA-II generations

NSGA-II (Deb et al., 2002) ranks candidates into nondominated layers and maintains objective-space spread within each layer using crowding distance.
Create the initial population with `StartDesign` (default uniform box sampling); each generation proceeds as follows.

1. Select two parents by tournaments comparing randomly selected pairs from the population.
   Lower rank wins; ties favor larger crowding distance, then earlier creation.
2. Normalize coordinates to $[0,1]$ and apply simulated binary crossover (SBX, distribution index 15) with probability 0.9.
   When crossing over, each coordinate uses the crossover value with probability 1/2; other coordinates copy the first parent.
   Then apply polynomial mutation (distribution index 20) to each coordinate with probability $1/d$ ($d$ variables).
   Repeat $P$ times to produce $P$ offspring.
3. Nondominated-sort the $2P$ parents and offspring into layers and compute crowding distances within each layer.
4. Retain $P$ candidates in layer order, selecting candidates from the last, partially retained layer by decreasing crowding distance (elitism).

Crowding distance sums, over objectives, the difference between a point's two neighbors within its layer after sorting by objective $m$, divided by that objective's range; endpoints have infinite distance.
With constraints, use Deb's constrained dominance: feasible points beat infeasible ones, and among infeasible points the smaller sum of constraint violations wins.
Every evaluated point is submitted to the archive, which retains feasible nondominated points.
Nondominated sorting uses $O(M(2P)^2)$ comparisons per generation; workspace also grows quadratically with population size.

The `Nsga2Problem::new(objective, bounds)` constructor borrows `SampledObjective` and box `FiniteBounds`, returning `InvalidInput` for zero objectives or a dimension differing from the box.
Add constraints with `with_constraints` and a sampler with `with_sampler`.
The `Nsga2Options::new(population, archive, generations)` constructor is infallible; `Nsga2::new` rejects population sizes below four, zero archive capacity, probabilities outside $[0,1]$ and nonpositive or nonfinite distribution indices with `InvalidInput`.
Defaults are one objective observation, seed 42, crossover and mutation values above, default feasibility tolerances and sequential execution; change them with `with_replicates`, `with_seed`, `with_variation` and `with_feasibility`.
For noisy objectives, `with_replicates` averages observations at each point, but dominance uses sample means without accounting for standard errors.
Sampled objective contracts are collected in [Sampled objectives and gradients](oxref:optimize-sampled-objective).

The `Nsga2Result` result returns `population()` (final population, including infeasible points), `archive()` (feasible nondominated candidates found over all generations), `least_violating()` (minimum total violation point) and `generations()`.
Pass `Nsga2Workspace` to `solve_into` to reuse workspace, and continue with `resume_into` from a `Nsga2Checkpoint` saved by `checkpoint()`.
The `Nsga2WorkspaceRequirements` returned by `workspace_requirements(&problem)` stores variable, objective, equality and inequality constraint counts and population size; workspace holds twice as many candidates for parents and offspring together.
The `Nsga2Workspace` workspace records the preceding call; read completed generations `generations()` and archive size `archived()` from the `Nsga2Progress` returned by `progress()` of `last_attempt()`.
Reaching the generation limit yields `SearchBudgetCompleted`; NSGA-II never returns success.
This termination does not mean the true Pareto front has been found, nor guarantee Pareto optimality of archive points.

## Comparison with an analytical front

For two-variable ZDT1, set $f_1=x_0$, $g=1+9x_1$ and $f_2=g(1-\sqrt{x_0/g})$ ($x\in[0,1]^2$).
Fixing $f_1$ makes $f_2=g-\sqrt{f_1g}$ increase with $g\ge1$ ($\partial f_2/\partial g=1-\sqrt{f_1}/(2\sqrt g)>0$), so $g=1$, equivalently $x_1=0$, gives analytical front $f_2=1-\sqrt{f_1}$.
For reference point $r=(1.1,1.1)$, analytical-front hypervolume sums $\int_0^1(1.1-f_2)\,df_1=0.1+\tfrac23$ over $0\le f_1\le1$ and strip $0.1\times1.1$ over $1\le f_1\le1.1$ dominated by $(1,0)$, giving $0.87667$.

The next cell uses population 40 and archive capacity 80.
Inputs are generation count and seed.
The objective-space plot overlays the analytical front (dashed) and final population (points).
The table gives archive candidate count, count outside the reference point, hypervolume and analytical value, mean and maximum population vertical gap $f_2-(1-\sqrt{f_1})$, and termination reason.
Hypervolume is undefined when candidates remain outside the reference point, so leave it blank in that case.
Finally, report the termination status as the outcome.

```rust
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":80}
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0.865,"max":0.8767}}
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.876667,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":"SearchBudgetCompleted"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: long-run
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0.865,"max":0.8767}}
//|   - case: short-run
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":1,"max":80}}
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":null}
//|   - case: seed-1
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"front","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0.865,"max":0.8767}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    FiniteBounds, NoiseModel, Nsga2, Nsga2Options, Nsga2Problem, SampledObjective,
};
struct Zdt;
impl SampledObjective<f64> for Zdt {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn objectives(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn noise_model(&self) -> NoiseModel {
        NoiseModel::Deterministic
    }
    fn sample_into(&self, x: &[f64], _: u64, out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        let g = 1.0 + 9.0 * x[1];
        out[0] = x[0];
        out[1] = g * (1.0 - RealScalar::sqrt(x[0] / g));
        Ok(())
    }
}
let bounds = FiniteBounds::new(vec![(0.0, 1.0); 2]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = Nsga2Problem::new(&Zdt, &bounds).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = Nsga2Options::new(
    40,
    80,
    Budget::try_from(generations as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
)
.with_seed(seed as u64);
let result = Nsga2::new(options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .solve(&problem)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let points: Vec<_> = result
    .population()
    .filter(|p| p.is_feasible())
    .map(|p| [p.objectives()[0], p.objectives()[1]])
    .collect();
// Reference curve: analytical front f₂ = 1 − √f₁ (independent formula using an inherent f64 method).
let exact: Vec<_> = (0..=100)
    .map(|i| {
        let x = i as f64 / 100.0;
        [x, 1.0 - x.sqrt()]
    })
    .collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "xLabel": "Objective f₁ [-]",
    "yLabel": "Objective f₂ [-]",
    "layers": [
        {"id": "front", "kind": "line", "name": "Analytical front", "points": exact, "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}},
        {"id": "population", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Final population", "points": points}
    ]
}));
// Analytical-front hypervolume (reference point (1.1, 1.1)): 0.1 + 2/3 + 0.11.
let exact_volume = 0.1 + 2.0 / 3.0 + 0.11;
// Define hypervolume only when all candidates lie inside the reference point.
let outside = result
    .archive()
    .entries()
    .filter(|e| e.objectives()[0] > 1.1 || e.objectives()[1] > 1.1)
    .count();
let volume = if outside == 0 {
    Some(
        result
            .archive()
            .hypervolume_2d([1.1, 1.1])
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    )
} else {
    None
};
// Independent check: vertical gap from the analytical front (formula using an inherent f64 method).
let gaps: Vec<f64> = points.iter().map(|p| p[1] - (1.0 - p[0].sqrt())).collect();
let worst = gaps.iter().copied().fold(0.0, f64::max);
let mean = gaps.iter().sum::<f64>() / gaps.len() as f64;
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed5 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 5 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Archive candidates [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Outside reference point [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Hypervolume [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Analytical-front hypervolume [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Mean population vertical gap [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Maximum population vertical gap [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        result.archive().len(),
        outside,
        volume,
        exact_volume,
        mean,
        worst,
        result.termination().to_string()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "front" })
);
// NSGA-II never returns success; generation-limit termination is partial.
let mut outcome =
    Outcome::partial("Searched to the generation limit and returned the archive as an approximate Pareto front")
        .explanation("Archive points are not guaranteed Pareto optimal.")
        .status(result.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Generation", result.generations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Archive candidates", result.archive().len() as f64, None);
if let Some(v) = volume {
    outcome = outcome.diagnostic("Hypervolume shortfall", exact_volume - v, None);
}
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

At the default 40 generations and seed 7, the archive fills its capacity of 80 candidates; hypervolume is 0.87060, with shortfall 0.0061 from analytical value 0.87667.
Final-population vertical gaps have mean $4.1\times10^{-4}$ and maximum 0.014; the plotted points follow the dashed front across all of $f_1\in[0,1]$.
Integrating the front gives the area lost by a staircase of 80 points placed on the analytical front: 0.0066 for equally spaced $f_1$, or 0.0057 for equal spacing in the sum of normalized changes in both objectives.
The observed shortfall 0.0061 lies within this range, so finite point count explains most of the hypervolume shortfall.
Even 100 generations reduce the shortfall only to 0.0059; at 10 generations, candidates outside the reference point prevent hypervolume calculation.
Seed 1 gives hypervolume 0.87002, differing from seed 7 by about $6\times10^{-4}$.
The outcome is partial because termination is due to the generation limit.
The vertical gap is not Euclidean distance to the front.
Even near-front candidates do not fully show trade-offs if concentrated on only part of it.

## Summary

Multiobjective optimization presents trade-offs as candidate sets; hypervolume against an analytical or known front measures approximation quality.
NSGA-II hypervolume shortfalls include the finite-point staircase approximation, so check archive capacity and candidate distribution before increasing generations.
NSGA-II stops with unsuccessful `SearchBudgetCompleted` even after reaching the generation limit and never returns success; interpret archive points as an approximate Pareto front and check with another method or a known front.
For constrained problems with an empty archive, inspect the least-violating point through `least_violating()`.
