Newton's method approximates the objective by a quadratic and obtains a search direction from that model.
Examining the range over which the model is accurate and the sign of its curvature rejects unsuitable trials while retaining fast local convergence.
Following [symbols and terminology](../../../reference/), this page writes the true Hessian as $\mathbf{H}_k=\nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}_k)$ ($\mathbf{H}_k$ on the [quasi-Newton page](../first-order-and-quasi-newton/) approximates the inverse Hessian and has a different meaning).
Assume that $f$ is twice continuously differentiable, and obtain its Hessian analytically, by automatic differentiation, or by differences of the gradient.
This page explains `NewtonOptimizer`, which handles indefinite curvature with line search, and the default dogleg method of `TrustRegionOptimizer`, which bounds the step length first.
Solving the subproblem by truncated conjugate gradients is covered in [truncated conjugate gradients and negative curvature](../truncated-cg-trust-region/); second-order stopping to avoid stopping at a saddle point is covered in [second-order stopping and saddle points](../second-order-stopping-saddle/).

## Local convergence and breakdown of pure Newton's method

Approximate the objective near $\mathbf{x}_k$ by a second-order Taylor expansion.

$$
f(\mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{p}) \approx m_k(\mathbf{p}) = f(\mathbf{x}_k) + \mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{p}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{p}
$$

where $\mathbf{g}_k = \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)$ and $\mathbf{H}_k = \nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}_k)$.
The stationarity condition $\nabla_{\mathbf{p}} m_k(\mathbf{p}) = \mathbf{0}$ of the quadratic model $m_k(\mathbf{p})$ gives the Newton equation

$$
\mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{p}_k = -\mathbf{g}_k
$$


Assume that $\nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}^*)$ is positive definite at the local minimizer $\mathbf{x}^*$ and that the Hessian is Lipschitz continuous with constant $L$ near $\mathbf{x}^*$.
Then, when $\mathbf{x}_k$ is sufficiently close to $\mathbf{x}^*$, the error $\mathbf{e}_k = \mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}^*$ satisfies

$$
\|\mathbf{e}_{k+1}\| \le M \|\mathbf{e}_k\|^2
$$


Here $M$ is a constant proportional to $L\,\|\nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}^*)^{-1}\|$, and convergence is quadratic: the number of correct digits roughly doubles at each iteration (Nocedal–Wright, Theorem 3.5).

However, the Newton equation finds a stationary point of the quadratic model and does not assume that $\mathbf{H}_k$ is positive definite.
If $\mathbf{H}_k$ has negative eigenvalues, the quadratic model has a saddle point or local maximum, and the Newton direction $\mathbf{p}_k$ can increase the objective ($\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}_k > 0$).
Since $\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}_k=-\mathbf{p}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{p}_k$, the sign of the curvature along $\mathbf{p}_k$ determines whether the direction is a descent direction.
In one dimension, if $g<0$ and $H<0$, as at $x=0.25$ in the cell below, the Newton point $-g/H<0$ points toward an increase in the objective.

## Diagonal modification of the Hessian

For an indefinite or singular Hessian, solve the modified system obtained by adding the identity term $\lambda \mathbf{I}$ ($\lambda > 0$):

$$
(\mathbf{H}_k + \lambda \mathbf{I}) \mathbf{p}_k = -\mathbf{g}_k
$$


Taking $\lambda$ larger than the absolute value of the smallest eigenvalue makes $\mathbf{H}_k + \lambda \mathbf{I}$ positive definite, so the search direction is a descent direction ($\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}_k < 0$).

`NewtonOptimizer` normalizes the Hessian by dividing by its largest absolute entry $\eta$ and takes the symmetric part $(\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{H}^\mathsf{T})/(2\eta)$.
For this normalized matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$, it factors $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}+\lambda\mathbf{I}$ by symmetric indefinite LDLᵀ factorization, starting with the shift $\lambda=0$, and examines the inertia (the numbers of positive, negative, and zero eigenvalues).
If there are no negative or zero eigenvalues and the computed direction satisfies $\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}_k<0$, it uses that direction.
Otherwise it refactors with shifts increasing by factors of 10: $\lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$, $10\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$, $100\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$, … .
If no descent direction is found after 17 attempts including the unshifted system (maximum shift $10^{15}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}\approx1.5\times10^{7}$), it returns a `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`) error.
The absolute entries of the normalized matrix are at most 1, so Gershgorin's theorem bounds the absolute eigenvalues by $n$, and a shift greater than $n$ makes the matrix positive definite.
Thus, for $n<1.5\times10^7$, this range yields a direction apart from rounding effects.

The default step length uses Armijo backtracking ($c_1=10^{-4}$, contraction factor 0.5, at most 40 attempts); `NewtonOptions::with_line_search` selects another line search.
If no acceptable step exists, it stops with `LineSearchFailed`.
In `NewtonDiagnostics`, `inertia()` is the inertia of the unmodified (zero-shift) normalized Hessian examined at the last iteration, and `normalized_shift()` is the shift adopted at that iteration, in normalized units.
The shift in the original units is $\eta\lambda$.
Each iteration evaluates an $n\times n$ Hessian and performs one $O(n^3)$ LDLᵀ factorization for each shift attempted.

## Dogleg trust-region methods

A line search fixes a direction $\mathbf{p}_k$ and chooses a step length $\alpha_k$; a trust-region method first sets a radius $\Delta_k > 0$ within which the quadratic model $m_k(\mathbf{p})$ is trusted, then seeks a step minimizing the model in that closed ball.

$$
\min_{\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^n} m_k(\mathbf{p}) = f(\mathbf{x}_k) + \mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{p}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{p} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \|\mathbf{p}\|_2 \le \Delta_k
$$

The model matrix of `TrustRegionOptimizer` is the symmetrized Hessian $\mathbf{B}_k=(\mathbf{H}_k+\mathbf{H}_k^\mathsf{T})/2$.
The ball constraint ensures that the subproblem has a solution even if $\mathbf{B}_k$ is indefinite.

### Cauchy point and dogleg path

To avoid solving the subproblem exactly, Powell's dogleg method searches along the piecewise linear path joining the Cauchy point and the Newton point.
The Cauchy point minimizes the model within the radius along the steepest descent direction $-\mathbf{g}_k$.

$$
\mathbf{p}_k^\mathrm{C} = -\tau_k\frac{\Delta_k}{\|\mathbf{g}_k\|}\mathbf{g}_k,\qquad
\tau_k=\begin{cases}1 & (\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{g}_k\le0)\\ \min\!\left(\dfrac{\|\mathbf{g}_k\|^3}{\Delta_k\,\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{g}_k},1\right) & (\text{otherwise})\end{cases}
$$

When $\tau_k<1$, it coincides with the unconstrained one-dimensional minimizer $-(\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{g}_k/\mathbf{g}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{g}_k)\,\mathbf{g}_k$.
The dogleg path $\mathbf{p}(\tau)$ is the piecewise linear path from the origin through this unconstrained minimizer $\mathbf{p}^\mathrm{U}$ to the Newton point $\mathbf{p}^\mathrm{N}=-\mathbf{B}_k^{-1}\mathbf{g}_k$.

$$
\mathbf{p}(\tau) = \begin{cases} \tau \mathbf{p}^\mathrm{U} & (0 \le \tau \le 1) \\ \mathbf{p}^\mathrm{U} + (\tau - 1)(\mathbf{p}^\mathrm{N} - \mathbf{p}^\mathrm{U}) & (1 < \tau \le 2) \end{cases}
$$

If $\mathbf{B}_k$ is positive definite, $\|\mathbf{p}(\tau)\|_2$ increases monotonically with $\tau$ and the model value $m_k(\mathbf{p}(\tau))$ decreases monotonically, so the intersection of the path with the sphere is the best point along the path (Nocedal–Wright, Lemma 4.2).
`TrustRegionOptimizer` chooses the step in the following order.

1. Compute the Cholesky factorization of $\mathbf{B}_k$ and, if it succeeds, solve for the Newton point.
   If $\|\mathbf{p}^\mathrm{N}\|\le\Delta_k$, use it as the step.
2. If the factorization fails ($\mathbf{B}_k$ is singular or indefinite), or $\mathbf{p}^\mathrm{U}$ lies outside the radius, use the Cauchy point $\mathbf{p}_k^\mathrm{C}$ as the step.
3. Otherwise solve a quadratic equation for the intersection of the segment from $\mathbf{p}^\mathrm{U}$ to $\mathbf{p}^\mathrm{N}$ with the sphere.

Iterations that reject the trial and change only the radius reuse the Cholesky factorization and Newton point at the same location.

## Acceptance ratio and radius updates

After obtaining the trial step $\mathbf{p}_k$, compute the ratio of the actual function reduction to the reduction predicted by the model (the acceptance ratio).

$$
\rho_k = \frac{f(\mathbf{x}_k) - f(\mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{p}_k)}{m_k(\mathbf{0}) - m_k(\mathbf{p}_k)}
$$

The closer $\rho_k$ is to 1, the more accurate the model is within the radius; a negative value means that the objective increased.
Step acceptance and radius updates follow separate rules; `TrustRegionOptimizer` uses the rules below (“boundary” means a step satisfying $\|\mathbf{p}_k\|=\Delta_k$).

| Condition | Step | Radius $\Delta_{k+1}$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Predicted reduction $m_k(\mathbf{0}) - m_k(\mathbf{p}_k) \le 0$ | Reject without evaluating the trial | $\frac{1}{4}\Delta_k$ |
| Trial point cannot be represented in floating point, or objective is NaN or infinite | Reject | $\frac{1}{4}\Delta_k$ |
| $\rho_k \le 0.15$, or objective does not strictly decrease | Reject | $\frac{1}{4}\Delta_k$ because $\rho_k < 0.25$ |
| $0.15 < \rho_k < 0.25$ and objective decreases | Accept | $\frac{1}{4}\Delta_k$ |
| $\rho_k \ge 0.25$ and the following row does not apply | Accept | Retain $\Delta_k$ |
| $\rho_k > 0.75$ and a boundary step | Accept | $\min(2\Delta_k, 10^8)$ |

The initial radius is the norm $\|\mathbf{g}_0\|_2$ of the initial gradient (1 if zero, or $\max(\|\mathbf{g}_0\|_2,1)$ when [second-order stopping](oxref:optimize-trust-second-order) is enabled), with upper bound $10^8$.
The initial radius and the radius update factors are not configurable.
Before each iteration, a gradient 2-norm at most $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}+\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\max(1,|f|/\max(1,\|\mathbf{x}\|_\infty))$ stops with `GradientSmall`; if the radius falls below $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$, the model prediction is considered unusable and the method stops with `Stagnation`.
`GradientSmall` is a first-order stopping criterion, not a proof of a local minimum.

## Experiment with negative curvature and the trust region

Use the double well $f(x) = \frac{1}{4}x^4 - \frac{\mu}{2}x^2$ ($x = 0$ is a local maximum and $x = \pm\sqrt{\mu}$ are local minima with $f = -\mu^2/4$).
The first half computes and plots the quadratic model at the current position $x$, the unconstrained Newton point, the step minimizing the model on the interval $|s| \le \Delta$ of radius $\Delta$, and the acceptance ratio from their formulas.
The second half runs `TrustRegionOptimizer` and `NewtonOptimizer` from the same $x$ and compares their final points with the known local minima.
It also runs `NewtonOptimizer` with an iteration limit of 1 and reads the first Hessian's inertia and diagonal shift from `NewtonDiagnostics`.
The solver's trust radius starts from the norm of the initial gradient; the input $\Delta$ is used only in the model in the first half.
Outputs comprise the plot; a table of model quantities from the first half (gradient, curvature, Newton point, chosen step, predicted and actual reduction, acceptance ratio); a table of both solvers' results; and a table of the first iteration's inertia and shift.

At the defaults $\mu=1$ and $x=0.25$, the curvature $H=-0.8125$ is negative, and the unconstrained Newton displacement −0.2885 points toward an objective increase (toward the local maximum $x=0$).
The trust-region model of radius 0.4 selects the positive endpoint $s=0.4$; the predicted reduction is 0.1588, the actual reduction 0.1364, and the acceptance ratio 0.859.
This ratio exceeds 0.75 and the step lies on the boundary, so the solver's rules would accept the step and double the radius.
Both solvers reach $x=1$ in 5 iterations and 6 objective evaluations, terminate with `GradientSmall`, and differ from the local minimum $\sqrt{\mu}=1$ by about $10^{-9}$.
The modified Newton method's first diagnostics show one negative eigenvalue.
In one dimension the normalized matrix is $\tilde H=-1$, so shifts increase from $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$ by factors of 10, and the first value $10^8\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}\approx1.490$ satisfying $\tilde H+\lambda>0$ is adopted.
The shift in original units is $1.490\times0.8125=1.211$; Armijo backtracking accepts the direction $-g/(H+1.211)=0.589$ at $\alpha=1$, giving a point of approximately 0.8386 after 1 iteration.
With $\Delta=1.5$, the model predicts a reduction of 1.27 at endpoint $s=1.5$, but the objective actually increases by 0.84; the acceptance ratio −0.67 therefore corresponds to rejection of this trial.
At $x=1.5$ the curvature is positive and the Newton point lies within the radius, so the shift is 0.
Vary the radius and initial value to distinguish predicted reduction from actual reduction.

## Options and workspace

The defaults of `NewtonOptions` are gradient tolerance $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$ (absolute and relative), iteration limit 1000, derivative acquisition `AnalyticOrCentral` (central differences if analytic derivatives are unavailable), no time limit, and Armijo backtracking line search.
`TrustRegionOptions` configures the gradient tolerance, iteration limit (default 1000), derivative acquisition (default `AnalyticOrForward`, forward differences if analytic derivatives are unavailable), time limit, and subproblem method (default `TrustRegionSubproblem::Dogleg`).
Both reject objectives with bounds (`BoundedObjective`) using `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).
A starting-point length different from the dimension gives `DimensionMismatch`; NaN or infinity in the start, initial objective, or initial gradient gives `NonFinite`.

Among termination reasons, `GradientSmall` and `Converged` returned by second-order stopping indicate success.
`IterationLimit`, a time limit, and `Stagnation` return the last accepted point as an estimate; `LineSearchFailed` (`NewtonOptimizer` only) means that no acceptable step was found.

`NewtonOptimizerWorkspace` and `NewtonOptimizerWorkspaceRequirements` prepare storage for Newton Hessians, factorizations, and evaluations.
Check capacity with `TrustRegionOptimizerWorkspaceRequirements` and reuse `TrustRegionOptimizerWorkspace`.
Dogleg uses $O(n^2)$ memory and an $O(n^3)$ Cholesky factorization per iteration.
`TrustRegionWorkspaceRequest` indicates either a workspace containing a dense Hessian (`dense(n)`) or a workspace tailored to the objective (`of(&f)`).
The workspace using only Hessian–vector products is explained in [truncated conjugate gradients and negative curvature](../truncated-cg-trust-region/).

## Summary

The Newton direction is a descent direction under positive definite curvature and converges quadratically near a local minimum, but an indefinite Hessian can make it point toward an objective increase.
`NewtonOptimizer` constructs a descent direction by trying up to 17 shifts of the normalized Hessian: zero and shifts increasing from $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$ by factors of 10; it records the unmodified inertia and adopted shift in `NewtonDiagnostics`.
`TrustRegionOptimizer` accepts a trial when the acceptance ratio satisfies $\rho>0.15$ and the objective strictly decreases; it quarters the radius when $\rho<0.25$ and doubles it for a boundary step when $\rho>0.75$.
For an indefinite model, dogleg falls back to the Cauchy point; if repeated rejection brings the radius below $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$, it stops with `Stagnation`.
For both methods, `GradientSmall` is a first-order stopping criterion; curvature must be checked separately to establish a local minimum.
