An interior-point method handles inequalities by introducing slack variables and adding a logarithmic barrier to the objective that diverges as the slacks approach 0.
Rather than estimating an active set as in [sequential quadratic programming](../sqp-and-interior-point/), it tracks solutions while bringing the barrier parameter toward 0.
Notation and residual definitions follow [nonlinear constraints and KKT conditions](../nonlinear-constraints/).

## Logarithmic barrier and central path

Introduce slack variables $s_j = -g_j(\mathbf{x}) > 0$ for inequalities $g_j(\mathbf{x}) \le 0$ and consider the logarithmic barrier problem with barrier parameter $\mu > 0$:

$$
\min_{\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{s}} \; f(\mathbf{x}) - \mu \sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}} \log s_j \quad \text{subject to} \quad c_i(\mathbf{x}) = 0\ (i\in\mathcal{E}),\quad g_j(\mathbf{x}) + s_j = 0\ (j\in\mathcal{I})
$$


As the boundary $s_j \to 0$ approaches, $-\log s_j$ diverges to $+\infty$, keeping the slacks positive.
Writing the inequality multipliers as $z_j$, the stationarity conditions for this problem relax KKT complementarity $z_j s_j = 0$ to

$$
z_j s_j = \mu, \qquad z_j > 0, \; s_j > 0
$$


The curve traced by solutions $(\mathbf{x}(\mu), \mathbf{s}(\mu), \mathbf{z}(\mu))$ as $\mu$ approaches 0 is called the central path.
A primal-dual interior-point method applies Newton's method to the relaxed KKT system containing primal variables, slacks, and multipliers, and decreases $\mu$ while taking steps that keep slacks and multipliers positive.
Variable bounds also receive slacks as inequalities $l_k-x_k\le0$ and $x_k-u_k\le0$.

## Primal-dual Newton system and inertia

Write $\mathbf{S}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{s})$ and $\mathbf{Z}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{z})$, and denote the Lagrangian Hessian (or its approximation) by $\mathbf{W}$.
$\mathbf{J}_c$ and $\mathbf{J}_g$ are the Jacobians of equalities and inequalities (including bounds), and $\mathbf{e}$ is the vector of all ones.
Eliminating the slack correction $\Delta\mathbf{s}$ from the Newton equations gives the symmetric KKT system

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{W} & \mathbf{J}_c^\mathsf{T} & \mathbf{J}_g^\mathsf{T} \\ \mathbf{J}_c & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{J}_g & \mathbf{0} & -\mathbf{Z}^{-1}\mathbf{S} \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \Delta\mathbf{x} \\ \Delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} \\ \Delta\mathbf{z} \end{bmatrix}
= -\begin{bmatrix} \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{L} \\ \mathbf{c} \\ \mathbf{g}+\mu\mathbf{Z}^{-1}\mathbf{e} \end{bmatrix}
$$

and recover the slack correction using $\Delta\mathbf{s}=\mu\mathbf{Z}^{-1}\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{Z}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\Delta\mathbf{z}$.
The coefficient matrix has order $n+m+b$ ($n$ counts variables, $m$ nonlinear rows, and $b$ finite bound faces of nonfixed variables).

If $\mathbf{W}$ is positive definite on the constraint tangent space and the constraint Jacobian has full row rank, this matrix has inertia $(n,\ m+b,\ 0)$ (numbers of positive, negative, and zero eigenvalues).
With different inertia, the resulting $\Delta\mathbf{x}$ need not be a descent direction.
The method therefore adds a positive value $\delta_w$ to the diagonal of $\mathbf{W}$ and refactors.
Dependent equality rows introduce zero eigenvalues, in which case a small negative diagonal $-\delta_c$ is added to the equality block as well.

For `InteriorPoint`, $\delta_w$ starts at $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}\max(1,\max_{ij}|W_{ij}|)$ and increases by a factor of 10 each time, with at most 16 factorizations per iteration.
$\delta_c$ is $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}\max(\mu,\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}})$.
Read the last values from `primal_regularization()` and `dual_regularization()` of `InteriorPointDiagnostics`, and the number of refactorizations from `regularization_attempts()`.
Regularization is used only to compute the direction; KKT residuals for termination use the original equations without regularization.

The step is limited to at most 0.995 times the distance to the boundary so that slacks, inequality multipliers, and distances to bounds remain positive (the fraction-to-boundary rule).
For example, if the correction of slack $s_j$ is negative, the maximum step length is $\alpha\le-0.995\,s_j/\Delta s_j$.

## InteriorPoint iterations

`InteriorPoint` combines the nonlinear primal-dual interior-point method above with line search and a restoration phase.
The objective and constraints are normalized using derivatives at the initial point, as explained in [KKT residuals and termination](oxref:optimize-kkt-residuals).

- Equalities, nonlinear inequalities, and variable bounds are supplied in one `Constraints`.
  Variables with equal lower and upper bounds are fixed variables and receive no slacks.
  The initial point is moved strictly inside the box, and subsequent trial points also remain inside the bounds of nonfixed variables.
- Initial slacks are $\max(1,-g_j(\mathbf{x}_0))$ for nonlinear inequalities and distances to bounds for variable bounds; initial multipliers are $z_j=\mu/s_j$.
- The initial point need not be feasible.
  When constraint violation exceeds the threshold, a bounded restoration phase first reduces the violation.
  Restoration solves a linearized least-squares problem for equalities and violated inequalities.
  Its step stops at half the distance to each bound to avoid starting barrier iterations with slacks pressed against the boundary.
- The barrier parameter starts at $\mu=0.1$ when there are inequalities or finite bounds (otherwise 0).
  At the beginning of each iteration, if the barrier problem error (the maximum of stationarity, $|c_i|$, $|g_j+s_j|$, and $|s_jz_j-\mu|$) is at most $10\mu$, $\mu$ is multiplied by 0.2.
  Its lower bound is 0.1 times the minimum of the complementarity, stationarity, and primal-feasibility thresholds of `OptimalityTolerances` (reference scale 1), approximately $3.0\times10^{-9}$ by default.
  This parameter is readable through `barrier_parameter()` of `OptimizeDiagnostics::InteriorPoint` but has no user-configurable input.
- The step length is chosen by Armijo backtracking on the merit function $f-\mu\sum_j\log s_j+\nu\sum_i|r_i|$ ($r_i$ contains $c_i$ for equalities and $g_j+s_j$ for inequalities), starting from the fraction-to-boundary upper bound.
  The penalty parameter $\nu$ starts at 2 and increases to stay at least 1 plus the largest absolute multiplier.
  If the directional derivative along the search direction is not negative, $\nu$ increases further by factors of 10 (at most 8 times).
  When the first trial is rejected, one second-order correction using the same factorization is attempted.
- Failure to obtain a direction or accept a step triggers restoration.
  Successful restoration reinitializes slacks by the initial-value rule and multipliers by $z_j=\mu/s_j$, and resets the approximate Hessian to the identity.
  If the point where restoration begins is already feasible, or violation cannot be reduced, the method stops with `Stagnation`.
- Lagrangian curvature is approximated by damped BFGS by default.
  Supplying `LagrangianHessian` through `ConstrainedProblem::with_lagrangian_hessian` enables the exact Hessian.
- `Converged` occurs only when the original problem's primal-feasibility, stationarity, dual-feasibility, and complementarity residuals all meet their thresholds.
  The iteration limit gives `IterationLimit` and failed restoration gives `Stagnation`; neither indicates success.
- The defaults of `InteriorPointOptions` are 1000 iterations, 20 restoration iterations, and 1000 iterations for least squares within restoration.
  The differentiation method, `OptimalityTolerances`, and time limit are also configurable.

`LagrangianHessian::hessian_into(x, multipliers, output)` writes $\nabla^2f+\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla^2c_i$ as an $n\times n$ symmetric matrix.
`multipliers` supplies multipliers of nonlinear rows (equalities first) in original units; bounds contribute nothing.
A nonfinite or not exactly symmetric matrix produces an error.

Read multipliers and slacks from the `InteriorPointState` returned by `InteriorPointWorkspace::state()`.
`multipliers()` returns nonlinear row multipliers (equalities first), `lower_multipliers()` and `upper_multipliers()` return bound multipliers for each variable (0 for components without that bound), and `slacks()` returns slacks.
Slacks list nonlinear inequalities first, followed by finite bound faces of nonfixed variables in variable order, lower then upper for each variable.
They do not include equalities.
Besides the barrier parameter and regularization, `InteriorPointDiagnostics` contains the last inertia `inertia()`, restoration count `restoration_solves()` (including one restoration from an infeasible initial point), second-order correction counts (`second_order_corrections()`, `accepted_corrections()`), and availability of fixed-variable derivatives `fixed_derivatives_available()`.
Check prepared capacity with `InteriorPointWorkspaceRequirements`.

## Experiment with the interior-point solution and central path

Use `InteriorPoint` to solve $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}(x - a)^2$, whose unconstrained minimizer is negative ($a < 0$), subject to the lower bound $x \ge 0$ (inequality $g(x) = -x \le 0$).
The exact solution is $x^* = 0$ and its multiplier is $z^* = -a$.
The barrier problem solution is $x_\mu = \frac{a + \sqrt{a^2 + 4\mu}}{2} = \frac{2\mu}{-a + \sqrt{a^2 + 4\mu}}$; the right-hand form avoids cancellation when $\mu$ is small.

The following cell compares the solver's solution, multiplier, slack, and final barrier parameter with the KKT solution and the central path computed by formula (dashed curve).
Inputs are the unconstrained minimizer $a$ and initial point $x_0$; $x_0 < 0$ starts from an infeasible point.

```rust

//| ref: optimize-interior-point-central-path
//| title: "Vary the target outside the bound and initial point to compare the interior-point solution and central path"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| exports: [target, barrier, point]
//| inputs:
//|   outside_target: {"type":"range","label":"Unconstrained minimizer a [-]","description":"Move the unconstrained minimizer on the negative side. The exact multiplier is −a.","min":-3,"max":-0.1,"step":0.1,"value":-1}
//|   initial_x: {"type":"range","label":"Initial point x₀ [-]","description":"Negative values start from an infeasible point.","min":-2,"max":2,"step":0.1,"value":-1.5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"feasible-start","label":"Feasible initial point x₀ = 2","description":"No restoration phase is needed; the method stops at x = 3.07×10⁻⁸ after 13 iterations and 20 objective evaluations.","inputs":{"initial_x":2}}
//|   - {"id":"far-target","label":"a = −3 (large multiplier)","description":"The multiplier is 3.0000000256; the original KKT residuals meet their thresholds and terminate the method at barrier parameter 5.12×10⁻⁸.","inputs":{"outside_target":-3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/7/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":8,"max":15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":1e-9,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: feasible-start
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: far-target
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":3,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    ConstrainedProblem, ConstraintFn, Constraints, InteriorPoint, InteriorPointOptions,
    ObjectiveFn, OptimizeDiagnostics, OptimizeResult,
};

let a = outside_target;
let objective = ObjectiveFn::new(1, move |x: &[f64]| Ok(0.5 * (x[0] - a) * (x[0] - a)))
    .with_gradient(move |x: &[f64], g: &mut [f64]| {
        g.copy_from_slice(&[x[0] - a]);
        Ok(())
    });
// Supply x ≥ 0 as the inequality g(x) = −x ≤ 0.
let lower =
    ConstraintFn::new(1, |x: &[f64]| Ok(-x[0])).with_jacobian(|_: &[f64], row: &mut [f64]| {
        row.copy_from_slice(&[-1.0]);
        Ok(())
    });
let constraints = Constraints::new(1)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .inequality(lower)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = ConstrainedProblem::new(&objective, &constraints).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solver = InteriorPoint::new(InteriorPointOptions::default());
let mut work = solver.workspace(&problem).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut out = OptimizeResult::empty();
solver
    .minimize_into(
        &problem,
        &[initial_x],
        &mut out,
        &mut work,
        scioxide_core::ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let OptimizeDiagnostics::InteriorPoint(ip) = out.method_diagnostics() else {
    return Err("No interior-point diagnostics".to_string());
};
let (x, z, s) = (
    out.x()[0],
    work.state().multipliers()[0],
    work.state().slacks()[0],
);
let barrier = ip.barrier_parameter();
// Compute the central path x_μ = 2μ / (−a + √(a² + 4μ)) in a form without cancellation (independent formula).
let path = |m: f64| 2.0 * m / (-a + RealScalar::sqrt(a * a + 4.0 * m));
let d = out.diagnostics();
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let on_path = path(barrier);
let rows: Vec<(&str, Option<f64>, Option<f64>, &str)> = vec![
    ("x", Some(x), Some(0.0), "KKT solution x* = 0"),
    ("Inequality multiplier z", Some(z), Some(-a), "KKT solution z* = −a"),
    ("Slack s", Some(s), Some(x), "s = −g(x) = x"),
    ("s·z", Some(s * z), Some(barrier), "μ on the central path"),
    ("x and central path", Some(x), Some(on_path), "xμ at the final μ"),
    ("Stationarity", d.stationarity(), None, "Reported value"),
    ("Complementarity", d.complementarity(), None, "Reported value"),
    ("Primal feasibility", d.primal_feasibility(), None, "Reported value"),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Solver [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Check value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Source of check value" }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "comparison" })
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Termination reason" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Restoration phases [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Final barrier parameter [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
    ]),
    vec![(
        out.termination().to_string(),
        out.iterations(),
        out.fun_evals(),
        ip.restoration_solves(),
        barrier
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
export!(target, a);
export!(barrier, barrier);
export!(point, x);
let termination = out.termination();
let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The original problem's KKT residuals meet their thresholds")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped before satisfying the KKT residual tests")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(termination.to_string())
        .diagnostic("Iterations", out.iterations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Final barrier parameter", barrier, None)
);
```

At the defaults $a=-1$ and $x_0=-1.5$, the infeasible initial point passes through one restoration phase and reaches `Converged` after 11 iterations and 13 objective evaluations.
The returned point is $x=5.12\times10^{-9}$ and multiplier 1.0000000051, differing from the exact multiplier 1 by $5.1\times10^{-9}$.
The displayed complementarity residual is $5.12\times10^{-9}$, while stationarity and primal-feasibility residuals are 0.
At the final barrier parameter $1.024\times10^{-8}$ ($0.1\times0.2^{10}$), the central path point is $1.024\times10^{-8}$; the returned point and $s\cdot z$ are only half that value.
The interior-point method does not solve each barrier problem exactly; it terminates when the original KKT residuals meet their thresholds, so the returned point does not coincide with the central path point.
Starting from the feasible $x_0=2$ eliminates restoration and stops at $x=3.07\times10^{-8}$ after 13 iterations and 20 objective evaluations.
With $a=-3$, the multiplier is 3.0000000256 and termination occurs at barrier parameter $5.12\times10^{-8}$.

## Supplying a sparse KKT system solver

Passing a type implementing `KktProvider` to `InteriorPointWorkspace::with_kkt_provider` uses it to factor and solve the KKT system in place of the default `DenseKktProvider`.
The provider receives a real symmetric matrix of order $n+m+b$ and must return the inertia of the factored matrix.
`InteriorPoint` checks the original system residual for every returned direction; replacing the provider does not change acceptance and termination criteria for the original nonlinear problem.

`SparseKktProvider` is SciOxide's sparse $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{L}^\mathsf{T}$ factorization provider; `new(SparseSymmetricLimits)` sets symbolic factorization, fill, and workspace limits.
It collects the diagonal and nonzero entries of the supplied matrix into a sparse matrix; when the nonzero pattern matches the previous one, it reuses symbolic factorization (including fill-reducing ordering) and repeats only numerical factorization.
A changed pattern triggers symbolic factorization again; exceeding a limit produces `ResourceLimit`.
`InteriorPoint` assembles and supplies a dense coefficient matrix, so workspace memory and conversion effort remain quadratic in its order; sparse factorization reduces factorization fill and computational cost.

## Choosing between SQP and interior-point methods

SQP and interior-point methods differ in their treatment of the active set.
SQP can carry the subproblem's active set over from the preceding solution, making it suitable for repeated solves from good initial points and problems with few active constraints.
Interior-point methods follow the central path without estimating an active set, so iteration counts tend to grow slowly even with many inequalities and bounds, but the KKT system is refactored at each iteration.
An interior-point method returns a point strictly inside the boundary; even active inequalities retain nonzero slack.

## Summary

Interior-point methods solve KKT systems with complementarity relaxed to $z_js_j=\mu$ by Newton's method and follow the central path as the barrier parameter $\mu$ approaches 0.
`InteriorPoint` starts at $\mu=0.1$ and decreases by a factor of 0.2 whenever barrier problem error falls to at most $10\mu$; if the KKT inertia is incorrect, diagonal regularization is added and the matrix is refactored.
The fraction-to-boundary rule keeps trial points strictly inside the box, and infeasible initial points begin with bounded restoration.
`Converged` depends only on the original problem's KKT residuals; small internal slacks or a small barrier parameter do not establish optimality.
For large sparse problems, `SparseKktProvider` can reduce factorization cost, but the assembled matrix remains dense.
