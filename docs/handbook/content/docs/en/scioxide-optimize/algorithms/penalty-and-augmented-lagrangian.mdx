Penalty and augmented Lagrangian methods replace a constrained problem by a sequence of unconstrained minimizations.
Each subproblem minimizes an objective penalizing constraint violation; SciOxide solves it with BFGS.
Notation and KKT conditions follow [nonlinear constraints and KKT conditions](../nonlinear-constraints/); write equality multipliers as $\lambda_i$ and inequality multipliers as $\mu_j$.
Write the penalty parameter controlling penalty strength as $\rho>0$.

## Quadratic penalty method

Add squared constraint violation to the objective and minimize the penalized objective

$$
\Phi_\rho(\mathbf{x}) = f(\mathbf{x}) + \frac{\rho}{2} \left[ \sum_{i \in \mathcal{E}} c_i(\mathbf{x})^2 + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}} \max(0, g_j(\mathbf{x}))^2 \right]
$$

without constraints while increasing $\rho$ in stages.
This is also called an exterior penalty method because it approaches the solution from the infeasible side.
For equality constraints, comparing the stationarity condition

$$
\nabla \Phi_\rho(\mathbf{x}) = \nabla f(\mathbf{x}) + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{E}} \rho\, c_i(\mathbf{x})\, \nabla c_i(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{0}
$$

with KKT stationarity shows that $\lambda_i \approx \rho\, c_i(\mathbf{x})$ approximates the Lagrange multipliers.
Similarly, for inequalities, $\mu_j \approx \rho\max(0,g_j(\mathbf{x}))$.
Since the multipliers approach finite values $\lambda_i^*$, constraint violation remains of order $c_i(\mathbf{x}) \approx \lambda_i^* / \rho$; reducing it requires increasing $\rho$.

Meanwhile, in the Hessian

$$
\nabla^2 \Phi_\rho(\mathbf{x}) = \nabla^2 f(\mathbf{x}) + \rho\, \mathbf{J}_c(\mathbf{x})^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{J}_c(\mathbf{x}) + \rho \sum_{i \in \mathcal{E}} c_i(\mathbf{x}) \nabla^2 c_i(\mathbf{x})
$$

only curvature normal to the constraint surface grows proportionally to $\rho$ ($\mathbf{J}_c$ is the equality constraint Jacobian).
The final term remains bounded because $\rho c_i\approx\lambda_i^*$.
Tangential curvature is independent of $\rho$, so the condition number deteriorates roughly in proportion to $\rho$, slowing convergence of the inner unconstrained solver.
Reducing violation to $\varepsilon$ requires $\rho\approx|\lambda^*|/\varepsilon$, giving a condition number of order $|\lambda^*|/\varepsilon$ as well.

The next cell isolates one quadratic-penalty subproblem and examines it by formula for minimization of $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}(x - a)^2$ ($a > 1$) subject to $x \le 1$.
The exact solution is $x^* = 1$ and its multiplier is $\mu^* = a - 1$.
The penalized minimizer is $x_\rho = \frac{a + \rho}{1 + \rho}$, violation is $x_\rho-1=\frac{a-1}{1+\rho}$, the multiplier estimate is $\rho(x_\rho - 1)$, and curvature outside the boundary is $1 + \rho$.
The cell computes these by formula and plots $\Phi_\rho$ as a dashed curve.
It calls no solver; SciOxide functions are used only to compute $10^q$.

At the defaults $a=2$ and $\rho=100$, violation $x_\rho-1$ is $9.90\times10^{-3}$ and the multiplier estimate is 0.990, leaving a difference from the exact multiplier 1 in the ratio $1/(1+\rho)$.
With $\rho=10^6$, violation falls to $1.00\times10^{-6}$, but curvature outside the boundary becomes $1.0\times10^{6}$; the inner solver must solve a function whose curvature differs by six orders of magnitude across the boundary.
At $\rho=0.1$ the minimizer lies 0.909 from the boundary, and the multiplier estimate 0.0909 is far from the exact multiplier 1.
The dashed curve joins parabolas of curvature 1 inside the boundary and $1+\rho$ outside; as $\rho$ increases, the minimizer line approaches the boundary line.

## Augmented Lagrangian method

The augmented Lagrangian method (method of multipliers) incorporates multiplier estimates into the objective to avoid the ill-conditioning of quadratic penalties.
For equalities only, it uses the augmented Lagrangian

$$
\mathcal{L}_A(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}, \rho) = f(\mathbf{x}) + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{E}} \lambda_i c_i(\mathbf{x}) + \frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{E}} c_i(\mathbf{x})^2
$$


After solving $\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{x}} \mathcal{L}_A(\mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k, \rho_k)$ in the inner iteration, update the multipliers by

$$
\lambda_{i, k+1} = \lambda_{i, k} + \rho_k c_i(\mathbf{x}_{k+1})
$$


This update takes the coefficients in the inner stationarity condition $\nabla f+\sum_i(\lambda_{i,k}+\rho_kc_i)\nabla c_i=\mathbf{0}$ directly as the next multipliers.
Violation becomes $c_i\approx(\lambda_i^*-\lambda_{i,k})/\rho_k$; unlike the quadratic penalty method, multiplier error determines violation.
If the multipliers converge, violation approaches 0 while $\rho$ remains finite.
At a KKT point satisfying LICQ and the second-order sufficient condition, once $\rho$ exceeds a finite value, $\mathbf{x}^*$ becomes a strict local minimizer of $\mathcal{L}_A(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^*,\rho)$.
Multiplier error starting there contracts at each outer iteration by a ratio of roughly $M/\rho$ ($M$ is a problem-dependent constant).
Thus $\rho$ need not be as large as for quadratic penalties.

For inequalities, add the Powell–Hestenes–Rockafellar (PHR) term

$$
\psi(g,\mu_j,\rho)=\frac{1}{2\rho}\left(\max(0,\mu_j+\rho g)^2-\mu_j^2\right)
$$

and update multipliers by $\mu_{j,k+1}=\max(0,\mu_{j,k}+\rho_kg_j(\mathbf{x}_{k+1}))$.
This term is $\mu_jg+\tfrac{\rho}{2}g^2$ when $\mu_j+\rho g>0$ and the constant $-\mu_j^2/(2\rho)$ otherwise; its value and first derivative are continuous even at the transition.
The second derivative is discontinuous at the transition, but inner BFGS uses only first derivatives.

## Penalty parameter updates and restoration

`PenaltyMethod` and `AugmentedLagrangian` share the same outer iteration, differing only in whether they retain multipliers.
`PenaltyMethod` minimizes $\Phi_\rho$ without multiplier terms; `AugmentedLagrangian` minimizes the function augmented by $\mathcal{L}_A$ for equalities and PHR terms for inequalities.
Both normalize at the initial point as explained in [KKT residuals and termination](oxref:optimize-kkt-residuals), applying penalties and termination criteria to the normalized problem.
The penalty parameter starts at $\rho=1$ and has no directly configurable user input.

Each outer iteration solves the subproblem by inner BFGS from the last accepted point.
The inner gradient tolerance is the smaller of the inner `Bfgs` setting and the outer stationarity threshold.
The inner `Bfgs` can be replaced by one configured through `with_inner_optimizer`.
Let $v$ denote normalized violation at the solved subproblem point, $v_{\mathrm{prev}}$ violation at the preceding accepted point, and $\tau$ the primal-feasibility threshold; penalty and acceptance follow these rules.

- If violation decreases sufficiently ($v\le\tau$ or $v\le v_{\mathrm{prev}}/2$), retain the penalty parameter.
- If violation does not decrease sufficiently, the inner solution is stationary, and the preceding iteration did not raise the penalty parameter by this rule, multiply the penalty parameter by 10.
- If violation does not decrease sufficiently and either the inner solution is not stationary or the preceding iteration raised the penalty parameter by this rule, or if neither penalized objective nor violation decreases, solve a restoration subproblem when the preceding accepted point is infeasible ($v_{\mathrm{prev}}>\tau$).
  Restoration removes the objective and reduces only $\tfrac12\sum_ic_i^2+\tfrac12\sum_j\max(0,g_j)^2$ with BFGS, accepts the resulting point, and multiplies the penalty parameter by 10.
  Multipliers do not change.
- Even when violation meets its threshold, multiply the penalty parameter by 10 if complementarity exceeds its threshold.

The outer iteration stops in the following cases.

- Return `Converged` when every KKT residual is at most its threshold.
- Stop with `Stagnation` if violation at the restored point exceeds both $\tau$ and $v_{\mathrm{prev}}/2$.
- Also stop with `Stagnation` if the preceding accepted point is feasible and neither penalized objective nor violation decreases.
- If an iteration without restoration has unsuccessful inner BFGS termination and its accepted point is feasible, return the inner termination reason (such as `IterationLimit` or `LineSearchFailed(_)`) directly.
- Return `IterationLimit` at the outer iteration limit.

`PenaltyOptions` and `AugmentedLagrangianOptions` have the same fields and configure `OptimalityTolerances`, the outer iteration limit (`with_max_outer_iterations`, default 100), and the time limit.
`PenaltyMethodWorkspace` and `AugmentedLagrangianWorkspace` retain accepted points, internal multipliers, and inner BFGS state.
Check required capacity with `PenaltyMethodWorkspaceRequirements` and `AugmentedLagrangianWorkspaceRequirements`.
Neither workspace exposes multipliers; use `Sqp` or `InteriorPoint` when multipliers are needed.

Multipliers for the returned KKT residuals are computed separately from internal multipliers.
At points meeting the violation threshold, multipliers cancel the objective gradient in the least-squares sense using equality and nearly active inequality normals; rows with negative inequality multipliers are removed and the system solved again.
At other points, `PenaltyMethod` uses a $\rho c_i$-type estimate, while `AugmentedLagrangian` uses updated multipliers.
Stationarity and dual feasibility in the returned `diagnostics()`, and reference quantities used in termination thresholds, are all computed from these same multipliers.

Both methods also treat each finite variable-bound face as one inequality row.
Since bounds are enforced by penalties, trial points can leave the box before convergence.
Do not use these methods for an objective undefined outside its domain without checking this behavior.
If trial points must remain inside the box, use [sequential quadratic programming](../sqp-and-interior-point/), [nonlinear interior-point methods](../nonlinear-interior-point/), or [derivative-free constrained optimization](../derivative-free-constrained/).

## Comparing the two methods

Solve the preceding problem (minimize $\frac12(x-a)^2$ subject to $x\le1$) from $x_0=0$ using the defaults of `PenaltyMethod` and `AugmentedLagrangian`.
The independent check is the difference from the exact solution $x^*=1$; read success from each row's termination reason.
The input is the unconstrained minimizer $a$, which changes the exact multiplier $a-1$.
The solvers normalize scales and update the penalty parameter themselves, so the preceding cell's $\rho$ is not used here.

```rust
//| id: optimize-penalty-conditioning
//| ref: optimize-penalty-conditioning
//| title: "Compare accuracy and evaluation counts of penalty and augmented Lagrangian methods"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   unconstrained_target: {"type":"range","label":"Unconstrained minimizer a [-]","description":"Change the desired distance outside the boundary and the exact multiplier a − 1.","min":1.1,"max":3,"step":0.1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"far-target","label":"a = 3 (large multiplier)","description":"The exact multiplier is 2; the penalty method reaches x* = 1 within 2×10⁻⁸ using 180 objective evaluations, and the augmented Lagrangian method uses 28.","inputs":{"unconstrained_target":3}}
//|   - {"id":"near-target","label":"a = 1.1 (small multiplier)","description":"The exact multiplier is 0.1; the penalty method's evaluation count also falls to 152.","inputs":{"unconstrained_target":1.1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":120,"max":240}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/1/6","range":{"min":15,"max":45}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: far-target
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: near-target
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_optimize::{
    AugmentedLagrangian, AugmentedLagrangianOptions, ConstrainedProblem, ConstraintFn,
    Constraints, ObjectiveFn, PenaltyMethod, PenaltyOptions,
};

let a = unconstrained_target;
// Objective f(x) = (x − a)²/2 and inequality g(x) = x − 1 ≤ 0.
let objective = ObjectiveFn::new(1, move |x: &[f64]| Ok(0.5 * (x[0] - a) * (x[0] - a)))
    .with_gradient(move |x: &[f64], g: &mut [f64]| {
        g.copy_from_slice(&[x[0] - a]);
        Ok(())
    });
let g = ConstraintFn::new(1, |x: &[f64]| Ok(x[0] - 1.0)).with_jacobian(
    |_: &[f64], row: &mut [f64]| {
        row.copy_from_slice(&[1.0]);
        Ok(())
    },
);
let constraints = Constraints::new(1)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .inequality(g)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = ConstrainedProblem::new(&objective, &constraints).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let penalty = PenaltyMethod::new(PenaltyOptions::default())
    .minimize(&problem, &[0.0])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let augmented = AugmentedLagrangian::new(AugmentedLagrangianOptions::default())
    .minimize(&problem, &[0.0])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed10 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 });
let solvers = [
    ("PenaltyMethod", &penalty),
    ("AugmentedLagrangian", &augmented),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Termination reason" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "x [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "|x − x*| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Stationarity [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Outer iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "6", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    solvers
        .iter()
        .map(|(name, r)| {
            (
                *name,
                r.termination().to_string(),
                r.x()[0],
                (r.x()[0] - 1.0).abs(),
                r.stationarity(),
                r.iterations(),
                r.fun_evals(),
            )
        })
        .collect::<Vec<_>>(),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "solvers" })
);
let both = penalty.is_success() && augmented.is_success();
let status = format!("{} / {}", penalty.termination(), augmented.termination());
let outcome = if both {
    Outcome::satisfied("Both methods meet the KKT residual criteria")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("One method stopped before meeting the KKT residual criteria")
        .explanation("Interpret the stopped method's row as its last accepted point.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(status)
        .diagnostic("Penalty method evaluations", penalty.fun_evals() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Multiplier method evaluations", augmented.fun_evals() as f64, None)
);
```

At the default $a=2$, both terminate with `Converged` and their displayed stationarity residuals are 0.
The reported multipliers are chosen to cancel the objective gradient in the least-squares sense using active constraint normals; in this one-variable problem they cancel it exactly, making the reported value 0.
The penalty method reaches $|x-x^*|=5.00\times10^{-9}$ after 16 outer iterations and 177 objective evaluations; the augmented Lagrangian method reaches $3.89\times10^{-9}$ after 7 outer iterations and 29 evaluations.
The penalty method repeatedly multiplies its penalty parameter by 10 to reduce violation and resolves an increasingly ill-conditioned subproblem each time, using approximately six times as many objective evaluations as the augmented Lagrangian method.
At $a=3$, the methods stop after 180 and 28 objective evaluations, respectively; at $a=1.1$, they use 152 and 28.
For these three values of $a$, the augmented Lagrangian evaluation count is almost independent of multiplier magnitude.

## Summary

Quadratic penalties require a penalty parameter of order $|\lambda^*|/\varepsilon$ to obtain violation $\varepsilon$, and the subproblem's condition number deteriorates to the same order.
The augmented Lagrangian method reduces violation by updating multipliers, attaining the same accuracy with a finite penalty parameter and, in this example, about one-sixth as many objective evaluations.
`PenaltyMethod` and `AugmentedLagrangian` start the penalty parameter at 1 and increase it automatically by factors of 10, inserting restoration subproblems when violation fails to decrease.
Both treat bounds as penalized rows, so choose other methods when trial points must not leave the box or when multipliers are needed.
Read results together with `termination()` and the residuals in `diagnostics()`; do not judge by objective value alone.
