Linear programming (LP) and convex quadratic programming (QP) solutions can be checked without trusting the solver by supplying multipliers alongside the point.
Dual-feasible multipliers give a lower bound on the optimal value by weak duality; complementarity makes this bound equal the objective value, establishing optimality.
This page explains the form and sign conventions of SciOxide's result certificates, the checks performed by `ProgrammingVerifier`, and how to read verification statuses.
Duality itself is derived in [linear programming and duality](oxref:optimize-lp-duality).

## Multipliers in the original formulation

SciOxide's LP and QP express multipliers in the original formulation

$$
\min_{\mathbf{x}}\ f(\mathbf{x})\quad\text{subject to}\quad \mathbf{a}_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}\ (\le,\,=,\,\ge)\ b_i\ (i=1,\dots,m),\qquad \mathbf{l}\le\mathbf{x}\le\mathbf{u}
$$

before conversion to standard form ($f(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{c}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}$ for LP, $f(\mathbf{x})=\tfrac12\mathbf{x}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{c}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}$ for QP).
With row multipliers $\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^m$ and lower- and upper-bound multipliers $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}\in\mathbb{R}^n$, stationarity is

$$
\nabla f(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{A}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}=\mathbf{0}
$$


The sign conventions are as follows.

- Equality row multipliers have unrestricted sign.
- Multipliers of $\le$ rows are nonnegative; those of $\ge$ rows are nonpositive.
- Bound multipliers are nonnegative and must be 0 for components without that bound.

Under this convention, multiplier $y_i\ge0$ of a $\le$ row is the rate of decrease in optimal value when the right-hand side $b_i$ increases (decrease per unit of right-hand side).
The sign of $\mathbf{y}$ is opposite to the textbook form (the dual with $\mathbf{A}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}\le\mathbf{c}$) in [linear programming and duality](oxref:optimize-lp-duality).

When multiplier signs meet the convention, the LP objective has the following lower bound for every feasible $\mathbf{x}$.
Writing the stationarity residual as $\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{A}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}$ gives

$$
\mathbf{c}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}\ \ge\ -\mathbf{b}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{l}^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}-\mathbf{u}^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}+\sum_{j}\ \min_{l_j\le x_j\le u_j} r_j x_j
$$


Substitute $\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{A}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}$ and use $-y_i\mathbf{a}_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}\ge-y_ib_i$, $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}\ge\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{l}$, and $-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}\ge-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{upper}}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{u}$ to derive it.
The final sum is minimized at lower bound $l_j$ when $r_j>0$ and upper bound $u_j$ when $r_j<0$; without the required bound, no lower bound is obtained.
With exact stationarity, the final sum vanishes and the lower bound is the dual objective itself.
For convex QP, the Wolfe dual value with $-\tfrac12\mathbf{x}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{x}$ added serves the same role.

## Optimality certificates

`ProgrammingCertificate` links a solver's claim to the original problem and has three forms.

- `Optimality { point, dual }`: a point and multipliers claiming optimality.
- `Infeasibility(dual)`: Farkas multipliers claiming infeasibility.
- `Unboundedness { point, direction }`: a feasible point and recession direction claiming unboundedness.

This page covers optimality certificates; the other two are covered in [infeasibility and unboundedness certificates and verification of external results](../infeasibility-certificates/).
An optimality certificate checks five quantities: primal feasibility (row and bound violation), multiplier signs, stationarity, complementarity $y_i(\mathbf{a}_i^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{x}-b_i)=0$ and bound complementarity, and the primal-dual objective gap.
Exact stationarity and complementarity make the objective gap 0, so the lower bound equals the primal objective and the point is optimal.
In floating point, each of the five quantities is compared with a tolerance.

`DualCertificate` borrows multipliers through three public slice fields: row multipliers `rows`, lower-bound multipliers `lower`, and upper-bound multipliers `upper`; `ProgrammingCertificate` borrows it together with a point.
`OwnedDualCertificate::new(rows, lower, upper)` and `OwnedProgrammingCertificate` own the same contents without a point.
`ProgrammingResult` stores certificates in this owning type, and `certificate()` returns a borrow paired with the point.
Lengths, finiteness, and signs are checked during verification rather than construction.

## Verifier decisions

`ProgrammingVerifier::verify(&problem, claim)` recomputes residuals from the original problem data and certificate.
It does not solve for missing multipliers.
Pass `ProgrammingCertificate` or `&ProgrammingResult` to `claim`; both convert to `ProgrammingClaim`.
Supplying `&ProgrammingResult` additionally checks that the termination reason matches the certificate kind and that the reported objective agrees with the recomputed value within tolerance.
A result without a certificate is rejected.

`VerificationReport::status()` is one of the following `VerificationStatus` values.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Accepted` | Every check required by the certificate passed within tolerance |
| `Rejected` | At least one check failed |
| `KktOnly` | QP KKT conditions passed, but convexity could not be established, so a global minimum cannot be claimed |
| `Incomplete` | Verification stopped early because of an evaluation limit, cancellation, or deadline |

Read success through `is_accepted()` rather than reconstructing it from status variants.
`primal_violation()`, `bound_violation()`, `dual_violation()`, `stationarity()`, and `complementarity()` are the largest residuals for each check; `primal_objective()` is the objective recomputed at the supplied point, `objective_gap()` the primal-dual objective gap, `dual_bound()` the lower bound from the formula above, and `witness_margin()` the margin of an infeasibility or unboundedness certificate.
The lower bound is `Some` only when multiplier signs satisfy the convention exactly without tolerance, convexity is established, and the necessary bounds exist.
Thus even a `Rejected` report may contain a lower bound; read bound availability and status separately.

Each check uses the tolerance for its role from `VerificationOptions::with_tolerances`.
Row and bound residuals use feasibility tolerance; multiplier signs use dual feasibility; stationarity and Farkas combinations use gradient; multiplier–slack products use complementarity; objective gaps and certificate margins use gap; and Hessian convexity uses rank tolerance.
Each tolerance has absolute and relative parts; both default to $\sqrt{\epsilon}$ for every role (approximately $1.5\times10^{-8}$ for `f64`).
These defaults are independent of the LP and QP solver defaults (both $10^{-8}$).
`with_max_evaluations` limits operator applications in a single verification.
Checks use floating-point arithmetic, so `Accepted` means residuals are within tolerance; it is not an enclosure from interval arithmetic.

## Problem types for verification

`LpProblem`, `QpProblem`, `LpVerificationProblem`, and `QpVerificationProblem` implement the sealed trait `VerificationProblem`.
`LpVerificationProblem::from_problem(&lp)` borrows `LpProblem` without cloning and also inherits $\mathbf{x}\ge\mathbf{0}$ when `LpProblem` has no explicit bounds.
When constructing through `LpVerificationProblem::new(objective)`, any `AdjointOperator` can provide row coefficients; variables without bounds are free.
The construction method changes how nonnegativity is treated even for the same problem; explicit bounds prevent misinterpretation.
`LpProblem::from_view(&view, maximum_entries)` applies operator-defined rows column by column and converts them into sparse `LpProblem` for the solver.

`QpVerificationProblem` computes the gradient as $(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{Q}^\mathsf{T})\mathbf{x}/2+\mathbf{c}$, interpreting nonsymmetric $\mathbf{Q}$ through its symmetric part.
With visible coefficients from `from_dense`, `from_sparse`, or `from_problem`, positive semidefiniteness is checked in order using Gershgorin disks, Cholesky factorization, and eigenvalues; successful verification can then yield `Accepted`.
If the smallest eigenvalue of the symmetric part is below $-(\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}+\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\max_{ij}|Q_{ij}|)$ ($\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}$ are absolute and relative rank tolerances), the status is `Rejected`; a negative value between that threshold and 0 leaves convexity unresolved and gives `KktOnly`.
An operator-supplied `QpVerificationProblem::new` does not permit convexity verification, so even passing every KKT check yields at most `KktOnly`.
`convexity_checked()` reports whether convexity was established and is always true for LP.

Reusing workspace across verifications is covered in [infeasibility and unboundedness certificates and verification of external results](../infeasibility-certificates/).

## Reconstructing dual certificates and incorrect multipliers

Minimize $-x_1-2x_2$ subject to $x_1+x_2\le3$ and $x_1,x_2\ge0$.
The optimum is $(0,3)$ with optimal value $-6$.
Stationarity $(-1,-2)+y(1,1)-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}=\mathbf{0}$ and complementarity ($\alpha_{\mathrm{lower},2}=0$ because $x_2>0$) give row multiplier $y=2$ and lower-bound multipliers $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{lower}}=(1,0)$.
Pass the `SimplexSolver` result and input-selected combinations of multipliers and points to `ProgrammingVerifier::verify` to check the original equations.
Inputs are multiplier $y$ paired with vertex $(0,3)$ and the point $(x_1,3-x_1)$ on a constraint edge paired with analytic multipliers.
The table shows verification status, primal objective, dual lower bound, and stationarity and complementarity residuals.

```rust
//| id: optimize-lp-independent-evidence
//| ref: optimize-lp-independent-evidence
//| title: "Check correct dual data and altered multipliers in original coordinates"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-optimize, scioxide-linalg]
//| inputs:
//|   trial_multiplier: {"type":"range","label":"Trial row multiplier y [-]","description":"Change the row multiplier paired with vertex (0, 3).","min":0,"max":3,"step":0.25,"value":1}
//|   candidate_x: {"type":"range","label":"Trial point x₁ [-]","description":"Pair point (x₁, 3 − x₁) on the constraint edge with analytic multipliers.","min":0,"max":3,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"exact-multiplier","label":"Multiplier y = 2 and vertex (0, 3)","description":"Both input rows pair analytic multipliers with the optimal point; all 4 rows are Accepted and both primal objective and lower bound are −6.","inputs":{"trial_multiplier":2,"candidate_x":0}}
//|   - {"id":"dual-feasible","label":"Multiplier y = 3","description":"The stationarity residual 1 still gives Rejected, but the multipliers satisfy the sign convention and yield lower bound −9.","inputs":{"trial_multiplier":3}}
//|   - {"id":"far-vertex","label":"Point (3, 0)","description":"Stationarity holds, but complementarity residual 3 leaves a gap of 3 between primal objective −3 and lower bound −6, giving Rejected.","inputs":{"candidate_x":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":2,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":-6,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/1/6","approx":{"value":-6,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"Rejected"}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/2/6","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/2/7","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/3/4","equals":"Rejected"}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":-5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/3/8","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: exact-multiplier
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/3/4","equals":"Accepted"}
//|   - case: dual-feasible
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"Rejected"}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/2/6","approx":{"value":-9,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: far-vertex
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/3/4","equals":"Rejected"}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":-3,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"reports","path":"/rows/3/8","approx":{"value":3,"atol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    DualCertificate, LinearConstraintSense, LinearConstraints, LpProblem,
    LpVerificationProblem, ProgrammingCertificate, ProgrammingVerifier, SimplexSolver,
    VerificationOptions, VerificationReport,
};
let general = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let rows = LinearConstraints::new(
    Matrix::from_vec(1, 2, vec![1.0_f64, 1.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    vec![3.0],
    vec![LinearConstraintSense::LessEqual],
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lp = LpProblem::new(vec![-1.0, -2.0])
    .and_then(|p| p.with_constraints(rows))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// The x ≥ 0 condition of an LpProblem without explicit bounds is inherited by the verification problem.
let problem = LpVerificationProblem::from_problem(&lp);
let verifier = ProgrammingVerifier::new(VerificationOptions::default());
// Pass the solver result as a &ProgrammingResult claim to check its termination reason and objective value as well.
let solved = SimplexSolver::default()
    .solve(&lp)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solver_multiplier = match solved.certificate() {
    Some(ProgrammingCertificate::Optimality { dual, .. }) => Some(dual.rows[0]),
    _ => None,
};
let solver_report = verifier
    .verify(&problem, &solved)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Analytic multipliers satisfying c + Aᵀy − α_lower + α_upper = 0 are y = 2 and α_lower = (1, 0).
let (lower, upper) = ([1.0, 0.0], [0.0, 0.0]);
let inspect = |point: &[f64], y: f64| {
    verifier
        .verify(
            &problem,
            ProgrammingCertificate::Optimality {
                point,
                dual: DualCertificate {
                    rows: &[y],
                    lower: &lower,
                    upper: &upper,
                },
            },
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let edge = [candidate_x, 3.0 - candidate_x];
let cases: [(&str, [f64; 2], Option<f64>, VerificationReport<f64>); 4] = [
    (
        "SimplexSolver result",
        [solved.x()[0], solved.x()[1]],
        solver_multiplier,
        solver_report,
    ),
    (
        "Analytic multipliers and vertex",
        [0.0, 3.0],
        Some(2.0),
        inspect(&[0.0, 3.0], 2.0)?,
    ),
    (
        "Trial multiplier and vertex",
        [0.0, 3.0],
        Some(trial_multiplier),
        inspect(&[0.0, 3.0], trial_multiplier)?,
    ),
    ("Analytic multipliers and trial point", edge, Some(2.0), inspect(&edge, 2.0)?),
];
let table: Vec<_> = cases
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, point, y, report)| {
        (
            name,
            point[0],
            point[1],
            y,
            format!("{:?}", report.status()),
            report.primal_objective(),
            report.dual_bound(),
            report.stationarity(),
            report.complementarity(),
        )
    })
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Certificate" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Point x₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Point x₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Row multiplier y [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Verification status" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Primal objective [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Dual lower bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": general, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Stationarity residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Complementarity residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": general }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    table,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "reports" })
);
```

The `SimplexSolver` result and the analytic multiplier pair give status `Accepted`; primal objective and dual lower bound are both $-6$.
At the default multiplier $y=1$, stationarity residual 1 gives `Rejected`; there is also no lower bound because no upper bound can absorb residual $\mathbf{r}=(-1,-1)$.
With $y=3$, stationarity residual remains 1 and the status `Rejected`, but residual $\mathbf{r}=(1,1)$ is minimized at lower bound 0, giving lower bound $-9$.
This is a valid weak-duality lower bound $-9\le-6$ and does not prove optimality.
Changing the point to $(1,2)$ preserves stationarity, but the positive lower-bound multiplier 1 on $x_1=1>0$ makes complementarity residual 1, leaving a gap of 1 between primal objective $-5$ and lower bound $-6$.

## Summary

Passing LP or QP points and multipliers in the original formulation to `ProgrammingVerifier` checks results without relying on solver internals.
Multiplier signs are nonnegative for $\le$ rows, nonpositive for $\ge$ rows, and nonnegative for bounds; $\mathbf{y}$ has the opposite sign to the textbook dual.
`Accepted` is a numerical decision that feasibility, signs, stationarity, complementarity, and the objective gap all lie within tolerance $\sqrt{\epsilon}$; it is not an interval-arithmetic enclosure.
A dual lower bound may also accompany a `Rejected` report; read status and bound separately, and assess candidates using the fact that changing multipliers cannot make the lower bound exceed the optimum.
For QP status `KktOnly`, supply visible coefficients to establish convexity.
