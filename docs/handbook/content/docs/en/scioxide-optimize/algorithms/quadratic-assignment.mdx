Place $n$ facilities one each at $n$ locations.
Let $A_{ij}$ be flow from facility $i$ to facility $j$ and $B_{kl}$ distance from location $k$ to location $l$; define transport cost when facility $i$ is placed at location $\pi(i)$ by

$$
F(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij}B_{\pi(i),\pi(j)}
$$


This is the Koopmans–Beckmann quadratic assignment problem (QAP).
$\pi$ is a bijection, so every facility occupies a distinct location.
Even for symmetric matrices the formula counts both directions; align factors when comparing with a cost defined as a one-direction sum.

Unlike linear assignment, where each facility's cost depends only on its location, QAP cost depends on the locations of pairs of facilities.
QAP is NP-hard; even finding a constant-factor approximation is NP-hard in general (Sahni and Gonzalez, 1976).
There are $n!$ candidates: approximately $4.8\times10^8$ at $n=12$ and $2.4\times10^{18}$ at $n=20$.
All methods on this page are heuristics seeking good permutations and do not guarantee global optimality.

## Permutation matrix representation

Represent permutation $\pi$ by permutation matrix $X$ with $X_{ik}=1$ when $\pi(i)=k$ and $0$ otherwise.
Since $(XBX^T)_{ij}=B_{\pi(i),\pi(j)}$, the objective can be written using the Frobenius inner product $\langle U,V\rangle=\sum_{ij}U_{ij}V_{ij}$ as

$$
F(X)=\langle A,\,XBX^T\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(A^TXBX^T)
$$


`QapDirection::Minimize` minimizes this value for costs such as transportation.
`QapDirection::Maximize` maximizes it for graph matching, maximizing overlap of two adjacency matrices $A,B$.
Supplying a pair fixing facility $i$ at location $k$ restricts the search to the remaining $k_{\mathrm{free}}=n-|\text{fixed}|$ free facilities and locations.
In graph matching, known correspondences of this kind are called seeds.

## FAQ relaxation

Every row and column of a permutation matrix sums to 1, and its entries are 0 or 1.
Relaxing entries to nonnegative real numbers gives the set of doubly stochastic matrices

$$
\mathcal D=\{P\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}: P\ge0,\ P\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1,\ P^T\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1\}
$$


By the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem, the extreme points of $\mathcal D$ are precisely permutation matrices.
FAQ (Fast Approximate QAP, Vogelstein et al. 2015) improves $f(P)=\langle A,PBP^T\rangle$ over $\mathcal D$ using Frank–Wolfe, then converts it back into a permutation.

### Gradient and Frank–Wolfe direction

Perturbing $P$ by $E$ gives

$$
f(P+E)=f(P)+\langle A,EBP^T\rangle+\langle A,PBE^T\rangle+\langle A,EBE^T\rangle
$$

where the two first-order terms equal $\langle AP B^T,E\rangle$ and $\langle A^TPB,E\rangle$, respectively.
The gradient is therefore

$$
\nabla f(P)=APB^T+A^TPB
$$

and becomes $2APB$ when $A,B$ are symmetric.
Frank–Wolfe uses the point minimizing the linear approximation over $\mathcal D$ as the direction endpoint:

$$
Q=\arg\min_{Q\in\mathcal D}\ \langle\nabla f(P),Q\rangle
$$


A linear function attains its minimum at an extreme point of a polytope, so $Q$ can be a permutation matrix and this subproblem is linear assignment with cost matrix $\nabla f(P)$.
For maximization, select an assignment maximizing $\langle\nabla f(P),Q\rangle$.

### Exact line search

Since $f$ is quadratic in $P$, values along $D=Q-P$ form a quadratic in $t$:

$$
f(P+tD)=f_0+g\,t+c\,t^2,\qquad g=\langle\nabla f(P),D\rangle,\quad c=f(Q)-f_0-g
$$

Here $f_0=f(P)$.
Obtain $c$ from the value $f(Q)$ of $t=1$; for permutation $Q$, compute $f(Q)$ using $O(k_{\mathrm{free}}^2)$.
For minimization, choose $t^*=\min(\max(-g/(2c),0),1)$ if $c>0$; otherwise choose endpoint $t\in\{0,1\}$ with the better value among $f_0$ and $f(Q)$, and update $P\leftarrow P+t^*D$.

### Frank–Wolfe gap and termination

$\mathrm{gap}(P)=\max\bigl(0,\,-\langle\nabla f(P),Q-P\rangle\bigr)$ is called the Frank–Wolfe gap (reverse the sign for maximization).
For convex $f$, $f(P)-\min_{\mathcal D}f\le\mathrm{gap}(P)$; however, for general matrices $A,B$, $f$ is neither convex nor concave.
Thus `fw_gap` indicates first-order stationarity over $\mathcal D$ rather than a permutation optimality gap or relaxation lower bound.
The relaxed objective is generally nonconvex as well, so its computed value is not guaranteed to bound the original minimum from below.

Iterations stop when the update $\|t^*D\|_F/\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{free}}}$ falls to at most the step threshold (default 0.03), or with `IterationLimit` at the iteration limit (default 30).
Without an initial permutation, the first trial starts at barycenter $J/k_{\mathrm{free}}$ (all entries $1/k_{\mathrm{free}}$).
The first trial with a supplied initial permutation, and additional restarts, start at the average of the barycenter and a permutation matrix.
Restart permutations are generated randomly from `with_seed`'s seed plus the restart number.

### Projection to a permutation

Select the permutation matrix closest to the final $P$ in Frobenius norm.
Permutation matrices satisfy $\|X\|_F^2=n$, giving $\|P-X\|_F^2=\|P\|_F^2+n-2\langle P,X\rangle$; the nearest point is therefore found by linear assignment maximizing $\langle P,X\rangle$.
Each iteration costs $O(k_{\mathrm{free}}^3)$ for the two gradient matrix products and $O(k_{\mathrm{free}}^3\log k_{\mathrm{free}})$ for dense linear assignment by the shortest augmenting path method using binary-heap Dijkstra.
Matrices and work arrays use $O(n^2)$ memory.

## 2-opt and fixed correspondences

2-opt examines objective changes from exchanging the locations of two facilities and repeats improving swaps.
Swapping locations $u=\pi(i)$ and $v=\pi(j)$ of facilities $i,j$ changes only terms containing $i$ or $j$, giving difference

$$
\Delta_{ij}=\sum_{m\ne i,j}\Bigl[(A_{im}-A_{jm})(B_{v\,\pi(m)}-B_{u\,\pi(m)})+(A_{mi}-A_{mj})(B_{\pi(m)\,v}-B_{\pi(m)\,u})\Bigr]+R_{ij}
$$


$R_{ij}=(A_{ii}-A_{jj})(B_{vv}-B_{uu})+(A_{ij}-A_{ji})(B_{vu}-B_{uv})$ contains the two facilities' diagonal terms and their mutual terms.
One swap check costs $O(n)$ and a pass over all pairs costs $O(n^3)$.

Facilities in fixed pairs are excluded from swaps.
The implementation examines free-facility pairs $(i,j)$ in lexicographic order and accepts the first improving swap (first improvement).
For an accepted swap it recomputes the double sum in $O(n^2)$ to confirm improvement, then restarts from the beginning.
When no swap improves the value, it stops at a 2-opt local optimum with `Converged`; reaching the swap-check limit (default 100 000) gives `IterationLimit`.
Even without an improving swap, moving three or more facilities simultaneously can improve the value.
Exhaustive search has $n!$ candidates, so distinguish small-scale checks from practical heuristics.

## Quadratic assignment API

`QapProblemView::new(a, b, fixed)` borrows two square matrices of the same size and a sequence of fixed pairs `(facility, location)`.
Mismatched shapes, out-of-range fixed pairs, or repeated indices give `InvalidInput`; NaN or infinite entries give `NonFinite`.
In the returned permutation, `permutation[i]` is the `b` index assigned to index $i$ of `a`.
`prepare()` clones both matrices and creates `QapPlan` by preprocessing the unfixed facility and location blocks in $O(n^2)$ time and memory.
`prepare_with_limit(max_bytes)` returns `ResourceLimit` before allocation if the plan and workspace do not fit `max_bytes`.
For sparse matrices use `QapPlan::from_sparse(a, b, fixed)` (missing entries are 0) and `from_sparse_with_limit`.
`QapPlan::workspace_bytes()` returns an upper bound on memory for the plan and one workspace.

Pass optimization direction `QapDirection` (`Minimize` or `Maximize`) as an argument for every solve.
`solve(direction, initial)` solves with the default `QapOptions` and allocates workspace and result internally.
`initial` is the first trial's initial permutation; incorrect length or violation of a fixed pair gives `InvalidInput`.
Use `solve_with_options(direction, initial, &options)` to change options.

`QapOptions::default()` uses FAQ followed by 2-opt (`QapMethod::FaqThenTwoOpt`), 30 FAQ iterations, 100 000 swap checks, step threshold 0.03, 0 additional restarts, seed 42, sequential execution, and no workspace limit (`usize::MAX` bytes).
Change these through `with_method`, `with_max_fw_iterations`, `with_max_swap_checks`, `with_step_tolerance`, `with_restarts`, `with_seed`, `with_execution`, and `with_max_workspace_bytes`; no configuration method fails.
The workspace limit is checked on every solve: 0 bytes gives `InvalidInput`, while a requirement exceeding the limit gives `ResourceLimit`.
`QapMethod` has three variants: `Faq` (FAQ and projection), `TwoOpt` (swap search only from the initial permutation), and `FaqThenTwoOpt` (swap search from the FAQ projection).
Without a supplied initial permutation, the first trial of `TwoOpt` starts from the identity permutation (free facilities assigned to free locations in index order).

Prepare `QapWorkspace` with `QapPlan::workspace(&options)` and call the output-supplying API `solve_into(direction, initial, &options, &mut result, &mut workspace, scope)`.
Create `result` with `QapResult::empty(n)`.
Sequential execution (the default) processes restarts in order using the same workspace, without allocation even on the first call.
Selecting a parallel policy through `with_execution` runs restarts concurrently on the execution context's executor; all per-restart workspaces and results must fit the memory limit.
Results are compared in restart-number order to choose the best.

`QapResult` returns `permutation()`, `objective()`, the selected trial's `relaxation_objective()` and `fw_gap()`, totals `fw_iterations()` and `swap_checks()` over all trials, the selected trial number `selected_restart()`, and `termination()`.
`Converged` of `termination()` means that all included stages (FAQ step criterion and 2-opt local optimality) met their stopping conditions in every trial; it does not prove global optimality.
Even if the selected trial meets its stopping conditions, another trial reaching a limit gives `IterationLimit`.
`IterationLimit` means the FAQ iteration limit, swap-check limit, or parent context's iteration limit was reached; `ProposalLimit`, `Cancelled`, and `TimeLimit` represent parent-context limits or stops.
The `QapPlan::solve` family returns results containing a permutation and objective even at a limit.
The one-shot function `quadratic_assignment(a, b, direction)` solves an unfixed problem with default options and converts limit termination to a `ConvergenceFailure` error.
Use `QapPlan` to inspect results that stop at a limit as well.

## Fixed pairs and workspace reuse

Use a small seven-facility problem to examine how fixed pairs change the optimum and how calls reuse workspace.
Flows $A_{ij}$ are integers 0–9 (zero diagonal, asymmetric), locations are random points on a $10\times10$ grid, and distances $B_{kl}$ are Manhattan distances; both are generated from the random seed.
Inputs are the location fixing facility 0 and the random seed.
The cell creates the plan and workspace once, then solves minimization and maximization with `solve_into` using the same workspace.
It recomputes reported values with `QapPlan::objective` and compares them with optima over all permutations satisfying fixed pairs ($7!=5040$ without fixing, $6!=720$ with fixing).

```rust
//| id: optimize-qap-fixed-pairs
//| ref: optimize-qap-fixed-pairs
//| title: "Minimize and maximize a QAP with fixed pairs using the same workspace"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   pinned: {"type":"select","label":"Location fixing facility 0","options":["Unanchored","Location 0","Location 1","Location 2","Location 3","Location 4","Location 5","Location 6"],"value":"Unanchored"}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","min":1,"max":20,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"pin-site-3","label":"Fix facility 0 at location 3","description":"Search for the optimum among 720 permutations satisfying the fixed pair; facility 0 in the reported permutation occupies location 3.","inputs":{"pinned":"Location 3"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"directions","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"directions","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"directions","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"directions","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"directions","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":5040}
//|   - case: pin-site-3
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"directions","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"directions","path":"/rows/1/5","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"directions","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":720}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, PermutationView, permutation_validation_scratch_bytes};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{QapDirection, QapOptions, QapProblemView, QapResult};
use scioxide_random::{SeedableRng, Xoshiro256StarStar, uniform_index};

let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let n = 7;
let mut rng = Xoshiro256StarStar::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let mut flow = vec![0.0; n * n];
for i in 0..n {
    for j in 0..n {
        if i != j {
            flow[i * n + j] = uniform_index(10, &mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? as f64;
        }
    }
}
let mut sites = Vec::with_capacity(n);
for _ in 0..n {
    let x = uniform_index(10, &mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let y = uniform_index(10, &mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    sites.push((x, y));
}
let distance: Vec<f64> = (0..n * n)
    .map(|k| {
        let ((ax, ay), (bx, by)) = (sites[k / n], sites[k % n]);
        (ax.abs_diff(bx) + ay.abs_diff(by)) as f64
    })
    .collect();
// “Location k” fixes facility 0 at location k.
let fixed: Vec<(usize, usize)> = pinned
    .strip_prefix("Location ")
    .and_then(|k| k.parse().ok())
    .map(|site| vec![(0, site)])
    .unwrap_or_default();
let a = Matrix::from_vec(n, n, flow.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let b = Matrix::from_vec(n, n, distance.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plan = QapProblemView::new(a.view(), b.view(), &fixed)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .prepare_with_limit(1_000_000)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = QapOptions::default();
// Create the plan and workspace only once and reuse them for both directions.
let mut workspace = plan.workspace(&options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut result = QapResult::empty(n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: generate every permutation satisfying the fixed pairs with Heap's algorithm and find the minimum and maximum double sum.
let double_sum = |p: &[usize]| -> f64 {
    (0..n)
        .map(|i| {
            (0..n)
                .map(|j| flow[i * n + j] * distance[p[i] * n + p[j]])
                .sum::<f64>()
        })
        .sum()
};
let allowed = |p: &[usize]| fixed.iter().all(|&(i, k)| p[i] == k);
let mut perm: Vec<usize> = (0..n).collect();
let mut counter = vec![0_usize; n];
let mut values = Vec::new();
if allowed(&perm) {
    values.push(double_sum(&perm));
}
let mut level = 1;
while level < n {
    if counter[level] < level {
        let other = if level % 2 == 0 { 0 } else { counter[level] };
        perm.swap(other, level);
        if allowed(&perm) {
            values.push(double_sum(&perm));
        }
        counter[level] += 1;
        level = 1;
    } else {
        counter[level] = 0;
        level += 1;
    }
}
let low = values.iter().copied().fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
let high = values.iter().copied().fold(f64::NEG_INFINITY, f64::max);
let count = values.len();
let mut scratch = vec![0_u8; permutation_validation_scratch_bytes(n)];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, direction, optimum) in [
    ("Minimize", QapDirection::Minimize, low),
    ("Maximize", QapDirection::Maximize, high),
] {
    plan.solve_into(
        direction,
        None,
        &options,
        &mut result,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let p = result.permutation().ok_or("Missing permutation")?;
    let reported = result.objective().ok_or("Missing objective value")?;
    let view = PermutationView::new(p, &mut scratch).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let recomputed = plan.objective(view).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        name,
        reported,
        (reported - recomputed).abs(),
        optimum,
        (reported - optimum).abs(),
        p[0],
        count,
        result.fw_iterations(),
        result.swap_checks(),
        result.termination().to_string(),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Direction" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Reported objective [flow×distance]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from objective [flow×distance]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Exhaustive optimum [flow×distance]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Difference from optimum [flow×distance]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Location of facility 0", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Permutations examined [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "FAQ iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Swap checks [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "9", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "directions" })
);
```

At the default seed 3 without fixing facility 0, minimization matches optimum 1018 among 5040 permutations and places facility 0 at location 5.
Maximization gives 1275, which is 40 below optimum 1315, but its termination reason is `Converged`.
Fixing facility 0 at location 3 reduces the permutations examined to 720, and the reported permutation places facility 0 at location 3.
The minimization optimum is then 1043, above the unfixed optimum 1018; the reported value 1050 is 7 above it.
Fixing at location 5 matches the unfixed optimal arrangement, so the minimization value remains 1018.
For every input, the reported objective and the value recomputed by `QapPlan::objective` differ by 0.
Both directions are solved consecutively with the same workspace without recreating it.

## Summary

Quadratic assignment links facility flows with location distances through a permutation, has $n!$ candidates, and is generally difficult even to approximate within a constant factor.
FAQ solves a doubly stochastic relaxation using Frank–Wolfe and linear assignment before projecting to a permutation; 2-opt examines swaps with $O(n)$ differences and accepts the first improvement.
Both are heuristics; `Converged` and `fw_gap` = 0 do not establish global optimality.
Known correspondences supplied as fixed pairs are excluded from search; direction is chosen on every solve, and sequential solves with reused workspace allocate nothing.
Check reported objectives by recomputing the original double sum or using `QapPlan::objective`.
