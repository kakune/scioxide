The objective $\tfrac12\|\mathbf{r}\|^2$ of [nonlinear least squares](../nonlinear-least-squares/) gives every observation equal weight and penalizes squared residuals.
This form cannot be used unchanged when measurement uncertainty differs among observations, outliers are present, or residuals are complex.
The objective minimized by `LeastSquares` is generally

$$
f(\boldsymbol\theta) = \frac12\sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i\,c^2\,\rho(t_i), \qquad t_i = \frac{|r_i(\boldsymbol\theta)/\sigma_i|^2}{c^2}
$$


$w_i\ge0$ is the observation weight, $\sigma_i>0$ the residual scale, $c>0$ the loss scale, and $\rho$ the loss function.
The default squared loss $\rho(t)=t$ with $w_i=\sigma_i=1$ recovers $f=\tfrac12\|\mathbf{r}\|^2$.
This page explains the roles of weights and scales, robust-loss properties and linearization, then complex residual handling.

## Observation weights and scales

Supply residual scale $\sigma_i$ through `LeastSquaresProblem::residual_scale`; the default is 1.
Setting $\sigma_i$ to the measurement standard deviation of observation $i$ makes $r_i/\sigma_i$ dimensionless; with independent normally distributed errors, the squared-loss minimizer is the maximum likelihood estimate.
Supply weight $w_i$ through `LeastSquaresProblem::residual_weight`; the default is 1.
$w_i=0$ excludes that observation from the objective.
This differs from observation value $y_i$ being 0; a zero-valued observation remains in the objective with residual $m_i(\boldsymbol\theta)$.

For squared loss, the term is $w_i r_i^2/\sigma_i^2$, so only $w_i/\sigma_i^2$ determines the minimizer.
For robust losses, $\sigma_i$ also determines the transition $|r_i|=c\,\sigma_i$ away from squared loss, while $w_i$ only multiplies the whole term.
`LeastSquares::solve` returns `InvalidInput` when a scale is not positive and finite or a weight is negative or nonfinite.
The result's `residual()` contains raw residuals $\mathbf{r}$ without weights or scales.
Parameter scales $\mathbf{D}$ (`parameter_scale`) apply to parameter coordinates rather than observations and determine the shape of the [LM trust region](oxref:optimize-gauss-newton-lm-derivation).

## Robust loss functions

With outliers, squared loss assigns quadratic penalties to large residuals and pulls the overall fit toward them.
`LeastSquaresLoss` provides losses that reduce contributions of observations with $t_i$ above 1; set scale $c$ using `LeastSquaresOptions::with_loss(loss, c)` (default 1).
Viewing one observation's term as a function $\tfrac12c^2\rho(u^2/c^2)$ of dimensionless residual $u=r/\sigma$, its first derivative $\psi(u)=\rho'(t)\,u$ (influence function) and second derivative $\rho'(t)+2t\rho''(t)$ with respect to $u$ determine the loss properties.

| Loss (`LeastSquaresLoss`) | $\rho(t)$ | $\rho'(t)$ | Curvature $\rho'+2t\rho''$ | Influence function $\psi(u)$ behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Quadratic` (default) | $t$ | $1$ | $1$ | $\psi=u$, unbounded |
| `Huber` | $t$ ($t\le1$), $2\sqrt t-1$ ($t>1$) | $1$, $1/\sqrt t$ | $1$, $0$ | Saturates at $c\,\mathrm{sign}(u)$ for $\lvert u\rvert>c$ |
| `SoftL1` | $2(\sqrt{1+t}-1)$ | $(1+t)^{-1/2}$ | $(1+t)^{-3/2}$ | Increases smoothly, $\lvert\psi\rvert<c$ |
| `Cauchy` | $\ln(1+t)$ | $1/(1+t)$ | $(1-t)/(1+t)^2$ | Maximum $c/2$ at $\lvert u\rvert=c$, approaching 0 for increasingly distant observations (redescending) |

Squared loss gives maximum likelihood estimation for normal errors; one outlier's influence increases proportionally to its residual.
Huber and Soft-L1 have nonnegative curvature, so a linear residual model preserves objective convexity, and each outlier's influence is bounded by $c$.
Huber loss defines Huber's M-estimator; $c=1.345$ (in units of $u$), giving 95% efficiency under normal errors, is commonly used.
Cauchy loss has the form of maximum likelihood estimation for Cauchy errors; its curvature becomes negative for $|u|>c$, so the objective is nonconvex and may have multiple local solutions.
Since different initial values can reach different local solutions with Cauchy loss, compare results starting from, for example, the Huber solution.
Choosing a loss changes assumptions about the error distribution and determines how large a residual is treated as an outlier.

### Curvature-corrected linearization

A simple way to use robust losses in least-squares iteration is iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS), multiplying each row by $\sqrt{w_i\rho'(t_i)}/\sigma_i$.
This gives the correct gradient but ignores the second-order loss term $2t\rho''$.
Following the correction of Triggs et al. (2000), `LeastSquares` replaces the linearization row and residual of observation $i$ by

$$
\tilde{\mathbf{J}}_i = \frac{\sqrt{w_i\,\kappa_i}}{\sigma_i}\,\mathbf{J}_i, \qquad
\tilde r_i = \frac{\sqrt{w_i}\,\rho'(t_i)}{\sigma_i\sqrt{\kappa_i}}\,r_i, \qquad
\kappa_i = \max(\rho'(t_i)+2t_i\rho''(t_i),\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}})
$$


Then $\tilde{\mathbf{J}}^\mathsf{T}\tilde{\mathbf{r}}=\sum_i w_i\rho'(t_i)\mathbf{J}_i^\mathsf{T}r_i/\sigma_i^2=\nabla f$ preserves the gradient and $\tilde{\mathbf{J}}^\mathsf{T}\tilde{\mathbf{J}}$ approximates the Hessian including loss curvature.
For Huber loss at $t>1$ and Cauchy loss at $t>1$, curvature is nonpositive; clamp it from below at $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ to permit taking its square root.
Both dense-Jacobian and products-only problems use this linearization.
Trial acceptance uses actual reduction in the original objective $f$ rather than the linearization, so an objective-increasing point is rejected even if the corrected model is inaccurate.

## Location estimation with an outlier

Fix four observations at 1 and vary a single large observation $b$ to estimate location $\mu$.
Residuals are $r_i=\mu-y_i$ and every Jacobian entry is 1.
Use Gauss–Newton for squared loss and LM for Huber loss (scale $c=1$).
The analytic squared-loss solution is the mean $(4+b)/5$.
For Huber loss, each of the four observations at $|\mu-1|\le1$ contributes gradient $\mu-1$ and the outlier gradient saturates at $-1$, giving $\mu=1.25$ by hand from $4(\mu-1)-1=0$.
Validate against the analytic minimizers of the different objectives rather than agreement between the two methods.
The table shows both location estimates, analytic solutions, differences, and termination reasons.

```rust
//| id: optimize-robust-location
//| ref: optimize-robust-location
//| title: "Check the different analytic minimizers of two losses"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-optimize, scioxide-linalg]
//| inputs:
//|   outlier_value: {"type":"range","label":"Fifth observation value b [-]","description":"Change the outlier location.","min":10,"max":100,"step":10,"value":50}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"outlier-100","label":"b = 100","description":"Squared-loss location is pulled to (4 + 100)/5 = 20.8, while Huber location stays at 1.25.","inputs":{"outlier_value":100}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"location","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":10.8,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"location","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.25,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"location","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"location","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|   - case: outlier-100
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"location","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":20.8,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"location","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.25,"atol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_optimize::{
    LeastSquares, LeastSquaresFn, LeastSquaresGlobalization, LeastSquaresLoss,
    LeastSquaresOptions,
};

let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let b = outlier_value;
let problem = LeastSquaresFn::new(1, 5, move |x: &[f64], r: &mut [f64]| {
    r.copy_from_slice(&[x[0] - 1.0, x[0] - 1.0, x[0] - 1.0, x[0] - 1.0, x[0] - b]);
    Ok(())
})
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
.with_jacobian(|_x: &[f64], j: &mut scioxide_linalg::Matrix<f64>| {
    j.as_mut_slice().fill(1.0);
    Ok(())
});
let ordinary = LeastSquares::new(
    LeastSquaresOptions::default()
        .with_globalization(LeastSquaresGlobalization::gauss_newton()),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
.solve(&problem, &[0.0])
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = LeastSquaresOptions::default().with_loss(LeastSquaresLoss::Huber, 1.0);
let robust = LeastSquares::new(
    options.with_globalization(LeastSquaresGlobalization::levenberg_marquardt()),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
.solve(&problem, &[0.0])
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Check: the squared-loss minimizer is the mean (4 + b)/5.
// The Huber minimizer (c = 1) is 1.25, solving 4(μ − 1) − 1 = 0.
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Loss" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Location estimate μ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Analytical solution [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![
        (
            "Squared (Gauss–Newton)",
            ordinary.x()[0],
            (4.0 + b) / 5.0,
            (ordinary.x()[0] - (4.0 + b) / 5.0).abs(),
            ordinary.termination().to_string()
        ),
        (
            "Huber scale 1 (LM)",
            robust.x()[0],
            1.25,
            (robust.x()[0] - 1.25).abs(),
            robust.termination().to_string()
        ),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "location" })
);
```

At default $b=50$, squared-loss location is pulled by the outlier to 10.8, while Huber location remains 1.25.
Differences from analytic solutions are $1.8\times10^{-15}$ and $2.2\times10^{-16}$, respectively; both terminate with `GradientSmall`.
As $b$ varies from 10 to 100, squared-loss location moves from 2.8 to 20.8, while Huber location stays at 1.25.
The outlier gradient saturates at constant $-c$, so its influence on location is unchanged however far away it is.

## Complex residuals

Impedance measurements and fitting signals with phase involve complex models and observations.
`ComplexLeastSquares` transforms residuals $\mathbf{r}:\mathbb{C}^n\to\mathbb{C}^m$ into real coordinates `LeastSquaresProblem`.
Parameters and residuals interleave components in order $[\operatorname{Re}z_0,\operatorname{Im}z_0,\operatorname{Re}z_1,\operatorname{Im}z_1,\dots]$; the real problem has $2n$ parameter and $2m$ residual components.
The objective is $\tfrac12\sum_i|r_i|^2$; optimization does not order complex numbers.

A real–imaginary pair is treated as one observation (`residual_group_size()` is 2).
Each complex residual has one weight $w_i$ and scale $\sigma_i$; robust loss applies to $|r_i|$.
`ComplexLeastSquares` does not configure weights or scales; both default to 1.
For weighted or scaled complex residuals, implement `LeastSquaresProblem` yourself with `residual_group_size()` returning 2, and return `residual_weight` and `residual_scale` by observation (complex residual) index.
Linearization corrects loss curvature only along $r_i$ (the radial direction).
Differentiation uses real coordinates, so residuals need not be holomorphic (complex differentiable).
The analytic Jacobian supplied through `with_jacobian` is an $2m\times2n$ real matrix for the interleaved real coordinates.
If the derivative of holomorphic $r$ is $r'=\alpha+i\beta$, the corresponding $2\times2$ block is $\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&-\beta\\\beta&\alpha\end{bmatrix}$.

`ComplexLeastSquares::new(residual, parameter_scratch, residual_scratch)` converts values using two caller-supplied scratch slices of lengths $n$ and $m$ without allocation.
If $2n$ or $2m$ does not fit `usize`, it returns `SizeOverflow`.
It mutably borrows scratch slices internally and therefore does not implement `Sync`; recursively calling the same problem during a residual call gives `InvalidInput`.

Consider lock-in detection.
Estimate complex amplitude (phasor) $z$ [V] of signal $y_k=z\,e_k$ synchronized to reference $e_k=\exp(i\omega t_k)$ from $N=32$ samples containing three cycles per measurement interval.
The true value is $z=0.8-0.6i$ V; add a deterministic deviation of amplitude 0.02 V to each sample and an outlier of magnitude $g$ [V] only to the eighth sample ($k=7$).
Residuals are $r_k=z e_k-y_k$; $|e_k|=1$ gives the closed-form squared-loss minimizer $\hat z=\frac1N\sum_k y_k\overline{e_k}$.
Let $\hat z_0$ be the closed-form estimate from samples without the outlier; the squared-loss estimation shift is predicted as $|\hat z-\hat z_0|=g/N$, which provides an independent check.
For Huber loss ($c=0.1$ V), the outlier gradient saturates at magnitude $c$, suggesting a shift bounded around $c/(N-1)\approx3.2\times10^{-3}$ V.
The table shows both loss estimates, shifts from $\hat z_0$, the squared-loss prediction, termination reasons, and iteration counts.

```rust
//| id: optimize-complex-phasor-huber
//| ref: optimize-complex-phasor-huber
//| title: "Estimate an outlier-contaminated phasor with squared and Huber losses"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   glitch: {"type":"range","label":"Outlier magnitude g [V]","description":"Change the absolute outlier magnitude added to the eighth sample.","min":0,"max":5,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"no-glitch","label":"g = 0","description":"Without an outlier all residuals are below c, and both losses return the same estimate.","inputs":{"glitch":0}}
//|   - {"id":"large-glitch","label":"g = 5 V","description":"The squared-loss shift grows to g/N = 0.156 V, while the Huber shift remains 3.1×10⁻³ V.","inputs":{"glitch":5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.0625,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.0625,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0.002,"max":0.005}}
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/1/5","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|   - case: no-glitch
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: large-glitch
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.15625,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"phasor","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0.002,"max":0.005}}
use scioxide_core::{Complex, SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    ComplexLeastSquares, LeastSquares, LeastSquaresLoss, LeastSquaresOptions,
};

let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
// Three cycles in N samples. Reference phase factor e_k = exp(iωt_k), true phasor z = 0.8 − 0.6i [V].
let n = 32_usize;
let truth = Complex::new(0.8_f64, -0.6);
let reference: Vec<Complex<f64>> = (0..n)
    .map(|k| Complex::from_polar(1.0, 2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI * 3.0 * k as f64 / n as f64))
    .collect();
let clean: Vec<Complex<f64>> = reference
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, &e)| {
        let drift = Complex::new(
            0.02 * RealScalar::sin(1.3 * k as f64),
            0.02 * RealScalar::cos(0.7 * k as f64),
        );
        truth * e + drift
    })
    .collect();
// Add an outlier of magnitude g only to the eighth sample (k = 7).
let mut data = clean.clone();
data[7] += Complex::from_polar(glitch, 1.0);
// Residual r_k = z e_k − y_k. Rows in real coordinates (Re z, Im z) are [Re e, −Im e] and [Im e, Re e].
let residual = |z: &[Complex<f64>], r: &mut [Complex<f64>]| -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
    for ((rk, &e), &y) in r.iter_mut().zip(&reference).zip(&data) {
        *rk = z[0] * e - y;
    }
    Ok(())
};
let jacobian = |_z: &[Complex<f64>], j: &mut Matrix<f64>| -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
    for (rows, e) in j.as_mut_slice().chunks_exact_mut(4).zip(&reference) {
        rows.copy_from_slice(&[e.re, -e.im, e.im, e.re]);
    }
    Ok(())
};
let mut parameter_scratch = [Complex::new(0.0, 0.0)];
let mut residual_scratch = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); n];
let problem = ComplexLeastSquares::new(residual, &mut parameter_scratch, &mut residual_scratch)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .with_jacobian(&jacobian);
let quadratic = LeastSquares::new(LeastSquaresOptions::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .solve(&problem, &[0.0, 0.0])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let huber =
    LeastSquares::new(LeastSquaresOptions::default().with_loss(LeastSquaresLoss::Huber, 0.1))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .solve(&problem, &[0.0, 0.0])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Check (formula): squared-loss minimizer without the outlier, ẑ₀ = (1/N) Σ y_k conj(e_k).
let baseline = clean
    .iter()
    .zip(&reference)
    .fold(Complex::new(0.0, 0.0), |sum, (&y, &e)| sum + y * e.conj())
    .unscale(n as f64);
let rows: Vec<(&str, f64, f64, f64, Option<f64>, String, usize)> = [
    ("Squared", &quadratic, Some(glitch / n as f64)),
    ("Huber（c = 0.1 V）", &huber, None),
]
.into_iter()
.map(|(name, fit, predicted)| {
    let z = Complex::new(fit.x()[0], fit.x()[1]);
    (
        name,
        z.re,
        z.im,
        (z - baseline).norm(),
        predicted,
        fit.termination().to_string(),
        fit.iterations(),
    )
})
.collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Loss" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Re ẑ [V]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Im ẑ [V]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Shift from ẑ₀ |ẑ − ẑ₀| [V]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Prediction g/N [V]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "phasor" })
);
```

At default $g=2$ V, the squared-loss estimate shifts from $\hat z_0$ by $0.0625$ V, matching prediction $g/N=2/32$.
The Huber shift $3.1\times10^{-3}$ V is close to the expected $c/(N-1)\approx3.2\times10^{-3}$ V, limiting outlier influence to that magnitude.
Without an outlier ($g=0$), all residuals lie below $c=0.1$ V in the quadratic region, and both losses return the same estimate.
At $g=5$ V, squared-loss shift grows to $0.156$ V, while Huber shift remains $3.1\times10^{-3}$ V, almost independent of $g$.
Both stop with `GradientSmall` as two-parameter problems in real coordinates.

`NoiseModel` is a [stochastic optimization](../stochastic-gradient-and-trust-region/) type declaring relationships between evaluations of a sampled objective evaluated repeatedly at the same point.
It does not configure observation noise in nonlinear least squares; represent measurement uncertainty through $\sigma_i$ and $w_i$ above.

## Summary

For differing measurement standard deviations, supply $\sigma_i$ through `residual_scale`; to exclude or weight observations, supply $w_i$ through `residual_weight`, and remember that `residual()` returns raw residuals.
With outliers, choose convexity-preserving `Huber` or `SoftL1` with a specified scale $c$; combine `Cauchy` with a good initial value.
`LeastSquares` handles robust losses through curvature-corrected linearization and trial acceptance using the original objective.
`ComplexLeastSquares` turns complex residuals into an interleaved real–imaginary problem; robust loss applies to each complex residual's absolute value.
