Find a local minimizer of a real univariate function $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ using only function-value comparisons without derivatives.
Line searches in multivariate optimization often need only an approximation satisfying [Armijo or Wolfe conditions](oxref:optimize-armijo-condition); here the univariate minimizer itself is sought to tolerance.
Line minimization in `Powell` also uses the methods on this page.
Root finding seeks a sign change with $f(x) = 0$, whereas minimization compares function values, giving coarser attainable accuracy ([stopping criteria and attainable accuracy](#stopping-criteria-and-attainable-accuracy)).

## Minimum brackets and unimodality

Three points $a < b < c$ form a minimum bracket when they satisfy

$$
f(b) \le f(a), \qquad f(b) \le f(c)
$$

with at least one inequality strict.
If $f$ is continuous, the bracket's open interval $(a, c)$ contains a local minimizer.
A function strictly decreasing to one interior point and strictly increasing after it is unimodal; shrinking its bracket converges to its minimizer.
For a multimodal function, shrinking a bracket finds one local minimizer inside it, which need not minimize over the entire interval.

The basic bracket contraction places one new interior point $u$ and compares $f(u)$ with $f(b)$ to select a new bracket.
If $f(u) < f(b)$, $u$ becomes the new middle point; otherwise $u$ becomes a new endpoint.
Either way, the new bracket lies inside the old one and needs one evaluation.

## Golden-section search

The placement of the new point determines contraction speed.
Normalize the interval length to 1 and place the middle point at $1 - \tau$ from the left endpoint ($1/2 < \tau < 1$).
Placing the new point symmetrically at $\tau$ gives new interval length $\tau$ for either comparison result.
To retain the ratio at the next stage, the surviving middle point must occupy the same relative position in the new interval; $1 - \tau = \tau^2$ gives

$$
\tau = \frac{\sqrt5 - 1}{2} \approx 0.618
$$


Golden-section search converges linearly, shrinking the interval by a factor $\tau$ per evaluation; shrinking length $L$ to $\delta$ needs approximately $\ln(\delta/L)/\ln\tau \approx 4.78\log_{10}(L/\delta)$ evaluations.
Among comparison-only methods, Fibonacci search contracts the interval most for a fixed evaluation count; golden-section search is its limit without a fixed count (Kiefer 1953).

`ScalarMinimumMethod::GoldenSection` places the new point from middle point $x$ toward the longer subinterval by $(3 - \sqrt5)/2 \approx 0.382$ times its length.
If rounding makes the new point equal $x$ or fall outside the interval, it tries the adjacent floating-point number to $x$; if that also cannot be placed, it stops with `Stagnation`.

## Brent's method

Near the minimizer, $f$ is well approximated by a parabola.
The vertex of the parabola through points $(x, f_x)$, $(w, f_w)$, and $(v, f_v)$ is

$$
u = x - \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{(x - w)^2 (f_x - f_v) - (x - v)^2 (f_x - f_w)}{(x - w)(f_x - f_v) - (x - v)(f_x - f_w)}
$$


Successive parabolic interpolation using this point converges superlinearly with order about 1.32 near a minimizer with positive second derivative, but alone does not guarantee convergence from farther away.

Brent's method (Brent 1973, Chapter 5) forms a parabolic interpolation point using best point $x$, second-best point $w$, and $v$ as the preceding $w$, accepting interpolation only when safe.
`ScalarMinimumMethod::Brent` accepts the interpolation point when all these conditions hold.

- The vertex is strictly inside the bracket and differs from $x$.
- $|u - x|$ is less than half the preceding step width (the longer subinterval width if the preceding step was golden-section).

Otherwise it uses the golden-section point.
The second condition prevents repeated interpolation steps without contraction, switching to golden-section points when interpolation performs poorly.
Brent's original algorithm compares against the step width two steps earlier; `Brent` uses the immediately preceding width.
`Brent` is the default of `ScalarMinimizer`.

## Stopping criteria and attainable accuracy

`ScalarMinimizer` stops with `Converged` when bracket $a < x < b$ meets both following conditions.

$$
|b - a| \le \mathrm{atol}_x + \mathrm{rtol}_x \max(|a|, |b|), \qquad
\max(|f(a) - f(x)|, |f(b) - f(x)|) \le \mathrm{atol}_f + \mathrm{rtol}_f \max(|f(a)|, |f(x)|, |f(b)|)
$$

The position tolerance is `StepTolerance` and objective tolerance `ResidualTolerance`; both have default absolute and relative parts $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$.

Near minimizer $x^*$, $f(x^* + \delta) \approx f(x^*) + \tfrac12 f''(x^*)\delta^2$, so objective differences are hidden by rounding of size $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}|f(x^*)|$ over the range

$$
|\delta| \approx \sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}|f(x^*)|}{|f''(x^*)|}}
$$


Function-value comparisons alone determine relative position only to about $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}} \approx 1.5\times10^{-8}$; default tolerances match this limit.
Smaller tolerances do not improve positional accuracy and instead add evaluations or lead to `Stagnation`.

## Scalar minimization API

`ScalarObjective` is a univariate objective returning a value; a `Fn(S) -> Result<S, SciOxideError>` closure implements it directly.
Nonfinite values reject that trial and are treated as greater than every finite value.
An objective error stops search and propagates unchanged.

`ScalarMinimizer::new(method, options)` holds the method and options, and `minimize(&objective, start)` solves.
`ScalarMinimumStart` selects how to start.

- `Bounded(interval)` searches the closed interval $[a, b]$.
  It evaluates both endpoints and the midpoint, taking the best point as its middle.
  The minimizer may be an endpoint.
- `Bracket(bracket)` starts from `MinimumBracket`.
  It reevaluates all three points to check bracket conditions.
- `Seeds(a, b)` searches downhill for a bracket from two distinct points.
  From the lower-valued point $b$, it advances by $c = b + 2(b - a)$, doubling the previous width each time.

`bracket_minimum(&objective, a, b)` only finds a bracket without shrinking it, placing the bracket in the result's `bracket()`.
`ScalarMinimumOptions` configures `with_step_tolerance`, `with_function_tolerance`, `with_max_iterations` (including bracket search, default 1000), and `with_max_evaluations` (including initial evaluations, default 2000).
Each iteration uses at most one evaluation and stores only the three observed points, so there is no output-writing workspace.

In `ScalarMinimumResult`, `x()` and `fun()` are the best observed point and value, `samples()` the final three points, and `bracket()` the final bracket.
When the minimizer is an interval endpoint or bracket search has not finished, `bracket()` is `None`.
`termination()` returns `OptimizeTermination`; only `Converged` indicates success (`is_success()`).
Limits give `IterationLimit` or `EvaluationLimit`; rounding preventing placement of a point or constant objective values along the bracketing direction gives `Stagnation`; all return the best point.
`require_success()` converts unsuccessful termination into an error.
`fun_evals()` and `iterations()` include bracket search and initial evaluations in their counts.

`minimize_scoped` solves within a parent solve context and also counts evaluations against the parent's limit.
The one-shot function `minimize_scalar(f, a, b)` applies Brent's method with default options on interval $[a, b]$, returning unsuccessful termination through `Err`.

## Interior and endpoint minima

The next cell minimizes $f(x) = (x - v)^2$ over interval $[-1, 1]$ using golden-section search and Brent's method.
The input is parabola vertex $v$.
The table shows both methods' minimizers, differences from analytic solution $\min(1, \max(-1, v))$, objective evaluations and iterations, bracket availability, and termination reasons.
Moving the vertex outside the interval moves the minimizer to an endpoint.

```rust
//| id: optimize-scalar-brent-golden
//| ref: optimize-scalar-brent-golden
//| title: "Examine interior and endpoint minima with golden-section and Brent searches"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   vertex: {"type":"range","label":"Parabola vertex v [-]","description":"Moving outside [−1, 1] makes the minimizer an endpoint.","min":-2,"max":2,"step":0.25,"value":0.25}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"vertex-outside","label":"v = 2 (outside interval)","description":"The minimizer moves to endpoint x = 1; both methods match it in 23 evaluations. There is no final bracket.","inputs":{"vertex":2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/6","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":38,"max":44}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":20,"max":30}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: vertex-outside
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"methods","path":"/rows/1/5","equals":"None"}
use scioxide_core::Interval;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    ScalarMinimizer, ScalarMinimumMethod, ScalarMinimumOptions, ScalarMinimumStart,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig10 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let interval = Interval::new(-1.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: the minimizer on [−1, 1] is the vertex projected into that interval.
let exact = vertex.clamp(-1.0, 1.0);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut brent = None;
for method in [
    ScalarMinimumMethod::GoldenSection,
    ScalarMinimumMethod::Brent,
] {
    let result = ScalarMinimizer::new(method, ScalarMinimumOptions::default())
        .minimize(
            &|x: f64| Ok((x - vertex) * (x - vertex)),
            ScalarMinimumStart::Bounded(interval),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let bracket = if result.bracket().is_some() {
        "Available"
    } else {
        "None"
    };
    rows.push((
        format!("{method:?}"),
        result.x(),
        (result.x() - exact).abs(),
        result.fun_evals(),
        result.iterations(),
        bracket,
        result.termination().to_string(),
    ));
    brent = Some(result);
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Minimizer [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig10 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Analytical-solution difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Final bracket" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "methods" })
);
// Use the default method's (Brent's) termination reason as the cell's conclusion.
let result = brent.ok_or("No Brent result")?;
let outcome = if result.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Brent's method met position and objective tolerances")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Brent's method stopped before meeting tolerances and returned the best point")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(result.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Objective evaluations", result.fun_evals() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Analytical-solution difference", (result.x() - exact).abs(), None)
);
```

At default $v = 0.25$, golden-section search reaches difference $4.0\times10^{-9}$ from the analytic solution in 41 evaluations, while Brent reaches zero difference in 25; both terminate with `Converged`.
Contracting an interval of width 2 to position threshold $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}(1 + 0.25) \approx 1.9\times10^{-8}$ takes $\ln(1.9\times10^{-8}/2)/\ln 0.618 \approx 38.4$ steps, agreeing well with golden-section search's 38 iterations.
For an exactly parabolic objective, Brent interpolation gives the vertex itself; remaining evaluations shrink bracket width to tolerance.
At $v = 2$ the minimizer moves to endpoint $x = 1$; both methods match it in 23 evaluations.
At an endpoint minimum, the final three points fail bracket conditions, so `bracket()` returns `None`.

## Bracket search and local minima

For a multimodal function, the start determines which local minimizer is reached.
The example is $f(x) = \sin x + \sin(10x/3)$, used to test univariate global optimization (one of Hansen, Jaumard, and Lu's 1992 test functions).
The next cell searches for a bracket from points $x_0$ and $x_0 + 0.1$ with `bracket_minimum`, then shrinks it using Brent's method.
$[0, 8]$ has four local minimizers at $x \approx 1.398$, $3.387$, $5.146$, and $7.000$; the published minimizer over interval $[2.7, 7.5]$ is $x^* = 5.145735$ with $f(x^*) = -1.899599$.
The input is starting point $x_0$.
The plot shows $f$, the bracket found, and the minimizer.
The table gives the three bracket points, minimizer, $f$ value, closed-form $f'(x) = \cos x + \tfrac{10}{3}\cos(10x/3)$ and $f''(x)$ (independent check), evaluation counts for bracketing and contraction, and termination reason.

```rust
//| id: optimize-scalar-seeds-local-minima
//| ref: optimize-scalar-seeds-local-minima
//| title: "Find a bracket from two points and reach a local minimum of a multimodal function"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   start: {"type":"range","label":"Starting point x₀ [-]","description":"The second point is x₀ + 0.1. Expand the bracket downhill from these two points.","min":2,"max":7.5,"step":0.1,"value":4.5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"left-basin","label":"x₀ = 2.5","description":"Converges to the left valley's local minimizer x = 3.387 (f = −1.200).","inputs":{"start":2.5}}
//|   - {"id":"right-basin","label":"x₀ = 6.6","description":"Converges to the right valley's local minimizer x = 7.000 (f = −0.317).","inputs":{"start":6.6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"seeds","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":5.145735,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"seeds","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":-1.899599,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: left-basin
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"seeds","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":3.387252,"atol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: right-basin
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"seeds","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":7.000149,"atol":1e-5}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_optimize::{ScalarMinimizer, ScalarMinimumStart};

let sig7 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 7 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let f = |x: f64| RealScalar::sin(x) + RealScalar::sin(10.0 * x / 3.0);
let objective = |x: f64| Ok(f(x));
let solver = ScalarMinimizer::<f64>::default();
// Bracket search: expand the width downhill from x₀ and x₀ + 0.1 by factors of 2.
let found = solver
    .bracket_minimum(&objective, start, start + 0.1)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let bracket = found.bracket().ok_or("No bracket found")?;
let [a, b, c] = bracket.samples().map(|sample| sample.x());
// Contract from the bracket with Brent's method. Reevaluate its three points.
let result = solver
    .minimize(&objective, ScalarMinimumStart::Bracket(bracket))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let x = result.x();
// Independent check: closed-form f′(x) and f″(x). At a minimizer f′ ≈ 0 and f″ > 0.
let first = RealScalar::cos(x) + 10.0 / 3.0 * RealScalar::cos(10.0 * x / 3.0);
let second = -RealScalar::sin(x) - 100.0 / 9.0 * RealScalar::sin(10.0 * x / 3.0);
let curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=300)
    .map(|i| {
        let t = 8.0 * i as f64 / 300.0;
        [t, f(t)]
    })
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "f(x) = sin x + sin(10x/3)", "points": curve })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "x [-]",
        "yLabel": "f(x) [-]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 8.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "range", "axis": "x", "from": a, "to": c, "label": "Bracket found" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": x, "label": "Minimizer" }
        ]
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Left bracket endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Bracket middle [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Right bracket endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Minimizer [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "f(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "f′(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "f″(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Bracketing evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Contraction evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "9", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
let row = (
    a,
    b,
    c,
    x,
    result.fun(),
    first,
    second,
    found.fun_evals(),
    result.fun_evals(),
    result.termination().to_string(),
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "seeds" })
);
let outcome = if result.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Converged to a local minimizer inside the bracket")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped before meeting tolerances and returned the best point")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(result.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("f′(x)", first, None)
        .diagnostic(
            "Objective evaluations",
            (found.fun_evals() + result.fun_evals()) as f64,
            None
        )
);
```

At default $x_0 = 4.5$, bracketing finds $(4.8, 5.2, 6.0)$ in 5 evaluations; Brent converges to $x = 5.145735$ with $f = -1.899599$ in 11 evaluations (including reevaluation of the three bracket points).
This matches the published minimizer on interval $[2.7, 7.5]$ to the displayed digits; the closed-form $f'(x)$ is $-3.9\times10^{-10}$, with $f''(x) = 11.93 > 0$.
$x_0 = 2.5$ converges to the left valley's $x = 3.387$ ($f = -1.200$), while $x_0 = 6.6$ reaches the right valley's $x = 7.000$ ($f = -0.317$).
Both are local minima with $f' \approx 0$ and $f'' > 0$, but neither minimizes over interval $[2.7, 7.5]$.
At $x_0 = 2$, the second point has a larger value, so the bracket expands left and reaches shallow local minimum $x = 1.398$ ($f = -0.0135$).
`Converged` indicates convergence to a local minimizer inside the bracket, not which valley was entered.

## Summary

Scalar minimization by function-value comparisons retains and contracts a minimum bracket: golden-section search contracts the interval by 0.618 per evaluation, and Brent converges superlinearly when parabolic interpolation applies.
Function comparisons determine a smooth minimizer's position only to relative accuracy about $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$, so tolerances below the defaults do not improve accuracy.
Use `Bounded` for interval search and `Seeds` for two-point search; distinguish endpoint minima by `bracket()` being `None`.
Multimodal results are local minimizers determined by the start; compare different starts or combine with [global search](../global-search/).
