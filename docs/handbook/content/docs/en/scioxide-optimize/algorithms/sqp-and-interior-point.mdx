Sequential quadratic programming (SQP) applies Newton's method directly to the KKT conditions.
Rather than increasing a penalty parameter to satisfy constraints as in [penalty and augmented Lagrangian methods](../penalty-and-augmented-lagrangian/), it obtains multipliers from the subproblem solution at each iteration.
Notation and residual definitions follow [nonlinear constraints and KKT conditions](../nonlinear-constraints/).
Inequalities handled through slacks and logarithmic barriers are explained in [nonlinear interior-point methods](../nonlinear-interior-point/).

## Sequential quadratic programming (SQP)

First consider equality constraints only.
Viewing KKT conditions $\nabla f+\mathbf{J}_c^\mathsf{T}\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{0}$ as simultaneous equations in unknowns $(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ and applying Newton's method gives correction $\mathbf{p}$ and next multipliers $\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k+1}$ satisfying

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \nabla^2_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{L}_k & \mathbf{J}_c(\mathbf{x}_k)^\mathsf{T} \\ \mathbf{J}_c(\mathbf{x}_k) & \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p} \\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k+1} \end{bmatrix}
= -\begin{bmatrix} \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k) \\ \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}_k) \end{bmatrix}
$$

where $\mathbf{J}_c$ is the equality constraint Jacobian.
This is exactly the KKT system for minimizing quadratic model $\nabla f^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p}+\tfrac12\mathbf{p}^\mathsf{T}\nabla^2_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{L}_k\mathbf{p}$ subject to linearized constraints $\mathbf{c}+\mathbf{J}_c\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{0}$.
Adding linearized inequalities gives correction $\mathbf{p}$ at the current point $(\mathbf{x}_k, \boldsymbol{\lambda}_k, \boldsymbol{\mu}_k)$ as the solution of the following quadratic programming (QP) subproblem.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^n} \quad & \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p} + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{p}^\mathsf{T} \mathbf{B}_k \mathbf{p} \\
\text{subject to} \quad & c_i(\mathbf{x}_k) + \nabla c_i(\mathbf{x}_k)^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p} = 0 \quad (i \in \mathcal{E}) \\
& g_j(\mathbf{x}_k) + \nabla g_j(\mathbf{x}_k)^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{p} \le 0 \quad (j \in \mathcal{I}) \\
& \mathbf{l} - \mathbf{x}_k \le \mathbf{p} \le \mathbf{u} - \mathbf{x}_k
\end{aligned}
$$

$\mathbf{B}_k$ approximates the Lagrangian Hessian.
Solving the subproblem gives step $\mathbf{p}$ and the next multiplier estimates together.
Near the solution, an unchanged active set and exact Hessian $\mathbf{B}_k$ give quadratic convergence as in Newton's method.
A quasi-Newton approximation offers at most superlinear convergence.

`Sqp` passes variable bounds directly as a step box in the subproblem, as in the final row above, rather than as linearized rows.
The initial point is projected into the box, and all trial points remain inside.
The objective and constraints are normalized using derivatives at the initial point, as explained in [KKT residuals and termination](oxref:optimize-kkt-residuals).

## Merit function and second-order correction

Far from a solution, a Newton correction may reduce neither objective nor violation, so a merit function decides step acceptance.
`Sqp` uses the normalized problem's L1 merit function

$$
\phi_\nu(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x})+\nu\Big(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{E}}|c_i(\mathbf{x})|+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}\max(0,g_j(\mathbf{x}))\Big)
$$

and halves step length $\alpha$ from 1 until the Armijo condition holds.
Penalty parameter $\nu$ starts at 2 and increases to stay at least 1 plus the largest absolute subproblem multiplier, including bound multipliers.
$\nu$ never decreases.
If $\mathbf{B}_k$ is positive definite and $\nu$ exceeds the largest absolute multiplier, the directional derivative of $\phi_\nu$ along subproblem solution $\mathbf{p}$ is negative, making $\mathbf{p}$ a descent direction.
A nonnegative directional derivative skips line search and enters the restoration phase described below.

If the first trial $\alpha=1$ is rejected, one second-order correction is attempted.
Solve $\min\tfrac12\|\mathbf{d}\|_2^2$ subject to constraints linearized to match constraint values at the trial point; if correction $\mathbf{d}$ is shorter than the original step, add it to the trial point and reassess.
With an L1 merit function, constraint curvature can increase violation and reject even a full Newton correction near the solution (the Maratos effect).
Second-order correction returns the trial point along the curved constraint surface to avoid this rejection.

$\mathbf{B}_k$ starts at $\mathbf{B}_0=\mathbf{I}/\max(1,\|\mathbf{x}_0\|_\infty)$ and receives BFGS updates from Lagrangian gradient difference $\mathbf{y}_k$ and displacement $\mathbf{s}_k$.
Constrained problems do not guarantee $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k>0$.
Powell damping therefore moves $\mathbf{y}_k$ toward $\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k$ until $\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{y}_k\ge0.2\,\mathbf{s}_k^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{B}_k\mathbf{s}_k$, preserving positive definiteness of $\mathbf{B}_k$.
`Sqp` always uses this approximation instead of the exact Hessian of `ConstrainedProblem::with_lagrangian_hessian`, so quadratic convergence is not expected.

## Subproblem solution and restoration

The default `DenseSqpSubproblem` solves subproblems with a primal active-set method.
It first solves with only equalities active; if this satisfies every linearized inequality and the step box, that point is the subproblem solution.
Otherwise a problem augmented by one elastic variable representing violation constructs an initial point satisfying linearized constraints (the elastic phase).
If the elastic phase cannot reduce violation to tolerance, linearized constraints are considered inconsistent and `SuspectedInfeasible` is returned.
This does not imply infeasibility of the nonlinear problem.

Each active-set stage selects linearly independent active constraint normals by column-pivoted Householder QR.
Equality-constrained KKT systems use `DenseKktProvider` ($\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{L}^\mathsf{T}$ with Ruiz equilibration and bounded rook pivoting); singular least-squares systems use minimum-norm SVD solutions.
The subproblem objective must be positive definite; supplying nonpositive-definite $\mathbf{B}_k$ gives `InvalidInput`.
Subproblem solutions marked `Converged` are checked again against subproblem KKT conditions: linearized constraints, stationarity, multiplier signs, and complementarity.
Failure clears the active set and retries once; another failure gives `Stagnation`.

A subproblem returning `SuspectedInfeasible` or a line search unable to accept a step triggers bounded restoration.
For equality and violated inequality rows $A$, restoration solves linear least squares minimizing $\tfrac12\|\mathbf{J}_A\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{c}_A\|_2^2$ within the step box, then halves the step length to seek reduced violation.
It returns to the original iteration once violation is within tolerance or at most half its value at restoration entry.
If violation only decreases, it resolves least squares from there, repeating at most 20 times (`with_max_restoration_iterations`).
Failure to reduce violation, failure to return within 20 iterations, or starting restoration at an already feasible point stops with `Stagnation`.
After restoration, $\mathbf{B}_k$ resets to the identity and multipliers are estimated again from 0.

Termination statuses are as follows.

- `Converged`: only when every original-problem KKT residual meets its threshold; a small subproblem step alone does not establish stationarity.
- `StepSmall`: at a feasible point, subproblem step $\|\mathbf{p}\|_2$ falls below step tolerance twice consecutively; this is not success.
- `Stagnation`: restoration fails or a subproblem solution fails verification.
- `IterationLimit`: the iteration limit is reached.
  A subproblem stopping for its iteration limit or another reason also propagates that termination reason directly.

Defaults of `SqpOptions` are 1000 outer iterations, 1000 subproblem active-set iterations (`with_max_subproblem_iterations`), and 20 restoration iterations; step tolerance (default absolute and relative parts $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}$), differentiation method, `OptimalityTolerances`, and time limit are also configurable.
The one-shot function `minimize_constrained(f, &x0, &constraints)` solves an objective closure with default `Sqp` (forward-difference derivatives), returning unsuccessful termination through `Err`.

## Subproblem providers and accepted state

`SqpSubproblem` represents the current linearized subproblem, borrowing public fields for objective model `objective`, constraint Jacobian `jacobian`, constraint values `values`, equality row count `equalities`, step bounds `bounds`, tolerances `tolerances`, and iteration limit `max_iterations`.
Linearized constraints are `values + jacobian * d`, equalities first and inequalities oriented as $\le0$.
`SqpSubproblemObjective` distinguishes quadratic models (positive definite `hessian` and `gradient`) from least-squares models (`design` and `target`); restoration uses the latter.
`SqpSubproblemReport` is a solved subproblem result, constructed by following `new(termination)` with `with_active_rank`, `with_ambiguous_multipliers`, and `with_inertia`, and read through same-named accessors.
Return subproblem success as `Converged`; returning `GradientSmall` gives `InvalidInput`.
A small objective gradient does not establish constrained KKT conditions.

A type implementing `SqpSubproblemProvider`, configured through `SqpWorkspace::with_subproblem_provider`, replaces `DenseSqpSubproblem`.
`Sqp` applies the same checks to provider solutions and separately recomputes original KKT residuals at accepted points, independently of subproblem success.

The `SqpState` returned by `SqpWorkspace::state()` contains multipliers and active set of the last accepted point in original units.
`multipliers()` returns nonlinear row multipliers (equalities first), `lower_multipliers()` and `upper_multipliers()` return per-variable bound multipliers, `step()` the last accepted step, and `active_constraints()` active constraint indices.
Index $i<m$ represents nonlinear row $i$; $m+2j$ and $m+2j+1$ represent the lower and upper bounds of variable $j$ ($m$ is the nonlinear row count).
Passing `SqpState` to `SqpWorkspace::set_warm_start` initializes the next solve's multipliers and active set; $\mathbf{B}_k$ is reinitialized.
A state with mismatched dimensions or negative inequality multipliers makes the next `minimize_into` return `InvalidInput`.

The `SqpDiagnostics` in `OptimizeDiagnostics::Sqp(sqp, globalization)` returned by `OptimizeResult::method_diagnostics()` contains the last accepted subproblem's active-normal rank `active_rank()`, possible multiplier nonuniqueness `multipliers_ambiguous()`, and inertia `inertia()`.
Other diagnostics include second-order correction attempts and acceptances (`second_order_corrections()`, `accepted_corrections()`), restoration count `restoration_solves()`, and fixed-variable derivative availability `fixed_derivatives_available()`.
Check prepared capacity with `SqpWorkspaceRequirements`.

## Dependent equalities and multipliers

Minimize $\tfrac12(x^2+y^2)$ subject to $x+y=b$.
The independent analytic solution is $(b/2,b/2)$ with equality multiplier $\lambda=-b/2$.
Adding twice the same equality leaves the optimum unchanged but makes the normals parallel, violating LICQ.
Every multiplier pair satisfying $\lambda_1+2\lambda_2=-b/2$ meets stationarity; the two equality multipliers are not unique.

The next cell solves from the origin using `Sqp` and displays the point, equality violation, stationarity residual recomputed from multipliers, accepted-point multipliers, and rank and ambiguity flag in `SqpDiagnostics`.
Inputs are right-hand side $b$ and whether a dependent constraint is present.

```rust
//| id: optimize-sqp-kkt-residuals
//| ref: optimize-sqp-kkt-residuals
//| title: "Compare original KKT residuals with and without a dependent constraint"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   rhs: {"type":"range","label":"Equality right-hand side b [-]","min":0.5,"max":2,"step":0.25,"value":1}
//|   redundant: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Add the doubled equality","value":true}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"independent","label":"No dependent constraint","description":"Active-normal rank remains 1 but the multiplier becomes unique, leaving only multiplier −0.5.","inputs":{"redundant":false}}
//|   - {"id":"rhs-two","label":"Right-hand side b = 2","description":"The solution moves to (1, 1) and the first multiplier becomes −1.","inputs":{"rhs":2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"point","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"point","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"point","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"multipliers","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-0.5,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rank","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"rank","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":true}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: independent
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"point","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"multipliers","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-0.5,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rank","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":false}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: rhs-two
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"point","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"point","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rank","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":true}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    ConstrainedProblem, ConstraintFn, Constraints, ObjectiveFn, OptimizeDiagnostics,
    OptimizeResult, Sqp,
};
let b = rhs;
let objective = ObjectiveFn::new(2, |x: &[f64]| Ok(0.5 * (x[0] * x[0] + x[1] * x[1])))
    .with_gradient(|x: &[f64], g: &mut [f64]| {
        g.copy_from_slice(x);
        Ok(())
    });
// The second equality is twice the first, 2(x + y − b) = 0, making the normals parallel.
let mut constraints = Constraints::new(2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let scales: &[f64] = if redundant { &[1.0, 2.0] } else { &[1.0] };
for &scale in scales {
    let c = ConstraintFn::new(2, move |x: &[f64]| Ok(scale * (x[0] + x[1] - b))).with_jacobian(
        move |_: &[f64], row: &mut [f64]| {
            row.copy_from_slice(&[scale, scale]);
            Ok(())
        },
    );
    constraints = constraints.equality(c).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
let problem = ConstrainedProblem::new(&objective, &constraints).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solver = Sqp::default();
let mut work = solver.workspace(&problem).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut result = OptimizeResult::empty();
solver
    .minimize_into(
        &problem,
        &[0.0, 0.0],
        &mut result,
        &mut work,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: compute stationarity x + Σ k λ_k from the original equation by summing normals (k, k) weighted by their multipliers.
let multipliers = work.state().multipliers();
let weighted: f64 = scales.iter().zip(multipliers).map(|(k, v)| k * v).sum();
let x = result.x();
let stationarity = (x[0] + weighted).abs().max((x[1] + weighted).abs());
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "x [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
        { "key": "1", "label": "y [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Equality violation [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Stationarity residual (check) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Analytical-solution difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Termination reason" }
    ]),
    vec![(
        x[0],
        x[1],
        (x[0] + x[1] - b).abs(),
        stationarity,
        (x[0] - b / 2.0).abs().max((x[1] - b / 2.0).abs()),
        result.termination().to_string()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "point" })
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Equality index", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Accepted-point multiplier [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 }
    ]),
    multipliers.iter().copied().enumerate().collect::<Vec<_>>(),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "multipliers" })
);
let OptimizeDiagnostics::Sqp(sqp, _) = result.method_diagnostics() else {
    return Err("No SQP diagnostics".to_string());
};
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Active-normal rank", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Nonunique multipliers" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Stationarity [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    vec![(
        sqp.active_rank(),
        sqp.multipliers_ambiguous(),
        result.stationarity(),
        result.iterations()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rank" })
);
let termination = result.termination();
let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The original problem's KKT residuals meet tolerance")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped before satisfying the KKT residual tests")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(termination.to_string())
        .diagnostic("Iterations", result.iterations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Stationarity residual (check)", stationarity, None)
);
```

At default $b=1$ with the dependent constraint, `Sqp` reaches $(0.5, 0.5)$ in 1 iteration and terminates with `Converged`; equality violation and stationarity residual are 0.
At the origin the objective gradient is zero, making the objective scale 1 and the normalized problem's $\mathbf{B}_0=\mathbf{I}$ equal the exact Hessian.
Thus the first QP subproblem solution is already the solution.
The multipliers $(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=(-0.5,0)$ are one solution assigning all weight to the first constraint.
Active-normal rank is 1 and `multipliers_ambiguous()` is `true`, reporting dependent normals.
Removing the dependent constraint leaves rank 1, changes `multipliers_ambiguous()` to `false`, and uniquely determines multiplier $-0.5$.
At $b=2$, the solution moves to $(1,1)$ and the first multiplier becomes $-1$.
With dependent constraints, do not require individual multipliers to match; check that the weighted normal sum $\lambda_1+2\lambda_2$ balances the objective gradient.

## Summary

SQP solves a quadratic model under linearized constraints and obtains multipliers from the subproblem solution at every iteration.
`Sqp` combines a damped-BFGS Hessian approximation, Armijo search on an L1 merit function, one second-order correction, and bounded restoration; variable bounds are enforced as a subproblem step box.
`Converged` occurs only when original KKT residuals meet their thresholds; `StepSmall`, `Stagnation`, and `IterationLimit` are not success.
Read multipliers from `SqpWorkspace::state()` and subproblem rank and second-order correction counts from `method_diagnostics()`.
With dependent constraints, individual multipliers are not unique; inspect `multipliers_ambiguous()` and assess whether the weighted normal sum balances the objective gradient.
