Parameter estimation in scioxide-stats constructs objectives such as negative log-likelihood or moment mismatch but provides no minimization algorithm.
A caller-supplied solver performs minimization through the traits `FitSolver` (fitting) and `TransportSolver` (discrete transport).
scioxide-optimize supplies implementations of both traits.
This page explains which types support each estimation problem, how solver termination reasons map to statistical statuses, and which side exposes each result.

## Connecting optimization and statistical estimation

The scioxide-stats `fit_into` (and its simpler form `fit_with_options`) fits in four stages.

1. Check shapes, starting points, solver support for requested bounds and integer constraints, and availability of residuals or derivatives required by the solver.
2. Check the objective domain at the starting point.
3. Run the solver through `FitSolver::solve`.
4. Check that the result satisfies bounds and integer constraints, then reevaluate the objective there.

Output parameters are written only after stage 4 succeeds.
Optimization obtains the point and termination reason; statistics handles data meaning, weights, estimators, and uncertainty.
Changing solvers does not also change likelihood or observation-noise assumptions.

| Type | Implemented trait | Construction | Supported problems |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LeastSquaresFitSolver` | `FitSolver` | `new(solver: LeastSquares<S>, parameters, residuals)` | Residual-returning objectives (moment or quantile matching). No bounds, integer variables, or operator Jacobians |
| `LbfgsBFitSolver` | `FitSolver` | `new(solver: LbfgsB<S>, dimension)` | Scalar objectives with finite, one-sided, or fixed bounds. No integer variables |
| `SqpFitSolver` | `FitSolver` | `new(solver: Sqp<S>, constraints: Constraints<S>)` | Nonlinearly constrained objectives. Request bounds must match constraint bounds |
| `SimplexTransportSolver` | `TransportSolver` | `new(solver: SimplexSolver<S>, max_pairs, max_model_bytes)` | Transport LP with marginal equality constraints |

`LeastSquaresFitSolver::new` fixes parameter and residual counts and allocates dense storage at construction.
Empty shapes, nonsquared losses, and `LeastSquares` using `LeastSquaresLinearSolver::Lsmr` are rejected with `InvalidInput`.
Robust losses and operator-only linearization change the statistical meaning of residuals and are unavailable through this type.
Objectives without residuals (maximum likelihood) give `NotImplemented` in stage 1.
Tolerance and globalization come from the supplied `LeastSquares`; the iteration limit is the smaller of its configured limit and the request's remaining iteration allowance.
`result()` returns the last solve's `LeastSquaresResult`; read optimization termination, numerical rank, and condition estimate there.
Its `cost()` is $\tfrac12\sum_i r_i^2$, differing by a factor of 2 from the scioxide-stats matching objective $\sum_i r_i^2$.

`LbfgsBFitSolver` receives an analytic gradient from the request's `derivatives` or approximates it using the differentiation policy configured in `LbfgsB`.
`SqpFitSolver` owns `Constraints`, and `result()` returns `OptimizeResult` including KKT and constraint residuals.

The three `FitSolver` types map optimization termination reasons into the statistical classification `StatsTermination`.

| Optimization termination reason | Statistical status |
|---|---|
| `Converged`, `GradientSmall` | `Converged` |
| Limits such as `IterationLimit`, `EvaluationLimit`, or `MemoryLimit` | `BudgetExhausted` |
| `Cancelled`, `TimeLimit` | `Stopped` |
| Others (such as `StepSmall` or `Stagnation`) | `NotConverged` |

Statistical `Converged` means only that optimization success (`is_success()`) was true.
Assess estimator uncertainty and model validity separately on the statistical side.

## Normal fitting with two solvers

Assuming resistance values of nominal 100 Ω resistors follow normal distribution $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, estimate $(\mu,\sigma)$ from $n$ measurements $x_i$ [Ω].
Generate normal measurements with true values $\mu=100.3$ Ω and $\sigma=1.5$ Ω; inputs are sample size $n$ and random seed.
Fit the same data in two ways.

- Solve maximum likelihood (`FitMethod::Likelihood`) inside box $[80,120]\times[0.1,10]$ Ω with `LbfgsBFitSolver`.
  Supply the analytic gradient through `ExactFitDerivatives`.
- Solve the method of moments (`FitMethod::Moments`) without bounds using `LeastSquaresFitSolver`.
  Residuals are $r_1=\mu-\bar x$ and $r_2=(\mu^2+\sigma^2-\overline{x^2})/200$, with $\overline{x^k}=\frac1n\sum_i x_i^k$ the sample raw moment (200 Ω² scales the two residuals to similar magnitudes).

For a normal distribution, both estimates have closed forms $\hat\mu=\bar x$ and $\hat\sigma=\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2}$, providing independent checks.
The table shows both estimates, differences from closed forms, statistical statuses and optimization termination reasons, and objectives reevaluated by statistics.

```rust
//| id: optimize-statistical-fit-solvers
//| ref: optimize-statistical-fit-solvers
//| title: "Solve normal maximum likelihood and moment estimation with two solvers"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize, scioxide-random, scioxide-stats]
//| inputs:
//|   sample_size: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size n [count]","description":"Change the number of measured resistors.","min":5,"max":200,"step":5,"value":40}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","description":"Change the pseudorandom measurement sequence.","min":1,"max":50,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"small-sample","label":"n = 5","description":"The small sample moves estimates away from the true values, but both methods return the same closed-form values.","inputs":{"sample_size":5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/5","equals":"Converged"}
//|   - case: small-sample
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-5}}
use scioxide_core::{Bounds, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    LbfgsB, LbfgsBFitSolver, LeastSquares, LeastSquaresFitSolver, LeastSquaresOptions,
};
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};
use scioxide_stats::{
    DistributionFitModel, ExactFitDerivatives, FitMethod, FitObservation, FitRequest,
    PreparedFit, StatisticsOptions, fit_with_options,
};

let sig7 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 7 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
// Resistance measurements x_i ~ N(100.3, 1.5²) [Ω].
let n = sample_size as usize;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let samples = Normal::new(100.3_f64, 1.5)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .sample_n(&mut rng, n)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let observations: Vec<FitObservation<f64>> =
    samples.iter().map(|&x| FitObservation::Exact(x)).collect();
let options = StatisticsOptions::new().with_max_bytes(1 << 20);
let model = DistributionFitModel::Normal;
let integrality = [false; 2];
let initial = [95.0, 5.0];

// Maximum likelihood: L-BFGS-B minimizes negative log-likelihood inside box [80, 120] × [0.1, 10] Ω.
let boxed = Bounds::new(vec![(Some(80.0), Some(120.0)), (Some(0.1), Some(10.0))])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let likelihood = PreparedFit::new(
    &model,
    &observations,
    FitMethod::Likelihood,
    None,
    &boxed,
    &integrality,
    options,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let derivatives = ExactFitDerivatives {
    model,
    samples: &samples,
};
let mut lbfgsb = LbfgsBFitSolver::new(LbfgsB::default(), 2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let request = FitRequest {
    objective: &likelihood,
    derivatives: Some(&derivatives),
    bounds: &boxed,
    integrality: &integrality,
    initial: &initial,
};
let mle = fit_with_options(request, &mut lbfgsb, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Method of moments: LeastSquares zeros first- and second-raw-moment residuals (without bounds).
let free = Bounds::new(vec![(None, None); 2]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (orders, scales) = ([1_usize, 2], [1.0, 200.0]);
let method = FitMethod::Moments {
    orders: &orders,
    scales: &scales,
};
let moments = PreparedFit::new(
    &model,
    &observations,
    method,
    None,
    &free,
    &integrality,
    options,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solver = LeastSquares::new(LeastSquaresOptions::default()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut least_squares = LeastSquaresFitSolver::new(solver, 2, 2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let request = FitRequest {
    objective: &moments,
    derivatives: None,
    bounds: &free,
    integrality: &integrality,
    initial: &initial,
};
let matched =
    fit_with_options(request, &mut least_squares, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check (formula): normal maximum likelihood and moment estimates are the sample mean and sample standard deviation with divisor n.
let mean = samples.iter().sum::<f64>() / n as f64;
let spread = samples.iter().map(|x| (x - mean) * (x - mean)).sum::<f64>() / n as f64;
let closed = [mean, RealScalar::sqrt(spread)];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, fit, inner) in [
    (
        "Maximum likelihood (LbfgsBFitSolver)",
        &mle,
        lbfgsb.result().termination(),
    ),
    (
        "Method of moments (LeastSquaresFitSolver)",
        &matched,
        least_squares.result().termination(),
    ),
] {
    let p = fit
        .parameters()
        .ok_or("The fit did not write parameters".to_string())?;
    let gap = (p[0] - closed[0]).abs().max((p[1] - closed[1]).abs());
    rows.push((
        name,
        p[0],
        p[1],
        closed[1],
        gap,
        format!("{:?}", fit.report().termination()),
        inner.to_string(),
        fit.report().objective(),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method (solver)" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "μ̂ [Ω]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "σ̂ [Ω]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Closed-form σ̂ [Ω]", "type": "number", "format": sig7 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Largest difference from closed form [Ω]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Statistical status" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Optimization termination reason" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Statistical objective [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig7, "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "fits" })
);
```

At default $n=40$ and seed 3, both methods estimate $\hat\mu=100.010$ Ω and $\hat\sigma=1.72665$ Ω, stopping with statistical `Converged` and optimization `GradientSmall`.
Differences from closed form are $5.9\times10^{-12}$ Ω for maximum likelihood and $1.5\times10^{-9}$ Ω for moments.
The moment difference is larger because extracting $\sigma^2$ of about 3 Ω² from raw moment $\overline{x^2}$ of about $10^4$ Ω² amplifies a small remaining residual $r_2$ into $\delta\sigma\approx200\,\delta r_2/(2\sigma)$.
The objective reevaluated by scioxide-stats is negative log-likelihood 78.6 for maximum likelihood and $\sum_i r_i^2\approx7\times10^{-22}$ for moments.
At $n=5$, estimates $\hat\mu=98.85$ Ω and $\hat\sigma=1.264$ Ω lie farther from true values, but agreement between both methods and the closed form remains.
The deviation from truth is statistical variation from the small sample, separate from solver accuracy.

## Linear programming for discrete transport

`SimplexTransportSolver` solves discrete optimal-transport LPs used by scioxide-stats Wasserstein distances and related methods using sparse revised simplex (`SimplexSolver`).
For source weights $a\in\mathbb{R}^m$ and destination weights $b\in\mathbb{R}^n$ (both summing to 1) and costs $c_{ij}\ge0$, it solves

$$
\min_{x}\ \sum_{i,j}c_{ij}x_{ij}\quad\text{subject to}\quad \sum_j x_{ij}=a_i,\quad \sum_i x_{ij}=b_j,\quad x_{ij}\ge0
$$

and returns transport plan $x$ and dual variables for marginal equalities.
One marginal equality follows from the others through total mass, so construction omits one destination constraint.
`max_pairs` limits source–destination pairs $mn$, and `max_model_bytes` limits the assembled LP size (estimated by `required_model_bytes`); exceeding either returns `ResourceLimit`.
The simplex factorization memory limit is configured separately in the supplied `SimplexSolver`.
LP `Converged` maps to statistical `Converged`, cancellation and time limits to `Stopped`, and iteration, evaluation, and memory limits to `BudgetExhausted`; other terminations become `ConvergenceFailure` errors.
Although it connects to the same statistics crate, its structure differs from differentiable residual fitting; [simplex LP](../linear-programming/) termination interpretation applies directly.
Constructing transport problems and checking results are covered in [stats probability distances and optimal transport](../../../scioxide-stats/algorithms/circular-information-and-distances/).

## Summary

Supply `LeastSquaresFitSolver` for residual-returning matching methods, `LbfgsBFitSolver` for bounded scalar objectives such as maximum likelihood, or `SqpFitSolver` for nonlinear constraints in scioxide-stats fitting.
Statistical `Converged` maps successful optimization termination; limits map to `BudgetExhausted`, while `StepSmall` and `Stagnation` map to `NotConverged`.
Read detailed optimization termination and diagnostics through each type's `result()`, remembering that `cost()` of `LeastSquaresFitSolver` is half the statistical objective.
Supply `SimplexTransportSolver` for discrete transport LPs and limit construction size with `max_pairs` and `max_model_bytes`.
