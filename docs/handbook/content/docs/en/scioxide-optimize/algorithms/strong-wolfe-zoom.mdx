[Wolfe and strong Wolfe conditions](oxref:optimize-wolfe-conditions) specify properties of accepted steps but do not specify how to find a suitable $\alpha$.
The strong Wolfe conditions used by `StrongWolfeLineSearch` are

$$
\phi(\alpha) \le \phi(0) + c_1 \alpha \phi'(0), \qquad |\phi'(\alpha)| \le c_2 |\phi'(0)|
$$

This page explains how to find $\alpha$ satisfying them by bracketing an interval containing acceptable steps and contracting it through zoom.
Notation follows the [line search page](../line-search-and-scaling/), with $\phi(\alpha) = f(\mathbf{x} + \alpha\mathbf{p})$ and $0 < c_1 < c_2 < 1$.

## Bracketing and zoom procedure

`StrongWolfeLineSearch` follows Nocedal–Wright Algorithms 3.5 and 3.6.
Each trial evaluates objective and gradient once to obtain $\phi(\alpha)$ and $\phi'(\alpha)$.

```text
Bracketing: set α₁ = α_init, α₀ = 0; for i = 1, 2, …
  1. If Armijo fails, or i > 1 and φ(αᵢ) ≥ φ(αᵢ₋₁), call zoom(αᵢ₋₁, αᵢ)
  2. If strong Wolfe holds, accept αᵢ
  3. If φ′(αᵢ) ≥ 0, call zoom(αᵢ, αᵢ₋₁)
  4. αᵢ₊₁ = 2αᵢ
zoom(lo, hi): lo is the endpoint with lowest objective among trials satisfying Armijo
  1. Find minimizer α of the quadratic through φ(lo), φ′(lo), and φ(hi);
     if within 1/10 of the interval width of either endpoint, use the midpoint
  2. If Armijo fails or φ(α) ≥ φ(lo), set hi = α
  3. If strong Wolfe holds, accept α
  4. If φ′(α)(hi − lo) ≥ 0, set hi = lo; then set lo = α
```

Bracketing doubles $\alpha$ until it finds an Armijo point $\alpha_{i-1}$ and a later point $\alpha_i$ where the objective increased or the slope became positive.
The argument in the proof of Nocedal–Wright Lemma 3.1 shows that a strong Wolfe interval exists between these points.
Zoom contracts the interval while retaining Armijo at lo and a slope at lo descending toward hi.

## Safeguarded quadratic interpolation

Write lo as $\alpha_\ell$, hi as $\alpha_h$, and set $\Delta = \alpha_h - \alpha_\ell$.
The quadratic $q(\alpha) = \phi(\alpha_\ell) + \phi'(\alpha_\ell)(\alpha - \alpha_\ell) + c(\alpha - \alpha_\ell)^2$ through $\phi(\alpha_\ell)$, $\phi'(\alpha_\ell)$, and $\phi(\alpha_h)$ has coefficient $c = \{\phi(\alpha_h) - \phi(\alpha_\ell) - \phi'(\alpha_\ell)\Delta\}/\Delta^2$ and, for $c > 0$, minimizer

$$
\alpha_q = \alpha_\ell - \frac{\phi'(\alpha_\ell)\,\Delta^2}{2\{\phi(\alpha_h) - \phi(\alpha_\ell) - \phi'(\alpha_\ell)\Delta\}}
$$


If $\phi$ is quadratic, $\alpha_q$ is its line minimizer.
If $\alpha_q$ lies within 1/10 of the interval width of either endpoint, lies outside the interval, or cannot be computed, the midpoint becomes the trial.
This safeguard contracts the next interval by at least 10%, preventing stagnation from repeated trials immediately beside an endpoint.

## Trial limits and failure

Bracketing and zoom together permit at most `max_iterations` trials of `new` (default 40); reaching this limit gives `IterationLimit`.
An interval width at most $8\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\max(|\mathrm{lo}|, |\mathrm{hi}|)$ or an unrepresentable interior midpoint stops with `Stagnation`; a next trial below the minimum step length stops with `StepSizeLimit`.
Configuring interval width tolerance through `with_step_tolerance` also stops with `StepSizeLimit` when the width falls below it.
If a trial is outside the domain or its objective is nonfinite, that point becomes an endpoint and the method moves to its midpoint with lo (the last Armijo trial, initially $\alpha = 0$).

Defaults are $\alpha_{\mathrm{init}} = 1$, $c_1 = 10^{-4}$, $c_2 = 0.9$, and minimum step length equal to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ times the smallest positive normal number ($2^{-1074} \approx 4.9\times10^{-324}$ for f64).
`for_nonlinear_cg` uses $c_2 = 0.1$ to seek $\alpha$ closer to the line minimizer.
Armijo and curvature comparisons allow a rounding margin $8\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ relative to the magnitudes compared.
`BacktrackingLineSearch` compares Armijo without this margin.
Each trial evaluates objective and gradient once, so strong Wolfe search uses twice as many evaluations per trial as `BacktrackingLineSearch` (more still for difference-based gradients).
Interpreting search results without acceptance is explained in the [line search API](oxref:optimize-line-search-api).

## Trial-sequence experiment

For the same quadratic $f(x, y) = \tfrac12(x^2 + \kappa y^2)$ as the [line search experiment](oxref:optimize-armijo-scaling), run `StrongWolfeLineSearch` ($c_1 = 10^{-4}$, 40 trials) from $(1, 1)$ along steepest descent $\mathbf{p} = -(1, \kappa)$ and list bracketing and zoom trials in order.
The line minimizer is $\alpha^* = (1 + \kappa^2)/(1 + \kappa^3)$.
The objective records trial step length $\alpha = 1 - x$ from the evaluated point's $x$ coordinate.
Inputs are curvature ratio $\kappa$, curvature constant $c_2$ ($c_2 = 0.1$ is the nonlinear conjugate gradient default), and initial step length $\alpha_{\mathrm{init}}$.
Each table row is one trial, showing $\alpha/\alpha^*$, $\phi(\alpha)$, closed-form $\phi'(\alpha)/\phi'(0)$, and both conditions' outcomes (independent closed-form decisions).
For quadratics, $\phi'(\alpha)/\phi'(0) = 1 - \alpha/\alpha^*$ makes the curvature condition equivalent to $|1 - \alpha/\alpha^*| \le c_2$.

```rust
//| id: optimize-strong-wolfe-trials
//| ref: optimize-strong-wolfe-trials
//| title: "Trace strong Wolfe bracketing and zoom trials"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize, scioxide-differentiate]
//| inputs:
//|   condition_number: {"type":"range","label":"Curvature ratio κ [-]","description":"Vary curvature in the y direction.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":100}
//|   curvature: {"type":"select","label":"Curvature constant c₂","description":"Smaller values seek α closer to the line minimizer.","options":["0.9","0.1"],"value":"0.9"}
//|   initial_step: {"type":"range","label":"Initial step length α_init [-]","description":"First bracketing trial. A small value introduces a doubling phase.","min":0.001,"max":1,"step":0.001,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"short-start","label":"α_init = 0.002","description":"The first α = 0.002 (0.2α*) gives φ′(α)/φ′(0) = 0.80 ≤ 0.9 and is accepted immediately.","inputs":{"initial_step":0.002}}
//|   - {"id":"short-start-cg","label":"α_init = 0.002, c₂ = 0.1","description":"Doubling α from 0.002 to 0.016 finds φ above the previous trial; interpolation over [0.008, 0.016] accepts α* on the fifth trial.","inputs":{"initial_step":0.002,"curvature":"0.1"}}
//|   - {"id":"kappa-1","label":"κ = 1","description":"The first α = 1 gives φ′(α) = 0 and is accepted in one trial.","inputs":{"condition_number":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rowCount","equals":6}
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rows/4/1","approx":{"value":0.0625,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rows/5/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rows/5/6","equals":"Satisfied"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: short-start
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rowCount","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.80002,"atol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: short-start-cg
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rowCount","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rows/3/4","approx":{"value":-0.59984,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: kappa-1
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rowCount","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"trials","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError};
use scioxide_differentiate::{DerivativeOperation, DerivativeStrategy};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    DerivativeWorkspace, LineSearch, ObjectiveFunction, StrongWolfeLineSearch,
};
use std::cell::RefCell;

// f(x, y) = ½(x² + κy²). Record the evaluated trial's step length α = 1 − x.
struct Valley {
    kappa: f64,
    trials: RefCell<Vec<f64>>,
}
impl ObjectiveFunction<f64> for Valley {
    fn dim(&self) -> usize {
        2
    }
    fn eval(&self, v: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        self.trials.borrow_mut().push(1.0 - v[0]);
        Ok(0.5 * (v[0] * v[0] + self.kappa * v[1] * v[1]))
    }
    fn gradient_into(&self, v: &[f64], output: &mut [f64]) -> Result<bool, SciOxideError> {
        output.copy_from_slice(&[v[0], self.kappa * v[1]]);
        Ok(true)
    }
}

let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let c2: f64 = curvature
    .parse()
    .map_err(|e: std::num::ParseFloatError| e.to_string())?;
let kappa = condition_number;
let valley = Valley {
    kappa,
    trials: RefCell::new(Vec::new()),
};
// Comparison (formulas): φ(α), φ′(α) = ∇f(x + αp)ᵀp, line minimizer α* = (1 + κ²)/(1 + κ³).
let phi = |a: f64| {
    let (u, v) = (1.0 - a, 1.0 - kappa * a);
    0.5 * (u * u + kappa * v * v)
};
let dphi = |a: f64| -(1.0 - a) - kappa * kappa * (1.0 - kappa * a);
let exact = (1.0 + kappa * kappa) / (1.0 + kappa * kappa * kappa);
// Base (1, 1), steepest descent p = −(1, κ), φ(0), and φ′(0).
let (x, p) = ([1.0_f64, 1.0], [-1.0, -kappa]);
let (f0, slope) = (phi(0.0), dphi(0.0));
let trials = Budget::try_from(40).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let minimum = f64::MIN_POSITIVE * f64::EPSILON;
let search = StrongWolfeLineSearch::new(initial_step, minimum, 1.0e-4, c2, trials)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut derivatives =
    DerivativeWorkspace::new(DerivativeOperation::Gradient, 2, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut buffers = [[0.0_f64; 2]; 4];
let [trial, gradient, candidate, candidate_gradient] = &mut buffers;
let result = search
    .search_into(
        &valley,
        &x,
        f0,
        &p,
        slope,
        DerivativeStrategy::default(),
        trial,
        gradient,
        candidate,
        candidate_gradient,
        &mut derivatives,
        ExecutionContext::sequential().into(),
        None,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: evaluate both conditions from closed-form φ and φ′ at every recorded trial α.
let rows: Vec<_> = valley
    .trials
    .borrow()
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, &a)| {
        let mark = |holds: bool| if holds { "Satisfied" } else { "Not satisfied" };
        let ratio = dphi(a) / slope;
        let armijo = mark(phi(a) <= f0 + 1.0e-4 * a * slope);
        let curvature = mark(ratio.abs() <= c2);
        (i + 1, a, a / exact, phi(a), ratio, armijo, curvature)
    })
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Trial", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "α [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "α/α* [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "φ(α) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "φ′(α)/φ′(0) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Armijo condition" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Strong curvature condition" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "trials" })
);
let outcome = if result.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Accepted a step satisfying strong Wolfe conditions")
} else if result.has_trial() {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped without acceptance and returned only the best trial")
} else {
    Outcome::failed("No trial with a finite objective")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{:?}", result.termination()))
        .diagnostic("Objective evaluations", result.fun_evals() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Gradient evaluations", result.grad_evals() as f64, None)
);
```

At defaults $\kappa = 100$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{init}} = 1$, the first $\alpha = 1$ fails Armijo and enters zoom on interval $[0, 1]$.
Quadratic interpolation gives minimizer $\alpha^* = 0.0100$, but it lies within 1/10 of the width of an endpoint and is replaced by the midpoint; trials 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, and 0.0625 halve the interval.
When the interval contracts to $[0, 0.0625]$, $\alpha^*$ lies farther than 1/10 of the width from an endpoint; since $\phi$ is quadratic, interpolation gives $\alpha^*$ exactly and the sixth trial is accepted.
$c_2 = 0.1$ gives the same trial sequence because the accepted point has $\phi'(\alpha) = 0$ and meets the curvature condition for either $c_2$.
At $\kappa = 1000$, seven interval halvings precede acceptance of $\alpha^*$ on the ninth trial.

Starting from $\alpha_{\mathrm{init}} = 0.002$ with $c_2 = 0.9$ accepts the first point ($\alpha = 0.2\alpha^*$, $\phi'(\alpha)/\phi'(0) = 0.80$) immediately; $\phi$ falls only from 50.5 to 32.5.
With $c_2 = 0.1$, doubling through 0.004, 0.008, and 0.016 makes $\phi$ exceed $\phi(0.008)$ at 0.016; interpolation over $[0.008, 0.016]$ accepts $\alpha^*$ on the fifth trial.
$c_2$ controls how strongly overly short steps are excluded; smaller $c_2$ trades additional trials for a step closer to the line minimizer.
For nonquadratic $\phi$, the interpolated minimizer is approximate and acceptance generally requires more trials.

## Summary

`StrongWolfeLineSearch` doubles $\alpha$ to bracket a suitable interval, then contracts it with safeguarded quadratic interpolation and midpoints.
For quadratics, interpolation gives the exact line minimizer, but points near an endpoint are replaced by midpoints and repeated halvings continue.
$c_2$ determines the accepted slope range; $c_2 = 0.9$ also accepts short steps, while $c_2 = 0.1$ uses more trials to seek steps near the line minimizer.
Reaching the trial limit (default 40) or rounding-scale interval width stops without acceptance; inspect the reason through `LineSearchTermination`.
