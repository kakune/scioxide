When one objective evaluation takes seconds to hours, evaluation count itself determines computational cost.
Surrogate-based optimization builds a cheap objective approximation from observed points and selects the next evaluation on that model.
If the model also reports predictive uncertainty, an acquisition function balances evaluating low predictions and uncertain points.
Using a Gaussian process (kriging) with expected improvement is known as Jones et al.'s EGO (efficient global optimization), also called Bayesian optimization.
This page derives models and acquisition functions, explains SciOxide's `SurrogateSearch` contracts, and examines evaluation counts and best observations on Branin's function.
`SurrogateSearch` claims no global optimality; its termination `SearchBudgetCompleted` is not success.

## Surrogate models and acquisition functions

The surrogate search problem is `SurrogateHistory`, which stores observations.
`SurrogateHistory::new(bounds, capacity)` accepts a box `FiniteBounds` and observation capacity, returning `InvalidInput` for zero dimension or capacity or a box dimension differing from type dimension `D`.
`SurrogateObservation::new(point, value, noise_variance)` pairs a point, observed value, and known observation noise variance (0 for an exact value).
`push` returns `InvalidInput` if history is full, the value or noise variance is NaN or infinite, noise variance is negative, or the point lies outside the box.
Repeated observations at the same point are allowed, permitting noisy observations to accumulate.

`SurrogateFitter` fits a `SurrogateModel` from history; the model's `predict(point, workspace)` returns `SurrogatePrediction`.
In `SurrogatePrediction::new(mean, variance)`, `mean()` is the predicted mean and `variance()` the conditional variance of the latent function (objective without observation noise); models without variance return `None`.
Model interpretation and numerical stability follow [local models and kriging](../../../scioxide-interp/algorithms/local-models-and-kriging/).

### Kriging and RBF

`KrigingSurrogateFitter::new(covariance, numerical)` is ordinary kriging (a Gaussian process with an unknown constant mean), using user-fixed covariance $k(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}) = \sigma^2 \rho(r)$ and per-observation noise variance.
$\sigma^2$ is signal variance, $\rho$ the correlation function, and $r$ distance divided by the length scale.
Let $K$ be the training covariance matrix with diagonal noise variances, $\mathbf{k}_*$ the covariance vector between query $\mathbf{x}$ and training points, and $\mathbf{y}$ the observation vector; predicted mean and variance are

$$
\mu_* = \hat m + \mathbf{k}_*^T K^{-1} (\mathbf{y} - \hat m \mathbf{1}), \qquad
s_*^2 = \sigma^2 - \mathbf{k}_*^T K^{-1} \mathbf{k}_* + \frac{(1 - \mathbf{1}^T K^{-1} \mathbf{k}_*)^2}{\mathbf{1}^T K^{-1} \mathbf{1}}
$$


$\hat m = \mathbf{1}^T K^{-1} \mathbf{y} / \mathbf{1}^T K^{-1} \mathbf{1}$ is the generalized least-squares mean estimate; the final term of $s_*^2$ adds uncertainty from its estimation.
Covariance parameters are not estimated; users supply length scales and signal variance.
`numerical` is scioxide-interp's `LocalModelOptions`, limiting dense fit order, memory, rank, and condition number.
$K$ is factored by Cholesky; the method switches to singular value decomposition at rank or condition limits and never automatically adds a diagonal value.

`RbfSurrogateFitter::new(kernel, regularization, numerical)` fits radial basis function (RBF) interpolation $s(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_i w_i \phi(\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_i\|) + p(\mathbf{x})$ using every observation.
The kernel type determines whether a low-degree polynomial $p$ is added.
Diagonal smoothing uses only the amount explicitly specified by `regularization`.
RBF predictions provide only a mean; `variance()` is `None`.

A dense fit to $N$ observations uses $O(N^3)$ factorization and $O(N^2)$ memory; mean prediction costs $O(N)$ and kriging variance $O(N^2)$.
Search refits the model each batch, making fitting more expensive as observations grow.
The capacity of `SurrogateHistory` bounds observation count and therefore this computational cost.

### Expected improvement

`SurrogateAcquisition` computes a candidate score from its prediction; search proposes the highest-scoring point.
In `score(prediction, incumbent)`, `incumbent` is current best value $f_*$, the smallest predicted mean at an observed point.

Let $\mu$ be predicted mean, $s > 0$ predicted standard deviation ($s_*$ for kriging), and $\xi \ge 0$ the improvement margin; model the objective value as $Y \sim N(\mu, s^2)$.
Set $a = f_* - \xi$ and $z = (a - \mu)/s$, and let $\Phi$ and $\phi$ denote the standard normal distribution function and density.
Substituting $Y = \mu + s t$ gives the expectation of the improvement $I = \max(a - Y, 0)$ as

$$
\mathrm{E}[I] = \int_{-\infty}^{z} (a - \mu - s t)\,\phi(t)\,dt = (a - \mu)\,\Phi(z) + s\,\phi(z)
$$

using $\int_{-\infty}^{z} t\,\phi(t)\,dt = -\phi(z)$.
This is expected improvement:

$$
\operatorname{EI} = (f_* - \xi - \mu)\,\Phi(z) + s\,\phi(z)
$$


The first term favors low predicted means, and the second favors high uncertainty; both contribute to candidate value.
The limit as $s = 0$ is $\max(f_* - \xi - \mu, 0)$.

`GaussianAcquisition` provides three built-in acquisitions based on Gaussian predictions.

- `ExpectedImprovement { margin }`: EI above, with `margin` corresponding to $\xi$.
- `ProbabilityOfImprovement { margin }`: probability of improvement $\Phi(z)$; at $s = 0$ it is 1 for an improvement and 0 otherwise.
- `LowerConfidenceBound { weight }`: $\kappa s - \mu$, the negation of lower confidence bound $\mu - \kappa s$, with `weight` corresponding to $\kappa$.

All require predictive variance; missing variance or negative $\xi$ or $\kappa$ returns `InvalidInput`.
With RBF, implement your own `SurrogateAcquisition` using only the mean.
Acquisition values rank candidates; model uncertainty is not a deterministic error bound for the original objective.

### Closed-loop search

`SurrogateFeasibility` checks candidate feasibility against known constraints; `Fn(&[S]) -> Result<bool, SciOxideError>` closures also implement it.
The decision must depend only on the point; search proposes only feasible points.
An infeasible point in history stops proposal with `InvalidInput`.

`SurrogateSearch::new(options, fitter, acquisition, feasibility, design, factory)` constructs the inner `MultiStart` (starting design and local solver factory) for acquisition maximization.
Zero type dimension `D` or NaN, infinite, or negative separation distance (described below) returns `InvalidInput`.
A batch is a set of points selected and evaluated from one fit.
`minimize(history, objective)` and `minimize_into(history, objective, workspace, scope)` repeat these steps for each batch.

1. Fit the model to history once.
2. Minimize the negated acquisition with inner `MultiStart`; among starting points and local solutions, select the highest-acquisition feasible point sufficiently separated from history and other proposals in the batch.
   Repeat for the number of batch points.
3. Append the (value, noise variance) returned by `objective` to history.

`SurrogateSearchOptions::with_batch_size` sets points per batch and `with_batches` the batch count.
Points in a batch use the same model; candidates must have Euclidean distance greater than separation `with_separation` from every history point and other batch point, after dividing coordinates by box widths.
Defaults are 10 acquisition starts, seed 42, separation 0 (excluding only identical points), and one batch of one point.
The final batch shrinks to remaining history capacity; full history stops further batches.
`propose_batch(history, count)` and `propose_batch_into` only propose next points without modifying history.
`SurrogateSearchWorkspace` stores inner search and proposal memory.

`SurrogateSearchReport` returns termination reason `termination()` (`SearchBudgetCompleted` when all batches finish or history fills), objective evaluations in this call `objective_evaluations()`, completed batches `completed_batches()`, and index `best_observation()` of the lowest observed value in history.
Observations added before an error remain in history.

## Saving evaluations on Branin's function

Search the same Branin function as [DIRECT and SHGO](oxref:optimize-direct-branin), treating it as an expensive objective.
The box is $x \in [-5, 10]$ and $y \in [0, 15]$, the global minimum $5/(4\pi) \approx 0.397887$, and minimizers $(-\pi, 12.275)$, $(\pi, 2.275)$, and $(3\pi, 2.475)$.
Initial observations are 8 Halton points (indices 1–8); covariance for `KrigingSurrogateFitter` uses a Matérn 5/2 kernel, common coordinate length scale $\ell$, and signal variance equal to the initial sample variance.
Inner `MultiStart` uses 10 uniform starts and built-in `PowellLocalFactory`; separation 0.01 in the normalized box avoids singular $K$ from overly close points.
Each batch evaluates one point.
Inputs are batch count, acquisition function, and length scale $\ell$.
Outputs are an observation scatter plot, the gap between best-so-far and known minimum by evaluation count (logarithmic axis), a summary, and outcome.
The known minimum and minimizers provide independent checks.

```rust
//| id: optimize-surrogate-branin
//| ref: optimize-surrogate-branin
//| title: "Vary acquisition and length scale to examine surrogate search approaching Branin's minimum"
//| run: button
//| timeoutMs: 120000
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-interp, scioxide-optimize, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   batches: {"type":"range","label":"Batches [count]","description":"Number of batches evaluating one point each.","min":4,"max":30,"step":1,"value":20}
//|   acquisition: {"type":"select","label":"Acquisition function","options":["Expected improvement EI","Probability of improvement PI","Lower confidence bound κ = 2"],"value":"Expected improvement EI"}
//|   length_scale: {"type":"range","label":"Length scale ℓ [-]","description":"Matérn 5/2 kernel length scale, common to x and y.","min":1,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"lower-confidence-bound","label":"Lower confidence bound","description":"Switch to lower confidence bound with κ = 2 and compare its best value with expected improvement at the same 28 evaluations.","inputs":{"acquisition":"Lower confidence bound κ = 2"}}
//|   - {"id":"short-length","label":"ℓ = 1","description":"Shortening the length scale to 1 leaves a gap of 0.17 from the known minimum after the same 28 evaluations.","inputs":{"length_scale":1}}
//|   - {"id":"few-rounds","label":"4 batches","description":"Stop after the initial 8 points and 4 search batches, checking that the known-minimum gap still exceeds 1.","inputs":{"batches":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"surrogate","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"SearchBudgetCompleted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"surrogate","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":20}
//|       - {"artifact":"surrogate","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0.005,"max":0.03}}
//|       - {"artifact":"surrogate","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":0,"max":0.1}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: lower-confidence-bound
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"surrogate","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0.001,"max":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"surrogate","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":0,"max":0.1}}
//|   - case: short-length
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"surrogate","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0.1,"max":0.3}}
//|   - case: few-rounds
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"surrogate","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":3,"max":4}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, RealScalar};
use scioxide_interp::{Covariance, CovarianceKernel, LocalModelOptions};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    FiniteBounds, GaussianAcquisition, KrigingSurrogateFitter, PowellLocalFactory,
    SurrogateHistory, SurrogateObservation, SurrogateSearch, SurrogateSearchOptions,
    UniformStartDesign,
};
use scioxide_random::QmcPlan;

let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let branin = |x: &[f64]| {
    let bowl = x[1] - 5.1 * x[0] * x[0] / (4.0 * pi * pi) + 5.0 * x[0] / pi - 6.0;
    bowl * bowl + 10.0 * (1.0 - 1.0 / (8.0 * pi)) * RealScalar::cos(x[0]) + 10.0
};
let initial = 8;
let rounds = batches.round() as usize;
let bounds = FiniteBounds::new(vec![(-5.0, 10.0), (0.0, 15.0)]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut history =
    SurrogateHistory::<f64, 2>::new(bounds, initial + rounds).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Initial observations: map Halton points 1–8 into the box and evaluate. Noise variance is 0.
let plan = QmcPlan::halton(2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
for index in 1..=initial {
    let mut unit = [0.0; 2];
    plan.point_into(index as u64, &mut unit)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let point = [-5.0 + 15.0 * unit[0], 15.0 * unit[1]];
    let observation = SurrogateObservation::new(point, branin(&point), 0.0);
    history.push(observation).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
// Use the initial observations' sample variance as signal variance.
let values: Vec<f64> = history.observations().iter().map(|o| o.value()).collect();
let mean = values.iter().sum::<f64>() / initial as f64;
let variance =
    values.iter().map(|v| (v - mean) * (v - mean)).sum::<f64>() / (initial - 1) as f64;
let kernel = CovarianceKernel::Matern52;
let covariance =
    Covariance::new(kernel, variance, [length_scale; 2]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fitter = KrigingSurrogateFitter::new(covariance, LocalModelOptions::new());
let rule = match acquisition.as_str() {
    "Probability of improvement PI" => GaussianAcquisition::ProbabilityOfImprovement { margin: 0.0 },
    "Lower confidence bound κ = 2" => GaussianAcquisition::LowerConfidenceBound { weight: 2.0 },
    _ => GaussianAcquisition::ExpectedImprovement { margin: 0.0 },
};
let options = SurrogateSearchOptions::default()
    .with_batches(rounds)
    .with_batch_size(Budget::ONE)
    .with_separation(0.01);
let feasible = |_: &[f64]| Ok(true);
let search = SurrogateSearch::new(
    options,
    fitter,
    rule,
    feasible,
    UniformStartDesign,
    PowellLocalFactory::default(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let report = search
    .minimize(&mut history, |x| Ok((branin(x), 0.0)))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check: known minimum 5/(4π) and three minimizers.
let exact = 5.0 / (4.0 * pi);
let observations = history.observations();
let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = observations.iter().map(|o| o.point()).collect();
let minima = [[-pi, 12.275], [pi, 2.275], [3.0 * pi, 2.475]];
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Initial observations",
            "points": &points[..initial],
            "style": { "symbol": "circle" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Search-selected points",
            "points": &points[initial..],
            "style": { "symbol": "diamond" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Known minimizers",
            "points": minima,
            "style": { "symbol": "emptyCircle" }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "x [-]",
        "yLabel": "y [-]",
        "xMin": -5.0,
        "xMax": 10.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 15.0
    })
);
// Gap between the best value so far and the known minimum by evaluation count.
let mut best = f64::INFINITY;
let gaps: Vec<[f64; 2]> = observations
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, o)| {
        best = best.min(o.value());
        [(i + 1) as f64, best - exact]
    })
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Best value − minimum", "points": gaps, "style": { "step": "end" } })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Objective evaluations [count]",
        "yLabel": "Best-value gap from known minimum [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": initial as f64, "label": "End of initial observations" }
        ]
    })
);
let chosen = observations[report.best_observation()];
let distance = minima
    .iter()
    .map(|m| (chosen.point()[0] - m[0]).hypot(chosen.point()[1] - m[1]))
    .fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let digits5 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 5 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Termination reason" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Search evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Completed batches [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Best observation index", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Best observed value [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits5 },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Known-minimum gap [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "6", "label": "Distance to minimizer [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "7", "label": "Signal variance σ² [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits5 }
    ]),
    vec![(
        report.termination().to_string(),
        report.objective_evaluations(),
        report.completed_batches(),
        report.best_observation(),
        chosen.value(),
        chosen.value() - exact,
        distance,
        variance
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "surrogate" })
);
emit_outcome!(
    Outcome::partial("All batches completed. The best observation is a global minimizer candidate")
        .status(report.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Known-minimum gap", chosen.value() - exact, None)
        .diagnostic(
            "Objective evaluations",
            (initial + report.objective_evaluations()) as f64,
            None
        )
);
```

“Gap from known minimum” subtracts the known minimum from the best of initial 8 and search observations; “distance to minimizer” is the distance from the best observation to its nearest minimizer.
The initial sample variance 1191 is used as signal variance.
The gap plot steps down whenever an observation improves the best-so-far value.
A downward step to the right of the vertical line ending initial observations means the model-selected point found a lower value than the initial design.

At the default expected improvement, $\ell = 5$, and 20 batches, the best initial point has gap 5.39 from the known minimum.
Search reduces this gap to 0.41 at evaluation 17 and 0.014 at evaluation 25; the following 3 evaluations do not improve it.
The best observation is $(9.371, 2.421)$ near $(3\pi, 2.475)$, at distance 0.076 from the minimizer.
Increasing to 30 batches leaves gap 0.014 after 38 evaluations; model-selected points do not refine the neighborhood of the minimizer.
Lower confidence bound $\kappa = 2$ selects a point near $(\pi, 2.275)$ at evaluation 28, giving gap $5.3 \times 10^{-3}$.
Probability of improvement leaves gap 0.25; length scale 1 gives 0.17 and scale 8 gives 0.040.
Stopping after 4 batches leaves gap 3.5 and only one point improving the initial observations.
Fixed parameters and acquisition choices affect results at the same evaluation count.
Selected points also depend on the inner acquisition-maximizing local solver (Powell here) and starting-point seed; interpret table values as one example of this configuration.

On the same function, DIRECT with evaluation limit 100 (actually 97 evaluations) attained gap $1.4 \times 10^{-3}$ ([DIRECT evaluation counts](oxref:optimize-direct-branin)).
Surrogate search selects a point within 0.1 of a minimizer in fewer than 30 evaluations, but precise minimum refinement requires local optimization, such as starting a local solver from the best observation.

## Summary

Surrogate search fits kriging or RBF models to observations and evaluates acquisition-maximizing points to seek low values with few evaluations.
Expected improvement combines low predicted mean and high uncertainty in one score; probability of improvement and lower confidence bounds balance them differently.
Kriging length scales and signal variance are not estimated; users choose them for the objective scale and examine result dependence on that choice.
`SurrogateHistory` capacity bounds observations and fitting cost; `SearchBudgetCompleted` is not success.
Check the best observation using known properties or another method and refine with a local solver if needed.
