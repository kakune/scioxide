This page summarizes public items for composite objectives with smooth parts and proximal maps, two-block splitting linked by equalities (ADMM), stochastic first-order methods sampling objectives with observation noise, and Pareto search simultaneously minimizing multiple objectives.
Every method returns `Err` for shape mismatches, non-finite inputs, callback or provider failures and numerical breakdown, leaving the caller's result unchanged.
Iteration, evaluation or sample limits, cancellation and deadlines return `Ok`, retaining accepted points and a single authoritative termination reason (`OptimizeTermination`).
Every method returns a structured error if a limit, cancellation or deadline stops execution before the first point is accepted.
Only `Converged` and `GradientSmall` count as success.
Prepare workspace with `workspace_requirements` and `workspace`; solve through `minimize_into` or `solve_into`, accepting the problem, output, workspace and execution scope.
Owned-result APIs `minimize` or `solve` allocate workspace and results internally; unsuccessful terminations also return results in `Ok`.
Proximal, ADMM and stochastic first-order results allow success to be required through `require_success()`.
Pass an execution context or parent `SolveContext` as the execution scope; read trial records from workspace `last_attempt()`.
To resume stochastic first-order methods or NSGA-II, pass an owned checkpoint created from workspace to `resume_into`.

## Proximal gradient for composite objectives

A composite objective has form $F(x) = f(x) + g(x)$, where $f$ is a smooth real-valued function with Lipschitz-continuous gradient and $g$ is proper, closed and convex (such as the L1 norm or a box indicator, and need not be differentiable).
The proximal map $\operatorname{prox}_{t g}(v) = \arg\min_u \{ g(u) + \lVert u - v \rVert_2^2 / (2t) \}$ supplies information about $g$ in one mapping.
Variables may be real or complex, but $f$ is real-valued and gradients follow the real-inner-product convention $\operatorname{Re}(g^{\mathsf H} \Delta x)$.

## Two-block ADMM

ADMM (alternating direction method of multipliers) alternately solves subproblems for $x$ and $z$, updating scaled dual variable $u = y/\rho$ to solve $\min f(x) + g(z)$ subject to equality constraint $Ax + Bz = c$.
`Admm` does not solve the two subproblems itself; the caller supplies them as `AdmmBlockUpdate`.
Operators use scioxide-core `LinearOperator` ($A$ also needs adjoints, hence `AdjointOperator`), accepting dense matrices, sparse matrices and injected operators.

## Sampled observations and sample keys

A sampled objective returns noisy values (minibatch losses or simulation outputs) on each call, even at the same point.
The method creates sample key `OptimizationSampleKey` for every observation; a sampler returns a token from that key whose contents the solver does not interpret, and the objective callback interprets the token as an observation seed or data index.
Sample keys are independent of executor partitioning, so sequential and injected-executor execution produce identical observation sequences; means and variances aggregate in index order.
One observation consumes one sample and two provider evaluations (sampling and objective or gradient); failed observations also count.

## Stochastic first-order methods

Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) steps along the minibatch mean gradient with a prescribed step size and always accepts trial points.
The stochastic trust-region method uses first-order model $m(p) = \bar f + \bar g^{\mathsf T} p$ only inside a ball of radius $\Delta$, deciding acceptance from the difference of sample mean objectives at reference and trial points and its empirical standard error.
For both methods, termination reasons `EstimatedStationarity` and `NoiseLimit` are statistical-estimation outcomes, not proofs of stationary points.

## Pareto dominance and multiobjective search

When minimizing objectives $F(x) = (F_1(x), \dots, F_m(x))$, $a$ Pareto-dominates $b$ if $a_i \le b_i$ in every component and $a_i < b_i$ in at least one component.
The set of points dominated by no other point is the Pareto front; `Nsga2` searches for a population approximation within a finite box.
Retained values are sample estimates and establish neither completeness of the front nor guarantees against noise.

## Summary

For objectives containing nondifferentiable regularizers or constraint indicator functions, supply a proximal map and solve with `ProximalGradient` or `Fista`, interpreting `ProximalResult::mapping_norm` as a first-order stationarity measure (not an upper bound on distance to a minimizer).
When splitting variables into two blocks makes subproblems easier, inject subproblem solvers into `Admm` and inspect both primal and dual residuals.
For noisy observations, present `NoiseModel` correctly, read standard errors in SGD or stochastic trust-region `StochasticResult` together with termination reasons, and do not reinterpret `EstimatedStationarity` or `NoiseLimit` as convergence.
When comparing multiple objectives, treat the `Nsga2` archive as a finite approximation and inspect retained estimates with `ParetoArchive::verify` and `hypervolume_2d`.
