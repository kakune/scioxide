Consider a particle sliding from rest along a frictionless slope.
Find the curve reaching endpoint $(X,-H)$ from starting point $(0,0)$ in the shortest time.
This is the brachistochrone problem posed by Johann Bernoulli in 1696.
With downward depth $h=-y$, conservation of energy gives speed $v=\sqrt{2gh}$.
Descent time is $\int ds/v$ along the curve; the continuous problem can be solved by calculus of variations.
Here we approximate the curve by a polygonal line and solve a finite-dimensional unconstrained minimization with L-BFGS.

## Segment times and variables

Fix equally spaced horizontal coordinates over $n$ segments and use interior-node depths as variables.
The tangential gravity component is constant along each straight segment, so acceleration is constant and speed varies linearly with time.
Thus the average speed is the mean of endpoint speeds, $(v_i+v_{i+1})/2$, and the exact traversal time for segment length $d_i$ is

$$
T_i=\frac{2d_i}{v_i+v_{i+1}}
=\sqrt{\frac2g}\frac{\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2+(h_{i+1}-h_i)^2}}{\sqrt{h_i}+\sqrt{h_{i+1}}}
$$


Here $\Delta x=X/n$ is segment width, $h_i$ is the depth at node $i$, and $h_0=0$ and $h_n=H$.
Starting speed is zero, but the denominator remains nonzero when the adjacent node has positive depth.
A curve that rises above the starting point along the way is inaccessible from rest.
We therefore write interior-node depths as $h_i=\exp(q_i)$ to keep them positive and exclude endpoints from the variables.
There are $n-1$ variables $q_1,\ldots,q_{n-1}$, and depths may exceed endpoint depth $H$.

For a segment with depths $a,b$, set $d=\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2+(a-b)^2}$ and $s=\sqrt a+\sqrt b$; the derivative with respect to the interior-node depth is

$$
\frac{\partial T_i}{\partial a}=\sqrt{\frac2g}
\left(\frac{a-b}{ds}-\frac{d}{2\sqrt a\,s^2}\right)
$$


Each node appears in two adjacent segments, so we add both contributions and pass $\partial T/\partial q_i=h_i\,\partial T/\partial h_i$ to L-BFGS.
One time-and-gradient evaluation costs $O(n)$.

L-BFGS forms the inverse-Hessian approximation's action by a two-loop recursion using the latest $m$ step/gradient-difference pairs, requiring $O(mn)$ operations and $O(mn)$ memory per iteration.
`LbfgsOptions::default()` uses $m=10$ and a strong-Wolfe line search.
Here the gradient 2-norm tolerance is absolute $10^{-6}$ (relative 0), with at most 2000 iterations.
Stopping after the gradient test passes gives termination reason `GradientSmall`.

## Analytic solution and refinement

The continuous solution is a cycloid, written $x=a(\theta-\sin\theta)$ and $h=a(1-\cos\theta)$.
The endpoint condition determines $\theta_1$ through $(\theta_1-\sin\theta_1)/(1-\cos\theta_1)=X/H$, giving $a=H/(1-\cos\theta_1)$.
Descent time is $T_*=\theta_1\sqrt{a/g}$.
For $X/H>\pi/2$, $\theta_1>\pi$; the curve dips below the endpoint to maximum depth $h=2a$ before rising.
The straight-line reference time is $\sqrt{2\ell^2/(gH)}$, from length $\ell=\sqrt{X^2+H^2}$ and acceleration $gH/\ell$.

Every polygonal line connects the same endpoints as the cycloid, so its time is at least $T_*$.
Difference $T_n-T_*$ combines discretization and optimization errors; when stopping with a sufficiently small gradient, it is mainly discretization error.
Use the ratio of differences at $n$ and $2n$ segments to compute observed order $p=\log_2(e_n/e_{2n})$.
Here $e_n=T_n-T_*$.

Below we fix $H=1\,\mathrm m$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
Inputs are segment count $n$ and endpoint horizontal position $X$.
Outputs are a plot of polygonal and analytic curves; a table of descent time, exact time, difference, iterations and termination reason for $n$ and $2n$ segments; an observed-order table; and outcomes for both solves.
Bisection for cycloid $\theta_1$ and trigonometric functions are used only as independent checks, computed with f64 inherent methods.

```rust
//| id: optimize-brachistochrone
//| ref: optimize-brachistochrone
//| title: "Compare polygonal descent time with the cycloid"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   segments: {"type":"range","label":"Segment count n [segments]","min":8,"max":32,"step":8,"value":16}
//|   endpoint: {"type":"range","label":"Endpoint horizontal position X [m]","min":0.5,"max":3,"step":0.25,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fine","label":"32 and 64 segments","description":"The time difference roughly halves from 2.35×10⁻³ s to 1.15×10⁻³ s, giving observed order 1.03. Iterations increase from 152 to 384.","inputs":{"segments":32}}
//|   - {"id":"short","label":"X = 0.5 m (steep slope)","description":"With θ₁ < π, the curve does not dip below the endpoint, and the 16-segment time difference is small at 7.4×10⁻⁴ s.","inputs":{"endpoint":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"long","label":"X = 3 m (dips below the endpoint)","description":"The exact time is 1.0183 s; the curve dips about 0.24 m below the endpoint before rising. The 16-segment time difference is 6.8×10⁻³ s.","inputs":{"endpoint":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.8055638295,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":4.881e-3,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":2.348e-3,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":5,"max":2000}}
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/1/5","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.056,"atol":0.02}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: fine
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":1.146e-3,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.035,"atol":0.02}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: short
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.4907024394,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":7.39e-4,"atol":1e-5}}
//|   - case: long
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.0183129452,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"times","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":6.838e-3,"atol":1e-5}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, GradientTolerance, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{Lbfgs, LbfgsOptions, ObjectiveFn};
// q is the logarithm of interior-node depth, qᵢ = ln hᵢ. Endpoint depths are fixed at 0 and H = 1 m.
// Return descent time T = Σ √(2/g) dᵢ / (√hᵢ + √hᵢ₊₁) and the chain-rule gradient ∂T/∂qᵢ = hᵢ ∂T/∂hᵢ.
fn model(q: &[f64], width: f64) -> (f64, Vec<f64>) {
    let n = q.len() + 1;
    let mut h = vec![0.0];
    h.extend(q.iter().map(|&v| RealScalar::exp(v)));
    h.push(1.0);
    let mut gradient = vec![0.0; q.len()];
    let mut time = 0.0;
    let c = RealScalar::sqrt(2.0_f64 / 9.81);
    for i in 0..n {
        let (a, b) = (h[i], h[i + 1]);
        let d = RealScalar::sqrt((width / n as f64).powi(2) + (a - b).powi(2));
        let s = RealScalar::sqrt(a) + RealScalar::sqrt(b);
        time += c * d / s;
        if i > 0 {
            gradient[i - 1] +=
                a * c * ((a - b) / (d * s) - d / (2.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(a) * s * s));
        }
        if i + 1 < n {
            gradient[i] +=
                b * c * ((b - a) / (d * s) - d / (2.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(b) * s * s));
        }
    }
    (time, gradient)
}
// Independent check: from endpoint condition (θ − sin θ)/(1 − cos θ) = X/H,
// find cycloid θ₁ and a by bisection. Use f64 trigonometric functions for this reference.
let (mut lo, mut hi) = (1e-6_f64, 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI - 1e-6);
for _ in 0..80 {
    let t = 0.5 * (lo + hi);
    if (t - t.sin()) / (1.0 - t.cos()) < endpoint {
        lo = t;
    } else {
        hi = t;
    }
}
let theta = 0.5 * (lo + hi);
let radius = 1.0 / (1.0 - theta.cos());
let exact_time = theta * (radius / 9.81).sqrt();
let reference: Vec<_> = (0..=100)
    .map(|i| {
        let t = theta * i as f64 / 100.0;
        [radius * (t - t.sin()), -radius * (1.0 - t.cos())]
    })
    .collect();
let mut series = vec![serde_json::json!({
    "name": "Cycloid (analytic solution)",
    "points": reference,
    "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
})];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut all_success = true;
for n in [segments as usize, 2 * segments as usize] {
    let width = endpoint;
    let objective = ObjectiveFn::new(n - 1, move |q: &[f64]| Ok(model(q, width).0))
        .with_gradient(move |q: &[f64], g: &mut [f64]| {
            g.copy_from_slice(&model(q, width).1);
            Ok(())
        });
    // Start from the straight line connecting the endpoints (hᵢ = i/n).
    let start: Vec<_> = (1..n)
        .map(|i| RealScalar::ln(i as f64 / n as f64))
        .collect();
    let options = LbfgsOptions::default()
        .with_gradient_tolerance(GradientTolerance::new(1e-6, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
        .with_max_iterations(Budget::try_from(2000_usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    let result = Lbfgs::new(options)
        .minimize(&objective, &start)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    all_success &= result.is_success();
    let mut points = vec![[0.0, 0.0]];
    points.extend(
        result
            .x()
            .iter()
            .enumerate()
            .map(|(i, &q)| [width * (i + 1) as f64 / n as f64, -RealScalar::exp(q)]),
    );
    points.push([width, -1.0]);
    series.push(serde_json::json!({"name": format!("{n} segments"), "points": points}));
    rows.push((
        n,
        result.fun(),
        exact_time,
        result.fun() - exact_time,
        result.iterations(),
        result.termination().to_string(),
    ));
}
// Compute observed discretization order p = log₂(eₙ/e₂ₙ) from the ratio of differences after doubling segments.
let order = RealScalar::log2(rows[0].3 / rows[1].3);
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Horizontal position x [m]",
        "yLabel": "Height y [m]",
        "annotations": [{"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": -1.0, "label": "Endpoint height"}]
    })
);
let fixed5 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 5 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let time_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Segment count n [segments]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Descent time Tₙ [s]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Exact time T* [s]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference Tₙ − T* [s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    time_columns,
    rows.clone(),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "times" })
);
let order_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Coarse segment count n [segments]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Fine segment count 2n [segments]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Observed order p [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 3 } }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    order_columns,
    vec![(rows[0].0, rows[1].0, order)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "order" })
);
let outcome = if all_success {
    Outcome::satisfied("Both polygonal lines satisfy the gradient test")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("A polygonal line stopped before satisfying the gradient test")
        .explanation("The differences include optimization error, so do not interpret observed order as discretization error.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{} / {}", rows[0].5, rows[1].5))
        .diagnostic("Observed order", order, None)
        .diagnostic("Fine polygonal time difference", rows[1].3, Some("s"))
);
```

For default $X=2\,\mathrm m$, $\theta_1=3.508>\pi$, and the curve dips 0.034 m below the endpoint.
The 16-segment time is 0.81044 s and the 32-segment time 0.80791 s; the difference from exact time 0.80556 s roughly halves from $4.88\times10^{-3}$ s to $2.35\times10^{-3}$ s.
Compared with straight-line descent time 1.0096 s, even the 16-segment polygonal line reduces the difference from exact time to about 2% of the straight-line difference.
Observed order is 1.06, and 1.03 for the 32/64-segment pair, so discretization error on this grid is approximately inversely proportional to segment count.
This agrees with the cycloid rising vertically near the start as $h\propto x^{2/3}$, where an equally spaced horizontal grid resolves the curve only coarsely.
Defaults and every case terminate with `GradientSmall`; for $X=2\,\mathrm m$, iterations increase from 44 to 152 to 384 for 16, 32 and 64 segments.

For $X=0.5\,\mathrm m$, $\theta_1<\pi$ and the curve does not dip; the 16-segment difference is small at $7.4\times10^{-4}$ s.
For $X=3\,\mathrm m$, the curve dips about 0.24 m below the endpoint and the 16-segment difference is $6.8\times10^{-3}$ s.
Stopping at the iteration limit mixes in optimization error, so when the outcome is `partial` do not interpret the difference and observed order as discretization error.

## Summary

Descent time depends on speed gained along the way as well as slope length; each straight-segment time follows exactly from the mean endpoint speed.
Representing depth as $h=\exp(q)$ excludes curves rising above the start, allowing segment-time gradients to be derived by the chain rule and passed to L-BFGS.
Differences from the analytic curve combine discretization and optimization errors, and can be read as discretization error only when termination reason is `GradientSmall`.
Observed order after doubling segments is nearly 1, suggesting that clustering nodes near the start may reduce discretization error more effectively than adding segments to this uniform grid.
