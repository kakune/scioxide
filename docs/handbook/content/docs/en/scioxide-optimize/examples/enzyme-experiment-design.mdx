[Identify enzyme kinetic parameters](../enzyme-identifiability/) showed that measuring only concentrations below $K_m$ cannot determine $V_{\max}$ and $K_m$ separately.
Before measurement, this page compares which substrate concentrations and point counts make both parameters easier to determine.
The comparison measure is the local Fisher information determinant, giving a local design assuming true parameters $V_{\max}=2.4\,\mathrm{mmol/L/min}$ and $K_m=1.3\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$.

## Local Fisher information and D-optimality

Assume errors $\varepsilon_i$ in observations $y_i=v(S_i)+\varepsilon_i$ are independent normal variables with constant variance $\sigma^2$.
The Fisher information for parameters $\beta=(V_{\max},K_m)$ is expressed using residual Jacobian $J$ as

$$
I(\beta)=\sigma^{-2}J^\mathsf TJ
$$

and asymptotic least-squares estimator covariance is its inverse $\sigma^2(J^\mathsf TJ)^{-1}$.
Row $i$ of $J$ is $\bigl(S_i/(K_m+S_i),\ -V_{\max}S_i/(K_m+S_i)^2\bigr)$; its parameter dependence means design comparisons require assumed parameter values.

The area of the two-parameter confidence ellipse is proportional to $\det(J^\mathsf TJ)^{-1/2}$.
A design maximizing the determinant is called D-optimal.
$\det(J^\mathsf TJ)$ is the squared area of the parallelogram spanned by the two columns of $J$ (the Gram determinant); the Cauchy–Binet formula also gives

$$
\det(J^\mathsf TJ)=\sum_{i<k}\bigl(J_{i1}J_{k2}-J_{k1}J_{i2}\bigr)^2
$$

as a sum of squared 2×2 minors for every row pair.
When $S_i\ne S_k$, the minor from rows $i$ and $k$ is nonzero; at least two distinct positive concentrations therefore give a positive determinant.
For $J^\mathsf TJ$, component $(1,1)$ is dimensionless and $(2,2)$ has units $\mathrm{min}^{-2}$, so the determinant has units $\mathrm{min}^{-2}$.

## Two-point D-optimal design

For concentrations chosen from $(0,S_{\max}]$, the local D-optimal two-parameter Michaelis–Menten design is known to allocate half of measurements to each of two concentrations (R. G. Duggleby, *J. Theor. Biol.* 81, 671–684, 1979).
One concentration is upper limit $S_{\max}$.
To determine the other concentration $x$, maximize the absolute value of the minor from rows $x$ and $S_{\max}$,

$$
J_{x1}J_{S_{\max}2}-J_{S_{\max}1}J_{x2}
=\frac{V_{\max}\,x\,S_{\max}(S_{\max}-x)}{(K_m+x)^2(K_m+S_{\max})^2}
$$

over $x$.
Differentiating $x(S_{\max}-x)/(K_m+x)^2$ and setting it to 0 gives

$$
x^\ast=\frac{K_mS_{\max}}{S_{\max}+2K_m}
$$


For $S_{\max}\gg K_m$, $x^\ast\to K_m$, giving measurements at half-saturation concentration and the upper limit.
For $n$ measurements, with $m_1=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ at $x^\ast$ and $m_2=n-m_1$ at $S_{\max}$, same-concentration pairs contribute 0 to Cauchy–Binet, so the determinant is $m_1m_2$ times the squared minor.

## Maximum concentration in a log-spaced design

Measurements often use $n$ log-spaced concentrations from minimum $S_{\min}$ to maximum $S_{\max}$.
The following cell varies $S_{\max}\in[0.2,200]\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$, plots $\det(J^\mathsf TJ)$ and condition number $\mathrm{cond}(J^\mathsf TJ)=\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$, and finds determinant-maximizing $S_{\max}$.
This condition number is the square of $\kappa_2(J)$ on the previous page.
Optimize $S_{\max}$ by minimizing $-\ln\det(J^\mathsf TJ)$ over $u=\log_{10}S_{\max}$ using `ScalarMinimizer` (Brent by default).
“Best” refers only to this one-variable design family; overlay the two-point D-optimal design at the same $S_{\max}$ and $n$ as a dashed reference.
Inputs are minimum concentration and measurement-point count; where $S_{\min}$ exceeds $0.95\,S_{\max}$, replace $S_{\min}$ with $0.95\,S_{\max}$.

There are three checks.
Compare Brent's maximum with the maximum of the 121-point plotting grid.
Compare determinants at the maximizing point computed from entries of $J^\mathsf TJ$ and from the Cauchy–Binet sum.
Use the plot and table to check that the log-spaced determinant does not exceed the two-point D-optimal value at the same $S_{\max}$.

```rust
//| id: optimize-enzyme-experiment-design
//| ref: optimize-enzyme-experiment-design
//| title: "Inspect effects of substrate range and measurement count on Fisher information"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   design_minimum_substrate: {"type":"range","label":"Minimum substrate concentration Smin [mmol/L]","description":"Change the endpoint concentration measuring the low-concentration slope.","min":0.01,"max":0.5,"step":0.01,"value":0.05}
//|   design_measurements: {"type":"range","label":"Log-spaced measurement count n [points]","description":"Change the number of measurements within the same concentration range.","min":3,"max":20,"step":1,"value":8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"three-points","label":"3 measurement points","description":"The determinant maximum moves to interior Smax ≈ 56 mmol/L, with det = 0.18 and cond = 10.1. At upper limit 200, det falls to 0.142.","inputs":{"design_measurements":3}}
//|   - {"id":"twenty-points","label":"20 measurement points","description":"At Smax = 200, det grows to 8.3, but at Smax = 0.2 it remains only 4.6×10⁻⁵.","inputs":{"design_measurements":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":200,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.27504,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1.27504,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/4/1","approx":{"value":13.043,"atol":1e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":9.0499e-6,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/7/1","approx":{"value":3.27833,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: three-points
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":56.02,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.18153,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":0.14240,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/7/1","approx":{"value":0.37125,"atol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: twenty-points
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":4.5951e-5,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"design","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":8.3053,"atol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::{Interval, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{ScalarMinimizer, ScalarMinimumStart};
use serde_json::json;

// Assumed true parameters Vmax [mmol/L/min] and Km [mmol/L], with sensitivity row [∂v/∂Vmax, ∂v/∂Km].
let (vmax, km) = (2.4_f64, 1.3_f64);
let row = |s: f64| [s / (km + s), -vmax * s / ((km + s) * (km + s))];
let n = design_measurements.round() as usize;
// n log-spaced points from Smin to Smax.
let design = |maximum: f64| -> Vec<f64> {
    let minimum = design_minimum_substrate.min(0.95 * maximum);
    let log_ratio = RealScalar::ln(maximum / minimum);
    (0..n)
        .map(|k| minimum * RealScalar::exp(k as f64 / (n - 1) as f64 * log_ratio))
        .collect()
};
// Determinant of JᵀJ = [[a, b], [b, d]] and condition number λmax/λmin.
let information = |maximum: f64| {
    let (mut a, mut b, mut d) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for s in design(maximum) {
        let [j0, j1] = row(s);
        a += j0 * j0;
        b += j0 * j1;
        d += j1 * j1;
    }
    let disc = RealScalar::sqrt((a - d) * (a - d) + 4.0 * b * b);
    (a * d - b * b, (a + d + disc) / (a + d - disc))
};
// Two-point D-optimal design: ⌊n/2⌋ measurements at x* = Km Smax/(Smax + 2Km), the rest at Smax.
let two_point = |maximum: f64| {
    let (u, w) = (row(km * maximum / (maximum + 2.0 * km)), row(maximum));
    let m1 = (n / 2) as f64;
    m1 * (n as f64 - m1) * (u[0] * w[1] - u[1] * w[0]) * (u[0] * w[1] - u[1] * w[0])
};
// Minimize −ln det(JᵀJ) over u = log₁₀ Smax (one-variable D-optimization).
let start = ScalarMinimumStart::Bounded(
    Interval::new(RealScalar::log10(0.2_f64), RealScalar::log10(200.0_f64))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
);
let best = ScalarMinimizer::default()
    .minimize(
        &|u: f64| Ok(-RealScalar::ln(information(RealScalar::exp10(u)).0)),
        start,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let s_best = RealScalar::exp10(best.x());
let (det_best, cond_best) = information(s_best);
// Independent check: compute the same determinant as Cauchy–Binet sum Σ_{i<k} (2×2 minor)².
let rows: Vec<[f64; 2]> = design(s_best).into_iter().map(row).collect();
let mut binet = 0.0_f64;
for (i, p) in rows.iter().enumerate() {
    for q in &rows[i + 1..] {
        binet += (p[0] * q[1] - q[0] * p[1]) * (p[0] * q[1] - q[0] * p[1]);
    }
}
// 121-point plotting grid. Distinct positive concentrations give det > 0, so there are no nonpositive points.
let grid: Vec<f64> = (0..=120)
    .map(|i| 0.2 * RealScalar::exp10(3.0 * f64::from(i) / 120.0))
    .collect();
let det_curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = grid.iter().map(|&m| [m, information(m).0]).collect();
let two_curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = grid.iter().map(|&m| [m, two_point(m)]).collect();
let cond_curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = grid.iter().map(|&m| [m, information(m).1]).collect();
let grid_best = det_curve.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |acc, p| acc.max(p[1]));
let mark = json!([{"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": s_best, "label": "Determinant-maximizing Smax"}]);
let axes = |label: &str| {
    json!({
        "link": {"group": "enzyme-design-smax"},
        "xLabel": "Maximum design substrate concentration Smax [mmol/L]",
        "yLabel": label,
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": mark
    })
};
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        json!({"name": "n log-spaced points", "points": det_curve}),
        json!({
            "name": "Two-point D-optimal design (reference)",
            "points": two_curve,
            "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
        }),
    ],
    options = &axes("det(JᵀJ) [min⁻²]")
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![json!({"name": "n log-spaced points", "points": cond_curve})],
    options = &axes("cond(JᵀJ) [-]")
);
let columns = json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 6 } }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![
        ("Determinant-maximizing Smax [mmol/L]", s_best),
        ("Corresponding det(JᵀJ) [min⁻²]", det_best),
        ("Maximum det on the 121-point grid [min⁻²]", grid_best),
        (
            "Relative difference from Cauchy–Binet sum [-]",
            (binet - det_best).abs() / det_best
        ),
        ("Corresponding cond(JᵀJ) [-]", cond_best),
        ("det(JᵀJ) at Smax = 0.2 [min⁻²]", information(0.2).0),
        ("det(JᵀJ) at Smax = 200 [min⁻²]", information(200.0).0),
        (
            "Two-point D-optimal det at the same Smax [min⁻²]",
            two_point(s_best)
        ),
        ("Objective evaluations [count]", best.fun_evals() as f64),
    ],
    metadata = json!({ "id": "design" })
);
let outcome = if best.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Brent found the determinant-maximizing Smax within tolerance")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Brent stopped before satisfying tolerance")
        .explanation("Smax is the best sample; treat it as an estimate.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(best.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Determinant-maximizing Smax", s_best, Some("mmol/L"))
        .diagnostic("Objective evaluations", best.fun_evals() as f64, None)
);
```

For defaults $n=8$ and $S_{\min}=0.05\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$, the determinant increases over the entire scan; Brent reaches upper limit $S_{\max}=200\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ and returns `Converged` ($\det=1.275\,\mathrm{min^{-2}}$, condition number 13.0).
The grid maximum is also 1.275, and relative difference from the Cauchy–Binet sum is at rounding-error level.
At $S_{\max}=0.2$, $\det=9.0\times10^{-6}$; when maximum concentration is far below $K_m$, additional points merely repeat almost the same sensitivity direction.
Even with 20 points, the determinant at $S_{\max}=0.2$ remains $4.6\times10^{-5}$; adding points and widening the concentration range are different improvements.

The two-point D-optimal design at the same $S_{\max}=200$ and $n=8$ (4 measurements each at $x^\ast=1.28\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ and $200\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$) has determinant 3.28, about 2.6 times the log-spaced design.
Log spacing allocates some points to low-concentration regions with weak sensitivity, giving less information for the same measurement count.
However, two-point designs strongly depend on assumed $K_m$ and include no additional concentrations for assessing model fit through residuals.

With 3 measurement points, the maximum moves to interior $S_{\max}=56\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$ ($\det=0.18$), while the determinant at upper limit 200 falls to 0.142.
For 3 log-spaced points, the middle point lies at $\sqrt{S_{\min}S_{\max}}$ (1.67 for $S_{\max}=56$); an excessive maximum concentration moves it away from $K_m$, leaving no point sensitive to $K_m$.
For $S\gg K_m$, sensitivity to $K_m$ decreases on the order of $V_{\max}/S$.
At the same $S_{\max}=56$, the two-point D-optimal design (one measurement at $x^\ast=1.24$ and two at 56) has determinant 0.371.

Log spacing is only one way to cover several orders of magnitude; practical optimal design also depends on measurement count, concentration-dependent error variance, prior ranges for unknown parameters and experimental limits such as solubility.
The 200 mmol/L upper limit here serves only the scan.

## Summary

Local Fisher information determinant $\det(J^\mathsf TJ)$ compares concentration designs before measurement under assumed parameters.
When maximum concentration is far below $K_m$, extra points barely increase the determinant; first ensure a range spanning $K_m$.
The 8-point log-spaced determinant reaches only about 40% of the two-point D-optimal design at the same upper limit (half of measurements each at $x^\ast=K_mS_{\max}/(S_{\max}+2K_m)$ and $S_{\max}$).
Interpret optimal-design results within their assumed parameters, error model and searched design family.
