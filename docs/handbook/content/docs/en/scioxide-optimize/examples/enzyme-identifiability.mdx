Initial enzyme reaction rates saturate as substrate concentration increases; the Michaelis–Menten equation describes this saturation curve.
With a narrow observed concentration range, estimates of maximum reaction rate $V_{\max}$ and Michaelis constant $K_m$ become strongly correlated, preventing separate determination of both parameters.
The parameters are then called practically unidentifiable.
This page derives the rate equation from the mechanism, fits by Levenberg–Marquardt (LM), and uses the residual Jacobian to examine what is and is not determined as the observation range narrows.

## From reaction mechanism to saturation curve

Consider the reaction in which enzyme $E$ binds substrate $S$ to form complex $ES$ and releases product $P$:

$$
E+S
\mathrel{\mathop{\rightleftharpoons}^{k_1}_{k_{-1}}}
ES
\mathrel{\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{k_{\mathrm{cat}}}}
E+P
$$


During the short interval for initial-rate measurement, neglect the reverse reaction from product and assume the complex is quasi-steady:

$$
\frac{d[ES]}{dt}
=k_1[E][S]-(k_{-1}+k_{\mathrm{cat}})[ES]
\approx0
$$


Eliminating $[E]$ using total enzyme concentration $[E]_T=[E]+[ES]$ gives

$$
[ES]=\frac{[E]_T[S]}{K_m+[S]},
\qquad
K_m=\frac{k_{-1}+k_{\mathrm{cat}}}{k_1}
$$


Writing substrate concentration as $S=[S]$, production rate $v=k_{\mathrm{cat}}[ES]$ becomes

$$
v(S;V_{\max},K_m)
=\frac{V_{\max}S}{K_m+S},
\qquad
V_{\max}=k_{\mathrm{cat}}[E]_T
$$


$V_{\max}$ has rate units [mmol/L/min], $K_m$ has substrate concentration units [mmol/L], and at $S=K_m$ we have $v=V_{\max}/2$.

## Two limits and sensitivities

In the low-substrate regime $S\ll K_m$,

$$
v(S)\approx\frac{V_{\max}}{K_m}S
$$


Observations confined to this regime determine slope $V_{\max}/K_m$, but have difficulty determining both parameters separately.
For example, doubling both scarcely changes low-concentration predictions.

In the high-substrate regime $S\gg K_m$,

$$
v(S)=V_{\max}\left(1-\frac{K_m}{S}+O\!\left(\frac{K_m^2}{S^2}\right)\right)
$$

so the plateau determines $V_{\max}$, while sensitivity $\partial v/\partial K_m\approx-V_{\max}/S$ to $K_m$ weakens inversely with $S$.
Measurements therefore need concentrations well below, near and well above $K_m$.

Row $i$ of Jacobian $J$ for residuals $r_i=v(S_i)-y_i$ is

$$
\frac{\partial r_i}{\partial V_{\max}}
=\frac{S_i}{K_m+S_i},
\qquad
\frac{\partial r_i}{\partial K_m}
=-\frac{V_{\max}S_i}{(K_m+S_i)^2}
$$


The second-to-first component ratio is $-V_{\max}/(K_m+S_i)$; for $S_i\ll K_m$ it is nearly $-V_{\max}/K_m$ in every row, making the columns nearly parallel.
With at least two distinct positive concentrations, ratios differ by row and $J$ retains rank 2.
The problem is not rank deficiency: column angle $\theta$ approaches 0 or $\pi$, increasing condition number $\kappa_2(J)$ of $J$.

Writing $J^\mathsf TJ=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&d\end{pmatrix}$ gives column cosine $\cos\theta=b/\sqrt{ad}$.
For independent observation errors with constant variance, asymptotic estimator covariance is proportional to $\sigma^2(J^\mathsf TJ)^{-1}$, and correlation of $V_{\max}$ and $K_m$ estimators is $-b/\sqrt{ad}=-\cos\theta$.
As $|\cos\theta|$ approaches 1, both estimates move together and the objective valley becomes elongated.
Compute $\kappa_2(J)$ from eigenvalues $\lambda_{\max}\ge\lambda_{\min}$ of $J^\mathsf TJ$ using $\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}}$.

## Narrow the observation range

Generate synthetic observations from known parameters $V_{\max}=2.4\,\mathrm{mmol/L/min}$ and $K_m=1.3\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$, then fit by LM using only concentrations at or below the specified upper limit.
Candidate concentrations are 10 points over 0.05–10 mmol/L, each perturbed by a deterministic sinusoid of specified amplitude.
Inputs are initial values for both parameters, perturbation amplitude and maximum substrate concentration used.

The plot shows the LM fit, true rate curve (dashed) and synthetic observations (symbols).
The first table gives estimates and relative errors against truth; the second gives observation count, residual RMSE, column cosine, estimator correlation, $\kappa_2(J)$ and iterations.
Column cosine and $\kappa_2(J)$ are independent values formed by building $J^\mathsf TJ$ from the analytic Jacobian at the LM solution and computing 2×2 eigenvalues in closed form.
With zero perturbation, observations lie exactly on the model curve, allowing truth recovery to check the model and solver.
The final outcome follows `is_success()` of the LM termination reason; this establishes only that its stopping test passed, not that parameters were separately determined.

```rust
//| id: optimize-enzyme-identifiability
//| ref: optimize-enzyme-identifiability
//| title: "Vary Michaelis–Menten initial parameters, noise and concentration range"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   enzyme_initial_vmax: {"type":"range","label":"Initial Vmax [mmol/L/min]","description":"Change the initial plateau estimate for LM iterations.","min":0.8,"max":5,"step":0.1,"value":1.5}
//|   enzyme_initial_km: {"type":"range","label":"Initial Km [mmol/L]","description":"Change the initial half-saturation concentration estimate for LM iterations.","min":0.2,"max":4,"step":0.2,"value":0.6}
//|   enzyme_noise: {"type":"range","label":"Rate perturbation amplitude [mmol/L/min]","description":"Change the deterministic observation perturbation magnitude.","min":0,"max":0.15,"step":0.01,"value":0.03}
//|   maximum_substrate: {"type":"range","label":"Maximum substrate concentration used [mmol/L]","description":"Choose observations restricted to low concentrations or extending to the plateau.","min":0.4,"max":10,"step":0.2,"value":6}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"noise-free","label":"Zero perturbation (truth recovery)","description":"Observations lie exactly on the model curve; LM recovers Vmax = 2.4 and Km = 1.3 within 3×10⁻¹⁴ relative error in 4 iterations.","inputs":{"enzyme_noise":0}}
//|   - {"id":"low-range","label":"Maximum substrate concentration 0.4 mmol/L","description":"Only 4 observations remain; RMSE stays small at 0.0135, while Vmax and Km differ from truth by −34% and −40%. The ratio Vmax/Km differs by only +10%, and column cosine is −0.9935.","inputs":{"maximum_substrate":0.4}}
//|   - {"id":"low-range-noise-free","label":"Maximum substrate concentration 0.4 mmol/L, zero perturbation","description":"Without perturbation, the same 4 points recover truth within 7×10⁻¹⁰ relative error.","inputs":{"maximum_substrate":0.4,"enzyme_noise":0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.42387,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.32767,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.020606,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":-0.89840,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.89840,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/4/1","approx":{"value":4.6619,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/5/1","range":{"min":3,"max":12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: noise-free
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-9,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-1e-9,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-9}}
//|   - case: low-range
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":-0.3381,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":-0.3975,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":0.0986,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":4}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":-0.99352,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: low-range-noise-free
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-7,"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-1e-7,"max":1e-7}}
use scioxide_core::{GradientTolerance, RealScalar, StepTolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    LeastSquares, LeastSquaresFn, LeastSquaresGlobalization, LeastSquaresOptions,
};
use serde_json::json;

// Use only candidate concentrations below the limit and add deterministic perturbations to known parameters Vmax = 2.4, Km = 1.3.
let candidates = [0.05_f64, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.3, 2.0, 3.5, 6.0, 10.0];
let substrate: Vec<f64> = candidates
    .into_iter()
    .filter(|&s| s <= maximum_substrate + 1.0e-12)
    .collect();
let rates: Vec<f64> = substrate
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, &s)| 2.4 * s / (1.3 + s) + enzyme_noise * RealScalar::sin(1.73 * i as f64 + 0.2))
    .collect();
let (s_r, v_r, s_j) = (substrate.clone(), rates.clone(), substrate.clone());
// Residual rᵢ = Vmax Sᵢ/(Km + Sᵢ) − vᵢ and analytic Jacobian row [S/(Km+S), −Vmax S/(Km+S)²].
let problem = LeastSquaresFn::new(2, substrate.len(), move |p: &[f64], r: &mut [f64]| {
    for ((ri, &s), &v) in r.iter_mut().zip(&s_r).zip(&v_r) {
        *ri = p[0] * s / (p[1] + s) - v;
    }
    Ok(())
})
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
.with_jacobian(move |p: &[f64], j: &mut Matrix<f64>| {
    for (row, &s) in j.as_mut_slice().chunks_exact_mut(2).zip(&s_j) {
        row[0] = s / (p[1] + s);
        row[1] = -p[0] * s / ((p[1] + s) * (p[1] + s));
    }
    Ok(())
});
let options = LeastSquaresOptions::default()
    .with_gradient_tolerance(
        GradientTolerance::new(1.0e-8_f64, 1.0e-8).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    )
    .with_step_tolerance(StepTolerance::new(1.0e-14_f64, 1.0e-14).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
    .with_globalization(LeastSquaresGlobalization::levenberg_marquardt());
let result = LeastSquares::new(options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .solve(&problem, &[enzyme_initial_vmax, enzyme_initial_km])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (vmax, km) = (result.x()[0], result.x()[1]);
let curve = |vm: f64, k: f64| {
    (0..=240)
        .map(|i| {
            let s = maximum_substrate * i as f64 / 240.0;
            [s, vm * s / (k + s)]
        })
        .collect::<Vec<_>>()
};
let observed: Vec<[f64; 2]> = substrate
    .iter()
    .zip(&rates)
    .map(|(&s, &v)| [s, v])
    .collect();
emit_chart!(json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "xLabel": "Substrate concentration S [mmol/L]",
    "yLabel": "Initial reaction rate v [mmol/L/min]",
    "layers": [
        { "id": "fit", "kind": "line", "name": "LM fit", "points": curve(vmax, km) },
        {
            "id": "truth",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "Known true curve",
            "points": curve(2.4, 1.3),
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
        },
        { "id": "observed", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Synthetic observations", "points": observed }
    ]
}));
let sig6 = json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let estimate_columns = json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "LM estimate", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Known truth", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
let relative = |estimate: f64, truth: f64| (estimate - truth) / truth;
emit_table_with_columns!(
    estimate_columns,
    vec![
        ("Vmax [mmol/L/min]", vmax, 2.4, relative(vmax, 2.4)),
        ("Km [mmol/L]", km, 1.3, relative(km, 1.3)),
        (
            "Low-concentration slope Vmax/Km [1/min]",
            vmax / km,
            2.4 / 1.3,
            relative(vmax / km, 2.4 / 1.3)
        ),
    ],
    metadata = json!({ "id": "estimates" })
);
// Independent diagnostic: form JᵀJ = [[a, b], [b, d]] at the LM solution and compute 2×2 eigenvalues in closed form.
let (mut a, mut b, mut d) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
for &s in &substrate {
    let (j0, j1) = (s / (km + s), -vmax * s / ((km + s) * (km + s)));
    a += j0 * j0;
    b += j0 * j1;
    d += j1 * j1;
}
let disc = RealScalar::sqrt((a - d) * (a - d) + 4.0 * b * b);
let (largest, smallest) = (0.5 * (a + d + disc), 0.5 * (a + d - disc));
let cosine = b / RealScalar::sqrt(a * d);
let rmse =
    RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 * result.cost().map_err(|e| e.to_string())? / substrate.len() as f64);
let diagnostic_columns = json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Diagnostic" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": sig6 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    diagnostic_columns,
    vec![
        ("Observations [points]", substrate.len() as f64),
        ("Residual RMSE [mmol/L/min]", rmse),
        ("J column cosine cos θ [-]", cosine),
        ("Estimator correlation −cos θ [-]", -cosine),
        ("Condition number κ₂(J) [-]", RealScalar::sqrt(largest / smallest)),
        ("Iterations [count]", result.iterations() as f64),
    ],
    metadata = json!({ "id": "diagnostics" })
);
let outcome = if result.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("LM satisfies its stopping test")
        .explanation("Assess separate parameter determination independently through column cosine and estimate relative errors.")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("LM stopped before satisfying its stopping test")
        .explanation("Returned parameters are the last accepted iterate; treat them as estimates.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(result.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Residual RMSE", rmse, Some("mmol/L/min"))
        .diagnostic("Iterations", result.iterations() as f64, None)
);
```

With default inputs (9 points at $S\le6\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$, perturbation amplitude 0.03), LM reaches `GradientSmall` in 5 iterations, returning $V_{\max}=2.424$, $K_m=1.328$ and residual RMSE $0.021\,\mathrm{mmol/L/min}$.
Estimate relative errors are 1% and 2%; column cosine is $-0.898$, estimator correlation $0.898$, and $\kappa_2(J)=4.66$.
Changing initial values reaches the same point; setting initial $K_m$ to 4 raises iterations to 9.
With zero perturbation, LM recovers $V_{\max}=2.4$ and $K_m=1.3$ within relative error $3\times10^{-14}$ in 4 iterations.

With maximum concentration $0.4\,\mathrm{mmol/L}$, only 4 observations below $K_m$ remain available.
The fitted curve matches observations well (RMSE $0.0135$), and terminates with `GradientSmall`, but estimates $V_{\max}=1.589$ and $K_m=0.783$ differ from truth by 34% and 40%.
By comparison, ratio $V_{\max}/K_m=2.028$ differs from its true value 1.846 by only 10%.
The data determine one combination, the low-concentration slope, rather than two separate parameters; column cosine approaches $-1$ at $-0.9935$, while $\kappa_2(J)$ increases to 18.8.
Passing a stopping test with small residuals does not imply separate parameter identifiability.

With the same 4 points and zero perturbation, truth is recovered within relative error $7\times10^{-10}$.
$J$ retains rank 2, so the model and solver can determine parameters.
A narrow concentration range causes large parameter shifts because observation perturbations are amplified along the weak direction formed by nearly parallel columns.
This weak direction is the eigenvector of the small eigenvalue of $J^\mathsf TJ$; it changes $V_{\max}$ and $K_m$ by nearly equal proportions, leaving ratio $V_{\max}/K_m$ little changed.

Condition numbers also depend on parameter units, so they do not supply an absolute threshold.
Use them to compare observation designs with identical units and scales.

## The temptation to linearize

The reciprocal Lineweaver–Burk transformation

$$
\frac1v=\frac{K_m}{V_{\max}}\frac1S+\frac1{V_{\max}}
$$

resembles linear regression.
However, $1/v$ strongly amplifies errors in small $v$, making errors with constant original-scale variance highly heterogeneous.
Although useful for plotting or initial values, it can bias final estimation if the error model is ignored.
Fitting the original nonlinear equation directly with weights matching observation errors makes assumptions easier to state explicitly.

Substrate depletion, product inhibition, cooperativity or enzyme inactivation can make the Michaelis–Menten equation itself inadequate.
Inspect residuals in concentration order and distinguish systematic deviations near the plateau from numerical nonconvergence.

A final report should include concentration design, repeated-measurement counts, error scales, initial values, Jacobian diagnostics (column cosine and condition number), parameter covariance or profiles, and termination reasons.
A fitted-curve plot alone does not convey identifiability.

## Summary

First verify truth recovery with zero perturbation, then inspect estimate changes when narrowing the observation range.
With observations only below $K_m$, $V_{\max}$ and $K_m$ may differ from their true values by tens of percent despite small RMSE and termination `GradientSmall`; the data determine ratio $V_{\max}/K_m$.
Assess weak identifiability by an absolute column cosine near 1 or large $\kappa_2(J)$, and secure concentrations spanning $K_m$ before adding more measurement points.
LM termination reasons describe stopping tests and cannot determine identifiability.
