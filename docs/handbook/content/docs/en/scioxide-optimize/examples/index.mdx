Obtaining a number resembling a minimum and obtaining evidence sufficient for a decision are different achievements.
Residual structure, objective convexity, linear-constraint polyhedra, variable integrality and parameter identifiability determine which algorithms apply and how to assess results.
Every example derives objectives and constraints from a concrete scientific, engineering or economic problem, returns the numerical solution to original units and constraints, and compares it with independent checks such as analytic solutions, exhaustive enumeration or other methods.
The following table lists each example's subject, APIs and independent checks.

## Four layers from model to algorithm

Construct optimization problems in four layers.
First, the physical model determines variables and prediction equations from material balances, reaction mechanisms, return tables, covariances or Bernoulli observations.
Second, the statistical or decision model selects least squares, negative log-likelihood, risk penalties or profit.
Third, the feasible set specifies equalities, inequalities, bounds and integer conditions.
Fourth comes the numerical algorithm.

Greater accuracy in a lower layer cannot repair an error in a higher layer.
For example, if the one-compartment drug model is wrong, solving Gauss–Newton residuals to $10^{-12}$ will not correct physical predictions.
Solving integer lots as a continuous LP means LP optimality evidence does not establish the original production problem's optimum.

## Indicators to read alongside objective values

For local nonlinear methods, inspect gradients or stationarity, step acceptance and Hessian/Jacobian conditioning.
For constrained problems, substitute the solution into the original equations to check primal feasibility, bounds and complementarity.
For LP, QP and MIP, dual evidence and gaps supported by relaxation lower bounds provide stronger evidence than agreement between two methods alone.

For stochastic search, report the distribution across seeds and total evaluations, rather than a single best value.
For parameter estimation, distinguish identifiable parameter combinations, observation design and performance on unseen data from training objective values.

## Termination reasons and evidence of success

A termination reason classified as successful indicates that the method has met its own stopping test.
The examples also check validity through the following quantities.

| Problem family | Success classification | Supporting quantities |
|---|---|---|
| Local derivative methods | `GradientSmall`, `Converged` | Gradient norm. `StepSmall`, `FunctionChangeSmall` and `Stagnation` are not success |
| Nonlinear least squares | `GradientSmall` (default original-gradient test), `Converged` (Moré's convergence test) | Original gradient, Jacobian singular values, recovery of true values with zero noise |
| Constrained nonlinear | `Converged` | Primal feasibility, stationarity, complementarity |
| LP and QP | `Converged` and independent-verification `Accepted` | Primal and dual feasibility, stationarity, complementarity |
| MIP | `Converged` | Verified integer solution, search lower bound (floating-point estimate), absolute gap |
| Proximal methods and ADMM | `Converged` | KKT violations of the original objective. ADMM primal and dual residuals alone are insufficient |
| Multistart, evolutionary methods, basin hopping, annealing and NSGA-II | Not classified as success (`SearchBudgetCompleted`, `PopulationStagnation`) | Finite search limits and best observed values. Establish neither global optimality nor approximation of the entire front |
| Quadratic assignment | `Converged` means each stage has stopped | Recomputed permutation cost and gap to reference values. Does not establish global optimality |

`StepSmall` and `FunctionChangeSmall` mean that movement has ceased, not that first-order optimality holds.
`SearchBudgetCompleted` means only that the planned search has finished.
Read results in three stages: whether a value was returned, whether termination is classified as successful, and whether the application has the evidence it needs.
See [Termination reason classification](oxref:optimize-termination-classes) for the full set of termination reasons.

## Summary

Choose examples by constraint type and desired diagnostics as well as objective form.
Examine least-squares parameter estimation through pharmacokinetics and enzyme reactions, LP/QP/MIP duals and lower bounds through production planning, portfolios and facility location, and the physical meaning of equality multipliers through catenaries.
For global search, Rastrigin, the Thomson problem, Lennard-Jones clusters and facility layouts show differences caused by seeds and evaluation counts.
Every example compares numerical solutions with analytic solutions, exhaustive searches, literature values or substitutions into original equations, reading termination reasons beside independent checks.
Vary controls to determine whether solution changes originate in the model, observations or numerical algorithm.
