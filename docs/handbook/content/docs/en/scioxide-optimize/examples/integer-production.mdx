Production lot counts and equipment counts must be indivisible integers.
Simply rounding a continuous linear-programming (LP) solution may violate resource constraints or leave a low-profit plan.
This page formulates integer-lot production planning as a mixed-integer program (MIP), examining LP profit upper bounds, branch-and-bound search, and incumbents and gaps after early stops.

## Integer-lot production planning

Produce $x$ lots of standard sets and $y$ lots of premium sets.
Battery-cell use, assembly time and profit per lot are:

| Product | Battery cells [cells/lot] | Assembly time [h/lot] | Profit [thousand yen/lot] |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard set $x$ | 4 | 4 | 190 |
| Premium set $y$ | 7 | 3 | $p_y$ |

Available resources are $C$ battery cells and $H$ assembly hours, with demand limits $x\le18$ and $y\le12$.
Indivisible lots give the problem

$$
\begin{aligned}
\max\quad&190x+p_yy\\
\text{s.t.}\quad&
4x+7y\le C,\\
&4x+3y\le H,\\
&0\le x\le18,\quad0\le y\le12,\\
&x,y\in\mathbb Z
\end{aligned}
$$


All variables are integer, making this a pure integer program; SciOxide handles it as mixed-integer program `MipProblem` with specified integer-variable indices.

## LP-relaxation upper bounds and rounding failures

Removing integer condition $x,y\in\mathbb Z$ gives the LP relaxation.
Its feasible region includes every original integer point, so for profit maximization the relaxed optimum $V_{\mathrm{LP}}$ and integer optimum $V_{\mathrm{MIP}}$ satisfy

$$
V_{\mathrm{LP}}\ge V_{\mathrm{MIP}}
$$


LP profit is an upper bound, not an attainable profit.

At defaults $C=80$, $H=52$ and $p_y=310$, both resource constraints are active equalities.
Subtracting $4x+3y=52$ from $4x+7y=80$ gives $4y=28$; the relaxation solution is $(x,y)=(7.75,7)$ with profit upper bound $3642.5$ thousand yen.
Componentwise rounding gives $(8,7)$, using $81$ cells and violating the $80$-cell limit.
Rounding down to $(7,7)$ is feasible but gives profit $3500$ thousand yen, which is suboptimal as shown below.

## Branch and bound

Branch and bound begins by solving the root-node LP relaxation.
If relaxed solution $v$ for integer variable $x_j$ is fractional, split into two child nodes (branching):

$$
x_j\le\lfloor v\rfloor
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
x_j\ge\lceil v\rceil
$$


The children remove only strip $\lfloor v\rfloor<x_j<\lceil v\rceil$, excluding the parent relaxation solution without losing any integer point.
Branching on $x$ at default inputs gives child $x\le7$ with relaxed solution $(7,52/7)$ and upper bound $3632.9$, and child $x\ge8$ with solution $(8,20/3)$ and upper bound $3586.7$.
Each node's relaxation value upper-bounds the profit achievable by integer points in that subregion.

A feasible integer solution becomes the incumbent, with recorded profit $V^{\mathrm{inc}}$.
If node upper bound $\bar V$ satisfies $\bar V\le V^{\mathrm{inc}}$, its subtree cannot improve the incumbent, so the node is pruned (bounding).
Nodes with infeasible relaxations or already-integer relaxation solutions also close.

`BranchAndBound` solves minimization problems, so pass negated profit.
Unprocessed nodes are handled in increasing relaxation value (minimization lower bound), called best-bound search.
Incumbent objective $f^{\mathrm{inc}}$ minus global lower bound $f_{\mathrm{low}}$, including unprocessed nodes, is the absolute gap.
Search stops with `Converged` when the absolute gap is at most tolerance $a+r\max(1,|f^{\mathrm{inc}}|,|f_{\mathrm{low}}|)$ (default $a=r=10^{-8}$), or when an integer solution exists and no unprocessed nodes remain.
Exhausting unprocessed nodes without an integer solution gives `Infeasible`.
Reaching the node limit gives `NodeLimit`; even a returned integer solution has unestablished optimality while nodes remain unprocessed.
Worst-case node counts grow exponentially, but good incumbents and strong relaxations prune many branches.
[Mixed-integer programming and branch and cut](../../algorithms/mixed-integer-programming/) explains presolve, cutting planes and branching-variable selection.

## Resources, profit and node limits

Vary both resource limits, premium-product profit and the processed-node limit, solving the LP relaxation and MIP.
The scatter plot shows only feasible integer points inside the continuous polygon, with LP-relaxation and MIP solutions overlaid.

The first table compares LP-relaxation, MIP and exhaustive-enumeration solutions and profits.
Exhaustive enumeration independently substitutes all 247 integer points with $0\le x\le18$ and $0\le y\le12$ into the original inequalities and maximizes feasible profit; it is usable only for this small problem.
The second table gives search evidence returned by MIP (lower bound, gaps, violations and node count).
Without a verified integer solution, MIP solution and profit display “No integer solution”.
The final outcome is satisfied when `is_success()` is true, partial when false with an integer solution, and failed without one.

```rust
//| id: optimize-integer-production
//| ref: optimize-integer-production
//| title: "Vary resources, profits and the search-node limit to solve a MIP"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   integer_cell_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Battery-cell limit C [cells]","description":"Change the feasible integer-point set and LP-relaxation vertices.","min":50,"max":120,"step":1,"value":80}
//|   integer_labor_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Assembly-time limit H [h]","description":"Change scarcity of the second resource.","min":35,"max":80,"step":1,"value":52}
//|   premium_kit_profit: {"type":"range","label":"Premium-set profit pᵧ [thousand yen/lot]","description":"Change objective contour slopes and the selected integer point.","min":240,"max":420,"step":10,"value":310}
//|   branch_node_budget: {"type":"range","label":"Processed-node limit [count]","description":"Change the search effort available to reduce the gap.","min":1,"max":40,"step":1,"value":20}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"root-only","label":"Node limit 1","description":"Solving only the root relaxation gives NodeLimit without a verified integer solution.","inputs":{"branch_node_budget":1}}
//|   - {"id":"early-stop","label":"Node limit 2","description":"An integer solution (7, 7) with profit 3500 thousand yen is found, but NodeLimit leaves absolute gap 142.5 thousand yen.","inputs":{"branch_node_budget":2}}
//|   - {"id":"premium-heavy","label":"Premium-set profit pᵧ = 420","description":"The LP relaxation moves to (0, 11.43), upper bound 4800 thousand yen; MIP returns (0, 11), profit 4620 thousand yen, matching exhaustive enumeration.","inputs":{"premium_kit_profit":420}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":7.75,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":3642.5,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":6,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":8,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":3620,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":3620,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"evidence","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"evidence","path":"/rows/8/1","approx":{"value":22.5,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"evidence","path":"/rows/9/1","equals":104}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: root-only
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":"NodeLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"evidence","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-3642.5,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"failed"}
//|   - case: early-stop
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":3500,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":"NodeLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"evidence","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":142.5,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: premium-heavy
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":4800,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":4620,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solutions","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":4620,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::{Bounds, Budget};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    BranchAndBound, LinearConstraintSense, LinearConstraints, LpProblem, MipProblem,
    SimplexSolver,
};
use serde_json::json;

let fixed = json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
// Battery cells 4x + 7y ≤ C, assembly 4x + 3y ≤ H, demand 0 ≤ x ≤ 18, 0 ≤ y ≤ 12.
// Pass profit maximization as negated minimization.
let rows = LinearConstraints::new(
    Matrix::from_vec(2, 2, vec![4.0_f64, 7.0, 4.0, 3.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    vec![integer_cell_capacity, integer_labor_capacity],
    vec![LinearConstraintSense::LessEqual; 2],
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let relaxation = LpProblem::new(vec![-190.0_f64, -premium_kit_profit])
    .and_then(|p| p.with_constraints(rows))
    .and_then(|p| {
        p.with_bounds(Bounds::new(vec![
            (Some(0.0), Some(18.0)),
            (Some(0.0), Some(12.0)),
        ])?)
    })
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Solve the LP relaxation without integrality and the MIP with both variables integer.
let lp = SimplexSolver::<f64>::default()
    .solve(&relaxation)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let budget =
    Budget::try_from(branch_node_budget.round() as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mip = BranchAndBound::<f64>::default()
    .with_max_nodes(budget)
    .solve(&MipProblem::new(relaxation, vec![0, 1]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: substitute all integer points into original inequalities and find maximum feasible profit.
let (mut feasible, mut best) = (Vec::new(), (f64::NEG_INFINITY, [0.0_f64; 2]));
for x in 0..=18 {
    for y in 0..=12 {
        let (x, y) = (f64::from(x), f64::from(y));
        if 4.0 * x + 7.0 * y <= integer_cell_capacity
            && 4.0 * x + 3.0 * y <= integer_labor_capacity
        {
            feasible.push([x, y]);
            let profit = 190.0 * x + premium_kit_profit * y;
            if profit > best.0 {
                best = (profit, [x, y]);
            }
        }
    }
}
// Without a verified integer solution, the MIP point is not an integer solution; omit it from the plot and leave table entries blank.
let incumbent = mip
    .has_incumbent()
    .then(|| (mip.x()[0], mip.x()[1], -mip.fun()));
let mip_points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = incumbent.iter().map(|&(x, y, _)| [x, y]).collect();
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &vec![
        json!({"name": "Feasible integer points", "points": feasible}),
        json!({"name": "LP-relaxation solution", "points": [lp.x()], "style": {"symbol": "triangle"}}),
        json!({"name": "MIP integer solution", "points": mip_points, "style": {"symbol": "diamond"}}),
    ],
    options = &json!({
        "xLabel": "Standard sets x [lots]",
        "yLabel": "Premium sets y [lots]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 18.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 12.0
    })
);
let missing = "No integer solution";
let columns = json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Problem" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "x [lots]", "type": "number", "format": fixed, "nullText": missing },
    { "key": "2", "label": "y [lots]", "type": "number", "format": fixed, "nullText": missing },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Profit [thousand yen]", "type": "number", "format": fixed, "nullText": missing },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Termination status", "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![
        (
            "LP relaxation (profit upper bound)",
            Some(lp.x()[0]),
            Some(lp.x()[1]),
            Some(-lp.fun()),
            Some(lp.termination().to_string()),
        ),
        (
            "MIP (branch and bound)",
            incumbent.map(|v| v.0),
            incumbent.map(|v| v.1),
            incumbent.map(|v| v.2),
            Some(mip.termination().to_string()),
        ),
        (
            "Exhaustive enumeration (independent check)",
            Some(best.1[0]),
            Some(best.1[1]),
            Some(best.0),
            None
        ),
    ],
    metadata = json!({ "id": "solutions" })
);
// Search evidence: floating-point numerical values, not exact-arithmetic certificates.
let stats = mip.statistics();
let evidence_columns = json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "MIP search evidence" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": fixed, "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    evidence_columns,
    vec![
        ("Global lower bound (minimization) [thousand yen]", mip.global_lower_bound()),
        ("Absolute gap [thousand yen]", mip.absolute_gap()),
        ("Relative gap [-]", mip.relative_gap()),
        ("Integrality violation [lots]", mip.integrality_violation()),
        ("Primal feasibility violation [-]", mip.primal_feasibility()),
        ("Processed nodes [count]", Some(stats.nodes() as f64)),
        ("LP relaxations [count]", Some(stats.relaxations() as f64)),
        ("Strong-branching trials [count]", Some(stats.probes() as f64)),
        ("Difference from LP upper bound [thousand yen]", incumbent.map(|v| -lp.fun() - v.2)),
        ("Feasible enumerated integer points [count]", Some(feasible.len() as f64)),
    ],
    metadata = json!({ "id": "evidence" })
);
let outcome = if mip.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Branch and bound stopped after satisfying its gap test")
} else if mip.has_incumbent() {
    Outcome::partial("A feasible integer solution exists, but optimality is unestablished").explanation(
        "Unprocessed nodes remain. A solution better by up to the absolute gap may remain.",
    )
} else {
    Outcome::failed("Stopped without a verified integer solution")
        .explanation("The returned point is not an integer solution. Increase the node limit and solve again.")
};
let mut outcome = outcome.status(mip.termination().to_string()).diagnostic(
    "Processed nodes",
    stats.nodes() as f64,
    None,
);
if let Some(gap) = mip.absolute_gap() {
    outcome = outcome.diagnostic("Absolute gap", gap, Some("thousand yen"));
}
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

At default inputs the LP-relaxation solution is $(7.75,7)$, with profit upper bound 3642.5 thousand yen.
`BranchAndBound` processes 5 nodes and stops with `Converged`, returning integer solution $(6,8)$ and profit 3620 thousand yen.
This matches the best of 104 feasible enumerated points, with absolute gap 0 and 22.5 thousand yen below the LP upper bound.
Optimum $(6,8)$ uses exactly 80 battery cells and 48 assembly hours, leaving 4 hours unused.
Rounded relaxation $(8,7)$ is infeasible; rounded-down $(7,7)$ gives profit 3500 thousand yen, so rounding does not yield the optimum.

Continuous LP optima can be chosen from polygon vertices; the corresponding integer-problem vertices belong to the convex hull of feasible integer points.
$(6,8)$ is such a hull vertex, distinct from original constraint-line intersection $(7.75,7)$.

At premium profit 420 thousand yen, the LP moves to $(0,11.43)$ with upper bound 4800 thousand yen; MIP processes 11 nodes and returns $(0,11)$ with profit 4620 thousand yen, matching enumeration.

At node limit 1, solving only the root relaxation gives `NodeLimit` without a verified integer solution (`has_incumbent()` is `false`).
The returned point is then not an integer solution, so neither plot nor table displays its values and the outcome is failed.
At limit 2, integer solution $(7,7)$ gives profit 3500 thousand yen, but termination `NodeLimit` gives a partial outcome.
The global lower bound remains $-3642.5$ (root profit upper bound 3642.5 thousand yen), leaving absolute gap 142.5 thousand yen; the optimum may improve this solution by up to 142.5 thousand yen.
The enumerated best is indeed 3620 thousand yen, improving the incumbent by 120 thousand yen.

## Reporting optimality

After a node-limit stop, a returned integer solution is usable as a feasible plan but need not be optimal.
`MipResult` reports these properties separately.
Check feasibility through `has_incumbent()`, `integrality_violation()`, `primal_feasibility()` and substitution into original constraints.
Read optimality through termination status, `global_lower_bound()`, `absolute_gap()` and `relative_gap()`.

`absolute_gap()` is a numerical gap computed in floating point.
`certified_optimality_gap()` is reserved for exact-arithmetic certificates; floating-point branch and bound always returns `None`.
Even a zero-gap solution is optimal only for the supplied model under floating-point tolerances.

Final checks compare integrality of every production quantity, all resource slacks, profit recomputed from the selected solution and difference from the LP upper bound.
Objective agreement alone cannot reveal reversed constraint signs, unit-conversion mistakes or omitted integer-variable specifications.
Larger problems cannot be exhaustively enumerated, so return incumbents, lower bounds, gaps and termination status together.

Assuming known demand limits, profits and resource usage is itself strong in practice.
Adding demand scenarios, equipment failures and purchasing-price fluctuations leads to stochastic-programming or robust-optimization MIPs.

## Summary

Removing integrality gives an LP profit upper bound; for the default plan, upper bound 3642.5 thousand yen exceeds integer optimum 3620 thousand yen by 22.5 thousand yen.
Rounding relaxed solutions preserves neither constraints nor optimality; specify integer variables with `MipProblem`, solve with `BranchAndBound` and check original resource equations.
After `NodeLimit`, use `has_incumbent()` to check integer-solution availability and `absolute_gap()` for the remaining gap; do not report optimality unless the gap is within tolerance.
Compare small problems with exhaustive enumeration; for larger ones, report incumbents, lower bounds, gaps and termination status together.
