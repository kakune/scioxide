[LASSO regularization paths and sparse signal recovery](../lasso-compressed-sensing/) used FISTA solutions to examine shrinkage and support recovery.
This page solves the same problem

$$
\min_x F(x)=f(x)+\lambda\|x\|_1,\qquad f(x)=\frac12\|Ax-y\|_2^2
$$

with FISTA and ADMM, checking how well returned coefficients satisfy optimality conditions.
Solvers stop through gradient mappings or residuals, which do not directly measure original-objective optimality.
We therefore derive KKT-violation bounds from stopping quantities and compare violations recomputed at returned coefficients.

Data match the previous defaults: $m=10$ measurements, $n=12$ coefficients, 2 true nonzeros ($+1$ and $-1$), noise half-width 0.02 and seed 17.
This gives $\lambda_{\max}=\|A^\mathsf Ty\|_\infty=0.26306$.

## Defining KKT violation

Let $g=\nabla f(x)=A^\mathsf T(Ax-y)$; the optimality (KKT) condition is $0\in g+\lambda\partial\|x\|_1$.
Define component $i$ violation as $|g_i+\lambda\operatorname{sign}(x_i)|$ for nonzeros and $\max(|g_i|-\lambda,0)$ for zeros; the maximum is the KKT violation.
It equals the $\infty$-norm distance between $0$ and subdifferential $\partial F(x)$, and is at most $\|v\|_\infty$ for every element $v$ of $\partial F(x)$.
See [KKT conditions on the previous page](../lasso-compressed-sensing/) for derivation.

## Proximal gradient and ADMM

The proximal map of $\lambda\|\cdot\|_1$ solves componentwise one-variable problem $\min_z\tfrac12(z-v)^2+s\lambda|z|$ as

$$
\operatorname{prox}_{s\lambda\|\cdot\|_1}(v)_i=\operatorname{sign}(v_i)\max(|v_i|-s\lambda,0)
$$

and is called soft thresholding.
Entries with $|v_i|\le s\lambda$ become exactly 0, so count nonzero support through $x_i\ne0$.

### FISTA stopping and KKT-violation bounds

FISTA steps as $x^+=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda\|\cdot\|_1/L}(y_k-\nabla f(y_k)/L)$ at point $y_k$ extrapolated from the previous two iterates, reducing objective error as $O(1/k^2)$ with step $1/L$.
$L$ need only be at least Lipschitz constant $\|A\|_2^2$ of $\nabla f$; here use easily computed upper bound $L=\|A\|_F^2=4.6246$.
Step $1/L$ is at most optimal $1/\|A\|_2^2$, and strictly smaller when $A$ has rank at least 2.
Each iteration costs $O(mn)$ for products with $A$ and $A^\mathsf T$.

Defaults of `ProximalOptions::new(L)` use iteration limit 1000 and enable gradient restarts.
When $(y_k-x^+)^\mathsf T(x^+-x_k)>0$, restart stops extrapolation and resets acceleration.
Stopping uses gradient mapping $G=L\|x^+-y_k\|_\infty$; default `GradientTolerance` gives `Converged` at $G\le\sqrt\epsilon+\sqrt\epsilon\cdot1\approx2.98\times10^{-8}$ ($\epsilon$ is double-precision machine epsilon).

$G$ is not itself KKT violation but supplies a bound.
The proximal optimality condition defining $x^+$ is $L(y_k-x^+)-\nabla f(y_k)\in\lambda\partial\|x^+\|_1$, so

$$
\nabla f(x^+)-\nabla f(y_k)+L(y_k-x^+)\in\partial F(x^+)
$$


Since $\|\nabla f(x^+)-\nabla f(y_k)\|_\infty\le\|A\|_2^2\|x^+-y_k\|_2\le\sqrt n\,L\|x^+-y_k\|_\infty$, KKT violation at $x^+$ is at most $(1+\sqrt n)\,G$.

### ADMM stopping and KKT-violation bounds

ADMM (alternating direction method of multipliers) splits variables as $x=z$, repeating the following three updates with scaled dual variable $u$.

$$
(A^\mathsf TA+\rho I)x=A^\mathsf Ty+\rho(z-u),\qquad
z=\operatorname{prox}_{(\lambda/\rho)\|\cdot\|_1}(x+u),\qquad
u\leftarrow u+x-z
$$

For fixed penalty $\rho$, factor $A^\mathsf TA+\rho I$ by Cholesky only once initially ($O(mn^2+n^3)$).
Subsequent iterations cost $O(n^2)$ for forward/backward substitution.
`Admm` accepts constraints in form $\mathcal Ax+\mathcal Bz=c$; here set $\mathcal A=I$, $\mathcal B=-I$ and $c=0$.
Primal residual is $r=x-z$; dual residual is $s=\rho\,\mathcal A^\mathsf T\mathcal B(z-z_{\text{prev}})=-\rho(z-z_{\text{prev}})$.
Default `ResidualTolerance` gives `Converged` when both $\|r\|\le\sqrt\epsilon\,(1+\max(\|x\|,\|z\|))$ and $\|s\|\le\sqrt\epsilon\,(1+\rho\|u\|)$ hold.

Both residuals differ from original-objective KKT violation.
The $x$ update satisfies optimality condition $\nabla f(x)+\rho(x-z_{\text{prev}}+u_{\text{prev}})=0$; the $z$ update satisfies $\rho(x+u_{\text{prev}}-z)\in\lambda\partial\|z\|_1$.
Using updated $u=u_{\text{prev}}+x-z$, rewrite the latter as $\rho u\in\lambda\partial\|z\|_1$ and the former as $\nabla f(x)=-\rho u+s$.
Combining them gives

$$
\nabla f(z)-\nabla f(x)+s\in\partial F(z)
$$


Since $\|\nabla f(z)-\nabla f(x)\|_\infty\le\|A\|_2^2\|r\|_2$, KKT violation at $z$ is at most $L\|r\|+\|s\|$.

Both bounds use only solver-reported quantities and upper-bound KKT violation.
See [Proximal-gradient convergence and stopping](oxref:optimize-proximal-gradient-convergence) for general proximal stopping, and [ADMM stopping and penalty adjustment](oxref:optimize-admm-stopping-penalty) for penalty adjustment and ADMM stopping.

## Model and ADMM updates

The following definition block is shared by both cells on this page.
`lasso_data` generates data by the same procedure as the previous defaults; `Loss` defines $f$ and its gradient, `Shrink` soft thresholding, and `kkt_violation` KKT violation.
`Normal` performs the $x$ update and `Threshold` the $z$ update; both return subproblem optimality residuals.
`solve_admm` solves the problem with $\mathcal A=I$, $\mathcal B=-I$ and $c=0$ from zero.

```rust
//| id: optimize-lasso-admm-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "LASSO data, loss, soft thresholding and ADMM updates"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize, scioxide-random]
use scioxide_core::{Budget, SciOxideError, NumericScalar, RealScalar, SolveContext};
use scioxide_linalg::{CholeskyDecomposition, CholeskyPivoting, HermitianInput, Matrix};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    Admm, AdmmBlockUpdate, AdmmOptions, AdmmProblem, AdmmResult, AdmmStart, AdmmUpdateInput,
    AdmmUpdateReport, ProximalMap, SmoothGradient,
};
use scioxide_random::{BitGenerator, SeedableRng, SplitMix64, decode_u64};

/// Smooth part f(x) = ½‖Ax − y‖². a stores m×n matrix A row by row.
pub(crate) struct Loss {
    pub(crate) a: Vec<f64>,
    pub(crate) y: Vec<f64>,
    pub(crate) n: usize,
}

impl SmoothGradient<f64> for Loss {
    fn dimension(&self) -> usize {
        self.n
    }
    fn value(&self, x: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        let rows = self.a.chunks_exact(self.n).zip(&self.y);
        let squares = rows.map(|(row, y)| (dot(row, x) - y) * (dot(row, x) - y));
        Ok(0.5 * squares.sum::<f64>())
    }
    fn gradient_into(&self, x: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        out.fill(0.0);
        for (row, y) in self.a.chunks_exact(self.n).zip(&self.y) {
            let r = dot(row, x) - y;
            out.iter_mut().zip(row).for_each(|(g, a)| *g += a * r);
        }
        Ok(())
    }
}

fn dot(u: &[f64], v: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    u.iter().zip(v).map(|(a, b)| a * b).sum()
}

/// Data and true coefficients for previous defaults (m = 10, n = 12, 2 nonzeros, noise half-width 0.02, seed 17).
pub(crate) fn lasso_data() -> Result<(Loss, Vec<f64>), SciOxideError> {
    let (m, n) = (10, 12);
    let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(17);
    let mut uniform = || {
        rng.next_uniform_u64()
            .map(|w| decode_u64::<f64>(w) * 2.0 - 1.0)
    };
    let scale = RealScalar::sqrt(m as f64);
    let a: Vec<f64> = (0..m * n)
        .map(|_| uniform().map(|v| v / scale))
        .collect::<Result<_, _>>()?;
    let truth: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
        .map(|i| [1.0, -1.0].get(i).copied().unwrap_or(0.0))
        .collect();
    let mut y = Vec::with_capacity(m);
    for row in a.chunks_exact(n) {
        y.push(dot(row, &truth) + 0.02 * uniform()?);
    }
    Ok((Loss { a, y, n }, truth))
}

/// Soft thresholding for g(x) = λ‖x‖₁: prox_{sλ‖·‖₁}(v)ᵢ = sign(vᵢ) max(|vᵢ| − sλ, 0). The value is λ.
pub(crate) struct Shrink(pub(crate) f64);

impl ProximalMap<f64> for Shrink {
    fn apply_into(&self, x: &[f64], step: f64, out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        for (o, &v) in out.iter_mut().zip(x) {
            let shrunk = RealScalar::max(NumericScalar::abs(v) - step * self.0, 0.0);
            *o = RealScalar::copysign(shrunk, v);
        }
        Ok(())
    }
}

/// Return each x component's KKT violation from g = ∇f(x) and λ.
/// Use f64 inherent methods for this solver-independent check.
fn violations(g: &[f64], lambda: f64, x: &[f64]) -> f64 {
    let each = x.iter().zip(g).map(|(&v, &g)| {
        if v == 0.0 {
            (g.abs() - lambda).max(0.0)
        } else {
            (g + lambda * v.signum()).abs()
        }
    });
    each.fold(0.0, f64::max)
}

/// Original-objective KKT violation: maximum of max(|gᵢ| − λ, 0) for zeros and |gᵢ + λ sign(xᵢ)| for nonzeros.
pub(crate) fn kkt_violation(loss: &Loss, lambda: f64, x: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    let mut g = vec![0.0; x.len()];
    loss.gradient_into(x, &mut g)?;
    Ok(violations(&g, lambda, x))
}

/// x update: (AᵀA + ρI) x = Aᵀy + ρ(z − u). Return the maximum equation residual.
struct Normal {
    factor: CholeskyDecomposition<f64>,
    matrix: Vec<f64>,
    aty: Vec<f64>,
}

impl AdmmBlockUpdate<f64> for Normal {
    fn update_into(
        &self,
        input: AdmmUpdateInput<'_, f64>,
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<AdmmUpdateReport<f64>, SciOxideError> {
        let rho = input.rho();
        let rhs: Vec<f64> = (self.aty.iter().zip(input.other()).zip(input.scaled_dual()))
            .map(|((&b, &z), &u)| b + rho * (z - u))
            .collect();
        out.copy_from_slice(self.factor.solve(&rhs[..])?.as_slice());
        let rows = self.matrix.chunks_exact(out.len()).zip(&rhs);
        let defect = rows
            .map(|(row, b)| (dot(row, out) - b).abs())
            .fold(0.0, f64::max);
        Ok(AdmmUpdateReport::new(true, defect))
    }
}

/// z update: z = prox_{(λ/ρ)‖·‖₁}(x + u). Return z-subproblem optimality violation.
struct Threshold(f64);

impl AdmmBlockUpdate<f64> for Threshold {
    fn update_into(
        &self,
        input: AdmmUpdateInput<'_, f64>,
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<AdmmUpdateReport<f64>, SciOxideError> {
        let rho = input.rho();
        let v: Vec<f64> = (input.other().iter().zip(input.scaled_dual()))
            .map(|(x, u)| x + u)
            .collect();
        Shrink(self.0).apply_into(&v, 1.0 / rho, out)?;
        // The z-subproblem gradient is ρ(z − v); measure whether its sum with the subdifferential of λ‖z‖₁ contains 0.
        let g: Vec<f64> = out.iter().zip(&v).map(|(z, v)| rho * (z - v)).collect();
        Ok(AdmmUpdateReport::new(true, violations(&g, self.0, out)))
    }
}

/// Express x = z through 𝒜 = I, ℬ = −I, c = 0; solve from zero with fixed penalty ρ.
pub(crate) fn solve_admm(
    loss: &Loss,
    lambda: f64,
    rho: f64,
    limit: Budget,
) -> Result<AdmmResult<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let n = loss.n;
    let mut matrix = vec![0.0; n * n];
    for row in loss.a.chunks_exact(n) {
        for (i, &ai) in row.iter().enumerate() {
            row.iter()
                .enumerate()
                .for_each(|(j, &aj)| matrix[i * n + j] += ai * aj);
        }
    }
    (0..n).for_each(|i| matrix[i * n + i] += rho);
    // The gradient at zero is −Aᵀy; negate it to obtain Aᵀy.
    let mut aty = vec![0.0; n];
    loss.gradient_into(&vec![0.0; n], &mut aty)?;
    aty.iter_mut().for_each(|v| *v = -*v);
    let dense = Matrix::from_vec(n, n, matrix.clone())?;
    let factor =
        CholeskyDecomposition::factor(&dense, HermitianInput::Exact, CholeskyPivoting::None)?;
    let normal = Normal {
        factor,
        matrix,
        aty,
    };
    let identity = Matrix::<f64>::identity(n)?;
    let minus = Matrix::from_vec(n, n, identity.as_slice().iter().map(|v| -v).collect())?;
    let (identity, minus) = (identity.view().try_finite()?, minus.view().try_finite()?);
    let zero = vec![0.0; n];
    let threshold = Threshold(lambda);
    let problem = AdmmProblem::new(&identity, &minus, &zero, &normal, &threshold)?;
    Admm::new(AdmmOptions::new(rho).with_max_iterations(limit))?
        .solve(&problem, AdmmStart::new(&zero, &zero, &zero))
}
```

At defaults ($\lambda=0.1\lambda_{\max}=0.026306$, $\rho=1$), FISTA reaches `Converged` in 164 iterations and ADMM in 287.
Coefficient differences between methods are at most $6\times10^{-7}$; true nonzeros shrink to 0.8295 and $-0.8147$, while true-zero entries 4, 7 and 9 remain at magnitudes 0.009–0.033.
KKT violations are $2.6\times10^{-8}$ for FISTA and $9.3\times10^{-9}$ for ADMM, within respective bounds $1.2\times10^{-7}$ and $2.3\times10^{-8}$.
Both nearly satisfy optimality, so differences from true coefficients reflect $\ell_1$ shrinkage and false positives rather than optimization error.

At ADMM limit 200, it stops with `IterationLimit` and a partial outcome.
The final iterate's KKT violation $6.2\times10^{-7}$ remains within residual-derived bound $1.3\times10^{-6}$.
Coefficients agree with FISTA to four decimal places, hiding nonconvergence in a casual table inspection; assess termination and KKT-violation columns.
For $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}=1.2$, zero is optimal; FISTA returns all zeros in 1 iteration with KKT violation 0.

## Penalty ρ and ADMM iterations

ADMM residual convergence is at best linear, with speed strongly dependent on $\rho$.
Larger $\rho$ penalizes differences between $x$ and $z$ more strongly, tending to reduce primal residuals but enlarge dual residual $\|s\|=\rho\|z-z_{\text{prev}}\|$; smaller $\rho$ reverses this.
At specified $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}$, the following cell solves ADMM at 17 values $\rho=10^{-2},10^{-1.75},\dots,10^{2}$ with iteration limit 3000.
The log–log plot shows iterations with a dashed reference for FISTA iterations on the same problem.
The table gives iterations, termination and KKT violations per $\rho$.

```rust
//|   ratio: {"type":"range","label":"λ / λmax [-]","min":0.02,"max":0.9,"step":0.02,"value":0.1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"strong","label":"λ/λmax = 0.9","description":"The iteration-minimizing ρ moves from 0.18 to 1, with minimum 31 iterations.","inputs":{"ratio":0.9}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/8/0","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/8/1","equals":287}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/4/1","equals":91}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/13/2","equals":"IterationLimit"}
//|   - case: strong
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/8/1","equals":31}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, NumericScalar, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{CompositeProblem, Fista, ProximalOptions, SmoothGradient};
let (loss, _) = lasso_data().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let n = loss.n;
let zero = vec![0.0; n];
let mut gradient = vec![0.0; n];
loss.gradient_into(&zero, &mut gradient)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lambda_max = gradient
    .iter()
    .fold(0.0, |m, &g| RealScalar::max(m, NumericScalar::abs(g)));
let lambda = ratio * lambda_max;
let lipschitz = loss.a.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>();
let limit = Budget::try_from(3000_usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fista = Fista::new(ProximalOptions::new(lipschitz).with_max_iterations(limit))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .minimize(&CompositeProblem::new(&loss, &Shrink(lambda)), &zero)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut points = Vec::new();
for k in -8..=8 {
    // ρ = 10^(k/4), k = −8, …, 8.
    let rho = RealScalar::powf(10.0, f64::from(k) / 4.0);
    let admm = solve_admm(&loss, lambda, rho, limit).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let kkt = kkt_violation(&loss, lambda, admm.z()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    points.push([rho, admm.iterations() as f64]);
    rows.push((rho, admm.iterations(), admm.termination().to_string(), kkt));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({
        "name": "ADMM iterations",
        "points": points,
        "style": { "showSymbol": true }
    })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Penalty ρ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Iterations [count]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{
            "kind": "line",
            "axis": "y",
            "value": fista.iterations(),
            "label": "FISTA iterations",
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }]
    })
);
let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "ρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Termination reason" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "KKT violation [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sweep" })
);
```

At default $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}=0.1$, iterations reach minimum 64 at $\rho\approx0.18$ and increase on either side.
$\rho=0.01$ requires 990 iterations and $\rho=10$ 2821; on log–log axes the sides approximate lines of slopes $-1$ and $+1$.
For $\rho\ge17.8$, the 3000 limit gives `IterationLimit`; KKT violations grow to $2.5\times10^{-6}$ at $\rho=17.8$ and $7.4\times10^{-3}$ at $\rho=100$.
ADMM converges in fewer than FISTA's 164 iterations only for $\rho$ from 0.1 to 0.56.
For $\lambda/\lambda_{\max}=0.9$, the minimum moves to 31 iterations at $\rho=1$; a good $\rho$ depends on $\lambda$.
`AdmmOptions::with_residual_balancing` automates this adjustment by enabling residual balancing, changing $\rho$ during iteration according to the residual ratio.

## Summary

FISTA gradient mapping $G$ and ADMM residuals $\|r\|$ and $\|s\|$ are not KKT violations themselves, but $(1+\sqrt n)G$ and $L\|r\|+\|s\|$ bound them above.
KKT violations evaluated at returned coefficients can be compared independently of solver reports with these bounds.
After termination `IterationLimit`, judge accuracy by KKT violation even when coefficients look close to converged solutions.
ADMM iterations vary by orders of magnitude with $\rho$, so interpret speed comparisons against FISTA at fixed $\rho$ as applying to that $\rho$.
FISTA iterations cost $O(mn)$, whereas ADMM iterations cost $O(n^2)$ after one Cholesky factorization; which is faster depends on $m$, $n$, $\lambda$ and $\rho$.
