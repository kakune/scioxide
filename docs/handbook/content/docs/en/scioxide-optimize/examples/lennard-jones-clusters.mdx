Consider atoms like noble gases, strongly repulsive at short distances and weakly attractive farther apart.
Represent total energy by the Lennard-Jones (LJ) potential, summing pair potentials at interatomic distances $r_{ij}$:

$$
E=4\varepsilon\sum_{i<j}\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r_{ij}}\right)^{12}-\left(\frac{\sigma}{r_{ij}}\right)^{6}\right]
$$


$\varepsilon$ is well depth and $\sigma$ the zero-potential distance; here energy and length units use $\varepsilon=\sigma=1$.
A pair's equilibrium separation is $2^{1/6}\sigma\approx1.1225\,\sigma$, with energy $-\varepsilon$.
An equilateral triangle of 3 atoms and regular tetrahedron of 4 put every pair at equilibrium, giving minimum energies $-3\varepsilon$ and $-6\varepsilon$.
With at least 5 atoms, not all pair distances can simultaneously be at equilibrium, producing many local minima.
Their number grows rapidly with atom count, so one local solve from one start often misses the lowest configuration.

## Local gradients and structure comparisons

For difference vector $d=r_i-r_j$ and $s=\|d\|^2$, pair energy is $4(s^{-6}-s^{-3})$.
From $\partial s/\partial r_i=2d$, the contribution to particle $i$'s gradient is $24(s^{-4}-2s^{-7})d$, with opposite sign for particle $j$.
Each evaluation loops over $N(N-1)/2$ pairs, costing $O(N^2)$.
There are $n=3N$ coordinate variables; dense BFGS stores a $n\times n$ inverse-Hessian approximation and costs $O(n^2)$ operations per iteration.

Translation and rotation preserve energy, so local minima form six-dimensional families rather than isolated points, leaving 6 Hessian zero eigenvalues.
Atom relabeling and rotation change coordinates without changing structure; compare energies, force magnitude $\|\nabla E\|$ and interatomic distances instead of coordinates themselves.
Here a contact is a pair separated by less than $1.35\,\sigma$, with contact count used as a structural indicator.
In structures found on this page, nearest-neighbor distances are $1.08$–$1.17\,\sigma$ and other pairs at least $1.77\,\sigma$, so this threshold counts nearest neighbors.
The $N=7$ minimum is a pentagonal bipyramid (point group $D_{5h}$) with 16 contacts; the $N=13$ minimum is an icosahedron ($I_h$) with 42 contacts: 12 center-to-surface and 30 surface-to-surface.

## The transformed basin-hopping landscape

Basin hopping walks landscape $\tilde E(x)=E(\mathrm{loc}(x))$, replacing configuration $x$ by the value reached after local descent.
$\tilde E$ becomes flat steps for individual local minima, removing barriers between steps.
Each trial randomly displaces the current configuration, relaxes by BFGS, and decides acceptance using relaxed energy.
Only the first trial relaxes the starting point itself.

Metropolis acceptance takes relaxed-energy increases $\Delta E>0$ with probability $\exp(-\Delta E/T)$, always accepting decreases.
Here $T$ has objective units, namely $\varepsilon$ in this example.
At $T=0$, basin hopping is monotone and accepts no uphill moves.
Displacements use `UniformBasinProposal`, independently moving coordinates uniformly within $\pm8\%$ of box width (here $\pm0.4\,\sigma$) and reflecting at box boundaries.
Each trial costs one BFGS solve, so equal trial counts can require different objective evaluations in proportion to local iterations.

$T$ is a search setting, not an estimate of cluster equilibrium temperature.
Acceptance compares local minima, so the generated sequence differs from the thermal distribution of vibrating atoms.

## Generalized annealing temperature

Dual annealing combines generalized simulated annealing with local optimization.
In coordinates $u\in[0,1)$ normalized by box widths, it draws displacements from a heavy-tailed visiting distribution (Tsallis $q$ distribution, $q_v=2.62$) and connects the box periodically to create new points.
Each visit evaluates the objective once; local optimization runs only at the initial point, points whose raw values beat the best value, and reheating points.

Starting from initial temperature $T_0$, temperature decreases with sweep index $k$ as

$$
T_k=T_0\,\frac{2^{q_v-1}-1}{(k+2)^{q_v-1}-1}
$$


A sweep has $2n$ visits: the first $n$ move all coordinates, and the remaining $n$ move one coordinate at a time.
Visiting-distribution scale is $T_k^{1/(3-q_v)}/\sqrt{3-q_v}$ (box width 1); when $T_k$ is around 1 or greater, displacements spread over the whole box.
Uphill acceptance follows the generalized Metropolis rule with $q_a=-5$.
For acceptance temperature $T_k/(k+1)$, reject if $(1-q_a)\Delta E\ge T_k/(k+1)$; otherwise accept with probability $[1-(1-q_a)\Delta E(k+1)/T_k]^{1/(1-q_a)}$.
When $T_k$ falls to $2\times10^{-5}T_0$ or below, reset temperature to $T_0$ and restart from a uniform point in the box (reheating).
For $q_v=2.62$ this ratio is reached at sweep $k\approx1250$; with $N=7$ this requires over 50000 visits, so this page's computation limits allow no reheating.

Thus one number $T_0$ sets both the normalized-coordinate visiting scale and acceptance temperature in objective units $\varepsilon$.
Defaults of `DualAnnealingOptions::new` are $T_0=5230$, $q_v=2.62$, $q_a=-5$ and reheating ratio $2\times10^{-5}$.
Acceptance temperature $T_k/(k+1)$ decreases with sweeps, serving a different role from basin hopping's constant $T$.
The following cell therefore gives separate inputs for basin-hopping $T$ and dual-annealing $T_0$.

## Search box and local solver

Constrain coordinates to $[-2.5,2.5]$, comparing both methods in the same finite box.
Both global methods require finite boxes and reject out-of-box starts.
BFGS does not accept boxed problems, so the local adapter removes the box from the supplied objective but retains box checks as domain test `is_in_domain`.
Line search rejects out-of-domain trial points, keeping BFGS iterates inside the box.
This adapter does not replace L-BFGS-B for optima on box boundaries.
The table therefore also reports structure clearance from box boundaries.

The start places atoms equally around a circle of radius $1\,\sigma$, slightly staggering $z$.
Both methods relax this point on their first trial; subsequent search causes their differences.

The following definition block is shared by both cells on this page.
`Cluster` defines energy and gradient, `Factory` and `Local` local BFGS for search, `REFERENCES` Cambridge Cluster Database minimum energies and point groups, `ring_start` starts, and `search_box` the search box.
References use six-decimal values from the [Cambridge Cluster Database LJ table](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/~wales/CCD/jon/structures/LJ/tables.html) ($N=3$–$13$); for $N\ge5$ these are numerical-search values.

```rust
//| id: optimize-lj-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "LJ cluster energy, local BFGS and known minimum energies"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
use scioxide_core::{Bounds, SciOxideError, RealScalar, SolveContext};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    Bfgs, BfgsWorkspace, BoundedObjective, LocalSolverFactory, ObjectiveFunction, OptimizeResult,
    SearchLocalSolver,
};

/// N-atom LJ cluster (ε = σ = 1). Coordinates are 3N entries ordered (x, y, z) per atom.
pub(crate) struct Cluster(pub(crate) usize);

impl Cluster {
    /// E = Σ_{i<j} 4(s⁻⁶ − s⁻³) and its gradient. s = |rᵢ − rⱼ|².
    pub(crate) fn value_gradient(&self, x: &[f64]) -> (f64, Vec<f64>) {
        let mut value = 0.0;
        let mut gradient = vec![0.0; x.len()];
        for i in 0..self.0 {
            for j in 0..i {
                let d: [f64; 3] = std::array::from_fn(|k| x[3 * i + k] - x[3 * j + k]);
                let inverse = 1.0 / d.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>();
                let sixth = inverse * inverse * inverse;
                value += 4.0 * (sixth * sixth - sixth);
                // ∂/∂rᵢ 4(s⁻⁶ − s⁻³) = 24(s⁻⁴ − 2s⁻⁷) d = 24 s⁻¹ s⁻³ (1 − 2 s⁻³) d.
                let coefficient = 24.0 * inverse * sixth * (1.0 - 2.0 * sixth);
                for k in 0..3 {
                    gradient[3 * i + k] += coefficient * d[k];
                    gradient[3 * j + k] -= coefficient * d[k];
                }
            }
        }
        (value, gradient)
    }
}

impl ObjectiveFunction<f64> for Cluster {
    fn dim(&self) -> usize {
        3 * self.0
    }
    fn eval(&self, x: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(self.value_gradient(x).0)
    }
    fn gradient_into(&self, x: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<bool, SciOxideError> {
        out.copy_from_slice(&self.value_gradient(x).1);
        Ok(true)
    }
    // Overlapping atoms make E undefined, so exclude them from the domain.
    fn is_in_domain(&self, x: &[f64]) -> Result<bool, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(x.iter().all(|v| v.is_finite()) && pairs(x).all(|(_, _, r)| r > 1e-6))
    }
}

/// Atom pairs (i, j) and distance rᵢⱼ [σ].
pub(crate) fn pairs(x: &[f64]) -> impl Iterator<Item = (usize, usize, f64)> + '_ {
    let n = x.len() / 3;
    (0..n).flat_map(move |i| {
        (0..i).map(move |j| {
            let d: [f64; 3] = std::array::from_fn(|k| x[3 * i + k] - x[3 * j + k]);
            let s = d.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>();
            (i, j, RealScalar::sqrt(s))
        })
    })
}

/// BFGS does not accept boxed problems; remove the box but retain box checks as domain checks.
struct Domain<'a, 'b>(&'a BoundedObjective<'b, f64>);

impl ObjectiveFunction<f64> for Domain<'_, '_> {
    fn dim(&self) -> usize {
        self.0.dim()
    }
    fn eval(&self, x: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        self.0.eval(x)
    }
    fn gradient_into(&self, x: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]) -> Result<bool, SciOxideError> {
        self.0.gradient_into(x, out)
    }
    fn is_in_domain(&self, x: &[f64]) -> Result<bool, SciOxideError> {
        self.0.is_in_domain(x)
    }
}

/// Pass default BFGS with reusable workspace to each search trial.
#[derive(Debug)]
pub(crate) struct Factory;

pub(crate) struct Local {
    solver: Bfgs<f64>,
    work: BfgsWorkspace<f64>,
}

impl LocalSolverFactory<f64> for Factory {
    type Solver = Local;
    fn prepare(&self, n: usize) -> Result<Local, SciOxideError> {
        let solver = Bfgs::default();
        let work = solver.workspace(n)?;
        Ok(Local { solver, work })
    }
}

impl SearchLocalSolver<f64> for Local {
    fn minimize_into(
        &mut self,
        problem: &BoundedObjective<'_, f64>,
        start: &[f64],
        _: u64,
        out: &mut OptimizeResult<f64>,
        scope: &mut SolveContext<'_, '_>,
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        self.solver
            .minimize_into(&Domain(problem), start, out, &mut self.work, scope)
    }
}

/// Cambridge Cluster Database minimum energies [ε] and point groups (N = 3, …, 13).
pub(crate) const REFERENCES: [(f64, &str); 11] = [
    (-3.0, "D3h"),
    (-6.0, "Td"),
    (-9.103852, "D3h"),
    (-12.712062, "Oh"),
    (-16.505384, "D5h"),
    (-19.821489, "Cs"),
    (-24.113360, "C2v"),
    (-28.422532, "C3v"),
    (-32.765970, "C2v"),
    (-37.967600, "C5v"),
    (-44.326801, "Ih"),
];

/// Start with N equally spaced atoms on a radius-1σ circle, staggering z by 0.2 i/N σ.
pub(crate) fn ring_start(n: usize) -> Vec<f64> {
    (0..n)
        .flat_map(|i| {
            let angle = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * i as f64 / n as f64;
            let height = 0.2 * i as f64 / n as f64;
            [RealScalar::cos(angle), RealScalar::sin(angle), height]
        })
        .collect()
}

/// Search box restricting every coordinate to [−2.5σ, 2.5σ].
pub(crate) fn search_box(n: usize) -> Result<Bounds<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    Bounds::new(vec![(Some(-2.5), Some(2.5)); 3 * n])
}
```

At defaults ($N=7$, 10 basin-hopping trials, $T=0.5\,\varepsilon$, $T_0=5230$, 1000 visits, seed 7), basin hopping reaches $-16.505384\,\varepsilon$ in 2707 objective evaluations, matching the literature table to 6 decimal places.
Returned coordinates have $\|\nabla E\|$ of $6.7\times10^{-8}\,\varepsilon/\sigma$, the pentagonal bipyramid's 16 contacts, minimum separation $1.1152\,\sigma$ slightly below pair equilibrium $1.1225\,\sigma$, and box clearance $1.25\,\sigma$.
Minimum separation is below pair equilibrium because attractions from surrounding atoms compress the pair.
Even after 1179 evaluations, dual annealing retains relaxed-start $-14.568061\,\varepsilon$ (14 contacts).
Only the initial trial has a local solution; no remaining visit's raw energy beats the best value.

The quantities compared by each method explain this difference.
Basin hopping descends to local minima every trial, comparing local minima with each other.
Dual-annealing visits compare raw energies; arbitrary box points tend to have some pair too close, producing large positive energies.
At $T_0=5230$, the visiting scale far exceeds box width, making displacements nearly uniform.
Even increasing to 5000 visits and 5179 evaluations, nearly twice basin hopping's evaluations, retains the same $-14.568061\,\varepsilon$.

At $T_0=1$, cooling concentrates visits near the current point; a second local solve within 1000 visits lowers energy to $-15.593211\,\varepsilon$.
At $T_0=0.2$ and below, 1000 visits still retain the relaxed-start value.
No setting reaches the LJ7 minimum, so walking local minima by basin hopping is more efficient for this landscape and computation limit.
This observation applies to this problem and computation limit and does not establish general inferiority of dual annealing.

At 5 basin-hopping trials, the default seed stops at another local minimum $-15.593211\,\varepsilon$ (15 contacts).
For $N=13$ with 5 trials, seed 7 reaches icosahedral $-44.326801\,\varepsilon$ (42 contacts), while seed 1 retains $-41.471980\,\varepsilon$ (39 contacts).
Temperature $T$ of 0 rejects uphill moves, reducing default acceptances from 9 of 10 trials to 4.
Increasing $T$ admits higher local minima more readily, broadening search while reducing residence near low minima.
For $N=3$, both methods reach an equilateral triangle with analytic value $-3\varepsilon$.

## Success rates across seeds

Whether one search reaches the minimum depends on its random seed.
The following cell repeats basin hopping alone over seeds $1,2,\dots,S$, counting the fraction whose best energy is within $10^{-4}\,\varepsilon$ of literature as the success rate.
For success rate $\hat p=k/S$, use the 95% Wilson interval, which stays within $[0,1]$ even for few trials or proportions near 0 or 1:

$$
\frac{\hat p+\dfrac{z^2}{2S}\pm z\sqrt{\dfrac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{S}+\dfrac{z^2}{4S^2}}}{1+\dfrac{z^2}{S}},\qquad z=1.96
$$


Treating searches across seeds as independent trials gives binomial success counts.
Inputs are atom count, trials, temperature $T$ and seed count $S$; start and box match the previous cell.
The plot gives best candidate energy by seed, with a dashed literature reference.
The table gives successes, success rate and confidence interval, and mean objective evaluations per search.

```rust
//| id: optimize-lennard-jones-seeds
//| ref: optimize-lennard-jones-seeds
//| title: "Vary seeds to read basin-hopping success rates with confidence intervals"
//| run: button
//| timeoutMs: 120000
//| uses: [optimize-lj-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   atoms: {"type":"range","label":"Atoms N [count]","min":3,"max":13,"step":1,"value":13}
//|   attempts: {"type":"range","label":"Trials [count]","min":5,"max":30,"step":5,"value":5}
//|   temperature: {"type":"range","label":"Metropolis temperature T [ε]","min":0,"max":2,"step":0.1,"value":0.5}
//|   seeds: {"type":"range","label":"Seeds S [count]","min":5,"max":40,"step":5,"value":20}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"more-hops","label":"15 trials","description":"Successes increase to 17 of 20, with 95% confidence interval [0.64, 0.95].","inputs":{"attempts":15}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rate","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"rate","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.2582,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rate","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.6579,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: more-hops
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rate","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":17}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, EvaluationRole, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    BasinHopping, BasinHoppingOptions, BoundedObjective, GlobalSearchOptions,
    UniformBasinProposal,
};
let n = atoms as usize;
let f = Cluster(n);
let bounds = search_box(n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = BoundedObjective::new(&f, &bounds).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (reference, _) = REFERENCES[n - 3];
let start = ring_start(n);
let hops = Budget::try_from(attempts as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let proposal = UniformBasinProposal::new(0.08).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut found, mut successes, mut evaluations) = (vec![], 0_usize, 0_usize);
for seed in 1..=seeds as u64 {
    let options = GlobalSearchOptions::default()
        .with_seed(seed)
        .with_starts(hops);
    let search = BasinHopping::new(
        BasinHoppingOptions::new(options, temperature).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
        proposal.clone(),
        Factory,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let report = search
        .minimize(&problem, &start)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let best = report
        .best()
        .ok_or(format!("Seed {seed}: no finite candidate"))?;
    // Independent check: decide success using energy recomputed at returned coordinates.
    let energy = f.value_gradient(best.x()).0;
    successes += usize::from((energy - reference).abs() < 1e-4);
    evaluations += report
        .counts()
        .total()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .provider(EvaluationRole::Objective);
    found.push([seed as f64, energy]);
}
// 95% Wilson confidence interval.
let s = seeds as f64;
let (p, z) = (successes as f64 / s, 1.96);
let center = (p + z * z / (2.0 * s)) / (1.0 + z * z / s);
let half = z * RealScalar::sqrt(p * (1.0 - p) / s + z * z / (4.0 * s * s)) / (1.0 + z * z / s);
// With zero successes the lower bound is exactly 0; set only tiny negative roundoff to 0.
let (lower, upper) = ((center - half).max(0.0), center + half);
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Best candidate", "points": found })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Seed [-]",
        "yLabel": "Best candidate energy [ε]",
        "annotations": [{
            "kind": "line",
            "axis": "y",
            "value": reference,
            "label": "Literature value",
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }]
    })
);
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Seeds S [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Successes [count]", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Success rate [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "95% CI lower bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
        { "key": "4", "label": "95% CI upper bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Mean objective evaluations [count]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 0 } }
    ]),
    vec![(
        seeds as usize,
        successes,
        p,
        lower,
        upper,
        evaluations as f64 / s
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rate" })
);
```

At defaults ($N=13$, 5 trials, $T=0.5\,\varepsilon$, $S=20$), 9 of 20 seeds reach the minimum, giving success rate 0.45 and 95% interval $[0.26,0.66]$.
All 11 unsuccessful seeds' best candidates line up at $-41.471980\,\varepsilon$ in the plot.
This is the relaxed-start minimum, showing failure to escape its step within 5 trials.
With 15 trials, successes increase to 17 of 20, rate 0.85 and interval $[0.64,0.95]$; the two intervals overlap slightly over 0.64–0.66.
Mean evaluations per search rise from 2264 to 5952, so select settings using both success rates and evaluation counts.
For $S=20$, interval widths are 0.3–0.4; comparing small success-rate differences requires more seeds.

## Connecting to normal modes

Displayed coordinates use units $\sigma=1$ and energies $\varepsilon=1$.

Check sufficiently small $\|\nabla E\|$ before studying normal modes; a nonstationary configuration's Hessian does not describe equilibrium vibrations.
An equilibrium Hessian has 6 translation/rotation zero eigenvalues; positivity of the remaining $3N-6$ establishes a local minimum.

## Summary

Basin-hopping $T$ is Metropolis temperature in objective units $\varepsilon$; dual-annealing $T_0$ is initial temperature setting both visiting breadth (normalized coordinates) and acceptance temperature ($\varepsilon$ units).
Compare methods at equal objective evaluations rather than trials, reading best energy, $\|\nabla E\|$, contacts and literature differences in one table.
Termination `SearchBudgetCompleted` means only that scheduled trials finished, not that a global minimum was found.
Minimum attainment depends on seeds; assess settings by success rates and confidence intervals over several seeds rather than one success.
