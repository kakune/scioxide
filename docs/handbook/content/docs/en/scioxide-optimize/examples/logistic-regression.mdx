Logistic regression is a standard model predicting event probabilities from predictors for binary outcomes such as response/no response or pass/fail.
Using dose response, this page derives objective and gradient from negative Bernoulli log-likelihood, uses `scioxide_core::softplus` and `scioxide_core::sigmoid` for overflow-safe calculations, and finds coefficients by BFGS.
Check the solution against symmetry-derived intercept $\beta_0=0$, assessing parameter determination through the analytic Hessian.
Completely dose-separated classes have no finite maximum-likelihood estimate; [Complete separation and ridge regularization paths](../logistic-separation-path/) examines this case.

## From linear predictors to probabilities

Predict the probability of binary response $Y\in\{0,1\}$ from predictors $x$ such as drug dose, temperature or component dimensions.
The linear predictor

$$
z=\beta_0+\beta_1x
$$

can take any real value, so is not itself a probability.
Passing it through the logistic function

$$
p(x)=\sigma(z)=\frac1{1+e^{-z}}
$$

gives $0<p<1$.
Taking logarithms of odds $p/(1-p)$ gives

$$
\log\frac{p(x)}{1-p(x)}=\beta_0+\beta_1x
$$

so $\beta_1$ is the log-odds change per unit increase in $x$, and $e^{\beta_1}$ the corresponding odds ratio.
$\beta_0$ is dimensionless; $\beta_1$ has reciprocal units of $x$.

## Deriving the objective from likelihood

At dose $x_i$, perform $n_i$ trials with success fraction $y_i$.
The success count is $n_iy_i$; up to constants, negative log-likelihood is

$$
L(\beta)
=-\sum_i n_i
\{y_i\log p_i+(1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)\}
$$


This equals cross-entropy between observed fractions and predicted probabilities, summed with weights $n_i$.
Substituting $p_i=\sigma(z_i)$ makes one group's contribution

$$
n_i\bigl\{\log(1+e^{z_i})-y_iz_i\bigr\}
$$


Large $z_i$, however, overflows $e^{z_i}$.
Computing softplus as

$$
\log(1+e^z)
=\max(z,0)+\log\bigl(1+e^{-|z|}\bigr)
$$

keeps exponential arguments nonpositive.
The expressions are mathematically equal, but floating-point calculation needs this transformation to avoid overflow.
`scioxide_core::softplus` implements this form, and `scioxide_core::sigmoid` computes only $e^{-|z|}$; both have accuracy within 16 ulp for real arguments.
Both return `NonFinite` errors for non-finite inputs.

Add a ridge penalty on the slope and minimize

$$
\Phi(\beta)
=L(\beta)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\beta_1^2
$$


The gradient is

$$
\nabla\Phi
=\sum_i n_i(p_i-y_i)
\begin{pmatrix}1\\x_i\end{pmatrix}
+\lambda
\begin{pmatrix}0\\\beta_1\end{pmatrix},
$$

and the Hessian is

$$
H=\sum_i n_ip_i(1-p_i)
\begin{pmatrix}1\\x_i\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&x_i\end{pmatrix}
+\lambda
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
$$


Finite $\beta$ gives $p_i(1-p_i)>0$; with at least two distinct doses the first term is positive definite and $\Phi$ strictly convex.
A minimizer, if it exists, is therefore unique.
Existence depends on data; completely dose-separated classes have no minimizer at $\lambda=0$.
The intercept is unpenalized, so when all groups share one class it has no finite optimum even at $\lambda>0$.

## Symmetric design and intercept checks

Doses in this example are symmetric about $x\mapsto-x$, and success fractions reverse as $y\mapsto1-y$.
Paired groups at $x$ and $-x$ satisfy $\Phi(\beta_0,\beta_1)=\Phi(-\beta_0,\beta_1)$ because $\mathrm{softplus}(-u)=\mathrm{softplus}(u)-u$.
Strict convexity makes the minimizer unique, so its intercept must be $\beta_0=0$, giving $p(0)=1/2$.
Use this as an independent check of the BFGS solution.

One objective-and-gradient evaluation costs $O(m)$, proportional to dose-group count $m$.
BFGS keeps a $2\times2$ inverse-Hessian approximation, updating it in $O(n^2)$ per iteration (here $n=2$).
Stopping compares Euclidean gradient norm $\lVert\nabla\Phi\rVert_2$ with default tolerance $\sqrt\varepsilon\,(1+s)$.
Here $\varepsilon$ is double-precision machine epsilon and $s=\max\bigl(1,|\Phi|/\max(1,\lVert\beta\rVert_\infty)\bigr)$ the objective scale.

## Computing coefficients and the Hessian

Use 8 dose groups $x=\pm0.4,\pm1,\pm2,\pm3$, 4 negative and 4 positive.
Normalized dose $x$ and $\lambda$ are dimensionless.
Mix opposite-class fraction $f$ only into the 2 groups nearest the boundary ($x=\pm0.4$), giving success fraction $(0,0,0,f)$ for $x<0$ groups and $(1-f,1,1,1)$ for $x>0$ groups.
At $f=0$, classes are completely separated.
Equal group weights $n=20$ yield aggregated-binomial likelihood when weight times fraction is integer.
Inputs also allow continuous fractions, so not every setting represents actual integer success counts.

Inputs are opposite-class fraction $f$, ridge-coefficient exponent $q=\log_{10}\lambda$, and initial intercept and slope.
Outputs are coefficient and objective/curvature tables.
Hessian eigenvalues are independent values from the analytic formula above, without BFGS's internal approximation.
The final outcome follows `OptimizeResult::is_success()`.
This cell exports coefficients to the next probability-curve cell.

```rust
//| id: optimize-logistic-regression
//| ref: optimize-logistic-regression
//| title: "Vary logistic-regression overlap, ridge and initial values"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| exports: [intercept, slope, groups]
//| inputs:
//|   overlap_fraction: {"type":"range","label":"Opposite-class fraction in boundary groups f [-]","description":"Move from complete separation to overlapping data.","min":0,"max":0.45,"step":0.025,"value":0.1}
//|   logistic_regularization_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Ridge-coefficient exponent q = log₁₀ λ [-]","description":"Vary slope-shrinkage strength logarithmically.","min":-6,"max":1,"step":0.5,"value":-2}
//|   logistic_initial_intercept: {"type":"range","label":"Initial intercept β₀ [-]","description":"Change the starting iterate.","min":-5,"max":5,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//|   logistic_initial_slope: {"type":"range","label":"Initial slope β₁ [-]","description":"Change the initial probability-curve direction.","min":-6,"max":6,"step":0.5,"value":-1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"complete-separation","label":"Opposite-class fraction 0 (complete separation)","description":"At the same λ = 10⁻², slope increases from 5.645 to 12.18 and minimum Hessian eigenvalue decreases from 0.70 to 0.058, while condition number barely changes to 5.16.","inputs":{"overlap_fraction":0}}
//|   - {"id":"strong-ridge","label":"Ridge exponent q = 1 (λ = 10)","description":"The penalty dominates likelihood, shrinking slope to 1.553; minimum Hessian eigenvalue becomes 16.9 and condition number 1.61.","inputs":{"logistic_regularization_exponent":1}}
//|   - {"id":"far-start","label":"Initial values (β₀, β₁) = (5, 6)","description":"Changing the start gives the same coefficients within 10⁻⁸; only iteration count changes.","inputs":{"logistic_initial_intercept":5,"logistic_initial_slope":6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":-1e-6,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":5.64507,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"curvature","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":13.1515,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"curvature","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.159334,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"curvature","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.70089,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"curvature","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":5.0919,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"curvature","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: complete-separation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":12.18004,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"curvature","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.05847,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"curvature","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":5.1627,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: strong-ridge
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.55313,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"curvature","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":16.872,"atol":1e-2}}
//|   - case: far-start
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":5.64507,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar, sigmoid, softplus};
use scioxide_optimize::{Bfgs, ObjectiveFn, OptimizeTermination};

// Compute σ(z) and softplus(z) = log(1 + e^z) with overflow-safe scioxide_core functions.
// 8 groups, each with weight n = 20. Mix opposite classes only in the 2 boundary groups (x = ±0.4).
let doses = [-3.0_f64, -2.0, -1.0, -0.4, 0.4, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0];
let f = overlap_fraction;
let props = [0.0_f64, 0.0, 0.0, f, 1.0 - f, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0];
let n = 20.0_f64;
let lambda = RealScalar::powf(10.0_f64, logistic_regularization_exponent);
// L(β) = Σ n{softplus(zᵢ) − yᵢzᵢ}, Φ = L + (λ/2)β₁², ∇Φ = Σ n(pᵢ − yᵢ)(1, xᵢ) + (0, λβ₁).
let nll = move |b: &[f64]| -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    let mut total = 0.0;
    for (&x, &y) in doses.iter().zip(&props) {
        let z = b[0] + b[1] * x;
        total += n * (softplus(z)? - y * z);
    }
    Ok(total)
};
let objective = ObjectiveFn::new(2, move |b: &[f64]| Ok(nll(b)? + 0.5 * lambda * b[1] * b[1]))
    .with_gradient(move |b: &[f64], g: &mut [f64]| {
        g[0] = 0.0;
        g[1] = lambda * b[1];
        for (&x, &y) in doses.iter().zip(&props) {
            let d = n * (sigmoid(b[0] + b[1] * x)? - y);
            g[0] += d;
            g[1] += d * x;
        }
        Ok(())
    });
let start = [logistic_initial_intercept, logistic_initial_slope];
let result = Bfgs::<f64>::default()
    .minimize(&objective, &start)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (b0, b1) = (result.x()[0], result.x()[1]);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let digits6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let coefficient_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Intercept β₀ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Slope β₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Odds ratio e^β₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    coefficient_columns,
    vec![(b0, b1, RealScalar::exp(b1))],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "coefficients" })
);
// Independent curvature diagnostic: eigenvalues of analytic Hessian H = Σ n p(1−p)(1, x)(1, x)ᵀ + diag(0, λ).
let (mut h00, mut h01, mut h11) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, lambda);
for &x in &doses {
    let px = sigmoid(b0 + b1 * x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let w = n * px * (1.0 - px);
    h00 += w;
    h01 += w * x;
    h11 += w * x * x;
}
let disc = RealScalar::hypot(h00 - h11, 2.0 * h01);
let (largest, smallest) = (0.5 * (h00 + h11 + disc), 0.5 * (h00 + h11 - disc));
let gradient = result.grad().ok_or("BFGS returned no gradient")?;
// Euclidean norm ‖∇Φ‖₂, as used for stopping.
let gradient_norm = RealScalar::hypot(gradient[0], gradient[1]);
let curvature_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Negative log-likelihood L [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Penalty (λ/2)β₁² [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Minimum H eigenvalue [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "H condition number [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "‖∇Φ‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Objective evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    curvature_columns,
    vec![(
        nll(result.x()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
        0.5 * lambda * b1 * b1,
        smallest,
        largest / smallest,
        gradient_norm,
        result.iterations(),
        result.fun_evals(),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "curvature" })
);
export!(intercept, &b0);
export!(slope, &b1);
let groups: Vec<[f64; 2]> = doses.iter().zip(&props).map(|(&x, &y)| [x, y]).collect();
export!(groups, &groups);
let termination = result.termination();
let outcome = if result.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Satisfies the gradient stopping test")
} else if matches!(termination, OptimizeTermination::LineSearchFailed(_)) {
    Outcome::failed("Line search could not find an acceptable step")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped before satisfying the test and returned the last accepted point")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(termination.to_string())
        .diagnostic("‖∇Φ‖₂", gradient_norm, None)
        .diagnostic("Iterations", result.iterations() as f64, Some("count"))
);
```

At defaults ($f=0.1$, $\lambda=10^{-2}$), BFGS reaches `GradientSmall` in 18 iterations, returning $\beta_0=1.2\times10^{-9}$, $\beta_1=5.645$ and odds ratio 283.
The intercept agrees with symmetry-derived $\beta_0=0$ within gradient tolerance, giving $p(0)=0.5$.
Negative log-likelihood is 13.15 and penalty 0.159; the analytic Hessian has minimum eigenvalue 0.70 and condition number 5.09.
Initial intercept $\pm5$ and slope $\pm6$ still produce the same coefficients within $10^{-8}$; only iterations change (7–18).

Opposite-class fraction 0 (complete separation), at the same $\lambda=10^{-2}$, gives $\beta_1=12.18$ and odds ratio increasing to $1.9\times10^5$.
Many groups' $p(1-p)$ approach 0, lowering minimum Hessian eigenvalue to 0.058.
Condition number barely changes to 5.16 because both eigenvalues shrink together, losing over an order of magnitude of curvature (parameter determination).
Condition numbers alone miss this weakening of parameter determination.
Conversely, ridge exponent 1 ($\lambda=10$) makes the penalty dominate likelihood, shrinking slope to 1.553 and raising minimum eigenvalue to 16.9.

## Probability curves and representative probabilities

Large odds ratios need not imply large probability differences.
The dose-probability slope

$$
\frac{dp}{dx}=\beta_1p(1-p)
$$

peaks at $\beta_1/4$ when $p=1/2$ and is small near probability 0 or 1.
The following cell receives coefficients, overlays probability curve $p(x)$ with group success fractions, and tabulates $p(x)$ and $dp/dx$ at $x=-1,0,1$.
It has no inputs of its own and redraws according to the previous cell's inputs.

```rust

//| ref: optimize-logistic-probability-curve
//| title: "Fitted probability curve and probability slopes at representative points"

    serde_json::from_value(fit["groups"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
```

Plot symbols show group success fractions, not individual 0/1 outcomes.
Weight 20 per group makes data terms 20 times as influential as unit weights.
When success counts are integer, interpret each group as 20 trials.
The curve smoothly connects fractions by summarizing data through a two-parameter probability model, rather than interpolation.

At defaults, $p(0)=0.5$; the slope at $x=0$ is $\beta_1/4=1.41$ but only 0.020 at $x=\pm1$.
Reading table values $p(x)$ and $dp/dx$ alongside coefficients shows effects on the actual probability scale.

## Predictive performance and preprocessing

Smaller training negative log-likelihood need not improve performance on unseen data.
Choose $\lambda$ by cross-validation or external data, and choose classification thresholds separately based on misclassification losses.
With imbalanced classes, inspect log loss, probability calibration, precision and recall as well as accuracy.

Shifting predictors changes intercept meaning; rescaling changes slope and penalty meaning.
Share standardization rules between training and prediction, storing coefficients, preprocessing, $\lambda$ and termination diagnostics together.

## Summary

Logistic objectives and gradients follow from Bernoulli likelihood; use `scioxide_core::softplus` and `scioxide_core::sigmoid` for overflow-safe calculations.
Symmetric designs have zero intercept, independently checking BFGS solutions.
Interpret coefficients through representative probabilities and $dp/dx$ as well as odds ratios, and inspect minimum Hessian eigenvalue magnitude alongside condition number.
Near complete separation, even coefficients stopped with `GradientSmall` strongly depend on $\lambda$; examine regularization paths for finite limits before reporting them.
