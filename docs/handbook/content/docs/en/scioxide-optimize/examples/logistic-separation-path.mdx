Completely predictor-separated logistic classes may appear well fitted but have no finite maximum-likelihood estimate.
Penalized optimization can stop normally at a small gradient, but its coefficients are merely values determined by penalty strength.
Using the same 8 dose groups as [Estimate binary responses by logistic regression](../logistic-regression/), this page computes slope paths by BFGS while sweeping ridge coefficient $\lambda$ from $10^{-6}$ to $10$.
Compare asymptotic formulas to confirm that overlapping data produce finite plateaus, whereas complete separation grows nearly proportionally to $\log(1/\lambda)$ without stopping.

## Why complete separation removes maximum-likelihood estimates

Suppose all negative observations have $x<0$ and all positive observations $x>0$.
This is called complete separation (Albert and Anderson, 1984).
Keeping $\beta_0=0$ while taking $\beta_1\to+\infty$ sends all predicted probabilities toward the observed-class endpoints; negative log-likelihood $L$ decreases monotonically toward 0.
$L$ is positive for every finite $\beta$, so no finite point attains infimum 0 and no maximum-likelihood estimate exists.
Error-free classification does not imply precise parameter estimation.

Ridge penalty $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\beta_1^2$ charges large slopes, keeping $\beta_1$ finite when $\lambda>0$.
This is a model assumption against extreme slopes, rather than merely a device for stable iteration.
Changing $\lambda$ changes probability calibration and coefficient interpretation.

Any opposite-class mixture in boundary groups sends their log-likelihood toward $-\infty$ as $\beta_1\to\infty$, giving a finite minimum even at $\lambda=0$.
The $\lambda\to0$ path then converges to the unpenalized maximum-likelihood estimate and plateaus.

## Slope asymptotics under complete separation

Under complete separation, the zero-gradient condition for component $\beta_1$ at the optimum is

$$
\lambda\beta_1=\sum_i n_i(y_i-p_i)x_i
$$

whose right-hand side can be approximated for large $\beta_1$.
Symmetric design gives $\beta_0=0$; errors for $x>0$ groups are $1-\sigma(\beta_1x)\approx e^{-\beta_1x}$, with equal magnitude for $x<0$ groups.
The nearest boundary groups $x=\pm x_m$ (here $x_m=0.4$) dominate the right side; even the next groups at $x=\pm1$ contribute only $e^{-(1-x_m)\beta_1}/x_m$ times the boundary contribution.
With group weight $n$, this gives

$$
\lambda\beta_1\approx2nx_me^{-x_m\beta_1}
$$


Taking logarithms gives $x_m\beta_1+\log\beta_1=\log(2nx_m/\lambda)$.
Reducing $\lambda$ by a decade increases the left side by $\log10$; the slope increment is therefore smaller than $\log10/x_m\approx5.76$ by the increase in $\log\beta_1$ divided by $x_m$.
Slope has no finite limit as $\lambda\to0$ and keeps growing nearly proportionally to $\log(1/\lambda)$.
The per-decade increment depends on boundary positions $x_m$, scarcely on outer-group positions.

## Computing the path

Solve BFGS independently at 29 points $\lambda=10^{q}$, $q=-6,-5.75,\dots,1$, all from initial values $(0,1)$.
Inputs are opposite-class fraction $f$ in boundary groups and trial count $n$ per group.

Outputs are slope and minimum analytic-Hessian eigenvalue paths, plus a table every fourth point.
Both plots share $\lambda$ as horizontal axis, linking zoom and cursors.
For complete separation ($f=0$), solve the asymptotic equation by bisection and overlay dashed results, also tabulating them.
With overlap the asymptotics do not apply; the table displays “Overlap”.
Exclude unsuccessful points from the path and return an error containing their $\lambda$.

```rust
//| id: optimize-logistic-regularization-path
//| ref: optimize-logistic-regularization-path
//| title: "Regularization paths versus data overlap and trial counts"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   path_overlap_fraction: {"type":"range","label":"Opposite-class fraction in boundary groups f [-]","description":"Change whether complete separation occurs.","min":0,"max":0.45,"step":0.025,"value":0.1}
//|   path_trials_per_group: {"type":"range","label":"Trials per group n [count]","description":"Change relative likelihood information and penalty strength.","min":2,"max":100,"step":2,"value":20}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"complete-separation","label":"Opposite-class fraction 0 (complete separation)","description":"Each decade decrease in λ increases slope by about 5 (12.18 at λ = 10⁻², 32.75 at 10⁻⁶); at λ ≤ 10⁻² it agrees with asymptotics within 0.2%.","inputs":{"path_overlap_fraction":0}}
//|   - {"id":"many-trials","label":"Trials per group n = 100","description":"Likelihood becomes 5 times stronger; at the same λ = 10⁻² slope rises from 5.645 to 5.711, approaching unpenalized 5.728.","inputs":{"path_trials_per_group":100}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":5.72836,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":5.64507,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/7/2","approx":{"value":1.55313,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|   - case: complete-separation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":32.748,"atol":1e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":32.748,"atol":2e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":12.1800,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: many-trials
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"path","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":5.71125,"atol":1e-4}}
use scioxide_core::{RealScalar, sigmoid, softplus};
use scioxide_optimize::{Bfgs, ObjectiveFn};

let doses = [-3.0_f64, -2.0, -1.0, -0.4, 0.4, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0];
let f = path_overlap_fraction;
let props = [0.0_f64, 0.0, 0.0, f, 1.0 - f, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0];
let n = path_trials_per_group;
// Solve complete-separation asymptotic equation λβ = 2n·x_m·e^{−x_m β} (x_m = 0.4) by bisection (independent check).
let asymptote = |lambda: f64| {
    let (mut lo, mut hi) = (0.0_f64, 200.0_f64);
    for _ in 0..100 {
        let mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi);
        if lambda * mid > 2.0 * n * 0.4 * RealScalar::exp(-0.4 * mid) {
            hi = mid;
        } else {
            lo = mid;
        }
    }
    0.5 * (lo + hi)
};
let (mut slope, mut reference, mut eigen, mut rows) =
    (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
// 29 points λ = 10^{−6} … 10^{1}. Solve independently from initial values (0, 1).
for k in 0..=28 {
    let lambda = RealScalar::powf(10.0_f64, -6.0 + 0.25 * k as f64);
    let objective = ObjectiveFn::new(2, move |b: &[f64]| {
        let mut total = 0.5 * lambda * b[1] * b[1];
        for (&x, &y) in doses.iter().zip(&props) {
            let z = b[0] + b[1] * x;
            total += n * (softplus(z)? - y * z);
        }
        Ok(total)
    })
    .with_gradient(move |b: &[f64], g: &mut [f64]| {
        g[0] = 0.0;
        g[1] = lambda * b[1];
        for (&x, &y) in doses.iter().zip(&props) {
            let d = n * (sigmoid(b[0] + b[1] * x)? - y);
            g[0] += d;
            g[1] += d * x;
        }
        Ok(())
    });
    let r = Bfgs::<f64>::default()
        .minimize(&objective, &[0.0, 1.0])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if !r.is_success() {
        return Err(format!(
            "Unsuccessful at λ = {lambda:.1e}: {}",
            r.termination()
        ));
    }
    let (b0, b1) = (r.x()[0], r.x()[1]);
    // 2×2 eigenvalues of analytic Hessian H = Σ n p(1−p)(1, x)(1, x)ᵀ + diag(0, λ) (independent curvature diagnostic).
    let (mut h00, mut h01, mut h11) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, lambda);
    for &x in &doses {
        let p = sigmoid(b0 + b1 * x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let w = n * p * (1.0 - p);
        h00 += w;
        h01 += w * x;
        h11 += w * x * x;
    }
    let disc = RealScalar::hypot(h00 - h11, 2.0 * h01);
    let (largest, smallest) = (0.5 * (h00 + h11 + disc), 0.5 * (h00 + h11 - disc));
    let predicted = (f == 0.0).then(|| asymptote(lambda));
    slope.push([lambda, b1]);
    eigen.push([lambda, smallest]);
    if let Some(value) = predicted {
        reference.push([lambda, value]);
    }
    if k % 4 == 0 {
        let termination = r.termination().to_string();
        rows.push((
            lambda,
            b0,
            b1,
            predicted,
            smallest,
            largest / smallest,
            termination,
        ));
    }
}
let mut series = vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "BFGS slope β₁", "points": slope })];
if !reference.is_empty() {
    series.push(serde_json::json!({
        "name": "Asymptotic λβ = 2n·x_m·e^(−x_m β)",
        "points": reference,
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "logistic-lambda" },
        "xLabel": "Ridge coefficient λ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Slope β₁ [-]",
        "xType": "log"
    })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Minimum H eigenvalue", "points": eigen })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "logistic-lambda" },
        "xLabel": "Ridge coefficient λ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Smallest Hessian eigenvalue [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let digits5 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 5 });
let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "λ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Intercept β₀ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Slope β₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Asymptotic β₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits5, "nullText": "Overlap" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Minimum H eigenvalue [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "H condition number [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination status" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "path" })
);
```

At default overlap $f=0.1$, reducing $\lambda$ to $10^{-3}$ or below gives a slope plateau at 5.72–5.73, indicating a finite unpenalized maximum-likelihood estimate.
For complete separation ($f=0$), each decade decrease in $\lambda$ adds about 5 to slope (12.18 at $\lambda=10^{-2}$, 32.75 at $10^{-6}$), without plateauing.
The increment from $\lambda=10^{-5}$ to $10^{-6}$ is 5.31, nearly matching 5.32 from subtracting $\log(32.75/27.43)/x_m=0.44$ from 5.76.
The dashed asymptotics match BFGS slope within 0.2% for $\lambda\le10^{-2}$.
At $\lambda\ge1$ slope is small and contributions $e^{-\beta_1}$ from groups at $x=\pm1$ and beyond are not negligible, so asymptotics fail.
Every $\lambda$ terminates with `GradientSmall`.
Successful termination means only that each regularized problem at $\lambda$ was solved, not that limit $\lambda\to0$ has a finite solution.

For complete separation, the minimum-eigenvalue plot shows curvature shrinking continually as $\lambda$ decreases.
With overlap, minimum eigenvalues plateau at finite values just as slope does.

Increasing trial count $n$ strengthens only likelihood proportionally, making the same numerical $\lambda$ relatively weaker.
At $n=100$, slope for $\lambda=10^{-2}$ rises from 5.645 to 5.711, approaching unpenalized 5.728.
Implementations differ in whether penalties use $\lambda$ or $\lambda/n$; named parameters alone cannot reproduce results without the objective formula.

Each path point is solved independently from the same initial values.
Larger problems solve faster using the previous solution as the next start, but failure at one point can propagate to neighboring points.
Even then, inspect each point's gradient and termination status.

## Summary

Completely separated data have no finite point attaining negative log-likelihood infimum 0, so no maximum-likelihood estimate exists.
With ridge penalties BFGS stops at `GradientSmall` for every $\lambda$, but slope follows $\lambda\beta_1\approx2nx_me^{-x_m\beta_1}$ and keeps growing nearly proportionally to $\log(1/\lambda)$.
Whether decreasing-$\lambda$ paths plateau helps determine whether finite maximum-likelihood estimates exist.
Without a plateau, do not report coefficients as estimates; state $\lambda$ as a model assumption or report data separation itself.
