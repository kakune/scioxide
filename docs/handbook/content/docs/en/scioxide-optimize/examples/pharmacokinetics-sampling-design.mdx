[Fit a drug concentration decay rate](../pharmacokinetics/) estimated initial concentration $C_0\,[\mathrm{mg/L}]$ and elimination rate $k\,[\mathrm h^{-1}]$ in intravenous one-compartment model $C(t)=C_0e^{-kt}$ by nonlinear least squares.
Small objectives and precise data-driven determination of each parameter are different properties.
This page studies how the latest sampling time changes $k$ uncertainty through Jacobian column correlation, a profile objective eliminating $C_0$, and linearized standard errors.
Agreement between profile and linearized intervals provides an independent check.

## Which times inform which parameters

Residual $r_i=C_0e^{-kt_i}-y_i$ has Jacobian row $i$ equal to $[e^{-kt_i},\,-C_0t_ie^{-kt_i}]$.
At $t=0$, the row is $[1,0]$, so that observation directly informs only $C_0$.
At very late times both entries shrink as $e^{-kt}$, providing little information under additive errors.
Differentiating the magnitude $C_0te^{-kt}$ of sensitivity to $k$ with respect to $t$ gives $C_0e^{-kt}(1-kt)$, so sensitivity peaks at $t=1/k$ ($3.1\,\mathrm h$ for $k=0.32\,\mathrm h^{-1}$).
This explains combining early observations, observations near the time constant and tail observations.

Observations confined to early times give $C_0e^{-kt}\approx C_0-C_0kt$ for $kt\ll1$, so data approximately determine intercept $C_0$ and slope $C_0k$.
The two columns of $J$ are then nearly proportional, with the smaller eigenvalue of $J^\mathsf TJ$ approaching 0.
The closer the absolute column correlation

$$
\rho=\frac{\sum_iJ_{i1}J_{i2}}{\sqrt{\sum_iJ_{i1}^2\sum_iJ_{i2}^2}}
$$

is to 1, the harder separate determination of $C_0$ and $k$ becomes.

Condition number $\kappa_2(J)$ cannot establish this alone.
The second column of $J$ depends on units of $k$; expressing $k$ in $\mathrm{min}^{-1}$ multiplies it by 60 and changes $\kappa_2(J)$.
Equalizing column lengths makes the condition number depend only on column correlation:

$$
\kappa_2=\sqrt{\frac{1+|\rho|}{1-|\rho|}}
$$

the eigenvalues of column-normalized $J^\mathsf TJ$ are proportional to $1\pm|\rho|$.

## Profile objectives and 95% intervals

For fixed $k$, objective $\Phi=\frac12\sum_ir_i^2$ is quadratic in $C_0$.
Setting $\partial\Phi/\partial C_0=0$ gives

$$
C_0^\star(k)
=\frac{\sum_i y_i e^{-kt_i}}
       {\sum_i e^{-2kt_i}}
$$

eliminating $C_0$.
A function fixing one parameter and optimizing all others is called a profile objective.

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{prof}}(k)
=\Phi\bigl(C_0^\star(k),k\bigr)
$$

This is a one-variable curve along the two-parameter valley floor, with the same minimum as the two-parameter least-squares solution.

For normal observation errors $N(0,\sigma^2)$ and known $\sigma$, negative log-likelihood is $\Phi/\sigma^2$ plus a constant.
At true $k$, likelihood-ratio statistic $\Lambda(k)=2\{\Phi_{\mathrm{prof}}(k)-\Phi_{\min}\}/\sigma^2$ approximately follows a one-degree-of-freedom $\chi^2$ distribution, so

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{prof}}(k)\le\Phi_{\min}+\frac{3.84}{2}\sigma^2
$$

defines an approximate 95% confidence interval in $k$ (3.84 is the 95th percentile of $\chi^2_1$).
Linearizing at the estimate approximates $\Phi_{\mathrm{prof}}$ by parabola

$$
\Phi_{\min}+\frac{(k-k^\star)^2}{2\,[(J^\mathsf TJ)^{-1}]_{kk}}
$$

giving interval $k^\star\pm1.96\,\sigma\sqrt{[(J^\mathsf TJ)^{-1}]_{kk}}$ (the Wald interval).
Close agreement means linearization holds over the interval width.
[Identifiability and statistical fitting](../../algorithms/identifiability-and-fitting/) discusses the general case.

## Observation endpoint and measurement error

Use the previous known parameters $C_0=12\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ and $k=0.32\,\mathrm h^{-1}$ and synthetic noise $s\sin(1.91i+0.4)$.
Candidate times are 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10 h; use only times no later than the endpoint.
Shortening the endpoint reduces observations, changing both sample size and time range.
`ScalarMinimizer` (Brent by default) minimizes $\Phi_{\mathrm{prof}}$ on interval $[0.08, 0.72]\,\mathrm h^{-1}$.

The three inputs are noise scale $s$, observation endpoint and measurement-error standard deviation $\sigma$ used for intervals.
Supply $\sigma$ as measurement precision rather than estimating it from data (the interpretation for comparing observation designs).
Outputs are:

- Plot of $\Phi_{\mathrm{prof}}(k)$ and linearized parabola (dashed), marking interval threshold, $k^\star$ and true $k$ with lines
- Valley-floor plot $C_0^\star(k)$ ($k$ axis linked to the upper plot)
- Table of minimizer, objective, residual standard error $\sqrt{2\Phi_{\min}/(m-2)}$, evaluations and termination
- Table of profile interval, Wald width, column correlation and $\kappa_2(J)$
- Outcome based on `ScalarMinimizer` termination

Read interval endpoints from a 1601-point grid (spacing $4\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm h^{-1}$); sides reaching the search boundary display “Outside search interval”.

```rust
//| id: optimize-pharmacokinetics-profile
//| ref: optimize-pharmacokinetics-profile
//| title: "Measure the 95% k interval with a profile objective eliminating C₀"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   profile_noise: {"type":"range","label":"Observation noise scale s [mg/L]","description":"Vary amplitude of deterministic deviations s·sin(1.91i + 0.4).","min":0,"max":0.8,"step":0.05,"value":0.2}
//|   profile_horizon: {"type":"range","label":"Observation endpoint [h]","description":"Use candidate sampling times no later than this time.","min":2,"max":10,"step":0.5,"value":8}
//|   profile_sigma: {"type":"range","label":"Measurement-error standard deviation σ [mg/L]","description":"Set threshold Φmin + 1.92σ² and Wald-interval width.","min":0.1,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"noise-free","label":"Zero noise (truth recovery)","description":"The minimum matches k = 0.32 h⁻¹ and C₀ = 12 mg/L, with nearly the same interval width as noisy defaults.","inputs":{"profile_noise":0}}
//|   - {"id":"short-horizon","label":"Observation endpoint 2 h","description":"Sampling falls to 5 points, column correlation strengthens and interval width increases about 1.7-fold.","inputs":{"profile_horizon":2}}
//|   - {"id":"coarse-assay","label":"σ = 1 mg/L","description":"Intervals widen nearly proportionally to σ, with profile intervals deviating asymmetrically from parabolic intervals.","inputs":{"profile_sigma":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"minimum","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"minimum","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.321935,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"minimum","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":12.0577,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"minimum","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.0340,"atol":8e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.034227,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":-0.65466,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/4/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: noise-free
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"minimum","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.32,"atol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"minimum","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":12,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.034185,"atol":1e-5}}
//|   - case: short-horizon
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"minimum","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.0576,"atol":8e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":-0.72278,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":16.80,"atol":0.01}}
//|   - case: coarse-assay
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":0.2436,"atol":8e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.4164,"atol":8e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.17113,"atol":1e-4}}
use scioxide_core::{Interval, RealScalar};
use scioxide_optimize::{ScalarMinimizer, ScalarMinimumStart};

let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
// Use only fixed candidate times no later than the observation endpoint.
let candidates = [0.0_f64, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 6.0, 8.0, 10.0];
let times: Vec<f64> = candidates
    .into_iter()
    .filter(|&t| t <= profile_horizon + 1.0e-12)
    .collect();
let observed: Vec<f64> = times
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, &t)| {
        12.0 * RealScalar::exp(-0.32 * t)
            + profile_noise * RealScalar::sin(1.91 * i as f64 + 0.4)
    })
    .collect();
// For fixed k, the model is linear in C₀; eliminate it using C₀*(k) = Σ yᵢ e^{−k tᵢ} / Σ e^{−2k tᵢ}.
let profile = |k: f64| {
    let (mut num, mut den) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for (&t, &y) in times.iter().zip(&observed) {
        let e = RealScalar::exp(-k * t);
        num += y * e;
        den += e * e;
    }
    let c0 = num / den;
    let cost = 0.5
        * times
            .iter()
            .zip(&observed)
            .map(|(&t, &y)| {
                let r = c0 * RealScalar::exp(-k * t) - y;
                r * r
            })
            .sum::<f64>();
    (c0, cost)
};
// Minimize the one-variable profile objective within the interval by Brent (default).
let start = ScalarMinimumStart::Bounded(Interval::new(0.08, 0.72).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
let best = ScalarMinimizer::default()
    .minimize(&|k: f64| Ok(profile(k).1), start)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (k_star, (c0_star, cost_star)) = (best.x(), profile(best.x()));
// Independent check: compute column correlation, κ₂(J) and [(JᵀJ)⁻¹]ₖₖ by hand from 2×2 JᵀJ at the minimum.
let (mut a, mut b, mut d) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
for &t in &times {
    let j0 = RealScalar::exp(-k_star * t);
    let j1 = -c0_star * t * j0;
    a += j0 * j0;
    b += j0 * j1;
    d += j1 * j1;
}
let disc = RealScalar::sqrt((a - d) * (a - d) + 4.0 * b * b);
let kappa = RealScalar::sqrt((a + d + disc) / (a + d - disc));
let variance_k = a / (a * d - b * b);
// Grid range of k satisfying Φprof ≤ Φmin + 1.92σ² (half the χ²₁ 95th percentile 3.84).
let threshold = cost_star + 0.5 * 3.841_458_820_694_124 * profile_sigma * profile_sigma;
let grid: Vec<(f64, f64, f64)> = (0..=1600)
    .map(|i| {
        let k = 0.08 + 0.64 * i as f64 / 1600.0;
        let (c0, cost) = profile(k);
        (k, c0, cost)
    })
    .collect();
let first = grid
    .iter()
    .position(|g| g.2 <= threshold)
    .ok_or("No grid point in the interval")?;
let last = grid
    .iter()
    .rposition(|g| g.2 <= threshold)
    .ok_or("No grid point in the interval")?;
// If a side reaches the search boundary, its interval endpoint is undetermined; use None.
let lower = (first > 0).then(|| grid[first].0);
let upper = (last < grid.len() - 1).then(|| grid[last].0);
let profile_points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = grid.iter().map(|g| [g.0, g.2]).collect();
let parabola: Vec<[f64; 2]> = grid
    .iter()
    .map(|g| {
        let dk = g.0 - k_star;
        [g.0, cost_star + dk * dk / (2.0 * variance_k)]
    })
    .collect();
let valley_points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = grid.iter().map(|g| [g.0, g.1]).collect();
let y_max = 4.0 * (threshold - cost_star) + cost_star;
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Φprof(k)", "points": profile_points }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Linearized parabola",
            "points": parabola,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "pk-rate" },
        "xLabel": "Fixed elimination rate k [h⁻¹]",
        "yLabel": "Profile objective Φprof(k) [(mg/L)²]",
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": y_max,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": threshold, "label": "Φmin + 1.92σ²" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": k_star, "label": "Minimizer k*" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": 0.32, "label": "True k" }
        ]
    })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Valley floor C₀*(k)", "points": valley_points })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "pk-rate" },
        "xLabel": "Fixed elimination rate k [h⁻¹]",
        "yLabel": "Optimal initial concentration C₀*(k) [mg/L]",
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 12.0, "label": "True C₀ = 12" }]
    })
);
let m = times.len();
let minimum_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Observations m [points]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "k* [h⁻¹]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "C₀*(k*) [mg/L]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Φmin [(mg/L)²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Residual standard error [mg/L]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    minimum_columns,
    vec![(
        m,
        k_star,
        c0_star,
        cost_star,
        RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 * cost_star / (m - 2) as f64),
        best.fun_evals(),
        best.termination().to_string(),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "minimum" })
);
let interval_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Interval lower endpoint [h⁻¹]", "type": "number", "format": sig4, "nullText": "Outside search interval" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Interval upper endpoint [h⁻¹]", "type": "number", "format": sig4, "nullText": "Outside search interval" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Profile width [h⁻¹]", "type": "number", "format": sig4, "nullText": "Undefined" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Wald-interval width [h⁻¹]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Column correlation ρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "κ₂(J) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    interval_columns,
    vec![(
        lower,
        upper,
        lower.zip(upper).map(|(l, u)| u - l),
        2.0 * 1.959_963_984_540_054 * profile_sigma * RealScalar::sqrt(variance_k),
        b / RealScalar::sqrt(a * d),
        kappa,
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "interval" })
);
let termination = best.termination();
let outcome = if best.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Brent satisfies its interval and objective tests")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("One-variable minimization stopped before satisfying its tests")
        .explanation("Treat the minimizer and interval as estimates and review the evaluation limit.")
};
let outcome = outcome
    .status(termination.to_string())
    .diagnostic("Evaluations", best.fun_evals() as f64, None)
    .diagnostic("Φmin", cost_star, Some("(mg/L)²"));
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

At defaults (9 observations, noise scale 0.2, $\sigma=0.2\,\mathrm{mg/L}$), `ScalarMinimizer` reaches `Converged` in 18 evaluations, returning $k^\star=0.32194\,\mathrm h^{-1}$, $C_0^\star=12.058\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ and $\Phi_{\min}=0.0771\,(\mathrm{mg/L})^2$.
The profile 95% interval is $[0.3052, 0.3392]\,\mathrm h^{-1}$ (width 0.0340), containing true $k=0.32$.
Wald width 0.0342 agrees with profile width within grid spacing $4\times10^{-4}$.
Agreement of independent calculations shows linearization holds over this design's interval width.

With zero noise, the minimum matches truth $k=0.32$ and $C_0=12$ to around $10^{-14}$, with width 0.0336.
Even noise scale 0.8 gives width 0.0348, so noise magnitude scarcely changes width.
Threshold $1.92\sigma^2$ and $J$ depend on supplied $\sigma$ and observation times; noise only shifts valley location and bottom height $\Phi_{\min}$.
Check this assumption through residual standard error $\sqrt{2\Phi_{\min}/(m-2)}$.
At defaults it is 0.148 mg/L, comparable to supplied $\sigma=0.2\,\mathrm{mg/L}$.
At noise scale 0.8 it becomes 0.594 mg/L; assumption $\sigma=0.2$ then disagrees with data and its interval is too narrow.

At endpoint 2 h, 5 observations remain, column correlation strengthens from $-0.655$ to $-0.723$, and width grows about 1.7-fold to 0.0576 (Wald 0.0574).
Meanwhile $\kappa_2(J)$ decreases from 22.6 to 16.8.
The second column $-C_0te^{-kt}$ is smaller at short times, so condition number alone misleadingly suggests better identifiability.
For column correlation $-0.655$, the equal-column-length condition number is $\sqrt{1.655/0.345}=2.19$; most of $\kappa_2(J)=22.6$ reflects units $\mathrm{mg/L}$ versus $\mathrm h^{-1}$.

With $\sigma=1\,\mathrm{mg/L}$, the interval is $[0.2436, 0.4164]$ (width 0.1728), and Wald width 0.1711; both grow nearly proportionally to $\sigma$.
The profile interval is asymmetric about $k^\star$, extending 0.078 downward and 0.094 upward, and shifts above symmetric Wald interval $[0.236, 0.408]$.
Wider intervals make exponential curvature within the interval nonnegligible.
The upper plot then shows solid $\Phi_{\mathrm{prof}}$ diverging from the dashed parabola.

The lower plot's valley floor $C_0^\star(k)$ increases with $k$.
This curve shows negative column correlation: larger $k$ paired with larger $C_0$ explains observations about equally well.

## Using this for observation design

Wald width $2\times1.96\,\sigma\sqrt{[(J^\mathsf TJ)^{-1}]_{kk}}$ depends only on observation times, parameter values and $\sigma$, without directly using observations $y_i$.
Anticipated parameter values therefore allow design comparison before sampling (called locally optimal design).
Extending the endpoint from 8 to 10 h adds one observation but shrinks width only 2%, from 0.0340 to 0.0332.
At 10 h concentration is about $0.49\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ and sensitivity $C_0te^{-kt}$ to $k$ is about one third of that near $t=1/k$.
Extending 2 h to 8 h shrinks width from 0.0576 to 0.0340, so observations through 2–3 times time constant $1/k\approx3.1\,\mathrm h$ mainly determine $k$ precision.

Intervals here approximate uncertainty under a correct model, independent normal errors and known $\sigma$.
If $\sigma$ is also estimated, adjust thresholds using $t$ or $F$ distributions with degrees of freedom $m-2$; inspect time-ordered residuals if model error is suspected.
Construct intervals without an assumed error distribution by resampling (bootstrap).

## Summary

Compare sampling-time designs through $k$ interval widths rather than small objectives.
For known measurement precision $\sigma$, the range where the profile objective eliminating $C_0$ is at most $\Phi_{\min}+1.92\sigma^2$ gives a 95% interval; agreement with linearized Wald intervals supports linearization.
Shortening the endpoint from 8 to 2 h widens intervals about 1.7-fold and strengthens column correlation, but decreases $\kappa_2(J)$; do not judge identifiability from condition number alone.
Before using intervals, check residual standard error is comparable to assumed $\sigma$.
Wald widths need no observed values, allowing design comparisons before sampling using anticipated parameters.
