Pharmacokinetics (PK) estimates drug elimination rates from blood concentration over time, supporting dosage-interval and dose design.
In a one-compartment model after an intravenous bolus, concentration decays exponentially according to a first-order linear differential equation.
This page derives the mass-balance model and simultaneously estimates initial concentration $C_0$ and elimination rate constant $k$ by Gauss–Newton and Levenberg–Marquardt nonlinear least squares.
Truth recovery from known-parameter observations provides an independent check; termination and residual plots indicate how much to trust the fit.

## From administered dose to observed concentration

Assume an instantaneous intravenous dose distributes into one well-mixed compartment and is removed at a constant fraction per unit time.
Mass balance for concentration $C(t)$ is

$$
\frac{dC}{dt}=-kC,\qquad C(0)=C_0
$$


Separating variables gives

$$
\frac{dC}{C}=-k\,dt
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\log C(t)-\log C_0=-kt
$$

hence

$$
C(t;C_0,k)=C_0e^{-kt}
$$


Concentration $C_0$ has units $\mathrm{mg/L}$; elimination rate $k$ has units $\mathrm{h}^{-1}$.
Dose $D\,[\mathrm{mg}]$ and distribution volume $V\,[\mathrm L]$ satisfy $C_0=D/V$.
This page estimates $C_0$ directly without using dose, so does not estimate $V$ (if $D$ is known, obtain it from $V=D/C_0$).

The half-life is

$$
t_{1/2}=\frac{\log2}{k}
$$

so $k=0.32\,\mathrm{h}^{-1}$ gives about $2.17\,\mathrm h$.
This transformation expresses $k$ errors on the model's time scale.

## From observation errors to least squares

Suppose observations $y_i$ occur at times $t_i$ $(i=1,\dots,m)$.
Assume independent additive normal errors

$$
y_i=C_0e^{-kt_i}+\varepsilon_i,
\qquad \varepsilon_i\sim N(0,\sigma^2)
$$

so negative log-likelihood, up to constants, is proportional to residual sum of squares.
Define residuals and objective

$$
r_i(C_0,k)=C_0e^{-kt_i}-y_i,\qquad
\Phi(C_0,k)=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^m r_i^2
$$

and minimize $\Phi$.
For squared loss, `LeastSquaresResult::cost()` also equals this $\frac12\|r\|_2^2$, rather than the sum of squares itself.

Residual Jacobian row $i$ is

$$
J_{i,:}
=\left[
\frac{\partial r_i}{\partial C_0},
\frac{\partial r_i}{\partial k}
\right]
=\left[
e^{-kt_i},
-C_0t_i e^{-kt_i}
\right]
$$


Minimizing linearization $r(\theta+\Delta)\approx r(\theta)+J\Delta$ gives the Gauss–Newton equation

$$
J^\mathsf TJ\,\Delta=-J^\mathsf Tr
$$

with $\theta=(C_0,k)$.
`LeastSquaresGlobalization::gauss_newton()` backtracks along this step direction to find an Armijo-acceptable length (up to 40 trials per iteration).
No acceptable backtracking trial gives termination `Stagnation`.

Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) restricts the trusted linearization to radius $\Delta_k$; its step, with diagonal scale $D$ and damping $\lambda\ge0$, satisfies

$$
(J^\mathsf TJ+\lambda D^\mathsf TD)\Delta=-J^\mathsf Tr
$$


`LeastSquaresGlobalization::levenberg_marquardt()` (default) follows Moré, finding $\lambda$ with $\|D\Delta\|$ within 10% of the radius, and accepting actual-to-predicted reduction ratios at least $10^{-4}$.
For small $\lambda$, the step approximates a Gauss–Newton step; for large values, it approaches scaled steepest descent.
Both avoid normal equations, factoring the dense Jacobian with column-pivoted QR.
With 2 parameters, main work per iteration is proportional to observation count $m$.
See [Nonlinear least squares](../../algorithms/nonlinear-least-squares/) for derivation and radius updates.

## Initial values and noise

Observe the curve with known parameters $C_0=12\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ and $k=0.32\,\mathrm h^{-1}$ at 17 equally spaced times over 0–8 h, adding deterministic synthetic noise $s\sin(1.91i+0.4)$ ($i=0,\dots,16$ is observation index, $s$ noise scale).
Solve the same data with Gauss–Newton and LM from identical initial values.
Absolute and relative gradient tolerances are both $10^{-8}$ (test threshold $2\times10^{-8}$), with step tolerance $10^{-14}$.

Inputs are both initial values and noise scale.
Outputs are:

- Plot of LM fit, true curve (dashed) and observations (symbols)
- Time-ordered LM residual plot $r_i$ (time axis linked to the upper plot)
- Table of both methods' estimates, objectives, iterations, evaluations and termination
- Table of truth differences, inter-method solution differences and LM condition estimate
- Outcome based on LM termination

LM condition estimates appear only when requested with `LeastSquaresOptions::with_condition_estimation(Estimation::Estimate)` (default `Estimation::Skip` makes `condition_estimate()` return `None`).

```rust
//| ref: optimize-pharmacokinetics
//| title: "Fit two-parameter exponential decay with Gauss–Newton and LM"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   initial_concentration: {"type":"range","label":"Starting value for initial concentration [mg/L]","description":"Change C₀ at the start of local iteration.","min":5,"max":20,"step":0.5,"value":9}
//|   initial_rate: {"type":"range","label":"Starting elimination rate constant [h⁻¹]","description":"Change k at the start of local iteration.","min":0.05,"max":0.8,"step":0.025,"value":0.15}
//|   concentration_noise: {"type":"range","label":"Observation noise scale s [mg/L]","description":"Vary amplitude of deterministic deviations s·sin(1.91i + 0.4).","min":0,"max":0.8,"step":0.05,"value":0.2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"noise-free","label":"Zero noise (truth recovery)","description":"Observations lie exactly on the model; both methods recover C₀ = 12 and k = 0.32 within 4×10⁻¹¹ in 4 iterations.","inputs":{"concentration_noise":0}}
//|   - {"id":"heavy-noise","label":"Noise scale 0.8 mg/L","description":"Gauss–Newton stops with Stagnation and LM with StepSmall. Their points agree; RMSE increases to 0.543 mg/L and |ΔC₀| to 0.25 mg/L.","inputs":{"concentration_noise":0.8}}
//|   - {"id":"fast-start","label":"Initial elimination rate 0.8 h⁻¹","description":"Gauss–Newton stops with Stagnation; LM reaches GradientSmall in 8 iterations. The same solution's condition estimate changes to 6.08.","inputs":{"initial_rate":0.8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":12.0621,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.322227,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":0.13577,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":4,"max":7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/8","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/8","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.06209,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":1.272,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/4/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: noise-free
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":12,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.32,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-9}}
//|   - case: heavy-noise
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/8","equals":"Stagnation"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/8","equals":"StepSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":12.2497,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":0.54307,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/4/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: fast-start
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/8","equals":"Stagnation"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/8","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":6.08,"atol":0.05}}
use scioxide_core::{GradientTolerance, RealScalar, StepTolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::{Estimation, Matrix};
use scioxide_optimize::{
    LeastSquares, LeastSquaresFn, LeastSquaresGlobalization, LeastSquaresOptions,
};

let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
// 17 points at 0.5 h intervals over 0–8 h.
let times: Vec<f64> = (0..=16).map(|i| 0.5 * i as f64).collect();
// Add deterministic synthetic noise to the known-parameter curve C₀ = 12 mg/L, k = 0.32 h⁻¹.
let truth = |t: f64| 12.0 * RealScalar::exp(-0.32 * t);
let observed: Vec<f64> = times
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, &t)| truth(t) + concentration_noise * RealScalar::sin(1.91 * i as f64 + 0.4))
    .collect();
let (t_r, y_r, t_j) = (times.clone(), observed.clone(), times.clone());
// Residual rᵢ = C₀ e^{−k tᵢ} − yᵢ and analytic Jacobian row [e^{−k tᵢ}, −C₀ tᵢ e^{−k tᵢ}].
let problem = LeastSquaresFn::new(2, times.len(), move |p: &[f64], r: &mut [f64]| {
    for ((ri, &t), &y) in r.iter_mut().zip(&t_r).zip(&y_r) {
        *ri = p[0] * RealScalar::exp(-p[1] * t) - y;
    }
    Ok(())
})
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
.with_jacobian(move |p: &[f64], j: &mut Matrix<f64>| {
    for (row, &t) in j.as_mut_slice().chunks_exact_mut(2).zip(&t_j) {
        let e = RealScalar::exp(-p[1] * t);
        row[0] = e;
        row[1] = -p[0] * t * e;
    }
    Ok(())
});
// Supply gradient and step tolerances; request a final linearization condition estimate (default Skip).
let options = LeastSquaresOptions::default()
    .with_gradient_tolerance(
        GradientTolerance::new(1.0e-8_f64, 1.0e-8).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    )
    .with_step_tolerance(StepTolerance::new(1.0e-14_f64, 1.0e-14).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
    .with_condition_estimation(Estimation::Estimate);
let initial = [initial_concentration, initial_rate];
let solve = |globalization| {
    LeastSquares::new(options.with_globalization(globalization))?.solve(&problem, &initial)
};
let gn = solve(LeastSquaresGlobalization::gauss_newton()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lm = solve(LeastSquaresGlobalization::levenberg_marquardt()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let curve = |c0: f64, k: f64| {
    (0..=160)
        .map(|i| {
            let t = 0.05 * i as f64;
            [t, c0 * RealScalar::exp(-k * t)]
        })
        .collect::<Vec<_>>()
};
let observed_points: Vec<[f64; 2]> =
    times.iter().zip(&observed).map(|(&t, &y)| [t, y]).collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "link": { "group": "pk-time" },
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "xLabel": "Time t [h]",
    "yLabel": "Concentration C(t) / observations y [mg/L]",
    "layers": [
        { "id": "lm", "kind": "line", "name": "LM fit", "points": curve(lm.x()[0], lm.x()[1]) },
        {
            "id": "truth",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "True curve 12e^{−0.32t}",
            "points": curve(12.0, 0.32),
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        },
        { "id": "observed", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Observed concentration yᵢ", "points": observed_points }
    ]
}));
// Plot raw residuals rᵢ at the LM solution in time order.
let residual_points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = times
    .iter()
    .zip(lm.residual())
    .map(|(&t, &r)| [t, r])
    .collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "link": { "group": "pk-time" },
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "xLabel": "Time t [h]",
    "yLabel": "Residual rᵢ [mg/L]",
    "yMin": -1.0,
    "yMax": 1.0,
    "layers": [
        { "id": "residual", "kind": "scatter", "name": "LM solution residuals rᵢ", "points": residual_points }
    ],
    "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.0, "label": "r = 0" }]
}));
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, r) in [("Gauss–Newton", &gn), ("Levenberg–Marquardt", &lm)] {
    let cost = r.cost().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        name,
        r.x()[0],
        r.x()[1],
        std::f64::consts::LN_2 / r.x()[1],
        cost,
        (2.0 * cost / times.len() as f64).sqrt(),
        r.iterations(),
        r.fun_evals(),
        r.termination().to_string(),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Estimated C₀ [mg/L]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Estimated k [h⁻¹]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Half-life [h]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Objective ½‖r‖² [(mg/L)²]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Residual RMSE [mg/L]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Residual evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "fits" })
);
// Independent check: truth and inter-method differences. Condition estimate is 1/rcond₁ of the column-scaled linearization.
let reported = lm
    .condition_estimate()
    .ok_or("No condition estimate reported")?
    .reciprocal()
    .value();
let checks = vec![
    ("Truth difference |ΔC₀| [mg/L]", (lm.x()[0] - 12.0).abs()),
    ("Truth difference |Δk| [h⁻¹]", (lm.x()[1] - 0.32).abs()),
    (
        "Inter-method difference max|θ_GN − θ_LM| [-]",
        (gn.x()[0] - lm.x()[0])
            .abs()
            .max((gn.x()[1] - lm.x()[1]).abs()),
    ),
    ("LM condition estimate 1/rcond₁ [-]", 1.0 / reported),
];
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Check" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    checks,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
let termination = lm.termination();
let outcome = if lm.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("LM gradient norm fell below tolerance")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("LM stopped before the gradient test passed, returning the last accepted point")
        .explanation("Treat the point as an estimate; compare its gradient or solutions from other starts.")
};
let outcome = outcome
    .status(termination.to_string())
    .diagnostic("Iterations", lm.iterations() as f64, None)
    .diagnostic(
        "Objective ½‖r‖²",
        lm.cost().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
        Some("(mg/L)²"),
    );
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

At defaults, both methods reach `GradientSmall` (successful gradient norm within tolerance) in 5 iterations, returning $C_0=12.062\,\mathrm{mg/L}$, $k=0.32223\,\mathrm h^{-1}$ (half-life $2.151\,\mathrm h$), objective $0.1567\,(\mathrm{mg/L})^2$ and residual RMSE $0.136\,\mathrm{mg/L}$.
Truth differences $|\Delta C_0|=0.062\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ and $|\Delta k|=2.2\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm h^{-1}$ arise from synthetic noise, not iteration error.
With zero noise, both methods agree with truth within $4\times10^{-11}$ in 4 iterations.
This independently checks the model, Jacobian and algorithm together.

The upper plot shows the fit as solid, truth dashed and 17 observations as symbols.
Where the fitted line misses symbols, differences reflect noise, not interpolation errors.
Lower-plot residuals scatter above and below zero within $\pm0.2\,\mathrm{mg/L}$; equal signs persist for at most 2 points.
They neither widen over time nor favor one sign, showing no systematic deviations indicating model error.
Noise scale 0.8 quadruples residuals without changing their sign pattern.
Truth differences grow to $|\Delta C_0|=0.25\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ and $|\Delta k|=8.9\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm h^{-1}$, with RMSE $0.543\,\mathrm{mg/L}$.
Compare datasets using per-point RMSE or normalization by known $\sigma$, rather than raw objectives.

## Reading termination and condition estimates

Read termination separately from estimates.
At noise scale 0.8 or initial $k=0.8\,\mathrm h^{-1}$, Gauss–Newton exhausts acceptable backtracking trials and stops with `Stagnation`.
Residual evaluations rise to 46 and 57, showing all 40 backtracking trials used in the last iteration.
The returned point differs from LM by at most $5\times10^{-9}$, but `Stagnation` is unsuccessful and means the last accepted point was returned.
LM reaches `GradientSmall` in 8 iterations from $k=0.8\,\mathrm h^{-1}$, but at noise scale 0.8 stops after 11 with `StepSmall` (step within tolerance).
`StepSmall` also establishes no first-order optimality, so the cell outcome is partial.
Check gradient magnitude or compare solutions from other starts or methods.

LM condition estimate $1/\mathrm{rcond}_1$ estimates the 1-norm condition number of a column-scaled linearization factored by column-pivoted QR.
At defaults it is 1.27.
The solver normalizes columns by the maximum column norm observed in iterations so far.
Consequently the same solution can have different estimates along different iteration paths.
Initial $k=0.8\,\mathrm h^{-1}$ gives 6.08 despite the same solution; initial $C_0=20\,\mathrm{mg/L}$ gives 8.73.
This measures numerical solvability of the linearization; assess data-driven parameter precision separately ([Blood sampling times and parameter identifiability](../pharmacokinetics-sampling-design/)).

## Checking model errors

If one-compartment first-order elimination is wrong, systematic residual curves remain despite more observations.
Oral dosing with absorption phases, two-compartment distribution and concentration-dependent elimination require other equations.
Plot residuals in time order, inspecting sign runs, concentration-dependent variance and outliers.

For concentration-proportional errors, fitting $\log y$ or weighted least squares with $r_i/\sigma_i$ is natural.
Logarithms cannot handle zero or negative observations and change the error model.
Do not choose logarithms solely because they linearize the exponential.

Other checks include multiple starts, analytic-Jacobian/finite-difference comparisons, leaving out observations individually, changing time units and obtaining corresponding $k$, and saving termination and conditioning indicators.
Together these provide stronger evidence than visual curve agreement.

## Summary

For one-compartment fitting, first check truth recovery with zero-noise observations to rule out model, Jacobian and algorithm errors.
Treat only `GradientSmall` as success; compare Gauss–Newton `Stagnation` and LM `StepSmall` returned points with other methods or starts before using them.
Time-ordered residuals distinguish noise-explained deviations from systematic model errors.
LM condition estimates are not computed by default and depend on iteration paths when requested, so do not use them to judge parameter-estimation precision.
If distribution volume is needed, supply dose $D$ and compute $V=D/C_0$.
