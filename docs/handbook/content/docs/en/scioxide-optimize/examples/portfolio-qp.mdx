The Markowitz (1952) mean–variance model combines higher expected return and smaller return variance in one objective.
This page solves the model for low-volatility and growth assets as a convex quadratic program (QP) using `QpSolver`.
Check allocations against an analytic solution after eliminating the equality, and locate them on the efficient frontier.
[Sensitivity of optimal weights](../portfolio-sensitivity/) examines allocation changes caused by errors in estimated correlations or expected returns.

## Mean–variance model

Allocate fractions $w_1,w_2$ to low-volatility and growth assets, with

$$
w_1+w_2=1,\qquad 0\le w_1,w_2\le1
$$


Invest everything, allowing neither short selling (negative fractions) nor borrowing (fractions above 1).
For one-period return vector $R$, mean $\mu=\operatorname E[R]$ and covariance $\Sigma$, portfolio return $R_p=w^\mathsf TR$ has

$$
\operatorname E[R_p]=\mu^\mathsf Tw,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(R_p)=w^\mathsf T\Sigma w
$$


Expected return alone favors investing everything in the asset with largest $\mu_i$.
The simplest compromise for a volatility-averse investor is

$$
\min_w
\left\{
\frac{\lambda}{2}w^\mathsf T\Sigma w-\mu^\mathsf Tw
\right\},
\qquad \lambda>0
$$


$\lambda$ is risk aversion; larger values penalize variance more strongly.
The first term has squared-return units and the second return units, so $\lambda$ has reciprocal-return units and depends on period and return units.
Mixing percentages and decimals changes the $\lambda$ representing the same allocation by a factor of 100.

The low-volatility asset has $\mu_1=0.03$ and $\sigma_1=0.08$; the growth asset has $\mu_2=0.09$ (default) and $\sigma_2=0.20$.
These are hypothetical illustrative values; returns are annual rates expressed as decimals (0.03 means 3% per year), with standard deviations in the same annual-rate units.
Use the same $\mu_1$ and $\sigma_1$ as asset 1 in [Efficient frontiers of mean–variance models](../efficient-frontier/).

SciOxide `QpProblem` minimizes

$$
\frac12w^\mathsf TQw+c^\mathsf Tw
$$

so pass $Q=\lambda\Sigma$ and $c=-\mu$.
Supply the sum equality as one row of `LinearConstraints` and $0\le w_i\le1$ through `Bounds`.

## Covariance and convexity

For standard deviations $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ and correlation $\rho$,

$$
\Sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
\sigma_1^2&\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2\\
\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2&\sigma_2^2
\end{pmatrix}
$$


When $|\rho|\le1$,

$$
\det\Sigma=\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^2(1-\rho^2)\ge0
$$

and nonnegative diagonals make $\Sigma$ positive semidefinite.
The objective and feasible set are convex, so local minima are global.
`QpProblem::new` accepts only exactly symmetric $Q$, returning `InvalidInput` otherwise.
`QpSolver` checks positive semidefiniteness of $Q$ before solving, rejecting $Q$ with eigenvalues negative beyond tolerance using a `InvalidInput` error.
Roundoff or inconsistent estimates can produce negative covariance eigenvalues; check correlation ranges, symmetry and eigenvalues at input.

Below correlation 1, mixing assets gives smaller variance than perfect positive correlation.
It need not give smaller variance than either individual asset.
For $\sigma_1<\sigma_2$, adding a little growth asset to the low-volatility-only allocation reduces variance when $\rho\sigma_2<\sigma_1$.
Default $\rho=0.25$ gives $\rho\sigma_2=0.05<0.08$ and room to reduce variance; at $\rho\ge0.4$ this room disappears.

## Elimination to one variable and analytic solution

Set $z=w_2$ and $w_1=1-z$, satisfying the sum equality independently of $z$.
For covariance $\sigma_{12}=\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2$, variance is

$$
V(z)=\sigma_1^2(1-z)^2
+2\sigma_{12}z(1-z)+\sigma_2^2z^2
$$


Differentiating objective $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}V(z)-\mu_1-(\mu_2-\mu_1)z$ in $z$ and setting it to zero gives the solution without bounds:

$$
z_{\mathrm{free}}
=z_{\mathrm{mv}}+\frac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{\lambda D},
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{mv}}=\frac{\sigma_1^2-\sigma_{12}}{D},
\qquad
D=\sigma_1^2-2\sigma_{12}+\sigma_2^2
$$


$D=\operatorname{Var}(R_2-R_1)$ is at least $(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2$ and positive for $\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2$, making the objective strictly convex quadratic in $z$.
$z_{\mathrm{mv}}$ minimizes variance alone (the $\lambda\to\infty$ limit); decreasing $\lambda$ moves the solution toward growth by $(\mu_2-\mu_1)/(\lambda D)$.
$z_{\mathrm{free}}$ is also the optimal weight with only the sum equality, allowing short selling and borrowing.

For a strictly convex one-variable quadratic, projection of its vertex onto interval $[0,1]$ gives the interval minimum:

$$
z^\star=\min\bigl(1,\max(0,z_{\mathrm{free}})\bigr)
$$


For $z_{\mathrm{free}}>1$, upper bound $w_2\le1$ is active (holds with equality); for $z_{\mathrm{free}}<0$, upper bound $w_1\le1$ is active.
This formula independently checks QP allocations without solving the KKT system again.

Defaults give $D=0.0384$, $z_{\mathrm{mv}}=0.0625$ and $(\mu_2-\mu_1)/(\lambda D)=0.390625$, hence $z^\star=z_{\mathrm{free}}=0.453125$.

## Computing and checking allocations

Vary growth expected return $\mu_2$, correlation $\rho$ and risk aversion $\lambda$ to solve the QP.
The general sum-equality row makes default `QpMethod::Automatic` of `QpSolver` use an [active-set method](../../algorithms/quadratic-programming/), checking KKT certificates against the original problem at termination.

The first table compares QP allocations, projected analytic $z^\star$ and short-selling $z_{\mathrm{free}}$ allocations.
The second gives analytic differences, sum residuals, `ProgrammingResult` diagnostics, certificate-verification status and iterations.
The final outcome follows `ProgrammingResult::is_success()`.
This cell exports asset parameters and solution to the next plotting cell.

```rust
//| id: optimize-portfolio-qp
//| ref: optimize-portfolio-qp
//| title: "Vary expected returns, correlation and risk aversion to solve a convex QP"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| exports: [mu, sd, correlation, risk_aversion, weight, free]
//| inputs:
//|   growth_expected_return: {"type":"range","label":"Growth expected return μ₂ [annual rate]","description":"Change the benefit of holding the growth asset.","min":0.04,"max":0.16,"step":0.005,"value":0.09}
//|   asset_correlation: {"type":"range","label":"Asset correlation ρ [-]","description":"Change variance reduction and covariance.","min":-0.8,"max":0.95,"step":0.05,"value":0.25}
//|   risk_aversion: {"type":"range","label":"Risk aversion λ [-]","description":"Change the tradeoff between variance penalties and expected return.","min":0.2,"max":20,"step":0.2,"value":4}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"low-risk-aversion","label":"Risk aversion λ = 0.2","description":"The unbounded solution z_free = 7.875 lies outside the interval; QP returns w₂ = 1, activating the variable upper bound.","inputs":{"risk_aversion":0.2}}
//|   - {"id":"high-correlation","label":"Asset correlation ρ = 0.95","description":"Variance reduction nearly disappears and weight falls to w₂ = 0.3875. QP/analytic differences remain at most 10⁻¹⁶.","inputs":{"asset_correlation":0.95}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"allocation","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.453125,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"allocation","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.453125,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"allocation","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.0571875,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"allocation","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.11004,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":-1e-14,"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: low-risk-aversion
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"allocation","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"allocation","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":7.875,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: high-correlation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"allocation","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.3875,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{Bounds, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{LinearConstraintSense, LinearConstraints, QpProblem, QpSolver};

// μ = (0.03, μ₂), σ = (0.08, 0.20). Set Q = λΣ, c = −μ; impose w₁ + w₂ = 1 and 0 ≤ wᵢ ≤ 1.
let (mu, sd) = ([0.03_f64, growth_expected_return], [0.08_f64, 0.20]);
let (s11, s12, s22) = (
    sd[0] * sd[0],
    asset_correlation * sd[0] * sd[1],
    sd[1] * sd[1],
);
let q = [s11, s12, s12, s22]
    .iter()
    .map(|s| risk_aversion * s)
    .collect();
let hessian = Matrix::from_vec(2, 2, q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let budget = LinearConstraints::new(
    Matrix::from_vec(1, 2, vec![1.0_f64, 1.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    vec![1.0],
    vec![LinearConstraintSense::Equal],
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let problem = QpProblem::new(hessian, vec![-mu[0], -mu[1]])
    .and_then(|p| p.with_constraints(budget))
    .and_then(|p| p.with_bounds(Bounds::new(vec![(Some(0.0), Some(1.0)); 2])?))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let result = QpSolver::<f64>::default()
    .solve(&problem)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let w = result.x();
// Independent check: eliminate to one variable w₂ = z and project the analytic solution onto [0, 1].
let d = s11 - 2.0 * s12 + s22;
let free = (s11 - s12) / d + (mu[1] - mu[0]) / (risk_aversion * d);
let analytic = free.clamp(0.0, 1.0);
let variance = |z: f64| s11 * (1.0 - z) * (1.0 - z) + 2.0 * s12 * z * (1.0 - z) + s22 * z * z;
let row = |name: &str, z: f64| {
    let (expected, deviation) = (mu[0] + (mu[1] - mu[0]) * z, RealScalar::sqrt(variance(z)));
    (name.to_owned(), 1.0 - z, z, expected, deviation)
};
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let allocation_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Allocation" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "w₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "w₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Expected return μₚ [annual rate]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Standard deviation σₚ [annual rate]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    allocation_columns,
    vec![
        row("QP solution", w[1]),
        row("Analytic z* (projected onto [0, 1])", analytic),
        row("Short-selling solution z_free", free),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "allocation" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "|w₂ − z*| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "w₁ + w₂ − 1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Constraint violation [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Stationarity [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Bound violation [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Certificate verification", "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let verification = result
    .verification_report()
    .map(|report| format!("{:?}", report.status()));
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![(
        (w[1] - analytic).abs(),
        w[0] + w[1] - 1.0,
        result.primal_feasibility(),
        result.stationarity(),
        result.bound_violation(),
        verification,
        result.iterations(),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
export!(mu, &mu);
export!(sd, &sd);
export!(correlation, &asset_correlation);
export!(risk_aversion, &risk_aversion);
export!(weight, &w[1]);
export!(free, &free);
// After unsuccessful termination, interpret the last point as an allocation with unestablished optimality.
let outcome = if result.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("The QP satisfies stopping and its certificate was checked on the original problem")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped before satisfying the test, returning the last point")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(result.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("|w₂ − z*|", (w[1] - analytic).abs(), None)
        .diagnostic("Iterations", result.iterations() as f64, Some("count"))
);
```

At defaults ($\mu_2=0.09$, $\rho=0.25$, $\lambda=4$), `QpSolver` reaches `Converged` in 5 iterations, returning weights $(w_1,w_2)=(0.546875, 0.453125)$.
The solution without bounds $z_{\mathrm{free}}=0.453125$ lies inside the interval, differing from QP by $5.6\times10^{-17}$.
Expected portfolio return is 0.0572, standard deviation 0.110, and certificate verification `Accepted`.

For $\lambda=0.2$, $z_{\mathrm{free}}=7.875$ lies outside the interval; QP returns upper bound $w_2=1$ and activates it.
The third table row shows optimum $(w_1,w_2)=(-6.875,7.875)$ when short selling and borrowing are allowed.
Correlation 0.95 nearly removes variance reduction and lowers weight to $w_2=0.3875$.

Analytic differences independently check this two-asset problem.
With more assets and no analytic solution, inspect sum residuals, bound violations, stationarity, certificate verification and termination together.
Plausible-looking weights that fail the equality are invalid portfolios.

## Efficient frontier and objective

Read the previous solution in mean/standard-deviation space and on the objective.
This cell redraws from previous inputs and has no independent inputs.

The first plot shows standard deviation horizontally and expected return vertically, drawing the attainable $z\in[0,1]$ curve from closed form.
The $z\ge z_{\mathrm{mv}}$ branch is the dashed efficient frontier; the lower branch is dotted and QP solution overlaid as a symbol.
The second plot shows the objective for the same $z$, marking the QP solution and the solution $z_{\mathrm{free}}$ without bounds by vertical lines.

```rust
//| id: optimize-portfolio-qp-frontier
//| ref: optimize-portfolio-qp-frontier
//| title: "Inspect the QP solution on the efficient frontier and objective"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| dependsOn:
//|   qp: optimize-portfolio-qp
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

let read = |name: &str| qp[name].as_f64().ok_or(format!("Missing {name}"));
let mu: [f64; 2] = serde_json::from_value(qp["mu"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sd: [f64; 2] = serde_json::from_value(qp["sd"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (rho, lambda, weight, free) = (
    read("correlation")?,
    read("risk_aversion")?,
    read("weight")?,
    read("free")?,
);
let (s11, s12, s22) = (sd[0] * sd[0], rho * sd[0] * sd[1], sd[1] * sd[1]);
let variance = |z: f64| s11 * (1.0 - z) * (1.0 - z) + 2.0 * s12 * z * (1.0 - z) + s22 * z * z;
let point = |z: f64| [RealScalar::sqrt(variance(z)), mu[0] + (mu[1] - mu[0]) * z];
// The z ≥ z_mv branch is efficient; z < z_mv is inferior in variance and return.
let z_mv = ((s11 - s12) / (s11 - 2.0 * s12 + s22)).clamp(0.0, 1.0);
let dominated: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=100).map(|i| point(z_mv * i as f64 / 100.0)).collect();
let efficient: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=100)
    .map(|i| point(z_mv + (1.0 - z_mv) * i as f64 / 100.0))
    .collect();
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "xLabel": "Portfolio standard deviation σₚ [annual rate]",
        "yLabel": "Expected return μₚ [annual rate]",
        "layers": [
            {
                "id": "efficient",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Efficient frontier (closed form)",
                "points": efficient,
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
            },
            {
                "id": "dominated",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Inferior branch (closed form)",
                "points": dominated,
                "style": { "lineType": "dotted" }
            },
            { "id": "qp", "kind": "scatter", "name": "QP solution", "points": [point(weight)] }
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "frontier" })
);
let objective: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=200)
    .map(|i| {
        let z = i as f64 / 200.0;
        [z, 0.5 * lambda * variance(z) - mu[0] - (mu[1] - mu[0]) * z]
    })
    .collect();
// Vertical lines mark QP and unbounded solutions (outside the display if outside [0, 1]).
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Mean–variance objective", "points": objective })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Growth weight w₂ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Objective (λ/2)wᵀΣw − μᵀw [annual rate]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": weight, "label": "QP solution" },
            {
                "kind": "line",
                "axis": "x",
                "value": free,
                "label": "z_free",
                "style": { "lineType": "dotted" }
            }
        ]
    })
);
```

Not every point on the attainable curve is efficient.
An allocation dominated by a point with no greater standard deviation and higher expected return is inefficient.
For two assets the dotted branch below minimum-variance point $z_{\mathrm{mv}}$ is inferior; $\mu_2>\mu_1$ gives $z^\star\ge z_{\mathrm{mv}}$, so QP solutions always lie on the dashed branch.
Moving upward/right along the frontier increases both expected return and standard deviation.
$\lambda$ chooses the point; decreasing $\lambda$ moves solutions upward/right along the dashed curve.
At defaults, minimum variance occurs at $z_{\mathrm{mv}}=0.0625$ (standard deviation 0.0791); QP moves 0.39 from there toward growth.

When the objective minimum reaches an interval endpoint, bounds become active.
Decreasing $\lambda$ further cannot move weights beyond $w_2=1$.

## Summary

Pass the mean–variance model to `QpProblem` with $Q=\lambda\Sigma$ and $c=-\mu$, supplying the sum equality through `LinearConstraints` and short-selling prohibition through `Bounds`.
Eliminating to one variable independently checks two-asset QP weights against $z_{\mathrm{free}}=z_{\mathrm{mv}}+(\mu_2-\mu_1)/(\lambda D)$ projected onto $[0,1]$, agreeing within $10^{-16}$.
When $z_{\mathrm{free}}$ leaves the interval, bounds activate and further decreases in $\lambda$ no longer change allocation.
For many assets without analytic solutions, inspect sum residuals, bound violations, stationarity, certificate verification `Accepted` and termination `Converged` together.
