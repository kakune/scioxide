Mean–variance expected returns $\mu$ and covariance $\Sigma$ are estimates from past samples.
If small estimate changes strongly move optimal weights, reporting many decimal places is meaningless.
Using the same assets as [Design a two-asset portfolio by quadratic programming](../portfolio-qp/), solve QPs at each correlation $\rho$ and risk-aversion $\lambda$ grid point to create a weight heatmap.
Derive boundaries between bound-active and interior regions analytically and compare grid QPs.

## Eliminating the equality to a one-variable QP

Fix low-volatility $\mu_1=0.03$ and $\sigma_1=0.08$; input growth standard deviation $\sigma_2$ and expected-return difference $\Delta\mu=\mu_2-\mu_1$.
All use annual rates; defaults match previous $\sigma_2=0.20$ and $\Delta\mu=0.06$.

Substitute $z=w_2$ and $w_1=1-z$ before grid solves.
The sum equality then holds independently of $z$, and $0\le w_1\le1$ is equivalent to $0\le z\le1$.
With covariance $\sigma_{12}=\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2$ and $D=\sigma_1^2-2\sigma_{12}+\sigma_2^2$, the objective up to constants is

$$
\frac12\lambda Dz^2+\bigl[\lambda(\sigma_{12}-\sigma_1^2)-\Delta\mu\bigr]z,
\qquad 0\le z\le1
$$


This one-variable convex QP with bounds alone has the same optimal weights as the original two-asset problem.
For bounds alone, default `QpMethod::Automatic` of `QpSolver` uses [projected gradient and Barzilai–Borwein steps](oxref:optimize-qp-projected-gradient-bb).
Even on a 546-point grid, each solve is a small one-variable calculation.

## Boundaries between endpoint and interior solutions

The unbounded solution is

$$
z_{\mathrm{free}}=\frac{\Delta\mu/\lambda+\sigma_1^2-\sigma_{12}}{D}
$$

and optimal weight $z^\star$ is its projection onto $[0,1]$.
Solving $z_{\mathrm{free}}\ge1$ for $\lambda$ gives

$$
\lambda\le\lambda_{\mathrm{up}}(\rho)=\frac{\Delta\mu}{\sigma_2(\sigma_2-\rho\sigma_1)}
$$


The input range gives $\sigma_2\ge0.1>\rho\sigma_1$, so the denominator is positive; throughout this region, every $\rho$ gives growth-only allocation $w_2=1$.
Condition $z_{\mathrm{free}}\le0$ holds only for $\rho\sigma_2>\sigma_1$, when

$$
\lambda\ge\lambda_{\mathrm{low}}(\rho)=\frac{\Delta\mu}{\sigma_1(\rho\sigma_2-\sigma_1)}
$$

gives low-volatility-only allocation $w_2=0$.
For $\rho\sigma_2\le\sigma_1$, adding some growth reduces variance, so no size of $\lambda$ yields $w_2=0$.

Between the curves, interior weights depend continuously on $\lambda$ and $\rho$.
Near the curves, tiny estimate changes can switch active bounds.

## Sensitivity heatmap

Solve QPs on a 26×21 grid with correlation $\rho\in[-0.9,0.95]$ horizontally and risk aversion $\lambda\in[0.2,20]$ vertically, coloring growth weight $w_2$.
Inputs are expected-return difference $\Delta\mu$ and growth standard deviation $\sigma_2$.

Outputs are a weight heatmap, summary table and boundary table.
The summary gives solved-QP count, maximum projected-analytic difference and fraction of interior solutions.
For 5 values of $\rho$, the boundary table compares analytic $\lambda_{\mathrm{up}}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{low}}$ with the largest grid $\lambda$ returning $w_2=1$ and smallest grid $\lambda$ returning $w_2=0$.
Grid $\lambda$ spacing is 0.99; if a boundary lies inside range $[0.2,20]$ and QP is correct, $\lambda_{\mathrm{up}}$ lies at most 0.99 above the largest $\lambda$ with $w_2=1$, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{low}}$ at most 0.99 below the smallest $\lambda$ with $w_2=0$.
An unsuccessful QP returns an error containing its $(\rho,\lambda)$.

```rust
//| id: optimize-portfolio-sensitivity
//| ref: optimize-portfolio-sensitivity
//| title: "Inspect optimal-weight sensitivity to correlation and risk aversion"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   return_advantage: {"type":"range","label":"Expected-return difference μ₂ − μ₁ [annual rate]","description":"Change the benefit of holding the growth asset.","min":0.005,"max":0.13,"step":0.005,"value":0.06}
//|   growth_volatility: {"type":"range","label":"Growth standard deviation σ₂ [annual rate]","description":"Change the growth-variance penalty.","min":0.1,"max":0.35,"step":0.01,"value":0.2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"low-growth-volatility","label":"Growth standard deviation σ₂ = 0.10","description":"Weaker variance penalties expand w₂ = 1 toward larger λ, remove w₂ = 0 from the grid, and reduce the interior fraction from 0.810 to 0.595.","inputs":{"growth_volatility":0.1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":546}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.80952,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"thresholds","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.10294,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"thresholds","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.2,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"thresholds","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":null}
//|   - case: low-growth-volatility
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.59524,"atol":1e-4}}
use scioxide_core::{Bounds, SciOxideError};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{ProgrammingResult, QpProblem, QpSolver};

// Fix μ₁ = 0.03 and σ₁ = 0.08; solve identically shaped QPs on a 26×21 (ρ, λ) grid.
let (sd1, sd2) = (0.08_f64, growth_volatility);
// Substitute w₁ = 1 − z first, solving a one-variable convex QP after eliminating equality.
let solve = |rho: f64, lambda: f64| -> Result<ProgrammingResult<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let s12 = rho * sd1 * sd2;
    let d = sd1 * sd1 - 2.0 * s12 + sd2 * sd2;
    let q = Matrix::from_vec(1, 1, vec![lambda * d])?;
    let linear = lambda * (s12 - sd1 * sd1) - return_advantage;
    let problem = QpProblem::new(q, vec![linear])?
        .with_bounds(Bounds::new(vec![(Some(0.0), Some(1.0))])?)?;
    QpSolver::<f64>::default().solve(&problem)
};
let rhos: Vec<f64> = (0..=25)
    .map(|col| -0.9 + 1.85 * col as f64 / 25.0)
    .collect();
let lambdas: Vec<f64> = (0..=20).map(|row| 0.2 + 0.99 * row as f64).collect();
let (mut map, mut max_error, mut interior) = (Vec::new(), 0.0_f64, 0_usize);
for &lambda in &lambdas {
    for &rho in &rhos {
        let result = solve(rho, lambda).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if !result.is_success() {
            return Err(format!(
                "Unsuccessful QP at ρ = {rho:.3}, λ = {lambda:.2}: {}",
                result.termination()
            ));
        }
        let w2 = result.x()[0];
        // Independent check: difference from the one-variable analytic solution projected onto [0, 1].
        let s12 = rho * sd1 * sd2;
        let free =
            (return_advantage / lambda + sd1 * sd1 - s12) / (sd1 * sd1 - 2.0 * s12 + sd2 * sd2);
        let analytic = free.clamp(0.0, 1.0);
        max_error = max_error.max((w2 - analytic).abs());
        if free > 0.0 && free < 1.0 {
            interior += 1;
        }
        map.push([rho, lambda, w2]);
    }
}
// Weights lie in [0, 1], so fix the color range.
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "heatmap",
        "data": map,
        "xLabel": "Asset correlation ρ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Risk aversion λ [-]",
        "colorMin": 0.0,
        "colorMax": 1.0,
        "tooltip": true,
        "style": { "animation": false }
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({
        "id": "allocation-map",
        "title": "QP growth weight w₂ [-]"
    })
);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let summary_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "QPs solved [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "max |w₂ − z*| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Interior-solution fraction [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    summary_columns,
    vec![(map.len(), max_error, interior as f64 / map.len() as f64)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
// Analytic boundaries and each grid column's largest λ giving w₂ = 1 and smallest λ giving w₂ = 0.
let mut thresholds = Vec::new();
for col in [0_usize, 10, 15, 20, 25] {
    let rho = rhos[col];
    let upper = return_advantage / (sd2 * (sd2 - rho * sd1));
    let lower = (rho * sd2 > sd1).then(|| return_advantage / (sd1 * (rho * sd2 - sd1)));
    let column: Vec<[f64; 3]> = map[col..].iter().step_by(rhos.len()).copied().collect();
    let last_growth = column.iter().filter(|p| p[2] == 1.0).map(|p| p[1]).last();
    let first_low = column.iter().find(|p| p[2] == 0.0).map(|p| p[1]);
    thresholds.push((rho, upper, last_growth, lower, first_low));
}
let threshold_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Correlation ρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "λ_up (analytic) [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Largest λ with w₂ = 1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "λ_low (analytic) [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Smallest λ with w₂ = 0 [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4, "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    threshold_columns,
    thresholds,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "thresholds" })
);
```

At defaults all 546 QPs succeed, with maximum projected-analytic difference $2.2\times10^{-16}$ and interior fraction 0.810.
The heatmap has a $w_2=1$ strip along its bottom and a $w_2=0$ region in the upper-right corner ($\rho\ge0.59$ and $\lambda\ge\lambda_{\mathrm{low}}$).
Even for $\rho>0.4$, regions where $\lambda_{\mathrm{low}}$ exceeds grid maximum 20 contain no $w_2=0$ points.
At every $\rho$, analytic $\lambda_{\mathrm{up}}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{low}}$ bracket the grid QP transitions.

Lowering growth standard deviation to 0.10 reduces the interior fraction to 0.595.
Weaker variance penalties raise $\lambda_{\mathrm{up}}$ to 3.5–25, expanding the $w_2=1$ strip toward larger $\lambda$.
Only $\rho>0.8$ permits $\rho\sigma_2>\sigma_1$, and even there $\lambda_{\mathrm{low}}$ is at least 50, so the $w_2=0$ region disappears from the grid.

## Estimation errors and model limitations

Inside broad bound-active color regions, small estimate changes leave weights unchanged.
Near interior boundaries, small return-difference or correlation changes switch active bounds.
Many decimal places from a QP supplied only point estimates have no statistical support.

Mean–variance models reduce return distributions to means and variances, excluding skewness, heavy tails, maximum loss, liquidity and transaction costs.
Past-sample $\mu$ estimates are especially unstable; small errors in the linear term readily push weights toward endpoints.
Shrinkage estimation, robust optimization or penalties on weight changes may be needed.

“Risk” here means one-period return standard deviation, not loss probability itself.
Report optimal weights alongside $\lambda$, estimation period, asset set, short-selling/full-investment constraints and diagnostic residuals.

## Summary

Eliminating the sum equality turns two-asset allocation into a one-variable convex QP with bounds alone; `QpSolver` solves by projected gradient.
The upper bound activates for $\lambda\le\Delta\mu/(\sigma_2(\sigma_2-\rho\sigma_1))$ and lower bound for $\rho\sigma_2>\sigma_1$ with $\lambda\ge\Delta\mu/(\sigma_1(\rho\sigma_2-\sigma_1))$; grid QP transitions agree with these formulas.
Check whether estimates lie near a boundary; if so, avoid reporting fine decimal weights and show estimation uncertainty.
The heatmap distinguishes regions where correlation or return-difference estimates do not move weights from regions where active bounds switch.
