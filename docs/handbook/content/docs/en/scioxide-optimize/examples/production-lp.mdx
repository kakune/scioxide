Factory production planning chooses the product mix maximizing total profit under limited materials, labor and production-line capacity.
When objectives and constraints are linear, this is linear programming (LP); if a finite optimum exists, at least one feasible-region vertex attains it.
This page builds an LP for solar and battery equipment, solving by revised simplex (`SimplexSolver`) and interior point (`InteriorPointLp`).
Enumerate vertex profits manually to check the optimum and substitute into original resource equations to identify active constraints through slack.

## From production tables to linear constraints

Produce $x$ solar units and $y$ battery units per period.
A solar unit needs 2 material units and 1 labor hour; a battery needs 3 material units and 2 labor hours.
For materials $M$, labor $H\,[\mathrm h]$ and battery-specific process capacity $B$ units, constraints are

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x+3y&\le M &&\text{materials}\\
x+2y&\le H &&\text{labor}\\
y&\le B &&\text{dedicated process}\\
x,y&\ge0 &&\text{nonnegativity}
\end{aligned}
$$


For per-unit solar and battery profits $p_x,p_y$, solve

$$
\max_{x,y}\;p_xx+p_yy
$$

with $p_x=300$ fixed and $p_y$ varied below (profits in thousand yen).
Treat $x,y$ as continuous quantities here.
This suits intermediate goods or production-time allocation; finished-product counts need integer constraints.

SciOxide LP APIs minimize, so pass objective coefficients

$$
c=(-p_x,-p_y)^\mathsf T
$$


Negating the objective preserves feasibility; negating the minimum gives maximum profit.

## Why checking vertices suffices

Linear inequalities intersect in a convex polygon.
Along the segment joining feasible points $u,v$,

$$
p^\mathsf T\{(1-\alpha)u+\alpha v\}
=(1-\alpha)p^\mathsf Tu+\alpha p^\mathsf Tv
\qquad(0\le\alpha\le1)
$$

so interior profit is a weighted average of endpoint profits.
A finite optimum therefore has at least one vertex with equal profit.
In two variables, vertices are pairs of boundary-line intersections satisfying every constraint.
Choosing two of the 5 boundaries here (3 resources, $x=0$ and $y=0$) gives 10 pairs, allowing full vertex enumeration to verify the optimum.

Simplex moves between adjacent vertices until reduced costs (objective changes per unit increase of a nonbasic variable) indicate no improvement.
A pivot updates and resolves the basis; worst-case pivot counts can grow exponentially with variable count (Klee–Minty examples).
`InteriorPointLp` applies Mehrotra predictor–corrector to a homogeneous self-dual embedding, moving through the interior away from boundaries to reduce primal/dual residuals and complementarity together.
Short-step path following has an iteration bound $O(\sqrt n\log(1/\varepsilon))$; Mehrotra's practical low-iteration variant has no proof of this bound.
Each iteration factors the normal-equation matrix once, sharing it between predictor and corrector.
Interior point refines the last iterate on an active face before constructing optimality evidence.
See [Linear programming and duality](../../algorithms/linear-programming/) for algorithms.

## Slack and active constraints

For resource $i$ constraint $a_{i1}x+a_{i2}y\le b_i$, slack is

$$
s_i=b_i-a_{i1}x-a_{i2}y\ge0
$$


$s_i=0$ indicates an active constraint with a fully used resource; $s_i>0$ indicates unused resources.
At an optimal vertex, at least as many constraints as variables (here 2), including nonnegativity, are active.
Slightly increasing unused resources does not change profit; profit's rate of change with an active resource is its shadow price ([Resource shadow prices and basis changes](../production-shadow-prices/)).

## Feasible regions and both solutions

Vary resources, dedicated-process capacity and battery profit.
Inputs are material, labor and dedicated-process limits and per-battery profit.
Outputs are:

- Plot of resource boundaries, feasible vertices and both solutions (dedicated-process limit $y=B$ is a horizontal annotation)
- Table of both solutions, maximum profits, iterations, original-coordinate residuals, verification and termination
- Table of every feasible vertex and its profit (independent check)
- Table of slacks after substituting the simplex solution into original resource equations
- Outcome from the simplex verification report

The feasible region lies in the first quadrant below both resource lines and horizontal $y=B$; translating profit contours upward/right last touches an optimal vertex.
The `Independent verification` column reports original-coordinate verification of returned points and dual multipliers (`VerificationStatus`); `Duality gap` is that report's primal–dual objective difference.

```rust
//| id: optimize-production-lp
//| ref: optimize-production-lp
//| title: "Vary resources, process limits and profit to solve a linear program"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   material_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Material limit M [units]","description":"Change the right-hand side of 2x + 3y ≤ M.","min":70,"max":170,"step":5,"value":120}
//|   labor_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Labor limit H [h]","description":"Change the right-hand side of x + 2y ≤ H.","min":45,"max":110,"step":5,"value":70}
//|   battery_line_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Battery-process limit B [units]","description":"Move horizontal constraint y ≤ B.","min":10,"max":40,"step":1,"value":25}
//|   battery_unit_profit: {"type":"range","label":"Profit per battery pᵧ [thousand yen]","description":"Change profit-contour slopes.","min":350,"max":750,"step":25,"value":500}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"high-battery-profit","label":"Battery profit pᵧ = 750","description":"Changed contour slopes move the optimum to (20, 25), profit 24750. Labor and dedicated process are active, with 5 material units unused.","inputs":{"battery_unit_profit":750}}
//|   - {"id":"low-battery-profit","label":"Battery profit pᵧ = 350","description":"No-battery (60, 0) becomes optimal at profit 18000; only materials are active, with 10 labor hours unused.","inputs":{"battery_unit_profit":350}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":30,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":20,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":19000,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":19000,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/8","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/1/8","equals":"Accepted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/10","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/1/10","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"vertices","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":19000,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"slacks","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"slacks","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"slacks","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":5,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/4/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: high-battery-profit
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":20,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":25,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":24750,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"vertices","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":24750,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"slacks","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":5,"atol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: low-battery-profit
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":60,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solvers","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":18000,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"slacks","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":10,"atol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::Bounds;
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    InteriorPointLp, LinearConstraintSense, LinearConstraints, LpProblem, SimplexSolver,
};

let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
// Materials 2x+3y ≤ M, labor x+2y ≤ H, dedicated process y ≤ B, x, y ≥ 0.
let constraints = LinearConstraints::new(
    Matrix::from_vec(3, 2, vec![2.0_f64, 3.0, 1.0, 2.0, 0.0, 1.0])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    vec![material_capacity, labor_capacity, battery_line_capacity],
    vec![LinearConstraintSense::LessEqual; 3],
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Pass maximization of profit 300x + pᵧy as negated minimization.
let problem = LpProblem::new(vec![-300.0_f64, -battery_unit_profit])
    .and_then(|p| p.with_constraints(constraints))
    .and_then(|p| p.with_bounds(Bounds::new(vec![(Some(0.0), None); 2])?))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let simplex = SimplexSolver::<f64>::default()
    .solve(&problem)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let interior = InteriorPointLp::<f64>::default()
    .solve(&problem)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: enumerate feasible intersections from pairs of the 5 boundaries aᵀ(x, y) = b.
let boundaries = [
    ([2.0_f64, 3.0], material_capacity, "Materials"),
    ([1.0, 2.0], labor_capacity, "Labor"),
    ([0.0, 1.0], battery_line_capacity, "Dedicated process"),
    ([-1.0, 0.0], 0.0, "x = 0"),
    ([0.0, -1.0], 0.0, "y = 0"),
];
let mut vertices = Vec::new();
for (i, &([a1, b1], c1, n1)) in boundaries.iter().enumerate() {
    for &([a2, b2], c2, n2) in &boundaries[i + 1..] {
        // Parallel boundaries (such as y ≤ B and y ≥ 0) have no intersection.
        let det = a1 * b2 - a2 * b1;
        if det == 0.0 {
            continue;
        }
        let (x, y) = ((c1 * b2 - c2 * b1) / det, (a1 * c2 - a2 * c1) / det);
        if boundaries
            .iter()
            .all(|(a, c, _)| a[0] * x + a[1] * y <= c + 1e-9)
        {
            let profit = 300.0 * x + battery_unit_profit * y;
            vertices.push((format!("{n1} / {n2}"), x, y, profit));
        }
    }
}
vertices.sort_by(|p, q| q.3.total_cmp(&p.3));
// Plot resource boundaries as lines, vertices and solutions as symbols. Fix axes to maximum input ranges.
let line = |a: f64, b: f64, cap: f64| {
    (0..=120)
        .map(|i| {
            let x = 90.0 * i as f64 / 120.0;
            [x, (cap - a * x) / b]
        })
        .filter(|p| p[1] >= 0.0)
        .collect::<Vec<_>>()
};
let vertex_points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = vertices.iter().map(|v| [v.1, v.2]).collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "xLabel": "Solar units x [units]",
    "yLabel": "Battery units y [units]",
    "xMin": 0.0,
    "xMax": 90.0,
    "yMin": 0.0,
    "yMax": 45.0,
    "layers": [
        {
            "id": "material",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "Material boundary 2x+3y=M",
            "points": line(2.0, 3.0, material_capacity)
        },
        {
            "id": "labor",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "Labor boundary x+2y=H",
            "points": line(1.0, 2.0, labor_capacity)
        },
        { "id": "vertices", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Feasible vertices", "points": vertex_points },
        {
            "id": "simplex",
            "kind": "scatter",
            "name": "Simplex solution",
            "points": [simplex.x()],
            "style": { "symbol": "diamond" }
        },
        {
            "id": "interior",
            "kind": "scatter",
            "name": "Interior-point solution",
            "points": [interior.x()],
            "style": { "symbol": "triangle" }
        }
    ],
    "annotations": [
        { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": battery_line_capacity, "label": "Dedicated process y=B" }
    ]
}));
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, r) in [("Revised simplex", &simplex), ("Interior point (HSD)", &interior)] {
    let report = r.verification_report();
    rows.push((
        name,
        r.x()[0],
        r.x()[1],
        -r.fun(),
        r.iterations(),
        r.primal_feasibility(),
        r.stationarity(),
        r.complementarity(),
        report.map(|v| format!("{:?}", v.status())),
        report.and_then(|v| v.objective_gap()),
        r.termination().to_string(),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "x [units]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "y [units]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum profit [thousand yen]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Primal feasibility violation", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Stationarity residual", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Complementarity", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Independent verification", "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "9", "label": "Duality gap [thousand yen]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "10", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "solvers" })
);
let vertex_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Intersecting boundaries" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "x [units]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "y [units]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Profit [thousand yen]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    vertex_columns,
    vertices.clone(),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "vertices" })
);
// Independent check: slack after substituting the simplex point into original resources (negative means violation).
let (x, y) = (simplex.x()[0], simplex.x()[1]);
let slacks = vec![
    (
        "Materials M − 2x − 3y [units]",
        material_capacity - 2.0 * x - 3.0 * y,
    ),
    ("Labor H − x − 2y [h]", labor_capacity - x - 2.0 * y),
    ("Dedicated process B − y [units]", battery_line_capacity - y),
];
let slack_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Constraint" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Slack [constraint-specific units]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    slack_columns,
    slacks,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "slacks" })
);
// Outcome: did simplex terminate successfully and did original-coordinate verification accept optimality evidence?
simplex.require_success().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let outcome = if simplex
    .verification_report()
    .is_some_and(|v| v.is_accepted())
{
    Outcome::satisfied("Original-coordinate verification accepted the simplex point and dual multipliers")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Simplex terminated but verification did not accept optimality evidence")
        .explanation("Treat the point as a candidate; independently inspect original resource equations and dual multipliers.")
};
let outcome = outcome
    .status(simplex.termination().to_string())
    .diagnostic("Iterations", simplex.iterations() as f64, None)
    .diagnostic(
        "Profit difference from vertex enumeration",
        (-simplex.fun() - vertices[0].3).abs(),
        None,
    );
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

At defaults both methods return $(x,y)=(30,20)$ and maximum profit 19000 thousand yen, with termination `Converged` and independent verification `Accepted`.
Simplex reaches the vertex in 2 iterations, with original-coordinate residuals and duality gap 0.
Interior point approaches the same point in 8 iterations, with residuals $10^{-13}$–$10^{-10}$ and duality gap $-1.6\times10^{-10}$.
Approaching the vertex from inside leaves coordinate errors around $10^{-12}$ at defaults.

Among 5 feasible vertices, the material/labor intersection $(30,20)$ gives largest profit 19000 thousand yen, matching the solvers.
Next is labor/dedicated-process intersection $(20,25)$ with 18500.
Slacks are 0 for materials and labor (active), with dedicated capacity for 5 units unused.
Constraint slacks have different units; do not compare material and time slacks by numerical magnitude alone.

Battery profit 750 changes contour slopes, moving the optimum to $(20,25)$ and profit 24750 thousand yen.
Labor and dedicated process become active, leaving 5 material units.
At 350, no-battery $(60,0)$ is optimal at profit 18000 thousand yen, with 10 labor hours unused.
Every input gives enumerated maximum profit matching solver maximum profit.

## Beyond the linear model

Real production has fixed startup costs, minimum lots, setup, demand limits and nonlinear yields.
Even integer unit counts make feasibility a set of lattice points; rounding continuous optima can violate constraints or lose substantial profit.
[Choose finished-product lots by mixed-integer programming](../integer-production/) treats integer lots and branch and bound.

Align coefficient units, check each right-hand side, and substitute returned points into original resource equations.
Saving slacks, residuals, duality gaps and termination alongside objectives supports decisions based on optimization.

## Summary

Linear production, resource-use and profit formulas allow visualization of feasible regions and optimal vertices.
For small two-variable LPs, independently check solver solutions against maximum profit over enumerated boundary intersections.
Here simplex returns the vertex exactly, while interior point returns the same point with residuals around $10^{-13}$–$10^{-10}$.
Check both through termination, verification and original-resource slacks.
Zero-slack resources constrain the plan, so begin resource-expansion considerations there.
