[Production planning for renewable energy equipment](../production-lp/) maximized profit from $x$ photovoltaic and $y$ battery units under material, labor and battery-specific process-capacity constraints.
Next determine how much profit increases from one extra unit of each resource.
This value is the shadow price, obtained as an optimal solution of the dual LP.
Interpret dual multipliers returned by `SimplexSolver` as shadow prices, independently checking resolves with perturbed right-hand sides and dual-objective/maximum-profit agreement (strong duality).

## Value functions and the dual problem

Write maximum profit as a function of resource quantities $b=(M,H,B)^\mathsf T$:

$$
V(b)=\max\{\,p^\mathsf Tq : Aq\le b,\ q\ge0\,\}
$$


Here $q=(x,y)^\mathsf T$ is production, $p=(300,p_y)^\mathsf T$ unit profits and $A$ resource usage.

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

The dual attaches unit price $\lambda_i\ge0$ to resource $i$ and solves

$$
\min_{\lambda}\;b^\mathsf T\lambda
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
A^\mathsf T\lambda\ge p,\ \lambda\ge0
$$


Constraint $A^\mathsf T\lambda\ge p$ means a product's resource usage valued at these prices is at least its profit.
Feasible primal/dual solutions $q,\lambda$ satisfy $p^\mathsf Tq\le\lambda^\mathsf TAq\le b^\mathsf T\lambda$ (weak duality), with equality $V(b)=b^\mathsf T\lambda^\star$ at optimality (strong duality).
Equality requires complementarity:

$$
\lambda_i\,(b_i-a_i^\mathsf Tq)=0,
\qquad
q_j\,(A^\mathsf T\lambda-p)_j=0
$$

where $a_i^\mathsf T$ is row $i$ of $A$.
Unused resources have price 0; produced goods have resource value equal to profit.

At default optimum $(30,20)$, materials and labor are active; $x,y>0$ makes both dual constraints equalities.

$$
2\lambda_M+\lambda_H=300,\qquad 3\lambda_M+2\lambda_H=500
$$

Solving gives $\lambda_M=\lambda_H=100$ and $\lambda_B=0$, with $b^\mathsf T\lambda=120\cdot100+70\cdot100=19000$ matching maximum profit.

## Local shadow prices and degeneracy

While the optimal basis (active constraint set) is unchanged, $V$ is linear in $b$ with slope $\lambda^\star$.
Basis changes bend $V$, making it globally a piecewise-linear concave function of $b$.
Approximate shadow prices by one-sided right-hand-side differences.

$$
\frac{V(b+\Delta e_i)-V(b)}{\Delta}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{V(b)-V(b-\Delta e_i)}{\Delta}
$$

For small $\Delta$ preserving the basis, both differences equal $\lambda_i^\star$.
At degenerate vertices with three or more active constraints, dual optima may be nonunique.
Then $V$ may have a corner; concavity gives forward difference $\le\lambda_i^\star\le$ backward difference, and returned multipliers lie between them.

Excessively small difference step $\Delta$ makes objective-subtraction roundoff dominate (for profit around $2\times10^4$, roundoff at $\Delta=0.1$ is around $10^{-10}$ or less).
Excessively large steps can cross basis changes.

## Compare multipliers and differences

Inputs are three baseline resource quantities and profit per battery.
Outputs are:

- Table of each dual multiplier $\lambda_i$ and forward/backward differences from increasing/decreasing the right-hand side by $\Delta=0.1$
- Table of baseline maximum profit $V$, dual objective $b^\mathsf T\lambda$ and their difference (strong-duality check)
- Plot of maximum profit when varying one resource over 0.7–1.3 times baseline
- Outcome based on verification of baseline-LP optimality evidence

Read original-row-order multipliers from `ProgrammingCertificate::Optimality { dual, .. }` of `ProgrammingResult::certificate()`.
SciOxide minimizes; a negated-profit LP has nonnegative multipliers on $\le$ rows, representing the minimum's decrease, hence profit increase, per unit right-hand-side increase.
Differences independently resolve the same LP with only the right-hand side changed.

```rust
//| id: optimize-production-shadow-prices
//| ref: optimize-production-shadow-prices
//| title: "Inspect the value of one extra resource unit and basis changes"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-optimize]
//| inputs:
//|   shadow_material_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Baseline materials M [units]","description":"Change the value-function reference point.","min":70,"max":170,"step":5,"value":120}
//|   shadow_labor_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Baseline labor H [h]","description":"Change competition with materials.","min":45,"max":110,"step":5,"value":70}
//|   shadow_line_capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Baseline process B [units]","description":"Change scarcity of battery-specific capacity.","min":10,"max":40,"step":1,"value":25}
//|   shadow_battery_profit: {"type":"range","label":"Profit per battery pᵧ [thousand yen]","description":"Change marginal resource values.","min":350,"max":750,"step":25,"value":500}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"high-battery-profit","label":"Battery profit pᵧ = 750","description":"Labor and dedicated process become active; multipliers are materials 0, labor 300 and process 150, matching both differences.","inputs":{"shadow_battery_profit":750}}
//|   - {"id":"scarce-material","label":"Baseline materials M = 70","description":"Only materials constrain the plan; their multiplier is 500/3 ≈ 166.67, while labor and process multipliers are 0.","inputs":{"shadow_material_capacity":70}}
//|   - {"id":"degenerate","label":"Baseline process B = 20","description":"All three resources activate at vertex (30, 20), splitting material forward and backward differences to 100 and 150.","inputs":{"shadow_line_capacity":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":100,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":100,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":100,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":100,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duality","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":19000,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duality","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: high-battery-profit
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":300,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":150,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":150,"atol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: scarce-material
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":166.666667,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":166.666667,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: degenerate
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":100,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":150,"atol":1e-6}}
use scioxide_core::{Bounds, SciOxideError};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_optimize::{
    LinearConstraintSense, LinearConstraints, LpProblem, ProgrammingCertificate,
    ProgrammingResult, SimplexSolver,
};

let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
// Resolve the same LP changing only right-hand side (M, H, B). Pass negated profit as minimization.
let solve = |rhs: [f64; 3]| -> Result<ProgrammingResult<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let rows = LinearConstraints::new(
        Matrix::from_vec(3, 2, vec![2.0_f64, 3.0, 1.0, 2.0, 0.0, 1.0])?,
        rhs.to_vec(),
        vec![LinearConstraintSense::LessEqual; 3],
    )?;
    let problem = LpProblem::new(vec![-300.0_f64, -shadow_battery_profit])?
        .with_constraints(rows)?
        .with_bounds(Bounds::new(vec![(Some(0.0), None); 2])?)?;
    let result = SimplexSolver::<f64>::default().solve(&problem)?;
    result.require_success()?;
    Ok(result)
};
let base = [
    shadow_material_capacity,
    shadow_labor_capacity,
    shadow_line_capacity,
];
let result = solve(base).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Original-coordinate dual evidence from simplex. Multipliers λᵢ ≥ 0 on ≤ rows are profit shadow prices.
let duals = match result.certificate() {
    Some(ProgrammingCertificate::Optimality { dual, .. }) => dual.rows.to_vec(),
    _ => return Err("No dual optimality evidence".to_owned()),
};
// Independent check: one-sided differences from right-hand-side resolves at Δ = 0.1. Profit is −fun.
let delta = 0.1_f64;
let names = [
    "Materials M [thousand yen/unit]",
    "Labor H [thousand yen/h]",
    "Dedicated process B [thousand yen/unit]",
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (i, name) in names.into_iter().enumerate() {
    let (mut up, mut down) = (base, base);
    up[i] += delta;
    down[i] -= delta;
    let forward = (result.fun() - solve(up).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.fun()) / delta;
    let backward = (solve(down).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.fun() - result.fun()) / delta;
    rows.push((name, duals[i], forward, backward));
}
let dual_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Resource" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Dual multiplier λᵢ", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Forward difference", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Backward difference", "type": "number", "format": sig6 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    dual_columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "duals" })
);
// Independent check: strong duality V = bᵀλ.
let dual_objective: f64 = base.iter().zip(&duals).map(|(b, l)| b * l).sum();
let duality_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum profit V [thousand yen]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Dual objective bᵀλ [thousand yen]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference |V − bᵀλ| [thousand yen]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    duality_columns,
    vec![(
        -result.fun(),
        dual_objective,
        (-result.fun() - dual_objective).abs()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "duality" })
);
// Vary one resource over 0.7–1.3 times baseline, plotting piecewise-linear maximum profit V.
let mut series = Vec::new();
for (i, label) in ["Vary materials only", "Vary labor only", "Vary dedicated process only"]
    .into_iter()
    .enumerate()
{
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for k in 0..=60 {
        let scale = 0.7 + 0.6 * k as f64 / 60.0;
        let mut rhs = base;
        rhs[i] *= scale;
        points.push([scale, -solve(rhs).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.fun()]);
    }
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": label, "points": points }));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Resource multiplier relative to baseline [-]",
        "yLabel": "Maximum profit V [thousand yen]",
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": 1.0, "label": "Baseline resources" }]
    })
);
// Outcome: did original-coordinate verification accept the dual evidence underlying shadow prices?
let outcome = if result
    .verification_report()
    .is_some_and(|v| v.is_accepted())
{
    Outcome::satisfied("Original-coordinate verification accepted baseline-LP solution and dual multipliers")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("The baseline LP terminated but verification did not accept dual evidence")
        .explanation("Treat multipliers as candidate shadow prices and compare finite differences.")
};
let outcome = outcome
    .status(result.termination().to_string())
    .diagnostic("Maximum profit", -result.fun(), None)
    .diagnostic("Strong-duality difference", (-result.fun() - dual_objective).abs(), None);
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

At baseline defaults, material, labor and dedicated-process multipliers are 100 thousand yen/unit, 100 thousand yen/h and 0, matching both one-sided differences.
Dual objective $b^\mathsf T\lambda=19000$ equals maximum profit and the hand-derived value above.
The dedicated process has unused capacity and multiplier 0, as complementarity $\lambda_i(b_i-a_i^\mathsf Tq)=0$ predicts.
Slight material increases add profit 100 thousand yen/unit, suggesting additions are worthwhile below purchase price 100 thousand yen/unit.

Battery profit 750 activates labor and dedicated process, with multipliers materials 0, labor 300 and process 150.
Baseline materials 70 makes materials the only limiting resource, with multiplier $500/3\approx166.67$ and all others 0.
Both cases have differences matching multipliers and dual objective matching maximum profit.

Baseline process $B=20$ makes all three resource boundaries pass through $(30,20)$, a degenerate vertex.
Simplex multipliers are materials 150, labor 0 and process 50; dual objective $120\cdot150+20\cdot50=19000$ equals maximum profit.
But forward/backward differences split: materials 100/150, labor 0/100, process 0/50.
Every multiplier lies between the differences, showing nonunique slope at a concave value-function corner.
Degenerate-vertex multipliers may overestimate gains from additions and underestimate losses from reductions; check differences on both sides.

The plotted piecewise-linear curves are $V$ when changing one resource.
Zero-slope intervals mean another constraint limits production before this resource does.
Corners indicate changes in active constraints; dual multipliers give slopes between corners.
Multipliers cannot predict large changes across corners; when using shadow prices for capital decisions, state that this is a local model with fixed prices, demand and yields.

## Summary

Read resource shadow prices as dual multipliers in simplex optimality evidence.
Check them independently through dual-objective/maximum-profit agreement (strong duality) and resolves with slightly perturbed right-hand sides.
In the default plan, one material unit and one labor hour each correspond to 100 thousand yen profit, while unused dedicated capacity has value 0.
Shadow prices describe changes while active constraints remain unchanged; near degeneracy or corners compute both differences, using forward differences for resource-addition value.
