Place $N$ like-signed point charges on the unit sphere; they tend to spread apart.
J. J. Thomson posed the minimum-energy configuration problem in 1904 while studying atomic models; it is called the Thomson problem.
Set charge magnitude and Coulomb constant to 1 and measure length in sphere radii; dimensionless energy is

$$
E=\sum_{i<j}\frac1{\|r_i-r_j\|_2},\qquad \|r_i\|_2=1
$$


Here $r_i\in\mathbb R^3$ is the position of charge $i$.
Representing each point as $r=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)$ automatically satisfies spherical constraints, allowing unconstrained minimization.
Variables are $2N$ angles $(\theta_i,\phi_i)$.

## Gradients and coordinate properties

The Cartesian gradient is $\partial E/\partial r_i=-\sum_{j\ne i}(r_i-r_j)/\|r_i-r_j\|^3$.
By the chain rule, angular gradients are its inner products with

$$
\frac{\partial r}{\partial\theta}=(\cos\theta\cos\phi,\cos\theta\sin\phi,-\sin\theta),\qquad
\frac{\partial r}{\partial\phi}=(-\sin\theta\sin\phi,\sin\theta\cos\phi,0)
$$


Each evaluation loops over $N(N-1)/2$ pairs and costs $O(N^2)$.
Dense BFGS stores a $2N\times2N$ inverse-Hessian approximation, updating it in $O(N^2)$ operations per iteration.

Global rotation preserves energy, so minima form three-dimensional families and the Hessian retains zero eigenvalues.
At poles $\sin\theta=0$, $\partial r/\partial\phi=0$ and azimuth does not move points, allowing additional angular-Hessian degeneracy.
Colliding charges make the objective diverge.
These coordinate and model properties can slow BFGS convergence.
Check termination for a gradient-test stop and recompute the angular gradient 2-norm at returned points.

The `Bfgs::default()` gradient test requires a 2-norm at most $\varepsilon_a+\varepsilon_r\max(1,|E|/\max(1,\|q\|_\infty))$, with $\varepsilon_a=\varepsilon_r=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\approx1.5\times10^{-8}$.
Here $q$ collects angular variables and $\varepsilon$ is double-precision machine epsilon.
Passing gives termination `GradientSmall`.

## Checks from symmetric configurations

Two antipodal points give $E=1/2$; three form a great-circle equilateral triangle with sides $\sqrt3$, giving $E=3/\sqrt3=\sqrt3$.
Four form a regular tetrahedron; 6 pairs at side length $\sqrt{8/3}$ give $E=6/\sqrt{8/3}=3.6742346142\ldots$.
The 6-point regular octahedron has 12 pairs at distance $\sqrt2$ and 3 at distance 2, giving $E=12/\sqrt2+3/2=9.9852813742\ldots$.
In the 12-point regular icosahedron, each point sees 5 at distance $a=1/\sin(2\pi/5)$, 5 at distance $\varphi a$ ($\varphi$ is the golden ratio), and one antipode, giving $E=30/a+30/(\varphi a)+6/2=49.1652530576\ldots$.
Symmetry derives these values but does not prove minimality.

Other counts use nine-decimal values from Wikipedia's [Thomson problem table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_problem).
Counts 10–12 also appear in the [Cambridge Cluster Database](https://www-wales.ch.cam.ac.uk/~wales/CCD/Thomson/table.html) at seven decimals and agree to seven digits.
Table rounding makes differences at most $5\times10^{-10}$ consistent with rounding.
For 11 points alone, every start reaches 40.5964505082; difference $-1.8\times10^{-9}$ from tabulated 40.596450510 exceeds rounding limits.
The returned energy corresponds to an actual configuration, so minimum energy is no greater.
The table therefore exceeds minimum energy by at least $1.8\times10^{-9}$, a difference unexplained by solver nonconvergence.
It agrees with Cambridge value 40.5964505; do not attach meaning to the last 1–2 digits of the nine-decimal table.

## Inputs and outputs

Inputs are point count, start count and random seed.
Draw starts uniformly on the sphere, using uniform $\cos\theta$ over $[-1,1]$ and $\phi$ over $[0,2\pi)$.
Outputs are a 3D best-configuration scene, per-start results table and overall outcome.
The table gives every start's energy, reference difference, angular gradient norm and termination; retaining all starts avoids hiding failures behind only the best value.
The scene shows the lowest-energy points and convex-hull edges.
Compute the hull with `ConvexHull` of `scioxide-spatial`; for at most 3 points no 3D hull exists, so connect every pair.
Point IDs are charge indices, preserving selected points and viewpoint across input changes.

```rust
//| id: optimize-thomson-multistart
//| ref: optimize-thomson-multistart
//| title: "Arrange spherical charges from multiple starts"
//| run: button
//| timeoutMs: 120000
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-optimize, scioxide-random, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   charges: {"type":"range","label":"Charges N [count]","min":2,"max":12,"step":1,"value":4}
//|   starts: {"type":"range","label":"Starts [count]","min":1,"max":8,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":7}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"icosahedron","label":"12 points (icosahedron)","description":"All 3 starts reach 49.165253058, within 10⁻¹⁰ of the icosahedron closed form. Each hull point has 5 edges.","inputs":{"charges":12}}
//|   - {"id":"eleven","label":"11 points","description":"Every start reaches 40.5964505082; difference −1.8×10⁻⁹ from Wikipedia's 40.596450510 exceeds table rounding.","inputs":{"charges":11}}
//|   - {"id":"single-start","label":"5 points, 1 start","description":"Even one start reaches a triangular bipyramid at 6.474691495, but one attainment is no global-minimum proof.","inputs":{"charges":5,"starts":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":3.674234614,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":3.674234614,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"GradientSmall"}
//|       - {"artifact":"configuration","path":"/points/3/id","equals":"charge-3"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: icosahedron
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":49.165253058,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":49.165253058,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":49.165253058,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: eleven
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":40.5964505082,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":-1.8e-9,"atol":5e-10}}
//|   - case: single-start
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"runs","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":6.474691495,"atol":1e-8}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_optimize::{Bfgs, ObjectiveFn};
use scioxide_random::{BitGenerator, SeedableRng, SplitMix64, decode_u64};
use scioxide_spatial::{ConvexHull, PointSet};
// q = (θ₀, φ₀, θ₁, φ₁, …). Form r, ∂r/∂θ and ∂r/∂φ and take inner products with Cartesian gradients.
fn energy(q: &[f64]) -> (f64, Vec<f64>) {
    let points: Vec<_> = q
        .chunks_exact(2)
        .map(|a| {
            let (s, c) = (RealScalar::sin(a[0]), RealScalar::cos(a[0]));
            let (sp, cp) = (RealScalar::sin(a[1]), RealScalar::cos(a[1]));
            (
                [s * cp, s * sp, c],
                [c * cp, c * sp, -s],
                [-s * sp, s * cp, 0.0],
            )
        })
        .collect();
    let mut value = 0.0;
    let mut gradient = vec![0.0; q.len()];
    for i in 0..points.len() {
        for j in 0..i {
            let d: [f64; 3] = std::array::from_fn(|k| points[i].0[k] - points[j].0[k]);
            let distance = RealScalar::sqrt(d.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>());
            value += 1.0 / distance;
            for k in 0..3 {
                let force = -d[k] / distance.powi(3);
                gradient[2 * i] += force * points[i].1[k];
                gradient[2 * i + 1] += force * points[i].2[k];
                gradient[2 * j] -= force * points[j].1[k];
                gradient[2 * j + 1] -= force * points[j].2[k];
            }
        }
    }
    (value, gradient)
}
let n = charges as usize;
// References for N = 2, …, 12 (closed forms at 2, 3, 4, 6, 12; literature tables otherwise, nine decimals).
let references = [
    0.5,
    1.732050808,
    3.674234614,
    6.474691495,
    9.985281374,
    14.452977414,
    19.675287861,
    25.759986531,
    32.716949460,
    40.596450510,
    49.165253058,
];
let objective = ObjectiveFn::new(2 * n, |q: &[f64]| Ok(energy(q).0)).with_gradient(
    |q: &[f64], g: &mut [f64]| {
        g.copy_from_slice(&energy(q).1);
        Ok(())
    },
);
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut best: Option<(f64, Vec<f64>)> = None;
let mut all_success = true;
for run in 0..starts as usize {
    // Uniform sphere sampling: cos θ uniform on [−1, 1], φ uniform on [0, 2π).
    let mut q = Vec::new();
    for _ in 0..n {
        let u: f64 = decode_u64(rng.next_uniform_u64().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
        let v: f64 = decode_u64(rng.next_uniform_u64().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
        q.push(RealScalar::acos(2.0 * u - 1.0));
        q.push(2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * v);
    }
    let result = Bfgs::default()
        .minimize(&objective, &q)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    all_success &= result.is_success();
    // Independent check: substitute returned angles into the objective and recompute angular gradient 2-norm.
    let gradient = energy(result.x()).1;
    rows.push((
        run + 1,
        result.fun(),
        references[n - 2],
        result.fun() - references[n - 2],
        RealScalar::sqrt(gradient.iter().map(|g| g * g).sum::<f64>()),
        result.termination().to_string(),
    ));
    if best.as_ref().is_none_or(|b| result.fun() < b.0) {
        best = Some((result.fun(), result.x().to_vec()));
    }
}
let (best_energy, best_q) = best.ok_or("No start")?;
// Convert the best configuration to Cartesian coordinates and draw triangulated-hull edges. Connect all pairs for at most 3 points.
let cartesian: Vec<f64> = best_q
    .chunks_exact(2)
    .flat_map(|a| {
        let s = RealScalar::sin(a[0]);
        [s * RealScalar::cos(a[1]), s * RealScalar::sin(a[1]), RealScalar::cos(a[0])]
    })
    .collect();
let mut pairs = std::collections::BTreeSet::new();
if n <= 3 {
    for i in 0..n {
        for j in 0..i {
            pairs.insert((j, i));
        }
    }
} else {
    let sites = PointSet::from_rows(&cartesian, 3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let hull = ConvexHull::new(&sites).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    for facet in hull.facets().chunks_exact(3) {
        for (a, b) in [(facet[0], facet[1]), (facet[1], facet[2]), (facet[2], facet[0])] {
            pairs.insert((a.min(b), a.max(b)));
        }
    }
}
let points: Vec<_> = cartesian
    .chunks_exact(3)
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, p)| {
        serde_json::json!({
            "id": format!("charge-{i}"),
            "position": [p[0], p[1], p[2]],
            "label": format!("Charge {i}"),
            "group": "charges"
        })
    })
    .collect();
let edges: Vec<_> = pairs
    .iter()
    .map(|&(a, b)| {
        serde_json::json!({ "from": format!("charge-{a}"), "to": format!("charge-{b}") })
    })
    .collect();
let unit = |label: &str| serde_json::json!({ "label": label, "unit": "Sphere radius" });
emit_scene3d!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "points": points,
        "edges": edges,
        "groups": [{ "id": "charges", "label": "Charges in the best configuration" }],
        "axes": { "x": unit("x"), "y": unit("y"), "z": unit("z") }
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "configuration", "title": "Best configuration and convex-hull edges" })
);
let fixed10 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 });
let fixed9 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 9 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Start", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Energy E [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Reference [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed9 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Angular gradient norm [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows.clone(),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "runs" })
);
let spread = rows.iter().map(|r| r.1).fold(f64::NEG_INFINITY, f64::max) - best_energy;
let outcome = if all_success {
    Outcome::satisfied("Every start satisfies the gradient test")
        .explanation("This is an observation over tested starts, not a global-minimum proof.")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Some starts stopped without satisfying the gradient test")
        .explanation("Treat their energies as estimates and inspect termination in the table.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(rows[0].5.clone())
        .diagnostic("Best energy", best_energy, None)
        .diagnostic("Maximum inter-start difference", spread, None)
);
```

For default 4 points, all 3 starts reach 3.6742346142, matching tetrahedral closed form $6/\sqrt{8/3}$ within $2\times10^{-10}$ (reference rounding).
The hull has 4 triangular faces forming a regular tetrahedron; measuring point distances gives side length $\sqrt{8/3}\approx1.633$.
For 12 points all starts reach 49.165253058, with an icosahedral hull and 5 edges per point.
Five points form a triangular bipyramid with 2 poles and an equatorial equilateral triangle.
With seed 7 and 8 starts over 3–12 points, every start at each count reached the same value (differences around $10^{-14}$).
Still, energy differences across seeds may indicate distinct local configurations; inspect every start's value.
Equal energy can correspond to different coordinates under rotations or particle relabeling.

The hull triangulates the exact convex hull of computed coordinates; configurations with quadrilateral faces, such as an 8-point square antiprism, gain one diagonal per face.
Scene edges help view configurations and do not establish symmetry.

## Summary

Spherical coordinates incorporate spherical constraints into variables, allowing unconstrained BFGS for the Thomson problem.
Angular gradients are Cartesian-gradient inner products with $\partial r/\partial\theta$ and $\partial r/\partial\phi$; these coordinates degenerate at poles.
Compare 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12 points with antipodal/regular-polyhedron closed forms and other counts with literature, without interpreting differences smaller than final table digits.
Agreement across starts is an observation over tested starts, not proof of a global minimum.
