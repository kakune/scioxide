An elliptic equation's interior equation alone does not determine a solution; one condition over the entire boundary is needed for a solvable problem.
This page expresses three boundary types using one `scioxide-pde` convention and checks signs with a one-dimensional linear solution.
Notation follows diffusion equation $-(k u')'+c\,u=f$ derived in [Conservation laws and grid fields](../models-grids-and-boundaries/).

## Specifying boundary conditions

A boundary condition `BoundaryCondition<R, V, D, Region>` has four parts: region, field component, condition type, and data.

- Region `Region` is a `BoundarySet` of `scioxide-spatial` (such as `BoundarySet::axis_side(&mesh, axis, AxisSide::Start)`) on structured grids, or a `u32` tag on boundary faces of simplex meshes.
- Components default to all; `for_component(i)` applies only to component $i$.
- Type `BoundaryKind<V>` is `Dirichlet`, `Natural`, or `Robin { alpha }`, constructed by `BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(region, data)`, `natural(region, data)`, and `robin(region, alpha, data)`.
- Data `BoundaryData<R, V, D>` is a boundary function $g$.
  Its argument `BoundaryPoint` contains physical coordinates `point`, outward unit face normal `normal`, and component `component`.
  Declare time dependence at construction [as for sources](oxref:pde-field-data).

Data evaluation locations depend on discretization and condition type.
Dirichlet data are evaluated at boundary-face nodes for FDM and FEM (including P2 edge midpoints), and boundary-face centers for FVM.
Neumann and Robin data are evaluated at boundary vertices for FDM, face centers for FVM, and face quadrature points for FEM (the endpoints themselves in one dimension).

### Dirichlet boundary conditions

Also called first-kind conditions, these prescribe boundary values $u=g$.
FDM and FEM decouple fixed-value rows and columns, set their diagonals to 1, and move columns multiplied by known values to the right-hand side (symmetric elimination).
Symmetric problems retain symmetric assembled matrices.
FVM does not include boundary values as unknowns; it constructs two-point fluxes using known boundary-face values and adjacent cell values.

## Neumann conditions and outward signs

Also called second-kind conditions, these prescribe flux across the boundary.
For every discretization, `scioxide-pde` Neumann data (`BoundaryCondition::natural`) specify outward conormal flux $\mathbf{n}\cdot K\nabla u=g$.
Here $\mathbf{n}$ is the outward unit normal and $K$ the diffusion coefficient (scalar $k$ in one dimension); conormal flux multiplies normal derivative $\partial u/\partial n=\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla u$ by the diffusion coefficient.
It is not the normal derivative itself, so for $k\neq1$ pass $k\,\partial u/\partial n$.

On interval $[a,b]$, outward normals are $-1$ at the left and $+1$ at the right, giving endpoint data

$$
\left.k\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\right|_{x=a}=-k(a)\,u'(a)=g_a,\qquad \left.k\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}\right|_{x=b}=k(b)\,u'(b)=g_b
$$

Using rightward flux $q=-k\,u'$ gives $g_a=q(a)$ and $g_b=-q(b)$.
At either endpoint, $g>0$ represents the amount entering the domain through the boundary.
An incorrect left-end sign reverses inflow and outflow.
For multidimensional tensor diffusion $K$, also pass $(K\nabla u)\cdot\mathbf{n}$.
Neumann data contribute only to the load, not the matrix.
In the FEM weak form, these data enter the integration-by-parts boundary term directly, hence the name natural boundary conditions ([Finite-element weak form](oxref:pde-fem-weak-form)).

## Robin boundary conditions

Also called third-kind conditions, these prescribe a linear combination of outward conormal flux and value (`BoundaryCondition::robin(region, alpha, data)`).

$$
k\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}+\alpha\,u=g
$$

The flux coefficient is fixed to 1; users supply only $\alpha$ and $g$.
There is no zero-flux-coefficient form; prescribe values with `Dirichlet`.
Heat transfer $-k\,\partial u/\partial n=h\,(u-u_\infty)$ to surrounding fluid at temperature $u_\infty$ ($h$ is the heat transfer coefficient) corresponds to $\alpha=h$ and $g=h\,u_\infty$.
The limit $\alpha\to0$ gives Neumann conditions, while $\alpha\to\infty$ with fixed $g/\alpha$ gives Dirichlet $u=g/\alpha$.

For $\alpha>0$, boundary term $\alpha u^2$ adds to the energy and gives a unique solution even without reaction.
Boundary-set construction checks only that $\alpha$ is finite, rejecting nonfinite values with `NonFinite`.
Negative $\alpha$ is accepted, but uniqueness is then not guaranteed.
Complex $\alpha$ is accepted by FDM and FEM, while FVM requires real values.

## Boundary sets and checks

Plans take a boundary set `BoundaryConditions` tied to one mesh revision rather than individual conditions.
Use `StructuredBoundaries::new(&mesh, components, conditions)` for structured grids and `TaggedBoundaries::new(&mesh, &tags, components, conditions)` with face tags (`SimplexBoundaryTags`) for simplex meshes.
Both can be constructed with `uniform(&mesh, components, kind, data)` when every exterior face has the same condition.

Construction checks the following without calling data functions:

- Exactly one condition covers every exterior face and component.
  Missing or duplicate conditions are rejected with `InvalidInput`.
- Condition regions belong to the same mesh revision, include no interior or periodic-seam faces, and select at least one exterior face.
- Component indices are in range and Robin coefficients are finite.
- For simplex meshes, tags belong to the same revision and cover every exterior face.

Periodic boundaries are represented by mesh topology (`AxisTopology::Periodic`), not boundary conditions.
Periodic-seam faces do not accept conditions.
Only `StructuredFdmPlan` supports periodic structured grids; `StructuredFvmPlan` rejects periodic meshes.
Construction work and memory scale with exterior faces rather than the entire mesh.

In two or more dimensions, two Dirichlet faces can share a corner node while specifying different values.
Choose handling with `DirichletConflictPolicy`, configured through `with_dirichlet_conflict_policy` before Plan preparation.
Default `Reject` rejects preparation when the value difference exceeds machine-epsilon absolute and relative tolerances.
`LastConditionWins` gives precedence to later conditions and, within one condition, larger face indices.
This ordering is independent of cell traversal order or parallel execution.
Both policies also evaluate values that do not take precedence.
For FDM vertices shared by Dirichlet and Neumann or Robin faces, Dirichlet determines the value.

## Checking signs and Robin coefficients

Recover the steady linear solution $u(x)=2+3x$ on rod $[0,1]$ with conductivity $k=2$ using a left Neumann and right Robin condition.
The left outward normal is $-1$, giving outward conormal flux $k\,\partial u/\partial n=-6$ for $u'=3$.
At the right endpoint, $u(1)=5$ and $k\,\partial u/\partial n=6$, so the right-hand side for Robin coefficient $\alpha$ is $g=6+5\alpha$.
Input $\alpha$ changes the right-hand side together with $\alpha$ to match the same linear solution.
Consistent coefficient and right-hand side should yield the same solution regardless of $\alpha$.

The cell below prepares FDM, FVM, and P1 FEM Plans on a grid of 8 cells, solving with the same conditions.
All three discretizations represent a linear solution exactly, so errors should be at the rounding level.
Unknowns lie at vertices for FDM and FEM and cell centers for FVM; each location is read using `dof_point` for exact-solution comparison.
Compute flux from the first and last unknowns using $-k\,\Delta u/\Delta x$.

```rust
//| id: pde-robin-normal-derivative
//| ref: pde-robin-normal-derivative
//| title: "Check outward conormal flux and Robin coefficients with three discretizations"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   robin_alpha: {"type":"range","label":"Robin coefficient α [-]","description":"Right boundary reaction coefficient. Change g = 6 + 5α to match the same linear solution.","min":0.25,"max":4,"step":0.25,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"weak-exchange","label":"Weak heat exchange α = 0.25 (g = 7.25)","description":"Even near the Neumann limit, all three discretizations return the same linear solution and flux −6.","inputs":{"robin_alpha":0.25}}
//|   - {"id":"strong-exchange","label":"Strong heat exchange α = 4 (g = 26)","description":"Even near the Dirichlet limit, all three discretizations return the same linear solution and flux −6.","inputs":{"robin_alpha":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-6,"atol":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":-6,"atol":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":-6,"atol":1e-11}}
//|   - case: weak-exchange
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":-6,"atol":1e-11}}
//|   - case: strong-exchange
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"robin","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":-6,"atol":1e-11}}
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, ConstantDiffusion, EllipticProblem, FieldData,
    SimplexFemGeometry, SimplexFemOrder, SimplexFemPlan, SpatialDiscretization,
    StructuredBoundaries, StructuredFdmPlan, StructuredFvmPlan, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{
    AxisSide, AxisTopology, BoundarySet, CoordinateAxis, Grid1d, IntervalMesh, Point,
    SegmentMesh, SimplexBoundaryTags,
};

// Solve and return maximum error from exact u = 2 + 3x and rightward flux −k Δu/Δx.
fn check<P: SpatialDiscretization<f64, f64, 1>>(plan: &P) -> Result<(f64, f64), String> {
    let values = EllipticProblem::new(plan, &FieldData::zero())
        .solve()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut xs = Vec::new();
    for i in 0..values.len() {
        xs.push(plan.dof_point(i).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point[0]);
    }
    let error = (xs.iter().zip(&values))
        .map(|(x, u)| (u - (2.0 + 3.0 * x)).abs())
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
    let last = values.len() - 1;
    let flux = -2.0 * (values[last] - values[0]) / (xs[last] - xs[0]);
    Ok((error, flux))
}
let alpha = robin_alpha;
let k = ConstantDiffusion::new(2.0);
// Left: outward conormal flux n·k u' = −k u'(0) = −6. Right: k du/dn + αu = 6 + 5α.
let left = BoundaryData::constant(-6.0);
let right = BoundaryData::constant(6.0 + 5.0 * alpha);
let axis = CoordinateAxis::uniform(0.0, 1.0, 9).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let grid = Grid1d::new([axis], [AxisTopology::Bounded]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let segment = SegmentMesh::new(grid).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let side = |side| BoundarySet::axis_side(&segment, 0, side).map_err(|e| e.to_string());
let conditions = vec![
    BoundaryCondition::natural(side(AxisSide::Start)?, left.clone()),
    BoundaryCondition::robin(side(AxisSide::End)?, alpha, right.clone()),
];
let structured =
    StructuredBoundaries::new(&segment, 1, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fdm =
    StructuredFdmPlan::new(segment.clone(), &k, &structured).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fvm = StructuredFvmPlan::new(segment, &k, &structured).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Tag interval-mesh endpoint x = 0 with 0 and x = 1 with 1.
let points = (0..9)
    .map(|i| Point::new([i as f64 / 8.0]))
    .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let interval = IntervalMesh::new(points, (0..8).map(|i| [i, i + 1]).collect())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut faces = Vec::new();
for face in interval.boundary_faces() {
    let vertex = interval.face_vertices(face).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?[0];
    faces.push((face, u32::from(vertex != 0)));
}
let tags = SimplexBoundaryTags::new(&interval, faces).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let conditions = vec![
    BoundaryCondition::natural(0, left),
    BoundaryCondition::robin(1, alpha, right),
];
let tagged =
    TaggedBoundaries::new(&interval, &tags, 1, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let geometry = SimplexFemGeometry::Affine;
let fem = SimplexFemPlan::new(interval, geometry, SimplexFemOrder::P1, &k, &tagged)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rows = vec![
    ("FDM (vertices)", check(&fdm)?),
    ("FVM (cell centers)", check(&fvm)?),
    ("FEM P1 (vertices)", check(&fem)?),
];
let rows: Vec<_> = rows
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, (e, q))| (name, e, q))
    .collect();
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed9 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 9 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Discretization (unknown locations)" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum error from exact solution [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Rightward flux −k Δu/Δx [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed9 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "robin" })
);
```

For each of $\alpha=0.25$, 1, and 4, all three discretizations return flux $-6$; maximum exact-solution error is 0 for FDM and FEM and below $10^{-14}$ for FVM.
FVM has no boundary-value unknowns, but boundary-face flux formulas represent lines exactly, so cell-center values lie on the same line.
Mistaking the left data sign for $+6$ instead gives a different linear solution with slope $-3$.
This checks linear solutions and does not guarantee errors for general solutions.

## Solvability and compatibility of pure Neumann problems

Impose Neumann conditions $k\,\partial u/\partial n=g_a,g_b$ at both ends of $-(k u')'=f$ and integrate over the interval.
The left side becomes $-(k u')(b)+(k u')(a)=-g_b-g_a$, so existence requires the following equality.

$$
g_a+g_b+\int_a^b f\,dx=0
$$

If boundary inflow plus internal generation is not zero, heat accumulates or is continually lost, leaving no steady solution.
This is the compatibility condition.
Even when it holds, $u+C$ is a solution for every constant $C$, so uniqueness fails.
The discrete matrix has the constant vector in its null space.
The same holds when all Robin coefficients are zero, or for reaction-free periodic boundaries.

The Plan detects this structurally and reports it through `has_constant_modes`.
Detection requires every reaction coefficient to be zero (and all advection zero for FDM), with neither Dirichlet conditions nor nonzero Robin coefficients.
Then `EllipticProblem` rejects with `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`) unless a gauge (a condition fixing the solution constant) is specified through `EllipticOptions::with_gauge`.
[Compatibility conditions, gauges, and conservative elliptic fluxes](../compatibility-gauges-and-fluxes/) covers discrete compatibility checks, gauge types, and load projection.

## Summary

For every discretization, Neumann and Robin data in `scioxide-pde` use outward conormal flux $k\,\partial u/\partial n$, so pass $-k\,u'$ at the left endpoint.
Robin conditions have fixed flux coefficient 1 in $k\,\partial u/\partial n+\alpha u=g$; for heat transfer pass $\alpha=h$ and $g=h\,u_\infty$.
Boundary sets require one condition per exterior face and component; use `DirichletConflictPolicy` to choose handling of conflicting Dirichlet values at corners in two or more dimensions.
Problems with only flux boundaries and no reaction require compatible loads and a gauge.
