An elastic solid returns to its original shape when forces are removed; elasticity is used to compute deformation and stress in machine parts, buildings, soils, and rubber products.
This page derives the displacement-based boundary-value problem of linear elasticity, explains its finite-element (FEM) discretization, and describes volumetric locking in nearly incompressible materials and a mixed formulation that avoids it.
Neo-Hookean hyperelasticity for large deformations is covered in [Hyperelasticity and consistent tangents](../hyperelasticity/).
Plans use finite elements on simplex meshes (intervals, triangles, and tetrahedra), sharing mesh, boundary-condition, and quadrature settings with [Simplex-mesh finite elements](../simplex-finite-elements/).

## Strain, stress, and Hooke's law

Let $u(x)$ be displacement at each point $x$ of the body.
When displacement gradients are small, deformation is measured by the small-strain tensor

```text
ε(u) = (∇u + ∇uᵀ) / 2,   ε_ij = (∂u_i/∂x_j + ∂u_j/∂x_i) / 2
```


Rigid translations and infinitesimal rotations give $\varepsilon=0$.
Internal forces are represented by the Cauchy stress tensor $\sigma$; traction (force per unit area) on the side of a face with unit normal $n$ is $t=\sigma n$.
Static equilibrium under body force $f$ (force per unit volume) is

```text
−∇·σ = f   (in Ω)
```


For an isotropic linear elastic solid, two constants determine the stress–strain relation (Hooke's law).

```text
σ = 2μ ε + λ (tr ε) I = 2μ dev ε + K (tr ε) I,   dev ε = ε − (tr ε) I / 3
```

$\mu$ is the shear modulus, $\lambda$ the first Lamé parameter, $K=\lambda+2\mu/3$ the bulk modulus, and $\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon=\nabla\cdot u$ the relative volume change.
The mean stress $p=\operatorname{tr}\sigma/3=K\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon$ corresponds to pressure with tension taken as positive.
From Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$, the conversion is $\mu=E/(2(1+\nu))$ and $K=E/(3(1-2\nu))$.
Strain energy is positive for every nonzero strain when $\mu>0$ and $K>0$, equivalently $E>0$ and $-1<\nu<1/2$.

`IsotropicElasticMaterial` stores these two elastic moduli.
`new(shear, bulk)` requires positive finite $\mu$ and $K$ with representable positive $1/K$; `from_young_poisson(young, poisson)` checks $E>0$ and $-1<\nu<1/2$ and converts them.
Values violating these conditions are rejected with `InvalidInput`.
Exact incompressibility at $\nu=1/2$ corresponds to $K=\infty$ and cannot be represented by this material.

## Plane strain and plane stress

For a two-dimensional mesh, reduce the three-dimensional Hooke law by assuming a state in the thickness direction $z$.
`ElasticityKinematics` selects this reduction: two-dimensional meshes accept `PlaneStrain` or `PlaneStress`, and three-dimensional meshes accept only `ThreeDimensional`.
An incompatible combination makes plan preparation return `InvalidInput`.

For bodies long in the thickness direction with a uniform cross section (dams, tunnels, and long-beam cross sections), thickness-direction strain is taken to be 0.
This is plane strain (`PlaneStrain`); $\varepsilon_{zz}=0$ produces out-of-plane stress $\sigma_{zz}=\lambda(\varepsilon_{xx}+\varepsilon_{yy})$.

For thin plates, out-of-plane stress is taken to be 0.
This is plane stress (`PlaneStress`); $\sigma_{zz}=0$ determines the out-of-plane strain as

```text
ε_zz = −λ (ε_xx + ε_yy) / (λ + 2μ)
```


Substitution into the in-plane equations gives an in-plane Hooke law with the Lamé parameter replaced by $\lambda^*=2\mu\lambda/(\lambda+2\mu)$.
The plan obtains the same result by static condensation of the $\varepsilon_{zz}$ component from the three-dimensional tangent matrix (solving and eliminating that component's equation).
Plane strain is strongly constrained by volume as $\nu\to1/2$.
Plane stress allows out-of-plane contraction, so $\lambda^*$ never exceeds $2\mu$ even as $\lambda\to\infty$, and the volume constraint does not strengthen.

## Weak form and total potential energy

Split the boundary into $\Gamma_D$ with prescribed displacement $u=g$ and $\Gamma_N$ with prescribed traction $\sigma n=t$.
Multiplying equilibrium by a test function $v$ (0 on $\Gamma_D$) and integrating by parts gives the weak form

```text
a(u, v) = ℓ(v)   (for every v vanishing on Γ_D)
a(u, v) = ∫_Ω σ(u) : ε(v) dx,   ℓ(v) = ∫_Ω f·v dx + ∫_{Γ_N} t·v ds
```


$a$ is symmetric and is positive definite if $\Gamma_D$ restrains all rigid motion (Korn's inequality).
The weak solution then equals the displacement minimizing total potential energy $\Pi(u)=a(u,u)/2-\ell(u)$.

Approximating each displacement component with continuous piecewise P1 or P2 polynomials turns the weak form into the linear system $Au=F$.
Entries of the stiffness matrix $A$ are assembled by quadrature of $\int\varepsilon(\phi_i):C:\varepsilon(\phi_j)\,dx$ over each cell using the Hooke elasticity tensor $C$ ($\sigma=C:\varepsilon$).
For this volume integral, the plan uses quadrature exact for polynomials of at least twice the displacement degree with affine geometry, or at least degree 7 with quadratic geometry.
The default rule has positive weights and nodes inside the simplex; rules explicitly supplied through `SimplexFemOptions` must also be exact for polynomials of at least this degree.
Degrees of freedom on $\Gamma_D$ are eliminated symmetrically, preserving symmetry of the assembled matrix.

Specify boundary conditions with a two- or three-component `TaggedBoundaries`, giving exactly one condition for each exterior-face/component pair.
`BoundaryCondition::dirichlet` prescribes displacement component $u_a$, and `BoundaryCondition::natural` prescribes traction component $t_a=\sigma_{ab}n_b$.
Time-dependent data and Robin conditions are rejected.
Conflicting Dirichlet values at nodes shared by multiple faces are handled by `DirichletConflictPolicy` (rejection by default).

## Plans and unknown ordering

`LinearElasticityPlan` is a plan for isotropic Hooke elasticity.
Elastic moduli are real, while the displacement and load type `V` may be real or complex (amplitudes).
`new(mesh, geometry, material, &boundaries, body, options)` prepares sequentially, while `build(…, scope)` prepares as one epoch of the caller's scope; failure returns no plan.
Preparation computes and stores every cell's element matrix and loads from body force `body` and traction.

`ElasticityOptions::new(kinematics, formulation)` explicitly selects the reduction and element.
`with_fem` replaces shared simplex-mesh settings (`SimplexFemOptions` quadrature, geometry conditions, execution, and resource limits); `with_plane_stress` replaces local neo-Hookean plane-stress solve settings ([Hyperelasticity and consistent tangents](../hyperelasticity/)).
`ElasticityFormulation` is either `Displacement(order)` (P1 or P2 displacement alone) or `MixedP2P1` (P2 displacement and vertex P1 pressure); it also determines displacement degree, avoiding disagreement with a separately supplied FEM degree.
Quadratic geometry (`SimplexFemGeometry::Quadratic`) is accepted only with P2 displacement.

Unknowns are ordered in displacement-component blocks (each containing `displacement_nodes` nodes), followed by vertex pressures for mixed formulations.
`node_point(node)` returns a displacement node's physical coordinates.
`fields(x)` splits the unknown vector into an `ElasticityField`.
`ElasticityField` owns displacement fields by component (`displacement`) and vertex pressures (`pressure`), which are `Some` only for mixed formulations.
The reverse operation `pack_fields_into(view, out)` packs fields borrowed through `ElasticityFieldView::new(displacement, pressure)` from the same mesh revision into an unknown vector, leaving `out` unchanged on failure.

For assembly, `assemble_csr` returns the CSR matrix after symmetric elimination, and `rhs` returns the right-hand side with Dirichlet lifting.
Dirichlet rows become identity rows with prescribed values on the right-hand side, so the solution $x$ of $Ax=b$ contains all unknowns, including Dirichlet values.
For repeated assembly with the same structure, use `assemble_csr_into` with an `assembly_workspace` workspace to avoid allocation.
The plan itself implements `LinearOperator` (`apply_into`) and `AdjointOperator`, so it can be passed to Krylov methods without forming a matrix.
The plan has no solve procedure: solve a positive-definite displacement system with supports restraining rigid motion by conjugate gradients or sparse Cholesky factorization, and a symmetric indefinite mixed system by MINRES or sparse LU factorization.

The next block defines the rectangular-plate mesh and edge tags shared by this page's cells.

```rust
//| id: pde-elasticity-plate
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Triangular mesh and edge tags for a rectangular plate"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_pde::{BoundaryCondition, TaggedBoundaries};
use scioxide_spatial::{Point, SimplexBoundaryTags, TriangleMesh};

/// Divide rectangle [0, a] × [0, b] into nx × ny rectangles, each split into two triangles.
/// Vertex index: j (nx + 1) + i.
pub(crate) fn plate_mesh(
    a: f64,
    b: f64,
    nx: usize,
    ny: usize,
) -> Result<TriangleMesh<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..=ny {
        for i in 0..=nx {
            points.push(Point::new([
                a * i as f64 / nx as f64,
                b * j as f64 / ny as f64,
            ])?);
        }
    }
    let mut cells = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..ny {
        for i in 0..nx {
            let v = j * (nx + 1) + i;
            cells.push([v, v + 1, v + nx + 2]);
            cells.push([v, v + nx + 2, v + nx + 1]);
        }
    }
    TriangleMesh::new(points, cells)
}

/// Exterior-face tags: 1 for the left edge x = 0, 2 for the right edge x = a, 3 for the bottom edge y = 0, and 4 for the top edge y = b.
pub(crate) fn plate_boundaries(
    mesh: &TriangleMesh<f64>,
    a: f64,
    b: f64,
    conditions: Vec<BoundaryCondition<f64, f64, 2, u32>>,
) -> Result<TaggedBoundaries<f64, f64, 2>, SciOxideError> {
    let mut faces = Vec::new();
    for face in mesh.boundary_faces() {
        let v = mesh.face_vertices(face)?;
        let [x0, y0] = *mesh.points()[v[0]].as_array();
        let [x1, y1] = *mesh.points()[v[1]].as_array();
        let (x, y) = (0.5 * (x0 + x1), 0.5 * (y0 + y1));
        let tag = if x < 1e-9 * a {
            1
        } else if x > (1.0 - 1e-9) * a {
            2
        } else if y < 1e-9 * b {
            3
        } else {
            4
        };
        faces.push((face, tag));
    }
    let tags = SimplexBoundaryTags::new(mesh, faces)?;
    TaggedBoundaries::new(mesh, &tags, 2, conditions)
}
```

## Comparison with the analytical uniaxial-tension solution

Pull the right edge of a steel plate of length $a=200$ mm and width $b=100$ mm ($E=200$ GPa) with 100 MPa tension.
Roller supports impose $u_x=0$ on the left edge and $u_y=0$ on the bottom; other edges are unloaded.
Stress is uniform throughout the plate, with $\sigma_{xx}=100$ MPa and $\sigma_{xy}=\sigma_{yy}=0$; under plane stress,

```text
ε_xx = σ/E,   ε_yy = ε_zz = −νσ/E,   σ_zz = 0
```

and under plane strain,

```text
ε_xx = (1 − ν²) σ/E,   ε_yy = −ν(1 + ν) σ/E,   ε_zz = 0,   σ_zz = νσ
```


Both displacement fields are linear in coordinates, so even P1 elements represent them exactly.
A check that a mesh reproduces linear displacement exactly on arbitrary meshes is called a patch test.
Internal energy is $U=\sigma\varepsilon_{xx}ab/2$ (per 1 mm thickness), and traction work is $W_\mathrm{ext}=2U$.

The next cell solves the plate with selectable reduction, element, and Poisson ratio, then compares upper-right-corner displacement, stress and strain at cell 0's centroid, and energy with the analytical solution.
The matrix is solved with sparse LU factorization in `scioxide-sparse`.

```rust

//| title: "Solve a plate in uniaxial tension and compare displacement, stress, and energy with the analytical solution"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [pde-elasticity-plate]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-sparse, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   tension_kinematics: {"type":"select","label":"Two-dimensional reduction","description":"Choose zero out-of-plane stress or zero out-of-plane strain.","options":[{"label":"Plane stress","value":"stress"},{"label":"Plane strain","value":"strain"}],"value":"stress"}
//|   tension_formulation: {"type":"select","label":"Entry","description":"Choose displacement degree and mixed formulation.","options":[{"label":"P1 displacement","value":"p1"},{"label":"P2 displacement","value":"p2"},{"label":"Mixed P2/P1","value":"mixed"}],"value":"p1"}
//|   tension_poisson: {"type":"range","label":"Poisson ratio ν [-]","description":"Change transverse contraction and out-of-plane stress.","min":0.0,"max":0.49,"step":0.01,"value":0.3}

//|   - {"id":"plane-strain","label":"Plane strain","description":"σ_zz = νσ = 30 MPa develops, and u_x becomes (1 − ν²) times its plane-stress value, 0.091 mm.","inputs":{"tension_kinematics":"strain"}}
//|   - {"id":"mixed-near-incompressible","label":"Mixed P2/P1, ν = 0.49","description":"The mixed formulation also reproduces the analytical solution within roundoff.","inputs":{"tension_formulation":"mixed","tension_poisson":0.49}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tension","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.1,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tension","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":-0.015,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tension","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":-1.5e-4,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tension","path":"/rows/8/1","approx":{"value":1000,"rtol":1e-12}}

//|       - {"artifact":"tension","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":33.333333,"rtol":1e-7}}

    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, ElasticityFormulation, ElasticityKinematics,

// Steel plate, 200 mm × 100 mm (per 1 mm thickness), E = 200 GPa, with 100 MPa tension at the right edge.
let (a, b, young, load) = (200.0, 100.0, 200_000.0, 100.0);
let nu = tension_poisson;
let (nx, ny) = (4, 2);
let mesh = plate_mesh(a, b, nx, ny).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Rollers: u_x = 0 on the left, u_y = 0 on the bottom. Other components and the top edge have zero traction.
let conditions = vec![
    BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(1, BoundaryData::zero()).for_component(0),
    BoundaryCondition::natural(1, BoundaryData::zero()).for_component(1),
    BoundaryCondition::natural(2, BoundaryData::constant(load)).for_component(0),
    BoundaryCondition::natural(2, BoundaryData::zero()).for_component(1),
    BoundaryCondition::natural(3, BoundaryData::zero()).for_component(0),
    BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(3, BoundaryData::zero()).for_component(1),
    BoundaryCondition::natural(4, BoundaryData::zero()),
];
let boundaries = plate_boundaries(&mesh, a, b, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plane_strain = tension_kinematics == "strain";
let kinematics = if plane_strain {
    ElasticityKinematics::PlaneStrain
} else {
    ElasticityKinematics::PlaneStress
};
let formulation = match tension_formulation.as_str() {
    "p1" => ElasticityFormulation::Displacement(SimplexFemOrder::P1),
    "p2" => ElasticityFormulation::Displacement(SimplexFemOrder::P2),
    _ => ElasticityFormulation::MixedP2P1,
};

    IsotropicElasticMaterial::from_young_poisson(young, nu).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plan = LinearElasticityPlan::new(
    mesh,
    SimplexFemGeometry::Affine,
    material,
    &boundaries,
    &|_| Ok([0.0; 2]),
    ElasticityOptions::new(kinematics, formulation),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let x = scioxide_sparse::solve(&csr, plan.rhs()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Displacement at the upper-right corner (a, b) and stress at cell 0's centroid.
let corner = ny * (nx + 1) + nx;
let field = plan.fields(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let ux = field.displacement().vertices()[0].values()[corner];
let uy = field.displacement().vertices()[1].values()[corner];
let s = plan
    .stress(0, [1.0 / 3.0; 2], &x)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let energy = plan.energy(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Analytical solution (reference). Plane stress: σ_zz = 0; plane strain: ε_zz = 0.
let (exx, eyy, ezz, szz) = if plane_strain {
    (
        (1.0 - nu * nu) * load / young,
        -nu * (1.0 + nu) * load / young,
        0.0,
        nu * load,
    )
} else {
    (load / young, -nu * load / young, -nu * load / young, 0.0)
};
let internal = 0.5 * load * exx * a * b;
let rows = vec![
    ("u_x(a, b) [mm]", ux, exx * a),
    ("u_y(a, b) [mm]", uy, eyy * b),
    ("σ_xx [MPa]", s.cauchy()[0][0], load),
    ("σ_yy [MPa]", s.cauchy()[1][1], 0.0),
    ("σ_zz [MPa]", s.cauchy()[2][2], szz),
    ("ε_zz [-]", s.kinematic_tensor()[2][2], ezz),
    ("Mean stress p [MPa]", s.pressure(), (load + szz) / 3.0),
    ("Internal energy U [N·mm/mm]", energy.internal(), internal),
    (
        "External work W_ext [N·mm/mm]",
        energy.external_work(),
        2.0 * internal,
    ),
    (
        "Potential U − W_ext [N·mm/mm]",
        energy.potential(),
        -internal,
    ),
];
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity [units]" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Finite element", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Analytical", "type": "number", "format": sig6 }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "tension" })
);
```

With the default plane stress and P1 displacement, $u_x(a,b)=0.1$ mm, $u_y(a,b)=-0.015$ mm, and $\varepsilon_{zz}=-1.5\times10^{-4}$ match the analytical solution within roundoff.
Switching to plane strain produces $\sigma_{zz}=30$ MPa and multiplies $u_x$ by $(1-\nu^2)$ to give 0.091 mm.
Mean stress also changes to $(\sigma+\sigma_{zz})/3=43.33$ MPa.
The energy rows satisfy $W_\mathrm{ext}=2U$ and potential $U-W_\mathrm{ext}=-U$.
Every element reproduces uniform stress, so element differences do not appear in this table.
They appear in bending problems with spatially varying stress ([Cantilever deflection and locking](../../examples/cantilever-beam/)).

## Energy and stress output

The function `energy(x)` returns `ElasticityEnergy`.
`internal` is $x^{\mathsf H}Ax/2$ from the element matrices (before elimination, so Dirichlet values are included); `external_work` is the product $\operatorname{Re}(x^{\mathsf H}F)$ with the load $F$ before elimination; `potential` is their difference $U-W_\mathrm{ext}$.
In a mixed formulation, `internal` is the saddle-point functional value described below and need not be positive.
These are values at the supplied state, not proofs of convergence or spatial error.

`stress(cell, point, x)` returns `ElasticityStress` for a specified cell and point in reference coordinates.
Reference coordinates are coordinates on the reference simplex; for a triangle, $(1/3,1/3)$ is its centroid.
`kinematic_tensor` is the symmetric small strain for Hooke elasticity and the deformation gradient $F$ for neo-Hookean elasticity; `cauchy` is Cauchy stress and `first_piola` is first Piola–Kirchhoff stress (equal to Cauchy stress for Hooke elasticity).
Tensors are always 3×3, including the condensed $\varepsilon_{zz}$ for plane stress or $\sigma_{zz}$ for plane strain.
`pressure` is the interpolated pressure unknown in a mixed formulation or the constitutive mean stress in a displacement formulation ($K\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon$ for Hooke elasticity, $K(J-1)$ for neo-Hookean elasticity).
With P1 displacement, stress is constant within each cell; with P2 it is linear, so quadrature points or centroids are natural locations for reading stress.

## Rigid-body modes and supports

The stiffness matrix is singular if $\Gamma_D$ does not restrain rigid motion (two translations and one rotation in two dimensions; three translations and three rotations in three dimensions).
Unrestrained rigid motions are rigid-body modes and span the matrix nullspace.
With a nullspace, the equation has a solution only if the load is orthogonal to every rigid-body mode ($F^{\mathsf T}m=0$, force and moment balance), and the solution is nonunique up to rigid-body modes ([Compatibility conditions, gauges, and conservative fluxes](../compatibility-gauges-and-fluxes/)).

`rigid_modes(tolerance, max_modes)` constructs translation and rotation fields for each mesh connected component and returns combinations whose Gram-matrix singular values over Dirichlet-fixed degrees of freedom are at most `tolerance` as rigid-body modes compatible with the supports.
The basis in the returned `ElasticityRigidModes` consists of columns orthonormal in the Euclidean inner product; `nullity` is the column count, and `basis` is a column-major `BlockVectorView`.
It returns `InvalidInput` if `max_modes` is less than the product of the connected-component count and $D(D+1)/2$, or if the tolerance makes the support classification ambiguous.
Classification uses support positions alone and does not guarantee a complete operator kernel.
Pass the basis to `scioxide_linalg::BorderedSystem` to check that the operator annihilates it, verify load compatibility and the caller-selected gauge, and solve the gauge-constrained augmented system.

The next cell varies supports in four ways on the same plate as the tension example, computing rigid-body mode counts, operator action $\max_k\|Am_k\|_\infty$, and load inner products $\max_k|F^{\mathsf T}m_k|$.
The right-edge load is 100 MPa in the $x$ direction.

```rust
//| id: pde-elasticity-rigid-modes
//| ref: pde-elasticity-rigid-modes
//| title: "Count rigid-body modes for each support combination and check their relation to the operator and load"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [pde-elasticity-plate]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":2581.9889,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"modes","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":3}
use scioxide_core::{LinearOperator, Tolerance};
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, ElasticityFormulation, ElasticityKinematics,
    ElasticityOptions, IsotropicElasticMaterial, LinearElasticityPlan, SimplexFemGeometry,
    SimplexFemOrder,
};

let (a, b) = (200.0, 100.0);
let material =
    IsotropicElasticMaterial::from_young_poisson(200_000.0, 0.3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = ElasticityOptions::new(
    ElasticityKinematics::PlaneStress,
    ElasticityFormulation::Displacement(SimplexFemOrder::P1),
);
let tolerance = Tolerance::new(1e-10, 1e-10).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
// (name, fix left-edge u_x, fix bottom-edge u_y). Apply 100 MPa in the x direction at the right edge.
let supports = [
    ("Left and bottom rollers", true, true),
    ("Left roller only", true, false),
    ("Bottom roller only", false, true),
    ("No supports", false, false),
];
for (name, left, bottom) in supports {
    let side = |fixed: bool, tag: u32, component: usize| {
        if fixed {
            BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(tag, BoundaryData::zero()).for_component(component)
        } else {
            BoundaryCondition::natural(tag, BoundaryData::zero()).for_component(component)
        }
    };
    let conditions = vec![
        side(left, 1, 0),
        side(false, 1, 1),
        BoundaryCondition::natural(2, BoundaryData::constant(100.0)).for_component(0),
        side(false, 2, 1),
        side(false, 3, 0),
        side(bottom, 3, 1),
        BoundaryCondition::natural(4, BoundaryData::zero()),
    ];
    let mesh = plate_mesh(a, b, 4, 2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let boundaries = plate_boundaries(&mesh, a, b, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let plan = LinearElasticityPlan::new(
        mesh,
        SimplexFemGeometry::Affine,
        material,
        &boundaries,
        &|_| Ok([0.0; 2]),
        options.clone(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Two-dimensional rigid motion has two translations and one rotation, so a three-column limit suffices.
    let modes = plan.rigid_modes(tolerance, 3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let basis = modes.basis().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut image = vec![0.0; plan.unknowns()];
    let mut work = vec![0.0; plan.apply_workspace_len().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?];
    let (mut action, mut compatibility) = (None::<f64>, None::<f64>);
    for k in 0..modes.nullity() {
        let mode = basis.column(k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        plan.apply_into(mode, &mut image, &mut work)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let norm = image.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, v| m.max(v.abs()));
        let dot: f64 = mode.iter().zip(plan.load()).map(|(m, f)| m * f).sum();
        action = Some(action.unwrap_or(0.0).max(norm));
        compatibility = Some(compatibility.unwrap_or(0.0).max(dot.abs()));
    }
    rows.push((name, modes.nullity(), action, compatibility));
}
```

With left and bottom rollers, there are no rigid-body modes and the matrix is positive definite.
With only the left roller, translation in the $y$ direction remains; the load is in the $x$ direction, so $F^{\mathsf T}m=0$ and compatibility holds.
A solution exists but is not unique, so a gauge fixes translation.
With only the bottom roller, translation in the $x$ direction remains and is not orthogonal to the right-edge load, giving $|F^{\mathsf T}m|=2582$ N/mm; no equilibrium solution exists.
This is the resultant force $100\times100=10^4$ N/mm per 1 mm thickness multiplied by the translation's nodal value $1/\sqrt{15}$ normalized to Euclidean norm 1 (15 vertices).
With no supports, all three modes remain.

## Volumetric locking and mixed formulations

As $\nu\to1/2$, $K/\mu\to\infty$, and the energy term $K(\nabla\cdot u)^2/2$ forces displacement to satisfy $\nabla\cdot u\approx0$.
If the discrete displacement space contains few divergence-free functions, this constraint almost prevents displacement, driving the solution toward 0.
This is called volumetric locking.
Continuous piecewise linear displacement (P1) is particularly affected; higher-order elements can also lock on some meshes (Brenner–Scott).

The mixed formulation treats mean stress $p$ as a separate unknown and imposes the volume constraint weakly.
The plan uses the energy density

```text
ψ(ε, p) = μ dev ε : dev ε + p tr ε − p² / (2K)
```

Making it stationary with respect to $p$ gives $p=K\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon$, recovering the original $\mu\,\mathrm{dev}\,\varepsilon:\mathrm{dev}\,\varepsilon+K(\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon)^2/2$.
Discretization gives the saddle-point system

```text
[ A_d   Bᵀ       ] [ u ]   [ F ]
[ B    −M_p / K  ] [ p ] = [ 0 ]
```


$A_d$ is the deviatoric stiffness, $B$ the divergence matrix, and $M_p$ the pressure mass matrix.
As $K\to\infty$, the bottom-right block approaches 0, but if the displacement and pressure spaces satisfy the inf-sup condition

```text
inf_q sup_v (q, ∇·v) / (‖q‖ ‖v‖₁) ≥ β > 0   (β is independent of the mesh)
```

the solution is stable independently of $K$ (Brezzi's theory).
`MixedP2P1` pairs continuous P2 displacement with vertex P1 pressure (Taylor–Hood elements), satisfying this condition under mild mesh assumptions (Boffi–Brezzi–Fortin).
Pressure is positive in tension and receives no Dirichlet conditions.
The matrix is symmetric indefinite, so conjugate gradients and Cholesky factorization, which require positive definiteness, cannot be used.

The [cantilever example](oxref:pde-cantilever-locking-section) compares deflection and mean stress in displacement and mixed formulations from $\nu=0.3$ to 0.4999.

## Summary

For linear elasticity, choose $\mu$ and $K$ in `IsotropicElasticMaterial`, a reduction in `ElasticityKinematics`, and an element in `ElasticityFormulation` to prepare `LinearElasticityPlan`; solve the `assemble_csr` and `rhs` system with conjugate gradients or Cholesky for displacement formulations, or MINRES or LU for mixed formulations.
Uniform stress represented by linear displacement is reproduced within roundoff by every element.
Use `rigid_modes` to determine whether supports restrain rigid motion; if modes remain, verify load compatibility and a gauge before solving.
For materials with $\nu$ near 1/2, choose `MixedP2P1` to avoid volumetric locking.
Read energy and stress through `energy` and `stress`; energy balance helps check boundary-condition treatment.
