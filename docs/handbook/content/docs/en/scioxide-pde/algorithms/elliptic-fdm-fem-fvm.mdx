Steady diffusion has no time dependence, so boundary data and interior sources determine the solution over the entire domain simultaneously.
Discretization produces a single linear system $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{b}$ connecting all unknowns.
This page derives finite-difference (FDM), finite-element (FEM), and finite-volume (FVM) matrices and loads from a one-dimensional elliptic equation, and measures convergence orders with manufactured solutions (choosing a solution first and deriving its source).
Unknown locations are covered in [Conservation laws and fields on grids](../models-grids-and-boundaries/), boundary conventions in [Boundary conditions and outward signs](../boundary-conditions/), and the resulting linear solves in [Solving elliptic linear systems](../elliptic-linear-solves/).

## Standard steady boundary-value problem

Consider a linear elliptic equation on the interval $\Omega=[a,b]$.

$$
-\frac{d}{dx}\left(k(x)\frac{du}{dx}\right)+c(x)\,u=f(x),\qquad x\in(a,b)
$$

$k(x)>0$ is the diffusion coefficient (such as thermal conductivity), $c(x)$ the reaction coefficient (such as heat loss to the surroundings), and $f(x)$ production per unit length (such as internal heating).
Impose Dirichlet, Neumann, or Robin conditions at both ends.
The solution is unique if $c\ge0$ and either $c$ is positive on an interval or at least one end has a Dirichlet condition or a Robin condition with $\alpha>0$.
Otherwise, the solution is determined only up to a constant ([pure Neumann problems](oxref:pde-pure-neumann-compatibility)).
`scioxide-pde` also accepts sign-changing $c$ when finite, but the matrix is then not guaranteed to be nonsingular.

## Specifying coefficients

A plan samples coefficients and assembles the matrix during preparation, without calling coefficients during solves.
Coefficients are supplied through discretization-specific traits with different sampling locations.

| Trait | Diffusion evaluation location | Reaction evaluation location | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| `StructuredFdmCoefficients` | Interval (face) midpoints; scalars by axis and component | Vertices; includes coupling between components | `DomainError` for negative diffusion |
| `FvmCoefficients` | Cell centers; $D\times D$ tensors | Cell centers | `DomainError` if the tensor is not positive definite |
| `SimplexFemCoefficients` | Element quadrature points; component and direction tensors | Element quadrature points | `DomainError` if the tensor is not positive definite |

`StructuredFdmCoefficients` can also return a conservative advection velocity `advection`.
Two common forms have types implementing all three traits.

- `ConstantDiffusion::new(k)` returns constant diffusion $k$; `with_reaction(c)` adds constant reaction $c$.
  It advertises spatial constancy to finite elements (`is_spatially_constant`), so coefficients are evaluated once per plan rather than at every quadrature point.
- `IsotropicDiffusion::new(|x: [f64; D]| Ok(k(x)))` returns isotropic diffusion as a function of position, with zero reaction.
  Each evaluation calls the function once and allocates no memory.

Neither type validates its values; the plan validates them when sampling.

## Conservative finite-difference stencil

Let the grid points be $a=x_0<x_1<\dots<x_N=b$, adjacent spacings $h_{i+1/2}=x_{i+1}-x_i$, and the control width of grid point $x_i$ be $w_i=(x_{i+1}-x_{i-1})/2$.
For spatially varying coefficients, expanding $(ku')'=ku''+k'u'$ and differencing it breaks conservation because neighboring points no longer share a flux.
Instead, construct an interval flux with $k_{i+1/2}=k(x_{i+1/2})$ sampled at the interval midpoint $x_{i+1/2}$.

$$
q_{i+1/2}=-k_{i+1/2}\frac{u_{i+1}-u_i}{h_{i+1/2}}
$$

At interior grid points, balance flux differences with reaction and source over the control width.

$$
q_{i+1/2}-q_{i-1/2}+c(x_i)\,u_i\,w_i=f(x_i)\,w_i
$$

Matrix row $i$ is tridiagonal, with diagonal $k_{i-1/2}/h_{i-1/2}+k_{i+1/2}/h_{i+1/2}+c(x_i)w_i$ and two neighboring entries $-k_{i\mp1/2}/h_{i\mp1/2}$.
Reaction and source are sampled at vertices and multiplied by control width (lumping).
Thus, one-dimensional `StructuredFdmPlan` produces the same linear system as P1 finite elements with midpoint-integrated stiffness and lumped reaction and load.

On uniform grids and smoothly varying grids whose neighboring spacings differ by $O(h^2)$, interior local truncation error is $O(h^2)$.
On grids with irregularly varying spacing, the local truncation error becomes $O(h)$.
This is a property of a refining grid family and cannot be determined from one grid, so plans do not report an order.

Endpoint Neumann and Robin conditions use balance over a half interval of width $h_{1/2}/2$.
The left-end equation is $q_{1/2}-(g_a-\alpha u_0)+c(x_0)\,u_0\,h_{1/2}/2=f(x_0)\,h_{1/2}/2$, where the parenthesized expression is flux entering from the boundary.
Here $g_a$ and $\alpha$ are that end's Robin datum and coefficient; for Neumann conditions, set $\alpha=0$.
Dividing this relation by the half width reduces local residual order by one, but solution convergence remains $O(h^2)$ for smooth coefficients and a stable boundary-value problem.

## Finite-element weak form and bases

Multiply the equation by a test function $v\in H^1(\Omega)$ (the space where $v$ and its derivative are square integrable), integrate over the interval, and integrate the diffusion term by parts.
Substituting the outward conormal flux $-k\,u'(a)=g_a$ at the left end and $k\,u'(b)=g_b$ at the right gives the following **weak form**.

$$
\int_a^b k\,u'v'\,dx+\int_a^b c\,u\,v\,dx=\int_a^b f\,v\,dx+g_a\,v(a)+g_b\,v(b)
$$

Neumann data enter the right-hand boundary terms directly; these are called natural boundary conditions because the weak form imposes them naturally.
For the Robin condition $k\,\partial u/\partial n+\alpha u=g$, the boundary term is $(g-\alpha u)v$, and $\alpha\,u\,v$ moves to the left-hand side.
Dirichlet conditions remove boundary degrees of freedom from the test space and impose known values by lifting; these are called essential boundary conditions.

`SimplexFemPlan` uses piecewise linear hat functions (`SimplexFemOrder::P1`) or piecewise quadratic bases (`P2`) on each element $e=[x_i,x_{i+1}]$ of an `IntervalMesh`.
Expanding the solution as $u_h=\sum_j u_j\phi_j$ and taking $v=\phi_i$ gives $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{b}$ by summing element matrices and loads.
For P1 with element length $h_e$, the local matrices and load are the following integrals.

$$
(\mathbf{K}_e)_{ij}=\int_e k\,\phi_i'\phi_j'\,dx=\frac{\pm1}{h_e^2}\int_e k\,dx,\qquad (\mathbf{M}_e)_{ij}=\int_e c\,\phi_i\phi_j\,dx,\qquad (\mathbf{f}_e)_i=\int_e f\,\phi_i\,dx
$$

$\pm$ is $+$ on the diagonal and $-$ off the diagonal.
For elliptic problems, the reaction matrix uses this integral without lumping.

Quadrature has positive weights and nodes inside the element.
For elements of degree $p$, default quadrature is exact through at least degree $2p$ (degree 7 for curved elements).
In one dimension, this is the Gauss–Legendre rule with the fewest nodes: a two-point rule for P1 (exact through degree 3) or a three-point rule for P2 (exact through degree 5).
For P1, quadrature exact through degree $d$ integrates polynomial diffusion coefficients through degree $d$, reaction coefficients through degree $d-2$, and sources through degree $d-1$ exactly.
Use `SimplexFemOptions::with_volume_rule` to select higher-degree quadrature.
For spatially constant coefficients such as `ConstantDiffusion` and affine elements, element matrices are constructed from reference-element integrals.

With exact integration, $c\ge0$, and the uniqueness conditions above, the FEM solution minimizes error in the energy norm $\|v\|_E=(\int_a^b k\,|v'|^2+c\,|v|^2\,dx)^{1/2}$ within the selected element space (Galerkin orthogonality).
For one-dimensional P1 with constant $k$ and $c=0$, nodal values match the exact solution when the load is integrated exactly (because the Green function is piecewise linear).
The default two-point rule leaves load quadrature error, and nodal error decreases as $O(h^4)$ ([Poisson grid convergence](../../examples/poisson-grid/)).

## Finite-volume balance and two-point flux approximation

The finite-volume method forms equations from the conservation law integrated over each cell $C_i=[x_{i-1/2},x_{i+1/2}]$.

$$
q(x_{i+1/2})-q(x_{i-1/2})+\int_{C_i}c\,u\,dx=\int_{C_i}f\,dx
$$

Unknowns are cell averages $u_i$, and diffusion $k_i$ evaluated at the cell center represents that cell's material.
Write the flux at face $x_{i+1/2}$ assuming a linear function in each half cell on either side.
Let $d_L$ and $d_R$ be distances from cell centers to the face and $u_f$ the face value; fluxes through the two half cells must agree.

$$
-k_L\frac{u_f-u_L}{d_L}=-k_R\frac{u_R-u_f}{d_R}
$$

Solving and eliminating $u_f$ gives the two-point flux approximation (TPFA).

$$
T_{i+1/2}=\frac{1}{d_L/k_L+d_R/k_R},\qquad
q_{i+1/2}=-T_{i+1/2}\,(u_{i+1}-u_i)
$$

$T$ is the reciprocal of the two half-cell thermal resistances $d/k$ connected in series: $k/(d_L+d_R)$ in uniform material, or a weighted harmonic mean when material changes at the face.
Reaction and source are sampled at cell centers and multiplied by volume (the midpoint rule).
At boundary faces, construct a half-cell flux from the Dirichlet value and neighboring cell value, or use the Neumann or Robin face equation.
Plan preparation rejects nonpositive half-cell conductance with `NumericalInstability`.

Flux $q_{i+1/2}$ leaving cell $C_i$ enters neighboring cell $C_{i+1}$ with the same value.
Thus, summing all cell equations cancels interior face fluxes, leaving only boundary flux and total production.

$$
q_{\mathrm{right}}-q_{\mathrm{left}}+\sum_i\int_{C_i}c\,u\,dx=\sum_i\int_{C_i}f\,dx
$$

This conservation holds within roundoff regardless of grid coarseness.
Conservation is separate from accuracy; cell values are only midpoint-rule approximations of cell averages.
`StructuredFvmPlan::face_fluxes` and `SimplexFvmPlan::face_fluxes` reconstruct face fluxes from the solution, allowing direct balance checks ([Interface flux checks](oxref:pde-interface-flux-check)).
In one dimension, FDM, FVM, and P1 FEM matrices are all tridiagonal.
However, plans on simplex meshes (`IntervalMesh`) order unknowns by vertex or cell index, so they are tridiagonal only when vertices and cells are numbered from left to right.

## Checking convergence orders with manufactured solutions

Measure error reduction under grid refinement with a problem whose exact solution is known.
For the analytical solution $u_*(x)=\sin(m\pi x)$, solve $-u''=(m\pi)^2\sin(m\pi x)$ with zero Dirichlet conditions at both ends.
For the central-difference operator on a uniform grid, $\sin(m\pi x_i)$ is an exact eigenvector with the following discrete eigenvalue.

$$
\lambda_h=\frac{4}{h^2}\sin^2\left(\frac{m\pi h}{2}\right)
$$

One-dimensional `StructuredFdmPlan` samples the source at vertices and multiplies it by control width, so the discrete solution on a uniform grid is $((m\pi)^2/\lambda_h)\sin(m\pi x_i)$.
The nodal error is $((m\pi)^2/\lambda_h-1)\,|\sin(m\pi x_i)|$, whose maximum can be predicted in closed form.
The cell below solves with `StructuredFdmPlan` and `EllipticProblem` for 25 interval counts $N=8,16,\dots,200$ and compares maximum nodal error with the closed-form prediction.
The [Poisson grid-convergence example](../../examples/poisson-grid/) compares all three methods, including FEM and FVM.

```rust
//| id: pde-elliptic-spatial-order
//| ref: pde-elliptic-spatial-order
//| title: "Vary solution wave number to measure central-difference grid convergence"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   mode_number: {"type":"range","label":"Sine mode m [-]","description":"Change the manufactured solution's spatial wave number.","min":1,"max":8,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fundamental","label":"Fundamental mode m = 1","description":"The finest grid has 400 points per wavelength, and the observed order is nearly 2.","inputs":{"mode_number":1}}
//|   - {"id":"short-wave","label":"Short wave m = 8","description":"At 50 points per wavelength, error increases to 1.3×10⁻³, and observed order 2.05 differs from the asymptotic value.","inputs":{"mode_number":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.8507563e-4,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.8507563e-4,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":2.0002,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"tridiagonal-direct"}
//|   - case: fundamental
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":2.0561929e-5,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":2.00003,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: short-wave
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.3143882e-3,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.3143882e-3,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"order","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":2.050,"atol":2e-3}}
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryData, BoundaryKind, ConstantDiffusion, EllipticOptions, EllipticProblem, FieldData,
    FieldPoint, SpatialDiscretization, StructuredBoundaries, StructuredFdmPlan,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{AxisTopology, CoordinateAxis, Grid1d, SegmentMesh};

let mode = mode_number.round();
let wavenumber = mode * std::f64::consts::PI;
// Source f = (mπ)² sin(mπx) for the manufactured solution u = sin(mπx) (using f64::sin to define data).
let source = FieldData::time_invariant(move |p: FieldPoint<f64, 1>| {
    Ok(wavenumber * wavenumber * (wavenumber * p.point[0]).sin())
});
let k = ConstantDiffusion::new(1.0);
let mut measured = Vec::new();
let mut predicted = Vec::new();
let mut method = String::new();
for intervals in (8..=200).step_by(8) {
    let axis = CoordinateAxis::uniform(0.0, 1.0, intervals + 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let grid = Grid1d::new([axis], [AxisTopology::Bounded]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mesh = SegmentMesh::new(grid).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let ends =
        StructuredBoundaries::uniform(&mesh, 1, BoundaryKind::Dirichlet, BoundaryData::zero())
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let plan = StructuredFdmPlan::new(mesh, &k, &ends).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let solution = EllipticProblem::new(&plan, &source)
        .solve_with_options(&EllipticOptions::new())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let report = solution.report();
    if !report.is_success() {
        return Err(format!("N = {intervals}: {}", report.termination()));
    }
    method = report.diagnostics().method().unwrap_or("-").to_owned();
    let mut maximum_error = 0.0_f64;
    for (node, u) in solution.values().iter().enumerate() {
        let x = plan.dof_point(node).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point[0];
        maximum_error = maximum_error.max((u - (wavenumber * x).sin()).abs());
    }
    // Independent check: product of the amplitude ratio (mπ)²/λ_h and the maximum grid-point |sin(mπx_i)|.
    let h = 1.0 / intervals as f64;
    let discrete_eigenvalue = 4.0 / (h * h) * (wavenumber * h / 2.0).sin().powi(2);
    let peak = (0..=intervals)
        .map(|i| (wavenumber * i as f64 * h).sin().abs())
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
    let closed_form = (wavenumber * wavenumber / discrete_eigenvalue - 1.0) * peak;
    measured.push([intervals as f64, maximum_error]);
    predicted.push([intervals as f64, closed_form]);
}
let count = measured.len();
let (coarse, finest) = (measured[count - 2][1], measured[count - 1][1]);
let observed_order = (coarse / finest).ln() / (200.0_f64 / 192.0).ln();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Prediction from discrete eigenvalues",
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" },
            "points": predicted
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Maximum nodal error",
            "style": { "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true },
            "points": measured
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Grid interval count N [-]",
        "yLabel": "Maximum nodal error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let sci4 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 4 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Mode m", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Points per wavelength (N = 200)", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum error (N = 200) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Closed-form error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Observed order (N = 192→200)", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Solver" }
]);
let rows = vec![(
    mode as u32,
    400.0 / mode,
    finest,
    predicted[count - 1][1],
    observed_order,
    method,
)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "order" })
);
```

On the log–log chart, error points lie on a line of slope $-2$; halving grid width $h$ reduces error to $1/4$ of its previous value.
At the default $m=3$, maximum error on the finest grid $N=200$ was $1.851\times10^{-4}$, differing from the closed-form prediction by less than $10^{-13}$.
The observed order is 2.0002.
The expansion $\lambda_h=(m\pi)^2\bigl(1-(m\pi h)^2/12+O(h^4)\bigr)$ gives leading relative error $(m\pi h)^2/12$.
Increasing mode number $m$ reduces the grid points per wavelength $P=2/(mh)$ on the same grid, increasing the error level.
At $m=8$, even the finest grid has $P=50$, error $1.31\times10^{-3}$, and observed order 2.05, so it has not fully entered the asymptotic regime.
`tridiagonal-direct` in the solver column indicates a tridiagonal direct solve of the one-dimensional FDM matrix.

## Summary

FDM uses conservative differences with midpoint-sampled coefficients; FEM integrates a weak form with positive-weight quadrature; FVM uses two-point fluxes from half-cell series resistances with cell-centered coefficients.
One-dimensional FDM, P1 FEM, and FVM all give tridiagonal matrices when nodes are ordered from left to right.
Supply coefficients through `ConstantDiffusion`, `IsotropicDiffusion`, or discretization-specific traits; sampling occurs at interval midpoints for FDM, cell centers for FVM, and quadrature points for FEM.
FDM accuracy depends on grid-family regularity; P1 FEM matches the exact solution at nodes with exactly integrated loads when $k$ is constant and $c=0$.
FVM conservation is separate from accuracy, and reconstructed face fluxes allow balance checks.
Algebraic residuals do not measure discretization error, so measure orders with manufactured solutions or grid refinement.
