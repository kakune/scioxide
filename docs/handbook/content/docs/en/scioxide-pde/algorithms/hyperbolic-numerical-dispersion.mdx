Even when the wave equation is solved with a stable step, wave speeds are not reproduced exactly.
A spatial grid slows shorter wavelengths more, and the delay accumulates in proportion to propagation time.

This page derives the semidiscrete dispersion relation for a wave operator discretized by second-order central differences on a uniform grid and expresses phase- and group-velocity errors in terms of grid points per wavelength.
It then compares how time integration methods (Dormand–Prince 5(4) and leapfrog) contribute to dispersion and lists quantities to check in long-time computations.
The semidiscrete system, Courant number $\nu$, and stability function $R(z)$ are defined in [The wave equation and CFL conditions](../hyperbolic-waves-and-cfl/).

## Numerical dispersion of the semidiscrete wave operator

Discretizing constant-coefficient $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$ with second-order central differences on a uniform grid and substituting a discrete plane wave $u_j(t)=e^{\mathrm{i}(jkh-\omega t)}$ gives the semidiscrete dispersion relation.

$$
\omega_h(k)=\frac{2c}{h}\sin\frac{kh}{2}
$$

$k$ is wave number, $\theta=kh$ is wave number measured in grid widths, and $P=2\pi/\theta$ is grid points per wavelength.
Compared with the true dispersion relation $\omega=ck$, the ratios of phase velocity $\omega_h/k$ and group velocity $d\omega_h/dk$ to the true speed are as follows.

$$
\frac{c_{\mathrm{phase}}}{c}=\frac{\sin(\theta/2)}{\theta/2}=1-\frac{\theta^2}{24}+O(\theta^4),\qquad\frac{c_{\mathrm{group}}}{c}=\cos\frac{\theta}{2}
$$

Both are below 1, with greater delay at shorter wavelengths.
Individual phases travel at phase velocity; a wave packet's envelope and energy travel at group velocity.
When a pulse containing different wavelengths propagates, short-wavelength components fall behind, leaving a tail of small grid-scale waves behind the waveform.
This is called numerical dispersion.
Phase-velocity error is approximately $\theta^2/24=\pi^2/(6P^2)$, so keeping it within 1% requires $P\ge13$.

Discretizing time makes the dispersion relation depend on the time integration method as well.
The factor multiplying a mode of eigenvalue $\pm\mathrm{i}\,\omega_h$ in one step is $R(\pm\mathrm{i}\,y)$, with $y=\omega_h\Delta t=2\nu\sin(\theta/2)$.
For real-coefficient $R$, $R(-\mathrm{i}y)$ is the complex conjugate of $R(\mathrm{i}y)$, so the numerical angular frequency is $\arg R(\mathrm{i}y)/\Delta t$ and the one-step amplitude ratio is $\lvert R(\mathrm{i}y)\rvert$.
For comparison, consider leapfrog $(u_j^{n+1}-2u_j^n+u_j^{n-1})/\Delta t^2=c^2(u_{j+1}^n-2u_j^n+u_{j-1}^n)/h^2$, using central differences in both time and space.
Its dispersion relation is $\sin(\omega\Delta t/2)=\nu\sin(\theta/2)$, and amplitude is unchanged when $\nu\le1$.
`scioxide-pde` does not provide leapfrog, so it is used only as a closed-form comparison.

The next cell evaluates the three dispersion relations in closed form.
Inputs are Courant number $\nu$, grid points per wavelength $P$, and propagation period count; this represents Dormand–Prince 5(4) with steps maintaining constant $\nu$.
Charts show phase- and group-velocity ratios against $P$; the table shows speed ratios at the selected $P$, amplitude ratio and phase lag after the specified periods, and one-step amplitude ratio for the shortest wave ($\theta=\pi$).
The [fixed-end string example](../../examples/fixed-string-wave/) measures harmonic phase lag from an actual `HyperbolicProblem` solve.

```rust
//| id: pde-wave-dispersion
//| ref: pde-wave-dispersion
//| title: "Vary Courant number and grid points per wavelength to compare dispersion relations"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   cfl_number: {"type":"range","label":"Courant number ν = cΔt/h [-]","description":"Change the fraction of a grid width traveled in one step.","min":0.05,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.45}
//|   points_per_wavelength: {"type":"range","label":"Grid points per wavelength P [-]","description":"Change spatial resolution for the wave shown in the table.","min":3,"max":30,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   propagation_periods: {"type":"range","label":"Propagation periods [-]","description":"Change the time over which phase lag and amplitude changes accumulate.","min":1,"max":50,"step":1,"value":12}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"coarse-wave","label":"Coarsely resolved wave P = 4","description":"The phase-velocity ratio falls to 0.900, with about 1.2 periods of lag after 12 periods.","inputs":{"points_per_wavelength":4}}
//|   - {"id":"unstable-step","label":"ν = 0.6","description":"The one-step amplitude ratio at θ = π exceeds 1, making Dormand–Prince 5(4) unstable.","inputs":{"cfl_number":0.6}}
//|   - {"id":"courant-limit","label":"ν = 1","description":"Leapfrog has phase-velocity ratio 1 and no dispersion.","inputs":{"cfl_number":1}}
//|   - {"id":"long-run","label":"Propagation over 50 periods","description":"Phase lag at P = 10 increases to 0.82 periods.","inputs":{"propagation_periods":50}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.98363164,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.95105652,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.98363186,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.95105795,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.99996868,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":0.99997518,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":0.98682964,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|   - case: coarse-wave
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.90031632,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.1962042,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: unstable-step
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":1.0003116,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: courant-limit
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: long-run
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"speeds","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.81841785,"rtol":1e-6}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

let nu = cfl_number;
let points = points_per_wavelength.round();
let periods = propagation_periods.round();
let pi = core::f64::consts::PI;
// Evaluate the Dormand–Prince 5(4) stability function R(z) and R'(z) at z = iy with Horner's method.
// Represent complex numbers as (real part, imaginary part) pairs.
let horner = |coefficients: &[f64], y: f64| {
    coefficients
        .iter()
        .rev()
        .fold((0.0, 0.0), |(re, im), &a| (a - im * y, re * y))
};
let stability = [
    1.0,
    1.0,
    0.5,
    1.0 / 6.0,
    1.0 / 24.0,
    1.0 / 120.0,
    1.0 / 600.0,
];
let derivative = [1.0, 1.0, 0.5, 1.0 / 6.0, 1.0 / 24.0, 1.0 / 100.0];
// Return (phase-velocity ratio, group-velocity ratio, one-step amplitude ratio) for [semidiscrete, DOPRI5, leapfrog].
let ratios = |theta: f64| {
    let half = theta / 2.0;
    let s = RealScalar::sin(half);
    let c = RealScalar::cos(half);
    let semi = (s / half, c, 1.0);
    let y = 2.0 * nu * s;
    let (re, im) = horner(&stability, y);
    let (dre, dim) = horner(&derivative, y);
    let modulus2 = re * re + im * im;
    // d arg R(iy)/dy = Re(R'(iy) / R(iy)); group-velocity ratio = cos(θ/2)·d arg R/dy.
    let dopri = (
        RealScalar::atan2(im, re) / (nu * theta),
        c * (dre * re + dim * im) / modulus2,
        RealScalar::sqrt(modulus2),
    );
    let leapfrog = (
        2.0 * RealScalar::asin(nu * s) / (nu * theta),
        c / RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 - nu * nu * s * s),
        1.0,
    );
    [semi, dopri, leapfrog]
};
let names = ["Semidiscrete (exact time evolution)", "Semidiscrete + DOPRI5", "Leapfrog"];
let mut phase_series = vec![Vec::new(); 3];
let mut group_series = vec![Vec::new(); 3];
for index in 0..=240 {
    let p = 3.0 + 37.0 * index as f64 / 240.0;
    for (method, r) in ratios(2.0 * pi / p).into_iter().enumerate() {
        phase_series[method].push([p, r.0]);
        group_series[method].push([p, r.1]);
    }
}
let theta = 2.0 * pi / points;
let steps = periods * points / nu;
let highest = ratios(pi);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (method, r) in ratios(theta).into_iter().enumerate() {
    // Exact time evolution preserves semidiscrete amplitude; no one-step quantity is defined.
    rows.push((
        names[method],
        r.0,
        r.1,
        RealScalar::powf(r.2, steps),
        periods * (1.0 - r.0),
        (method > 0).then_some(highest[method].2),
    ));
}
let series = |values: &[Vec<[f64; 2]>]| {
    (0..3)
        .map(|m| serde_json::json!({ "name": names[m], "points": values[m] }))
        .collect::<Vec<_>>()
};
let marker =
    serde_json::json!({ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": points, "label": "Selected P" });
for (values, label) in [
    (&phase_series, "Phase-velocity ratio [-]"),
    (&group_series, "Group-velocity ratio [-]"),
] {
    emit_line_chart!(
        &series(values),
        options = &serde_json::json!({
            "xLabel": "Grid points per wavelength P [-]",
            "yLabel": label,
            "link": { "group": "wave-points-per-wavelength" },
            "annotations": [
                { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 1.0, "label": "True wave speed" },
                marker
            ]
        })
    );
}
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Dispersion relation" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Phase-velocity ratio [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Group-velocity ratio [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Amplitude ratio after propagation [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Phase lag [periods]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "One-step amplitude ratio at θ = π [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6, "nullText": "No time steps" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "speeds" })
);
```

At the defaults $\nu=0.45$ and $P=10$, the semidiscrete phase-velocity ratio is 0.98363 and the group-velocity ratio is 0.95106, giving 0.196 periods of lag after 12 periods of propagation.
Adding Dormand–Prince 5(4) changes the phase-velocity ratio by only $2\times10^{-7}$, with amplitude ratio 0.99997 after 12 periods.
At this step size, time integration error is much smaller than spatial dispersion, so reducing phase lag requires increasing $P$.
For leapfrog, temporal error opposes spatial error, increasing the phase-velocity ratio to 0.98683.
At $\nu=1$, the two errors cancel and leapfrog has no dispersion for any $P$.
At $\nu=0.6$, the one-step amplitude ratio at $\theta=\pi$ is 1.00031, making the shortest wave grow at each step.
The [`CflPolicy::Automatic` limit](oxref:pde-wave-cfl-policy-steps) $\nu\le0.4487$ avoids this instability.

## Quantities to check in long-time computations

For long-time wave computations, check the following quantities in addition to local error and termination reason.

1. Grid points per wavelength: placing $P\ge13$ points on the shortest wavelength of interest keeps semidiscrete phase-velocity error within 1%.
2. Discrete energy $E_h$ over time: growth indicates instability; a large decrease suggests numerical dissipation.
3. Boundary reflection: a reflected wave reverses sign at a fixed end and retains its sign at a free end.
4. Grid dependence of arrival time: compare when a pulse reaches a fixed location on several grids and check that refinement brings it closer to theory.

## Summary

For second-order central differences on a uniform grid, semidiscrete phase- and group-velocity ratios are $\sin(\theta/2)/(\theta/2)$ and $\cos(\theta/2)$; shorter wavelengths are slower.
Phase-velocity error is approximately $\pi^2/(6P^2)$, so use at least 13 points on the shortest wavelength of interest to keep it within 1%.
Using the default Dormand–Prince 5(4) in `HyperbolicProblem` with a stable step makes temporal phase error and amplitude loss much smaller than spatial dispersion, leaving accuracy determined by grid resolution.
When comparing with leapfrog, do not generalize its special cancellation of temporal and spatial errors at $\nu=1$ to other methods.
For long runs, check grid resolution, discrete energy, boundary reflections, and arrival times separately from termination reason.
