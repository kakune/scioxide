The wave equation is a hyperbolic partial differential equation describing phenomena that propagate at finite speed while preserving waveform and energy.
Diffusion solutions become smoother over time, whereas wave solutions do not decay.

Thus, preventing numerical divergence (stability) alone is insufficient.
Wave speed, amplitude, and energy must also be checked for correct preservation over long times.

This page converts the second-order wave equation into a first-order displacement–velocity system and derives energy conservation, CFL conditions, and explicit stability limits.
It then varies `CflPolicy` to examine how `HyperbolicProblem` selects steps.
Remaining phase error at stable steps is covered in [Numerical dispersion of waves](../hyperbolic-numerical-dispersion/); transient output, checkpoint restart, and termination reasons are covered in [Transient output, restart, and termination reasons](../transient-outputs-and-restart/).

## The wave equation and energy conservation

Consider the one-dimensional variable-coefficient wave equation on $\Omega=[a,b]$.

$$
m(x)\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(k(x)\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right),\qquad m(x)>0,\quad k(x)>0
$$

$u$ is displacement, $m$ mass per unit length, and $k$ stiffness (tension for a string); local wave speed is $c(x)=\sqrt{k(x)/m(x)}$.
Two initial conditions select a solution: initial displacement $u(x,0)=u_0(x)$ and initial velocity $\partial_t u(x,0)=v_0(x)$.

Multiplying both sides by velocity $\partial_t u$, integrating over $\Omega$, and integrating the right-hand side by parts gives the energy identity below.

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac12\int_a^b m\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right)^2dx+\frac12\int_a^b k\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2dx\right]=\left[k\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right]_a^b
$$

The first bracketed term is kinetic energy, and the second is strain energy.
At fixed ends (Dirichlet conditions with constant $u$, hence $\partial_t u=0$) and free ends (Neumann conditions $k\,\partial_x u=0$), the boundary term on the right (boundary work) vanishes, so total energy $E(t)$ is constant in time.

## First-order conversion and the method of lines

Adding velocity $v=\partial_t u$ as an unknown gives a system first order in time.

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\begin{bmatrix}u\\ v\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}v\\ \dfrac{1}{m}\,\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(k\,\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)\end{bmatrix}
$$

Discretizing space alone by FDM, FEM, or FVM (the method of lines) produces a semidiscrete system with lumped diagonal mass matrix $\mathbf{M}$ and symmetric stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}$.

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\ \mathbf{v}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}\\ \mathbf{M}^{-1}\left(\mathbf{b}(t)-\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}\right)\end{bmatrix}
$$

With zero source and boundary data, discrete energy $E_h=\tfrac12\mathbf{v}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}+\tfrac12\mathbf{u}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}$ is also conserved.
Indeed, $dE_h/dt=\mathbf{v}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}'+\mathbf{u}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}'=-\mathbf{v}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}^\mathsf{T}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{v}=0$.
If the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{K}$ are $\omega_j^2$, the first-order system has eigenvalues $\pm\mathrm{i}\,\omega_j$ on the imaginary axis.
This differs from semidiscrete diffusion, whose eigenvalues lie on the negative real axis.

`HyperbolicProblem::new(discretization, source, end)` interprets the spatial discretization's diffusion coefficient as $c^2$ and prepares the semidiscrete system $\mathbf{M}\mathbf{u}''=\mathbf{b}(t)-\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}$ once, excluding Dirichlet degrees of freedom.
Coefficients are time independent; sources and boundary data may depend on time.
Boundaries may be Dirichlet (fixed), Neumann (free), or Robin.
`initial_state(displacement, velocity)` constructs the initial state by sampling two `FieldData` values at free unknown locations; coordinates in `PdeState` are ordered as `[free displacements, free velocities]`.
The field returned by `values(state)` contains only displacement with Dirichlet values restored; read velocity from the second half of `state.coordinates()`.

`HyperbolicOptions` holds time integration settings; the problem holds final time.
`HyperbolicOptions::new()` selects adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) (`scioxide_ode::DormandPrince5`), mean-square local error tolerances of $10^{-8}$ both absolute and relative, `CflPolicy::Automatic`, and a limit of 100,000 attempted steps.
`with_initial_step(step)` sets the first step for local error control, not a fixed step.
Change settings through `with_error_tolerance`, `with_cfl_policy`, `with_step_budget`, `with_budgets`, and `with_output_schedule`; use `with_ode_method` to select another built-in integrator from `scioxide-ode`.
Unlike parabolic `ParabolicOptions`, it has no scheme enumeration, stage-endpoint setting, or Rannacher startup setting.

## CFL conditions and explicit stability limits

Solutions of $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$ propagate at speed $c$ along characteristics $x\pm ct=\text{constant}$.
The initial-data range $[x-ct,\,x+ct]$ determining the solution at $(x,t)$ is called the physical domain of dependence.
The range of initial grid points used to compute $(x,t)$ by an explicit difference scheme with grid width $h$ and step $\Delta t$ is called the numerical domain of dependence.

The Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy theorem states that the numerical domain of dependence must contain the physical domain of dependence for convergence.
Otherwise, changing initial data outside the numerical domain does not change the numerical solution, preventing convergence to the true solution under grid refinement.
For a one-stage explicit method using only neighboring grid points, define the Courant number (CFL number) as

$$
\nu=\frac{c\,\Delta t}{h}
$$

and the condition is $\nu\le1$.

The CFL condition is necessary and does not guarantee stability.
For explicit Runge–Kutta in the method of lines, stability depends on whether the eigenvalues $\pm\mathrm{i}\,\omega_j$ multiplied by step size, $\pm\mathrm{i}\,\omega_j\Delta t$, lie in the stability region $\lvert R(z)\rvert\le1$ of the stability function $R(z)$.
Dormand–Prince 5(4) has stability function $R(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{5}z^k/k!+z^6/600$ and imaginary-axis stability interval $\lvert y\rvert\le0.9972$ ($z=\mathrm{i}y$).
For second-order central differences on a uniform grid, the largest $\omega_j$ approaches $2c/h$, giving stability condition $2\nu\le0.9972$, or $\nu\le0.4986$, stricter than the CFL condition $\nu\le1$.
Forward Euler's stability region intersects the imaginary axis only at the origin, making it unstable for the wave equation at every step size.

`CflPolicy` selects how to pass this stability limit to the ODE integrator as a step cap.

- `CflPolicy::Automatic` (default) computes step cap $0.9\,\beta_I/\sqrt\rho$ from the maximum absolute row sum $\rho$ of the mass-divided Jacobian $\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{K}$ (a Gershgorin eigenvalue bound) and the imaginary-axis stability interval $\beta_I$ advertised by the integrator through `scioxide_ode::OdeLinearStability`.
  Dormand–Prince 5(4) advertises $\beta_I=0.9971$, corresponding to $\nu\le0.4487$ for uniform-grid FDM ($\rho=4c^2/h^2$).
  Selecting through `with_ode_method` a method that advertises no stability region or is unstable on the imaginary axis (advertised length 0, as for forward Euler) makes `advance` return `InvalidInput` before numerical computation starts.
- `CflPolicy::MaximumStep(h_max)` uses the positive finite cap $h_{\max}$ directly.
- `CflPolicy::Disabled` imposes no cap, leaving steps to local error control alone.

Before each step, the integrator selects the minimum of this cap, the step proposed by local error control, and the remaining interval to the end.
Because $\rho$ is a Gershgorin upper bound, the cap remains conservative for nonuniform grids or coefficients.
This bound assumes eigenvalues on the imaginary axis (no damping terms).
On a multidimensional uniform grid, $\rho=4c^2\sum_d h_d^{-2}$, so the allowable step decreases with dimension.

## CflPolicy and step selection

Use a plucked string to examine how the automatic cap works in an actual computation.
Discretize a fixed-end string of length 1 and wave speed $c=1$ on a uniform `StructuredFdmPlan::dirichlet_laplacian` grid; start from a triangular displacement raised by 0.1 at $x=0.3$ with zero initial velocity, then advance with `HyperbolicProblem` to one fundamental period $T=2$.
The triangular displacement contains every wavelength representable on the grid, so short-wavelength components tend to grow when steps exceed the stability limit.

The cell solves the same problem with three `CflPolicy` choices.
These are `Automatic` with cap $0.9\beta_I h/(2c)$, `MaximumStep` with a multiple of that cap, and uncapped `Disabled`.
For each run, the table lists attempted, accepted, and rejected steps, mean accepted-step Courant number $\bar\nu=T/(n_{\mathrm{acc}}h)$, relative change in discrete energy $E_h$, and termination reason.
The independent check is $E_h$, which the semidiscrete system preserves exactly.

```rust
//| id: pde-wave-cfl-policy
//| ref: pde-wave-cfl-policy
//| title: "Vary CflPolicy to compare steps and energy for a plucked string"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-ode, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   nodes: {"type":"range","label":"Grid point count N [-]","description":"Change grid width h = 1/(N − 1) and the stability-limited step.","min":21,"max":201,"step":20,"value":81}
//|   tolerance_digits: {"type":"range","label":"Local error tolerance digits d [-]","description":"Change relative and absolute tolerances 10^(−d).","min":2,"max":10,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   step_factor: {"type":"range","label":"MaximumStep factor [-]","description":"Change the multiplier applied to the Automatic cap.","min":0.5,"max":3,"step":0.25,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"tight-tolerance","label":"Tolerance 10⁻⁶","description":"Error-controlled steps are below the stability limit, so all three policies choose nearly identical steps.","inputs":{"tolerance_digits":6}}
//|   - {"id":"very-loose","label":"Tolerance 10⁻²","description":"With Disabled, short wavelengths keep growing and energy exceeds a thousand times its initial value.","inputs":{"tolerance_digits":2}}
//|   - {"id":"half-step","label":"Factor 0.5","description":"MaximumStep is below the Automatic cap, doubling the step count.","inputs":{"step_factor":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"fine-grid","label":"N = 201","description":"As grid width decreases, Automatic steps decrease in the same proportion.","inputs":{"nodes":201}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0.44,"max":0.4487}}
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":-0.001,"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"min":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"min":0.05}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: tight-tolerance
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":0.4487}}
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"min":-0.001,"max":0.001}}
//|   - case: very-loose
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"min":100}}
//|   - case: half-step
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":714,"atol":2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"min":-0.0001,"max":0}}
//|   - case: fine-grid
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"policies","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":892,"atol":2}}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, ExecutionContext, NormSelection, Tolerance};
use scioxide_ode::{DormandPrince5, OdeLinearStability, OdeMethod};
use scioxide_pde::{
    CflPolicy, FieldData, FieldPoint, HyperbolicOptions, HyperbolicProblem, StructuredFdmPlan,
};

let n = nodes.round() as usize;
let digits = tolerance_digits.round() as u32;
let h = 1.0 / (n - 1) as f64;
let end = 2.0;
let plan =
    StructuredFdmPlan::dirichlet_laplacian([0.0], [1.0], [n]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let source = FieldData::zero();
let problem = HyperbolicProblem::new(&plan, &source, end).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// A triangle raised by 0.1 at x = 0.3 (a plucked string).
let pluck = FieldData::time_invariant(|p: FieldPoint<f64, 1>| {
    let x = p.point[0];
    Ok(if x < 0.3 {
        0.1 * x / 0.3
    } else {
        0.1 * (1.0 - x) / 0.7
    })
});
let rest = FieldData::zero();
// Discrete energy E_h = ½ h Σ v² + ½ Σ (u_{i+1} − u_i)²/h (free unknowns are interior grid points).
let energy = |coordinates: &[f64]| {
    let (u, v) = coordinates.split_at(n - 2);
    let kinetic: f64 = v.iter().map(|vi| 0.5 * h * vi * vi).sum();
    let strain: f64 = (0..n - 1)
        .map(|i| {
            let left = if i == 0 { 0.0 } else { u[i - 1] };
            let right = if i == n - 2 { 0.0 } else { u[i] };
            0.5 * (right - left) * (right - left) / h
        })
        .sum();
    kinetic + strain
};
let OdeLinearStability::Axes {
    imaginary: Some(beta),
    ..
} = DormandPrince5::<f64>::new().linear_stability()
else {
    return Err("DormandPrince5 does not advertise an imaginary-axis stability interval".to_string());
};
let automatic_cap = 0.9 * beta * h / 2.0;
let policies = [
    ("Automatic", CflPolicy::Automatic),
    (
        "MaximumStep",
        CflPolicy::MaximumStep(step_factor * automatic_cap),
    ),
    ("Disabled", CflPolicy::Disabled),
];
let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::from_tolerance(
    Tolerance::from_decimal_places(digits, digits),
    NormSelection::RootMeanSquare,
);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut automatic_report = None;
for (name, policy) in policies {
    let options = HyperbolicOptions::new()
        .with_error_tolerance(tolerance.clone())
        .with_cfl_policy(policy);
    let mut state = problem
        .initial_state(&pluck, &rest)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let initial_energy = energy(state.coordinates());
    let mut workspace = problem.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let report = problem
        .advance(
            &options,
            &mut state,
            &mut (),
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let d = report.diagnostics();
    rows.push((
        name,
        d.num_time_steps(),
        d.num_accepted_steps(),
        d.num_rejected_steps(),
        report.final_time() / (d.num_accepted_steps() as f64 * h),
        (energy(state.coordinates()) - initial_energy) / initial_energy,
        report.termination().to_string(),
    ));
    if name == "Automatic" {
        automatic_report = Some(report);
    }
}
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "CflPolicy" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Attempted steps [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Accepted [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Rejected [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Mean Courant number ν̄ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative E_h change [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "policies" })
);
let report = automatic_report.ok_or("Missing Automatic result")?;
let d = report.diagnostics();
let outcome = if report.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Automatic reached final time T = 2")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Automatic stopped before final time")
        .explanation("The state contains values at the last accepted time, not the final-time solution.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(report.termination().to_string())
        .diagnostic("Reached time", report.final_time(), None)
        .diagnostic("Accepted steps", d.num_accepted_steps() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Step cap 0.9β_I h/2", automatic_cap, None)
);
```

At the defaults, tolerance $10^{-3}$ and $N=81$, `Automatic` accepted 357 steps with cap $\Delta t=5.61\times10^{-3}$, mean Courant number 0.448, and relative $E_h$ change $-1.7\times10^{-4}$.
This decrease is weak numerical dissipation from $\lvert R(\mathrm{i}y)\rvert<1$ for Dormand–Prince 5(4).
Doubling the cap with `MaximumStep` reached the end at $\bar\nu=0.894$ without rejection, while $E_h$ increased by 27%.
`Disabled` also enlarged steps to $\bar\nu=0.879$, with only six rejections, and $E_h$ nearly doubled.
Both termination reasons were `Completed`.
Short-wavelength components grew while local error estimates remained below tolerance, so termination reason alone cannot establish stability.
At tolerance $10^{-2}$, $E_h$ with `Disabled` exceeds a thousand times its initial value.

At tolerance $10^{-6}$, error-controlled steps ($\bar\nu\approx0.33$) fall below the stability limit, and all three policies choose nearly identical steps.
`MaximumStep` with factor 0.5 doubles the count to 714 steps and reduces energy change to $-1.5\times10^{-5}$.
For $N=201$, `Automatic` steps decrease in proportion to grid width, increasing accepted steps to 892.

## Summary

Converting the wave equation to a first-order displacement–velocity system gives a semidiscrete system with imaginary-axis eigenvalues, preserving discrete energy at fixed and free ends.
The CFL condition is only necessary for convergence; with the default Dormand–Prince 5(4) in `HyperbolicProblem`, stability condition $\nu\le0.4986$ determines the step.
`CflPolicy::Automatic` computes step cap $0.9\beta_I/\sqrt\rho$ from a Gershgorin bound and the integrator's stability interval.
Enlarging the cap with `MaximumStep` or removing it with `Disabled` can make solutions unstable at loose tolerances while termination remains `Completed`.
After changing the step policy, inspect the evolution of discrete energy $E_h$ independently of termination reason.
