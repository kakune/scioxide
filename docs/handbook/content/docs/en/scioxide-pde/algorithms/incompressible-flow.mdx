This page covers mixed finite-element discretization of incompressible flow with constant density and viscosity on triangular and tetrahedral meshes.
`StokesPlan` represents velocity with P2 elements and pressure with P1 elements (Taylor–Hood elements), assembling a saddle-point system with velocity and pressure as simultaneous unknowns.
It explains governing equations, weak forms, discrete stability (the inf-sup condition), and pressure indeterminacy and gauges in that order, then checks the plan with plane Poiseuille flow, whose exact solution lies in the element space.
Saddle-point solvers and Newton's method for Navier–Stokes are covered in [Solving incompressible-flow saddle-point systems](../incompressible-solvers/), and time evolution in [Time-dependent incompressible flow](../incompressible-time-stepping/).
Element degrees of freedom, geometry mappings, and quadrature are shared with [Simplex-mesh finite elements](../simplex-finite-elements/).

---

## Governing equations and stress

For velocity $u$ [m/s] and pressure $p$ [Pa] in domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=2,3$), the steady Stokes equations are

$$
-\nabla\cdot\sigma(u,p)=f,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad \sigma(u,p)=-p\,I+\mu\left(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T}\right)
$$


$\mu$ [Pa·s] is dynamic viscosity (distinct from kinematic viscosity $\nu=\mu/\rho$), $f$ [N/m³] is body force per unit volume, and $\sigma$ is Cauchy stress.
Pressure is positive in compression (the isotropic component of $\sigma$ is $-p$).
The second equation's incompressibility condition is the constraint associated with pressure, which acts as its Lagrange multiplier.

Retaining inertia gives the Navier–Stokes equations with density $\rho$ [kg/m³]:

$$
\rho\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)\right)-\nabla\cdot\sigma(u,p)=f,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0
$$


Writing advection as the divergence $\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)$ of momentum flux $u\otimes u$ is called conservative form.
When $\nabla\cdot u=0$, this equals the convective form $(u\cdot\nabla)u$.
The Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}=\rho UL/\mu$, based on characteristic velocity $U$ and length $L$, measures the ratio of advection to viscosity; Stokes is a good approximation for $\mathrm{Re}\ll1$.

Split the boundary into $\Gamma_D$ prescribing velocity $u=g$ and $\Gamma_N$ prescribing traction $\sigma n=t$ [Pa] ($n$ is the outward unit normal).
Specify `StokesPlan` boundaries through `TaggedBoundaries`; on each face, all $D$ components must be Dirichlet conditions (velocity) or all natural conditions (traction).
Faces prescribing velocity in only some components, Robin conditions, and time-dependent data are rejected with `InvalidInput` (argument `boundaries`).
Values at nodes belonging to two conditions, such as lid corners, follow the Dirichlet conflict policy (`DirichletConflictPolicy`) in `TaggedBoundaries`.

## Weak form and saddle-point system

Multiplying by velocity test function $v$ (0 on $\Gamma_D$) and pressure test function $q$ and integrating by parts gives the weak form

$$
a(u,v)+b(v,p)=\int_\Omega f\cdot v\,dx+\int_{\Gamma_N}t\cdot v\,ds,\qquad b(u,q)=0
$$

$$
a(u,v)=\int_\Omega\mu\left(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T}\right):\nabla v\,dx=2\mu\int_\Omega\varepsilon(u):\varepsilon(v)\,dx,\qquad b(v,q)=-\int_\Omega q\,\nabla\cdot v\,dx
$$

where $\varepsilon(u)=(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T})/2$ is the strain-rate tensor.
In this form, the natural condition is traction $\sigma n$ itself.
Because $a$ has rigid motion (translation and rotation) as its nullspace, Korn's inequality requires at least one prescribed-velocity face on each connected component for $a$ to be positive definite.
A component without such a face produces `InvalidInput` (argument `boundaries`).

With velocity basis coefficients $\mathbf{u}$ and pressure coefficients $\mathbf{p}$, discretization gives the symmetric indefinite saddle-point system

$$
\begin{pmatrix}A&B^{\mathsf T}\\B&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\\mathbf{p}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{f}\\\mathbf{g}\end{pmatrix}
$$


$A$ is the viscous velocity block (symmetric positive definite), $B$ is discrete divergence, and $\mathbf{g}$ includes contributions from lifting Dirichlet values.

`StokesPlan::new(mesh, geometry, density, viscosity, &boundaries, body)` prepares a plan with defaults, and `with_options(…, options)` prepares one with specified `IncompressibleOptions`.
`build(…, options, scope)` performs the same preparation as one epoch of the caller's solve context.
`body` returns body force $f(x)$ at position $x$; boundaries and body forces are evaluated once during preparation.
Density does not enter steady Stokes; it is used for the velocity mass matrix, advection, and the Reynolds-number diagnostic.
Nonpositive or nonfinite density or viscosity, $D$ other than 2 or 3, or an empty mesh returns `InvalidInput` without a partial plan.

Unknowns are ordered as all P2 velocity nodes for component 0, then component 1 (and component 2 in three dimensions), all P1 pressure vertices, then gauge multipliers.
`velocity_nodes()` is the P2 node count per component (vertices and edge midpoints), `velocity_unknowns()` is total velocity coordinates, and `unknowns()` is the total including multipliers.
`node_point(i)` returns physical coordinates of node $i$.
Dirichlet rows are eliminated, and solution velocity coordinates contain prescribed values.
`assemble_csr` assembles the matrix including gauges in CSR; `element_matrix`, `element_velocity_mass`, and `element_pressure_mass` return element matrices for custom preconditioners.

## Inf-sup condition and Taylor–Hood elements

For a unique saddle-point solution stable independently of $h$, positive-definite $A$ must be supplemented by the discrete inf-sup condition (Ladyzhenskaya–Babuška–Brezzi condition)

$$
\inf_{q_h\in Q_h\setminus\{0\}}\ \sup_{v_h\in V_h\setminus\{0\}}\frac{b(v_h,q_h)}{\|v_h\|_{H^1}\,\|q_h\|_{L^2/\mathbb{R}}}\ \ge\ \beta>0
$$

with $\beta$ uniform over the mesh family.
If it fails, nonconstant pressures satisfying $B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{0}$ (spurious pressure modes) can make pressure indeterminate, or $\beta$ can approach 0 under refinement and amplify pressure errors.
Equal-degree continuous velocity and pressure elements (P1/P1) do not satisfy this condition.

Taylor–Hood elements (P2/P1), with velocity one degree higher, satisfy the inf-sup condition under mild mesh assumptions, such as each triangle having at least one interior vertex (Boffi 1994; Boffi–Brezzi–Fortin 2013).
For smooth solutions, the error satisfies

$$
\|u-u_h\|_{H^1}+\|p-p_h\|_{L^2}\le C\,h^2\left(|u|_{H^3}+|p|_{H^2}\right)
$$


Taylor–Hood velocity satisfies $\int q_h\,\nabla\cdot u_h\,dx=0$ for every continuous P1 $q_h$ but is not pointwise divergence free.
Because $\|\nabla\cdot u_h\|_{L^2}=\|\nabla\cdot(u_h-u)\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt{D}\,|u-u_h|_{H^1}$, this quantity also decreases as $O(h^2)$ for smooth solutions.
Preparation checks constant pressure modes and boundary fluxes (next section), but not the inf-sup constant.

## Pressure gauges and flux compatibility

On a connected component with velocity prescribed on every boundary (a closed channel), pressure is determined only up to a constant.
The divergence theorem gives $\int_\Omega\nabla\cdot u\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}g\cdot n\,ds$, so boundary velocities must satisfy flux compatibility $\int_{\partial\Omega}g\cdot n\,ds=0$.
The plan checks a discrete form during preparation; exceeding the `IncompressibleOptions::with_compatibility` tolerance (default $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$) returns `DomainError` (argument `rhs`).
On components with traction faces, natural conditions determine pressure level, so no gauge is needed.

For each closed component, add one gauge multiplier $\lambda$ and impose $\sum_v w_v p_v=c_{\mathrm{target}}$.
The multiplier enters pressure rows as $w_v\lambda$; compatible flux gives $\lambda=0$ in the solution.
`IncompressibleOptions::new()` defaults to automatic gauges: `EllipticGauge::WeightedMean(0)` ($\int p\,dx=0$) on closed components, and no gauge on components whose pressure is fixed by traction.
`with_gauges(gauges)` explicitly supplies one `EllipticGauge` per connected component; an empty sequence restores automatic selection.

| Gauge | Weight $w_v$ | Imposed condition | Applicable components |
|---|---|---|---|
| `WeightedMean(c)` | Integral of P1 basis function $\int\phi_v\,dx$ | Area- or volume-averaged pressure equals $c$ | Closed components |
| `Orthogonal` | 1 | Sum of vertex pressures equals 0 | Closed components |
| `Unspecified` | None | No condition | Components with traction faces |

All following settings produce `InvalidInput` (argument `options.gauges`).

- Assigning `Unspecified` to a closed component.
- Assigning any gauge except `Unspecified` to a component with traction faces.
- A nonempty sequence whose length differs from the connected-component count.

Gauges choose only the pressure constant and do not change velocity.
`with_fem` changes quadrature, geometry conditions, execution, and limits (`SimplexFemOptions`); `with_compatibility` changes flux compatibility tolerance; read these with `fem()`, `gauges()`, and `compatibility()`.

## Checking with plane Poiseuille flow

In channel $[0,L]\times[0,H]$ of height $H$ and length $L$, laminar flow with constant pressure-gradient magnitude $G=-\partial p/\partial x$ [Pa/m] is

$$
u(y)=\frac{G}{2\mu}\,y\,(H-y),\qquad v=0,\qquad p(x)=G\,(x_0-x)
$$

where $x_0$ sets the pressure level.
$\Delta u=-G/\mu$ balances $\nabla p=(-G,0)$, and $\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)=u\,\partial_x u=0$, so this is also a Navier–Stokes solution.
Velocity is quadratic and pressure linear, placing the exact solution in the P2/P1 space.
Thus, for a stable discretization, nodal error contains no discretization error, only linear-system algebraic error and roundoff.

Take maximum speed $U=GH^2/(8\mu)$ as 1 m/s, $H=1$ m, $L=2$ m, $\mu=0.1$ Pa·s, and $\rho=1$ kg/m³ ($\mathrm{Re}=10$).
The following definitions construct the channel mesh and plan.
Prescribe zero velocity on walls $y=0,H$ and $u(y)$ at inlet $x=0$.
At outlet $x=L$, choose the same $u(y)$ (closed channel), exact traction $\sigma n=(0,\ \mu u'(y))$ ($p(L)=0$), or zero traction.

```rust
//| id: pde-incompressible-channel
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Channel and plan for plane Poiseuille flow"
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, BoundaryPoint, EllipticGauge, IncompressibleOptions,
    SimplexFemGeometry, StokesPlan, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{Point, SimplexBoundaryTags, TriangleMesh};

/// Channel [0, 2]×[0, 1] [m], μ = 0.1 Pa·s, ρ = 1 kg/m³, maximum speed U = 1 m/s.
pub(crate) const LENGTH: f64 = 2.0;
pub(crate) const VISCOSITY: f64 = 0.1;
/// Pressure-gradient magnitude G = 8μU/H² [Pa/m] (H = 1 m).
pub(crate) const GRADIENT: f64 = 8.0 * VISCOSITY;

/// Exact velocity u(y) = G y (H − y) / (2μ) [m/s].
pub(crate) fn velocity(y: f64) -> f64 {
    GRADIENT / (2.0 * VISCOSITY) * y * (1.0 - y)
}

/// Stokes plan on a triangular mesh obtained by splitting 2n × n squares along diagonals.
/// Face tags: 0 = wall, 1 = inlet, 2 = outlet. `outflow` scales prescribed outlet velocity.
pub(crate) fn channel(
    cells: usize,
    outlet: &str,
    outflow: f64,
    gauges: Vec<EllipticGauge<f64>>,
) -> Result<StokesPlan<f64, f64, 2>, SciOxideError> {
    let (nx, ny) = (2 * cells, cells);
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..=ny {
        for i in 0..=nx {
            points.push(Point::new([
                LENGTH * i as f64 / nx as f64,
                j as f64 / ny as f64,
            ])?);
        }
    }
    let mut triangles = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..ny {
        for i in 0..nx {
            let a = j * (nx + 1) + i;
            triangles.push([a, a + 1, a + nx + 2]);
            triangles.push([a, a + nx + 2, a + nx + 1]);
        }
    }
    let mesh = TriangleMesh::new(points, triangles)?;
    let mut faces = Vec::new();
    for face in mesh.boundary_faces() {
        let vertices = mesh.face_vertices(face)?;
        let column = |k: usize| vertices[k] % (nx + 1);
        let tag = match (column(0), column(1)) {
            (0, 0) => 1,
            (a, b) if a == nx && b == nx => 2,
            _ => 0,
        };
        faces.push((face, tag));
    }
    let tags = SimplexBoundaryTags::new(&mesh, faces)?;
    let profile = |scale: f64| {
        BoundaryData::time_invariant(move |p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| {
            Ok(if p.component == 0 {
                scale * velocity(p.point[1])
            } else {
                0.0
            })
        })
    };
    // Outlet traction σn = (−p + 2μ ∂u/∂x, μ (∂u/∂y + ∂v/∂x)) = (0, μ u'(y)). Set p(L) = 0.
    let traction = BoundaryData::time_invariant(|p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| {
        let shear = 0.5 * GRADIENT * (1.0 - 2.0 * p.point[1]);
        Ok(if p.component == 0 { 0.0 } else { shear })
    });
    let exit = match outlet {
        "Velocity (closed channel)" => BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(2, profile(outflow)),
        "Exact traction" => BoundaryCondition::natural(2, traction),
        _ => BoundaryCondition::natural(2, BoundaryData::zero()),
    };
    let conditions = vec![
        BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(0, BoundaryData::zero()),
        BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(1, profile(1.0)),
        exit,
    ];
    let boundaries = TaggedBoundaries::new(&mesh, &tags, 2, conditions)?;
    StokesPlan::with_options(
        mesh,
        SimplexFemGeometry::Affine,
        1.0,
        VISCOSITY,
        &boundaries,
        &|_| Ok([0.0; 2]),
        IncompressibleOptions::new().with_gauges(gauges),
    )
}
```

The next cell varies outlet conditions and mesh resolution, solves the plan, and compares all P2 velocity nodes and P1 pressure vertices with the exact solution.
Linear residual tolerance is tightened from the default $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ to $10^{-12}$.
In a closed channel, the automatic gauge sets $\int p\,dx=0$, so the comparison pressure uses $x_0=L/2$; for a traction outlet, use $x_0=L$.
“Weak divergence” in the table is the norm of the discrete continuity equation $(\int\phi_v\nabla\cdot u_h\,dx)_v$, while $\|\nabla\cdot u_h\|$ is the $L^2$ norm of pointwise divergence.

```rust
//| id: pde-incompressible-poiseuille
//| ref: pde-incompressible-poiseuille
//| title: "Vary outlet conditions to check reproduction of plane Poiseuille flow"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [pde-incompressible-channel]
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg]
//| inputs:
//|   cells: {"type":"range","label":"Height-direction cell count n [-]","description":"Divide the channel into 2n × n squares, each split into two triangles.","min":1,"max":8,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   outlet: {"type":"select","label":"Outlet x = L condition","description":"Prescribing velocity closes the channel and requires a pressure gauge. Prescribing traction determines pressure.","options":["Velocity (closed channel)","Exact traction","Zero traction"],"value":"Velocity (closed channel)"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"traction","label":"Exact-traction outlet","description":"The gauge multiplier disappears, and outlet traction sets pressure level p(L) = 0.","inputs":{"outlet":"Exact traction"}}
//|   - {"id":"traction-free","label":"Zero-traction outlet","description":"Outlet shear stress also becomes zero, increasing velocity error to 0.12 m/s.","inputs":{"outlet":"Zero traction"}}
//|   - {"id":"fine","label":"n = 8","description":"Even with 1,276 unknowns, error remains algebraic, around 10⁻¹¹ m/s.","inputs":{"cells":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":106}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: traction
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-11}}
//|   - case: traction-free
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.1171563,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|   - case: fine
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":1276}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::{LinearOperator, ResidualTolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::LinearSolveTermination;
use scioxide_pde::IncompressibleSolveOptions;

let plan = channel(cells as usize, &outlet, 1.0, Vec::new()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let residual = ResidualTolerance::new(1e-12, 1e-12).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = IncompressibleSolveOptions::new().with_residual(residual);
let result = plan
    .solve_with_options(&options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let report = result.report();
let state = result.solution().as_slice();
// The closed channel uses gauge ∫p = 0; a traction outlet fixes the pressure constant through p(L) = 0.
let origin = if outlet == "Velocity (closed channel)" {
    0.5 * LENGTH
} else {
    LENGTH
};
let nodes = plan.velocity_nodes();
let mut velocity_error = 0.0_f64;
for i in 0..nodes {
    let point = plan.node_point(i).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    velocity_error = velocity_error.max((state[i] - velocity(point[1])).abs());
    velocity_error = velocity_error.max(state[nodes + i].abs());
}
let offset = plan.velocity_unknowns();
let mut pressure_error = 0.0_f64;
for (v, point) in plan.mesh().points().iter().enumerate() {
    let exact = GRADIENT * (origin - point.as_array()[0]);
    pressure_error = pressure_error.max((state[offset + v] - exact).abs());
}
let gauges = plan.unknowns() - offset - plan.mesh().vertex_count();
let mut scratch = vec![0.0; plan.apply_workspace_len().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?];
let diagnostics = plan
    .diagnostics(state, 1.0, 0.0, &mut scratch)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Unknowns [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Gauge multipliers [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "GMRES iterations [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum nodal velocity error [m/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum nodal pressure error [Pa]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "‖∇·u_h‖ [m/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Weak divergence [m²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
let rows = vec![(
    plan.unknowns(),
    gauges,
    report.iterations(),
    velocity_error,
    pressure_error,
    diagnostics.divergence_l2(),
    diagnostics.weak_divergence(),
)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
let outcome = if report.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("GMRES reached the true residual tolerance")
} else if matches!(report.termination(), LinearSolveTermination::Breakdown(_)) {
    Outcome::failed("GMRES broke down")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("GMRES stopped before reaching tolerance")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{:?}", report.termination()))
        .diagnostic("Maximum nodal velocity error", velocity_error, Some("m/s"))
        .diagnostic("GMRES iterations", report.iterations() as f64, None)
);
```

For the closed channel (default, $n=2$), GMRES solved 106 unknowns (including one multiplier) in eight iterations, with maximum nodal errors $3.8\times10^{-13}$ m/s in velocity and $7.1\times10^{-13}$ Pa in pressure.
Even at $n=8$ (1,276 unknowns), velocity error remains $1.0\times10^{-11}$ m/s and pressure error $1.0\times10^{-10}$ Pa, leaving only algebraic error and roundoff.
The exact-traction outlet removes the multiplier, and its natural condition sets pressure level $p(L)=0$.
A zero-traction outlet does not reproduce Poiseuille flow; velocity error becomes 0.12 m/s.
For the symmetric-gradient form $\mu(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T})$, this is because the natural outlet condition forces even shear stress $\mu u'(y)$, which is nonzero in Poiseuille flow, to vanish.
A do-nothing outlet condition with gradient form $\mu\nabla u$ admits Poiseuille flow (Heywood–Rannacher–Turek 1996), but `StokesPlan` uses actual traction as its natural condition, so supply the physical outlet traction.
Weak divergence is at algebraic-error level in every case, showing Taylor–Hood velocity satisfies discrete continuity.

The next cell compares pressures under different gauges in the same closed channel and shows rejection of incorrect gauge and flux settings during plan preparation.
The input is target mean $c$.

```rust
//| id: pde-incompressible-gauges
//| ref: pde-incompressible-gauges
//| title: "Vary gauges in a closed channel and inspect rejected settings"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [pde-incompressible-channel]
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   mean: {"type":"range","label":"Target mean pressure c [Pa]","description":"Area-averaged pressure imposed by WeightedMean(c).","min":-1,"max":1,"step":0.25,"value":0.5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"negative-mean","label":"c = −1 Pa","description":"Mean pressure becomes −1 Pa and the sum of vertex pressures −15 Pa. Velocity is unchanged.","inputs":{"mean":-1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gauges","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gauges","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gauges","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"min":-1e-10,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gauges","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":"InvalidInput"}
//|       - {"artifact":"gauges","path":"/rows/4/1","equals":"DomainError"}
//|       - {"artifact":"gauges","path":"/rows/5/1","equals":"InvalidInput"}
//|       - {"artifact":"gauges","path":"/rows/4/2","equals":null}
//|   - case: negative-mean
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gauges","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":-1.0,"atol":1e-10}}
use scioxide_pde::EllipticGauge;

// (setting, outlet, outlet flow-rate factor, gauge).
let settings = [
    (
        "WeightedMean(0)",
        "Velocity (closed channel)",
        1.0,
        vec![EllipticGauge::WeightedMean(0.0)],
    ),
    (
        "WeightedMean(c)",
        "Velocity (closed channel)",
        1.0,
        vec![EllipticGauge::WeightedMean(mean)],
    ),
    (
        "Orthogonal",
        "Velocity (closed channel)",
        1.0,
        vec![EllipticGauge::Orthogonal],
    ),
    (
        "Unspecified",
        "Velocity (closed channel)",
        1.0,
        vec![EllipticGauge::Unspecified],
    ),
    ("Outlet flow rate × 1.01", "Velocity (closed channel)", 1.01, Vec::new()),
    (
        "WeightedMean(0) at a traction outlet",
        "Exact traction",
        1.0,
        vec![EllipticGauge::WeightedMean(0.0)],
    ),
];
let mut reference: Option<Vec<f64>> = None;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, outlet, outflow, gauges) in settings {
    let plan = match channel(2, outlet, outflow, gauges) {
        Ok(plan) => plan,
        Err(error) => {
            let kind = format!("{:?}", error.kind());
            rows.push((name, kind, None, None, None));
            continue;
        }
    };
    let state = plan.solve().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (offset, vertices) = (plan.velocity_unknowns(), plan.mesh().vertex_count());
    let pressure = &state[offset..offset + vertices];
    // Area mean (1/|Ω|) ∫ p dx. On a P1 triangle, the mean equals the mean of three vertex values;
    // the uniform mesh gives every triangle area |Ω| / cell count = 2 m² / cell count.
    let cells = plan.mesh().cell_count();
    let mut average = 0.0;
    for cell in 0..cells {
        let corners = plan.mesh().cell_vertices(cell).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        average += corners.iter().map(|&v| pressure[v]).sum::<f64>() / (3.0 * cells as f64);
    }
    let base = reference.get_or_insert_with(|| state[..offset].to_vec());
    let change = base
        .iter()
        .zip(&state[..offset])
        .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max);
    let sum: f64 = pressure.iter().sum();
    rows.push((
        name,
        "Accepted".to_string(),
        Some(average),
        Some(sum),
        Some(change),
    ));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Settings" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Result" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Mean pressure [Pa]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Sum of vertex pressures [Pa]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Velocity difference from first row [m/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "gauges" })
);
```

`WeightedMean(c)` sets the area mean to $c$; velocity differs from the first row by $3.6\times10^{-13}$ m/s, showing that the gauge shifts only the pressure constant.
This channel's exact pressure is antisymmetric about $x=L/2$, so `Orthogonal` (zero vertex sum) also gives zero area mean.
On a general mesh with nonuniform vertex weights, the two gauges' pressures differ by a constant.
`Unspecified` in a closed channel and gauges at traction outlets are rejected with `InvalidInput`.
Increasing outlet flow by a factor 1.01 sends 1% more flow out of the boundary, violating flux compatibility and making preparation return `DomainError`.

## Diagnostics and extracting fields

`diagnostics(x, length, step, scratch)` independently samples physical quantities from solution $x$ and returns `IncompressibleDiagnostics`.
`scratch` requires `LinearOperator::apply_workspace_len()` scalars.
These values are diagnostics, not error bounds or stability guarantees.

| Accessor | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `divergence_l2` | $L^2$ norm of $\nabla\cdot u_h$ sampled at volume quadrature points |
| `integrated_divergence` | $\lvert\int_\Omega\nabla\cdot u_h\,dx\rvert$ |
| `boundary_flux` | Magnitude of total outward boundary flux $\lvert\int_{\partial\Omega}u_h\cdot n\,ds\rvert$ |
| `weak_divergence` | Norm of discrete continuity equations $(b(u_h,\phi_v))_v$ before gauge augmentation |
| `gauge_defect` | Maximum magnitude of gauge constraint $\sum_v w_vp_v-c_{\mathrm{target}}$ |
| `reynolds` | Maximum sampled speed × `length` / $\nu$ |
| `courant` | Maximum of speed × `step` / cell length $h$ |
| `diffusion_number` | Maximum of $\nu$ × `step` / $h^2$ |

$h$ is the minimum cell altitude (Jacobian determinant divided by opposite-face area).
`length` must be positive and `step` finite and nonnegative; otherwise, `InvalidInput` is returned.

`fields(x)` converts the unknown vector into an owned `IncompressibleField` field.
`velocity()` is a componentwise P2 `SimplexFemField` (`vertices` holds vertex values, `edges` edge-midpoint values), while `pressure()` is a vertex `VertexField`; `into_parts` splits the two apart.
Gauge multipliers are solver coordinates and are not included in fields.
In reverse, construct `IncompressibleFieldView::new(velocity, pressure)` from borrowed fields of the same mesh revision and pack the unknown vector with `pack_fields_into(view, out)`.
Multipliers are set to 0; invalid shapes or values leave `out` unchanged.

## Summary

The mixed Stokes formulation produces a symmetric indefinite saddle-point system and is stable when the velocity–pressure space pair satisfies the inf-sup condition.
`StokesPlan` uses Taylor–Hood elements (P2/P1), reproducing exact quadratic velocity and linear pressure up to algebraic error.
Closed channels require balanced boundary flux, and gauges in `IncompressibleOptions` select the pressure constant.
Zero traction at an outlet also sets shear stress to zero in the symmetric-gradient form, so developed flows such as Poiseuille require their physical traction.
Taylor–Hood velocity satisfies only discrete continuity, so read `divergence_l2` and `weak_divergence` separately.
