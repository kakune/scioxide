This page explains how to solve the saddle-point system assembled in [Mixed finite elements for incompressible flow](../incompressible-flow/).
Steady Stokes uses block-preconditioned GMRES, while steady Navier–Stokes uses Newton–Krylov.
Both share the same `StokesPlan` spaces, boundaries, and pressure gauges.
The cell counts GMRES and Newton iterations under grid refinement.
Time evolution using a velocity-mass saddle-point system is covered in [Time-dependent incompressible flow](../incompressible-time-stepping/).

---

## Block preconditioning of saddle-point systems

The [saddle-point system](oxref:pde-incompressible-weak-form) $K=\begin{pmatrix}A&B^{\mathsf T}\\B&0\end{pmatrix}$ is symmetric indefinite, so conjugate gradients, which requires a positive-definite matrix, cannot be used.
$K$ has the block LDU factorization

$$
K=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\BA^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\0&-S\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&A^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}\\0&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad S=BA^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}
$$

where $S$ is called the Schur complement.
For right-hand side $(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g})$, the inverse acts in three stages.

1. Predict velocity $\mathbf{u}_0=A^{-1}\mathbf{f}$.
2. Compute pressure $\mathbf{p}=-S^{-1}(\mathbf{g}-B\mathbf{u}_0)$.
3. Correct velocity $\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_0-A^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{p}$.

Because $S$ is dense, `IncompressiblePreconditioner` uses the inverse with $S$ replaced by an approximation $\hat S$ as a preconditioner.
For steady viscous problems, use $\hat S=M_p/\mu$ ($M_p$ is the P1 pressure mass matrix).
Under a uniform inf-sup condition, $BA^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}$ and $M_p/\mu$ are spectrally equivalent, placing preconditioned eigenvalues in an interval independent of $h$ (Elman–Silvester–Wathen 2014).
Thus, with an accurate $A^{-1}$ action, GMRES iteration counts remain nearly constant under refinement.
Gauge multipliers on closed components are handled exactly through an augmented Schur complement, computing $\hat S^{-1}w$ once during preparation.

This inverse is generally indefinite and can be nonsymmetric, so combine it with GMRES rather than methods requiring positive-definite preconditioning.
Select the velocity-block $A^{-1}$ construction with `IncompressibleVelocityInverse`.

- `SparseCholesky` (default) factors the coupled velocity block by sparse Cholesky and solves it accurately.
  Factor size follows the limits in `IncompressiblePreconditionerOptions::with_factor_limits`.
- `Amg` applies algebraic multigrid (AMG) cycles to componentwise velocity blocks.
  Korn's inequality makes inverses of the componentwise diagonal blocks spectrally equivalent to the coupled $A$ inverse; componentwise constants (translations) supply AMG's near nullspace.

`IncompressiblePreconditionerOptions::new()` defaults to sparse Cholesky, AMG cycles with two pre- and two post-smoothing steps, and a limit of 4,000,000 entries per assembled block.
Change these through `with_velocity`, `with_amg_setup`, `with_amg_cycle`, `with_amg_limits`, `with_factor_limits`, and `with_max_entries`.
If the plan's estimated matrix entry count (`entry_bound`) exceeds the limit, preparation returns `ResourceLimit`.
`IncompressiblePreconditioner::new(plan, saddle, options)` prepares sequentially, and `build(…, scope)` prepares as an epoch of the caller's context.
Preparation allocates memory; application does not, and failure leaves output unchanged.

`IncompressibleSaddle` selects the saddle-point system to approximate.
`Viscous` is the steady Stokes system above, also kept frozen as a preconditioner for Newton corrections in Navier–Stokes.
`VelocityMass` is the velocity-mass saddle-point system $\begin{pmatrix}M&B^{\mathsf T}\\B&0\end{pmatrix}$ used to compute acceleration in [time evolution](oxref:pde-incompressible-time) ($M$ is the density-weighted P2 mass matrix).
Its velocity block uses the diagonal of $M$; the Schur approximation assembles $\hat S=B\,\mathrm{diag}(M)^{-1}B^{\mathsf T}$ and approximates it with AMG.
A temporarily fixed vertex removes the constant pressure mode, and gauge conditions are restored exactly.
This fixing changes only the preconditioner, not the physical operator.

## Solve calls and workspaces

`StokesPlan` has three calling patterns.

- `solve()` solves from zero with defaults and returns velocity, pressure, and gauge multipliers in unknown order as a `Vec`.
  Unsuccessful termination returns an error: `ExecutionStopped` for cancellation or deadlines, `ConvergenceFailure` otherwise.
- `solve_with_options(&options)` solves from zero and returns an `IterativeSolveResult`, including incomplete termination.
  Read `is_success()` and `termination()` on the result's `report()`.
- Pass the `StokesWorkspace` created by `workspace(&options)` to `solve_into(solution, &mut workspace, scope)` to solve using `solution` as the initial guess and reuse the workspace for subsequent solves.
  Read termination, including incomplete results, in the returned `IterativeSolveReport`.
  Errors leave `solution` unchanged.

`StokesWorkspace` holds solver, preconditioner, and Krylov storage; `with_linear_solver` and `with_preconditioner` replace the solver and preconditioner for subsequent solves.
Injected solvers must accept nonsymmetric indefinite systems.
`linear()` returns an `IterativeWorkspace` including the latest trial.

`IncompressibleSolveOptions::new()` defaults to true-residual tolerances $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ (absolute and relative), 1,000 GMRES(50) iterations, the default preconditioner, and default Newton–Krylov settings.
Change these with `with_residual`, `with_iterations`, `with_restart`, `with_preconditioner`, and `with_nonlinear`.
True-residual tolerances, iteration limits, and restart length apply to the Stokes saddle-point solve and the velocity-mass saddle-point solve in [time evolution](oxref:pde-incompressible-time).
Navier–Stokes Newton corrections follow `NewtonKrylovOptions` in `with_nonlinear` and do not use these values.
Relative tolerance is measured against right-hand-side norm $\|\mathbf{b}\|_2$, so tighten it until algebraic error is below discretization differences when comparing refined grids ([Stokes driven cavity](../../examples/stokes-cavity/)).

`IncompressibleOperator` borrows a mixed operator whose main block is viscous $A$ or velocity-mass $M$; divergence, gauges, and Dirichlet rows are shared.
`StokesPlan::mass_system()` returns the velocity-mass form, which can be passed to custom solvers as `LinearOperator` and `AdjointOperator`.
`assemble_csr` assembles CSR; `assembly_workspace` with `assemble_csr_into(output, workspace, scope)` rewrites an existing CSR of the same structure without allocation.

## Newton's method for Navier–Stokes

`NavierStokesPlan::new(stokes)` reuses the Stokes plan's spaces, viscosity, density, loads, and gauges, adding advection through the conservative weak form

$$
c(u;v)=-\int_\Omega\rho\,(u\otimes u):\nabla v\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\rho\,(u\cdot n)(u\cdot v)\,ds
$$


Undoing integration by parts gives $c(u;v)=\int_\Omega\rho\,\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)\cdot v\,dx$, so the natural condition remains the same traction $\sigma n$ as Stokes.
The plan supports real values only, providing residual $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x})=K\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}+\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})$ as `ResidualOperator` and analytical Jacobian products as `JacobianOperator`.
The derivative in direction $w$ is $-\int\rho\,(w\otimes u+u\otimes w):\nabla v\,dx+\int\rho\left((w\cdot n)(u\cdot v)+(u\cdot n)(w\cdot v)\right)ds$.

`solve_with_options(initial, &options)` solves with Newton–Krylov from `scioxide-rootfind` and returns `NewtonKrylovResult`.
Newton correction equations are solved approximately by GMRES (by default, up to 200 GMRES(30) iterations per correction), always using the frozen `Viscous` Stokes preconditioner.
The default Newton iteration limit is 100.
Globalization (line search), forcing terms, nonlinear tolerances, and termination reasons follow the same `NewtonKrylovOptions` specification as [Nonlinear steady problems](oxref:pde-nonlinear-residual).
For advection-dominated flow, the frozen Stokes preconditioner departs from the tangent, so convergence independent of Reynolds number is not promised.
Picard iteration (Oseen linearization) is not provided; at high Reynolds numbers, start from a lower-Reynolds solution and increase Reynolds number gradually (continuation).
The default residual tolerance is relative $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ and absolute 0; the relative value is measured against the initial residual.
With a good initial guess, failure to supply an absolute threshold through `with_residual_tolerance` can cause termination with `Stagnation`.

`solve(initial)` uses defaults and converts unsuccessful termination into errors of the same classifications.
Passing the `NavierStokesWorkspace` from `workspace(&options)` to `solve_into(initial, &mut output, &mut workspace, scope)` reuses Newton–Krylov storage and preconditioning, leaving `output` unchanged on error.
`with_linear_solver` solves Newton corrections with an injected iterative method; choose its tolerance to satisfy the nonlinear forcing-term check.
For repeated tangent use at a fixed state, evaluate it once with `linearize(point, &mut workspace, scope)` into the `NavierStokesLinearizationWorkspace` from `linearization_workspace()` and borrow it as CSR ([Three Jacobian representations](oxref:pde-jacobian-modes)).

The following definitions construct a plan for a closed channel $[0,2]\times[0,1]$ m, prescribing Poiseuille velocity on every boundary ($U=1$ m/s, $\rho=1$ kg/m³).

```rust
//| id: pde-incompressible-closed-channel
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Stokes plan for a closed channel"
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core]
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryData, BoundaryKind, BoundaryPoint, IncompressibleOptions, SimplexFemGeometry,
    StokesPlan, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{Point, TriangleMesh};

/// Poiseuille velocity u(y) = 4U y (H − y) / H² [m/s] (U = 1 m/s, H = 1 m).
pub(crate) fn poiseuille(y: f64) -> f64 {
    4.0 * y * (1.0 - y)
}

/// Stokes plan for channel [0, 2]×[0, 1] [m], divided into 2n × n squares split diagonally into triangles.
/// Because u(y) = 0 at walls, one Dirichlet condition prescribes inlet, outlet, and wall velocities.
pub(crate) fn closed_channel(
    cells: usize,
    viscosity: f64,
) -> Result<StokesPlan<f64, f64, 2>, String> {
    let (nx, ny) = (2 * cells, cells);
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..=ny {
        for i in 0..=nx {
            let point = [2.0 * i as f64 / nx as f64, j as f64 / ny as f64];
            points.push(Point::new(point).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
        }
    }
    let mut triangles = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..ny {
        for i in 0..nx {
            let a = j * (nx + 1) + i;
            triangles.push([a, a + 1, a + nx + 2]);
            triangles.push([a, a + nx + 2, a + nx + 1]);
        }
    }
    let mesh = TriangleMesh::new(points, triangles).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let data = BoundaryData::time_invariant(|p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| {
        Ok(if p.component == 0 {
            poiseuille(p.point[1])
        } else {
            0.0
        })
    });
    let boundaries = TaggedBoundaries::uniform(&mesh, 2, BoundaryKind::Dirichlet, data)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    StokesPlan::with_options(
        mesh,
        SimplexFemGeometry::Affine,
        1.0,
        viscosity,
        &boundaries,
        &|_| Ok([0.0; 2]),
        IncompressibleOptions::new(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
}
```

The next cell solves the same flow at $n=2,4,8$ and lists iteration counts and maximum nodal errors against Poiseuille flow.
It counts GMRES iterations for Stokes and Newton iterations for Navier–Stokes.
Default tolerances are retained, so nodal errors show algebraic error levels.

```rust
//| id: pde-incompressible-iterations
//| ref: pde-incompressible-iterations
//| title: "Count GMRES and Newton iterations under grid refinement"
//| run: button
//| timeoutMs: 300000
//| uses: [pde-incompressible-closed-channel]
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-rootfind]
//| inputs:
//|   inverse: {"type":"select","label":"Velocity-block inverse action","description":"Construct the viscous velocity-block A inverse with sparse Cholesky or componentwise AMG.","options":["Sparse Cholesky","AMG"],"value":"Sparse Cholesky"}
//|   equation: {"type":"select","label":"Equation","description":"Navier–Stokes uses Newton–Krylov with frozen Stokes preconditioning for every Newton correction.","options":["Stokes","Navier–Stokes"],"value":"Stokes"}
//|   reynolds: {"type":"range","label":"Reynolds number Re = ρUH/μ [-]","description":"Vary viscosity μ = 1/Re Pa·s with ρ = 1 kg/m³, U = 1 m/s, H = 1 m fixed.","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"amg","label":"AMG velocity inverse","description":"AMG only approximates A⁻¹, so GMRES counts increase slowly: 22, 30, 35.","inputs":{"inverse":"AMG"}}
//|   - {"id":"navier-stokes","label":"Navier–Stokes, Re = 10","description":"Newton reaches ResidualTolerance in six iterations on every grid.","inputs":{"equation":"Navier–Stokes"}}
//|   - {"id":"navier-stokes-re100","label":"Navier–Stokes, Re = 100","description":"Starting from zero makes correction GMRES stop at its iteration limit, producing LinearFailure(IterationLimit).","inputs":{"equation":"Navier–Stokes","reynolds":100}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":1276}
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"min":8,"max":16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: amg
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"min":20,"max":50}}
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"Converged"}
//|   - case: navier-stokes
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"ResidualTolerance"}
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: navier-stokes-re100
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"iterations","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":"LinearFailure(IterationLimit)"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"failed"}
use scioxide_linalg::LinearSolveTermination;
use scioxide_pde::{
    IncompressiblePreconditionerOptions, IncompressibleSolveOptions,
    IncompressibleVelocityInverse, NavierStokesPlan,
};
use scioxide_rootfind::RootTermination;

let velocity = if inverse == "AMG" {
    IncompressibleVelocityInverse::Amg
} else {
    IncompressibleVelocityInverse::SparseCholesky
};
let preconditioner = IncompressiblePreconditionerOptions::new().with_velocity(velocity);
let options = IncompressibleSolveOptions::new().with_preconditioner(preconditioner);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
// (termination reason, breakdown flag) for the first unsuccessful grid. Limits give partial; breakdown gives failed.
let mut first_failure: Option<(String, bool)> = None;
for cells in [2_usize, 4, 8] {
    let plan = closed_channel(cells, 1.0 / reynolds)?;
    let unknowns = plan.unknowns();
    let (state, iterations, status, success, broken) = if equation == "Stokes" {
        let result = plan
            .solve_with_options(&options)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let report = result.report();
        let broken = matches!(report.termination(), LinearSolveTermination::Breakdown(_));
        let status = format!("{:?}", report.termination());
        let state = result.solution().as_slice().to_vec();
        (
            state,
            report.iterations(),
            status,
            report.is_success(),
            broken,
        )
    } else {
        let flow = NavierStokesPlan::new(plan.clone());
        let initial = vec![0.0; unknowns];
        let result = flow
            .solve_with_options(&initial, &options)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let broken = matches!(
            result.termination(),
            RootTermination::LinearFailure(_)
                | RootTermination::GlobalizationFailure
                | RootTermination::NumericalFailure(_)
        );
        let status = result.termination().to_string();
        let success = result.is_success();
        (
            result.point().to_vec(),
            result.iterations(),
            status,
            success,
            broken,
        )
    };
    if !success && first_failure.is_none() {
        first_failure = Some((status.clone(), broken));
    }
    let mut error = 0.0_f64;
    for i in 0..plan.velocity_nodes() {
        let y = plan.node_point(i).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?[1];
        error = error.max((state[i] - poiseuille(y)).abs());
    }
    rows.push((cells, unknowns, iterations, error, status));
}
let last_status = rows[rows.len() - 1].4.clone();
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "n [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Unknowns [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Iterations [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum nodal velocity error [m/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "iterations" })
);
let outcome = match first_failure {
    None => Outcome::satisfied("Iteration reached tolerance on every grid").status(last_status),
    Some((status, true)) => Outcome::failed("Inner linear solves or globalization broke down on some grid")
        .explanation("Increase Reynolds number gradually, using the previous solution as the initial guess.")
        .status(status),
    Some((status, false)) => Outcome::partial("Some grid stopped at a limit").status(status),
};
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

At the defaults (sparse Cholesky, Stokes), GMRES needs 8, 12, and 12 iterations as unknowns increase from 106 to 1,276, showing little grid dependence.
Maximum nodal error at $n=8$ is $3.3\times10^{-8}$ m/s, the algebraic error from stopping at the default tolerance $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
AMG approximates $A^{-1}$, so iterations increase slowly to 22, 30, and 35.
In exchange, AMG can avoid sparse Cholesky factor fill for large three-dimensional problems.

For Navier–Stokes ($\mathrm{Re}=10$), Newton reaches `ResidualTolerance` in six iterations on every grid, with error at most $10^{-9}$ m/s.
Poiseuille flow is an exact solution with vanishing advection, and Newton converges to it.
Starting from zero at $\mathrm{Re}=100$ makes correction GMRES stop at its 200-iteration limit on every grid.
The checked linear residual also fails forcing-term bound $\eta_{\max}=0.9$, so Newton stops with `LinearFailure(IterationLimit)`.
The returned point is the last accepted iterate, retaining error 0.023 m/s even at $n=8$.
The frozen Stokes preconditioner can no longer approximate the advection-dominated tangent; improve the initial guess by continuation or supply a tangent-appropriate preconditioner through `NavierStokesWorkspace::with_preconditioner`.

## Summary

Solve the indefinite Stokes saddle-point system with GMRES and preconditioning approximating the block LDU inverse.
An accurate sparse Cholesky velocity solve and pressure-mass Schur approximation give nearly grid-independent iteration counts.
Navier–Stokes passes a conservative residual and analytical tangent to Newton–Krylov; because frozen Stokes preconditioning loses accuracy at high Reynolds numbers, prepare initial guesses by continuation.
When Navier–Stokes solves fail, distinguish limit termination from inner breakdown in the termination column, then revise the initial guess or preconditioner.
