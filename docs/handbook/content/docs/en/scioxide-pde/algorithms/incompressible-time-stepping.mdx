This page explains time advancement of the spatial discretization in [Mixed finite elements for incompressible flow](../incompressible-flow/).
`IncompressibleSystem` is an ODE system whose state contains only velocity; pressure comes from a velocity-mass saddle-point solve at every right-hand-side evaluation.
It explains the derivation, constraint defects, step limits, and initial projection, then follows an initially resting fluid approaching Poiseuille flow in a cell.
Steady saddle-point preconditioning and Newton's method for Navier–Stokes are covered in [Solving incompressible-flow saddle-point systems](../incompressible-solvers/); ODE methods are in the [Ordinary differential equations](../../../scioxide-ode/) chapter.

---

## Velocity–pressure differential-algebraic equations

Discretizing unsteady Stokes in space alone gives

$$
M\dot{\mathbf{u}}+A\mathbf{u}+B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{f},\qquad B\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{g}
$$


Pressure has no time derivative.
Differentiating constraint $B\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{g}$ once in time determines $\mathbf{p}$ algebraically, and a second differentiation is needed for a differential equation for $\mathbf{p}$, making this an index-2 differential-algebraic equation (DAE).
For time-independent $\mathbf{g}$, $B\dot{\mathbf{u}}=0$, so acceleration $\mathbf{a}=\dot{\mathbf{u}}$ and pressure are determined by the velocity-mass saddle-point system

$$
\begin{pmatrix}M&B^{\mathsf T}\\B&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{a}\\\mathbf{p}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{f}-A\mathbf{u}\\-\mathbf{d}/\tau\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf{d}=B\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}
$$


For Navier–Stokes, subtract advection $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{u})$ from right-hand side $\mathbf{f}-A\mathbf{u}$.
The lower-right term $-\mathbf{d}/\tau$ restores the constraint defect to 0 over time constant $\tau$; the defect obeys $\dot{\mathbf{d}}=-\mathbf{d}/\tau+\mathbf{e}$ ($\mathbf{e}$ is the saddle-point residual).
Thus, long-time defects do not accumulate proportionally to step count, remaining at the scale of $\tau\|\mathbf{e}\|$ and roundoff.

## `IncompressibleSystem` and workspaces

`IncompressibleSystem::stokes(plan, &mut work, forcing, maximum_step)` and `navier_stokes(…)` bind this right-hand side as a `scioxide_ode::OdeSystem`.
State coordinates are P2 velocities without Dirichlet conditions (in `free_velocity_indices` order); pressure is not integrated but recovered from the saddle-point system at every right-hand-side evaluation.
`forcing` adds time-dependent body force per unit volume, while mesh and boundary velocities remain fixed.
No implicit Jacobian is provided, so use an explicit ODE method.
`IncompressibleTimeWorkspace::new(plan, &options)` prepares GMRES, the library `VelocityMass` preconditioner, and all scratch arrays; `with_linear_solver` and `with_preconditioner` replace the solver and preconditioner.
Each evaluation independently checks the true saddle-point residual and returns `ConvergenceFailure` if it exceeds the `options` tolerance.

## Step limits for explicit methods

Viscosity limits explicit steps.
From the P2 inverse inequality, $\tau$ is the cellwise minimum of $h^2\rho/(64D\mu)$ ($h$ is the minimum cell altitude); if `maximum_step` is smaller, `maximum_step` is used.
`step_limit` returns this $\tau$; for Navier–Stokes it returns the smaller of this and the minimum sampled advection limit $h/(10D|u|)$.
Managed `scioxide-ode` steps respect this cap; callers selecting their own steps must apply `step_limit`.
Because $\tau\propto h^2$, halving grid width quadruples step count.

## Initial velocity projection and field recovery

Initial velocity must satisfy discrete continuity.
`validate_velocity(y)` checks this and returns `InvalidInput` otherwise.
`project_velocity_into(y, &mut out, ctx)` projects to the nearest continuity-satisfying velocity in the mass-matrix inner product.
`out` changes only after saddle-point convergence and independent residual and continuity checks.
`recover_into(t, y, &mut out, ctx)` returns velocity, pressure, and gauge multipliers at time $t$ in the order of the unknowns.

## Computing startup flow

The next cell projects a closed-channel state with zero interior velocity as its initial condition and advances with Dormand–Prince 5(4) to 0.4 times the viscous time $T=\rho H^2/\mu$.
Set $\mu=0.1$ Pa·s ($T=10$ s) and read maximum nodal differences from Poiseuille flow and discrete continuity defects at each time.

```rust
//| id: pde-incompressible-startup
//| ref: pde-incompressible-startup
//| title: "Follow a resting fluid approaching Poiseuille flow with a constrained-velocity ODE"
//| run: button
//| timeoutMs: 120000
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core, scioxide-ode, scioxide-linalg]
//| inputs:
//|   cells: {"type":"range","label":"Height-direction cell count n [-]","description":"The step cap decreases proportionally to the square of minimum altitude.","min":1,"max":3,"step":1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"coarse","label":"n = 1","description":"Minimum altitude doubles, reducing accepted steps to 103, about one quarter of the default.","inputs":{"cells":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"startup","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.1703036,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"startup","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":4.05998e-6,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"startup","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"startup","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/diagnostics/0/value","equals":410}
//|   - case: coarse
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/diagnostics/0/value","equals":103}
//|       - {"artifact":"startup","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.0613021,"rtol":1e-5}}
use scioxide_core::{
    ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, LinearOperator, ProblemToken, SolveBudgets, SolveIdentity,
    SolveState, algorithm_id,
};
use scioxide_linalg::Vector;
use scioxide_ode::{
    DormandPrince5, OdeMethod, OdeOptions, OdeOutputSchedule, OdeProblem, OdeState, OdeSystem,
};
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryData, BoundaryKind, BoundaryPoint, IncompressibleOptions,
    IncompressibleSolveOptions, IncompressibleSystem, IncompressibleTimeWorkspace,
    SimplexFemGeometry, StokesPlan, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{Point, TriangleMesh};

// Poiseuille flow u(y) = 4U y (H − y) / H² (U = 1 m/s, H = 1 m).
let poiseuille = |y: f64| 4.0 * y * (1.0 - y);
// Divide channel [0, 2]×[0, 1] [m] into 2n × n squares and split them diagonally into triangles.
let (nx, ny) = (2 * cells as usize, cells as usize);
let mut points = Vec::new();
for j in 0..=ny {
    for i in 0..=nx {
        let point = [2.0 * i as f64 / nx as f64, j as f64 / ny as f64];
        points.push(Point::new(point).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    }
}
let mut triangles = Vec::new();
for j in 0..ny {
    for i in 0..nx {
        let a = j * (nx + 1) + i;
        triangles.push([a, a + 1, a + nx + 2]);
        triangles.push([a, a + nx + 2, a + nx + 1]);
    }
}
let mesh = TriangleMesh::new(points, triangles).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Because u(y) = 0 on walls, prescribe inlet, outlet, and wall velocity with one Dirichlet condition.
let data = BoundaryData::time_invariant(move |p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| {
    Ok(if p.component == 0 {
        poiseuille(p.point[1])
    } else {
        0.0
    })
});
let boundaries = TaggedBoundaries::uniform(&mesh, 2, BoundaryKind::Dirichlet, data)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// μ = 0.1 Pa·s, ρ = 1 kg/m³, no body force.
let viscosity = 0.1;
let plan = StokesPlan::with_options(
    mesh,
    SimplexFemGeometry::Affine,
    1.0,
    viscosity,
    &boundaries,
    &|_| Ok([0.0; 2]),
    IncompressibleOptions::new(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = IncompressibleSolveOptions::new();
let mut work = IncompressibleTimeWorkspace::new(&plan, &options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let system =
    IncompressibleSystem::stokes(&plan, &mut work, None, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Projection and recovery use the caller's solve context. Identity values may be arbitrary.
let mut solve_state = SolveState::new();
let mut budgets = SolveBudgets::unbounded();
let token = ProblemToken::new(1);
let identity = SolveIdentity::new(token, algorithm_id!("handbook", "startup", 1), 0, 0);
let mut context = solve_state
    .begin_solve(ExecutionContext::sequential(), identity, &mut budgets)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Project zero interior velocity to match boundary velocity and discrete continuity.
let zero = vec![0.0; system.dimension()];
let mut initial = vec![0.0; zero.len()];
system
    .project_velocity_into(&zero, &mut initial, &mut context)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Output at fractions of viscous time T = ρH²/μ.
let viscous_time = 1.0 / viscosity;
let fractions = [0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4];
let times: Vec<f64> = fractions.iter().map(|f| f * viscous_time).collect();
let end = times[times.len() - 1];
let schedule = OdeOutputSchedule::Times {
    times,
    initial: false,
    final_state: false,
};
let ode_options = OdeOptions::new().with_output_schedule(schedule);
let solution = DormandPrince5::new()
    .solve(
        &OdeProblem::new(&system, end),
        &ode_options,
        OdeState::new(0.0, Vector::from_vec(initial)),
    )
    .map_err(|e| SciOxideError::from(e).to_string())?;
let mut full = vec![0.0; plan.unknowns()];
let mut scratch = vec![0.0; plan.apply_workspace_len().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (fraction, state) in fractions.iter().zip(solution.trajectory().states()) {
    let time = fraction * viscous_time;
    system
        .recover_into(time, state.as_slice(), &mut full, &mut context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut error = 0.0_f64;
    for i in 0..plan.velocity_nodes() {
        let y = plan.node_point(i).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?[1];
        error = error.max((full[i] - poiseuille(y)).abs());
    }
    let diagnostics = plan
        .diagnostics(&full, 1.0, 0.0, &mut scratch)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((*fraction, time, error, diagnostics.weak_divergence()));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "t / T [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Time t [s]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum difference from Poiseuille flow [m/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Weak divergence [m²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "startup" })
);
let report = solution.report();
// termination() is None only for an intermediate failed trial, so classify it as failed.
let outcome = if report.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Integrated to terminal time")
} else if report.termination().is_none() {
    Outcome::failed("Integration stopped without recording a termination reason")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Integration stopped before final time")
};
let status = report
    .termination()
    .map_or_else(|| "None".to_string(), |t| format!("{t:?}"));
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(status)
        .diagnostic("Accepted steps", report.accepted_steps() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Rejected steps", report.rejected_steps() as f64, None)
);
```

At the default $n=2$, differences between the projected initial state and Poiseuille flow decay exponentially: 0.17 m/s at $t/T=0.01$, $1.2\times10^{-3}$ m/s at $0.05$, and $4.1\times10^{-6}$ m/s at $0.1$.
At $t/T=0.4$, the difference is $1.3\times10^{-9}$ m/s.
Discrete continuity defects remain at most $2.5\times10^{-11}$ m²/s at every time and do not accumulate over steps.
Dormand–Prince accepted all 410 steps.
Step size is determined by the $D=2$ cap $\tau=h^2\rho/(128\mu)$ rather than error control, with 103 steps at $n=1$ and 922 at $n=3$, proportional to $n^2$.

## Summary

Unsteady incompressible flow is an index-2 DAE with pressure as a Lagrange multiplier; `IncompressibleSystem` computes acceleration and pressure from a velocity-mass saddle-point system to obtain a velocity-only ODE.
Constraint defects are restored over time constant $\tau$, remaining at the scale of the saddle-point residual times $\tau$ even over long integrations.
The step size for explicit methods is set by $\tau=h^2\rho/(64D\mu)$; halving grid width quadruples step count, so choose mesh resolution together with integration cost.
Project initial velocity to discrete continuity with `project_velocity_into` before integration; obtain pressure at desired times through `recover_into`.
