Each page in this section explains the mathematics and APIs for discretizing partial differential equations in space and solving the resulting algebraic equations or ODE systems.
This page collects the assumed notation and conventions, error decomposition, and a page map.

## Notation

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\Omega=[a,b]$, $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ | Domain (a one-dimensional interval or a $D=2,3$-dimensional region) |
| $u$, $u_h$ | Exact and discrete solutions |
| $k(x)$, $K(x)$ | Scalar diffusion coefficient (such as thermal conductivity) and diffusion tensor |
| $c(x)$, $f(x,t)$ | Reaction coefficient and source term |
| $x_0<x_1<\dots<x_N$, $h_i=x_{i+1}-x_i$ | One-dimensional vertices and interval widths; $h=\max_i h_i$ |
| $\mathbf{n}$, $\partial u/\partial n$ | Outward unit normal and derivative in that direction; $-u'$ at an interval's left endpoint and $u'$ at its right endpoint |
| $\mathbf{n}\cdot K\nabla u$ | Outward conormal flux; $k\,\partial u/\partial n$ in one dimension |
| $q=-k\,\partial u/\partial x$ | Rightward physical flux |
| $A$, $M$, $\mathbf{b}(t)$ | Operator, lumped mass (positive diagonal), and load at time $t$ assembled by a spatial plan |
| $\mathbf{M}$, $\mathbf{K}$, $\mathbf{F}$ | Mass matrix, stiffness matrix, and load of semidiscrete system $\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{u}}+\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{F}$; API notation $M\mathbf{u}'=\mathbf{b}(t)-A\mathbf{u}$ ($A=\mathbf{K}$, $\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{F}$) |
| $\Delta t$, $\nu=c\Delta t/h$, $r=\alpha\Delta t/h^2$ | Time step, wave Courant number ($c$ is wave speed), and diffusion Fourier number ($\alpha$ is diffusivity) |

## Shared conventions

### Division between plans and solves

Spatial plans (`StructuredFdmPlan`, `SimplexFemPlan`, `StructuredFvmPlan`, `SimplexFvmPlan`) assemble time-independent operator $A$, positive lumped mass $M$, and arbitrary-time load $\mathbf{b}(t)$, implementing shared contract `SpatialDiscretization`.
`EllipticProblem`, `ParabolicProblem`, and `HyperbolicProblem` solve any plan through the same procedure; steady solves evaluate data at time 0.
Elasticity, incompressible-flow, conservation-law, and adaptive-refinement plans either provide their own solves or pass residuals or operators to `scioxide-linalg`, `scioxide-sparse`, `scioxide-rootfind`, or `scioxide-ode` solvers.

### Configuration, preparation, and execution

Every operation proceeds through configuration, preparation, and execution.
Prepared plans are immutable and `Send + Sync + Clone`; reusable solve storage and the last attempt record reside in caller-owned workspaces.
Functions take no caller identifiers.

### Unknown ordering and field locations

Unknowns are grouped by component (component-major).
FDM and P1 FEM unknowns lie at vertices, P2 FEM unknowns at vertices and edge midpoints, and FVM unknowns at cells.
One-dimensional FVM returns $N$ cell values for $N+1$ vertices, approximating cell averages by the midpoint rule.
Inspect each unknown's position and component with `SpatialDiscretization::dof_point`.
Vertex and cell fields have distinct `scioxide-spatial` types; compare by interpolating to common evaluation points or using fluxes or integral quantities.

### Eliminating Dirichlet conditions

Rows and columns of Dirichlet-constrained unknowns take identity-matrix form, with their values entering through the load.
Elimination is symmetric, preserving matrix symmetry for symmetric problems.

### Boundary signs

Across discretizations, Neumann and Robin data specify outward conormal flux $\mathbf{n}\cdot K\nabla u$, not the normal derivative itself.
Its sign is opposite to physical outward diffusive flux $q_n=-\mathbf{n}\cdot K\nabla u$; the data are $g=-q_n$.
Robin data have form $\mathbf{n}\cdot K\nabla u+\alpha u=g$, with the flux coefficient fixed to 1.

### Mesh identity

Boundary-condition sets and fields retain mesh family and revision (`MeshToken`); independently constructed meshes are rejected at runtime even with identical coordinates.

### Termination reasons and errors

Determine solve success solely from `PdeTermination`; `is_success()` is true only for `Completed`.
For example, an injected iterative solver reaching a limit returns an incomplete-termination report in `Ok`.
Input, callback, assembly, and factorization failures return `SciOxideError`; inspect classifications through `kind()` and `view()`.

## Error decomposition

Numerical-solution error is bounded above by the sum of spatial discretization, time integration, algebraic stopping, and rounding errors.

$$
\|u-u_h\|\le e_{\mathrm{space}}(h^p)+e_{\mathrm{time}}(\Delta t^q)+e_{\mathrm{linear}}+e_{\mathrm{round}}
$$

$p$ and $q$ are spatial and temporal convergence orders.
Refining space and time simultaneously obscures which error decreased.
Measure spatial order by halving $h$ with a fixed sufficiently small time step, and temporal order by halving $\Delta t$ on a fixed grid.
Choose inner linear-solver tolerances two to three orders of magnitude below discretization error.
Residuals of `PdeDiagnostics` indicate $e_{\mathrm{linear}}$, not estimates of $e_{\mathrm{space}}$.
Check independently using known solutions, manufactured solutions (the Method of Manufactured Solutions chooses a solution first and derives the source), conserved quantities, or comparisons between discretizations.

## Summary

Every page shares component-major unknown ordering, boundary data as outward conormal fluxes, symmetric Dirichlet elimination, and success determined by `PdeTermination`.
Understanding the spatial plan's $A$, $M$, and $\mathbf{b}(t)$ lets you solve any discretization's results with the same solver plans.
Measure spatial, temporal, algebraic, and rounding errors separately, and do not confuse small residuals with discretization accuracy.
