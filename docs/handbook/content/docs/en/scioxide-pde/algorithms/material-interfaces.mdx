Many problems have a jump in conductivity at a material boundary, as in a wall made of layers of insulation and metal.
The derivative of the solution is also discontinuous where a coefficient is discontinuous, so accuracy depends strongly on where the discretization reads the coefficient.
This page derives the series-resistance formula from the interface conditions and varies the interface position to examine how the three discretizations in [FDM, FEM, and FVM for elliptic problems](../elliptic-fdm-fem-fvm/) handle the interface.

## Interface conditions and series resistance

Suppose two materials with conductivities $k_L$ and $k_R$ meet at $x=s$.
If temperature is continuous at the interface and no heat accumulates there, the temperature and flux are equal on both sides of the interface.

$$
u(s^-)=u(s^+),\qquad -k_L\,u'(s^-)=-k_R\,u'(s^+)
$$

Because the flux is equal but the conductivities differ, the temperature gradient $u'$ jumps at the interface, producing a kink in the solution.

Consider heat flow between two points $x_L<s<x_R$ on opposite sides of the interface.
Writing the distances as $d_L=s-x_L$ and $d_R=x_R-s$, the thermal resistances of the two intervals, $R_L=d_L/k_L$ and $R_R=d_R/k_R$, are connected in series.
The flux between the two points is $q=(u_L-u_R)/(R_L+R_R)$, and the effective conductivity is a weighted harmonic mean.

$$
k^{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{d_L+d_R}{d_L/k_L+d_R/k_R}
$$

If $d_L=d_R$, then $k^{\mathrm{eff}}=2k_Lk_R/(k_L+k_R)$.
The arithmetic mean $(k_L+k_R)/2$ is pulled toward the higher conductivity and cannot represent the series structure in which the less conductive layer limits the flow.
For example, with $k_L=1$ and $k_R=100$, the harmonic mean is about 1.98 and the arithmetic mean is 50.5.

## Coefficient sampling locations and the interface

The three discretizations read coefficients at different locations, so the appropriate interface placement also differs.

- **FVM** (`StructuredFvmPlan`, `SimplexFvmPlan`) reads the diffusion coefficient at the cell center and assigns that material to the entire cell.
  The face conductance uses a [two-point flux approximation](oxref:pde-fvm-tpfa) that connects the resistances of two half-cells in series, so when the interface coincides with a cell face, simply returning each material's value gives the harmonic mean above.
- **FDM** (`StructuredFdmPlan`) reads the diffusion coefficient at the interval midpoint and uses it for the entire interval.
  If the interface coincides with a vertex, each interval contains only one material.
- **FEM** (`SimplexFemPlan`) reads coefficients at the element quadrature points.
  If the interface coincides with a vertex, each element contains only one material, and quadrature integrates a smooth coefficient.

On a one-dimensional structured grid, vertices and cell faces are the same points, so aligning the interface with a vertex provides the appropriate conditions for all three discretizations.
If the interface lies inside a cell, FVM and FDM treat that cell or interval as one material, shifting the effective interface to the nearest face or vertex.
The interface position error is of the order of the grid spacing $h$, and the solution error also degrades to $O(h)$.
In FEM, the quadrature points of the element containing the interface lie on both sides of it, and polynomial quadrature integrates a discontinuous coefficient, so the error depends on the relative positions of the interface and the quadrature points.

## Checking interface flux and temperature

Divide a 1 m rod into two materials at $x=s$ and maintain $u(0)=1$ and $u(1)=0$ (with no internal generation).
The exact solution is a piecewise linear function with a kink at the interface, and the flux has the constant value $q=1/(s/k_L+(1-s)/k_R)$ everywhere in the rod.
The cell below sets $k_L=1$ and takes the conductivity ratio $k_R/k_L$ and interface position as inputs.
On uniform grids with $N=20,40,80$ cells, it makes `IsotropicDiffusion` return the conductivity of each material and solves with the three FDM, FVM, and P1 FEM plans.
It compares the maximum temperature errors at the unknown locations and, for FVM, reconstructs the flux on every face with `StructuredFvmPlan::face_fluxes` and compares it with $q$.
`face_fluxes` returns the diffusive flux $-k\,u'$ in the direction of increasing $x$, integrated over the face (the face value itself in one dimension).

Choose between two interface positions.
$s=0.4$ coincides with a cell face on every grid.
$s=1/3$ does not coincide with a face on any grid, and its distance to the nearest face is $1/3$ of the grid spacing.

```rust
//| id: pde-interface-resistance
//| ref: pde-interface-resistance
//| title: "Vary the interface position and compare FDM, FVM, and FEM errors"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   conductivity_ratio: {"type":"range","label":"Conductivity ratio kR/kL [-]","description":"Vary how easily the right material conducts heat.","min":0.05,"max":20,"step":0.05,"value":5}
//|   interface: {"type":"select","label":"Interface position s","options":["s = 0.4 (aligned with a cell face)","s = 1/3 (inside a cell)"],"value":"s = 0.4 (aligned with a cell face)"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"inside-cell","label":"Place the interface inside a cell (s = 1/3)","description":"The errors of all three discretizations rise from roundoff level to about 10⁻², and those of FDM and FVM roughly halve each time N doubles.","inputs":{"interface":"s = 1/3 (inside a cell)"}}
//|   - {"id":"insulating-right","label":"Make the right material 20 times more insulating (kR/kL = 0.05)","description":"The flux falls to 0.080645, and the error stays at roundoff level as long as the interface coincides with a face.","inputs":{"conductivity_ratio":0.05}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":null}
//|   - case: inside-cell
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":5e-3,"max":5e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"min":2e-3,"max":2e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/7/2","range":{"min":1e-3,"max":1e-2}}
//|   - case: insulating-right
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0,"max":5e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interface","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryData, BoundaryKind, BoundaryPoint, EllipticProblem, FieldData, IsotropicDiffusion,
    SimplexFemGeometry, SimplexFemOrder, SimplexFemPlan, SpatialDiscretization,
    StructuredBoundaries, StructuredFdmPlan, StructuredFvmPlan, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{
    AxisTopology, Cell, CoordinateAxis, FieldView, Grid1d, IntervalMesh, Point, SegmentMesh,
};

// Solve and return the maximum error against the exact solution at the unknown locations, together with the unknown coordinates and values.
fn solve<P: SpatialDiscretization<f64, f64, 1>>(
    plan: &P,
    exact: &dyn Fn(f64) -> f64,
) -> Result<(f64, Vec<[f64; 2]>, Vec<f64>), String> {
    let values = EllipticProblem::new(plan, &FieldData::zero())
        .solve()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut profile = Vec::new();
    for (i, &u) in values.iter().enumerate() {
        profile.push([plan.dof_point(i).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point[0], u]);
    }
    let error = profile
        .iter()
        .map(|[x, u]| (u - exact(*x)).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max);
    Ok((error, profile, values))
}
let s = if interface.starts_with("s = 0.4") {
    0.4
} else {
    1.0 / 3.0
};
let kr = conductivity_ratio;
// Independent check: constant flux from series resistance and the exact piecewise linear temperature.
let q = 1.0 / (s + (1.0 - s) / kr);
let exact = move |x: f64| {
    if x <= s {
        1.0 - q * x
    } else {
        1.0 - q * s - q * (x - s) / kr
    }
};
let k = IsotropicDiffusion::new(move |x: [f64; 1]| Ok(if x[0] < s { 1.0 } else { kr }));
// Set u = 1 at the left end, whose outward normal is negative, and u = 0 at the right end.
let ends = BoundaryData::time_invariant(|p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 1>| {
    Ok(if p.normal[0] < 0.0 { 1.0 } else { 0.0 })
});
let exact_points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=300)
    .map(|i| i as f64 / 300.0)
    .map(|x| [x, exact(x)])
    .collect();
let dashed = serde_json::json!({ "lineType": "dashed" });
let mut series =
    vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Exact solution", "points": exact_points, "style": dashed })];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for n in [20_usize, 40, 80] {
    let axis = CoordinateAxis::uniform(0.0, 1.0, n + 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let grid = Grid1d::new([axis], [AxisTopology::Bounded]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mesh = SegmentMesh::new(grid).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let dirichlet = BoundaryKind::Dirichlet;
    let structured = StructuredBoundaries::uniform(&mesh, 1, dirichlet, ends.clone())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let fdm =
        StructuredFdmPlan::new(mesh.clone(), &k, &structured).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let fvm =
        StructuredFvmPlan::new(mesh.clone(), &k, &structured).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let points = (0..=n)
        .map(|i| Point::new([i as f64 / n as f64]))
        .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let interval = IntervalMesh::new(points, (0..n).map(|i| [i, i + 1]).collect())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let tagged = TaggedBoundaries::uniform(&interval, 1, dirichlet, ends.clone())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (geometry, p1) = (SimplexFemGeometry::Affine, SimplexFemOrder::P1);
    let fem =
        SimplexFemPlan::new(interval, geometry, p1, &k, &tagged).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (fdm_error, fdm_profile, _) = solve(&fdm, &exact)?;
    let (fvm_error, fvm_profile, values) = solve(&fvm, &exact)?;
    let (fem_error, fem_profile, _) = solve(&fem, &exact)?;
    // Reconstruct FVM face fluxes (in the direction of increasing x) on every face and compare with the constant flux q.
    let cells = FieldView::<_, Cell, 1>::new(&mesh, &values).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let fluxes = fvm
        .face_fluxes::<0>(cells, 0.0)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let flux_error = fluxes
        .values()
        .iter()
        .map(|f| (f - q).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max)
        / q;
    rows.push(("FDM", n, fdm_error, None));
    rows.push(("FVM", n, fvm_error, Some(flux_error)));
    rows.push(("FEM P1", n, fem_error, None));
    if n == 20 {
        for (name, profile) in [
            ("FDM", fdm_profile),
            ("FVM", fvm_profile),
            ("FEM P1", fem_profile),
        ] {
            let style = serde_json::json!({ "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true });
            let name = format!("{name}（N = 20）");
            series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": profile, "style": style }));
        }
    }
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Position along the rod x [m]",
        "yLabel": "Temperature u [-]",
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": s, "label": "Material interface" }]
    })
);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Discretization" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Number of cells N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum temperature error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum relative face-flux error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "-" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "interface" })
);
```

With the defaults ($k_R/k_L=5$, $s=0.4$), the exact flux is $q=1/(0.4+0.6/5)\approx1.923$.
The temperature errors of all three discretizations were at most $2\times10^{-14}$ on every grid, and the relative FVM face-flux error was at most $10^{-13}$.
The exact piecewise linear solution is representable by every discretization when the interface coincides with a vertex, so the error is at roundoff level.

With $s=1/3$, the temperature errors for $N=20$, 40, and 80 were $1.8\times10^{-2}$, $1.0\times10^{-2}$, and $4.9\times10^{-3}$ for FDM, and $1.9\times10^{-2}$, $9.7\times10^{-3}$, and $5.0\times10^{-3}$ for FVM.
They roughly halve each time $N$ doubles, indicating first-order convergence.
The relative FVM face-flux errors also halve, taking the values $2.8\times10^{-2}$, $1.4\times10^{-2}$, and $7.1\times10^{-3}$.
The flux remains the same on every face but differs from the exact $q$ by the amount caused by the shifted effective interface.

The P1 FEM errors are $2.9\times10^{-2}$, $5.0\times10^{-3}$, and $7.5\times10^{-3}$ and do not decrease monotonically with $N$.
The two-point Gauss rule has quadrature points at about 0.211 and 0.789 of the element length; in the element containing $s=1/3$, one point lies in each material for every $N$.
The element stiffness is integrated using the arithmetic mean conductivity $(k_L+k_R)/2$, and the difference from series resistance depends on where the interface lies within the element.
The interface lies at $2/3$ of the element length from its left end for $N=20$ and 80, and at $1/3$ for $N=40$, so the error magnitudes switch between grids.

## Summary

At an interface with discontinuous conductivity, temperature and flux are continuous, and the effective conductivity between two points spanning the interface is the weighted harmonic mean from series resistance.
FVM reads coefficients at cell centers, FDM at interval midpoints, and FEM at quadrature points, so in one dimension, align the interface with a vertex (cell face) and return each cell's material value.
When the interface cannot be aligned with the grid, expect the error to degrade to $O(h)$ and refine the grid to check how the error decreases.
In FVM, reconstructing fluxes on every face with `face_fluxes` directly verifies that the flux through the interface is constant.
