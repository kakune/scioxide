The method of lines first discretizes only the spatial derivatives of a partial differential equation to obtain a system of ordinary differential equations in time (a semidiscrete system), then solves it with an ODE integrator.
`scioxide-pde` handles spatial discretization and normalization of the semidiscrete system, while the integrators in `scioxide-ode` handle time steps, error control, and implicit-stage linear systems.
This page explains normalization when passing a semidiscrete system to an integrator, semidiscrete systems built from plans, second-order dynamics, implicit-stage preconditioning, and how to read the results.
Eigenvalues and stability of semidiscrete diffusion systems are covered in [Time integration of parabolic equations](../parabolic-time-integration/), integration of an actual coupled system in [Method of lines for a two-temperature model](../../examples/two-temperature-method-of-lines/), and steady solutions with the same right-hand side in [Nonlinear steady problems](../nonlinear-steady-problems/).

## Semidiscrete systems

Spatial discretization by finite differences, finite volumes, or finite elements gives the time evolution of the free unknowns $\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^n$ in the form

$$
\mathbf{M}\,\frac{d\mathbf{y}}{dt}=\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y})
$$

as described in the [shared spatial discretization contract](oxref:pde-spatial-discretization-contract).
The free unknowns exclude nodes whose values are prescribed by Dirichlet conditions.
$\mathbf{M}$ is the mass matrix; `scioxide-pde` uses lumped diagonal mass (grid control widths, cell volumes, or diagonally scaled finite element mass).
$\mathbf{f}$ is a residual in physical units; for heat conduction, for example, each nodal equation is multiplied by a volume.
A second-order system such as the wave equation can be written as $\mathbf{M}\,\ddot{\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{u})$.

An ODE integrator accepts the form $\dot{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{g}(t,\mathbf{z})$.
If the mass is a positive diagonal matrix, multiplying by $\mathbf{M}^{-1}$ gives this form, with no need to treat it as a differential-algebraic equation (DAE).
The role of `MethodOfLinesPlan` is to divide by the mass in one place rather than requiring providers to do so.

## Normalization by diagonal mass and coordinate scales

`MethodOfLinesPlan` holds positive real diagonal matrices $\mathbf{M}=\operatorname{diag}(m_i)$ and $\mathbf{D}=\operatorname{diag}(d_i)$.
$\mathbf{D}$ is the coordinate scale, with the physical state expressed as $\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{D}\mathbf{z}$.
Using $\mathbf{z}$ as the integrator state gives

$$
\frac{d\mathbf{z}}{dt}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\,\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{z}),\qquad
J_z=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\,J\,\mathbf{D}
$$

where $J=\partial\mathbf{f}/\partial\mathbf{y}$.
Componentwise, $(J_z)_{ij}=J_{ij}\,d_j/(m_i d_i)$, and the sparsity pattern is the same as that of $J$.
Each time the `MethodOfLinesSystem` returned by `MethodOfLinesPlan` receives $\mathbf{z}$ from the integrator, it converts it back to $\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{D}\mathbf{z}$, calls the provider's $\mathbf{f}$, and returns the result multiplied by $\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{D}^{-1}$.
The Jacobian and its products undergo the same transformation before being passed to the integrator.

Coordinate scales bring quantities whose magnitudes differ between fields (temperature and concentration, electron and lattice temperatures, and so on) to comparable sizes.
Because error control measures errors with componentwise weights $\mathrm{atol}_i+\mathrm{rtol}\,|z_i|$, the physical absolute tolerance $\mathrm{atol}^{\mathrm{phys}}_i$ must be converted to $\mathrm{atol}_i=\mathrm{atol}^{\mathrm{phys}}_i/d_i$.
`error_tolerance(&physical)` performs this conversion once, preserving the relative tolerance and norm type.
If a converted absolute tolerance rounds to 0, an error is returned.
With this conversion, the error criterion remains the same in physical units when scales change.
Changing scales does, however, change the 2-norm used when solving implicit-stage linear systems with a Krylov method and the perturbation size (proportional to the state magnitude) when an integrator approximates the Jacobian by finite differences.
The finite-difference Jacobian product in `scioxide-ode` chooses the perturbation from the maximum ratio of direction-vector components to state-component magnitudes, so bringing component magnitudes into line also brings their finite-difference accuracy into line.

Convert between the physical state and normalized coordinates with `encode_into` ($\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{y}$) and `decode_into` ($\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{D}\mathbf{z}$).
Normalize initial values with `encode_into` before placing them in `OdeState`, and convert integrator output back to the physical state with `decode_into`.
Incorrect shapes, non-finite values, and unrepresentable values return errors without changing the output.
When multiplication or division by a scale threatens to exceed the floating-point range, the calculation is repeated from the original mass and scales with an extended exponent range, so overflow or underflow of intermediate products alone does not cause failure.

The provider's `OdeSystem` must declare unit mass (`mass_matrix_revision` is `None`).
A provider declaring mass is rejected at binding with `InvalidInput`.
If the mass depends on parameters or state, use a residual DAE in `scioxide-ode` instead of this plan.
A step-size limit declared by the provider (`step_limit`) assumes unit mass, so it is not forwarded to the normalized system.
Preconditioners built in physical coordinates are also not forwarded because they do not match the stage equation after normalization ([Implicit-stage preconditioning](#implicit-stage-preconditioning)).

## Plans, workspaces, and binding

`MethodOfLinesPlan::new(dimension, mass, scales)` takes the dimension $n$, mass, and scales.
`None` denotes the identity matrix, and dimension 0 denotes a system with no free unknowns (all unknowns constrained).
Length mismatches, nonpositive or non-finite values, size overflow, and allocation failure return errors before numerical callbacks are called.
The following settings can be added to a constructed plan.

- `with_order(MethodOfLinesOrder)`: choose first-order (`First`, the default) or second-order (`Second`) dynamics ([Second-order dynamics](#second-order-dynamics)).
- `with_jacobian_pattern(pattern)`: declare the provider's Jacobian sparsity pattern.
  This is used for sparse Jacobians, automatic step-size limits, and the second-order pattern.
- `with_step_limit(cfl, stability)`: specify an explicit step-size limit policy (`CflPolicy`) and the integrator's stability region (`OdeLinearStability`).
  A plan starts without a limit (`CflPolicy::Disabled`).

`workspace()` allocates a `MethodOfLinesWorkspace`.
Default storage consists of three vectors of length $n$ and values for the nonzero entries in the declared pattern, requiring $O(n+\mathrm{nnz})$ space.
To supply a dense Jacobian to the integrator for a first-order system, preallocate $n\times n$ temporary storage with `with_dense_jacobian()` before binding.
The workspace is created without numerical callbacks and can be reused between independent bindings.

`bind(&provider, &mut workspace)` borrows the provider and workspace and returns a `MethodOfLinesSystem`.
`MethodOfLinesSystem` implements `scioxide_ode::OdeSystem` and can be passed directly to `OdeProblem::new(&system, end)`.
The integrator handles acceptance decisions and stage solves; this type performs only normalization.
Binding rejects dimension mismatches, providers declaring mass, and missing stability regions or patterns required for automatic step-size limits.

The derivatives that a normalized system returns to the integrator depend on the prepared storage and declared pattern.

| Integrator request | First-order system | Second-order system |
|---|---|---|
| Right-hand side, Jacobian product | Return transformed provider values | Return transformed provider values |
| Sparse Jacobian | Return only for requests matching the declared pattern | Return with the doubled pattern derived from the declared pattern |
| Dense Jacobian | Return only when `with_dense_jacobian` has been preallocated | Do not return |

Requests for unprepared derivatives return `None` without calling the provider.
With the default derivative selection of implicit integrators in `scioxide-ode` (`AnalyticOrAutomaticOrForward`), receiving `None` from both the analytic Jacobian and automatic differentiation switches to forward differences of the normalized system.
Dense and sparse Jacobians are normalized in temporary storage before being written to the output, so errors leave the output unchanged.

A provider that eliminates constrained unknowns can implement `MethodOfLinesFields` to reconstruct the full physical field.
When bound with `bind_with_fields(&provider, &fields, &mut workspace)`, `reconstruct_into(time, state, out)` writes the full physical field at time `time` from the normalized state (displacement only for a second-order system).
`field_len()` gives its length, which is $n$ for a system bound without reconstruction.
Bind with `bind_adjoint` to compute a discrete adjoint with respect to parameters (first-order systems only; [Discrete adjoints for time-dependent problems](../transient-adjoints/)).

## Second-order dynamics

`MethodOfLinesOrder::Second` passes the undamped system $\mathbf{M}\ddot{\mathbf{u}}=\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{u})$ to the integrator as the following first-order system, adding the velocity $\mathbf{v}=\dot{\mathbf{u}}$:

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}_z\\ \mathbf{v}_z\end{pmatrix}=
\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{v}_z\\ \mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{u}_z)\end{pmatrix}
$$

The state length becomes $2n$ (`state_dimension()`), and both displacement and velocity use the same scale $\mathbf{D}$.
Vector tolerances also specify the $2n$ components of $[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}]$.
The Jacobian pattern is $\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\P&0\end{pmatrix}$, derived from the provider's pattern $P$; second-order systems write only sparse Jacobians and products to the output.
Its use for the wave equation is described in [Conversion to a first-order system and the method of lines](oxref:pde-wave-first-order-system).

## Explicit step-size limits

`CflPolicy::Automatic` computes the maximum absolute row sum $\rho$ of the normalized Jacobian $J_z$ over the declared pattern (an upper bound on eigenvalue magnitudes from Gershgorin discs).
Using either the real-axis stability interval length $\beta_r$ or the imaginary-axis length $\beta_i$ declared by the integrator through `OdeLinearStability`, it limits the step to

$$
h\le0.9\,\frac{\beta_r}{\rho}\quad(\text{first order}),\qquad h\le0.9\,\frac{\beta_i}{\sqrt{\rho}}\quad(\text{second order})
$$

The first-order limit assumes real nonpositive eigenvalues (symmetric diffusion with diagonal mass); the second-order limit assumes real nonpositive eigenvalues of $J_z$, meaning that the eigenvalues of the converted first-order system are purely imaginary.
No limit is imposed on integrators with infinite stability intervals (such as backward Euler), and if the Jacobian is constant, $\rho$ is computed once and reused.
`CflPolicy::MaximumStep(h)` specifies a limit directly, while `CflPolicy::Disabled` leaves step selection entirely to error control.
Diffusion limits appear in [ParabolicProblem and time integration schemes](oxref:pde-parabolic-schemes), and amplification factors and stability in [Amplification factors and stability of the θ method](oxref:pde-explicit-implicit-stability).

## Semidiscrete systems built from plans

`SemidiscreteSystem::new(&plan, &source)` creates a semidiscrete system from a spatial discretization plan in `scioxide-pde` (such as `StructuredFdmPlan`).
From the matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and load $\mathbf{b}(t)$ assembled by the plan, it constructs the physical residual of the free unknowns,

$$
\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{b}_f(t)-\mathbf{A}_{ff}\,\mathbf{y}
$$

where the subscript $f$ denotes rows and columns for free unknowns.
Dirichlet unknowns are eliminated from the state, with their contribution included in the load $\mathbf{b}_f(t)$.
The Jacobian is the constant matrix $-\mathbf{A}_{ff}$, assembled once during construction.
The pattern retains a diagonal entry even for rows with a zero diagonal, so the pattern of the implicit-stage matrix $\mathbf{I}-\gamma J_z$ always includes the diagonal and sparse LU factorization never encounters a diagonal position missing from the pattern.

Construction prepares the free unknown indices (`free_indices`), their lumped mass (`free_mass`), and the Jacobian pattern (`jacobian_pattern`).
`method_of_lines(order)` returns a `MethodOfLinesPlan` configured with this mass and pattern.
`initial_state(&initial)` samples the initial-value data at free unknown locations at time 0.
`SemidiscreteSystem` also implements `MethodOfLinesFields`, so passing the same value as both provider and reconstruction to `bind_with_fields(&system, &system, &mut workspace)` allows `reconstruct_into` to retrieve the full field, including constrained nodes.

The load evaluates the source and boundary data on every call.
If both the source and the plan's load are declared time-independent, the first successfully computed load is reused for the lifetime of the system.
Recreate the system when data captured by callbacks changes.
The right-hand side and Jacobian products are computed without allocation, and errors leave the output unchanged.
`ParabolicProblem` and `HyperbolicProblem` integrate internally using this semidiscrete system and `MethodOfLinesPlan`.

The following cell constructs a semidiscrete system from a finite-difference Laplacian on the interval $[0,1]$ with $N$ grid points (Dirichlet conditions at both ends) and checks the result of elimination.
The first table lists each free unknown's node index in the plan, position, and lumped mass; the second gives the system sizes.

```rust
//| id: pde-semidiscrete-elimination
//| ref: pde-semidiscrete-elimination
//| title: "Check semidiscrete system sizes after eliminating Dirichlet nodes"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde]
//| inputs:
//|   elimination_nodes: {"type":"range","label":"Grid point count N [points]","description":"Number of grid points, including both ends.","min":5,"max":41,"step":4,"value":9}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fine","label":"N = 41","description":"There are 39 free unknowns, with mass h = 1/40.","inputs":{"elimination_nodes":41}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"free","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"free","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.125,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":7}
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":19}
//|   - case: fine
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":39}
//|       - {"artifact":"free","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.025,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_pde::{
    FieldData, MethodOfLinesFields, MethodOfLinesOrder, SemidiscreteSystem,
    SpatialDiscretization, StructuredFdmPlan,
};

let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let n = elimination_nodes as usize;
let plan =
    StructuredFdmPlan::dirichlet_laplacian([0.0], [1.0], [n]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let source = FieldData::zero();
let system = SemidiscreteSystem::new(&plan, &source).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// MethodOfLinesPlan configured with the mass and pattern of the free unknowns.
let adapter = system
    .method_of_lines(MethodOfLinesOrder::First)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (k, (&unknown, &mass)) in system
    .free_indices()
    .iter()
    .zip(system.free_mass())
    .enumerate()
{
    let x = plan.dof_point(unknown).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point[0];
    rows.push((k, unknown, x, mass));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Free unknown index", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Plan node index", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Position x [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Lumped mass [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "free" })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Grid points N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Free unknowns n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "ODE state length", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Jacobian nonzero entries", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Reconstructed field length", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        n,
        system.free_indices().len(),
        adapter.state_dimension(),
        system.jacobian_pattern().col_indices().len(),
        MethodOfLinesFields::field_len(&system),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sizes" })
);
```

With the default $N=9$, end nodes 0 and 8 are eliminated, leaving seven free unknowns at nodes 1–7.
The lumped mass equals the grid spacing $h=1/8$, and the tridiagonal Jacobian has $3\cdot7-2=19$ nonzero entries.
The reconstructed field length is 9, including both ends, and `reconstruct_into` fills the eliminated nodes with boundary data values.

## Implicit-stage preconditioning

Implicit integrators such as Rosenbrock methods and backward Euler solve the following linear equation in normalized coordinates at each stage:

$$
\left(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\,J_z\right)\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{r}
$$

Here $\gamma$ is the product of the step size and an integrator coefficient, called a shift in `scioxide-ode`.
Selecting `OdeStageBackend::Krylov` as the stage backend solves this equation with matrix-free GMRES and requests a preconditioner from `OdeSystem::stage_preconditioner(time, state, shift)`.
`MethodOfLinesSystem` does not forward the physical provider's preconditioner.
A preconditioner in physical coordinates approximates an operator without $\mathbf{M}$ and $\mathbf{D}$ and therefore does not approximate the normalized $\mathbf{I}-\gamma J_z$.

Supply a preconditioner for the normalized stage equation by binding a value implementing `MethodOfLinesStagePreconditioner` with `with_stage_preconditioner(&provider)`.
`preconditioner(time, state, shift)` takes the physical time, normalized state $\mathbf{z}$, and shift $\gamma$, and either returns a borrowed prepared inverse action or selects no preconditioning with `None`.
Non-finite times or shifts return errors before the provider is called.
A returned preconditioner whose dimension does not match the state dimension returns an error.
The provider is responsible for preparing and updating the preconditioner and is expected to avoid allocating numerical storage during a solve.
Preallocate the workspace size required by the preconditioner in the stage backend's `preconditioner_scratch`.

For coupled systems, creating field partitions (`PdeFieldLayout`) in the order of normalized free coordinates and arranging the inverses of the field diagonal blocks $(\mathbf{I}-\gamma J_z)_{ii}$ with `PdeFieldPreconditioner` gives a block-diagonal preconditioner ([Field partitions and field preconditioning](oxref:pde-field-layout-preconditioner)).
If the scale is constant within each field, the diagonal block in normalized coordinates is $\mathbf{I}-\gamma\,\mathbf{M}_i^{-1}J_{ii}$, with scales canceling out.
In the [two-temperature example](oxref:pde-two-temperature-stage-krylov) (63 nodes per field), a preconditioner solving a tridiagonal block for each field, including diffusion and the exchange diagonal, reduced total GMRES iterations from 22055 to 1556 and rejected steps from 101 to 0.

## Termination reasons and diagnostics

`MethodOfLinesPlan` and `MethodOfLinesSystem` have no iterations; `OdeSolveReport` in `scioxide-ode` holds the integration termination reason.
`PdeSolveReport::try_from(&ode_report)` converts it to a partial differential equation report.
Termination reasons map as follows.

| `OdeTermination` | `PdeTermination` | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `ReachedEnd`, `TerminalEvent` | `Completed` | Success (`is_success()` is true) |
| `BudgetLimit(Proposal)`, `BudgetLimit(Iteration)` | `TimeStepLimit` | Stopped at the step-count limit |
| Other `BudgetLimit(_)` | `EvaluationLimit` | Stopped at an evaluation limit |
| `StepSizeLimit` | `TimeStepSizeLimit` | Stopped because the step could not be reduced below the minimum |
| `ExecutionStopped(_)` | `ExecutionStopped(_)` | Stopped by cancellation or a deadline |

If `OdeSolveReport` has no termination reason (a report during a failed attempt), `try_from` returns an `InvalidInput` error.

When unsuccessful, `OdeState` holds the state at the last accepted step, and `final_time()` gives its time.
This state is an intermediate value, not the endpoint solution.
In `diagnostics()`, `num_accepted_steps` and `num_rejected_steps` count steps, while `num_rhs_evaluations`, `num_jacobian_evaluations`, `num_factorizations`, and `num_linear_solves` are totals including work in nested stage linear solvers.
Integration error is controlled by local error estimates, which are not upper bounds on the global error.
Check solution accuracy against exact solutions, conserved quantities, and refinement, as in the [two-temperature example](oxref:pde-two-temperature-integration).

## Summary

The method of lines normalizes the spatially discretized $\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{y}}=\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y})$ to $\dot{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{z})$ with `MethodOfLinesPlan` and integrates it with an integrator from `scioxide-ode`.
Providers express physical-unit residuals with unit mass, pass diagonal mass and field magnitude scales to the plan, and convert tolerances to normalized coordinates with `error_tolerance`.
When starting from a spatial discretization plan, `SemidiscreteSystem` prepares the free-unknown residual, lumped mass, and pattern, and `method_of_lines` returns the plan.
When solving stages with a Krylov method, supply a preconditioner matching the normalized stage equation through `MethodOfLinesStagePreconditioner`.
Read integration results using the termination reason in `PdeSolveReport`; if unsuccessful, treat the last accepted state as an intermediate value.
