Many physical governing equations start from a balance that counts the conserved quantity entering and leaving an arbitrary small interval.
This page derives the three types of one-dimensional equations from this balance and explains how `scioxide-pde` maps them to unknowns on a grid.
Boundary conditions are covered in [Boundary conditions and outward signs](../boundary-conditions/), and the three discretizations are derived in [FDM, FEM, and FVM for elliptic problems](../elliptic-fdm-fem-fvm/).

## Derivation from conservation laws

Consider a small interval $[x_-,x_+]$ within $a\le x\le b$.
Let $u(x,t)$ be the field, $m(x)$ the capacity per unit length (such as heat capacity times cross-sectional area), $q(x,t)$ the rightward flux, and $f(x,t)$ the generation per unit length per unit time.
The rate of change of the conserved quantity in the small interval equals the flux entering at the left end minus that leaving at the right end, plus internal generation.

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{x_-}^{x_+} m(x)\,u(x,t)\,dx = q(x_-,t)-q(x_+,t)+\int_{x_-}^{x_+} f(x,t)\,dx
$$

If the integrands are continuous, dividing both sides by $x_+-x_-$ and letting the width tend to 0 gives the local conservation law $m\,\partial_t u=-\partial_x q+f$.
An equation relating flux to the field is called a constitutive law.
Substituting Fourier's law of heat conduction or Fick's law of diffusion, $q=-k\,\partial_x u$ (where $k>0$ is conductivity or diffusivity), gives the diffusion equation.

$$
m(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(k(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)-c(x)\,u+f(x,t)
$$

Here $-c\,u$ is a loss proportional to the field, such as heat loss to the surroundings, and $c$ is called the reaction coefficient.
The form $(k\,u_x)_x$ is called conservative form.
Although algebraically equivalent to the expanded form $k\,u_{xx}+k'u_x$, only the conservative form yields the property of sharing fluxes with adjacent intervals after discretization.

A second-order partial differential equation $A\,u_{tt}+2B\,u_{tx}+C\,u_{xx}+(\text{lower-order terms})=0$ is classified by the sign of the discriminant $B^2-AC$.

- **Elliptic** ($B^2-AC<0$): at steady state ($\partial_t u=0$), this becomes the boundary value problem $-(k u')'+c\,u=f$.
  In two or more spatial dimensions, it is $-\nabla\cdot(k\nabla u)+c\,u=f$, and the principal part $-k(u_{xx}+u_{yy})$ has a negative discriminant.
  The solution depends simultaneously on boundary data and sources throughout the domain.
- **Parabolic** ($B^2-AC=0$): the diffusion equation above has no second derivative in $t$, so $A=B=0$ and the discriminant is 0.
  It is solved forward in time from initial values, with higher spatial wavenumber components decaying faster.
- **Hyperbolic** ($B^2-AC>0$): replacing the accumulation term by the second time derivative of displacement gives the wave equation $m\,u_{tt}=(k\,u_x)_x+f$.
  Since $A=m$ and $C=-k$, the discriminant is $mk>0$, and waves propagate at the finite speed $\sqrt{k/m}$.

Coefficient providers in `scioxide-pde` return the diffusion coefficient $k$ and reaction coefficient $c$ (and conservative-form advection velocity for finite differences on structured grids), with no argument for the capacity $m$.
Time-dependent plans integrate $Mu'=b(t)-Au$ or $Mu''=b(t)-Au$ using the spatially discretized operator $A$ and lumped mass $M$, whose diagonal entries are the volumes assigned to each unknown.
Thus they represent equations with $m=1$: $u_t=(k u_x)_x-c\,u+f$ for parabolic problems, and $u_{tt}=(c_w^2u_x)_x-c\,u+f$ for hyperbolic problems, passing the square of the wave speed $c_w$ as the diffusion coefficient.
For constant capacity, divide $k$, $c$, and $f$ by $m$ before passing them; spatially varying capacity cannot be represented in this form.

## Meshes and field locations

The one-dimensional structured grid is `SegmentMesh` in `scioxide-spatial`, and the simplex mesh is `IntervalMesh`.
`SegmentMesh` is built from a sequence of coordinate-axis vertices; `IntervalMesh` is built from vertex coordinates and the indices of the two endpoint vertices of each interval.
Below, consider both with the same vertices $x_0<x_1<\dots<x_N$ and $N$ cells (intervals).
Structured grids are accepted by `StructuredFdmPlan` and `StructuredFvmPlan`, and simplex meshes by `SimplexFemPlan` and `SimplexFvmPlan`.
Even with the same vertices, the locations of the unknowns differ between discretizations.

- **Vertex fields** (`VertexField<S, 1>`): the unknowns of finite differences (FDM) and P1 finite elements (FEM), with one at each vertex including the boundary, giving length $N+1$.
  P2 FEM places the values at interval midpoints (edge fields) after the vertex values, giving $2N+1$ unknowns.
- **Cell fields** (`CellField<S, 1>`): the unknowns of finite volumes (FVM), with one per cell $C_i=[x_i,x_{i+1}]$, giving length $N$.
  Each value approximates a cell average, and its representative point is the cell center $(x_i+x_{i+1})/2$.

For fields with multiple components, unknowns are grouped by component (all unknowns for component 0, then all for component 1, and so on).
`StructuredFvmPlan` and `SimplexFvmPlan` support only fields with one component.
Field locations are distinguished by type, so mixing up a vertex field and a cell field causes a compile error even when their lengths match.
A mesh has an identifier (`MeshToken`) representing its family and revision, which boundary-condition sets and fields record.
Passing boundary conditions or fields from a separately constructed mesh is rejected with `InvalidInput` even if the coordinates are identical.

## Shared spatial discretization contract

The four plans implement the shared trait `SpatialDiscretization`.
In addition to applying the time-independent operator $A$ (`LinearOperator` from `scioxide-core`), it returns the following.

- `unknowns` and `components`: the total number of unknowns, including boundary values, and the number of components.
- `dof_point(i)`: return the physical coordinates and component of unknown $i$ in a `FieldPoint`.
- `constrained()`: whether each unknown has its value fixed by a Dirichlet condition.
  Fixed rows and columns are decoupled from the others with diagonal entries set to 1, and their values enter the load.
  Symmetric problems retain symmetry in the assembled matrix.
- `lumped_mass()`: the lumped mass of each unknown, all positive.
  This is the vertex control width for FDM (halved at the ends), cell volume for FVM, and for FEM, the mass-matrix diagonal scaled elementwise to the total element mass and summed at each node (equal to the row sum for P1).
- `has_constant_modes()`: whether the structure establishes that constant vectors belong to the operator's null space.
  This is true when there is no reaction (nor advection for FDM), no prescribed-value condition, and no boundary reaction.
  This case is explained in [Compatibility conditions, gauges, and conservative fluxes for elliptic problems](../compatibility-gauges-and-fluxes/).
- `load(source, t)`: return the load $b(t)$ at time $t$.
  `load_into` writes the same load into caller-owned storage without allocating.
  The load consists of weighted sums of sources, weighted sums of Neumann and Robin data, and terms transferred from Dirichlet values.
  Steady solves evaluate it at time 0.
- `assemble_csr()`: return the eliminated operator as a compressed sparse row (CSR) matrix.
- `field(values)`: convert the sequence of unknowns back to a sequence of `VertexField` values, a `CellField`, or a `SimplexFemField`.

Before allocating storage, preparation of `StructuredFdmPlan`, `StructuredFvmPlan`, and `SimplexFvmPlan` compares the number of unknowns, number of matrix entries to assemble, and storage bytes to allocate against the limits in `SpatialLimits::new(max_unknowns, max_entries, max_storage_bytes)`, rejecting excesses with `InvalidInput`.
The defaults (plans built with `SpatialLimits::default()` and `new`) impose no limits.
`SimplexFemPlan` uses `SimplexFemLimits` for the same purpose.

## Source and initial-value data

Pass the source $f$ and initial values through `FieldData<R, V, D>`.
This is an alias of `PdeData`, which holds a function of position and time, $f(\text{site},t)$, in a shared pointer; its `FieldPoint` argument holds physical coordinates `point` and a component index `component`.
Boundary data `BoundaryData` use the same `PdeData`, with an argument `BoundaryPoint` that additionally holds the outward unit normal `normal`.

Time dependence is declared at construction (`TimeDependence`), rather than inferred from values.

- `FieldData::time_invariant(f)`, `constant(v)`, and `zero()` are `TimeInvariant`, and their functions do not receive time.
  A time-dependent plan may reuse a successfully evaluated load, so recreate the plan if values captured by the function change.
- `FieldData::time_dependent(f)` is `TimeDependent`; its function takes `(site, t)` and is evaluated at each requested time.

Evaluation checks that the time is finite before calling the function and rejects non-finite return values with `NonFinite`.
Errors returned by the function propagate unchanged.

## Checking field locations

The cell below divides the same interval $[0,1]\,\mathrm{m}$ into $N$ uniform cells, prepares four plans with diffusion coefficient 1, and lists the quantities returned by `SpatialDiscretization`.
The inputs are the number of cells and the conditions at both ends (Dirichlet or zero-flux Neumann).
There are two independent checks.
The sum of lumped masses equals the interval length 1, and P2 unknown number $N+1$ (counting from 0) lies at the first interval midpoint $h/2$.

```rust
//| id: pde-field-locations
//| ref: pde-field-locations
//| title: "List unknown locations and lumped mass for four plans"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   cells: {"type":"range","label":"Cell count N [-]","min":2,"max":10,"step":1,"value":4}
//|   ends: {"type":"select","label":"Conditions at both endpoints","options":["Dirichlet (prescribed values)","Neumann (zero flux)"],"value":"Dirichlet (prescribed values)"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"neumann","label":"Neumann at both ends","description":"No plan fixes values, and all report constant modes.","inputs":{"ends":"Neumann (zero flux)"}}
//|   - {"id":"fine","label":"N = 10","description":"FDM and P1 have 11 unknowns, FVM has 10, and P2 has 21.","inputs":{"cells":10}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":4}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/3/4","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"None"}
//|       - {"artifact":"dofs","path":"/rows/5/4","approx":{"value":0.125,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"dofs","path":"/rows/4/2","equals":null}
//|   - case: neumann
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"Yes"}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/3/5","equals":"Yes"}
//|   - case: fine
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":21}
//|       - {"artifact":"plans","path":"/rows/2/4","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-14}}
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryData, BoundaryKind, ConstantDiffusion, SimplexFemGeometry, SimplexFemOrder,
    SimplexFemPlan, SpatialDiscretization, StructuredBoundaries, StructuredFdmPlan,
    StructuredFvmPlan, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{AxisTopology, CoordinateAxis, Grid1d, IntervalMesh, Point, SegmentMesh};

// Read unknowns, constrained unknowns, total lumped mass, constant modes, and coordinates for each plan.
fn summary<P: SpatialDiscretization<f64, f64, 1>>(
    plan: &P,
) -> Result<(usize, usize, f64, bool, Vec<f64>), String> {
    let mut xs = Vec::new();
    for i in 0..plan.unknowns() {
        xs.push(plan.dof_point(i).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point[0]);
    }
    let fixed = plan.constrained().iter().filter(|&&c| c).count();
    let mass = plan.lumped_mass().iter().sum::<f64>();
    Ok((plan.unknowns(), fixed, mass, plan.has_constant_modes(), xs))
}
let n = cells as usize;
let kind = if ends.starts_with("Dirichlet") {
    BoundaryKind::Dirichlet
} else {
    BoundaryKind::Natural
};
let k = ConstantDiffusion::new(1.0);
// Structured grid: vertices x_i = i/N. Apply the same condition to the faces at both ends.
let axis = CoordinateAxis::uniform(0.0, 1.0, n + 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let grid = Grid1d::new([axis], [AxisTopology::Bounded]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let segment = SegmentMesh::new(grid).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let structured = StructuredBoundaries::uniform(&segment, 1, kind, BoundaryData::zero())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fdm =
    StructuredFdmPlan::new(segment.clone(), &k, &structured).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fvm = StructuredFvmPlan::new(segment, &k, &structured).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut summaries = vec![
    ("FDM", "Vertex", summary(&fdm)?),
    ("FVM", "Cell centers", summary(&fvm)?),
];
// Simplex mesh: connect the same vertices with intervals and apply the same condition to all boundary faces.
let points = (0..=n)
    .map(|i| Point::new([i as f64 / n as f64]))
    .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let interval = IntervalMesh::new(points, (0..n).map(|i| [i, i + 1]).collect())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tagged = TaggedBoundaries::uniform(&interval, 1, kind, BoundaryData::zero())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
for (name, place, order) in [
    ("FEM P1", "Vertex", SimplexFemOrder::P1),
    ("FEM P2", "Vertices and interval midpoints", SimplexFemOrder::P2),
] {
    let geometry = SimplexFemGeometry::Affine;
    let plan = SimplexFemPlan::new(interval.clone(), geometry, order, &k, &tagged)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    summaries.push((name, place, summary(&plan)?));
}
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let plan_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Discretization" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Unknown locations" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Unknowns [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Constrained unknowns [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Sum of lumped masses [m]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Constant modes" }
]);
let plan_rows: Vec<_> = summaries
    .iter()
    .map(|(name, place, (count, fixed, mass, modes, _))| {
        let modes = if *modes { "Yes" } else { "None" };
        (*name, *place, *count, *fixed, *mass, modes)
    })
    .collect();
emit_table_with_columns!(
    plan_columns,
    plan_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "plans" })
);
// Coordinates by unknown index. Leave entries empty for plans with fewer unknowns.
let x = |plan: usize, i: usize| summaries[plan].2.4.get(i).copied();
let dof_rows: Vec<_> = (0..summaries[3].2.4.len())
    .map(|i| (i, x(0, i), x(1, i), x(2, i), x(3, i)))
    .collect();
let dof_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Unknown index i", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "FDM x [m]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "FVM x [m]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "FEM P1 x [m]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "FEM P2 x [m]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "-" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    dof_columns,
    dof_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "dofs" })
);
```

With the defaults ($N=4$ and Dirichlet at both ends), FDM and P1 have five unknowns, of which the two at the ends have prescribed values.
FVM has four cell-average unknowns and no constrained unknowns.
This is because FVM includes boundary values in the flux equations at boundary faces rather than treating them as unknowns.
P2 has nine unknowns: indices 0–4 are vertices, and indices from 5 onward are interval midpoints at 0.125 m, 0.375 m, ….
The sum of lumped masses equals the interval length of 1 m for every plan.
With Neumann conditions at both ends, no plan fixes values, and all report constant modes.

## Summary

Substituting a constitutive law into the conservation balance gives elliptic, parabolic, or hyperbolic equations according to the presence and order of time derivatives.
`scioxide-pde` represents the form with capacity $m=1$, so divide the coefficients and source by any constant capacity before passing them.
Unknowns lie at vertices for FDM and P1 FEM, cell centers for FVM, and vertices and interval midpoints for P2 FEM; inspect them with `dof_point` and `lumped_mass`.
Create source and boundary data with `FieldData` and `BoundaryData`, declaring their time dependence at construction.
