This page covers cell-centered finite volume methods (FVM) in one to three dimensions.
The unknowns approximate cell averages, and balancing face fluxes in each cell makes the discrete solution locally conservative.
It derives face fluxes from two-point fluxes and nonorthogonal correction, explains boundary faces, constant modes, and accuracy at material discontinuities, and examines them in three cells.
One-dimensional FVM and two-point fluxes are covered in [FDM, FEM, and FVM for elliptic problems](../elliptic-fdm-fem-fvm/), and vertex-based differences on structured grids in [Multidimensional differences on structured grids](../multidimensional-spatial-plans/).

## Cell-centered finite volumes

`StructuredFvmPlan` (`CellMesh` with bounded segments, quadrilaterals, or hexahedra) and `SimplexFvmPlan` (`SimplexMesh` with intervals, triangles, or tetrahedra) discretize the scalar equation

$$
-\nabla\cdot(K\nabla u)+c\,u=f
$$

using cell-centered values.
$K$ is a $D\times D$ real symmetric positive definite diffusion tensor, $c$ the reaction coefficient, and $f$ the source.
Boundary conditions are limited to one component; `BoundaryConditions` with multiple components is rejected during preparation.
Unknowns are ordered by cell, with axis 0 varying fastest on structured grids.

Supply coefficients through the trait `FvmCoefficients`.
`diffusion(point)` returns the full tensor $[[K_{ij}]]$, and `reaction(point)` returns $c$, with reaction defaulting to 0.
Both are called once during preparation at each cell center (the box center on a structured grid or the centroid of a simplex).
Each cell takes the tensor at that point as its material; asymmetry at roundoff level is projected onto the symmetric part, and tensors that are not positive definite are rejected.
The reaction may be complex.

Integrating the equation over a cell $T$ and applying the divergence theorem gives

$$
\sum_{F\subset\partial T}\Phi_{T,F}+|T|\,c(\mathbf{x}_T)\,u_T=|T|\,f(\mathbf{x}_T)
$$


$\Phi_{T,F}=-\int_FK\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}_T\,dS$ is the physical flux leaving through face $F$, $|T|$ is the cell volume, and $\mathbf{x}_T$ is the cell center.
The reaction and source are represented by their center values (the midpoint rule).
The remaining task is to approximate $\Phi_{T,F}$ from cell values.

### Approximating face fluxes

Let $\mathbf{n}$ be the unit normal of an interior face $F$ (directed from cell $L$ to $R$), $|F|$ its area, and $\mathbf{x}_F$ its center.
Define the differences between the cell centers and the face as $\mathbf{d}_L=\mathbf{x}_F-\mathbf{x}_L$ and $\mathbf{d}_R=\mathbf{x}_R-\mathbf{x}_F$, and split each cell's $K_s\mathbf{n}$ into a component parallel to $\mathbf{d}_s$ and a remainder.

$$
K_s\mathbf{n}=a_s\mathbf{d}_s+\mathbf{t}_s,\qquad a_s=\frac{(K_s\mathbf{n})\cdot\mathbf{d}_s}{|\mathbf{d}_s|^2},\qquad s=L,R
$$

If $u$ is linear within a half-cell, $\mathbf{d}_s\cdot\nabla u$ equals the difference of endpoint values, so $K\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}$ at the face is $a_L(u_F-u_L)+\mathbf{t}_L\cdot\nabla u_L$ viewed from the left and $a_R(u_R-u_F)+\mathbf{t}_R\cdot\nabla u_R$ viewed from the right.
Eliminating the face value $u_F$ using continuity of flux across the face, reversing the sign, and multiplying by the area gives the flux from $L$ to $R$:

$$
\Phi_F=|F|\left[\frac{a_La_R}{a_L+a_R}(u_L-u_R)-\frac{a_R}{a_L+a_R}\,\mathbf{t}_L\cdot\nabla u_L-\frac{a_L}{a_L+a_R}\,\mathbf{t}_R\cdot\nabla u_R\right]
$$


The first term is a two-point flux approximation (TPFA) connecting the conductances of two half-cells in series; in one dimension it equals the harmonic mean in [One-dimensional FVM](../elliptic-fdm-fem-fvm/).
The second and third terms are nonorthogonal corrections using the cell gradients $\nabla u_s$ to account for the component of $K\mathbf{n}$ not parallel to $\mathbf{d}_s$.
Gradients are reconstructed by least squares, using differences to adjacent cell centers and boundary-face conditions as constraints, each normalized by its length.
If the mesh is orthogonal with respect to $K$ ($\mathbf{t}_L=\mathbf{t}_R=0$ on every face), the correction vanishes, leaving only the two-point flux.

The correction includes neighboring cell values, so the matrix is generally nonsymmetric and has more nonzeros per row than the two-point flux matrix.
Half-cells with $a_s\le0$ are rejected during preparation as `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).
Even with $a_s>0$, distorted geometry or strong anisotropy can prevent the matrix from being an M-matrix, so the discrete maximum principle is not guaranteed.
Meshes with rank-deficient gradient reconstruction are also rejected during preparation.
A multipoint flux approximation (MPFA), distinct from both the two-point flux and nonorthogonal correction, is not provided.

### Boundary faces

Boundary faces use a law of the form $\alpha u+\beta\,K\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}=g$.
Dirichlet uses $(\alpha,\beta)=(1,0)$, Neumann $(0,1)$, and Robin $(\alpha,1)$, with $\mathbf{n}$ outward and $\alpha$ restricted to real values.
Neumann data $g$ specify the [outward conormal flux](oxref:pde-neumann-outward-sign) $K\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}$ rather than the normal derivative $\partial u/\partial n$.
These differ for anisotropic $K$, so pass $K\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}$ when deriving boundary data from a manufactured solution.

The Neumann face flux is $\Phi_F=-|F|\,g$ and depends on no cell value, so it contributes only to the load.
At Dirichlet and Robin faces, the face value is eliminated using the cell's half-cell law and the face law, contributing to both the matrix and the load.
No unknowns are constrained, so `constrained()` returns all false, and Dirichlet values enter through the load.
`StructuredFvmPlan` does not accept periodic axes.

### Conservation and constant modes

The same coefficients of $\Phi_F$ are added positively to the left cell's row and negatively to the right cell's row.
Thus only boundary-face and reaction contributions remain in the column sums $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}A$; interior-face contributions cancel.
With Neumann conditions on the entire boundary and $c=0$, Neumann faces do not enter the matrix, so $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}A=0$ holds to roundoff.
Constant fields reconstruct to zero gradients and also give zero two-point flux, so $A\mathbf{1}=0$ as well.
The same holds for anisotropic tensors, and a zero sum of the load is the solvability condition of the discrete system.
`has_constant_modes` returns true in this case, and `check_constant_mode_compatibility` checks the load sum using relative error against the sum of its absolute values.
Gauge selection is explained in [Compatibility conditions, gauges, and conservative fluxes for elliptic problems](../compatibility-gauges-and-fluxes/).

### Reconstructing face fluxes

Recomputing face fluxes from the solution directly checks the balance in each cell.
`StructuredFvmPlan::face_fluxes::<A>` returns fluxes on faces perpendicular to axis `A` as a `FaceField`, with positive sign in the direction of increasing coordinates.
`SimplexFvmPlan::face_fluxes` returns them in canonical face order as an `UnstructuredFaceField`, directed outward from the first incident cell.
Both give the area-integrated physical flux $-\int_FK\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS$, evaluating boundary data at the specified time.

For repeated reconstruction with the same plan, pass a `FvmFluxWorkspace` created with `workspace` to `face_fluxes_into`.
The workspace is allocated once for the plan's face count and does not grow afterward.
`face_fluxes_into` writes all faces in axis order (structured grids) or canonical order (simplices), leaving the output unchanged on failure.

## Convergence and local balance with an anisotropic tensor

The following cell sets $K=\begin{pmatrix}1&\kappa\\\kappa&1\end{pmatrix}$ on the unit square and supplies the manufactured solution $u=\sin\pi x\sin\pi y$.
The source is $f=2\pi^2u-2\kappa\pi^2\cos\pi x\cos\pi y$, with Dirichlet conditions $u=0$ on the entire boundary.
It solves `StructuredFvmPlan` on $N\times N$ cells ($N=8,16,32$) with the default sparse LU factorization and measures the maximum error at cell centers.
For $\kappa\ne0$, $K\mathbf{n}$ tilts away from the face normal, activating the nonorthogonal correction.

It obtains fluxes on every face from the solution with `face_fluxes_into` and compares the face flux density (flux divided by face length) with the integral of the exact flux over the face.
The local balance residual for the cells is $\max_T|\sum_F\Phi_{T,F}-|T|f(\mathbf{x}_T)|$.
Because the balance is the linear system itself, the residual should be at the roundoff level of the linear solver rather than the discretization error.

```rust
//| id: pde-fvm-anisotropic-flux-balance
//| ref: pde-fvm-anisotropic-flux-balance
//| title: "Solve anisotropic FVM and measure convergence orders of cell values and face fluxes and local balance"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   kappa: {"type":"range","label":"Tensor off-diagonal κ [-]","description":"Off-diagonal entry of K = [[1, κ], [κ, 1]]. Positive definite for |κ| < 1.","min":0,"max":0.8,"step":0.2,"value":0.6}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"isotropic","label":"κ = 0","description":"For an isotropic tensor, the nonorthogonal correction vanishes and the two-point flux alone gives second-order convergence.","inputs":{"kappa":0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fvm","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fvm","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"min":1.6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fvm","path":"/rows/2/6","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: isotropic
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fvm","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fvm","path":"/rows/2/6","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, SciOxideError};
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, EllipticOptions, EllipticProblem, FieldData, FieldPoint,
    FvmCoefficients, SpatialDiscretization, StructuredBoundaries, StructuredFvmPlan,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{
    AxisSide, AxisTopology, BoundarySet, Cell, CoordinateAxis, FieldView, Grid2d, QuadMesh,
};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

struct Tensor(f64);
impl FvmCoefficients<f64, f64, 2> for Tensor {
    fn diffusion(&self, _: [f64; 2]) -> Result<[[f64; 2]; 2], SciOxideError> {
        Ok([[1.0, self.0], [self.0, 1.0]])
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let source = FieldData::time_invariant(move |p: FieldPoint<f64, 2>| {
    let [x, y] = p.point;
    let product = (PI * x).sin() * (PI * y).sin();
    Ok(2.0 * PI * PI * (product - kappa * (PI * x).cos() * (PI * y).cos()))
});
// Exact flux −∫K∇u·n dS. Normal coordinate c and interval [s0, s1] along the face.
// K and u are invariant under exchanging x and y, so the same formula applies to faces on both axes.
let exact_flux = |c: f64, s0: f64, s1: f64| {
    let along = ((PI * s0).cos() - (PI * s1).cos()) / PI;
    let across = ((PI * s1).sin() - (PI * s0).sin()) / PI;
    -PI * ((PI * c).cos() * along + kappa * (PI * c).sin() * across)
};
let mut rows: Vec<(usize, f64, Option<f64>, f64, f64, Option<f64>, f64)> = Vec::new();
for n in [8_usize, 16, 32] {
    let axis = CoordinateAxis::uniform(0.0, 1.0, n + 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let grid = Grid2d::new([axis.clone(), axis], [AxisTopology::Bounded; 2])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mesh = QuadMesh::new(grid).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut conditions = Vec::new();
    for axis in 0..2 {
        for side in [AxisSide::Start, AxisSide::End] {
            let region =
                BoundarySet::axis_side(&mesh, axis, side).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            conditions.push(BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(region, BoundaryData::zero()));
        }
    }
    let boundaries =
        StructuredBoundaries::new(&mesh, 1, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let plan = StructuredFvmPlan::new(mesh.clone(), &Tensor(kappa), &boundaries)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let solution = EllipticProblem::new(&plan, &source)
        .solve_with_options(&EllipticOptions::new())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if !solution.report().is_success() {
        return Err(format!("N={n}: {}", solution.report().termination()));
    }
    let values = solution.values();
    let h = 1.0 / n as f64;
    let mut error = 0.0_f64;
    for (cell, u) in values.iter().enumerate() {
        let [x, y] = plan.dof_point(cell).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point;
        error = error.max((u - (PI * x).sin() * (PI * y).sin()).abs());
    }
    // All face fluxes: (N+1)×N faces on axis 0, followed by N×(N+1) faces on axis 1.
    let mut fluxes = vec![0.0; 2 * n * (n + 1)];
    let mut workspace = plan.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let cells = FieldView::<_, Cell, 2>::new(&mesh, values).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    plan.face_fluxes_into(
        cells,
        0.0,
        &mut fluxes,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (x_faces, y_faces) = fluxes.split_at(n * (n + 1));
    // Flux-density error. Face (i, j) has normal index i (0..=N) and tangential index j (0..N).
    // far identifies faces for which neither adjacent cell is a boundary cell.
    let far = |i: usize, j: usize| i >= 2 && i + 2 <= n && j >= 1 && j + 2 <= n;
    let (mut all, mut inner) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for i in 0..=n {
        for j in 0..n {
            let (c, s0, s1) = (i as f64 * h, j as f64 * h, (j + 1) as f64 * h);
            let exact = exact_flux(c, s0, s1);
            for computed in [x_faces[i + (n + 1) * j], y_faces[j + n * i]] {
                let e = (computed - exact).abs() / h;
                all = all.max(e);
                if far(i, j) {
                    inner = inner.max(e);
                }
            }
        }
    }
    // Local balance: sum of outward fluxes for each cell minus generation |T| f(x_T).
    let mut balance = 0.0_f64;
    for j in 0..n {
        for i in 0..n {
            let out = x_faces[i + 1 + (n + 1) * j] - x_faces[i + (n + 1) * j]
                + y_faces[i + n * (j + 1)]
                - y_faces[i + n * j];
            let center = [(i as f64 + 0.5) * h, (j as f64 + 0.5) * h];
            let site = FieldPoint {
                point: center,
                component: 0,
            };
            let produced = h * h * source.evaluate(site, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            balance = balance.max((out - produced).abs());
        }
    }
    let order = rows.last().map(|r| (r.1 / error).log2());
    let flux_order = rows.last().map(|r| (r.4 / inner).log2());
    rows.push((n, error, order, all, inner, flux_order, balance));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Cells per side N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum cell-center error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Observed order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Flux-density error (all faces) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Flux-density error (faces not incident to boundary cells) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Observed interior-flux order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Maximum local balance residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "fvm" }));
```

With the default ($\kappa=0.6$), the maximum cell-center errors at $N=8,16,32$ were $2.34\times10^{-2}$, $5.81\times10^{-3}$, and $1.45\times10^{-3}$, giving an observed order of 2.005.
The local balance residual was at most $4\times10^{-15}$ on every grid, and each cell's flux sum matches generation $|T|f(\mathbf{x}_T)$ to roundoff.
FVM conservation holds to the accuracy of the linear solve, independently of the discretization error magnitude.

Over all faces, flux-density errors decrease only at first order, taking the values $9.57\times10^{-2}$, $5.49\times10^{-2}$, and $2.80\times10^{-2}$.
The maximum occurs on a face incident to a boundary cell.
Boundary-cell gradients are reconstructed from one-sided constraints that include boundary-face conditions and generally have only first-order accuracy.
This error is thought to enter the flux through the nonorthogonal correction.
Restricting attention to faces not incident to boundary cells reduces the error to $2.80\times10^{-3}$, with an observed order of 1.79 approaching 2.
For $\kappa=0$, the correction vanishes, and fluxes converge at second order on all faces.
Even when cell values converge at second order, fluxes near the boundary can be first order, so refine the grid when assessing quantities such as boundary heat flux.

## Material discontinuity location and convergence order

The following cell solves $-\nabla\cdot(k\nabla u)=f$ in a layered material whose diffusion coefficient jumps from $k_L=1$ to $k_R$ at $x=1/2$.
The exact solution is $u=s(x)\sin\pi y$, with $s(x)=x$ ($x<1/2$) and $s(x)=1/2+(x-1/2)/k_R$ ($x\ge1/2$).
Since $s$ is continuous and the flux $-k\,s'=-1$ is also continuous across the interface, this is a weak solution satisfying the interface conditions.
The source is $f=k\pi^2u$, with exact-solution Dirichlet values on the entire boundary.

If the cell count $N$ is even, the interface coincides with a cell face, and each cell contains only one material.
If $N$ is odd, cell centers in the middle column lie exactly at $x=1/2$; these cells take the center value $k_R$ as their material, effectively shifting the interface by half a cell.
Compare maximum cell-center errors and observed orders for these two grid families.
The one-dimensional derivation of interface conditions and series resistance appears in [Discontinuous coefficients and material interfaces](oxref:pde-interface-series-resistance).

```rust
//| id: pde-fvm-material-jump-order
//| ref: pde-fvm-material-jump-order
//| title: "Compare FVM convergence orders on grids with and without a face-aligned interface in a layered material"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   contrast: {"type":"range","label":"Right diffusion coefficient k_R [-]","description":"The left side has k_L = 1.","min":10,"max":1000,"step":10,"value":100}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"mild","label":"k_R = 10","description":"Even for a smaller jump, grids with the interface inside a cell remain first order.","inputs":{"contrast":10}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/5/3","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.2}}
//|   - case: mild
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"jump","path":"/rows/5/3","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.2}}
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, BoundaryPoint, EllipticOptions, EllipticProblem,
    FieldData, FieldPoint, FvmCoefficients, SpatialDiscretization, StructuredBoundaries,
    StructuredFvmPlan,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{AxisSide, AxisTopology, BoundarySet, CoordinateAxis, Grid2d, QuadMesh};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

// Layered material sampled at cell centers. Cells centered at x = 1/2 use the right material.
struct Layered(f64);
impl FvmCoefficients<f64, f64, 2> for Layered {
    fn diffusion(&self, p: [f64; 2]) -> Result<[[f64; 2]; 2], SciOxideError> {
        let k = if p[0] < 0.5 { 1.0 } else { self.0 };
        Ok([[k, 0.0], [0.0, k]])
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let exact = move |p: [f64; 2]| {
    let s = if p[0] < 0.5 {
        p[0]
    } else {
        0.5 + (p[0] - 0.5) / contrast
    };
    s * (PI * p[1]).sin()
};
let source = FieldData::time_invariant(move |p: FieldPoint<f64, 2>| {
    let k = if p.point[0] < 0.5 { 1.0 } else { contrast };
    Ok(k * PI * PI * exact(p.point))
});
let data = BoundaryData::time_invariant(move |p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| Ok(exact(p.point)));
let mut rows: Vec<(&str, usize, f64, Option<f64>)> = Vec::new();
for (family, sizes) in [("Aligned with a face", [8_usize, 16, 32]), ("Inside a cell", [9, 17, 33])] {
    let mut previous: Option<(usize, f64)> = None;
    for n in sizes {
        let axis = CoordinateAxis::uniform(0.0, 1.0, n + 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let grid = Grid2d::new([axis.clone(), axis], [AxisTopology::Bounded; 2])
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let mesh = QuadMesh::new(grid).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let mut conditions = Vec::new();
        for axis in 0..2 {
            for side in [AxisSide::Start, AxisSide::End] {
                let region =
                    BoundarySet::axis_side(&mesh, axis, side).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
                conditions.push(BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(region, data.clone()));
            }
        }
        let boundaries =
            StructuredBoundaries::new(&mesh, 1, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let plan = StructuredFvmPlan::new(mesh, &Layered(contrast), &boundaries)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let solution = EllipticProblem::new(&plan, &source)
            .solve_with_options(&EllipticOptions::new())
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if !solution.report().is_success() {
            return Err(format!("N={n}: {}", solution.report().termination()));
        }
        let mut error = 0.0_f64;
        for (cell, u) in solution.values().iter().enumerate() {
            let p = plan.dof_point(cell).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point;
            error = error.max((u - exact(p)).abs());
        }
        // Some grid families do not have a spacing ratio of 2, so compute order from the ratio of h values.
        let order = previous.map(|(m, e)| (e / error).ln() / (n as f64 / m as f64).ln());
        previous = Some((n, error));
        rows.push((family, n, error, order));
    }
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Interface position" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Cells per side N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum cell-center error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Observed order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "jump" })
);
```

With the default ($k_R=100$), face-aligned grids had errors $3.78\times10^{-3}$, $9.61\times10^{-4}$, and $2.41\times10^{-4}$ at $N=8,16,32$, giving an observed order of 1.994.
Each cell holds only one material, and the two-point face flux connects two half-cell conductances in series, correctly representing flux continuity at the interface.
For grids with the interface inside a cell, the error is still $1.43\times10^{-2}$ at $N=33$, and the observed order falls to 0.943, or first order.
This is because material is sampled once at the cell center and is not averaged within cells spanning the interface.
The order remains 1 even with $k_R=10$, and the error of $1.20\times10^{-2}$ is only slightly smaller.
For problems with material discontinuities, construct a mesh whose cell faces align with the interface.

## Constant modes with Neumann conditions on the entire boundary

The following cell solves an anisotropic problem with Neumann conditions on the entire boundary using `SimplexFvmPlan` on a triangular mesh.
The tensor is $K=\begin{pmatrix}1&\kappa\\\kappa&1\end{pmatrix}$, the exact solution $u=\cos\pi x$, and the source $f=\pi^2\cos\pi x$.
Since $K\nabla u=(-\pi\sin\pi x,\,-\kappa\pi\sin\pi x)$, the correct Neumann data $g=K\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}$ equal $\mp\kappa\pi\sin\pi x$ on the top and bottom edges, differing from the normal derivative $\partial u/\partial n$ (zero on those edges).
Choose between conormal flux and normal derivative boundary data and compare solution errors.

The mesh consists of triangles formed by splitting each square of an $N\times N$ grid along a diagonal, with $N=8,16,32$.
The maximum row sum $A\mathbf{1}$ and column sum $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}A$ of the assembled matrix are shown relative to its largest entry.
The solution is uniquely determined with `EllipticGauge::WeightedMean(0)` (a gauge setting the volume-weighted mean to 0); the exact solution also has mean 0.

```rust
//| id: pde-fvm-neumann-constant-mode
//| ref: pde-fvm-neumann-constant-mode
//| title: "Check constant modes, solvability conditions, and the meaning of Neumann data in anisotropic FVM with Neumann conditions on the entire boundary"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   neumann_data: {"type":"select","label":"Neumann data g","options":["Conormal flux K∇u·n","Normal derivative ∂u/∂n"],"value":"Conormal flux K∇u·n"}
//|   tensor_coupling: {"type":"range","label":"Tensor off-diagonal κ [-]","min":0,"max":0.8,"step":0.2,"value":0.6}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"normal-derivative","label":"Normal derivative","description":"Top and bottom fluxes are treated as zero, and the error does not decrease under grid refinement.","inputs":{"neumann_data":"Normal derivative ∂u/∂n"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"neumann","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"neumann","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"neumann","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"neumann","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"min":1.5}}
//|   - case: normal-derivative
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"neumann","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":0.05}}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, Tolerance};
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, BoundaryPoint, EllipticGauge, EllipticOptions,
    EllipticProblem, FieldData, FieldPoint, FvmCoefficients, SimplexFvmPlan,
    SpatialDiscretization, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{Point, SimplexBoundaryTags, TriangleMesh};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

struct Tensor(f64);
impl FvmCoefficients<f64, f64, 2> for Tensor {
    fn diffusion(&self, _: [f64; 2]) -> Result<[[f64; 2]; 2], SciOxideError> {
        Ok([[1.0, self.0], [self.0, 1.0]])
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let kappa = tensor_coupling;
let conormal = neumann_data.starts_with("Conormal");
// g = K∇u·n (conormal flux) or ∇u·n (normal derivative). u = cos πx.
let data = BoundaryData::time_invariant(move |p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| {
    let ux = -PI * (PI * p.point[0]).sin();
    let flux = if conormal {
        [ux, kappa * ux]
    } else {
        [ux, 0.0]
    };
    Ok(flux[0] * p.normal[0] + flux[1] * p.normal[1])
});
let source =
    FieldData::time_invariant(|p: FieldPoint<f64, 2>| Ok(PI * PI * (PI * p.point[0]).cos()));
let options = EllipticOptions::new().with_gauge(EllipticGauge::WeightedMean(0.0));
let mut rows: Vec<(usize, f64, f64, f64, f64, Option<f64>)> = Vec::new();
for n in [8_usize, 16, 32] {
    let scale = n as f64;
    let points = (0..(n + 1) * (n + 1))
        .map(|i| Point::new([(i % (n + 1)) as f64 / scale, (i / (n + 1)) as f64 / scale]))
        .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut cells = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..n {
        for i in 0..n {
            let v = j * (n + 1) + i;
            cells.push([v, v + 1, v + n + 2]);
            cells.push([v, v + n + 2, v + n + 1]);
        }
    }
    let mesh = TriangleMesh::new(points, cells).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let faces = mesh.boundary_faces().map(|face| (face, 1)).collect();
    let tags = SimplexBoundaryTags::new(&mesh, faces).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let conditions = vec![BoundaryCondition::natural(1, data.clone())];
    let boundaries =
        TaggedBoundaries::new(&mesh, &tags, 1, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let plan =
        SimplexFvmPlan::new(mesh, &Tensor(kappa), &boundaries).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if !plan.has_constant_modes() {
        return Err("Constant modes not recognized".into());
    }
    // Row and column sums. Interior-face contributions cancel in the column sums.
    let matrix = plan.assemble_csr().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (mut row_sums, mut column_sums) = (vec![0.0; n * n * 2], vec![0.0; n * n * 2]);
    for row in 0..matrix.rows() {
        for index in matrix.row_ptr()[row]..matrix.row_ptr()[row + 1] {
            row_sums[row] += matrix.values()[index];
            column_sums[matrix.col_indices()[index]] += matrix.values()[index];
        }
    }
    let largest = matrix.values().iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, v| m.max(v.abs()));
    let max_abs = |v: &[f64]| v.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, x| m.max(x.abs())) / largest;
    // Solvability condition: the load sum is zero relative to its absolute sum. Display the relative error Σb / Σ|b|.
    let load = plan.load(&source, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    plan.check_constant_mode_compatibility(
        &load,
        Tolerance::new(0.0, 1e-10).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let defect = load.iter().sum::<f64>().abs() / load.iter().map(|b| b.abs()).sum::<f64>();
    let solution = EllipticProblem::new(&plan, &source)
        .solve_with_options(&options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if !solution.report().is_success() {
        return Err(format!("N={n}: {}", solution.report().termination()));
    }
    let mut error = 0.0_f64;
    for (cell, u) in solution.values().iter().enumerate() {
        let p = plan.dof_point(cell).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point;
        error = error.max((u - (PI * p[0]).cos()).abs());
    }
    let order = rows.last().map(|r| (r.4 / error).log2());
    rows.push((
        n,
        max_abs(&row_sums),
        max_abs(&column_sums),
        defect,
        error,
        order,
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Subdivisions per side N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "max|A1| / max|a| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "max|1ᵀA| / max|a| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "|Σb| / Σ|b| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum cell-center error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Observed order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "neumann" })
);
```

With conormal flux data (the default, $\kappa=0.6$), maximum cell-center errors decreased at second order as $4.51\times10^{-3}$, $1.14\times10^{-3}$, and $2.86\times10^{-4}$, giving an observed order of 1.994.
Row and column sums were both at most $2.3\times10^{-16}$ times the largest matrix entry, so $A\mathbf{1}=0$ and $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}A=0$ hold to roundoff even for an anisotropic tensor.
The load sum was also at most $10^{-16}$ times the sum of absolute load values, and `check_constant_mode_compatibility` accepted it with relative tolerance $10^{-10}$.
With normal derivatives supplied as data, the error is still 1.22 at $N=32$ and does not decrease under grid refinement.
The solvability condition still holds and computation succeeds, but it solves a different problem with zero conormal flux on the top and bottom edges.

## Summary

`StructuredFvmPlan` and `SimplexFvmPlan` construct face fluxes for each cell's full diffusion tensor using series two-point fluxes and nonorthogonal corrections from least-squares gradients.
The flux balance in each cell holds to linear-solve accuracy and can be checked by obtaining face fluxes with `face_fluxes` and `face_fluxes_into`.
For smooth solutions, cell values converge at second order, but for anisotropic tensors, fluxes on faces incident to boundary cells become first order.
Material is sampled at cell centers, so convergence is second order when the discontinuity aligns with cell faces and first order when it lies inside cells.
Neumann data specify conormal flux $K\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}$; with Neumann conditions on the entire boundary, row and column sums vanish even with anisotropy, and the load sum gives the solvability condition.
