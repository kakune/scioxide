In parabolic partial differential equations describing heat conduction and material diffusion, spatial variations decay and become smoother over time.
Shorter wavelengths decay faster, making the spatially discretized ODE system stiff; the choice of time integration method determines step sizes and computational cost.

This page semidiscretizes the diffusion equation with the method of lines and derives θ-method stability and explicit step-size limits from the semidiscrete eigenvalues.
It then explains how `ParabolicProblem`, `ParabolicOptions`, and `ParabolicScheme` implement these properties and solve implicit-stage linear systems.
Crank–Nicolson oscillations and their treatment with `RannacherStartup` are covered in [Crank–Nicolson oscillations and Rannacher startup](../crank-nicolson-and-rannacher/); receiving intermediate fields, restarting, and interpreting termination reasons are covered in [Time-dependent outputs, restart, and termination reasons](../transient-outputs-and-restart/).

## Decay rates of the diffusion equation

Consider the one-dimensional diffusion equation with periodic boundaries:

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\alpha\,\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2},\qquad\alpha>0
$$

Substituting the Fourier mode $u(x,t)=\hat u_k(t)\,e^{\mathrm{i}kx}$ gives $d\hat u_k/dt=-\alpha k^2\hat u_k$, or $\hat u_k(t)=\hat u_k(0)\,e^{-\alpha k^2t}$.
$\alpha$ is the diffusivity, and $k$ is the wavenumber.
The decay rate is proportional to the squared wavenumber, so long wavelengths evolve slowly while wavelengths comparable to the grid spacing rapidly decay to 0.

When components evolve at very different rates, stability requires a much smaller step than accuracy does.
This property is called stiffness.

## Method of lines and semidiscrete eigenvalues

Discretizing only space with FDM, FEM, or FVM converts the partial differential equation into an ODE system (a semidiscrete system).

$$
\mathbf{M}\,\frac{d\mathbf{u}}{dt}+\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{b}(t)
$$

$\mathbf{M}$ is the mass matrix, $\mathbf{K}$ the diffusion stiffness matrix, and $\mathbf{b}(t)$ the right-hand side from sources and boundary data.
This is called the method of lines.

For second-order centered differences on a uniform grid (spacing $h$), the semidiscrete equation at grid point $j$ is as follows.

$$
\frac{du_j}{dt}=\frac{\alpha}{h^2}\left(u_{j-1}-2u_j+u_{j+1}\right)
$$

Substituting the discrete Fourier mode $u_j(t)=\hat u_\theta(t)\,e^{\mathrm{i}j\theta}$ (where $\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]$ is wavenumber measured in grid spacings) gives the semidiscrete eigenvalues.

$$
\lambda_h(\theta)=\frac{\alpha}{h^2}\left(e^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}-2+e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\right)=-\frac{4\alpha}{h^2}\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}
$$

All eigenvalues lie on the negative real axis.
As $\theta\to0$, they approach the continuous value $-\alpha(\theta/h)^2$; the mode $\theta=\pi$, whose sign alternates between adjacent grid points, has the maximum magnitude $4\alpha/h^2$.
Halving the grid spacing multiplies the largest eigenvalue magnitude by 4.
On an interval with Dirichlet conditions at both ends, the sine modes $\sin(j\pi x)$ are also eigenvectors, and the same formula holds with $\theta=j\pi h$.

## Amplification factors and stability of the θ method

Apply the θ method, mixing explicit and implicit terms with weight $\theta_t\in[0,1]$, to the semidiscrete system $\mathbf{u}'=-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{u}$ ($\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{K}$).

$$
\frac{\mathbf{u}^{n+1}-\mathbf{u}^n}{\Delta t}=-\mathbf{A}\left(\theta_t\,\mathbf{u}^{n+1}+(1-\theta_t)\,\mathbf{u}^n\right)
$$

$\theta_t=0$ gives forward Euler, $\theta_t=1$ backward Euler, and $\theta_t=\tfrac12$ Crank–Nicolson (the trapezoidal rule).
Writing $\mu=4r\sin^2(\theta/2)$ and the Fourier number $r=\alpha\Delta t/h^2$, the one-step amplification factor for a mode with eigenvalue $-\mu/\Delta t$ is

$$
G=\frac{1-(1-\theta_t)\,\mu}{1+\theta_t\,\mu}
$$


Since $\mu\ge0$, we always have $G\le1$, and $G\ge-1$ is equivalent to $\mu(1-2\theta_t)\le2$.
Thus $\theta_t\ge\tfrac12$ is stable for any $r$ (unconditionally stable), while $\theta_t<\tfrac12$ requires $r\le1/(2(1-2\theta_t))$ for the largest $\mu=4r$.

| Method | Accuracy | One-step amplification factor $G(\theta)$ | Stability condition | Limit as $r\to\infty$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact semidiscrete solution | — | $\exp(-4r\sin^2(\theta/2))$ | Always decays | $G\to0$ |
| Forward Euler ($\theta_t=0$) | First order | $1-4r\sin^2(\theta/2)$ | $r\le\tfrac12$ | $G\to-\infty$ |
| Backward Euler ($\theta_t=1$) | First order | $1/(1+4r\sin^2(\theta/2))$ | Unconditional | $G\to0$ (L-stable) |
| Crank–Nicolson ($\theta_t=\tfrac12$) | Second order | $(1-2r\sin^2(\theta/2))/(1+2r\sin^2(\theta/2))$ | Unconditional | $G\to-1$ (A-stable, not L-stable) |

L-stability requires A-stability (stability for all eigenvalues with negative real parts) and an amplification factor approaching 0 as the eigenvalue tends to $-\infty$.
For explicit methods, the largest eigenvalue determines the step, so doubling the grid-point count reduces the allowed step to $1/4$ and multiplies total work by 8 even in one dimension.

## ParabolicProblem and time integration schemes

`ParabolicProblem::new(discretization, source, end)` extracts lumped diagonal mass (`SpatialDiscretization::lumped_mass`: grid-point control volumes for FDM, cell volumes for FVM, and diagonally scaled HRZ lumping for simplex FEM) and stiffness from the spatial discretization and assembles the semidiscrete system $\mathbf{M}_f\mathbf{u}_f'=\mathbf{b}_f(t)-\mathbf{K}_{ff}\mathbf{u}_f$ once, excluding Dirichlet degrees of freedom.
Coefficients are time-independent, while sources and boundary data may depend on time through `FieldData::time_dependent` and `BoundaryData::time_dependent`.
They change only the right-hand side $\mathbf{b}_f(t)$.

`ParabolicOptions` holds time integration settings; the problem holds final time.
Defaults for `ParabolicOptions::new()` are as follows.

- Scheme `ParabolicScheme::RadauIia` (three-stage, fifth-order L-stable Radau IIA with adaptive steps)
- Both relative and absolute local error tolerances $10^{-8}$, with the root-mean-square norm
- Step-size limit `CflPolicy::Automatic`
- Output `PdeOutputSchedule::FinalOnly`, with up to 100,000 attempted steps per call
- No Rannacher startup (`RannacherStartup::Disabled`; see [Crank–Nicolson oscillations and Rannacher startup](oxref:pde-crank-nicolson-rannacher))

There are four `ParabolicScheme` choices through `with_scheme`.

| Scheme | Method | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| `BackwardEuler` | Backward Euler (`scioxide_ode::BackwardEuler`) | Fixed step from `with_step` |
| `CrankNicolson` | Crank–Nicolson (`scioxide_ode::ImplicitTrapezoid`) | Fixed step from `with_step` |
| `ExplicitDopri5` | Explicit Dormand–Prince 5(4) | Adaptive steps; `with_step` gives the initial step |
| `RadauIia` (default) | Radau IIA | Adaptive steps; `with_step` gives the initial step |

Backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson have no error estimator and use the fixed step specified with `with_step`.
Selecting either without specifying a step is rejected by `initial_state` and execution preflight with `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).
Even with fixed steps, the final step is shortened to the remaining interval to reach the endpoint.
If accumulated step times stop just short of the endpoint due to roundoff (within $4\varepsilon\,\lvert t_{\mathrm{end}}\rvert$, where $\varepsilon$ is machine epsilon), the step is extended to the endpoint, avoiding an extra roundoff-sized step.
For example, advancing to time 0.1 with step 0.01 takes exactly 10 steps.
`with_ode_method` selects another built-in integrator from `scioxide-ode` in place of a scheme, retaining that integrator's stage solution method.

Select explicit step-size limits with `with_cfl_policy`.
`CflPolicy::Automatic` computes the step-size limit $0.9\beta/\rho$ from the maximum absolute row sum $\rho$ of the mass-divided Jacobian $\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{K}$ (an eigenvalue bound from Gershgorin discs) and the negative-real-axis stability interval length $\beta$ declared by the integrator through `scioxide_ode::OdeLinearStability`.
Dormand–Prince 5(4) declares $\beta=3.3065$, corresponding to $r\le0.744$ for uniform-grid FDM ($\rho=4\alpha/h^2$).
Backward Euler, Crank–Nicolson, and Radau IIA are stable on the entire negative real axis, so no limit is imposed.
`CflPolicy::MaximumStep(h_max)` supplies a limit directly, while `CflPolicy::Disabled` imposes none and relies only on local error control.
This bound assumes eigenvalues on the negative real axis (symmetric diffusion and diagonal mass).

## Step counts under grid refinement

Compare explicit Dormand–Prince 5(4) with the default Radau IIA to see how stiffness affects computational work.
Solve $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $[0,1]$ with $\alpha=1$ and Dirichlet conditions at both ends, starting from $\sin\pi x$ and advancing to $t=0.1$ as the grid-point count $N$ doubles from 21 to 161.
The initial condition contains only the longest-wavelength sine mode, so the solution itself decays slowly regardless of the grid.

For each grid and scheme, the table lists accepted and rejected step counts, the difference between the value at $x=0.5$ and the exact semidiscrete solution $e^{-\lambda_h t}$ ($\lambda_h=(4/h^2)\sin^2(\pi h/2)$), and the termination reason.
The explicit-method columns also list the steps needed to reach the endpoint at the `CflPolicy::Automatic` limit $0.9\cdot3.3065\,h^2/4$.
Selecting `Disabled` for the explicit policy compares removing the step-size limit and relying only on local error control.

```rust
//| id: pde-parabolic-stiffness-steps
//| ref: pde-parabolic-stiffness-steps
//| title: "Compare explicit and Radau IIA step counts under grid refinement"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-pde, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   policy: {"type":"select","label":"Explicit CflPolicy","description":"Change how Dormand–Prince 5(4) limits its step size.","options":["Automatic","Disabled"],"value":"Automatic"}
//|   tolerance_digits: {"type":"range","label":"Local error tolerance digits d [-]","description":"Change relative and absolute tolerances 10^(−d).","min":3,"max":10,"step":1,"value":8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"disabled","label":"Disabled","description":"Without a limit, local error control selects steps near the stability boundary with repeated rejections.","inputs":{"policy":"Disabled"}}
//|   - {"id":"loose","label":"Tolerance 10⁻⁴","description":"Radau IIA drops to 4 steps, while explicit step counts remain set by the stability limit.","inputs":{"tolerance_digits":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":54,"atol":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":3442,"atol":2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/3/6","range":{"min":15,"max":35}}
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/3/9","equals":"Completed"}
//|   - case: disabled
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"min":50}}
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/3/5","equals":"Completed"}
//|   - case: loose
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":3442,"atol":2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"steps","path":"/rows/3/6","range":{"max":8}}
use scioxide_core::{ErrorTolerance, NormSelection, RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_pde::{
    CflPolicy, FieldData, FieldPoint, ParabolicOptions, ParabolicProblem, ParabolicScheme,
    StructuredFdmPlan,
};

let end = 0.1;
let digits = tolerance_digits.round() as u32;
let pi = core::f64::consts::PI;
let cfl = if policy == "Disabled" {
    CflPolicy::Disabled
} else {
    CflPolicy::Automatic
};
let tolerance = ErrorTolerance::from_tolerance(
    Tolerance::from_decimal_places(digits, digits),
    NormSelection::RootMeanSquare,
);
let initial = FieldData::time_invariant(move |p: FieldPoint<f64, 1>| {
    Ok(RealScalar::sin(pi * p.point[0]))
});
let source = FieldData::zero();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for nodes in [21_usize, 41, 81, 161] {
    let h = 1.0 / (nodes - 1) as f64;
    let plan = StructuredFdmPlan::dirichlet_laplacian([0.0], [1.0], [nodes])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let problem = ParabolicProblem::new(&plan, &source, end).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Exact semidiscrete solution (independent check): u(0.5, t) = exp(−λ_h t).
    let lambda = 4.0 / (h * h) * RealScalar::powi(RealScalar::sin(pi * h / 2.0), 2);
    let exact = RealScalar::exp(-lambda * end);
    let mut solve = |scheme: ParabolicScheme, cfl: CflPolicy<f64>| -> Result<_, String> {
        let options = ParabolicOptions::new()
            .with_scheme(scheme)
            .with_error_tolerance(tolerance.clone())
            .with_cfl_policy(cfl);
        let solution = problem
            .solve_with_options(&initial, &options)
            .map_err(|e| e.error().to_string())?;
        let d = solution.report().diagnostics();
        let error = (solution.values()[nodes / 2] - exact).abs();
        Ok((
            d.num_accepted_steps(),
            d.num_rejected_steps(),
            error,
            solution.report().termination(),
        ))
    };
    let explicit = solve(ParabolicScheme::ExplicitDopri5, cfl)?;
    let radau = solve(ParabolicScheme::RadauIia, CflPolicy::Automatic)?;
    let cap_steps = RealScalar::ceil(end / (0.9 * 3.3065 * h * h / 4.0)) as usize;
    rows.push((
        nodes,
        cap_steps,
        explicit.0,
        explicit.1,
        explicit.2,
        explicit.3.to_string(),
        radau.0,
        radau.1,
        radau.2,
        radau.3.to_string(),
    ));
}
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Grid points N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Steps at the limit [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "DOPRI5 accepted [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "DOPRI5 rejected [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "DOPRI5 difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "DOPRI5 termination reason" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Radau IIA accepted [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Radau IIA rejected [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Radau IIA difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "9", "label": "Radau IIA termination reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "steps" })
);
```

With the default tolerance $10^{-8}$, accepted explicit steps at $N=21,41,81,161$ were 54, 216, 861, and 3,442, increasing fourfold each time the grid-point count doubled.
On every grid they match the step count at the limit, so stability determines the step size rather than accuracy.
Radau IIA finished in 25 steps on every grid, and differences at $x=0.5$ were at most $10^{-11}$ for both schemes.
With `Disabled`, the explicit method accepted 3,079 steps and rejected 261 at $N=161$.
For diffusion, local error estimates detect short-wavelength components that begin to grow and reduce the step, so even without the limit this example terminated with `Completed` and differences no greater than $10^{-8}$.
The step nevertheless grows and shrinks near the stability boundary, wasting work on rejected steps.
Relaxing the tolerance to $10^{-4}$ lets Radau IIA finish in 4 steps, while explicit step counts remain unchanged.

## Implicit-stage linear systems and factorization

Backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson solve the following linear system at each step.

$$
\left(\mathbf{I}+\theta_t\,\Delta t\,\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{K}\right)\mathbf{u}^{n+1}=\mathbf{r}^n,\qquad\theta_t=1\ (\text{backward Euler}),\quad\theta_t=\tfrac12\ (\text{Crank–Nicolson})
$$

$\mathbf{r}^n$ is a known vector determined by $\mathbf{u}^n$ and the right-hand side $\mathbf{b}$.
The diagonal mass $\mathbf{M}$ is divided out within the normalized method-of-lines ODE system (`MethodOfLinesSystem`), so the coefficient matrix is tridiagonal for one-dimensional FDM, FEM, and FVM.
`ParabolicProblem` advances backward Euler through `scioxide_ode::BackwardEuler`, Crank–Nicolson through `scioxide_ode::ImplicitTrapezoid`, and the default scheme through `scioxide_ode::RadauIia`; the ODE stage backend (`OdeStageBackend`) solves the stage linear systems.
The default backend uses sparse LU factorization for the Jacobian sparsity pattern among free degrees of freedom; one-dimensional patterns with nonzeros only on the main and adjacent diagonals are factored as tridiagonal matrices.
The LU-factor entry limit is $2c+n$.
$c$ is the Cholesky-factor entry count from symbolic analysis of the same pattern with fill-reducing ordering, and $n$ is the number of free unknowns.
`ParabolicOptions::with_stage_backend` can select another backend (dense LU, GMRES, and so on).
When supplying a sparse backend, match its pattern to the free-unknown pattern.

Since the spatial operator is time-independent, the semidiscrete Jacobian $-\mathbf{K}_{ff}$ is declared constant to the ODE integrator.
The ODE stage backend decides when to rebuild factors; inspect the actual count through `PdeDiagnostics::num_factorizations`.
Stages with the same step size reuse one factorization: with the default ($r=1.5$) in the [mode amplification-factor cell](oxref:pde-parabolic-mode-damping), backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson each factored once, while Rannacher startup, which uses two matrices for half and full steps, factored twice.
With $r=0.05$, these counts rise to 2, 2, and 3, respectively.
The final step matches the remaining interval to the endpoint; roundoff in accumulated times makes it slightly different from other steps, requiring factor reconstruction at that stage.
Adaptive Radau IIA adds factorizations as step sizes change; solving heat conduction in the [checkpoint example](oxref:pde-transient-checkpoint-resume) in one call takes more than 10 factorizations for 25 steps.

## Summary

Semidiscrete diffusion is stiff, with eigenvalues on the negative real axis and maximum magnitude $4\alpha/h^2$, so explicit step sizes are limited to a constant multiple in terms of the Fourier number $r=\alpha\Delta t/h^2$.
The θ method is unconditionally stable for $\theta_t\ge\tfrac12$; backward Euler is L-stable and strongly damps short wavelengths, while Crank–Nicolson is second-order accurate but has short-wavelength amplification factors approaching $-1$.
`ParabolicProblem` defaults to adaptive Radau IIA; fixed-step backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson require `with_step`.
`CflPolicy::Automatic` limits explicit steps using the Gershgorin bound and the integrator's stability interval.
The stage backend in `scioxide-ode` solves implicit-stage linear systems with sparse LU factorization; inspect the factorization count in the diagnostic `num_factorizations`.
