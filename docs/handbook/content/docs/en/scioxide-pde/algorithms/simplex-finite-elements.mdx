This page covers continuous finite element methods (FEM) on meshes of intervals, triangles, and tetrahedra.
`SimplexFemPlan` assembles coupled elliptic weak forms with P1 and P2 elements and represents geometry through affine or quadratic mappings.
It explains the weak form, degree-of-freedom ordering, geometric mappings, quadrature, mass and stiffness assembly, and solution-field evaluation, then measures convergence orders and curved-boundary geometry errors in two cells.
One-dimensional FEM is derived in [FDM, FEM, and FVM for elliptic problems](../elliptic-fdm-fem-fvm/), and structured-grid differences are covered in [Multidimensional differences on structured grids](../multidimensional-spatial-plans/).

## Finite elements on simplex meshes

Consider the following system for a $q$-component field $u=(u_1,\dots,u_q)$ on $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^D$ ($D=1,2,3$).

$$
-\sum_{b=1}^{q}\nabla\cdot(K_{ab}\nabla u_b)+\sum_{b=1}^{q}C_{ab}u_b=f_a,\qquad a=1,\dots,q
$$

$K_{ab}$ is a $D\times D$ diffusion block coupling components $a$ and $b$, $C$ is a $q\times q$ reaction matrix, and $f_a$ is the source.
Multiplying by test functions $v_a$ and integrating by parts gives the weak form

$$
\sum_{a,b}\int_\Omega\left(\nabla v_a\cdot K_{ab}\nabla u_b+C_{ab}u_bv_a\right)dx+\sum_a\int_{\Gamma_R}\alpha\,u_av_a\,ds=\sum_a\int_\Omega f_av_a\,dx+\sum_a\int_{\Gamma_N\cup\Gamma_R}g_av_a\,ds
$$


The boundary-integral term $g_a=\mathbf{n}\cdot\sum_bK_{ab}\nabla u_b$ is the [outward conormal flux](oxref:pde-neumann-outward-sign) for Neumann conditions; Robin conditions add $\alpha u_a$ to it.
Dirichlet conditions are excluded from the test-function space, and their values are lifted into the load.

Supply coefficients through `evaluate(point, diffusion, reaction)` of the trait `SimplexFemCoefficients`.
`diffusion` stores a $(qD)\times(qD)$ matrix in row-major order, with indices ordered as (component, direction).
`reaction` is a $q\times q$ matrix; write every entry on every call.
The diffusion matrix must be Hermitian positive definite; elementwise relative asymmetry within $32\,n\,\varepsilon$ ($n=qD$) is projected to $(K+K^{\mathsf H})/2$ before use.
The reaction may be any finite matrix.
Coefficients are sampled only at quadrature points, so regularity and positivity between quadrature points are not guaranteed.
Returning `true` from `is_spatially_constant` declares spatial constancy, and preparation evaluates the coefficients only once.
Pass the source $f$ and boundary data $g$ through `FieldData` and `BoundaryData` when evaluating the load; both may depend on time.

### Elements and degrees of freedom

`SimplexFemOrder::P1` and `P2` are continuous piecewise polynomials with one degree of freedom at each vertex, and for P2 also one at each edge midpoint; `degree` returns 1 and 2, respectively.
A triangle has 3 local degrees of freedom for P1 and 6 for P2; a tetrahedron has 4 and 10.
Within each component, degrees of freedom follow vertex order and then canonical edge order (`dofs_per_component`); global unknowns are grouped by component.
`cell_dofs(cell)` returns global node indices for a cell's local degrees of freedom (before adding the component offset), and `node_point(node)` returns physical coordinates of interpolation points.

### Geometric mappings

Select the mapping from the reference simplex to a physical element with `SimplexFemGeometry`.
`Affine` is the affine mapping determined by mesh vertices, giving straight-edged elements.
`Quadratic(edges)` takes physical edge-midpoint coordinates in canonical edge order through `FieldView` and curves each element with a quadratic mapping.
Neighboring cells share the same edge-midpoint coordinates, so curved element boundaries also match and the function space remains continuous.
Geometry order is independent of solution order; a P2 solution with quadratic geometry gives isoparametric elements.

`map(cell, reference)` maps a reference-coordinate point and returns a `SimplexFemMappedPoint`.
`point` gives physical coordinates, `jacobian` the Jacobian $J=\partial x/\partial\hat x$, `inverse_jacobian` its inverse, `determinant` the value $\det J>0$, and `reciprocal_condition` an estimate of the reciprocal condition number in the $\infty$ norm.
Preparation checks these at every geometry interpolation point and rejects inverted elements and elements whose reciprocal condition number is below `with_minimum_reciprocal_condition` (default $64\varepsilon$).

### Quadrature

Element integrals use simplex quadrature.
By default, `scioxide_integrate::SimplexRule::positive` selects rules with all weights positive and all nodes interior.
Segments use Gauss–Legendre rules, while triangles and tetrahedra use tabulated Xiao–Gimbutas rules; the polynomial degree integrated exactly is $2p$ for affine mappings (where $p$ is element degree) and 7 for quadratic mappings.
Face integrals use $D-1$-dimensional rules of the same degree.

| Degree | 2 | 4 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triangle nodes | 3 | 6 | 15 |
| Tetrahedron nodes | 4 | 11 | 31 |

For affine mappings, $\det J$ is constant, so a degree-$2p$ rule exactly integrates constant-coefficient mass matrices (integrand degree $2p$) and stiffness matrices ($2p-2$).
For quadratic mappings, $\det J$ is quadratic and the inverse Jacobian is rational, so stiffness matrices are approximate.
Other rules can be supplied through `SimplexFemOptions::with_volume_rule` and `with_facet_rule`, but rules with insufficient dimension or exact degree are rejected.
Supplying a rule with negative weights loses the positivity property provided by positive weights.

### Mass and stiffness assembly

During preparation, each cell's element matrix (diffusion, reaction, and Robin terms) and consistent mass matrix are computed once and retained.
With affine mappings and spatially constant coefficients, integrals of basis products and gradient products are computed once on the reference element, leaving only Jacobian transformations for each cell.
`element_matrix(cell)` and `element_mass(cell)` return local matrices before boundary-condition elimination.

`assemble_csr` returns the global matrix in CSR form and eliminates Dirichlet degrees of freedom symmetrically.
If diffusion is Hermitian positive definite and reaction is symmetric, the eliminated matrix is symmetric and can be solved with CG.
`assemble_mass_csr` returns the uneliminated consistent mass $M_{ij}=\int\varphi_i\varphi_j\,dx$, and `mass_operator` returns the same mass as a `SimplexFemMassOperator`.
`SimplexFemMassOperator` implements `LinearOperator`; since mass is real symmetric, it is its own adjoint.
The `lumped_mass` used by time-dependent plans is HRZ lumped mass, scaling the element mass diagonal up to the total element mass; it is positive for both P1 and P2.

The matrix sparsity pattern is determined once during preparation from cell/degree-of-freedom connectivity and Dirichlet elimination; entries whose sums numerically vanish remain in the pattern.
`assembly_workspace` allocates a `SimplexCsrWorkspace` with one value per structural entry.
Using it, `assemble_csr_into` (or `mass_assembly_workspace` and `assemble_mass_csr_into` for mass) rewrites values in an existing CSR matrix of the same pattern without allocation and leaves the output unchanged on failure.
Contributions are summed in cell order, so results do not depend on the execution worker count (`with_execution_policy`, at most 32).

### Solution fields and point evaluation

`fields(values)` converts the unknown sequence to a `SimplexFemField`; `vertices` returns vertex fields by component, and `edges` returns P2 edge-midpoint fields.
In the reverse direction, construct `SimplexFemFieldView::new(vertices, edges)` from borrowed vertex and edge fields and pack the unknown sequence with `pack_fields_into`.
`apply_fields` applies the operator to the same borrowed fields and returns owned fields.

`evaluate_into(cell, reference, input, values, gradients)` computes solution values and physical-coordinate gradients by component at a reference-coordinate point within a cell.
It reads only that cell's degrees of freedom, needs no workspace, and writes outputs after every component has been evaluated successfully.
Gradients are computed by multiplying reference-element basis gradients by the transpose of the inverse Jacobian.
`basis_into` returns reference-element basis values and gradients.

### Limits and expected errors

`SimplexFemLimits::new(max_unknowns, max_element_entries, max_quadrature_nodes, max_storage_bytes)` checks limits on unknowns, total element-matrix entries, quadrature nodes, and preparation storage before preparation.
By default, only quadrature nodes are limited, to 31; other limits are unlimited.
`preparation_storage_bound` returns an estimate of peak storage during preparation.

For smooth solutions and shape-regular mesh families, degree-$p$ elements have errors $O(h^p)$ in the $H^1$ norm and $O(h^{p+1})$ in the $L^2$ norm (Brenner–Scott).
For P2, $O(h^3)$ is also expected in the maximum norm.
At nodes of uniform triangulations, superconvergence can give faster convergence than this.
Approximating a curved boundary by straight edges gives domain error $O(h^2)$, which limits P2 element accuracy.

## P1 and P2 convergence and field evaluation

The following cell supplies the manufactured solution $u=x(1-x)\sin(m\pi y)$ for $-\Delta u=f$ on the unit square and solves with P1 and P2 elements on a mesh formed by splitting each square of an $N\times N$-vertex grid ($N=9,17,33$) into two triangles.
Every exterior face receives tag 1, and one Dirichlet condition covers the boundary.
The eliminated matrix is symmetric positive definite, so CG iterates to relative residual $10^{-12}$.

Errors are measured in three ways.
First is the maximum error at all degree-of-freedom interpolation points; second is the maximum error in the value returned by `evaluate_into` at reference coordinates $(0.2,0.3)$ in each cell (an interior point rather than an interpolation point); last is the gradient error $\max|\nabla u_h-\nabla u|$ at the same points.
Physical point coordinates come from `map`.
The first table shows errors by grid, and the second shows observed orders from the two finest grids.

```rust

struct Poisson;
impl SimplexFemCoefficients<f64, f64, 2> for Poisson {
    fn is_spatially_constant(&self) -> bool {
        true
    }
    fn evaluate(&self, _: [f64; 2], k: &mut [f64], c: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        k.copy_from_slice(&[1.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0]);

let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });

    let [x, y] = p;

        (1.0 - 2.0 * x) * (k * y).sin(),
        k * x * (1.0 - x) * (k * y).cos(),
    ]
};

    let [x, y] = p.point;

            cells.push([v, v + 1, v + n + 1]);

        let plan = SimplexFemPlan::with_options(
            mesh.clone(),
            geometry,
            order,
            &Poisson,
            &boundaries,
            options,
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let cg = CgSolver::new(
            ResidualTolerance::new(0.0, 1e-12).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
            Budget::try_from(10 * plan.unknowns()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,

        let mut workspace = EllipticWorkspace::new().with_linear_solver(Box::new(cg));
        let mut values = vec![0.0; plan.unknowns()];

            .solve_into(
                &EllipticOptions::new(),
                &mut values,

                ExecutionContext::sequential(),

            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

            return Err(format!("{label} N={n}: CG did not converge {}", report.termination()));
        }
        let mut nodal = 0.0_f64;
        for (node, u) in values.iter().enumerate() {
            let p = plan.dof_point(node).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point;
            nodal = nodal.max((u - exact(p)).abs());
        }
        // Evaluate values and physical gradients at reference coordinates (0.2, 0.3) in every cell.
        let (mut inner, mut slope) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
        let (mut value, mut gradient) = ([0.0], [[0.0; 2]]);
        for cell in 0..plan.mesh().cell_count() {
            let p = plan
                .map(cell, [0.2, 0.3])
                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
                .point();
            plan.evaluate_into(cell, [0.2, 0.3], &values, &mut value, &mut gradient)
                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            inner = inner.max((value[0] - exact(p)).abs());
            let g = grad(p);
            slope = slope.max((gradient[0][0] - g[0]).hypot(gradient[0][1] - g[1]));
        }
        let iterations = report.diagnostics().num_linear_iterations();
        rows.push((label, n, plan.unknowns(), iterations, nodal, inner, slope));
    }
}

    { "key": "3", "label": "CG iterations", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum interpolation-point error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Maximum interior-point error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Maximum gradient error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);

        .iter()
        .filter(|r| r.0 == label)
        .map(|r| [r.4, r.5, r.6][column])

};
let orders: Vec<(&str, f64, f64, f64)> = ["P1", "P2"]
    .into_iter()
    .map(|label| {
        (
            label,
            observed(label, 0),
            observed(label, 1),
            observed(label, 2),
        )
    })
    .collect();
let order_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Entry" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Interpolation points [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Interior points [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Gradient [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    errors,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    order_columns,
    orders,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })
);
```

With the default ($m=1$), interpolation-point errors at $N=33$ were $1.96\times10^{-4}$ for P1 and $1.66\times10^{-7}$ for P2, with observed interpolation-point orders of 1.997 and 3.998, respectively.
At interior points, the orders are 1.994 for P1 and 3.010 for P2; the fourth-order P2 interpolation-point result is superconvergence on uniform triangulations.
The interior-point P2 error is $4.56\times10^{-6}$, 27 times the interpolation-point error, so evaluation points must be specified when discussing accuracy.
Gradient error orders are 0.969 for P1 and 1.990 for P2, agreeing with the theoretical $O(h^p)$.
For the same vertex count, P2 has about four times as many unknowns and requires more CG iterations (67 for P1 and 151 for P2 at $N=33$), but needs far fewer unknowns for the same error.

## Curved boundaries and quadratic geometry

The following cell solves $-\Delta u=1$ on the unit disk $\Omega=\{|x|<1\}$ with $u=0$ on the boundary, using P2 elements (switchable to P1 through the input).
The exact solution $u=(1-|x|^2)/4$ is quadratic, so P2 elements introduce almost no solution-approximation error.
The main error source is geometric error from approximating the boundary with element edges.

The mesh is constructed from the center and $6k$ equally spaced vertices on circles of radius $k/n$ ($k=1,\dots,n$), triangulating between adjacent circles ($n=4,8,16$).
The boundary is a regular $6n$-gon, and all triangles have roughly similar shapes.
With `Affine`, boundary edge-midpoint degrees of freedom lie at chord midpoints.
`Quadratic` receives all edge midpoints, with only boundary midpoints moved radially onto the circle, turning boundary edges into inscribed parabolic arcs.

The table reports the maximum error over all interpolation points.
$\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}M\mathbf{1}$ is the sum obtained by applying the consistent mass to an all-ones vector with `mass_operator` and equals the discretized domain area $\int_{\Omega_h}1\,dx$.
Compare it with the circle area $\pi$ and with the analytically computed area of the discrete domain.
The affine domain is a regular $6n$-gon with area $3n\sin(\pi/3n)$.
For quadratic mappings, each edge is a parabolic arc; by Archimedes' quadrature, the area between arc and chord is $4/3$ times the triangle area $\sin\theta\,(1-\cos\theta)$ ($\theta=\pi/6n$) formed by the two endpoints and midpoint.
The integrand is a fourth-degree polynomial for affine mappings and a sixth-degree polynomial including $\det J$ for quadratic mappings, so quadrature should reproduce both areas to roundoff.

```rust
use scioxide_pde::{

use std::f64::consts::PI;

let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });

    SimplexFemOrder::P1
} else {
    SimplexFemOrder::P2

// Place 6k vertices on circle k of radius k/n. Index of vertex j on circle k.
let vertex = |k: usize, j: usize| {
    if k == 0 {
        0
    } else {
        1 + 3 * k * (k - 1) + j % (6 * k)
    }
};
let mut rows: Vec<(&str, usize, usize, f64, Option<f64>, f64, f64, f64)> = Vec::new();
for kind in ["Affine", "Quadratic"] {
    let mut previous: Option<f64> = None;
    for n in [4_usize, 8, 16] {
        let mut points = vec![Point::new([0.0, 0.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?];
        for k in 1..=n {
            for j in 0..6 * k {
                let angle = 2.0 * PI * j as f64 / (6 * k) as f64;
                let r = k as f64 / n as f64;
                points.push(
                    Point::new([r * angle.cos(), r * angle.sin()])
                        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
                );
            }
        }
        // Fill each of the six sectors between circles k − 1 and k with 2k − 1 triangles.
        let mut cells = Vec::new();
        for k in 1..=n {
            for s in 0..6 {
                for t in 0..k {
                    let inner = |t: usize| vertex(k - 1, s * (k - 1) + t);
                    cells.push([inner(t), vertex(k, s * k + t), vertex(k, s * k + t + 1)]);
                    if t + 1 < k {
                        cells.push([inner(t), vertex(k, s * k + t + 1), inner(t + 1)]);
                    }
                }
            }
        }
        let mesh = TriangleMesh::new(points, cells).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        // Edge midpoints. Move only boundary midpoints radially onto the circle.
        let mut midpoints = Vec::new();
        for edge in 0..mesh.edge_count() {
            let [u, v] = *mesh.edge_vertices(edge).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            let (p, q) = (mesh.points()[u].as_array(), mesh.points()[v].as_array());
            midpoints.push([0.5 * (p[0] + q[0]), 0.5 * (p[1] + q[1])]);
        }
        for face in mesh.boundary_faces() {
            let m = midpoints[face];
            midpoints[face] = [m[0] / m[0].hypot(m[1]), m[1] / m[0].hypot(m[1])];
        }
        // Discrete domain area: a regular 6n-gon, plus the parabolic-arc area at each edge for quadratic mappings (4/3 of the triangle area).
        let half = PI / (6 * n) as f64;
        let mut area = 3.0 * n as f64 * (2.0 * half).sin();
        if kind == "Quadratic" {
            area += (6 * n) as f64 * 4.0 / 3.0 * half.sin() * (1.0 - half.cos());
        }
        let edges = UnstructuredEdgeField::new(&mesh, midpoints).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let geometry = if kind == "Affine" {
            SimplexFemGeometry::Affine
        } else {
            SimplexFemGeometry::Quadratic(edges.view())
        };
        let faces = mesh.boundary_faces().map(|face| (face, 1)).collect();
        let tags = SimplexBoundaryTags::new(&mesh, faces).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let conditions = vec![BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(1, BoundaryData::zero())];
        let boundaries =
            TaggedBoundaries::new(&mesh, &tags, 1, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let unit = ConstantDiffusion::new(1.0);
        let plan = SimplexFemPlan::new(mesh.clone(), geometry, order, &unit, &boundaries)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let solution = EllipticProblem::new(&plan, &FieldData::constant(1.0))
            .solve_with_options(&EllipticOptions::new())
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if !solution.report().is_success() {
            return Err(format!("{kind} N={n}: {}", solution.report().termination()));
        }
        let mut error = 0.0_f64;
        for (node, u) in solution.values().iter().enumerate() {
            let p = plan.dof_point(node).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.point;
            error = error.max((u - exact(p)).abs());
        }
        // 1ᵀM1: sum of the consistent mass applied to an all-ones vector.
        let mass = plan.mass_operator();
        let ones = vec![1.0; mass.cols()];
        let mut product = vec![0.0; mass.rows()];
        let mut scratch = vec![0.0; mass.apply_workspace_len().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?];
        mass.apply_into(&ones, &mut product, &mut scratch)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let measured = product.iter().sum::<f64>();
        let observed = previous.map(|e| (e / error).log2());
        previous = Some(error);
        let gap = (measured - area).abs();
        rows.push((
            kind,
            n,
            plan.unknowns(),
            error,
            observed,
            measured - PI,
            area,
            gap,
        ));
    }
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Geometry" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Subdivisions N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Unknowns", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum interpolation-point error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Observed order [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "1ᵀM1 − π [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Discrete domain area [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 8 } },
    { "key": "7", "label": "|1ᵀM1 − area| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "disk" })
);
```

With the default (P2), affine interpolation-point errors at $n=4,8,16$ were $4.26\times10^{-3}$, $1.07\times10^{-3}$, and $2.68\times10^{-4}$, decreasing at second order with observed order 1.998.
Quadratic mappings gave $1.15\times10^{-4}$, $1.52\times10^{-5}$, and $1.95\times10^{-6}$, approaching third order with observed order 2.963; the error at $n=16$ is about 1/140 of the affine error.
The unknowns are the same, so this difference comes solely from the boundary approximation.
For $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}M\mathbf{1}-\pi$ at $n=16$, affine mappings give $-2.24\times10^{-3}$ and quadratic mappings give $-1.20\times10^{-7}$; polygon area errors decrease as $O(h^2)$, while errors in areas bounded by parabolic arcs decrease as $O(h^4)$.
For both geometries, $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}M\mathbf{1}$ agrees with the analytical area to about $10^{-14}$, confirming that quadrature integrates the mass matrix exactly.

With P1, the observed order is 1.93 even for quadratic mappings; the second-order solution-approximation error remains, so increasing mapping order does not increase convergence order.
In this problem, the P1 error with quadratic mappings ($2.62\times10^{-4}$ at $n=16$) is larger than with affine mappings ($8.02\times10^{-5}$).
In boundary elements curved by quadratic mappings, linear functions in reference coordinates are not linear in physical coordinates, so the P1 space there does not contain physical-coordinate linear functions.
Use quadratic mappings to preserve curved-boundary accuracy with P2 or higher-order elements.

## Summary

`SimplexFemPlan` assembles coupled elliptic weak forms with P1 and P2 elements on intervals, triangles, and tetrahedra, returning a matrix with symmetric Dirichlet elimination and an uneliminated consistent mass.
Default quadrature uses positive weights and interior nodes; for affine mappings, degree $2p$ exactly integrates mass and constant-coefficient stiffness.
On uniform triangulations, P2 interpolation-point errors exhibit fourth-order superconvergence, but interior-point values from `evaluate_into` are third order and gradients second order; specify evaluation points and mesh families when discussing order.
For curved boundaries, affine P2 elements are limited to second order by geometric error; placing boundary edge midpoints on the curve with `SimplexFemGeometry::Quadratic` restores third order.
Comparing $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}M\mathbf{1}$ with the discrete domain area is a simple independent check of the mapping and quadrature.
