This page collects elasticity and incompressible-flow plans on triangular and tetrahedral meshes ($D=2,3$).
Neither plan accepts one-dimensional interval meshes.
Both share `SimplexFemOptions` (quadrature, geometry conditions, execution, and limits) and `TaggedBoundaries` (componentwise boundary conditions and the policy for conflicting Dirichlet data at shared nodes).
Boundary conditions are limited to time-independent Dirichlet data and traction (natural boundaries) with $D$ components; Robin and time-dependent data are rejected.
Linear plans provide operators and loads, while nonlinear plans provide residuals and consistent tangents (`scioxide_core::ResidualOperator` and `JacobianOperator`).
A tangent at a fixed state is represented either by `PdeTangent`, which calls the residual provider's product on each application, or by `SimplexLinearization`, which `linearize` evaluates once and stores in CSR form.
The matrix of a linear plan can be rewritten into an existing CSR matrix with the same structure, without allocation, using `assemble_csr_into` and the workspace of `assembly_workspace`.
Stokes and Navier–Stokes also provide solves whose workspaces hold the solver and preconditioner, both of which can be replaced by injected implementations.

## Elasticity

Discretize the isotropic Hooke law (small strain) and the compressible neo-Hooke law (finite strain) in displacement form or a mixed form with P2 displacement and P1 pressure.
Select plane strain, plane stress, or three dimensions explicitly.
When the bulk modulus is much larger than the shear modulus, the displacement formulation can suffer locking (excessive stiffness caused by the volume constraint), so choose the mixed formulation.

## Incompressible flow

Discretize the Stokes equations and conservative Navier–Stokes equations with constant density and viscosity using P2 velocity and P1 pressure (Taylor–Hood elements).
Pressure is positive in compression.
Set a gauge for the constant pressure mode on each connected component whose pressure is not determined by the boundary; by default, a zero weighted-mean gauge is selected automatically.
The library saddle-point preconditioner is an approximate block LDU inverse action, using $S\approx M_p/\mu$ for the Schur complement in steady viscous problems and $S\approx B\,\mathrm{diag}(M_\rho)^{-1}B^{H}$ in velocity-mass saddle-point systems.
This inverse action can generally be indefinite and nonsymmetric, so combine it with GMRES rather than methods requiring a positive preconditioner.

## Summary

Elasticity and incompressible-flow plans pass operators, residuals, and tangents to existing solvers; incompressible flow and mixed elasticity use a stable mixed element pair (P2/P1).
Choose the mixed formulation for materials with a large bulk modulus, and check the pressure gauge in closed channels.
When repeatedly using tangent products or CSR at the same state, evaluate the tangent once with `linearize` and retain it in the workspace.
Solve steady Stokes and Navier–Stokes with the workspace's solver and preconditioner; for time evolution, advance the constrained-velocity ODE with an explicit method and recover pressure from the saddle-point system at each evaluation.
Read the solve conclusion using both the termination reasons of `IterativeSolveReport` and `NewtonKrylovResult` and the physical diagnostics of `IncompressibleDiagnostics`.
