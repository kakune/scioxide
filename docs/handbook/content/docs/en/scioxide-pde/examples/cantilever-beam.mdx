A cantilever is a beam fixed at one end and loaded at the other, widely used as a check in structural mechanics.
Beam theory represents a slender beam using only cross-section rotation and deflection, whereas finite elements solve the beam as a two-dimensional elastic body.
This example solves the beam with the linear-elasticity plan `LinearElasticityPlan` and checks results against an exact elasticity solution, Euler–Bernoulli and Timoshenko beam theories, and work–energy balance.
The second half brings Poisson ratio ν toward 1/2 in plane strain and measures volumetric locking, where displacement-only elements become excessively stiff, by comparison with mixed elements.
See [Finite elements for elasticity](../../algorithms/elasticity/) for the elasticity weak form, plane-stress and plane-strain reductions, and derivation of the mixed formulation.

## Problem setup

The beam is a rectangle $[0,L]\times[-H/2,H/2]$ of length $L=10$, height $H=1$, and thickness 1; $y$ is measured from the neutral axis.
The material is isotropic, with Young's modulus $E=1000$ and Poisson ratio $\nu=0.3$.
At the tip $x=L$, impose shear traction distributed parabolically over the height, giving a downward resultant $P=1$.
The top and bottom faces are unloaded, and there is no body force.
Values are dimensionless in a consistent unit system; if length is measured in cm and force in N, $E$ has units N/cm².

In linear theory both deflection and stress are proportional to $P$, so $P=1$ is chosen to read the proportionality coefficients.
Tip deflection is about 40% of $L$, exceeding the small-deformation assumption for an actual member.
To evaluate a real beam of this shape, make $P$ sufficiently small or use the finite-strain `HyperelasticityPlan`.

Treat it as a thin plate with small thickness and no out-of-plane stress, and solve in plane stress (`ElasticityKinematics::PlaneStress`).
The second moment of area is $I=H^3/12=1/12$.

## Exact elasticity solution and beam theory

A rectangular cantilever subjected to parabolically distributed tip shear has an exact plane-stress elasticity solution (Timoshenko–Goodier).
The stresses are

```text
σ_xx = M(x) y / I,   M(x) = P (L − x),
σ_yy = 0,
τ_xy = −P/(2I) · (H²/4 − y²)
```

Here $M$ is the magnitude of the cross-sectional bending moment, and the top face $y>0$ is in tension.
The displacements are

```text
u_x = c y [ (6L − 3x) x + (2 + ν)(y² − H²/4) ],
u_y = −c [ 3ν y² (L − x) + (4 + 5ν) H² x / 4 + (3L − x) x² ],   c = P / (6EI)
```


Substituting these displacements into the plane-stress Hooke law gives the stresses above, which satisfy equilibrium and boundary conditions on the top, bottom, and tip.
However, displacements at the fixed end $x=0$ are nonzero: cross-section warping $u_x$ and Poisson-effect displacement $u_y$ remain.
This field is the exact solution when these displacements are imposed at $x=0$, allowing precise measurement of finite element error.

Tip deflection $\delta=-u_y(L,y)$ is independent of $y$ and equals

```text
δ = P L³ / (3EI) + (4 + 5ν) P H² L / (24EI) = 4 + 0.0275 = 4.0275
```


The first term is Euler–Bernoulli beam deflection $\delta_\mathrm{EB}=4$, assuming cross sections remain planar and normal to the neutral axis; the second is the shear-deformation contribution.
Timoshenko beam theory adds shear deformation as the difference between cross-section rotation and deflection angle; with shear correction factor $\kappa=5/6$ and shear modulus $G=E/(2(1+\nu))$, it gives

```text
δ_T = P L³ / (3EI) + P L / (κ G H) = 4 + 0.0312 = 4.0312
```


The difference 0.0037 between $\delta_\mathrm{T}$ and the exact solution equals the fixed-end reaction work $W_0$ described next, divided by $P$.
For a rectangular cross section, $\kappa=5/6$ is determined by the energy stored by parabolically distributed shear.
Indeed, strain energy $U$ of the exact solution satisfies $2U=P\delta_\mathrm{T}=4.0312$.

For a linear elastic body in equilibrium, total external work equals twice the strain energy (Clapeyron's theorem).
Tip traction work is $W_\mathrm{ext}=P\delta=4.0275$; the remainder is the work of fixed-end reactions through the exact displacement:

```text
W_0 = (4 − ν) P² L / (10 E H) = 0.0037
```


Setting all displacements at $x=0$ to zero gives $W_0=0$ and $2U=W_\mathrm{ext}$.

## Plan and solve

Divide the beam into $n_x\times n_y$ ($n_x=10n_y$) rectangles, splitting each diagonally into two triangles.
Tag exterior faces with 1 at the fixed end $x=0$, 2 at the tip $x=L$, and 3 on the top and bottom.
`TaggedBoundaries` requires exactly one condition per exterior-face/component pair for a two-component field.
For tag 1, set componentwise `BoundaryCondition::dirichlet`; for tag 2, set `BoundaryCondition::natural` with 0 in component $x$ and $t_y=-P/(2I)(H^2/4-y^2)$ in component $y$.
Elasticity natural-boundary data are components of traction $t_a=\sigma_{ab}n_b$.
Tag 3 uses one condition without specifying a component, setting traction to zero in both components.

Convert material parameters to shear and bulk moduli with `IsotropicElasticMaterial::from_young_poisson(E, ν)`, and choose the reduction and elements with `ElasticityOptions::new(kinematics, formulation)`.
`LinearElasticityPlan::new` prepares element matrices and loads; `assemble_csr` returns a CSR matrix with symmetric elimination of Dirichlet degrees of freedom, and `rhs` returns the lifted right-hand side.
Since elasticity plans do not provide solves, this example uses sparse LU from `scioxide-sparse` (`scioxide_sparse::solve`), checking the solve by measuring $Ax-b$ with the plan operator `apply_into`.
The solution is component-major; `fields` separates it into vertex displacement fields.
Compute stress with `stress(cell, point, x)` by specifying a cell and a point in reference coordinates.

The following block provides the mesh, boundary conditions, exact solution, and solve procedure shared by two cells.

```rust
//| id: pde-cantilever-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Cantilever mesh, boundary conditions, and exact elasticity solution"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-sparse, scioxide-spatial]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, LinearOperator};
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, BoundaryPoint, ElasticityFormulation, ElasticityKinematics,
    ElasticityOptions, IsotropicElasticMaterial, LinearElasticityPlan, SimplexFemGeometry,
    SimplexFemLimits, SimplexFemOptions, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{Point, SimplexBoundaryTags, TriangleMesh};

/// Beam of length L, height H, and thickness 1. Parabolic shear at tip x = L gives downward resultant P.
#[derive(Clone, Copy)]
pub(crate) struct Beam {
    pub(crate) length: f64,
    pub(crate) height: f64,
    pub(crate) young: f64,
    pub(crate) poisson: f64,
    pub(crate) load: f64,
    pub(crate) plane_strain: bool,
}

impl Beam {
    /// Second moment of area I = H³/12 (thickness 1).
    pub(crate) fn inertia(&self) -> f64 {
        self.height * self.height * self.height / 12.0
    }
    /// For plane strain, reinterpret the plane-stress formulas with E' = E/(1−ν²), ν' = ν/(1−ν).
    pub(crate) fn effective(&self) -> (f64, f64) {
        let (e, nu) = (self.young, self.poisson);
        if self.plane_strain {
            (e / (1.0 - nu * nu), nu / (1.0 - nu))
        } else {
            (e, nu)
        }
    }
    /// Displacements (u_x, u_y) of the Timoshenko–Goodier exact solution. Measure y from the neutral axis.
    pub(crate) fn exact(&self, x: f64, y: f64) -> [f64; 2] {
        let (e, nu) = self.effective();
        let (l, h) = (self.length, self.height);
        let c = self.load / (6.0 * e * self.inertia());
        let shear = (4.0 + 5.0 * nu) * h * h * x / 4.0;
        [
            c * y * ((6.0 * l - 3.0 * x) * x + (2.0 + nu) * (y * y - h * h / 4.0)),
            -c * (3.0 * nu * y * y * (l - x) + shear + (3.0 * l - x) * x * x),
        ]
    }
    /// Bending stress σ_xx = M y / I, M = P (L − x). The same in plane strain.
    pub(crate) fn bending_stress(&self, x: f64, y: f64) -> f64 {
        self.load * (self.length - x) * y / self.inertia()
    }
}

/// Condition at fixed end x = 0. Exact imposes exact displacements; Clamped imposes 0.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, PartialEq)]
pub(crate) enum Support {
    Exact,
    Clamped,
}

/// Triangulation of rectangles with vertex j (n_x + 1) + i and cell 2 (j n_x + i) + k.
pub(crate) fn beam_mesh(beam: &Beam, ny: usize) -> Result<TriangleMesh<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let nx = 10 * ny;
    let (dx, dy) = (beam.length / nx as f64, beam.height / ny as f64);
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..=ny {
        for i in 0..=nx {
            points.push(Point::new([
                i as f64 * dx,
                j as f64 * dy - beam.height / 2.0,
            ])?);
        }
    }
    let mut cells = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..ny {
        for i in 0..nx {
            let v = j * (nx + 1) + i;
            cells.push([v, v + 1, v + nx + 2]);
            cells.push([v, v + nx + 2, v + nx + 1]);
        }
    }
    TriangleMesh::new(points, cells)
}

/// Exterior-face tags: 1 at x = 0, 2 at x = L, 3 on the top and bottom.
pub(crate) fn beam_boundaries(
    beam: &Beam,
    mesh: &TriangleMesh<f64>,
    support: Support,
) -> Result<TaggedBoundaries<f64, f64, 2>, SciOxideError> {
    let mut faces = Vec::new();
    for face in mesh.boundary_faces() {
        let v = mesh.face_vertices(face)?;
        let x = 0.5 * (mesh.points()[v[0]].as_array()[0] + mesh.points()[v[1]].as_array()[0]);
        let tag = if x < 1e-9 * beam.length {
            1
        } else if x > (1.0 - 1e-9) * beam.length {
            2
        } else {
            3
        };
        faces.push((face, tag));
    }
    let tags = SimplexBoundaryTags::new(mesh, faces)?;
    let b = *beam;
    let support_data = |component: usize| match support {
        Support::Exact => BoundaryData::time_invariant(move |p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| {
            Ok(b.exact(p.point[0], p.point[1])[component])
        }),
        Support::Clamped => BoundaryData::zero(),
    };
    // Tip traction t_y = −P/(2I) (H²/4 − y²). Resultant is −P.
    let shear = BoundaryData::time_invariant(move |p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| {
        let y = p.point[1];
        Ok(-b.load / (2.0 * b.inertia()) * (b.height * b.height / 4.0 - y * y))
    });
    let conditions = vec![
        BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(1, support_data(0)).for_component(0),
        BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(1, support_data(1)).for_component(1),
        BoundaryCondition::natural(2, BoundaryData::zero()).for_component(0),
        BoundaryCondition::natural(2, shear).for_component(1),
        BoundaryCondition::natural(3, BoundaryData::zero()),
    ];
    TaggedBoundaries::new(mesh, &tags, 2, conditions)
}

/// Prepare the plan, solve with sparse LU, and also return relative residual ‖Ax − b‖∞/‖b‖∞ measured with the plan operator.
pub(crate) fn solve_beam(
    beam: &Beam,
    ny: usize,
    formulation: ElasticityFormulation,
    support: Support,
) -> Result<(LinearElasticityPlan<f64, f64, 2>, Vec<f64>, f64), SciOxideError> {
    let mesh = beam_mesh(beam, ny)?;
    let boundaries = beam_boundaries(beam, &mesh, support)?;
    let kinematics = if beam.plane_strain {
        ElasticityKinematics::PlaneStrain
    } else {
        ElasticityKinematics::PlaneStress
    };
    let limits = SimplexFemLimits::new(1 << 16, 1 << 22, 64, 1 << 28);
    let options = ElasticityOptions::new(kinematics, formulation)
        .with_fem(SimplexFemOptions::new().with_limits(limits));
    let material = IsotropicElasticMaterial::from_young_poisson(beam.young, beam.poisson)?;
    let plan = LinearElasticityPlan::new(
        mesh,
        SimplexFemGeometry::Affine,
        material,
        &boundaries,
        &|_| Ok([0.0; 2]),
        options,
    )?;
    let x = scioxide_sparse::solve(&plan.assemble_csr()?, plan.rhs())?;
    let mut ax = vec![0.0; plan.unknowns()];
    let mut work = vec![0.0; plan.apply_workspace_len()?];
    plan.apply_into(&x, &mut ax, &mut work)?;
    let norm = |v: &[f64]| v.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, a| m.max(a.abs()));
    let defect: Vec<f64> = ax.iter().zip(plan.rhs()).map(|(a, b)| a - b).collect();
    let residual = norm(&defect) / norm(plan.rhs());
    Ok((plan, x, residual))
}

/// Physical coordinates of a cell centroid, corresponding to reference centroid (1/3, 1/3).
pub(crate) fn centroid(mesh: &TriangleMesh<f64>, cell: usize) -> Result<[f64; 2], SciOxideError> {
    let mut c = [0.0; 2];
    for &v in mesh.cell_vertices(cell)? {
        c[0] += mesh.points()[v].as_array()[0] / 3.0;
        c[1] += mesh.points()[v].as_array()[1] / 3.0;
    }
    Ok(c)
}
```

`Beam::exact` implements the displacement formula above, and `bending_stress` evaluates $\sigma_{xx}=My/I$.
`solve_beam` prepares the plan, assembles, solves with sparse LU, and computes the operator residual.
For plane strain, use the same formulas with the replacements $E'$ and $\nu'$ described later.

## Comparing deflection and bending stress

The next cell solves the beam with the selected elements and subdivision count, comparing tip deflection, bending stress, work, and energy with reference values.
Tip deflection is $-u_y$ at the tip vertex nearest the neutral axis; in the exact solution, tip $u_y$ is independent of $y$.
Compare bending stress at the centroid of the uppermost cell in the middle column $x\approx L/2$ of the beam.
`energy` returns internal energy $U$ (`internal`) and external work (`external_work`), the inner product of load vector and solution.
External work includes only traction and body-force contributions, excluding work of fixed-end reactions.
Pass the solved displacement and stress distributions to the next cell through `exports`.

    / (10.0 * beam.young * beam.height);

With default P2 displacement, $n_y=4$, and exact displacements at the fixed end, tip deflection is 4.027474, differing from the exact value 4.0275 by $3\times10^{-5}$.
It is 0.7% larger than the Euler–Bernoulli beam value 4; the difference is the shear-deformation contribution.
Bending stress at the uppermost cell centroid $y=0.4167$ in the middle column is 24.560, differing by 0.09% from the exact value 24.583 at that centroid.
$2U=4.031157$ is close to the exact value 4.0312, and $2U-W_\mathrm{ext}=0.003683$ agrees well with fixed-end reaction work $W_0=0.0037$.

With P1 displacement, tip deflection is 3.320, only 82% of the exact value.
Triangular P1 elements have constant strain within each cell and cannot represent $\varepsilon_{xx}$ varying linearly through the height in bending.
The resulting excess shear strain in place of bending stiffens the beam; this is called shear locking.
Refinement gives convergence, but error is still 18% at $n_y=4$.

Setting all fixed-end displacements to 0 reduces tip deflection to 4.0219.
The beam stiffens by restraining cross-section warping and Poisson contraction at the fixed end.
Here the reactions do no work, so $2U$ and $W_\mathrm{ext}$ agree to rounding error.
This agreement is an identity satisfied by the discrete system, not an estimate of discretization error.
Measure error against the exact solution or by comparing subdivision counts.

## Nearly incompressible materials and volumetric locking

For materials such as rubber with almost no volume change, ν is near 1/2 and bulk modulus $K$ is much larger than shear modulus $\mu$.
$K/\mu=2(1+\nu)/(3(1-2\nu))$ is 2.2 at $\nu=0.3$ and 5000 at $\nu=0.4999$.
In plane strain (`ElasticityKinematics::PlaneStrain`), where thickness strain is zero, the constraint suppressing volume change $\nabla\cdot u$ acts strongly.
Obtain the exact solution by replacing $E$ and $\nu$ in the plane-stress formulas with $E'=E/(1-\nu^2)$ and $\nu'=\nu/(1-\nu)$; stress remains $\sigma_{xx}=My/I$ as in plane stress.
Thickness stress $\sigma_{zz}=\nu\sigma_{xx}$ arises, giving exact mean stress $p=\operatorname{tr}\sigma/3$ equal to $(1+\nu)\sigma_{xx}/3$.

For displacement-only elements, the energy term $K(\nabla\cdot u)^2/2$ forces discrete displacements to satisfy $\nabla\cdot u_h\approx 0$ as $K\to\infty$.
Very few continuous piecewise linear displacements have zero divergence, so the entire displacement field becomes constrained and cannot move.
This is volumetric locking: deflection approaches zero, and mean stress $K\nabla\cdot u_h$ oscillates as a product of a large and a small number.
The mixed formulation (`ElasticityFormulation::MixedP2P1`) introduces mean stress as a separate P1 pressure unknown and imposes the volume constraint weakly.
The P2 displacement/P1 pressure pair satisfies the inf-sup condition under mild mesh conditions, so it is stable independently of $K/\mu$ ([Finite elements for elasticity](../../algorithms/elasticity/)).

The next cell varies ν from 0.3 to 0.4999 in plane strain, computing the tip-deflection/exact-solution ratio and maximum mean-stress error at cell centroids for three element types.
Mean stress is `pressure` of `stress`: $K\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon$ in displacement form, and the interpolated pressure unknown in mixed form.
Normalize error by the maximum exact mean stress to obtain a relative value.
Each run solves 15 times, so subdivisions are limited to 3 to keep browser execution time down.

//|   - {"id":"finer","label":"n_y = 3","description":"The P1 deflection ratio rises to 0.71 at ν = 0.3, but stays at 0.019 at ν = 0.4999.","inputs":{"locking_layers":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default

//|       - {"artifact":"locking","path":"/rows/13/4","approx":{"value":0.016655,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"locking","path":"/rows/14/3","approx":{"value":0.999978,"rtol":1e-5}}

//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"locking","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.71148,"rtol":1e-4}}

use scioxide_pde::{ElasticityFormulation, SimplexFemOrder};

let ny = locking_layers as usize;
let nx = 10 * ny;
let forms = [
    (
        "P1 displacement",
        ElasticityFormulation::Displacement(SimplexFemOrder::P1),
    ),
    (
        "P2 displacement",
        ElasticityFormulation::Displacement(SimplexFemOrder::P2),
    ),
    ("Mixed P2/P1", ElasticityFormulation::MixedP2P1),
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut ratio_series = vec![Vec::new(); 3];
let mut pressure_series = vec![Vec::new(); 3];
for nu in [0.3, 0.45, 0.49, 0.499, 0.4999] {
    let beam = Beam {
        length: 10.0,
        height: 1.0,
        young: 1000.0,
        poisson: nu,
        load: 1.0,
        plane_strain: true,
    };
    let bulk_ratio = 2.0 * (1.0 + nu) / (3.0 * (1.0 - 2.0 * nu));
    for (k, (label, form)) in forms.iter().enumerate() {
        let (plan, x, _) =
            solve_beam(&beam, ny, *form, Support::Exact).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let field = plan.fields(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        // Ratio of deflection at tip vertex (L, height nearest the neutral axis) to the exact solution at the same point.
        let tip = (ny / 2) * (nx + 1) + nx;
        let [px, py] = *plan.mesh().points()[tip].as_array();
        let ratio = field.displacement().vertices()[1].values()[tip] / beam.exact(px, py)[1];
        // Divide maximum mean-stress error at cell centroids by maximum exact mean stress.
        let (mut error, mut scale) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);

                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

            error = error.max((s.pressure() - mean).abs());

        ratio_series[k].push([bulk_ratio, ratio]);

With default $n_y=2$, the P1 displacement deflection ratio falls from 0.525 at $\nu=0.3$ to 0.0174 at $\nu=0.4999$.
Relative mean-stress error grows from 1.03 to 20.3, giving stresses unrelated to exact values.
Even refining to $n_y=3$ leaves the deflection ratio at $\nu=0.4999$ at 0.019; grid refinement alone does not remove locking.

With P2 displacement, this beam's deflection ratio is 1.0006 even at $\nu=0.4999$, so deflection does not lock.
However, relative mean-stress error increases about 5.5-fold from $3.0\times10^{-3}$ to $1.7\times10^{-2}$, worsening with $K/\mu$.
The divergence-free subspace of continuous P2 displacement depends on mesh shape, so absence of locking cannot be asserted for every mesh.

With mixed P2/P1, the deflection ratio ranges from 0.99996 to 0.99998 and relative mean-stress error from $3.0\times10^{-3}$ to $3.4\times10^{-3}$; both are almost independent of $K/\mu$.
In the plots, P1 points fall toward zero, while mixed-form points lie horizontally near deflection ratio 1 and relative error $3\times10^{-3}$.
For nearly incompressible materials, check stress as well as deflection and choose `MixedP2P1`.
Mixed-form internal energy is the value of a saddle-point functional, so `internal` of `energy` need not be positive.

## Summary

Solving the matrix assembled by `LinearElasticityPlan` with sparse LU confirms that P2 displacement tip deflection agrees with the Timoshenko–Goodier exact solution to relative difference of about $10^{-5}$.
Differences from beam theory follow from shear deformation and fixed-end conditions; the exact deflection is 0.7% larger than that of an Euler–Bernoulli beam.
The difference between $2U$ and external work equals fixed-end reaction work and vanishes when all displacements at the fixed end are clamped, so energy balance checks boundary-condition treatment.
P1 displacement is stiff from shear locking even in plane-stress bending; with $\nu\to1/2$ in plane strain, volumetric locking reduces deflection to 2% or less.
For nearly incompressible materials, choose `ElasticityFormulation::MixedP2P1`, which keeps both deflection and mean stress stable.
