Natural frequencies of a circular membrane clamped around its perimeter, like a drumhead, are determined by zeros of Bessel functions.
Finding indexed zeros and tabulating frequencies are covered in scioxide-rootfind's [Natural frequencies of a circular membrane and Bessel zeros](../../../scioxide-rootfind/examples/circular-membrane/).
This page cites those values as references and examines how accurately finite elements (FEM) reproduce the same eigenvalues.

Triangulating a disk approximates its curved perimeter by line segments.
Errors then have two sources: discretization error determined by element polynomial degree, and geometry error determined by approximation of the domain shape.
Measure how these errors appear in four combinations of P1/P2 elements and affine/quadratic geometry.

## Membrane eigenvalue problem

Transverse displacement $w$ of a membrane stretched over disk $\Omega$ of radius $a$ [m] follows the wave equation $w_{tt} = c^2\Delta w$; wave speed $c = \sqrt{T/\sigma}$ [m/s] is determined by tension $T$ [N/m] and surface density $\sigma$ [kg/m²].
Setting $w = u(\mathbf x)\cos(\omega t)$, $u$ satisfies the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

$$
-\Delta u = \lambda u \quad (\Omega), \qquad u = 0 \quad (\partial\Omega), \qquad \lambda = \omega^2/c^2
$$


Separating variables in polar coordinates gives eigenfunctions $J_\nu(j_{\nu,m}r/a)\cos(\nu\varphi)$ and $J_\nu(j_{\nu,m}r/a)\sin(\nu\varphi)$, with eigenvalues

$$
\lambda_{\nu,m} = \left(\frac{j_{\nu,m}}{a}\right)^2,
\qquad
f_{\nu,m} = \frac{c\,j_{\nu,m}}{2\pi a}
$$


Here $j_{\nu,m}$ denotes the $m$th positive zero of $J_\nu$.
Eigenvalues with $\nu = 0$ are simple; those with $\nu \ge 1$ are double, with the two eigenfunctions $\cos$ and $\sin$.
The lowest eight are $(\nu, m) = (0, 1), (1, 1)^2, (2, 1)^2, (0, 2), (3, 1)^2$, where superscript 2 denotes multiplicity.
Match the rootfind example with $a = 0.33$ m, $\sigma = 0.26$ kg/m², and $T = 4000$ N/m ($c = 124.03$ m/s).

## Galerkin method and generalized eigenvalue problem

The weak form requires $\int_\Omega \nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,dx = \lambda\int_\Omega uv\,dx$ for every $v$ in $H^1_0(\Omega)$.
Expanding $u_h = \sum_j U_j\phi_j$ in a basis of the finite element space $V_h$ gives the generalized eigenvalue problem

$$
K\mathbf U = \lambda_h M\mathbf U,
\qquad
K_{ij} = \int \nabla\phi_i\cdot\nabla\phi_j\,dx,
\quad
M_{ij} = \int \phi_i\phi_j\,dx
$$


$K$ is the stiffness matrix and $M$ the consistent mass matrix; both are symmetric and, after removing boundary degrees of freedom, positive definite.

By the eigenvalue min-max principle (Courant–Fischer), if $V_h \subset H^1_0(\Omega)$ and $K$ and $M$ are integrated exactly, then $\lambda_{h,k} \ge \lambda_k$ (matching the $k$th eigenvalues).
For degree-$p$ elements on a smooth domain represented exactly, eigenvalue error is $O(h^{2p})$ (I. Babuška and J. Osborn, Eigenvalue problems, *Handbook of Numerical Analysis* II, 1991).
$h$ is the element size.

## Boundary approximation and geometry error

In a triangulated disk, even if perimeter vertices lie on the circle, straight edges give an inscribed regular polygon $\Omega_h \subset \Omega$.
P1/P2 elements with affine geometry solve the problem on $\Omega_h$; extending functions in $H^1_0(\Omega_h)$ by zero puts them in $H^1_0(\Omega)$, so eigenvalues approach from above.
Geometry error from the difference between $\Omega_h$ and $\Omega$ adds to the error.

Estimate geometry error using the first-order eigenvalue variation under domain deformation (Hadamard's formula).
Moving the boundary inward by $\delta(s)$ gives $\delta\lambda \approx \int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_n u)^2\,\delta\,ds$ for a normalized eigenfunction $u$.
On a disk, $\int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_n u)^2 ds = 2\lambda/a$ (Rellich's identity), and the perimeter average of the squared normal derivative is $\lambda/(\pi a^2)$.
$(\partial_n u)^2$ is uniform along the perimeter for $\nu = 0$ and varies slowly as $\cos^2(\nu\varphi)$ even for $\nu \ge 1$, while $\delta(s)$ oscillates rapidly on each perimeter edge.
Thus the integral of the product is approximated by the average times $\int\delta\,ds = |\Omega| - |\Omega_h|$, giving

$$
\frac{\delta\lambda}{\lambda} \approx \frac{|\Omega| - |\Omega_h|}{|\Omega|}
= 1 - \frac{N_b}{2\pi}\sin\frac{2\pi}{N_b}
\approx \frac{2\pi^2}{3N_b^2}
$$


$N_b$ is the number of perimeter vertices; since perimeter-edge length is $h_b \approx 2\pi a/N_b$, geometry error is $O(h^2)$.
Consequently, even though P2 elements with affine geometry have discretization error $O(h^4)$, total error remains $O(h^2)$.

Quadratic geometry (`SimplexFemGeometry::Quadratic`) curves elements with quadratic maps by specifying physical coordinates at each edge midpoint.
Placing perimeter-edge midpoints on the circle makes the perimeter a quadratic interpolant of circular arcs, reducing domain-approximation error to $O(h^3)$.
With maps of the same degree as P2 elements (isoparametric elements), eigenvalue error returns to $O(h^4)$ (M. Lenoir, Optimal isoparametric finite elements and error estimates for domains involving curved boundaries, *SIAM J. Numer. Anal.* 23, 1986).
With quadratic geometry, $\Omega_h$ need not be contained in $\Omega$, and quadrature is not exact, so approximation from above is not guaranteed.

## Triangulating the disk

Divide the disk into concentric circles by splitting the radius into $n$ equal parts.
Place $6k$ equally spaced vertices on circle $k$ (radius $ka/n$); in each of six 60° sectors, follow inner and outer circle vertices in angular order to form triangles.
There are $6n^2$ triangles, $1 + 3n(n + 1)$ vertices, and $N_b = 6n$ perimeter vertices, with mesh width $h \approx a/n$.
Instead of Delaunay triangulation, use the same triangle rule in each sector, making the mesh invariant under 60° rotations.

For quadratic geometry, project midpoints of edges whose endpoints both lie on the perimeter onto the circle, keeping other edges straight.
Store midpoint coordinates in `UnstructuredEdgeField` in canonical edge order, and pass them to the plan with `SimplexFemGeometry::Quadratic(edges.view())`.
Impose homogeneous Dirichlet conditions on the entire perimeter with `TaggedBoundaries::uniform`.

Extract stiffness with `assemble_csr()` and consistent mass with `assemble_mass_csr()`.
Because `assemble_csr()` replaces rows and columns of Dirichlet degrees of freedom with the identity, extract only rows and columns of free degrees of freedom where `constrained()` is false to form $K_{ff}$ and $M_{ff}$.
Compute eigenvalues with scioxide-linalg's `GeneralizedHermitianEigenvaluesResult::compute`.
The solver uses Cholesky factorization of $M_{ff}$ to reduce to standard form and returns all eigenvalues in ascending order; for $N$ degrees of freedom, cost is $O(N^3)$ and storage $O(N^2)$ ([Generalized eigenvalue problems](../../../scioxide-linalg/algorithms/generalized-eigenproblems/)).
Since assembled $K$ can retain asymmetry at the rounding level, use `HermitianInput::Projected` to take symmetric part $(K + K^\top)/2$, rejecting if change norm $\|K - (K + K^\top)/2\|_F$ exceeds $\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,(1 + \|K\|_F)$.
Read the relative change through `a_projection_report()`; it is smaller than $10^{-16}$ in this page's computations.

The following definitions block provides `disc_mesh` for mesh construction, `membrane_spectrum` for eigenvalue computation, and `MODES` containing the lowest eight modes and zeros from the rootfind example; both cells on this page use it.

```rust
//| id: pde-drum-membrane-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Disk triangulation and generalized membrane eigenvalue problem"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
use scioxide_core::{RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::{
    GeneralizedHermitianEigenvaluesResult, HermitianInput, HermitianProjectionPolicy, Matrix,
};
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryData, BoundaryKind, ConstantDiffusion, SimplexFemGeometry, SimplexFemOrder,
    SimplexFemPlan, SpatialDiscretization, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{Point, TriangleMesh, UnstructuredEdgeField};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

/// Membrane radius a [m] (same value as the rootfind example).
pub(crate) const RADIUS: f64 = 0.33;

/// (ν, m, j_{ν,m}) for the lowest eight eigenvalues. j comes from the rootfind example.
pub(crate) const MODES: [(usize, usize, f64); 8] = [
    (0, 1, 2.404825557695773),
    (1, 1, 3.831705970207512),
    (1, 1, 3.831705970207512),
    (2, 1, 5.135622301840683),
    (2, 1, 5.135622301840683),
    (0, 2, 5.520078110286311),
    (3, 1, 6.380161895923984),
    (3, 1, 6.380161895923984),
];

/// Disk subdivision with n concentric circles. Circle k (radius ka/n) has 6k vertices.
pub(crate) fn disc_mesh(rings: usize) -> Result<TriangleMesh<f64>, String> {
    let mut points = vec![[0.0, 0.0]];
    for k in 1..=rings {
        let radius = RADIUS * k as f64 / rings as f64;
        for j in 0..6 * k {
            let angle = 2.0 * PI * j as f64 / (6 * k) as f64;
            points.push([
                radius * RealScalar::cos(angle),
                radius * RealScalar::sin(angle),
            ]);
        }
    }
    // Index of vertex j on circle k. Circle 0 is the single central point.
    let index = |k: usize, j: usize| {
        if k == 0 {
            0
        } else {
            3 * k * (k - 1) + 1 + j % (6 * k)
        }
    };
    let mut cells = Vec::new();
    for k in 1..=rings {
        for sector in 0..6 {
            // Within a sector, the inner circle has k − 1 intervals and the outer circle k intervals. Advance from smaller angles.
            let (inner, mut i, mut j) = (k - 1, 0, 0);
            while i < inner || j < k {
                let outer_next = i == inner || (j < k && (j + 1) * inner <= (i + 1) * k);
                let a = index(k - 1, sector * inner + i);
                let b = index(k, sector * k + j);
                let c = if outer_next {
                    j += 1;
                    index(k, sector * k + j)
                } else {
                    i += 1;
                    index(k - 1, sector * inner + i)
                };
                cells.push([a, b, c]);
            }
        }
    }
    let points = points
        .into_iter()
        .map(Point::new)
        .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    TriangleMesh::new(points, cells).map_err(|e| e.to_string())
}

/// Eigenvalue computation results.
pub(crate) struct Spectrum {
    /// Eigenvalues λ_h [1/m²] in ascending order.
    pub(crate) eigenvalues: Vec<f64>,
    /// Number of free degrees of freedom.
    pub(crate) free: usize,
    /// Number of triangles.
    pub(crate) cells: usize,
    /// Change from symmetrizing stiffness: ‖K − (K + Kᵀ)/2‖_F / max(‖K‖_F, 1).
    pub(crate) asymmetry: f64,
}

/// Eigenvalues of a membrane clamped around its perimeter. If curved, use quadratic geometry with perimeter-edge midpoints projected onto the circle.
pub(crate) fn membrane_spectrum(
    rings: usize,
    order: SimplexFemOrder,
    curved: bool,
) -> Result<Spectrum, String> {
    let mesh = disc_mesh(rings)?;
    let cells = mesh.cell_count();
    let first_boundary = 3 * rings * (rings - 1) + 1;
    let mut midpoints = Vec::new();
    for edge in 0..mesh.edge_count() {
        let [a, b] = *mesh.edge_vertices(edge).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let (p, q) = (mesh.points()[a].as_array(), mesh.points()[b].as_array());
        let mid = [0.5 * (p[0] + q[0]), 0.5 * (p[1] + q[1])];
        let on_circle = curved && a >= first_boundary && b >= first_boundary;
        let scale = if on_circle {
            RADIUS / RealScalar::hypot(mid[0], mid[1])
        } else {
            1.0
        };
        midpoints.push([scale * mid[0], scale * mid[1]]);
    }
    let edges = UnstructuredEdgeField::new(&mesh, midpoints).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let geometry = if curved {
        SimplexFemGeometry::Quadratic(edges.view())
    } else {
        SimplexFemGeometry::Affine
    };
    let fixed = TaggedBoundaries::<f64, f64, 2>::uniform(
        &mesh,
        1,
        BoundaryKind::Dirichlet,
        BoundaryData::zero(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // −Δu = λu: stiffness and consistent mass with diffusion coefficient 1 and reaction 0.
    let unit = ConstantDiffusion::new(1.0);
    let plan =
        SimplexFemPlan::new(mesh, geometry, order, &unit, &fixed).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let stiffness = plan.assemble_csr().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mass = plan.assemble_mass_csr().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Number free degrees of freedom consecutively and copy K_ff and M_ff into dense matrices.
    let mut number = vec![None; plan.unknowns()];
    let mut free = 0;
    for (slot, &constrained) in number.iter_mut().zip(plan.constrained()) {
        if !constrained {
            *slot = Some(free);
            free += 1;
        }
    }
    let mut dense = [vec![0.0; free * free], vec![0.0; free * free]];
    for (target, matrix) in dense.iter_mut().zip([&stiffness, &mass]) {
        for (row, col, value) in matrix.iter() {
            if let (Some(i), Some(j)) = (number[row], number[col]) {
                target[i * free + j] = value;
            }
        }
    }
    let [k, m] = dense;
    let k = Matrix::from_vec(free, free, k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let m = Matrix::from_vec(free, free, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let input = HermitianInput::Projected(HermitianProjectionPolicy::Within(
        Tolerance::from_sqrt_epsilon(),
    ));
    let result = GeneralizedHermitianEigenvaluesResult::compute(&k, &m, input, input)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let asymmetry = result
        .a_projection_report()
        .map_or(0.0, |r| r.relative_frobenius());
    Ok(Spectrum {
        eigenvalues: result.into_eigenvalues(),
        free,
        cells,
        asymmetry,
    })
}
```

## Eigenvalues and multiplicities on one mesh

The next cell takes concentric-circle count $n$, element degree, and geometry, and computes the lowest eight eigenvalues.
Outputs are a table of rootfind zero $j_{\nu,m}$, exact frequency, FEM frequency, relative eigenvalue error $(\lambda_h - \lambda)/\lambda$, pitch difference (cents), and relative difference within double-eigenvalue pairs for each mode, plus a table of mesh and eigensolver diagnostics.
Match eigenvalues to modes by their index in ascending order.
For $n \ge 3$, the lowest eight appear in this order for all four combinations.

```rust

    SimplexFemOrder::P1

    SimplexFemOrder::P2

// Wave speed c = √(T/σ) (T = 4000 N/m, σ = 0.26 kg/m²). Frequency f = c√λ/(2π) [Hz].
let speed = RealScalar::sqrt(4000.0_f64 / 0.26);

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (index, &(nu, m, j)) in MODES.iter().enumerate() {
    let exact = RealScalar::powi(j / RADIUS, 2);
    let lambda = spectrum.eigenvalues[index];
    // For ν ≥ 1 pairs, report relative difference from the other eigenvalue with the same (ν, m).

        0 => None,
        _ if MODES[index - 1].0 == nu => Some(index - 1),
        _ => Some(index + 1),

    let split = partner.map(|p| (spectrum.eigenvalues[p] - lambda).abs() / lambda);
    let cents = 1200.0 * RealScalar::log2(hertz(lambda) / hertz(exact));
    let label = format!("({nu}, {m})");
    rows.push((
        label,
        j,
        hertz(exact),
        hertz(lambda),
        (lambda - exact) / exact,
        cents,
        split,
    ));
}

    { "key": "1", "label": "Free degrees of freedom [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative K symmetrization change [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }

    vec![(spectrum.cells, spectrum.free, spectrum.asymmetry)],
```

With defaults ($n = 4$, P2 elements, quadratic geometry), there are 96 triangles and 169 free degrees of freedom; fundamental-mode $(0, 1)$ relative eigenvalue error is $2.1 \times 10^{-4}$, with a pitch difference of 0.18 cents.
Relative error grows with mode index, reaching $1.0 \times 10^{-2}$ for $(3, 1)$.
Higher modes have shorter wavelengths, so the same mesh has fewer elements per wavelength.
In this example, all eigenvalues for all four combinations exceed the exact values, giving positive errors.

Switching to affine geometry increases fundamental-mode relative error 60-fold to $1.3 \times 10^{-2}$, with all eight relative eigenvalue errors at $1.3 \sim 2.5 \times 10^{-2}$.
The polygon's area deficit $1 - (24/2\pi)\sin(2\pi/24) = 1.1 \times 10^{-2}$ adds almost equally to every mode.
P1 elements give fundamental-mode relative error $2.5 \times 10^{-2}$ even with quadratic geometry, reaching 0.23–0.33 for $(3, 1)$.

Treatment of double eigenvalues depends on mode order $\nu$.
Pairs for $(1, 1)$ and $(2, 1)$ agree to relative difference at most $10^{-13}$; only the $(3, 1)$ pair separates by $8.0 \times 10^{-4}$.
The mesh is invariant under 60° rotation $R$, so discrete $K$ and $M$ commute with degree-of-freedom permutation $R$.
On the plane spanned by $\cos(\nu\varphi)$ and $\sin(\nu\varphi)$, $R$ acts as a rotation through $60\nu$°.
For $\nu = 1, 2$ this rotation has no invariant line in the plane, so the approximating eigenspace stays two-dimensional and eigenvalues coincide to rounding error.
For $\nu = 3$, $R$ simply multiplies both functions by $-1$; symmetry therefore does not couple them, and the mesh distinguishes the two differently oriented modes, splitting their eigenvalues.
The pair difference shrinks at the same rate as the error, reaching $5.6 \times 10^{-5}$ for $n = 8$.

## Refinement and convergence order

The next cell increases concentric-circle counts through values in $n = 3, 4, 6, 8$ no larger than $n_{\max}$, computing relative errors of the lowest eight eigenvalues for all four combinations and passing them to the next cell.
Input is the maximum concentric-circle count $n_{\max}$.
P2 elements have 721 degrees of freedom at $n = 8$, and dense generalized eigensolver cost $O(N^3)$ determines runtime, so run by button and reuse results for identical inputs.
Output is a table of degrees of freedom for each mesh.

```rust
//| id: pde-drum-convergence-runs
//| ref: pde-drum-convergence-runs
//| title: "Increase concentric-circle count and compute eigenvalues for all four combinations"
//| run: button
//| cache: inputs
//| uses: [pde-drum-membrane-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| timeoutMs: 420000
//| exports: [rings, errors]
//| inputs:
//|   drum_max_rings: {"type":"select","label":"Maximum concentric-circle count n_max [-]","options":["6","8"],"value":"8"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"coarse","label":"n_max = 6","description":"Reduce to four meshes to shorten computation.","inputs":{"drum_max_rings":"6"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/3/0","equals":8}
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":721}
//|   - case: coarse
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sizes","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":397}
use scioxide_pde::SimplexFemOrder;

let max_rings: usize = drum_max_rings.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot read n_max")?;
let rings: Vec<usize> = [3, 4, 6, 8]
    .into_iter()
    .filter(|&n| n <= max_rings)
    .collect();
let configurations = [
    (SimplexFemOrder::P1, false),
    (SimplexFemOrder::P1, true),
    (SimplexFemOrder::P2, false),
    (SimplexFemOrder::P2, true),
];
// errors[combination][mesh][mode] = (λ_h − λ)/λ.
let mut errors = vec![Vec::new(); configurations.len()];
let mut sizes = Vec::new();
for &n in &rings {
    let mut free = [0; 2];
    for (target, &(order, curved)) in errors.iter_mut().zip(&configurations) {
        let spectrum = membrane_spectrum(n, order, curved)?;
        free[order.degree() - 1] = spectrum.free;
        let relative: Vec<f64> = MODES
            .iter()
            .zip(&spectrum.eigenvalues)
            .map(|(&(_, _, j), &lambda)| {
                let exact = (j / RADIUS) * (j / RADIUS);
                (lambda - exact) / exact
            })
            .collect();
        target.push(relative);
    }
    sizes.push((n, free[0], free[1]));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Concentric-circle count n [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "P1 degrees of freedom [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "P2 degrees of freedom [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    sizes,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sizes" })
);
export!(rings, rings);
export!(errors, errors);
```

The next cell computes convergence orders from the supplied relative errors.
Input is the mode to track.
Outputs are a log-log plot of relative error against mesh width $h = a/n$, a table of relative errors and polygon area deficit $1 - (N_b/2\pi)\sin(2\pi/N_b)$ for each mesh, and a table of observed orders $p = \log(e_1/e_2)/\log(n_2/n_1)$ between adjacent meshes.
Dashed lines give reference slopes proportional to $h^2$ and $h^4$, passing through the finest-mesh points for P1/affine and P2/quadratic.

```rust
//| id: pde-drum-convergence-orders
//| ref: pde-drum-convergence-orders
//| title: "Compare relative-error convergence orders with polygon area deficit"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [pde-drum-membrane-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| dependsOn:
//|   runs: pde-drum-convergence-runs
//| inputs:
//|   drum_tracked_mode: {"type":"select","label":"Mode to track (ν, m)","options":["(0, 1)","(1, 1)","(2, 1)","(0, 2)","(3, 1)"],"value":"(0, 1)"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"mode-31","label":"(3, 1)","description":"Track the lower double eigenvalue. Observed orders are 3.8–4.2 for P2/quadratic; P2/affine decreases from 3.1 to 2.5.","inputs":{"drum_tracked_mode":"(3, 1)"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":2.3,"atol":0.2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":0.2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/4","approx":{"value":4.2,"atol":0.3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":2.853e-3,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: mode-31
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":3.5,"max":5}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let rings: Vec<usize> =
    serde_json::from_value(runs["rings"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let errors: Vec<Vec<Vec<f64>>> =
    serde_json::from_value(runs["errors"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let labels = ["(0, 1)", "(1, 1)", "(2, 1)", "(0, 2)", "(3, 1)"];
let position = labels
    .iter()
    .position(|&l| l == drum_tracked_mode)
    .ok_or("Mode not found")?;
// For double eigenvalues, track the lower one (first row in MODES).
let mode = [0, 1, 3, 5, 6][position];
let names = ["P1, affine", "P1, quadratic", "P2, affine", "P2, quadratic"];
let h = |n: usize| RADIUS / n as f64;
let mut series = Vec::new();
for (name, runs) in names.iter().zip(&errors) {
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = rings
        .iter()
        .zip(runs)
        .map(|(&n, e)| [h(n), e[mode]])
        .collect();
    series.push(
        serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "style": { "symbol": "circle" }, "points": points }),
    );
}
// Reference slopes: h² and h⁴ passing through finest-mesh P1/affine and P2/quadratic points.
let last = rings.len() - 1;
let dashed = serde_json::json!({ "lineType": "dashed", "symbol": "none" });
for (power, runs, name) in [(2, &errors[0], "h² slope"), (4, &errors[3], "h⁴ slope")]
{
    let anchor = runs[last][mode] / RealScalar::powi(h(rings[last]), power);
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = rings
        .iter()
        .map(|&n| [h(n), anchor * RealScalar::powi(h(n), power)])
        .collect();
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "style": dashed, "points": points }));

    serde_json::json!({

        "dataZoom": true,

        "yType": "log"
    }),

);
// Area deficit of the inscribed regular 6n-gon (first-order geometry-error estimate).
let deficit = |n: usize| {
    let sides = 6.0 * n as f64;
    1.0 - sides / (2.0 * PI) * RealScalar::sin(2.0 * PI / sides)
};
let rows: Vec<_> = rings
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, &n)| {
        (
            n,
            errors[0][i][mode],
            errors[1][i][mode],
            errors[2][i][mode],
            errors[3][i][mode],

        )
    })
    .collect();

    { "key": "0", "label": "Concentric-circle count n [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "P1, affine [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },

);

    let ratio = errors[c][i][mode] / errors[c][i + 1][mode];
    RealScalar::ln(ratio) / RealScalar::ln(rings[i + 1] as f64 / rings[i] as f64)
};
let orders: Vec<_> = (0..last)
    .map(|i| {
        (
            format!("{} → {}", rings[i], rings[i + 1]),
            order(0, i),
            order(1, i),
            order(2, i),
            order(3, i),

    })
    .collect();

    { "key": "4", "label": "P2, quadratic [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);

    order_columns,
    orders,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })
```

With defaults ($n_{\max} = 8$), fundamental-mode $(0, 1)$ observed orders on the finest interval $6 \to 8$ are 2.23 for P1/affine, 2.31 for P1/quadratic, 2.07 for P2/affine, and 4.21 for P2/quadratic.
P1 elements give $O(h^2)$ regardless of geometry; quadratic geometry only reduces the error constant to about 0.6 times its value.
The P2 order of 4 appears only with quadratic geometry.
P2 elements with affine geometry remain $O(h^2)$, differing from P1/quadratic only in the error constant.

The ratio of P2/affine relative error to polygon area deficit approaches 1, taking values 1.21, 1.13, 1.07, and 1.05 at $n = 3, 4, 6, 8$.
The error of P2 elements with affine geometry is explained by geometry error estimated from the area deficit.
For higher modes such as $(3, 1)$, P2 discretization error is also large on coarse meshes, and observed P2/affine order falls from 3.1 to 2.5 on its way toward 2.
P1 observed order for $(0, 2)$ rises from 1.5 to 2.05; coarse meshes have not reached the asymptotic order.

At $n = 10$, P2 degrees of freedom reach 1141; cost $O(N^3)$ estimates that dense generalized eigenvalue computation takes about four times as long as at $n = 8$.
For large problems requiring only a few lowest eigenvalues, methods such as scioxide-linalg's LOBPCG (`LobpcgSolver`) compute extreme eigenvalues using only matrix products.

## Summary

Compute circular-membrane Dirichlet eigenvalues as a generalized eigenvalue problem by extracting free degrees of freedom from stiffness and consistent mass of `SimplexFemPlan`.
With affine geometry, a polygonal perimeter makes even P2 eigenvalue errors depend on $O(h^2)$ geometry error nearly equal to the area deficit.
Quadratic geometry (`SimplexFemGeometry::Quadratic`), placing perimeter-edge midpoints on the circle, gives $O(h^4)$ convergence of P2 eigenvalues.
P1 elements give $O(h^2)$ regardless of geometry.
Mesh rotational symmetry makes only some double eigenvalues (here $\nu = 1, 2$) coincide to rounding error; check multiplicity by comparing relative pair differences with the error.
