Heat conduction is dissipative: it rapidly damps sharp changes (high-wavenumber Fourier components) in the initial temperature distribution, smoothing the temperature field.
This example derives the heat equation from a metal rod's heat balance and compares backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson in `ParabolicProblem` from two perspectives.
For smooth sinusoidal modes, measure spatial and temporal errors separately.
For step initial data, examine how Crank–Nicolson leaves high-wavenumber components almost undamped and how Rannacher startup helps.
The comparison reference is the exact solution of the system discretized only in space (the semidiscrete system).

## Heat balance and the one-dimensional heat equation

Consider a thin rod with uniform density $\rho$, specific heat $c_p$, thermal conductivity $k$, and cross-sectional area $A$.
Heat stored in a small segment of length $\Delta x$ changes by $\rho c_pA\Delta x\,\partial_tT$ per unit time.
By Fourier's law $q=-k\,\partial_xT$, net heat inflow through its end faces is $-A\,\partial_xq\,\Delta x=Ak\,\partial_x^2T\,\Delta x$.
With no internal heat generation these are equal, giving the heat equation:

$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}=\alpha\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial x^2},\qquad \alpha=\frac{k}{\rho c_p}
$$

$\alpha$ is thermal diffusivity.
Pure copper and pure aluminum have thermal diffusivities of about $1\ \mathrm{cm^2/s}$ at room temperature, so this example measures length in cm and time in s.
Holding both ends of a rod of length $L$ at a reference temperature makes the difference $u$ from that temperature equal to $0$ at both ends.
Expanding the initial distribution in a sine series $u(x,0)=\sum_m a_m\sin(m\pi x/L)$, each mode decays independently as follows.

$$
u_m(x,t)=a_m\exp\!\left[-\alpha\left(\frac{m\pi}{L}\right)^2t\right]\sin\frac{m\pi x}{L}
$$

Decay rate is proportional to $m^2$, so finer variations disappear faster.

## One-step amplification factors

Discretizing space by central differences with vertex spacing $h$ gives mode $m$ eigenvalue $-\mu_m$ of semidiscrete system $u'=-\mu_m u$.

$$
\mu_m=\frac{4\alpha}{h^2}\sin^2\frac{m\pi h}{2L}
$$

Setting $z=\mu_m\Delta t$, the amplification factor $G(z)$ multiplying modal amplitude in one step is as follows.

| Scheme | Temporal accuracy | $G(z)$ | Stability | Limit as $z\to\infty$ | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact semidiscrete solution | Exact | $e^{-z}$ | Decay | $0$ | $0<G\le1$ |
| Backward Euler | First order | $1/(1+z)$ | L-stable | $0$ | Monotone decay, but first-order temporal error |
| Crank–Nicolson | Second order | $(1-z/2)/(1+z/2)$ | A-stable | $-1$ | For $z>2$, $G<0$, leaving high-wavenumber components that alternate in sign |

Crank–Nicolson is A-stable, but its amplification factor for stiff modes ($z\gg1$) is close to $-1$.
Components that should rapidly disappear in the exact solution barely decay.
Smooth initial data contain only small amplitudes of these components, so they cause no problem; step initial data leave grid-scale oscillations.
See [Crank–Nicolson oscillations and Rannacher startup](oxref:pde-crank-nicolson-rannacher) for amplification-factor derivations.

Both cells use the following definitions of a difference plan for a rod held at $0$ at both ends and semidiscrete decay rate $\mu_m$.
`StructuredFdmPlan` uses thermal diffusivity $\alpha$ as the diffusion coefficient and lumped mass (length of the interval around each vertex, $h$ at interior vertices), so semidiscrete eigenvalues match $\mu_m$ above.

```rust
//| id: pde-heat-rod-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Difference plan for a rod held at 0 at both ends and semidiscrete decay rates"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryData, BoundaryKind, ConstantDiffusion, StructuredBoundaries, StructuredFdmPlan,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{AxisTopology, CoordinateAxis, Grid1d, SegmentMesh};

/// Plan with nodes equally spaced vertices on a 1 cm rod and thermal diffusivity α [cm²/s] as diffusion coefficient.
/// Temperature difference at both ends is 0 K (Dirichlet).
pub(crate) fn rod(
    nodes: usize,
    diffusivity: f64,
) -> Result<StructuredFdmPlan<f64, f64, 1>, SciOxideError> {
    let axis = CoordinateAxis::uniform(0.0, 1.0, nodes)?;
    let mesh = SegmentMesh::new(Grid1d::new([axis], [AxisTopology::Bounded])?)?;
    let ends =
        StructuredBoundaries::uniform(&mesh, 1, BoundaryKind::Dirichlet, BoundaryData::zero())?;
    StructuredFdmPlan::new(mesh, &ConstantDiffusion::new(diffusivity), &ends)
}

/// Semidiscrete decay rate of mode m: μ_m = (4α/h²) sin²(mπh/2) [1/s] (L = 1 cm).
/// Use f64 sin as a reference for checking.
pub(crate) fn semidiscrete_rate(mode: f64, h: f64, diffusivity: f64) -> f64 {
    4.0 * diffusivity / (h * h) * (std::f64::consts::PI * mode * h / 2.0).sin().powi(2)
}
```

## Temporal and spatial errors of a smooth mode

For $L=1$ cm and initial data $u(x,0)=\sin(\pi x)$ K, the exact continuous solution is $e^{-\alpha\pi^2t}\sin(\pi x)$ and exact semidiscrete solution is $e^{-\mu_1t}\sin(\pi x)$.
Their difference is spatial error, while the difference between numerical and exact semidiscrete solutions is temporal error, so the two can be measured separately.
Spatial error can be computed in closed form as $|e^{-\mu_1t}-e^{-\alpha\pi^2t}|$.

The next cell prepares the plan and `ParabolicProblem` holding the final time once.
`ParabolicProblem::new` prepares the semidiscrete system with Dirichlet unknowns eliminated, lumped mass, and Jacobian structure at this point.
Choose backward Euler or Crank–Nicolson with `ParabolicOptions::with_scheme` and supply fixed step $\Delta t$ through `with_step`.
Fixed-step methods require a step size; the last step is adjusted to reach the final time exactly.
Any remainder shortens the last step, which also absorbs rounding differences in accumulated step times.
Supply initial data through `FieldData`; `solve_with_options` returns the final field and report in `PdeSolution`.
The table shows both errors, step count, and factorization count (`num_time_steps` and `num_factorizations` of `PdeDiagnostics`).

With defaults ($\alpha=1$ cm²/s, 49 points, $\Delta t=0.002$ s, $t=0.05$ s), both methods take 25 steps and 2 factorizations.
Since the spatial operator is time-independent, the ODE integrator receives a constant Jacobian and reuses one factor for stages with identical step sizes.
$0.002$ is not exactly representable in binary, so the final step adjusted to the endpoint differs from the others at the rounding level, causing refactorization at that stage.
For exactly binary-representable $\Delta t=2^{-9}$ s and $t=2^{-4}$ s, 32 steps use 1 factorization.

Backward Euler maximum continuous-solution error is $3.05\times10^{-3}$ K, of which $2.94\times10^{-3}$ K is temporal error.
Crank–Nicolson temporal error is $9.8\times10^{-6}$ K; almost all maximum error $9.8\times10^{-5}$ K is spatial error $1.08\times10^{-4}$ K.
Setting the step to $\Delta t=0.00025$ s (1/8) gives backward Euler temporal error $3.7\times10^{-4}$ K (about 1/8) and Crank–Nicolson temporal error $1.5\times10^{-7}$ K (about 1/64), showing first- and second-order convergence respectively.
Crank–Nicolson maximum error is then nearly determined by spatial error; reduce it further by increasing vertex count.
For both methods, plotted error has the same shape as $\sin(\pi x)$ and vanishes at both ends.

## Step initial data and Rannacher startup

Solve top-hat initial data equal to $1$ K only in the central region $0.25<x<0.75$ cm and $0$ K elsewhere, with 65 grid points and $\alpha=1$ cm²/s.
Specify the step using Fourier number $r=\alpha\Delta t/h^2$.
For the stiffest mode, $z$ is about $4r$, so at $r>1/2$ some modes have negative Crank–Nicolson amplification factors.
`ParabolicOptions::with_rannacher_startup` replaces Crank–Nicolson steps $\Delta t$ in the specified number of initial intervals (supplied as `Budget`) with two backward Euler steps of size $\Delta t/2$.
`ParabolicOptions::startup` returns the setting as `RannacherStartup::BackwardEuler { intervals }`; the default is `RannacherStartup::Disabled`.
This startup is available only with Crank–Nicolson; attaching it to another method gives an `InvalidInput` error when solving begins.

The comparison reference is $u_j(t)=\sum_m c_me^{-\mu_mt}\sin(m\pi x_j)$, computed exactly for the same semidiscrete grid using a discrete sine transform.
Semidiscrete eigenvalues match $\mu_m$, so every difference from the numerical solution is time-integration error.
As an indicator of grid-scale oscillation, count sign changes of second differences $\Delta^2u_j=u_{j-1}-2u_j+u_{j+1}$ and compare with the count for the exact semidiscrete solution.

```rust
//| ref: pde-heat-rannacher
//| title: "Compare backward Euler, Crank–Nicolson, and Rannacher startup for step initial data"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [pde-heat-rod-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   heat_fourier_number: {"type":"range","label":"Fourier number r = αΔt/h² [-]","description":"Changes stiffness imposed on high-wavenumber modes in one step.","min":0.25,"max":8,"step":0.25,"value":4}
//|   heat_rannacher_intervals: {"type":"range","label":"Rannacher startup intervals [-]","description":"Changes the number of initial intervals replaced by backward Euler half-steps.","min":1,"max":4,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   heat_evolution_intervals: {"type":"range","label":"Time-interval count [-]","description":"Changes final time as a multiple of the step size.","min":4,"max":16,"step":1,"value":8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"stiff-step","label":"r = 8","description":"Crank–Nicolson error grows to 7.8×10⁻² K; Rannacher startup reduces it to 9.7×10⁻⁴ K.","inputs":{"heat_fourier_number":8}}
//|   - {"id":"gentle-step","label":"r = 0.25","description":"No modes have negative amplification factors; Crank–Nicolson error of 3.7×10⁻⁴ K is the smallest of the three methods.","inputs":{"heat_fourier_number":0.25}}
//|   - {"id":"short-startup","label":"1 startup interval","description":"With 1 Rannacher startup interval, 6 second-difference sign changes remain.","inputs":{"heat_rannacher_intervals":1}}

//|   - case: default

//|       - {"artifact":"profiles","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":9.2200351e-3,"rtol":1e-5}}

//|       - {"artifact":"profiles","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"profiles","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":10}
//|       - {"artifact":"profiles","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":2}

//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"profiles","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":3.6590344e-4,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"profiles","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":2}
//|   - case: short-startup
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"profiles","path":"/rows/2/4","equals":6}
use scioxide_core::Budget;
use scioxide_pde::{FieldData, FieldPoint, ParabolicOptions, ParabolicProblem, ParabolicScheme};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let n = 64_usize; // Interval count (65 vertices), α = 1 cm²/s
let h = 1.0 / n as f64;
let dt = heat_fourier_number * h * h;
let final_time = heat_evolution_intervals * dt;
let plan = rod(n + 1, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let source = FieldData::zero();
// Step equal to 1 K only in the central region 0.25 < x < 0.75 cm.
let top_hat = |x: f64| if (x - 0.5).abs() < 0.25 { 1.0 } else { 0.0 };
let initial = FieldData::time_invariant(move |p: FieldPoint<f64, 1>| Ok(top_hat(p.point[0])));
let problem = ParabolicProblem::new(&plan, &source, final_time).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: solve the semidiscrete system exactly using a discrete sine transform (f64 sin and exp as references).
let mut exact = vec![0.0; n + 1];
for m in 1..n {
    let c = (1..n)
        .map(|i| top_hat(i as f64 * h) * (PI * (m * i) as f64 / n as f64).sin())
        .sum::<f64>()
        * 2.0
        / n as f64;
    let decay = (-semidiscrete_rate(m as f64, h, 1.0) * final_time).exp();
    for (j, u) in exact.iter_mut().enumerate() {
        *u += c * decay * (PI * (m * j) as f64 / n as f64).sin();
    }
}
// Second-difference sign-change count and value range.
let flips = |u: &[f64]| {
    let curvature: Vec<f64> = u.windows(3).map(|w| w[0] - 2.0 * w[1] + w[2]).collect();
    curvature.windows(2).filter(|p| p[0] * p[1] < 0.0).count()
};
let range = |u: &[f64]| {
    u.iter()
        .fold((f64::INFINITY, f64::NEG_INFINITY), |(a, b), &v| {
            (a.min(v), b.max(v))
        })
};
let profile = |u: &[f64]| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    u.iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(j, v)| [j as f64 * h, *v])
        .collect()
};
let startup = Budget::try_from(heat_rannacher_intervals as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut rows, mut series) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for (label, scheme, rannacher) in [
    ("Backward Euler", ParabolicScheme::BackwardEuler, false),
    ("Crank–Nicolson", ParabolicScheme::CrankNicolson, false),
    (
        "Crank–Nicolson with Rannacher startup",
        ParabolicScheme::CrankNicolson,
        true,
    ),
] {
    let mut options = ParabolicOptions::new().with_step(dt).with_scheme(scheme);
    if rannacher {
        options = options.with_rannacher_startup(startup);
    }
    let solution = problem
        .solve_with_options(&initial, &options)
        .map_err(|e| e.error().to_string())?;
    let u = solution.values();
    let error = u
        .iter()
        .zip(&exact)
        .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
    let (lo, hi) = range(u);
    rows.push((label, lo, hi, Some(error), flips(u)));
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": label, "points": profile(u) }));
}
let (lo, hi) = range(&exact);
rows.push(("Exact semidiscrete solution", lo, hi, None, flips(&exact)));
series.push(serde_json::json!({
    "name": "Exact semidiscrete solution",
    "points": profile(&exact),
    "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
}));
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Position x [cm]",
        "yLabel": "Temperature difference u [K]",
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "range", "axis": "y", "from": 0.0, "to": 1.0, "label": "Maximum-principle range" }
        ]
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Minimum [K]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum [K]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum semidiscrete-solution difference [K]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "(reference)" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Δ²u sign changes [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "profiles" })
);
```

With defaults ($r=4$, 2 startup intervals, 8 time intervals), all three methods remain within $[0,1]$ K, with no maximum-principle violation for this setup.
Differences appear in comparison with the exact semidiscrete solution.
Backward Euler differs by $9.2\times10^{-3}$ K (first-order temporal error).
Crank–Nicolson differs by $2.2\times10^{-2}$ K, leaving grid-scale oscillations with 10 second-difference sign changes.
Adding Rannacher startup reduces the difference to $8.7\times10^{-4}$ K and gives 2 sign changes, matching the exact semidiscrete solution.
At $r=8$, Crank–Nicolson difference grows to $7.8\times10^{-2}$ K.
At $r=0.25$ no modes have negative amplification factors, so Crank–Nicolson difference $3.7\times10^{-4}$ K is the smallest of the three methods.
With 1 startup interval, some oscillation remains (6 sign changes).
The difference is then $5.5\times10^{-4}$ K, smaller than with 2 intervals, so maximum difference and presence of oscillation must be checked separately.

These results show that Crank–Nicolson's difficulty lies in undamped high-wavenumber components rather than the value range.
A sufficient condition for Crank–Nicolson to preserve the discrete maximum principle is $r\le1$ (backward Euler preserves it for every $r$).
At $r>1$ violations are possible but not inevitable; none occurred in this setup.
Rannacher startup replaces only initial intervals with backward Euler half-steps, damping high-wavenumber components of the step at the start.
Only a fixed number of steps are replaced, preserving second-order accuracy of subsequent Crank–Nicolson integration.

## Summary

For smooth initial data, Crank–Nicolson temporal error is more than two orders of magnitude smaller than backward Euler's; remaining error is spatial.
Spatial and temporal errors can be separated and measured in closed form using semidiscrete decay rate $\mu_m$.
For step initial data, Crank–Nicolson leaves high-wavenumber components undamped, appearing as differences from the exact semidiscrete solution and grid-scale oscillations.
Specifying startup intervals through `ParabolicOptions::with_rannacher_startup` damps these components over the first few intervals.
Check implicit-method factorization counts through `PdeDiagnostics::num_factorizations`; with time-independent operators, after the first factorization they increase only when step size changes.
