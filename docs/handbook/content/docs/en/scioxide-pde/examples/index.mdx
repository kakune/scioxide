The examples in this section derive equations from conservation laws over infinitesimal volumes for concrete physical and engineering problems and solve them with `scioxide-pde` plans.
Every example checks results against quantities independent of the numerical solution.
These include analytic solutions, closed-form semidiscrete solutions, conserved quantities and invariants, observed orders under grid refinement, and comparisons with other discretizations or methods.
No example uses algebraic residuals or termination reasons as evidence of accuracy.

## Working through the examples

The quantities to check depend on the equation type.
For elliptic problems, examine observed orders under refinement and boundary-flux balances; for parabolic problems, time-error orders, the maximum principle, and treatment of high-wavenumber components; for hyperbolic problems, phase lag, discrete energy, shock positions, and conservation balances.
Measure spatial orders by refining only the grid with a fixed small time step, and time orders by changing only the time step on a fixed grid.
When comparing FDM, FEM, and FVM solutions of the same problem, unknowns lie at different locations, so compare at common evaluation points or use location-independent quantities such as fluxes.

## Summary

Each example checks numerical results using analytic solutions, closed-form semidiscrete solutions, conserved quantities or invariants, refinement, or another method, as appropriate to the subject.
Use the “Independent check” column to choose a check applicable to your own problem.
No example treats a small residual on one grid or a `Completed` termination reason as evidence of accuracy.
