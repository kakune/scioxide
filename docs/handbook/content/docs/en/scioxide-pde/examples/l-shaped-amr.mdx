This example solves electrostatic potential in a domain with an L-shaped cross section.
The electric field diverges at the inner corner (reentrant corner), making the solution nonsmooth.
As a result, errors on uniformly refined meshes decrease only slowly with degree-of-freedom count.
Use error estimators and Dörfler marking to refine only near the corner, and measure recovery of the same rate as for smooth solutions.
See [Adaptive refinement based on error estimation](../../algorithms/adaptive-mesh-refinement/) for the mathematics of nodal spaces, estimators, and marking.

## Reentrant-corner singularity

Take length unit $a=1\ \mathrm{m}$ and the L-shaped domain $\Omega$ formed by removing the fourth-quadrant region $[0,1)\times(-1,0]$ from square $(-1,1)^2$.
The origin $(0,0)$ is a reentrant corner of interior angle $\omega=3\pi/2$.
In the charge-free domain, potential $\varphi$ [V] satisfies Laplace's equation $-\Delta\varphi=0$, with the following boundary potential ($V_0=1\ \mathrm{V}$).

$$
\varphi^*(r,\theta)=V_0\,r^{2/3}\sin\!\left(\frac{2\theta}{3}\right),\qquad 0\le\theta\le\frac{3\pi}{2}
$$

$(r,\theta)$ are polar coordinates centered at the origin; angle $\theta$ is measured counterclockwise from the positive $x$ axis.
On the two sides adjacent to the reentrant corner ($\theta=0$ and $\theta=3\pi/2$), $\varphi^*=0$.

This is the lowest-order separated solution $r^\lambda\sin(\lambda\theta)$ vanishing on both sides and bounded as $r\to0$.
The condition $\sin(\lambda\omega)=0$ for vanishing on both sides determines $\lambda=\pi/\omega=2/3$.
Since $\varphi^*$ is harmonic, it is the exact solution of this boundary-value problem.
Its gradient is

$$
\nabla\varphi^*=\frac{2V_0}{3}\,r^{-1/3}\bigl(-\sin(\theta/3),\ \cos(\theta/3)\bigr)
$$

The magnitude of electric field $E=-\nabla\varphi$ diverges like $r^{-1/3}$ at the reentrant corner.
$\varphi^*$ belongs to $H^1(\Omega)$, but belongs to $H^{1+s}$ only for $s<2/3$.

Measure P1 finite element error in the energy norm $\Vert\nabla(\varphi-\varphi_h)\Vert_{L^2(\Omega)}$ [V].
For a smooth solution, uniform-refinement error is $O(h)$, or $O(N^{-1/2})$ in terms of degree-of-freedom count $N\propto h^{-2}$.
This solution has regularity only up to $s=2/3$, reducing uniform-refinement error to $O(h^{2/3})=O(N^{-1/3})$.
Meshes graded toward the corner can recover the optimal P1 rate $O(N^{-1/2})$ (Babuška–Kellogg–Pitkäranta 1979).
Attainment of that rate by adaptive refinement with Dörfler marking is proved for newest-vertex bisection (Stevenson 2007).
This example uses regular four-way subdivision leaving hanging nodes, so verify the rate by measurement.

## Energy error through boundary integration

Since the gradient diverges at the origin, computing error $\Vert\nabla(\varphi-\varphi_h)\Vert^2$ by cellwise quadrature can give corner-cell quadrature error comparable to the error itself.
Instead, use harmonicity of $\varphi$ to convert volume integrals to boundary integrals.
Expanding $e=\varphi-\varphi_h$ gives

$$
\Vert\nabla e\Vert^2=\Vert\nabla\varphi\Vert^2-2\int_\Omega\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\varphi_h+\Vert\nabla\varphi_h\Vert^2
$$


Since $\Delta\varphi=0$, Green's formula gives $\int_\Omega\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\partial\Omega}v\,\partial_n\varphi\,ds$.
Here $v$ is either $\varphi$ or $\varphi_h$, both bounded.
Exclude a circle of radius $\epsilon$ around the origin and take $\epsilon\to0$; its contribution vanishes as $O(\epsilon^{2/3})$, so the formula remains valid despite the singularity.
On both sides adjoining the reentrant corner, $\varphi=0$; $\varphi_h$ is also zero as a linear interpolant of zero nodal values.
The remaining four outer sides are at least distance 1 from the origin, where $\partial_n\varphi$ is smooth.
Thus, writing the outer sides as $\Gamma$ gives

$$
\Vert\nabla e\Vert^2=\int_\Gamma(\varphi-2\varphi_h)\,\partial_n\varphi\,ds+\sum_K\vert\nabla\varphi_h\vert_K^2\,\vert K\vert
$$


P1 $\nabla\varphi_h$ is constant in each cell, so the final sum is computed exactly.
Use four-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature on each boundary face.
A four-point rule integrates polynomials through degree 7 exactly.
Since $\partial_n\varphi$ is smooth on outer sides, quadrature error in $\int_\Gamma\varphi\,\partial_n\varphi\,ds$ is $2\times10^{-6}$ when outer sides are split into faces of length 1, $5\times10^{-13}$ at length 1/4, and at rounding level at length 1/8.
The initial mesh containing length-1 faces has squared error $0.22$, so this quadrature error does not affect the three digits shown in the table.

Galerkin orthogonality $\int_\Omega\nabla e\cdot\nabla v_h=0$ holds only for $v_h$ vanishing on the boundary.
Since $\varphi_h$ is nonzero on the boundary, the formula $\Vert\nabla e\Vert^2=\Vert\nabla\varphi\Vert^2-\Vert\nabla\varphi_h\Vert^2$ used for homogeneous Dirichlet problems does not apply; compute the cross term above.

Check $\Vert\nabla\varphi\Vert^2$ independently against integration in polar coordinates.
Integrating $\vert\nabla\varphi\vert^2=\tfrac49V_0^2r^{-2/3}$ to distance $R(\theta)=1/\max(\vert\cos\theta\vert,\vert\sin\theta\vert)$ from the origin to the boundary gives

$$
\Vert\nabla\varphi\Vert^2=\frac{V_0^2}{3}\int_0^{3\pi/2}R(\theta)^{4/3}\,d\theta
$$


$R(\theta)$ is smooth on each interval of width $\pi/4$, so Gauss–Legendre quadrature on each interval gives rounding-level accuracy.

## Computation procedure

Each cycle proceeds as follows.

1. Construct a P1 space with constrained hanging nodes using `AmrSpace::new(mesh, AmrOrder::P1)`.
2. Label all exterior faces 0 and bind Dirichlet condition $\varphi=\varphi^*$ with `TaggedBoundaries::adaptive`.
3. Prepare `AmrDiffusionPlan` with $k=1$ and source 0, and solve the matrix from `assemble_csr` with Jacobi-preconditioned CG (absolute and relative tolerances both $10^{-12}$).
4. Extend the solution to all nodes through the constraints and compute $\eta_K$ with `AmrEstimator`.
5. Compute energy error with the boundary integral above and record effectivity index $\eta/\Vert\nabla e\Vert$.
6. Mark with `AmrMarking::new(θ)` and create a refined mesh (revision) through `AdaptiveMesh::adapt`.
7. Transfer the previous solution to the new space with `AmrNodalTransfer::interpolate_into` as the next CG initial guess.

The initial mesh consists of six triangles, obtained by splitting three unit squares along their diagonals.
Degree-of-freedom count $N$ is the number of independent nodes (including Dirichlet nodes).
The definitions block contains the mesh, exact solution, one solve, and error computation.

```rust
//| id: pde-lshape-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "L-shaped domain, exact solution r^(2/3) sin(2θ/3), one solve, and energy error"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-pde, scioxide-sparse, scioxide-spatial]
use scioxide_core::{
    Budget, ExecutionContext, SciOxideError, LinearOperator, NumericScalar, RealScalar,
    ResidualTolerance,
};
use scioxide_integrate::GaussianRulePlan;
use scioxide_linalg::{CgSolver, IterativeWorkspace, Preconditioning, Vector};
use scioxide_pde::{
    AmrBoundaryTags, AmrDiffusionData, AmrDiffusionPlan, AmrEstimator, AmrIndicators, AmrSpace,
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, BoundaryPoint, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_sparse::JacobiPreconditioner;
use scioxide_spatial::{AdaptationLimits, AdaptiveMesh, Cell, Field, Point, TriangleMesh};

/// Angle θ ∈ [0, 2π), measured counterclockwise from the positive x axis.
fn angle(p: [f64; 2]) -> f64 {
    let theta = RealScalar::atan2(p[1], p[0]);
    if theta < 0.0 {
        theta + 2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI
    } else {
        theta
    }
}

/// Exact solution φ* = r^(2/3) sin(2θ/3) [V] (V₀ = 1 V, a = 1 m).
pub(crate) fn potential(p: [f64; 2]) -> f64 {
    let r = RealScalar::hypot(p[0], p[1]);
    RealScalar::powf(r, 2.0 / 3.0) * RealScalar::sin(2.0 * angle(p) / 3.0)
}

/// ∇φ* = (2/3) r^(−1/3) (−sin(θ/3), cos(θ/3)) (use away from the origin).
fn gradient(p: [f64; 2]) -> [f64; 2] {
    let scale = 2.0 / 3.0 * RealScalar::powf(RealScalar::hypot(p[0], p[1]), -1.0 / 3.0);
    let third = angle(p) / 3.0;
    [
        -scale * RealScalar::sin(third),
        scale * RealScalar::cos(third),
    ]
}

/// Triangular mesh of L-shaped domain (−1, 1)² ∖ [0, 1) × (−1, 0], splitting squares of side 1/m diagonally.
pub(crate) fn l_shape(m: usize) -> Result<AdaptiveMesh<f64, 2>, String> {
    let n = 2 * m;
    let inside = |i: usize, j: usize| i < m || j >= m;
    let mut index = vec![usize::MAX; (n + 1) * (n + 1)];
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..=n {
        for i in 0..=n {
            // Include only vertices belonging to at least one retained square.
            let used = (i.saturating_sub(1)..=i.min(n - 1))
                .any(|a| (j.saturating_sub(1)..=j.min(n - 1)).any(|b| inside(a, b)));
            if used {
                index[j * (n + 1) + i] = points.len();
                let p = [i as f64 / m as f64 - 1.0, j as f64 / m as f64 - 1.0];
                points.push(Point::new(p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
            }
        }
    }
    let mut cells = Vec::new();
    for j in 0..n {
        for i in (0..n).filter(|&i| inside(i, j)) {
            let corner = |di: usize, dj: usize| index[(j + dj) * (n + 1) + i + di];
            cells.push([corner(0, 0), corner(1, 0), corner(1, 1)]);
            cells.push([corner(0, 0), corner(1, 1), corner(0, 1)]);
        }
    }
    let mesh = TriangleMesh::new(points, cells).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    AdaptiveMesh::from_simplex(&mesh, AdaptationLimits::new()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())
}

/// Independent check: ‖∇φ*‖² = (1/3) ∫₀^{3π/2} R(θ)^{4/3} dθ [V²] (R is the distance from origin to boundary).
/// R(θ) is smooth over each π/4 interval; integrate each with 20-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature.
pub(crate) fn polar_energy() -> Result<f64, String> {
    let rule = GaussianRulePlan::<f64>::gauss_legendre(20).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let quarter = core::f64::consts::FRAC_PI_4;
    let mut sum = 0.0;
    for piece in 0..6 {
        for (&t, &w) in rule.nodes().iter().zip(rule.weights()) {
            let theta = quarter * (piece as f64 + 0.5 * (t + 1.0));
            let (c, s) = (RealScalar::cos(theta), RealScalar::sin(theta));
            let distance = 1.0 / NumericScalar::abs(c).max(NumericScalar::abs(s));
            sum += 0.5 * quarter * w * RealScalar::powf(distance, 4.0 / 3.0) / 3.0;
        }
    }
    Ok(sum)
}

/// Charge-free domain: source 0.
struct Laplace;
impl AmrDiffusionData<f64, f64, 2> for Laplace {
    fn source(&self, _cell: usize, _point: [f64; 2]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(0.0)
    }
}

/// Outward unit normal of an outer side. None for the two sides adjacent to the reentrant corner (φ = φ_h = 0).
/// Vertex coordinates are binary fractions, so membership on a side can be tested with equality.
fn outer_normal(a: [f64; 2], b: [f64; 2]) -> Option<[f64; 2]> {
    for axis in 0..2 {
        for side in [-1.0, 1.0] {
            if a[axis] == side && b[axis] == side {
                let mut normal = [0.0; 2];
                normal[axis] = side;
                return Some(normal);
            }
        }
    }
    None
}

/// Solve in space and return a record, the solution at all nodes, and cell indicators. guess gives initial values at independent nodes.
pub(crate) fn solve(
    space: &AmrSpace<f64, 2>,
    guess: Vec<f64>,
) -> Result<(Cycle, Vec<f64>, AmrIndicators<f64, 2>), String> {
    let mesh = space.mesh();
    let exterior: Vec<(usize, u32)> = mesh
        .interfaces()
        .iter()
        .filter(|interface| interface.right.is_none())
        .map(|interface| (interface.left_face, 0))
        .collect();
    let tags = AmrBoundaryTags::new(mesh, &exterior).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let data = BoundaryData::time_invariant(|p: BoundaryPoint<f64, 2>| Ok(potential(p.point)));
    let conditions = vec![BoundaryCondition::dirichlet(0, data)];
    let boundaries =
        TaggedBoundaries::adaptive(mesh, &tags, 1, conditions).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let k = Field::<f64, Cell, 2>::new(mesh, vec![1.0; mesh.cell_count()])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let plan =
        AmrDiffusionPlan::new(space, k.view(), &boundaries, &Laplace).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let matrix = plan.assemble_csr().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let jacobi = JacobiPreconditioner::new(&matrix).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let rhs = Vector::from_vec(plan.rhs().to_vec());
    let mut x = Vector::from_vec(guess);
    let tolerance = ResidualTolerance::new(1e-12, 1e-12).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let solver = CgSolver::new(
        tolerance,
        Budget::try_from(5000).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    );
    let report = solver
        .solve_into(
            &matrix,
            &rhs,
            Preconditioning::Inverse(&jacobi),
            x.view_mut(),
            &mut IterativeWorkspace::new(),
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut full = vec![0.0; space.full_dofs()];
    let mut scratch = full.clone();
    space
        .constraints()
        .apply_into(x.as_slice(), &mut full, &mut scratch)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut indicators = AmrIndicators::new(mesh).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    {
        let residual = plan.residual(&full).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let estimator = AmrEstimator::new(space, k.view()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let mut workspace = estimator.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        estimator
            .estimate_into(
                &residual,
                &mut indicators,
                &mut workspace,
                ExecutionContext::sequential(),
            )
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }

    // ‖∇φ_h‖²: P1 gradients are constant per cell, so evaluate at the centroid and multiply by area.
    let mut discrete = 0.0;
    for cell in 0..mesh.cell_count() {
        let (_, g) = space
            .evaluate(cell, [1.0 / 3.0, 1.0 / 3.0], &full)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        discrete +=
            (g[0] * g[0] + g[1] * g[1]) * mesh.cell_measure(cell).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
    // ∫ φ ∂ₙφ and ∫ φ_h ∂ₙφ on outer sides Γ (four-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature per face).
    let rule = GaussianRulePlan::<f64>::gauss_legendre(4).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (mut exact_energy, mut cross) = (0.0, 0.0);
    for interface in mesh.interfaces().iter().filter(|i| i.right.is_none()) {
        let ends: Vec<usize> = mesh
            .face_vertices(interface.piece_face)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            .collect();
        let a = *mesh.point(ends[0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.as_array();
        let b = *mesh.point(ends[1]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.as_array();
        let Some(normal) = outer_normal(a, b) else {
            continue;
        };
        let length = RealScalar::hypot(b[0] - a[0], b[1] - a[1]);
        for (&t, &w) in rule.nodes().iter().zip(rule.weights()) {
            let s = 0.5 * (t + 1.0);
            let p = [a[0] + s * (b[0] - a[0]), a[1] + s * (b[1] - a[1])];
            let g = gradient(p);
            let flux = g[0] * normal[0] + g[1] * normal[1];
            let discrete_value = full[ends[0]] + s * (full[ends[1]] - full[ends[0]]);
            exact_energy += 0.5 * w * length * potential(p) * flux;
            cross += 0.5 * w * length * discrete_value * flux;
        }
    }
    let squared_error = exact_energy - 2.0 * cross + discrete;
    let cycle = Cycle {
        dofs: space.independent_dofs(),
        cells: mesh.cell_count(),
        error: RealScalar::sqrt(squared_error.max(0.0)),
        estimate: indicators.global(),
        exact_energy,
        iterations: report.iterations(),
        status: format!("{:?}", report.termination()),
        converged: report.termination().is_success(),
    };
    Ok((cycle, full, indicators))
}
```

`solve` performs one assembly-through-estimation computation in the supplied space, returning cycle record `Cycle`, the solution at all nodes, and cell indicators.
`exact_energy` of `Cycle` is $\Vert\nabla\varphi\Vert^2$ from boundary integration; the last cell compares it with polar-coordinate integral `polar_energy`.
`outer_normal` uses vertex coordinates to determine which of the four outer sides a boundary face lies on.
Vertex coordinates are binary fractions, so rounding does not affect this test.

## Adaptive refinement cycle

The next cell repeatedly refines the initial mesh with Dörfler marking.
Inputs are fraction $\theta$ and degree-of-freedom limit $N_{\max}$; stop at the cycle where $N$ reaches or exceeds $N_{\max}$.
The table shows $N$, active-cell count, energy error, estimator $\eta$, effectivity index, CG iterations, and termination reason for each cycle.
Finally show whether CG converged in every cycle.
The computation takes time, so run by button and reuse results for identical inputs.

```rust
    let (cycle, full, indicators) = solve(&space, guess)?;
    let stop = !cycle.converged || cycle.dofs >= limit;
    adaptive.push(cycle);
    if stop {
        marked.push(None);
        break;
    }

        .mark(space.mesh(), &indicators)

        .adapt(marks.request())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

        let mut workspace = transfer.workspace::<f64>().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

                &full,
                &mut moved,
                &mut workspace,
                ExecutionContext::sequential(),
            )
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
    guess = refined
        .independent_nodes()
        .iter()
        .map(|&node| moved[node])
        .collect();

} else {
    Outcome::partial("Stopped the cycle at a solve where CG failed to converge")
        .explanation("The last row is unconverged; treat its error and estimator as estimates.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(last.status.clone())
        .diagnostic("Cycle count", adaptive.len() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Final degrees of freedom N", last.dofs as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Final energy error", last.error, Some("V"))
);
```

With defaults $\theta=0.5$ and $N_{\max}=1000$, 12 cycles reach $N=1206$ and 2532 active cells, with energy error $2.76\times10^{-2}$ V.
This is about 1/17 of the initial-mesh ($N=8$) error $0.466$ V.
Every cycle ends CG with `Converged`, giving outcome satisfied.
Effectivity indices range from 2.47 to 3.98; the estimator takes values 2.5–4 times the error while decreasing at nearly the same rate.

With $\theta=0.2$, fewer cells are refined each time, increasing cycles needed to reach $N\ge1000$ to 24.
The first refinement leaves $N$ at 8, with unchanged error.
All three edges of the marked cell are shared with neighbors, so every new edge midpoint is a hanging node and the P1 space stays unchanged.
At $\theta=1$ every cell is marked, and the $N=8,21,65,225,833,3201$ sequence and errors match the uniform-refinement results below.

## Comparison with uniform refinement

The next cell solves the same problem on uniform meshes, doubling the initial squares' subdivision counts through $m=1,2,4,\dots$.
Input is the finest subdivision count $m_{\max}$.
Uniform meshes have no hanging nodes; estimator and error computations use the same functions as the preceding cell.

```rust
//| run: button

//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/4/1","equals":833}

emit_outcome!(
```

## Convergence rates and effectivity indices

The next cell receives results from the preceding two cells and plots energy error and estimator against $N$ on log-log axes.
Dashed reference lines have slopes $-1/3$ and $-1/2$, passing through the last uniform- and adaptive-refinement points respectively.
The table shows least-squares slopes fitted to the last three points (observed rates) and effectivity-index ranges.
The second table compares boundary and polar-coordinate integrals for $\Vert\nabla\varphi\Vert^2$; the plot shows vertex locations on the final adaptive mesh.

```rust
//| id: pde-lshape-rates
//| ref: pde-lshape-rates
//| title: "Convergence rates, effectivity indices, and final meshes for uniform and adaptive refinement"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [pde-lshape-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-integrate, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-pde, scioxide-sparse, scioxide-spatial]
//| dependsOn:
//|   adaptive_run: {cell: pde-lshape-adaptive, type: AdaptiveRun}

//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-0.349,"atol":2e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":-0.519,"atol":2e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":-1e-11,"max":1e-11}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

// Least-squares line slope through the last three (log N, log ‖∇e‖) points (observed rate).
let slope = |cycles: &[Cycle]| -> f64 {
    let tail = &cycles[cycles.len() - 3..];
    let xs: Vec<f64> = tail.iter().map(|c| RealScalar::ln(c.dofs as f64)).collect();
    let ys: Vec<f64> = tail.iter().map(|c| RealScalar::ln(c.error)).collect();
    let (mx, my) = (xs.iter().sum::<f64>() / 3.0, ys.iter().sum::<f64>() / 3.0);
    let sxy: f64 = xs.iter().zip(&ys).map(|(x, y)| (x - mx) * (y - my)).sum();
    let sxx: f64 = xs.iter().map(|x| (x - mx) * (x - mx)).sum();
    sxy / sxx
};
let points = |cycles: &[Cycle], estimate: bool| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    let value = |c: &Cycle| if estimate { c.estimate } else { c.error };
    cycles.iter().map(|c| [c.dofs as f64, value(c)]).collect()
};
// Place reference line C N^p through each series' last point.
let reference = |cycles: &[Cycle], power: f64| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    let last = &cycles[cycles.len() - 1];
    let scale = last.error / RealScalar::powf(last.dofs as f64, power);
    [8.0, 2.0e4]
        .iter()
        .map(|&n| [n, scale * RealScalar::powf(n, power)])
        .collect()
};

let adaptive_estimate = points(adaptive, true);
let (third, half) = (reference(uniform, -1.0 / 3.0), reference(adaptive, -0.5));
let circle = serde_json::json!({ "symbol": "circle" });
let diamond = serde_json::json!({ "symbol": "diamond" });
let hollow = serde_json::json!({ "symbol": "emptyCircle" });
let dashed = serde_json::json!({ "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false });
let series = vec![
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Uniform ‖∇e‖", "points": uniform_error, "style": circle }),
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Adaptive ‖∇e‖", "points": adaptive_error, "style": diamond }),
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Adaptive η", "points": adaptive_estimate, "style": hollow }),
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Slope −1/3", "points": third, "style": dashed }),

let effectivity = |cycles: &[Cycle]| {

    let low = ratios.clone().fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
    (low, ratios.fold(0.0, f64::max))
};
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
```

This agreement checks the first term of the error formula and the orientation of outward normals.
The final mesh's vertex plot shows clustering near the origin and increasing sparsity farther away.

$\varphi$ is smooth near the outer boundary, and uniform-mesh errors concentrate near the reentrant corner.
The estimator captures this distribution from flux jumps, and Dörfler marking refines only high-error corner cells, reducing error at the same degree-of-freedom count.

## Summary

Domains with reentrant corners reduce solution regularity, so uniform P1 refinement reduces error only as $N^{-1/3}$.
Refining near the corner with residual estimators and Dörfler marking restores rate $N^{-1/2}$ in this example, reducing error by about 2.2 times at $N\approx800$.
Estimator values are 2.5–4 times the error and are not error bounds, but decrease at nearly the same rate.
For singular problems, cellwise quadrature can give large corner-cell errors; avoid the singularity when computing error, for example through harmonicity-based boundary integration as here.
The caller constructs the refinement cycle, stopping conditions, and transfer of previous solutions, so the same procedure applies to your own problems.
