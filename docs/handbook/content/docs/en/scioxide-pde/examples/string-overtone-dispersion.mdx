An ideal string's overtone frequencies are integer multiples of its fundamental, a relation shaping string-instrument timbre.
Spatial discretization shifts higher overtone frequencies more strongly, breaking this integer relation.
[Reading waves on a stretched string through phase and pitch](../fixed-string-wave/) confirmed that time-integration error is small and numerical solutions follow the semidiscrete solution.
Thus grid-induced overtone shifts are determined solely by semidiscrete natural frequencies.

This page computes those frequencies from generalized eigenvalues of P1 stiffness and mass matrices, expressing pitch shifts in cents.
Compare closed forms with how mass construction (lumped, consistent, or their average) changes shift direction and magnitude.

## Semidiscrete natural frequencies

Divide a string of length $L$ [m] and wave speed $c$ [m/s] into $J$ equal elements ($h = L/J$).
P1 Galerkin elements give the semidiscrete system for interior nodal values $\mathbf u(t)$:

$$
M\ddot{\mathbf u} + K\mathbf u = 0
$$


$K$ is stiffness, with interior rows $(c^2/h)(-1, 2, -1)$.
$M$ is mass; consistent mass obtained by integrating basis-function products has rows $(h/6)(1, 4, 1)$.
Placing each row sum on the diagonal gives lumped mass with diagonal $h$, making the semidiscrete system identical to three-point differences.

Substituting $\mathbf u(t) = \hat{\mathbf u}\cos(\omega t)$ gives generalized eigenvalue problem $K\hat{\mathbf u} = \omega^2 M\hat{\mathbf u}$.
Discrete sine $\hat u_j = \sin(m\pi x_j/L)$ is an eigenvector for both masses; setting $\theta_m = m\pi h/(2L) = m\pi/(2J)$ gives

$$
\frac{\omega_{h,m}}{\omega_m} =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sin\theta_m}{\theta_m} & \text{lumped mass} \\[2ex]
\dfrac{\sin\theta_m}{\theta_m}\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{3 - 2\sin^2\theta_m}} & \text{consistent mass} \\[2ex]
\dfrac{\sin\theta_m}{\theta_m}\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{3 - \sin^2\theta_m}} & \text{averaged mass } (M_{\mathrm L} + M_{\mathrm C})/2
\end{cases}
$$


$\omega_m = m\pi c/L$ is continuous angular frequency.
For small $\theta_m$, the lumped ratio approaches $1 - \theta_m^2/6$ and the consistent ratio approaches $1 + \theta_m^2/6$.
Lumped mass shifts overtones down and consistent mass up; averaging cancels their $\theta_m^2$ terms.
Averaged-mass error is $O(\theta_m^4)$.

In $\theta_m = \pi/n_\lambda$, $n_\lambda = 2J/m$ is elements per wavelength.
Express pitch difference in cents (1200 per octave, 100 per semitone) as

$$
\Delta C_m = 1200\log_2\frac{\omega_{h,m}}{\omega_m}
$$


For lumped mass it is $\Delta C_m \approx -1200\,\theta_m^2/(6\ln 2) \approx -2847/n_\lambda^2$ cents.

## Extracting stiffness and mass matrices

`SimplexFemPlan` constructs the following three quantities from element matrices.

- `assemble_csr()`: CSR stiffness with Dirichlet nodal rows and columns replaced by the identity.
- `assemble_mass_csr()`: CSR consistent mass without boundary elimination.
- `lumped_mass()`: positive lumped mass at each node.
  For P1 it equals consistent-mass row sums; for P2 it uses HRZ diagonal scaling.

Boundary nodal values are fixed to zero, so extract only free-node rows and columns where `constrained()` is false to form $K_{ff}$ and $M_{ff}$.
Retaining identity-replaced rows in the generalized eigenproblem creates artificial boundary-row eigenvalues.
Compute eigenvalues with scioxide-linalg's `GeneralizedHermitianEigenvaluesResult::compute`.
This function uses Cholesky factorization of $M$ to reduce to standard form $L^{-1}KL^{-\top}$, returning every eigenvalue in ascending order.
For $n$ degrees of freedom, cost is $O(n^3)$ ([Generalized eigenvalue problems](../../../scioxide-linalg/algorithms/generalized-eigenproblems/)).

## Overtone pitch shifts

The next cell divides a string of length $L = 0.65$ m and $c = 143$ m/s (fundamental 110 Hz) into $J$ P1 elements, computing frequencies of harmonics 1–32 with three mass matrices.
Inputs are element count $J$ and harmonic index $m$ to tabulate.
Outputs are pitch shifts against harmonic index, a table of selected-harmonic frequencies and shifts, and a table of relative discrepancies against closed forms.
Closed forms provide an independent check: rounding-level agreement with generalized eigenvalues confirms assembly of the discretization above.

```rust
//| id: pde-string-overtone-dispersion
//| ref: pde-string-overtone-dispersion
//| title: "Compute overtone pitch shifts for each mass matrix"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   string_grid_intervals: {"type":"range","label":"Element count J [-]","description":"Changes elements per wavelength 2J/m.","min":40,"max":160,"step":8,"value":64}
//|   string_selected_overtone: {"type":"range","label":"Harmonic to tabulate m [-]","description":"Selects the table row and vertical line position in the plot.","min":1,"max":32,"step":1,"value":12}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fine-grid","label":"J = 160","description":"Harmonic 12 has 26.7 elements per wavelength, reducing the lumped pitch shift to −4.0 cents.","inputs":{"string_grid_intervals":160}}
//|   - {"id":"highest-overtone","label":"Harmonic 32","description":"At 4 elements per wavelength, lumped mass shifts by −182 cents and even averaged mass by several cents.","inputs":{"string_selected_overtone":32}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"selected","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":10.666667,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"selected","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":-25.101965,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|   - case: fine-grid
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"selected","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":-4.0065251,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: highest-overtone
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"selected","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":-181.79536,"rtol":1e-6}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_linalg::{GeneralizedHermitianEigenvaluesResult, HermitianInput, Matrix};
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryData, BoundaryKind, ConstantDiffusion, SimplexFemGeometry, SimplexFemOrder,
    SimplexFemPlan, SpatialDiscretization, TaggedBoundaries,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{IntervalMesh, Point};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

// Divide a string of length L = 0.65 m and c = 143 m/s (fundamental 110 Hz) into J P1 elements.
let (length, c) = (0.65, 143.0);
let (intervals, selected) = (
    string_grid_intervals as usize,
    string_selected_overtone as usize,
);
let h = length / intervals as f64;
let points = (0..=intervals)
    .map(|i| Point::new([i as f64 * h]))
    .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let cells = (0..intervals).map(|i| [i, i + 1]).collect();
let mesh = IntervalMesh::new(points, cells).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fixed = TaggedBoundaries::<f64, f64, 1>::uniform(
    &mesh,
    1,
    BoundaryKind::Dirichlet,
    BoundaryData::zero(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Passing c² as diffusion makes stiffness c²∫u'v'.
let plan = SimplexFemPlan::new(
    mesh,
    SimplexFemGeometry::Affine,
    SimplexFemOrder::P1,
    &ConstantDiffusion::new(c * c),
    &fixed,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let stiffness = plan.assemble_csr().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mass = plan.assemble_mass_csr().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Reindex free nodes and construct dense K_ff, consistent, lumped, and averaged mass matrices.
let free: Vec<usize> = (0..plan.unknowns())
    .filter(|&i| !plan.constrained()[i])
    .collect();
let n = free.len();
let position = |i: usize| free.iter().position(|&f| f == i);
let (mut k, mut consistent) = (vec![0.0; n * n], vec![0.0; n * n]);
for (target, matrix) in [(&mut k, &stiffness), (&mut consistent, &mass)] {
    for (row, col, value) in matrix.iter() {
        if let (Some(i), Some(j)) = (position(row), position(col)) {
            target[i * n + j] = value;
        }
    }
}
let mut lumped = vec![0.0; n * n];
for (i, &node) in free.iter().enumerate() {
    lumped[i * n + i] = plan.lumped_mass()[node];
}
let average: Vec<f64> = consistent
    .iter()
    .zip(&lumped)
    .map(|(a, b)| 0.5 * (a + b))
    .collect();
let stiffness = Matrix::from_vec(n, n, k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut frequencies = Vec::new();
for m in [lumped, consistent, average] {
    let m = Matrix::from_vec(n, n, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let exact = HermitianInput::Exact;
    let values = GeneralizedHermitianEigenvaluesResult::compute(&stiffness, &m, exact, exact)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Frequencies [Hz] in ascending order: square root of eigenvalue ω² divided by 2π.
    let hertz: Vec<f64> = values.eigenvalues()[..32]
        .iter()
        .map(|&w2| RealScalar::sqrt(w2) / (2.0 * PI))
        .collect();
    frequencies.push(hertz);
}
let names = ["Lumped mass", "Consistent mass", "Averaged mass"];
// Closed-form ratio ω_h/ω (independent check, θ = mπ/(2J)).
let ratio = |method: usize, m: usize| {
    let theta = m as f64 * PI / (2.0 * intervals as f64);
    let s2 = RealScalar::powi(RealScalar::sin(theta), 2);
    let base = RealScalar::sin(theta) / theta;
    match method {
        0 => base,
        1 => base * RealScalar::sqrt(3.0 / (3.0 - 2.0 * s2)),
        _ => base * RealScalar::sqrt(3.0 / (3.0 - s2)),
    }
};
let fundamental = c / (2.0 * length);
let cents = |f: f64, m: usize| 1200.0 * RealScalar::log2(f / (m as f64 * fundamental));
let mut series = Vec::new();
let mut check = Vec::new();
for (method, (name, hertz)) in names.iter().zip(&frequencies).enumerate() {
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (1..=32)
        .map(|m| [m as f64, cents(hertz[m - 1], m)])
        .collect();
    let deviation = (1..=32)
        .map(|m| (hertz[m - 1] / (m as f64 * fundamental * ratio(method, m)) - 1.0).abs())
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
    series.push(
        serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "style": { "symbol": "circle" }, "points": points }),
    );
    check.push((name.to_string(), deviation));
}
let mark = serde_json::json!([
    { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": selected as f64, "label": "Tabulated harmonic" },
    { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.0, "label": "Integer-multiple pitch" }
]);
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Harmonic index m [-]",
        "yLabel": "Pitch difference ΔC [cent]",
        "annotations": mark
    })
);
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Harmonic m [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Elements per wavelength [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Integer-multiple frequency [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Lumped mass [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Lumped mass [cent]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Consistent mass [cent]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Averaged mass [cent]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
let row = (
    selected,
    2.0 * intervals as f64 / selected as f64,
    selected as f64 * fundamental,
    frequencies[0][selected - 1],
    cents(frequencies[0][selected - 1], selected),
    cents(frequencies[1][selected - 1], selected),
    cents(frequencies[2][selected - 1], selected),
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "selected" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Mass matrix" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum relative closed-form discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    check,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "check" })
);
```

With default $J = 64$, harmonic 12 (10.67 elements per wavelength) has lumped-mass frequency 1301.00 Hz, 25.1 cents below the integer multiple 1320 Hz.
Consistent mass shifts the same harmonic 24.9 cents up, while averaged mass limits the shift to $-0.44$ cents.
All three mass matrices give relative discrepancies no greater than $1.2 \times 10^{-13}$ between generalized-eigenvalue frequencies and closed forms, agreeing to rounding error.

All three plotted series move farther from zero as harmonic index rises.
Lumped mass moves down and consistent mass up almost symmetrically, while averaged mass stays near zero through high harmonics.
At $J = 160$, harmonic-12 lumped shift (26.7 elements per wavelength) shrinks to $-4.0$ cents, with ratio $25.1/4.0 = 6.3 \approx (160/64)^2$ showing second-order convergence.
Averaged-mass shift shrinks about 40-fold from $-0.44$ to $-0.011$ cents, showing fourth-order convergence near $(160/64)^4 = 39$.
At harmonic 32 (4 elements per wavelength), shifts are $-182$ cents for lumped mass, $+169$ for consistent mass, and even $-24$ for averaged mass.

## Grid-resolution guidance

Lumped-mass approximation $\Delta C \approx -2847/n_\lambda^2$ requires $n_\lambda \ge \sqrt{2847/|\Delta C|}$ elements per wavelength to keep pitch error no greater than $|\Delta C|$ cents.
For 5 cents use 24 elements; for 1 cent use 54.
Place this many elements within one wavelength of the highest harmonic of interest.

`HyperbolicProblem` constructs semidiscrete systems with lumped mass, so [fixed-end string mode tracking](oxref:pde-string-mode-comparison) shifts overtones down as in the lumped column.
`HyperbolicProblem` and method-of-lines plans (`MethodOfLinesPlan`) handle only diagonal mass, with no time-integration option for consistent or averaged mass systems.

## Summary

Compute semidiscrete overtone frequencies from generalized eigenvalues of P1 stiffness and mass matrices and check them with closed forms.
Lumped mass shifts overtones down and consistent mass up, with shifts inversely proportional to squared elements per wavelength.
Averaging the two masses cancels $\theta^2$ terms, reducing shifts at fourth order.
To keep pitch within $|\Delta C|$ cents, place at least $\sqrt{2847/|\Delta C|}$ lumped-mass elements in one wavelength of the highest harmonic.
