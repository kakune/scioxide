When two reacting species diffuse at different rates, a uniform state stable without diffusion can become unstable to spatial perturbations of certain wavelengths.
This is diffusion-driven instability (Turing instability); growing perturbations form stripe or spot patterns (Turing patterns).
This page uses dispersion relations to determine growth rates for each perturbation wavenumber in the Schnakenberg system on a square and counts modes actually present on a finite grid.
See [Two-dimensional Turing pattern formation](../turing-pattern-formation/) for comparison with two-dimensional time evolution.

Match [Turing bifurcation in reaction–diffusion systems](../../../applications/reaction-diffusion/) with feed rates $a=0.1$ and $b=0.9$ and diffusion coefficients $D_u=1$ and $D_v=d$.
The application page finds critical diffusion ratios by root finding and measures single-mode growth rates.
This page covers dispersion on a two-dimensional square and discrete modes of a difference plan.
All quantities are dimensionless.

## Schnakenberg system and zero-flux boundaries

On square $\Omega=[0,L]^2$, activator concentration $u(x,y,t)$ and inhibitor concentration $v(x,y,t)$ obey the following reaction–diffusion system.

$$
\partial_t u = D_u \Delta u + f(u,v), \qquad \partial_t v = D_v \Delta v + g(u,v), \qquad f = a - u + u^2 v, \quad g = b - u^2 v
$$

Impose zero outward flux for both components ($\mathbf{n}\cdot D_u\nabla u=\mathbf{n}\cdot D_v\nabla v=0$; $\mathbf{n}$ is the outward unit normal).
No material crosses the boundary; only reaction changes totals.

The uniform steady state where reaction vanishes is $u^*=a+b=1$ and $v^*=b/(a+b)^2=0.9$, with the following Jacobian.

$$
\mathbf{J}_0=\begin{pmatrix} f_u & f_v \\ g_u & g_v \end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & 1 \\ -1.8 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \operatorname{tr}\mathbf{J}_0=-0.2, \quad \det\mathbf{J}_0=1
$$

Negative trace and positive determinant make this state asymptotically stable without diffusion.
$f_u>0$ represents autocatalysis of $u$, and $g_v<0$ consumption of $v$.

## Dispersion relation

Substitute the following cosine mode satisfying zero flux into the linearized equation for deviation $(\delta u,\delta v)$ from the uniform steady state:

$$
(\delta u,\delta v) = \mathbf{w}\, e^{\lambda t}\cos(k_x x)\cos(k_y y), \qquad k_x=\frac{m_x\pi}{L},\quad k_y=\frac{m_y\pi}{L},\quad m_x,m_y=0,1,2,\dots
$$

Growth rate $\lambda$ then becomes an eigenvalue of the $2\times2$ matrix $\mathbf{J}_k=\mathbf{J}_0-k^2\operatorname{diag}(D_u,D_v)$.
Here $\mathbf{w}$ is the two-component amplitude and $k^2=k_x^2+k_y^2$; the growth rate depends only on wavenumber magnitude $k$, not direction.
Write the larger of the two eigenvalues' real parts as $\operatorname{Re}\lambda(k)$ and call this the dispersion relation.

$$
\operatorname{Re}\lambda(k)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\tau+\operatorname{Re}\sqrt{\tau^2-4h}\right), \qquad \tau=\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{J}_0-(D_u+D_v)k^2, \quad h=D_uD_vk^4-(D_vf_u+D_ug_v)k^2+\det\mathbf{J}_0
$$

Since $\tau=\operatorname{tr}\mathbf{J}_k$ is negative for every $k$, $\operatorname{Re}\lambda(k)>0$ occurs only when $h=\det\mathbf{J}_k<0$.
Call the wavenumber range where $h<0$ the unstable band; the wavenumber maximizing $\operatorname{Re}\lambda$ within it is fastest-growing wavenumber $k_{\max}$.
Because $\tau$ also varies with $k$, $k_{\max}$ differs from the wavenumber satisfying $k^2=(D_vf_u+D_ug_v)/(2D_uD_v)$ that minimizes $h$.

## Critical diffusion ratio and wavenumber

The diffusion ratio making the minimum of $h$ exactly zero is critical diffusion ratio $d_c$; the wavenumber where $h=0$ then holds is critical wavenumber $k_c$.
For $D_u=1$, both have the following closed forms.

$$
d_c=\left(\frac{\sqrt{\det\mathbf{J}_0}+\sqrt{\det\mathbf{J}_0-f_ug_v}}{f_u}\right)^2\approx 8.5676, \qquad k_c^2=\sqrt{\frac{\det\mathbf{J}_0}{d_c}}\approx 0.3416,\quad k_c\approx 0.5845
$$

See [Turing bifurcation in reaction–diffusion systems](../../../applications/reaction-diffusion/) for derivation and computation of $d_c$ by root finding against these forms.

$k_c$ is the wavenumber first becoming unstable at $d=d_c$, distinct from fastest-growing wavenumber $k_{\max}$ at $d>d_c$.
At $d=20$, the unstable band opens over $k\in(0.2720,\,0.8222)$ ($k^2\in(0.0740,\,0.6760)$), with $\operatorname{Re}\lambda\approx0.2632$ at $k_{\max}\approx0.4867$.
Here $k_{\max}<k_c$.
At $d<d_c$, $\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0$ for every $k$ and all modes decay.

## Discrete Neumann modes

A finite square allows only discrete wavenumbers $k=\pi\sqrt{m_x^2+m_y^2}/L$.
If the domain is small, no mode enters the unstable band and no pattern forms even for $d>d_c$.

`StructuredFdmPlan` uses conservative differences with values at vertices.
Each vertex's lumped mass is its dual-control-volume area, half the interior value at boundary vertices and one quarter at corners.
With these weights on a uniform grid of spacing $h$, boundary rows of $M^{-1}A$ ($A$ is diffusion, $M$ the diagonal lumped-mass matrix) equal central differences with reflected ghost points.
Thus vertex cosine $\cos(m_x\pi x_i/L)\cos(m_y\pi y_j/L)$ ($m_x,m_y=0,\dots,L/h$) is an eigenvector of $M^{-1}A$, with eigenvalue $k_h^2$ below for diffusion coefficient 1.

$$
k_h^2=\frac{4}{h^2}\left(\sin^2\frac{m_x\pi h}{2L}+\sin^2\frac{m_y\pi h}{2L}\right)
$$

Linearized semidiscrete growth rates are $\operatorname{Re}\lambda_h=\operatorname{Re}\lambda(k_h)$, replacing $k$ in the dispersion relation by discrete wavenumber $k_h$.
Since $k_h<k$, discrete growth rates plotted at nominal wavenumber $k$ read the continuous curve at $k_h$ left of $k$; the offset $k-k_h$ increases with wavenumber.
These cosines form the DCT-I (type-I discrete cosine transform) basis, so a DCT-I of a vertex field yields mode amplitudes.
[Two-dimensional Turing pattern formation](../turing-pattern-formation/) uses this to find dominant pattern modes.

The following definitions block shares diffusion coefficients, a two-component difference plan, and continuous/discrete dispersion relations between this page's two cells.
Fix grid spacing at $h=1$, with $L+1$ vertices per side.
`StructuredFdmCoefficients::diffusion` returns diffusion coefficients by axis and component: component 0 is $u$, component 1 is $v$.
`BoundaryCondition::natural` prescribes outward conormal flux $\mathbf{n}\cdot D\nabla u$ and applies to both components when none is specified.
```rust
//| id: pde-turing-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Diffusion coefficients, two-component difference plan, and continuous/discrete dispersion relations"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_pde::{
    BoundaryCondition, BoundaryData, StructuredBoundaries, StructuredFdmCoefficients,
    StructuredFdmPlan,
};
use scioxide_spatial::{AxisSide, AxisTopology, BoundarySet, CoordinateAxis, Grid2d, QuadMesh};

/// Jacobian J₀ = [[f_u, f_v], [g_u, g_v]] at (u*, v*).
pub(crate) const JACOBIAN: [[f64; 2]; 2] = [[0.8, 1.0], [-1.8, -1.0]];

/// Diffusion coefficients D_u = 1, D_v = d. Component 0 is u and component 1 v, identical on both axes.
pub(crate) struct Diffusion {
    pub(crate) ratio: f64,
}
impl StructuredFdmCoefficients<f64, f64, 2> for Diffusion {
    fn diffusion(&self, _: usize, component: usize, _: [f64; 2]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
        Ok([1.0, self.ratio][component])
    }
}

/// Vertex grid on [0, L]² with h = 1 and zero outward flux on all four sides for both components.
pub(crate) fn turing_plan(
    length: usize,
    ratio: f64,
) -> Result<StructuredFdmPlan<f64, f64, 2>, SciOxideError> {
    let axis = CoordinateAxis::uniform(0.0, length as f64, length + 1)?;
    let grid = Grid2d::new([axis.clone(), axis], [AxisTopology::Bounded; 2])?;
    let mesh = QuadMesh::new(grid)?;
    let mut conditions = Vec::new();
    for axis in 0..2 {
        for side in [AxisSide::Start, AxisSide::End] {
            let region = BoundarySet::axis_side(&mesh, axis, side)?;
            conditions.push(BoundaryCondition::natural(region, BoundaryData::zero()));
        }
    }
    let boundaries = StructuredBoundaries::new(&mesh, 2, conditions)?;
    StructuredFdmPlan::new(mesh, &Diffusion { ratio }, &boundaries)
}

/// Largest real eigenvalue part of J₀ − κ diag(1, d). κ is continuous k² or discrete k_h².
pub(crate) fn growth_rate(kappa: f64, ratio: f64) -> f64 {
    let [[fu, fv], [gu, gv]] = JACOBIAN;
    let trace = fu + gv - (1.0 + ratio) * kappa;
    let det = (fu - kappa) * (gv - ratio * kappa) - fv * gu;
    let disc = trace * trace - 4.0 * det;
    if disc >= 0.0 {
        0.5 * (trace + RealScalar::sqrt(disc))
    } else {
        0.5 * trace
    }
}

/// On an h = 1 grid, one-axis mode m contributes 4 sin²(mπ/2L) to squared discrete wavenumber.
pub(crate) fn axis_wavenumber2(mode: usize, length: usize) -> f64 {
    let s = RealScalar::sin(mode as f64 * core::f64::consts::PI / (2.0 * length as f64));
    4.0 * s * s
}

/// (m_x, m_y, Re λ_h) for all modes (m_x, m_y) ≠ (0, 0).
pub(crate) fn discrete_modes(length: usize, ratio: f64) -> Vec<(usize, usize, f64)> {
    let mut modes = Vec::new();
    for my in 0..=length {
        for mx in 0..=length {
            if mx + my > 0 {
                let kh2 = axis_wavenumber2(mx, length) + axis_wavenumber2(my, length);
                modes.push((mx, my, growth_rate(kh2, ratio)));
            }
        }
    }
    modes
}

/// Unstable-band endpoints [k₁, k₂]. Two roots of h: k² = (s ∓ √(s² − 4d det J₀))/(2d), s = d f_u + g_v.
pub(crate) fn unstable_band(ratio: f64) -> Option<[f64; 2]> {
    let [[fu, fv], [gu, gv]] = JACOBIAN;
    let slope = ratio * fu + gv;
    let disc = slope * slope - 4.0 * ratio * (fu * gv - fv * gu);
    (slope > 0.0 && disc > 0.0).then(|| {
        let s = RealScalar::sqrt(disc);
        [(slope - s) / (2.0 * ratio), (slope + s) / (2.0 * ratio)].map(RealScalar::sqrt)
    })
}

/// Find continuous fastest-growing wavenumber k_max by scanning 0 < k ≤ 1.5 in increments of 10⁻⁴.
pub(crate) fn fastest_wavenumber(ratio: f64) -> f64 {
    (1..=15_000)
        .map(|i| 1e-4 * i as f64)
        .max_by(|a, b| growth_rate(a * a, ratio).total_cmp(&growth_rate(b * b, ratio)))
        .unwrap_or(0.0)
}
```

## Computing the continuous dispersion relation

The next cell takes diffusion ratio $d$ and tabulates continuous-system $d_c$, $k_c$, $k_{\max}$, and unstable-band endpoints.
Find $k_{\max}$ by scanning $0<k\le1.5$ in increments of $10^{-4}$, giving accuracy $10^{-4}$.
Plot $\operatorname{Re}\lambda(k)$ for selected $d$ solid and the curve for $d=d_c$ dashed, marking $k_c$ and $k_{\max}$ by vertical lines and the unstable band by a range.
The dashed curve touches $\operatorname{Re}\lambda=0$ at $k=k_c$.

let curve = |d: f64| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {

With default $d=20$, tabulated $d_c=8.5676$ and $k_c=0.5845$ agree with closed forms, and growth rate at $k_{\max}=0.4867$ is $0.2632$.
The solid curve crosses zero at $k_1=0.2720$ and $k_2=0.8222$ and is positive only between them.
The vertical line for $k_c$ lies right of that for $k_{\max}$; at $d=20$ the fastest-growing wavenumber is smaller than the critical wavenumber.
Setting $d=8<d_c$ makes unstable-band columns “None”, and $\operatorname{Re}\lambda(k_{\max})=-0.0271$ is negative.
At $d=40$, the band widens to $k\in(0.1836,\,0.8610)$ and $k_{\max}=0.4205$ decreases.

## Computing discrete modes

The next cell takes diffusion ratio $d$ and side length $L$ and computes $\operatorname{Re}\lambda_h$ for all modes $(m_x,m_y)\ne(0,0)$ of a grid with spacing $h=1$.
Plot discrete modes as points and the continuous dispersion relation as a dashed reference.
The horizontal coordinate is nominal wavenumber $k=\pi\sqrt{m_x^2+m_y^2}/L$ from mode numbers, rather than discrete wavenumber $k_h$.
The table shows the fastest-growing discrete mode, its $k$ and $k_h$, and the count of positive-growth discrete modes.
The last column checks the prediction by placing mode cosine $\boldsymbol{\phi}$ in both components, applying plan operator $A$, and computing relative residual $\max_i|(A\boldsymbol{\phi}-Dk_h^2M\boldsymbol{\phi})_i|/\max_i|(A\boldsymbol{\phi})_i|$ of $A\boldsymbol{\phi}=D\,k_h^2M\boldsymbol{\phi}$ ($D$ is each component's diffusivity).
A rounding-level residual shows that formula $k_h^2$ matches eigenvalues of `StructuredFdmPlan`.

//| ref: pde-turing-dispersion-modes
//| title: "Count discrete Neumann-mode growth rates and check eigenvalues with the plan operator"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [pde-turing-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-pde, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   ratio: {"type":"range","label":"Diffusion ratio d = D_v/D_u [-]","description":"Below d_c ≈ 8.57, every mode decays.","min":4,"max":40,"step":0.5,"value":20}
//|   length: {"type":"range","label":"Side length L [-]","description":"Grid spacing is h = 1, with L + 1 vertices per side.","min":4,"max":64,"step":4,"value":32}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"below-critical","label":"d = 8 < d_c","description":"Every discrete mode decays.","inputs":{"ratio":8}}
//|   - {"id":"small-domain","label":"d = 10, L = 4","description":"An unstable band opens, but no discrete mode lies within it.","inputs":{"ratio":10,"length":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"discrete","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrete","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.26315624593311115,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrete","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":59}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrete","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: below-critical
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"discrete","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrete","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":0}
//|   - case: small-domain
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"discrete","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrete","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{LinearOperator, RealScalar};
use scioxide_pde::SpatialDiscretization;
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let (size, length) = (length, length as usize);
let wavenumber =
    |mx: usize, my: usize| PI * RealScalar::sqrt((mx * mx + my * my) as f64) / size;
// Fastest-growing mode, count of positive-growth modes, and plotted points (m_x ≤ m_y, k ≤ 1).
let modes = discrete_modes(length, ratio);
let unstable = modes.iter().filter(|m| m.2 > 0.0).count();
let (mx, my, rate) = modes
    .iter()
    .copied()
    .fold((0, 0, f64::MIN), |b, m| if m.2 > b.2 { m } else { b });
let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = modes
    .iter()
    .filter(|m| m.0 <= m.1 && wavenumber(m.0, m.1) <= 1.0)
    .map(|m| [wavenumber(m.0, m.1), m.2])
    .collect();
// Check: place fastest-mode cosine φ in both components and inspect relative residual of A φ = D k_h² M φ.
let kh2 = axis_wavenumber2(mx, length) + axis_wavenumber2(my, length);
let plan = turing_plan(length, ratio).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let nodes = plan.unknowns() / 2;
let mut mode = vec![0.0; 2 * nodes];
for node in 0..nodes {
    let [x, y] = plan.node_point(node).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let phi =
        RealScalar::cos(mx as f64 * PI * x / size) * RealScalar::cos(my as f64 * PI * y / size);
    mode[node] = phi;
    mode[nodes + node] = phi;
}
let mut applied = vec![0.0; 2 * nodes];
let mut scratch = vec![0.0; plan.apply_workspace_len().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?];
plan.apply_into(&mode, &mut applied, &mut scratch)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mass = plan.lumped_mass();
let scale = applied.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, a| m.max(a.abs()));
let residual = (0..2 * nodes)
    .map(|i| (applied[i] - [1.0, ratio][i / nodes] * kh2 * mass[i] * mode[i]).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max)
    / scale;
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "m_x", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "m_y", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Nominal wavenumber k [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Discrete wavenumber k_h [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Re λ_h [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Growing discrete modes [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Relative eigenpair residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let (k, k_h) = (wavenumber(mx, my), RealScalar::sqrt(kh2));
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(mx, my, k, k_h, rate, unstable, residual)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "discrete" })
);
let curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=200)
    .map(|i| {
        [
            0.005 * i as f64,
            growth_rate(2.5e-5 * (i * i) as f64, ratio),
        ]
    })
    .collect();
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Nominal wavenumber k [-]",
        "yLabel": "Growth rate Re λ [-]",
        "xMin": 0,
        "xMax": 1,
        "yMin": -1,
        "yMax": 0.5,
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.0, "label": "Re λ = 0" }],
        "layers": [
            {
                "id": "continuous",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Continuous Re λ(k)",
                "points": curve,
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
            },
            { "id": "discrete", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Discrete modes Re λ_h", "points": points }

    }),

With defaults ($d=20$, $L=32$), 59 discrete modes have positive growth; the maximum is $\operatorname{Re}\lambda_h=0.2632$ for $(m_x,m_y)=(5,0)$ ($k=0.4909$, $k_h=0.4860$).
$(0,5)$ has the same growth rate; the table shows the one found first in mode-number order.
This mode's $k$ is the nominal wavenumber nearest $k_{\max}=0.4867$ ($(3,4)$ and $(4,3)$ share the same $k$).
Relative eigenpair residual $6.4\times10^{-15}$ confirms to rounding error that the cosine is an eigenvector of the plan operator.
Plotted points take the dashed curve's value at a position $k-k_h$ to the left; the shift grows with $k$.
Above wavenumber $k_{\max}$ the dashed curve slopes downward, so points lie above it.

At $d=8<d_c$, the maximum discrete growth rate is $-0.0272$ (mode $(6,1)$), with no growing modes.
For $d=10$ and $L=4$, continuous unstable band $k\in(0.4472,\,0.7071)$ opens, but $k_h=0.7654$ of lowest nonuniform mode $(1,0)$ lies outside, leaving zero growing discrete modes.
In this domain the uniform state remains stable even for $d>d_c$.

## Summary

The uniform Schnakenberg steady state ($a=0.1$, $b=0.9$) becomes unstable when the diffusion ratio exceeds $d_c\approx8.5676$.
Critical wavenumber $k_c\approx0.5845$, first becoming unstable, differs from $k_{\max}\approx0.4867$ growing fastest at $d=20$; the latter provides a guide to pattern wavelength.
On a finite square, count zero-flux cosine modes whose discrete wavenumbers $k_h$ enter the unstable band; if none do, no pattern forms even for $d>d_c$.
For vertex differences of `StructuredFdmPlan`, use the plan operator to confirm to rounding error that cosine modes are eigenvectors of $M^{-1}A$.
