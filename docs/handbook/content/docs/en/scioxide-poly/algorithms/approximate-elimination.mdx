Floating-point resultants are rarely exactly 0; their values alone cannot distinguish a small value due to common roots from one due to rounding.
Approximate elimination in `scioxide-poly` computes resultant and subresultant coefficients as Sylvester minors, attaching numerical rank and reciprocal conditioning of the same matrix.
This page explains computation, determinant error indicators, diagnostic interpretation, and Plans for repeated same-degree elimination, examining values and diagnostics near multiple roots.
Definitions and identities for resultants, discriminants, and subresultants, and exact-field remainder sequences appear in [Resultants, discriminants, and subresultants](../resultants-and-elimination/).
Following that notation, $f$ and $g$ have degrees $m$ and $n$, and the $j$th subresultant is $S_j$.
Here $u$ is unit roundoff and $\varepsilon=2u$ machine epsilon.

## Subresultants and approximate elimination

For floating-point coefficients, `approximate_resultant`, `approximate_subresultant`, and `approximate_discriminant` return determinant values and matrix diagnostics.
Each value is computed as follows.

1. Remove exact trailing coefficient zeros to determine actual degree.
   Empty coefficient sequences are rejected with `InvalidInput` (`Empty`), non-finite coefficients with `NonFinite`.
2. Divide each input's rows by a power of 2 placing its largest absolute coefficient (absolute real or imaginary component for complex coefficients) in $[1, 2)$ (equilibration).
   Power-of-two scaling introduces no rounding unless components enter the subnormal range, preserving matrix structure.
   If downscaling underflows a component to 0, return `NumericalInstability` (`Underflow`) because structure changes.
3. From singular values of the equilibrated matrix, compute numerical rank and the least-to-greatest singular-value ratio (reciprocal condition number).
   Numerical rank uses the rank policy within `PolynomialApproximationPolicy`.
   Rank is diagnostic; rank policy never truncates the value to 0.
4. Compute the determinant from LU factorization with partial pivoting of the same matrix.
   Set the value to 0 only when a pivot is exactly 0.
5. Restore powers of $2^{e}$ removed by equilibration through the exponent.
   Values are retained in range-aware `ScaledValue`, representing determinants beyond the range of `f64`.

The determinant differential identity (Jacobi's formula) $d(\det A) = \det A \cdot \operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}\,dA)$ explains why reciprocal conditioning indicates value accuracy.
For a perturbation $\Delta A$ to an $L \times L$ matrix $A$, the first-order approximation gives

$$
\frac{|\det(A + \Delta A) - \det A|}{|\det A|} \lesssim |\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}\Delta A)| \le L\,\kappa_2(A)\,\frac{\|\Delta A\|_2}{\|A\|_2}
$$

where $\kappa_2(A) = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$ is the reciprocal of the reported reciprocal condition number.
Partial-pivoted LU backward error $\|\Delta A\|_2/\|A\|_2$ is typically around $O(Lu)$ for ordinary matrices with small growth factors (worst-case bounds include growth factors and a polynomial in $L$; Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed., §§9.3–9.4).
Thus determinant relative error is indicated by about $L^2 \kappa_2(A)\, u$; significant digits are lost as reciprocal conditioning approaches $u$.
This is a first-order indicator assuming growth-factor behavior; SciOxide returns no error upper bound.

The resulting `ApproximateElimination` returns a `ScaledValue` in original-coefficient units through `value()` and an `EliminationReport` through `report()`.
Converting to an ordinary type with `try_value()` fails with `NumericalInstability` for overflowing values or nonzero values rounding to 0.
`round()` rounds with gradual underflow into subnormals.
`EliminationReport` contains actual degrees `degrees()`, equilibration exponents `equilibration_exponents()`, numerical rank `rank()`, `reciprocal_condition()`, and policy `policy()`.
A zero-polynomial input gives value and reciprocal condition number 0; two constants with matrix order 0 give both 1.
Neither case performs SVD, so `rank()` is `None`.
These diagnose the whole matrix, providing neither coefficientwise error bounds nor common-factor proofs.

Discriminant computation equilibrates $p$, forms $p'/(n 2^{e})$, multiplies $\operatorname{Res}(p, p'/(n 2^e))$ by $(n 2^e)^n$, divides by $a_n$, and reverses sign when $n \bmod 4$ is 2 or 3.
Coefficients are normalized before differentiation to prevent overflow in products such as $n a_n$.
Coefficients $(i+1)p_{i+1}/(n2^e)$ of $p'/(n 2^e)$ incur rounding for $n$ where $(i+1)/n$ is not binary-representable.
The report diagnoses matrix $\mathbf{S}(p, p'/(n 2^e))$ before discriminant normalization; its second equilibration exponent records further equilibration of $p'/(n 2^e)$.
For degree below 2, reported degrees are $[n, 0]$, reciprocal conditioning is 1, and `rank()` is `None`.

`approximate_subresultant(left, right, j)` returns every coefficient of $S_j$ at positive index $j$ in ascending-power order as $j + 1$ values of `ApproximateElimination`.
Index 0 is rejected with `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`); use `approximate_resultant` for resultants.
$j \ge \min(m, n)$ gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).

For repeated elimination at the same degrees, retain degrees and policy as validated values.
`SylvesterElimination::new(left_degree, right_degree, index, policy)` retains actual degrees, index, and report rank policy without precomputation.
A positive index at least either degree gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`) here.
`matrix_order()` returns matrix order $L = m + n - 2j$.
`coefficient(left, right, k)` returns one $x^k$ coefficient of $S_j$ ($k \le j$), allocating workspace per call.
`workspace()` returns `EliminationWorkspace` with matrix, LU, and singular-value storage allocated without numerical computation; `coefficient_scoped(left, right, k, &mut workspace, scope)` computes the same value there without SciOxide allocation.
The `_scoped` form runs as one epoch of the caller's scope (`ExecutionContext` or `SolveScope`), checking cancellation and deadlines before and after SVD and LU and recording the attempt in workspace `last_attempt()`.
Calls rejected before starting an epoch, such as empty inputs, are recorded as `PreflightRejected`.
Input degrees or indices mismatching the retained value give `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`), wrong-order workspace `DimensionMismatch`, and cancellation or deadline `ExecutionStopped`.
`compute(coefficients)` and `compute_scoped(coefficients, &mut workspace, scope)` of `SylvesterDiscriminant::new(degree, policy)` follow the same pattern; `DiscriminantWorkspace` retains derivative and elimination storage.
When an `_scoped` form fails, only the workspace's attempt record changes.
One coefficient costs $O(L^3)$ time and $O(L^2)$ memory; all coefficients of $S_j$ repeat this $j + 1$ times.
For single computations, use `approximate_resultant`, `approximate_subresultant`, and `approximate_discriminant`.
They determine numerical rank with the default rank policy `RankPolicy::DimensionScaledEpsilon` (threshold $\tau = L\varepsilon\,\sigma_{\max}$, where $L$ is matrix order).

## Nearby multiple roots and the first subresultant

For $p = (x-1)(x-1-\delta)(x+2)$ and $p'$, the next cell computes resultant $S_0$ and first subresultant $S_1$ ([definition](oxref:poly-subresultant-definition)) for $\delta = 2^{-2}, 2^{-4}, \dots, 2^{-26}$.
Degrees remain 3 and 2 independently of $\delta$, so create `SylvesterElimination` for the resultant and $S_1$ once and reuse their workspaces.
The input is rank-policy threshold $\tau = 2^{-q}$; it changes only reported numerical rank, not values.
Independent checks use symbolically derived closed forms.

$$
\operatorname{Res}(p, p') = -9\delta^2(3+\delta)^2, \qquad
S_1(x) = -2(\delta^2 + 3\delta + 9)\,x + (18 + 15\delta - \delta^2)
$$

The root of $S_1$ is $1 + \delta/2 - \delta^2/3 + O(\delta^3)$, near the midpoint of the two nearby roots.

```rust
//| id: poly-subresultant-near-double-root
//| ref: poly-subresultant-near-double-root
//| title: "Nearby multiple roots and the first subresultant"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   threshold_power: {"type":"range","label":"q in rank threshold τ=2⁻ᑫ","description":"Singular values at most τ times the largest are excluded from numerical rank.","min":10,"max":50,"step":2,"value":30}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"loose-threshold","label":"τ = 2⁻¹⁰","description":"Loosening the threshold reduces numerical rank to 4 from the δ = 2⁻⁴ row onward. Values match defaults.","inputs":{"threshold_power":10}}
//|   - {"id":"tight-threshold","label":"τ = 2⁻⁵⁰","description":"Tightening to rounding level reduces rank only in the two rows with δ ≤ 2⁻²⁴.","inputs":{"threshold_power":50}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/5/4","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/6/4","equals":4}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/12/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: loose-threshold
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":4}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: tight-threshold
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/10/4","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/11/4","equals":4}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-double","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, RankThreshold, RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::RankPolicy;
use scioxide_poly::{
    ApproxPolynomial, PolynomialApproximationPolicy, PolynomialBasis, SylvesterElimination,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sci6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 6 });
let fixed15 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 15 });

let tau = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -(threshold_power as i32));
let rank_policy =
    RankPolicy::Threshold(RankThreshold::new(0.0, tau).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
let policy = PolynomialApproximationPolicy::new(Tolerance::from_sqrt_epsilon(), rank_policy);
// p is cubic and p′ quadratic. Prepare elimination for S₀ = Res (order-5 matrix) and S₁ (order-3 matrix) once
let resultant_plan = SylvesterElimination::new(3, 2, 0, policy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let first_plan = SylvesterElimination::new(3, 2, 1, policy).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut resultant_workspace = resultant_plan.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut first_workspace = first_plan.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let context = ExecutionContext::sequential_native();
let (mut rows, mut values, mut conditions, mut theory) =
    (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for k in (2..=26).step_by(2) {
    let delta = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -k);
    let p = ApproxPolynomial::from_roots_with_options(&[1.0, 1.0 + delta, -2.0], policy)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let derivative = p.derivative().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (pc, dc) = (p.basis().coefficients(), derivative.basis().coefficients());
    let s0 = resultant_plan
        .coefficient_scoped(pc, dc, 0, &mut resultant_workspace, context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let s10 = first_plan
        .coefficient_scoped(pc, dc, 0, &mut first_workspace, context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let s11 = first_plan
        .coefficient_scoped(pc, dc, 1, &mut first_workspace, context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let resultant = s0.value().try_value().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let root = -s10.value().try_value().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        / s11.value().try_value().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Independent check: symbolic closed forms
    let resultant_exact = -9.0 * delta * delta * (3.0 + delta) * (3.0 + delta);
    let root_exact =
        (18.0 + 15.0 * delta - delta * delta) / (2.0 * (9.0 + 3.0 * delta + delta * delta));
    let rcond = s0.report().reciprocal_condition();
    let rank = s0.report().rank().map(|r| r.rank());
    rows.push((
        delta,
        resultant,
        ((resultant - resultant_exact) / resultant_exact).abs(),
        rcond,
        rank,
        root,
        (root - root_exact).abs(),
    ));
    values.push([delta, resultant.abs()]);
    conditions.push([delta, rcond]);
    theory.push([delta, resultant_exact.abs()]);
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "δ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Res(p, p′) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative closed-form discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Sylvester reciprocal condition number [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Numerical rank (maximum 5) [-]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Root of S₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed15 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Closed-form discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "near-double" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "|Res(p, p′)|",
            "points": values,
            "style": {"symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true}
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Closed form 9δ²(3+δ)²",
            "points": theory,
            "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Sylvester reciprocal condition number",
            "points": conditions,
            "style": {"symbol": "diamond", "showSymbol": true}
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Root separation δ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Magnitude [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": tau, "label": "Threshold τ"}]
    })
);
```

Both resultant and reciprocal condition number decrease proportionally to $\delta^2$.
At midpoint $1 + \delta/2$, $p'$ has value $-\delta^2/4$ and $p$ has value $O(\delta^2)$, so perturbations of $O(\delta^2)$ turn $p$ and $p'$ into a pair sharing a root.
From the row where reciprocal conditioning falls below $\tau$, numerical rank drops from 5 to 4, indicating numerical GCD degree $5 - 4 = 1$.
With default $q = 30$, the drop starts at row $\delta = 2^{-14}$; with $q = 10$ at $\delta = 2^{-4}$, and with $q = 50$ at $\delta = 2^{-24}$.
Changing $q$ changes only numerical rank; resultant values and relative closed-form discrepancies stay unchanged.

The resultant's relative closed-form discrepancy grows as $\delta$ decreases, reaching $0.17$ at $\delta = 2^{-24}$ and $1.7$ at $2^{-26}$, losing significant digits.
At $\delta = 2^{-24}$, reciprocal conditioning falls to $1.9 \times 10^{-16}$, and Jacobi's indicator $L^2 \kappa_2 u$ ($L = 5$) is about 15.
For determinants of matrices with $O(1)$ coefficients, values below $10^{-13}$ are obscured by rounding.
The leading coefficient of $S_1$, however, stays near $-18$ rather than shrinking; the root of $S_1$ agrees with the closed form within $5 \times 10^{-16}$ and lies near the midpoint of the two nearby roots.
The next subresultant locates nearby common roots accurately, whereas the resultant value does not.
The threshold for treating roots as common is a policy choice; the table supplies evidence for that choice.

## Summary

Read floating-point resultant values (`ScaledValue`) separately from equilibrated-matrix rank and reciprocal conditioning.
Rank policy changes only numerical-rank diagnostics, not values.
Since relative determinant error is indicated by $L^2 \kappa_2 u$, expect no significant digits in resultants with reciprocal conditioning near $u$.
Locate nearby common roots from the root of the next subresultant $S_1$ rather than the small resultant.
The common-root threshold is the user's policy choice; reciprocal conditioning and numerical rank support that decision without proving a common factor.
For repeated same-degree computations, create `SylvesterElimination` and `SylvesterDiscriminant` and reuse workspaces.
