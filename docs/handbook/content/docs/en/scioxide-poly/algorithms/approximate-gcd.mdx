Whether two polynomials share a factor matters in rational-function cancellation, multiple-root detection, and elimination in algebraic systems.
With exact coefficients, Euclid's algorithm determines the greatest common divisor (GCD) in finitely many operations.
With floating-point coefficients, slight rounding reduces the GCD degree to 0 (coprime), requiring a numerical definition of a common factor of a nearby polynomial pair.
This page covers exact GCDs, approximate-GCD degree estimation from Sylvester singular values and factor recovery, and report interpretation.
Division error scales are covered in [Polynomial division and deflation](../division-gcd-and-rational/); rational cancellation by approximate GCD in [Rational-function evaluation and cancellation](oxref:poly-rational-approximate-cancellation).
The symbol $u$ denotes unit roundoff ($2^{-53}$ for `f64`), and $\varepsilon$ machine epsilon ($2^{-52}$); polynomial coefficients are ordered by ascending powers.

## Polynomial GCDs and Euclid's algorithm

Over an exact field, the greatest common divisor $\gcd(f, g)$ of two polynomials $f, g$ follows from Euclid's algorithm in finitely many operations.

$$
r_{-1} = f, \quad r_0 = g, \quad r_{k-1} = q_k r_k + r_{k+1} \quad (\deg r_{k+1} < \deg r_k)
$$

The last nonzero remainder $r_k$ immediately before $r_{k+1} = 0$, made monic (leading coefficient 1), is $\gcd(f, g)$.
`ExactPolynomial::gcd` uses this algorithm and returns $\gcd(p, 0) = \operatorname{monic}(p)$ and $\gcd(0, 0) = 0$.
`ExactPolynomial::lcm` returns the least common multiple by making $(f/\gcd(f, g))\, g$ monic; if either input is 0, it returns 0.
Both give `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`) if coefficient-type division does not satisfy $(a/b) \cdot b = a$.

### Approximate GCDs and numerical rank

Even mathematically factor-sharing polynomial pairs can acquire a small constant final remainder $O(u)$ from coefficient perturbations of about $10^{-16}$, leading to $\gcd(f, g) = 1$ in floating-point arithmetic.
Continuing Euclid while treating “small” remainders as 0 amplifies error through cancellation in the remainder sequence and makes the threshold choice determine the result.
Since the GCD is discontinuous in the coefficients, numerical computation treats the highest-degree GCD available to a nearby polynomial pair as the approximate GCD.

Its degree can be inferred from singular values of the Sylvester matrix $\mathbf{S}(f, g)$.
Let $f$ have degree $m$ and $g$ degree $n$.
The matrix of the linear map from pairs $(v, w)$ with $\deg v < n$ and $\deg w < m$ to $vf + wg$ is $\mathbf{S}(f, g)^\top$; its kernel dimension equals the degree of $\gcd(f, g)$ ([Sylvester matrices and resultants](oxref:poly-sylvester-resultant)).
Thus, exactly, $\deg \gcd(f, g) = m + n - \operatorname{rank} \mathbf{S}(f, g)$; in floating-point arithmetic, the number of small singular values estimates this degree.
Since $\mathbf{S}$ is linear in the coefficients, if a degree-preserving perturbation $(\delta f, \delta g)$ produces a pair sharing a root, Weyl's singular-value inequality gives $\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{S}(f, g)) \le \|\mathbf{S}(\delta f, \delta g)\|_2$.
The nearer the input is to a pair with a common root, the smaller its least singular value.
A small least singular value does not rigorously imply a nearby pair with a common factor, so the degree decision is an estimate.

`approximate_gcd(left, right)` uses the following procedure.

1. If either input is the zero polynomial, return $\gcd(p, 0) = \operatorname{monic}(p)$ and $\gcd(0, 0) = 0$ with $O(m + n)$ without using a Sylvester matrix.
2. Build an equilibrated Sylvester matrix by dividing each input's rows by a power of 2 that places its largest coefficient in $[1, 2)$.
   Power-of-two scaling introduces no rounding and prevents one input with larger coefficients from dominating singular values.
3. Compute numerical rank by singular value decomposition and take $d = \min(m + n - \operatorname{rank}, m, n)$ as the GCD degree.
   Numerical rank uses the policy's rank policy (`rank_policy()`).
4. For equilibrated inputs $\tilde f, \tilde g$, initialize cofactors $v, w$ ($\tilde f \approx hv$, $\tilde g \approx hw$) from the right singular vector for the least singular value of the convolution matrix corresponding to the degree-$d$ subresultant; use least squares to form a degree-$d$ factor $h$ and make it monic (Z. Zeng, "The numerical greatest common divisor of univariate polynomials", *Contemp. Math.* 556, AMS, 2011, 187–217, §§7, 9, 10).
5. Reduce residual $\|hv - \tilde f\|_2^2 + \|hw - \tilde g\|_2^2$ by Gauss–Newton in $h, v, w$.
   Use at most 32 iterations and at most 16 step halvings per iteration.
   Stop when the residual is 0, fails to decrease after 16 halvings, or the largest step component is at most (sum of unknown and equation counts) $\times\, \varepsilon \times$ (coefficient magnitude, at least 1).
   Degree $d$ stays fixed throughout.
6. Long-divide each original input by $h$ once, retaining cofactors and remainders.

SVD and each Gauss–Newton iteration operate on matrices of order about $m + n$ and cost $O((m+n)^3)$.
Inconsistent policies give `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`); failure of SVD convergence gives `ConvergenceFailure`.
A zero leading coefficient in the least-squares factor from step 4 gives `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`); overflow when rounding extended-exponent values gives `NumericalInstability`.
An overly loose rank policy may treat nearby roots as common and select an inaccurate degree.

### GCD and simplification reports

`ApproximateGcd` contains monic factor `polynomial()` (0 for $\gcd(0,0)$), cofactors `cofactors()` and remainders `remainders()` in input order, and report `report()`.
The values in `ApproximateGcdReport` are as follows.

- `sylvester_rank()`: the equilibrated Sylvester matrix's `RankReport` (rank, maximum rank, threshold used, and scale).
  For zero-input handling, this is `None`.
- `reciprocal_condition()`: the least-to-greatest singular-value ratio, independent of the threshold.
  For zero-input handling, this is `None`.
- `operand_errors()`: $\max_i |\text{remainder}_i| / \max_i |\text{input}_i|$ per input; 0 for a zero-polynomial input.
- `backward_error()`: the maximum of the two `operand_errors()` values.
- `effective_degree()`: recovered factor degree (from algebraic identities for zero inputs).
- `equilibration_exponents()`: exponents of powers of 2 removed by equilibration (`[0, 0]` for zero inputs).
- `policy()`: the policy used.

`operand_errors()` is normalized by the maximum absolute coefficient, unlike the 1-norm definition of the [division reconstruction residual](oxref:poly-division-reconstruction-residual).
Rank policy determines degree, but rational cancellation (`ApproxRationalFunction::simplify`) occurs only when each remainder is at most the policy tolerance $\tau_{\text{abs}} + \tau_{\text{rel}} \max_i |\text{input}_i|$.
Both values are estimates, not proofs of common roots.

### Nearby common roots and GCD degree

The next cell computes approximate GCDs of $f = (x-1)(x+2)$ and $g = (x - 1 - \delta)(x - 3)$ for $\delta = 2^{-8}, 2^{-12}, \dots, 2^{-48}$.
For $\delta \ne 0$, the exact GCD is constant.
The input is $q$ of $\tau = 2^{-q}$, used for both the rank threshold and relative tolerance; the table lists reciprocal condition number, selected degree, recovered factor root, and normalized remainder.
The independent check uses the remainder theorem.
Division by a degree-1 factor $x - \rho$ leaves remainders $f(\rho)$ and $g(\rho)$, so the normalized remainder should be the larger of $|f(\rho)|/2$ and $|g(\rho)|/(4 + \delta)$ (denominators are each input's maximum absolute coefficient).
Degree-0 rows have no factor root, so root positions and remainder-theorem values are shown as “-”.

```rust
//| id: poly-approximate-gcd
//| ref: poly-approximate-gcd
//| title: "Common-factor perturbations and numerical rank"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   threshold_power: {"type":"range","label":"q in threshold τ=2⁻ᑫ","description":"Use the same τ for rank threshold and relative tolerance.","min":12,"max":48,"step":4,"value":24}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"loose","label":"q = 12","description":"For τ = 2⁻¹², degree 1 is selected from δ = 2⁻¹² onward.","inputs":{"threshold_power":12}}
//|   - {"id":"strict","label":"q = 48","description":"For τ = 2⁻⁴⁸, only the last row δ = 2⁻⁴⁸ has degree 1.","inputs":{"threshold_power":48}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/4/2","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/4/3","approx":{"value":0.1,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/4/4","approx":{"value":2.6822089593814276e-8,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/4/5","approx":{"value":2.6822089593814276e-8,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: loose
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":1}
//|   - case: strict
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/9/2","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/10/2","equals":1}
use scioxide_core::{RankThreshold, RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::RankPolicy;
use scioxide_poly::{
    ApproxPolynomial, PolynomialApproximationPolicy, PolynomialBasis, approximate_gcd,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let tau = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -(threshold_power as i32));
let policy = PolynomialApproximationPolicy::new(
    Tolerance::new(0.0, tau).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    RankPolicy::Threshold(RankThreshold::new(0.0, tau).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?),
);
// Fix f = (x − 1)(x + 2) = x² + x − 2 and move a root of g = (x − 1 − δ)(x − 3)
let left = ApproxPolynomial::from_roots_with_options(&[1.0_f64, -2.0], policy)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut rows, mut conditions, mut errors) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for p in (8..=48).step_by(4) {
    let delta = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -p);
    let right = ApproxPolynomial::from_roots_with_options(&[1.0 + delta, 3.0], policy)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let gcd = approximate_gcd(&left, &right).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let report = gcd.report();
    let factor = gcd.polynomial().basis().coefficients();
    // For degree 1, the factor is x − ρ. Independent check: form normalized remainders from f(ρ) and g(ρ) by the remainder theorem
    // (Denominators 2 and 4 + δ are the maximum absolute input coefficients)
    let (position, predicted) = if factor.len() == 2 {
        let rho = -factor[0];
        let f_rho = (rho - 1.0) * (rho + 2.0);
        let g_rho = (rho - 1.0 - delta) * (rho - 3.0);
        let predicted = (f_rho.abs() / 2.0).max(g_rho.abs() / (4.0 + delta));
        (Some((rho - 1.0) / delta), Some(predicted))
    } else {
        (None, None)
    };
    let rcond = report.reciprocal_condition();
    rows.push((
        delta,
        rcond,
        report.effective_degree(),
        position,
        report.backward_error(),
        predicted,
    ));
    if let Some(rcond) = rcond {
        conditions.push([delta, rcond]);
    }
    // Omit zero-remainder rows (degree 0) from the logarithmic plot
    if report.backward_error() > 0.0 {
        errors.push([delta, report.backward_error()]);
    }
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Root separation δ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Reciprocal condition number [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Selected degree [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "(ρ − 1)/δ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum normalized remainder [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Remainder-theorem value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "gcd" }));
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Sylvester reciprocal condition number",
            "points": conditions,
            "style": { "symbol": "diamond", "showSymbol": true }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Maximum normalized remainder (zeros omitted)",
            "points": errors,
            "style": { "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Root separation δ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Magnitude [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": tau, "label": "Threshold τ" }]
    })
);
```

For every $\delta$, the reciprocal condition number is about $0.14\,\delta$; degree 1 is selected from the row where it falls below $\tau$ onward.
With default $q = 24$ ($\tau = 6.0 \times 10^{-8}$), degree 1 starts at $\delta = 2^{-24}$; for $q = 12$ it starts at $\delta = 2^{-12}$, and for $q = 48$ only row $\delta = 2^{-48}$ has degree 1.
In degree-1 rows, recovered root $\rho$ lies between 1 and $1 + \delta$; $(\rho - 1)/\delta$ stays near $0.1$ ($0.125$ at $\delta = 2^{-48}$ because of rounding in $\rho - 1$).
This $0.1$ follows from the least-squares balance.
Equilibrated inputs are $\tilde f = f/2$ and $\tilde g = g/4$; setting $t = \rho - 1$ gives, to first order, $\tilde f(\rho) \approx 3t/2$ and $\tilde g(\rho) \approx -(t - \delta)/2$.
Since both inputs have the same degree, distances to multiples of $x - \rho$ are the same constant times $|\tilde f(\rho)|$ and $|\tilde g(\rho)|$, respectively; their squared sum $9t^2/4 + (t - \delta)^2/4$ is minimized at $t = \delta/10$.
Normalized remainders are about $0.45\,\delta$ and agree with remainder-theorem values to the displayed digits.
Normalized remainders are about three times the reciprocal condition number; even with the same $\tau$, a $\delta$ whose reciprocal condition number is just below $\tau$ can select degree 1 while failing the cancellation tolerance criterion.
Degree 1 approximates a common factor under the specified policy; it does not prove an exact common root.
For the same $\delta$, reducing $\tau$ selects degree 0 and concludes there is no common factor.

## Summary

For exact coefficients, `ExactPolynomial::gcd` and `lcm` return monic results by Euclid's algorithm.
For floating-point coefficients, use `approximate_gcd`, recognizing that equilibrated Sylvester numerical rank determines degree; compare `reciprocal_condition()` with the policy's rank threshold to assess the decision margin.
Check how well the recovered factor divides the inputs with `operand_errors()`; linear factors permit independent remainder-theorem checks.
Looser rank thresholds treat nearby roots as common, so match the threshold to coefficient error magnitude.
Degree decisions and remainders are estimates; use exact coefficients or verified computation when a common-root proof is needed.
