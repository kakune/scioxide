[Polynomial interpolation, divided differences, and Chebyshev nodes](../interpolation-and-chebyshev/) derives barycentric formulas and explains node selection through interpolation errors and Lebesgue constants.
This page covers rounding error when evaluating these formulas in floating-point arithmetic and the computation of weights.
The first half explains error bounds for both barycentric forms and condition estimates returned by SciOxide evaluation.
The second checks closed-form weights for common node families against `chebyshev_points` and general weight computation, then covers low-level divided-difference and weight functions and exact-field interpolation.

## Rounding errors of barycentric formulas

Notation for nodes $x_0,\dots,x_n$, nodal polynomial $\omega(x)=\prod_k(x-x_k)$, weights $w_j=1/\prod_{k\ne j}(x_j-x_k)$, and Lagrange basis $\ell_j$ follows the [barycentric Lagrange formula](oxref:poly-barycentric-formula).
Let $u$ be unit roundoff and define sample-value conditioning at evaluation point $x$ by

$$
\kappa(x,y) = \frac{\sum_{j=0}^n |\ell_j(x)\,y_j|}{|p(x)|},\qquad
\lambda_n(x) = \sum_{j=0}^n |\ell_j(x)|
$$


$\kappa(x,y)$ is the amplification of relative sample perturbations into relative changes of $p(x)$.
$\lambda_n$ is the [Lebesgue function](oxref:poly-lebesgue-constant), equal to $\kappa(x,1)$ when interpolating the constant $1$.
Higham (2004) bounds rounding error, including weight computation, through first-order terms as follows ($n$ is degree).

- The first form is backward stable.
  The computed value is the exact interpolated value for samples perturbed relatively by at most $\gamma_{5n+5}=(5n+5)u/(1-(5n+5)u)$, giving relative error at most $\gamma_{5n+5}\,\kappa(x,y)$.
- The second form has relative error at most $(3n+4)u\,\kappa(x,y) + (3n+2)u\,\lambda_n(x)$.
  It is not backward stable but is forward stable for nodes with small $\lambda_n$ (Chebyshev nodes).

The additional second-form term $\lambda_n(x)$ comes from separately rounding numerator and denominator.
The denominator sum $\sum_j w_j/(x-x_j)$ becomes $1$ when multiplied by $\omega(x)$; its relative condition number is exactly $\lambda_n(x)$.
Thus poor node placement reduces evaluation accuracy even with exact samples.

### SciOxide evaluation and condition estimates

Polynomials constructed with `ApproxPolynomial::interpolate_barycentric` use the second form.
Evaluation costs $O(n)$ without allocation, preserving accuracy through three techniques.

- Choose the nearest node $x_r$ as reference and use $\sum_j\ell_j=1$ to compute in the form $p(x) = y_r + \sum_{j\ne r}\ell_j(x)(y_j - y_r)$.
  Dividing numerator and denominator by reference term $w_r/(x-x_r)$ avoids reciprocals of the small difference $x-x_r$.
- Numerator and denominator use compensated summation, extracting each rounding error with an error-free transformation (TwoSum) and adding it back.
- Intermediate overflow or underflow retries the entire sum using extended exponent range.

At a node, all other terms vanish and the stored sample is returned unchanged.
`condition().condition_estimate()` returned by `evaluate_with_derivative` is $\max(1,\kappa(x,y)) + \lambda_n(x)$.
When $\sum_j|\ell_j(x)y_j|=0$ (all samples are 0 or evaluation is at a zero-valued sample node), $p(x)=0$ is exact, so return $1$.
This value corresponds to both terms of the second-form error bound and grows at clustered nodes or near endpoints of equally spaced nodes.
Higham's bound concerns the unmodified formula, so interpret the estimate above as an error-amplification indicator.

## Barycentric weights for node families

Common nodes have closed-form barycentric weights (Berrut and Trefethen, 2004).
Scaling all weights by one constant leaves the second form unchanged, so the following forms with common factors removed can be used directly.

| Nodes | Points $x_j$ ($j=0,\dots,n$) | Weights $w_j$ (common factor omitted) | $\max\lvert w_j\rvert/\min\lvert w_j\rvert$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Equally spaced | $-1+2j/n$ | $(-1)^j\binom{n}{j}$ | $\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\approx 2^n\sqrt{2/(\pi n)}$ |
| Chebyshev first kind | $\cos\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2n+2}$ | $(-1)^j\sin\frac{(2j+1)\pi}{2n+2}$ | $\approx 2(n+1)/\pi$ |
| Chebyshev second kind | $\cos\frac{j\pi}{n}$ | $(-1)^j\delta_j$ ($\delta_0=\delta_n=\tfrac12$; otherwise 1) | $2$ |

Equally spaced weights remove a common factor from $w_j = (-1)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}/(h^n n!)$ ($h=2/n$), spanning a magnitude range of about $2^n$.
This spread corresponds to large endpoint Lebesgue functions.
Closed forms compute weights in $O(n)$ without forming $O(n^2)$ products.

`backend::barycentric::chebyshev_points(n, kind)` constructs $n$ points in ascending order on $[-1,1]$ for `ChebyshevPoints::Roots` (first kind, zeros of $T_n$) or `Extrema` (second kind, extrema of $T_{n-1}$), returning them with the table's weights.
Here `n` is point count, one more than table $n$ (degree).
Points are formed from sines of the same angles as `OrthogonalFamily::zeros`, giving exact symmetry; odd counts have exact central 0, and second-kind endpoints are exactly $\pm1$.
Weights alternate in sign, positive at the first point; first-kind weights are normalized to maximum absolute value 1.
Costs $O(n)$ and allocates two length-$n$ vectors.
`n` equal to 0 or below 2 for the second kind gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`); unrepresentable weights give `NumericalInstability` (`Underflow`).

The next cell compares general product-based weights from `barycentric_weights` with the table's closed forms for three node families.
Closed forms are independent references computed with f64 `sin` and products for binomial coefficients, normalized to maximum absolute value 1 and with the initial sign matched to the compared weights.
Weights returned by `chebyshev_points` are compared with the same reference, and weight magnitudes are plotted logarithmically against node positions.

```rust
//| id: poly-barycentric-weights
//| ref: poly-barycentric-weights
//| title: "Barycentric weights and closed forms for three node families"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree n","description":"Change node count n+1.","min":2,"max":40,"step":1,"value":14}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"degree-40","label":"n = 40","description":"The equally spaced weight spread exceeds 10¹¹.","inputs":{"degree":40}}
//|   - {"id":"degree-3","label":"n = 3","description":"At low degree, the three families' weight spreads differ little.","inputs":{"degree":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":3432,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":2,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: degree-40
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":137846528820,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":26.1,"atol":0.1}}
//|   - case: degree-3
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":3,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"weights","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_poly::backend::barycentric::{ChebyshevPoints, chebyshev_points};
use scioxide_poly::backend::polynomial_basis::barycentric_weights;

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let n = degree.round() as usize;
let points = n + 1;
let equal: Vec<f64> = (0..points)
    .map(|j| -1.0 + 2.0 * j as f64 / n as f64)
    .collect();
let roots =
    chebyshev_points::<f64>(points, ChebyshevPoints::Roots).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lobatto =
    chebyshev_points::<f64>(points, ChebyshevPoints::Extrema).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent reference: closed-form |w_j| from the table (f64 sin and products for binomial coefficients)
let mut closed = [Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
let mut binomial = 1.0_f64;
for j in 0..points {
    closed[0].push(binomial);
    binomial *= (n - j) as f64 / (j + 1) as f64;
    let angle = (2 * j + 1) as f64 * std::f64::consts::PI / (2 * points) as f64;
    closed[1].push(angle.sin());
    closed[2].push(if j == 0 || j == n { 0.5 } else { 1.0 });
}
// Maximum difference after normalizing closed forms to maximum 1 and alternating signs from the first weight's sign
let gap = |weights: &[f64], magnitudes: &[f64]| {
    let largest = magnitudes.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, &c| m.max(c));
    let mut deviation = 0.0_f64;
    for (j, (&w, &c)) in weights.iter().zip(magnitudes).enumerate() {
        let sign = weights[0].signum() * if j % 2 == 0 { 1.0 } else { -1.0 };
        deviation = deviation.max((w - sign * c / largest).abs());
    }
    deviation
};
let families = [
    ("Equally spaced", &equal, None),
    ("Chebyshev first kind", &roots.0, Some(&roots.1)),
    ("Chebyshev second kind", &lobatto.0, Some(&lobatto.1)),
];
let (mut rows, mut series) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for ((name, nodes, returned), magnitudes) in families.into_iter().zip(&closed) {
    // Compute general weights w_j = 1/Π(x_j − x_k) from O(n²) products (normalized to maximum absolute value 1)
    let general = barycentric_weights(nodes).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // The normalized general weights have maximum 1, so the ratio is 1/min|w_j|
    let smallest = general.iter().fold(f64::INFINITY, |m, w| m.min(w.abs()));
    let reported = returned.map(|w| gap(w, magnitudes));
    rows.push((name, gap(&general, magnitudes), reported, 1.0 / smallest));
    let curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = nodes
        .iter()
        .zip(&general)
        .map(|(&x, w)| [x, w.abs()])
        .collect();
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": curve }));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Nodes" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "barycentric_weights versus closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    {
        "key": "2",
        "label": "chebyshev_points weights versus closed form [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": sci3,
        "nullText": "Not applicable"
    },
    { "key": "3", "label": "max|w_j| / min|w_j| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "weights" })
);
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Node x_j [-]",
        "yLabel": "Normalized weight magnitude |w_j| [-]",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
```

At the default $n=14$, both sets of weights differ from closed forms by about $10^{-16}$ to $10^{-15}$ for every family; general product-based weights also agree to rounding accuracy.
Weight magnitude ratios are $\binom{14}{7}=3432$ for equally spaced nodes, about 9.57 for the first kind, and exactly 2 for the second.
At $n=40$, the equally spaced ratio spreads to $\binom{40}{20}\approx1.38\times10^{11}$ while the first-kind ratio remains $1/\sin(\pi/82)\approx26.1$.
Equally spaced weights are largest in the center and smallest at endpoints; second-kind weights are flat except for endpoint $1/2$.

## Public divided-difference functions and exact interpolation

Functions in the `backend` module are low-level components used by other SciOxide crates.
Input checks and output behavior on failure differ by function; choose `ApproxPolynomial` constructors for ordinary interpolation.

`backend::polynomial_basis::barycentric_weights` computes weights for distinct real or complex nodes in $O(n^2)$, normalized to maximum absolute value 1.
Products use extended exponent range, preserving weights even when node differences overflow or underflow.
Normalized weights rounded to 0 give `NumericalInstability` (`Underflow`).
Use `barycentric_weights_into` to form barycentric weights in caller-supplied temporary storage.
Output and `ScaledValue` scratch both have node-count length, computing the same weights without allocation.
Invalid nodes and shapes preserve output, but numerical failure midway can partially change it.
The caller uses only successfully computed weights.

`backend::polynomial_basis::real_divided_differences_into` replaces real samples in a caller-owned array with divided differences in place.
The `dimension` components share nodes and one table traversal, costing $O(n^2\cdot\mathrm{dimension})$ without allocation.
Failure may change array contents.
`confluent_divided_differences_into` takes values and slopes to form Hermite divided differences with each node repeated twice.
`newton_value_from_iter` and `newton_value_and_derivative_from_iter` evaluate coefficient iterators; `newton_to_power_into` converts to monomials.
Difference-ratio helpers `linear_difference_ratio`, `normalized_linear_difference`, and `linear_multiply_difference` compute nearby node differences and products without intermediate overflow or underflow.
See [Bases and evaluation APIs](../../api/bases-and-evaluation/) for result types and range errors.

`backend::barycentric::chebyshev_points` and `ChebyshevPoints` were explained under [Barycentric weights for node families](#barycentric-weights-for-node-families).
The same module's `evaluate` evaluates the second barycentric form from prepared nodes, weights, and coefficients, returning value, derivative, the condition number of the denominator sum ($\lambda_n(x)$ for interpolating polynomials), and the sum of absolute numerator terms divided by $|D|$ (`numerator_bound`) in `BarycentricEvaluation`.
Coefficients as samples give an interpolating polynomial; independent numerator coefficients give a barycentric rational function.
Distinguish polynomial weights derived from nodes from arbitrary rational-interpolation weights.

For exact-field interpolation, construct a point product tree with `ExactPolynomialProductTree::new` and pass values to `interpolate`.
`new` builds trees even with repeated points, but `interpolate` rejects repeated points as `InvalidInput`.
`interpolate` computes $1/M'(x_i)$ ($M=\prod_i(x-x_i)$) through a remainder tree, then combines Lagrange terms upward; fast products give $O(M(n)\log n)$ field operations ($M(n)$ is the cost of a length-$n$ product).
$1/M'(x_i)$ is precisely the barycentric weight $w_i$ above.
No free function `interpolate_exact` is provided.
SciOxide implements `ExactField` for no types; users implement coefficient types such as rationals or finite fields.
`f64` lacks exact-field equality and operations, and Rust's orphan rules prevent implementing the trait for that external type, so it cannot be used for exact interpolation.
[Exact-coefficient operations and product trees](../exact-arithmetic-and-product-trees/) covers an example field and a product-tree round trip.

## Summary

Rounding error of the second form depends on terms $\kappa(x,y)$ and $\lambda_n(x)$; SciOxide's `condition_estimate` returns their sum $\max(1,\kappa)+\lambda_n$, giving a per-point accuracy indicator.
Common weight scaling leaves values unchanged, so equally spaced and Chebyshev nodes can use closed-form weights in $O(n)$.
Create Chebyshev points and weights together with `chebyshev_points`; for arbitrary nodes, `barycentric_weights` computes weights in $O(n^2)$ using extended exponent range.
Equally spaced nodes with weight ratios spreading to about $2^n$ lose accuracy in both evaluation and interpolation even with exact weights; avoid them at high degrees.
Since `backend` functions differ in failure-time output handling, use only successful results and prefer `ApproxPolynomial` constructors for ordinary interpolation.
