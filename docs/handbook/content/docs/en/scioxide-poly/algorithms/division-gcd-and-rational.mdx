Polynomial division with exact coefficients determines quotient and remainder in finitely many operations.
With floating-point coefficients, computed quotients and remainders do not satisfy the identity exactly; even a small residual need not imply accurate quotient coefficients.
This page covers long division, two error measures (reconstruction residual and sensitivity estimate), and how division direction affects error when deflation removes roots individually.
GCDs using division appear in [Polynomial GCDs and approximate GCDs](../approximate-gcd/), and rational functions in [Rational-function evaluation and cancellation](../rational-functions/).
The symbol $u$ denotes unit roundoff ($2^{-53}$ for `f64`), and $\varepsilon$ machine epsilon ($2^{-52}$).
Polynomial coefficients are ordered by ascending powers as $p(x) = p_0 + p_1 x + \dots + p_m x^m$, writing $\|p\|_1 = \sum_k |p_k|$.

## Euclidean division and backward-error assessment

In polynomial ring $F[x]$ over field $F$, dividend $p(x)$ (degree $m$) and nonzero divisor $d(x)$ (degree $n$) have a unique quotient $q(x)$ and remainder $r(x)$ satisfying

$$
p(x) = d(x) q(x) + r(x) \quad (r = 0 \text{ or } \deg r < \deg d)
$$

If $m < n$, then $q = 0$ and $r = p$.

### Long division

Long division starts with remainder candidate $r \leftarrow p$ and repeats two steps in the order $k = m - n, \dots, 0$.

1. Set quotient coefficient $q_k = r_{n+k} / d_n$.
2. Update $r \leftarrow r - q_k x^k d$ to eliminate the $x^{n+k}$ coefficient.

There are $m - n + 1$ iterations with $n$ multiply-adds each, giving cost $O((m - n + 1) n)$ and additional quotient/remainder storage $O(m)$.
A monic $d$ (leading coefficient 1) needs no coefficient division.

For linear divisor $d(x) = x - c$, long division becomes the same recurrence as synthetic division (Horner).
Quotient coefficients follow from the highest degree downward as

$$
q_{m-1} = p_m, \qquad q_{k-1} = p_k + c\, q_k \quad (k = m - 1, \dots, 1)
$$

with remainder $r = p_0 + c\, q_0 = p(c)$ (the remainder theorem).
If $c$ is a root of $p$, the remainder is 0 and the quotient is the polynomial with one occurrence of that root removed.
Finding each root, dividing to reduce degree, then finding remaining roots is called deflation.

### Relative reconstruction residual in floating-point division

Computed floating-point quotient $\hat q$ and remainder $\hat r$ do not satisfy the identity exactly.
SciOxide returns the following normalized reconstruction residual as backward error.

$$
\eta_{\text{div}} = \frac{\|p - (d \hat q + \hat r)\|_1}{\|p\|_1 + \|\hat q\|_1 \|d\|_1 + \|\hat r\|_1}
$$

The denominator is 0 only when $p = 0$, in which case set $\eta_{\text{div}} = 0$.
Writing the residual as $e = p - (d \hat q + \hat r)$, $\hat q$ and $\hat r$ are the exact quotient and remainder of dividend $p - e$.
Thus a small $\eta_{\text{div}}$ means exact division of a dividend shifted by about $\eta_{\text{div}}$ relative to the magnitudes of identity terms.
Residuals use extended-exponent arithmetic with a separate exponent, preventing overflow or underflow in residual computation itself.
Significand rounding remains, so $\eta_{\text{div}}$ is meaningful only down to about $u$.

Small backward error does not guarantee accurate quotient or remainder coefficients.
The report's `condition_estimate()` is sensitivity estimate $\kappa$: propagate absolute first-order coefficient perturbations $|\delta p| \le u|p|$ and $|\delta d| \le u|d|$ through long division, sum quotient and remainder perturbation bounds, then divide by $u(\|\hat q\|_1 + \|\hat r\|_1)$.
Values below 1 are raised to 1; those too large for `f64` return infinity.
`forward_error_estimate()` is forward-error estimate $\gamma_N \kappa$ ($\gamma_N = N\varepsilon/(1 - N\varepsilon)$; $N$ is the dividend coefficient count).
Both are first-order perturbation estimates, not upper bounds.
$\gamma_N$ and the evaluation error model are explained in [Representations and Horner](../representation-and-horner/).

`ApproxPolynomial::div_rem(&divisor)` divides monomial-basis polynomials, returning `PolynomialDivision`.
`quotient()` and `remainder()` borrow quotient and remainder polynomials; `condition()` returns `PolynomialConditionReport` with the backward-error, sensitivity, and forward-error estimates above.
Tolerances affect neither quotient nor remainder; `div_rem` removes only exact trailing coefficient zeros.
A zero remainder is represented by one coefficient 0.
Different polynomial policies `PolynomialApproximationPolicy` give `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`), a zero divisor gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), and quotient or remainder coefficients exceeding `f64` range give `NumericalInstability`.
`div_rem` allocates a Plan and workspace per call.

For repeated same-length division, prepare `PolynomialOperationPlan` from `PolynomialOperationRequest::Divide { dividend_len, divisor_len }` and use `div_rem_into(a, b, quotient, remainder, workspace, context)` ([Fast products, composition, and Taylor shifts](../fast-operations-and-product-trees/)).
Inputs must be nonempty canonical coefficient sequences without exact trailing zeros (`[0]` for zero); violations give `InvalidInput`.
For coefficient counts $N_a, N_b$, quotient length is $\max(1, N_a + 1 - N_b)$ and remainder length $\max(1, \min(N_a, N_b - 1))$; outputs retain zero padding.
Wrong output lengths or insufficient workspace give `DimensionMismatch`, non-finite coefficients `NonFinite`, and cancellation or deadline `ExecutionStopped`, preserving output on every failure.
Coefficients or intermediates approaching overflow or underflow retry the whole division in extended-exponent arithmetic, rounding each coefficient once at the end.
Neither path allocates with sufficient workspace.

### Root deflation and error amplification

In the linear-division recurrence $q_{k-1} = p_k + c\, q_k$, errors in $q_k$ are multiplied by $c$ at the next stage.
To first order, dividend coefficient perturbations $\delta p$ cause quotient error

$$
\delta q_{k-1} = \delta p_k + c\, \delta q_k = \sum_{j=k}^{m} c^{\,j-k}\, \delta p_j
$$


For $|c| > 1$, the error in $q_{k-1}$ can grow to about $|c|^{m-k}$ times $\delta p_m$, with larger errors in lower-degree coefficients.
For $|c| < 1$, error shrinks at each stage.
For zero-remainder deflation, reverse $p_k = q_{k-1} - c\, q_k$ to obtain the same quotient from the constant term upward, using $q_0 = -p_0/c$ and $q_k = (q_{k-1} - p_k)/c$.
In this direction, error is multiplied by $1/c$ per stage.
Peters and Wilkinson showed stable deflation by removing roots smaller in magnitude than remaining roots from the highest degree and larger ones from the constant term (G. Peters and J. H. Wilkinson, "Practical problems arising in the solution of polynomial equations", *J. Inst.
Maths Applics* 8, 1971, 16–35).

SciOxide long division always proceeds from the highest degree; no function divides from the constant term.
Dividing reversed coefficients $x^m p(1/x)$ by $x\, d(1/x) = 1 - cx$ and reversing quotient coefficients back implements constant-term-first division using the same `div_rem`.
For near-zero-remainder deflation, both directions give nearly the same quotient, with the remainder appearing at the coefficient at the opposite end.

The next cell forms product $p = (x - c)s$ from $s(x) = 1 + x + \dots + x^m$ (roots are the $m + 1$th roots of unity except 1, all of magnitude 1) and root $c$, replaces coefficients by $p_j(1 + (-1)^j \theta)$, then divides by $x - c$.
The cell's $m$ is quotient $s$ degree; dividend $p$ has degree $m + 1$.
Relative perturbation $\theta$ models coefficient errors from measurements or preceding computation.
When $c$ is a multiple of 0.5, the product is exact, so quotient error arises only from this perturbation and division rounding.
The exact unperturbed quotient is $s$, so differences of quotient coefficients from 1 directly give forward errors (independent check).
The table compares both directions' reconstruction residuals $\eta_{\text{div}}$, sensitivity estimates $\kappa$, $\kappa\theta$, relative quotient errors $\|\hat q - s\|_1/\|s\|_1$, and remainder magnitudes.
$\kappa\theta$ replaces $\gamma_N$ in forward-error estimate $\gamma_N \kappa$ with perturbation magnitude $\theta$.
The plot shows coefficient errors $|\hat q_k - 1|$ logarithmically against degree $k$; zero differences are omitted and counted in the table.

```rust
//| id: poly-division-forward-error
//| ref: poly-division-forward-error
//| title: "Backward and forward errors of root deflation"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   root: {"type":"range","label":"Removed root c [-]","description":"For |c| > 1, highest-degree-first division multiplies coefficient errors by c per stage.","min":0.5,"max":20,"step":0.5,"value":10}
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Quotient s degree m","description":"Change the number of long-division stages.","min":2,"max":14,"step":1,"value":6}
//|   perturbation_power: {"type":"range","label":"e in coefficient relative perturbation θ = 10ᵉ","description":"Replace dividend coefficients pⱼ by pⱼ(1 + (−1)ʲθ).","min":-15,"max":-6,"step":1,"value":-12}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"small-root","label":"c = 0.5","description":"For |c| < 1, even highest-degree-first division does not amplify error.","inputs":{"root":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"high-degree","label":"m = 14","description":"More stages increase highest-degree-first error to the order of c¹⁴θ.","inputs":{"degree":14}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"division","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"division","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":1e-7,"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"division","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"max":1e-11}}
//|   - case: small-root
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"division","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-11}}
//|   - case: high-degree
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"division","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"division","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"division","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"max":1e-11}}
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, PolynomialBasis};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let theta = 10.0_f64.powf(perturbation_power);
// s(x) = 1 + x + … + xᵐ and d(x) = x − c. If c is a multiple of 0.5, p = d·s is computed without rounding
let ones = vec![1.0_f64; degree as usize + 1];
let s = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(ones.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let divisor = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![-root, 1.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let product = divisor.try_mul(&s).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Data error model: apply alternating relative perturbations (−1)ʲθ to pⱼ
let perturbed: Vec<f64> = product
    .basis()
    .coefficients()
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, &value)| value * (1.0 + if j % 2 == 0 { theta } else { -theta }))
    .collect();
let dividend = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(perturbed.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Form reversed coefficients xⁿ f(1/x). Dividing reversed p by [1, −c] (1 − cx) gives reversed s
let reversed = |coefficients: &[f64]| {
    ApproxPolynomial::monomial(coefficients.iter().rev().copied().collect())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let forward = dividend.div_rem(&divisor).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let backward = reversed(&perturbed)?
    .div_rem(&reversed(divisor.basis().coefficients())?)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let backward_quotient = reversed(backward.quotient().basis().coefficients())?;

// Independent check: the exact unperturbed quotient is s = [1, …, 1], so |q̂ₖ − 1| gives forward error
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut series = Vec::new();
for (name, result, quotient) in [
    ("From highest degree (divide by x − c)", &forward, forward.quotient()),
    (
        "From constant term (divide reversed coefficients by 1 − cx)",
        &backward,
        &backward_quotient,
    ),
] {
    let errors: Vec<f64> = quotient
        .basis()
        .coefficients()
        .iter()
        .map(|&value| (value - 1.0).abs())
        .collect();
    let relative = errors.iter().sum::<f64>() / ones.len() as f64;
    let condition = result.condition();
    // Omit zero-difference coefficients from the logarithmic plot
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = errors
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .filter(|(_, error)| **error > 0.0)
        .map(|(k, &error)| [k as f64, error])
        .collect();
    rows.push((
        name,
        condition.backward_error(),
        condition.condition_estimate(),
        condition.condition_estimate() * theta,
        relative,
        result.remainder().basis().coefficients()[0].abs(),
        errors.len() - points.len(),
    ));
    series.push(
        serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points, "style": { "symbol": "circle" } }),
    );
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Division direction" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Reconstruction residual η_div [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Sensitivity estimate κ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "κθ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative quotient error ‖q̂ − s‖₁/‖s‖₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Remainder |r̂| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Zero-difference coefficients [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "division" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Quotient coefficient degree k [-]",
        "yLabel": "|q̂ₖ − 1| [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{
            "kind": "line",
            "axis": "y",
            "value": theta,
            "label": "Perturbation θ",
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }]
    })
);
```

With the defaults ($c = 10$, $m = 6$, $\theta = 10^{-12}$), highest-degree-first division has reconstruction residual 0 but relative quotient error $2.9 \times 10^{-7}$, about $3 \times 10^5$ times the perturbation.
Plotted error grows by nearly tenfold per stage, from $10^{-12}$ at $k = 6$ to $1.8 \times 10^{-6}$ at $k = 0$.
The sensitivity estimate is $\kappa = 6.7 \times 10^6$, and $\kappa\theta = 6.7 \times 10^{-6}$ is about 23 times actual error.
Constant-term-first division gives $\kappa = 2.8$ and relative quotient error $8.4 \times 10^{-13}$, staying at perturbation order.
At $c = 0.5$, the relationship reverses: highest-degree-first gives $4.0 \times 10^{-13}$, constant-term-first $2.4 \times 10^{-11}$.
At $m = 14$, highest-degree-first relative quotient error reaches 13, leaving no correct digit, yet reconstruction residual remains $2.9 \times 10^{-18}$.
Reconstruction residual measures only identity consistency; estimate quotient accuracy from $\kappa$ times perturbation magnitude.

### Exact-coefficient division

For polynomial `ExactPolynomial` with exact coefficients (`ExactField`), `div_rem` performs long division using field operations alone, returning quotient and remainder without rounding.
A zero divisor gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Coefficient-type division must satisfy $(a/b) \cdot b = a$.
Long division with a nonmonic divisor checks leading-term elimination at each stage; violating this law stops with `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).
Exact-operation costs count coefficient operations; the user's coefficient type determines coefficient bit-length growth.

## Summary

Read reconstruction residual $\eta_{\text{div}}$ and sensitivity estimate $\kappa$ separately from quotient and remainder, recognizing that even zero residual can accompany a quotient without a correct digit.
When deflating root $c$, if $c$ is smaller in magnitude than remaining roots, use `div_rem` unchanged; if larger, reverse coefficients and divide by $1 - cx$ to avoid error amplification.
Estimate quotient coefficient error from $\kappa$ times relative coefficient error magnitude.
For repeated same-length division, use `PolynomialOperationPlan` and `div_rem_into` without allocation.
For exact coefficients, `ExactPolynomial::div_rem` divides without rounding, returning `Breakdown` only for violations of coefficient-type division laws.
