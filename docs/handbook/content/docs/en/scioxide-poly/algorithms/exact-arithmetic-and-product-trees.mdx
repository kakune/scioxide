Exact coefficient types such as rationals or finite fields incur no rounding error, so algorithm choice depends on operation counts and coefficient growth.
This page explains Karatsuba products and composition over exact rings and multipoint evaluation and interpolation using subproduct trees, binary trees of linear nodal factors.
Floating-point products, composition, Taylor shifts, and their Plans and workspaces are covered in [Fast products, composition, and Taylor shifts](../fast-operations-and-product-trees/).

Coefficients are in ascending-power order; $a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1})$ denotes $a(x)=\sum_ia_ix^i$.
Let $M(n)$ count coefficient multiplications for a product of two polynomials each with $n$ coefficients.
Other notation follows [Shared conventions](oxref:poly-notation).

## Karatsuba over exact rings and field operations

`ExactCoefficient` specifies ring coefficients with exact equality, `+`, `-`, `*`, and negation.
`ExactField` adds division satisfying $(a/b)b=a$ for nonzero divisors to specify field coefficients.
SciOxide implements neither for any type, so floating-point types cannot be passed to exact operations.

Splitting factors as $a=a_0+x^ka_1$ and $b=b_0+x^kb_1$ gives

$$
ab=a_0b_0+x^k\{(a_0+a_1)(b_0+b_1)-a_0b_0-a_1b_1\}+x^{2k}a_1b_1
$$

reducing half-length products from four to three.
The recurrence $T(n)=3T(n/2)+O(n)$ gives $O(n^{\log_23})\approx O(n^{1.585})$ coefficient operations for equal-length products (Karatsuba and Ofman, 1962).
More additions and subtractions can increase floating-point cancellation error, but exact coefficients preserve the value.
Floating-point Plan `PolynomialOperationPlan` products use direct sums or FFT, never Karatsuba.

Exact products `exact_multiply_into`, composition `exact_compose_into`, and Taylor shifts `exact_taylor_shift_into` are functions without Plans and use no floating-point FFT.
Multiplication uses schoolbook below 32 coefficients on the shorter side, otherwise Karatsuba, splitting at half the longer coefficient count rounded upward.
If the shorter side is at most that half-length, split the longer into blocks matching the shorter and multiply blockwise.

Exact composition splits $p$ at $m=\lfloor(\text{coefficient count})/2\rfloor$ as $p=p_{\mathrm{low}}+x^mp_{\mathrm{high}}$ and recursively computes $p_{\mathrm{low}}(q)+q^m\,p_{\mathrm{high}}(q)$.
Each node's $q^m$ is formed by repeated squaring, without sharing between nodes as floating-point Plans do.
Taylor shift $p(x+s)$ composes with $x+s$ without factorials or division, supporting positive-characteristic fields where $k!$ vanishes.
`ExactPolynomial::compose` and `ExactPolynomial::taylor_shift` perform the same computations with workspace allocated per call.

`ExactPolynomialWorkspace::workspace_requirements(capacity)` returns requirements for at most `capacity` output coefficients as `ExactPolynomialWorkspaceRequirements`; 0 gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).
Its `coefficient_len()` combines temporary output retention with recursive intermediate coefficients, at most 7 times `capacity`.
`ExactPolynomialWorkspace::workspace(capacity)` allocates workspace meeting these requirements; the workspace also provides `new`, `try_reserve`, and `check` under the same conventions as approximate workspaces.
Larger-capacity workspace directly serves shorter outputs as well.
All three functions reject empty inputs with `InvalidInput`, wrong output lengths or insufficient workspace with `DimensionMismatch`, and cancellation or deadline with `ExecutionStopped`, preserving output on failure.

SciOxide allocates no memory in operations supplied with workspace.
Coefficient cloning or arithmetic allocations and coefficient bit-length growth lie outside SciOxide's operation counts.
With rationals, fewer multiplications can still increase coefficient digit counts and computation time.

## Product and remainder trees

For nodes $x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}$, a subproduct tree has linear leaves $x-x_j$ and internal nodes equal to products of their two children.
The root node contains the polynomial $P(x)=\prod_j(x-x_j)$.
A remainder tree passes the remainder of polynomial $f$ modulo the root downward, repeatedly reducing each remainder modulo child nodes.
Since $f\equiv f(x_j)\pmod{x-x_j}$ and reduction modulo a node containing factor $x-x_j$ preserves values modulo $x-x_j$, leaf remainders are constants $f(x_j)$.

Interpolation assembles Lagrange's formula

$$
p(x)=\sum_j\frac{y_j}{P'(x_j)}\prod_{k\ne j}(x-x_k),\qquad P'(x_j)=\prod_{k\ne j}(x_j-x_k)
$$

using the tree.
Weights $P'(x_j)$ are computed together by evaluating the root-node polynomial's derivative $P'$ through a remainder tree.
Put $y_j/P'(x_j)$ at leaves; at each node form $r_{\mathrm L}P_{\mathrm R}+r_{\mathrm R}P_{\mathrm L}$ from child results $r_{\mathrm L},r_{\mathrm R}$ and child subproducts $P_{\mathrm L},P_{\mathrm R}$, moving upward to obtain $p$ at the root.
$P'(x_j)=0$ indicates repeated nodes, so interpolation requires distinct nodes.

Remainders use only products if reversed divisor $\operatorname{rev}(b)$ of $b$ is inverted modulo $x^q$ ($q$ is quotient coefficient count).
Newton iteration $g\leftarrow g\,(2-\operatorname{rev}(b)\,g)$ computes the inverse, doubling correct coefficient count each iteration.
Every tree node is monic, making initial value $g=1$ exact.
The quotient reverses $\operatorname{rev}(f)\,g \bmod x^q$ back; the remainder consists of low-order coefficients of $f-(\text{quotient})\,b$.

With $M(n)$ multiplications per product, tree construction takes $O(M(n)\log n)$ multiplications.
Newton remainders also give evaluation and interpolation costs $O(M(n)\log n)$ (von zur Gathen and Gerhard, *Modern Computer Algebra*, Chapters 9 and 10).
FFT products with $M(n)=O(n\log n)$ give total $O(n\log^2n)$, but exact products here use Karatsuba, so $M(n)$ is $O(n^{1.585})$.

Classical monic division requires $(\ell-d)\,d$ multiplications for a remainder of a polynomial with $\ell$ coefficients modulo a degree-$d$ monic polynomial.
Reducing degree-$n-1$ polynomial $f$ at the root passes $f$ unchanged; each of the $2^k$ remainders at level $k$ ($k=1,\ldots,\log_2n$) needs $(n/2^k)^2$ multiplications.
The total $\sum_k2^k(n/2^k)^2=n^2-n$ is nearly the same as the $n^2$ multiplications for Horner evaluation of degree $n-1$ at $n$ points.
Thus without fast products, a subproduct tree need not require fewer multiplications than Horner at practical sizes.
`ExactPolynomialProductTree` uses Newton remainders only when quotient and divisor each have at least 32 coefficients and Newton multiplication counts estimated from Karatsuba products are below classical division counts.

`ExactPolynomialProductTree::new(&points)` builds the tree in original node order without sorting.
Point count is rounded up to a power of 2, with extra leaves filled by constant 1; those leaves incur no operations.
`product()` returns the root polynomial $P$ (1 without points); `points()` returns nodes.
`evaluate(&f)` returns values in node order and permits repeated nodes.
`interpolate(&values)` gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`) for mismatched value/node counts or repeated nodes, and returns the zero polynomial when neither nodes nor values exist.
Each query allocates remainder polynomials and result memory.

Floating-point product and remainder trees are not provided.
Fast floating-point multipoint evaluation can amplify rounding in remainders and be less accurate than pointwise Horner.
Use pointwise `ApproxPolynomial::evaluate` or `evaluate_batch` for approximate multipoint evaluation (Horner costs $O(d)$ per monomial-basis point), and `ApproxPolynomial::interpolate_barycentric` (barycentric) to interpolate samples.

## Multipoint evaluation and interpolation over exact fields

The next cell uses finite field $\mathbb F_P$ modulo prime $P=2^{31}-1$ as its coefficient type and counts coefficient multiplications.
Field elements always fit in 31 bits, so coefficient digits do not grow and multiplication count directly indicates work.
The following definitions block defines type `F` holding elements as `u64` in $0\le x<P$, a counter incremented for every multiplication `*`, and `take_multiplications` reading and resetting it to 0.
Division computes inverses $a^{P-2}$ by Fermat's little theorem using uncounted `u64` operations, so interpolation's $y_j/P'(x_j)$ is excluded from counts.

```rust
//| id: poly-finite-field-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Finite field F_P with multiplication counts (P = 2³¹ − 1)"
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
use scioxide_poly::{ExactCoefficient, ExactField};
use std::ops::{Add, Div, Mul, Neg, Sub};
use std::sync::atomic::{AtomicUsize, Ordering};

/// Prime modulus P = 2³¹ − 1. Products of two elements are below 2⁶² and fit in u64.
pub(crate) const P: u64 = 2_147_483_647;

/// Coefficient multiplication `*` count.
static MULTIPLICATIONS: AtomicUsize = AtomicUsize::new(0);

/// Return multiplication count since the preceding call and reset it to 0.
pub(crate) fn take_multiplications() -> usize {
    MULTIPLICATIONS.swap(0, Ordering::Relaxed)
}

/// Element of F_P, 0 ≤ x < P.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub(crate) struct F(pub(crate) u64);

impl Add for F {
    type Output = Self;
    fn add(self, other: Self) -> Self {
        Self((self.0 + other.0) % P)
    }
}

impl Sub for F {
    type Output = Self;
    fn sub(self, other: Self) -> Self {
        Self((self.0 + P - other.0) % P)
    }
}

impl Mul for F {
    type Output = Self;
    fn mul(self, other: Self) -> Self {
        MULTIPLICATIONS.fetch_add(1, Ordering::Relaxed);
        Self(self.0 * other.0 % P)
    }
}

impl Neg for F {
    type Output = Self;
    fn neg(self) -> Self {
        Self((P - self.0) % P)
    }
}

impl Div for F {
    type Output = Self;
    fn div(self, other: Self) -> Self {
        assert_ne!(other.0, 0, "Finite-field division by zero");
        // By Fermat's little theorem, nonzero a has inverse a^(P−2). Compute with uncounted u64 operations.
        let (mut base, mut exponent, mut inverse) = (other.0, P - 2, 1_u64);
        while exponent > 0 {
            if exponent & 1 == 1 {
                inverse = inverse * base % P;
            }
            base = base * base % P;
            exponent >>= 1;
        }
        Self(self.0 * inverse % P)
    }
}

impl ExactCoefficient for F {
    fn zero() -> Self {
        F(0)
    }
    fn one() -> Self {
        F(1)
    }
    fn from_usize(value: usize) -> Self {
        F(value as u64 % P)
    }
}

impl ExactField for F {}
```

For each node count $n=4,8,\ldots$, the cell evaluates a pseudorandom degree-$n-1$ polynomial at nodes $1,2,\ldots,n$.
Compute the same values with `ExactPolynomial::evaluate` (Horner with counted multiplications) and direct substitution using uncounted `u64` operations, comparing with product-tree values.
Finally, check whether tree interpolation reproduces the original polynomial.
Selecting the control that repeats the last node at its predecessor verifies successful evaluation and failed interpolation.

```rust
//| id: poly-product-tree-evaluation
//| ref: poly-product-tree-evaluation
//| title: "Multipoint evaluation and multiplication counts with product trees"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-finite-field-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   max_points: {"type":"select","label":"Maximum node count n [-]","options":["16","64","256","1024"],"value":"64"}
//|   nodes: {"type":"select","label":"Nodes","options":["Distinct 1, 2, …, n","Last node placed at the preceding node"],"value":"Distinct 1, 2, …, n"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"newton-1024","label":"n ≤ 1024","description":"At n = 1024, Newton handles upper-level remainders, reducing evaluation multiplications below n² − n.","inputs":{"max_points":"1024"}}
//|   - {"id":"repeated-node","label":"Repeat a node","description":"Repeated nodes still evaluate successfully; interpolation detects weight P′(xⱼ) = 0 and returns InvalidInput.","inputs":{"nodes":"Last node placed at the preceding node"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":59}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/4/3","equals":4032}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/4/6","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/4/7","equals":"Matches the original polynomial"}
//|   - case: newton-1024
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/7/3","equals":261632}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/8/3","equals":1023144}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/8/6","equals":0}
//|   - case: repeated-node
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/4/6","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/4/7","equals":"InvalidInput"}
use scioxide_poly::{ExactPolynomial, ExactPolynomialProductTree};

let max_points: usize = max_points
    .parse()
    .map_err(|e: std::num::ParseIntError| e.to_string())?;
let repeated = nodes.starts_with("Last");
let mut state = 1_u64;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut n = 4;
while n <= max_points {
    // Construct degree n − 1 coefficients with a multiplicative congruential generator.
    let coefficients: Vec<F> = (0..n)
        .map(|_| {
            state = state * 48_271 % P;
            F(state)
        })
        .collect();
    let polynomial = ExactPolynomial::new(coefficients.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // For repeated nodes, replace node n by n − 1. Evaluation permits duplicates; interpolation rejects them.
    let points: Vec<F> = (1..=n as u64)
        .map(|x| F(if repeated && x == n as u64 { x - 1 } else { x }))
        .collect();
    take_multiplications();
    let horner: Vec<F> = points.iter().map(|&x| polynomial.evaluate(x)).collect();
    let horner_count = take_multiplications();
    let tree = ExactPolynomialProductTree::new(&points).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let build_count = take_multiplications();
    let values = tree.evaluate(&polynomial).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let evaluation_count = take_multiplications();
    let interpolation = match tree.interpolate(&values) {
        Ok(recovered) if recovered == polynomial => "Matches the original polynomial".to_string(),
        Ok(_) => "Differs from the original polynomial".to_string(),
        Err(e) => format!("{:?}", e.kind()),
    };
    let interpolation_count = take_multiplications();
    // Independent check: direct u64 Horner substitution from coefficients (uncounted).
    let mismatches = points
        .iter()
        .zip(values.iter().zip(&horner))
        .filter(|(x, (tree_value, horner_value))| {
            let direct = coefficients
                .iter()
                .rev()
                .fold(0, |v, c| (v * x.0 + c.0) % P);
            direct != tree_value.0 || tree_value != horner_value
        })
        .count();
    rows.push((
        n,
        horner_count,
        build_count,
        evaluation_count,
        evaluation_count as f64 / horner_count as f64,
        interpolation_count,
        mismatches,
        interpolation,
    ));
    n *= 2;
}
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Node count n [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Horner multiplications [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Tree construction multiplications [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Tree evaluation multiplications [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Evaluation multiplication ratio tree/Horner [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Interpolation multiplications [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Value mismatches [points]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Interpolation result" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "counts" })
);
```

Multiplication counts depend only on point count and node placement, not coefficient values.
The 59 construction multiplications at $n=8$ match schoolbook linear, quadratic, and quartic products $4\cdot2^2+2\cdot3^2+5^2$.
Horner uses $n$ multiplications per point, totaling $n^2$.
Without Newton, tree evaluation at $n\le512$ exactly matches the derived $n^2-n$ count, with Horner ratio $1-1/n$.
At $n=1024$, Newton estimates for upper-level remainders beat classical division, reducing evaluations to 1023144 multiplications (Horner ratio 0.976).
Karatsuba tree construction uses 189312 multiplications at $n=1024$, about 18% of $n^2$.
Interpolation includes derivative evaluation and assembly products, requiring 1401896 multiplications at $n=1024$, about 1.4 times evaluation alone.
With repeated nodes, evaluation still succeeds; interpolation detects weight $P'(x_j)=0$ and returns `InvalidInput`.
This finite-field cell checks operation counts and identities, without examining real rounding errors or conditioning of nearby nodes.

## Summary

Exact products, composition, and Taylor shifts use Karatsuba with at least 32 coefficients on the shorter side ($O(n^{1.585})$ ring operations); with `ExactPolynomialWorkspace` supplied, SciOxide allocates no memory.
Coefficient bit-length growth and coefficient-type allocation are outside operation counts; for rationals, estimate coefficient digit counts separately.
At sizes where Newton iteration is not used, evaluation with `ExactPolynomialProductTree` uses classical division with $n^2-n$ multiplications, almost the same as Horner's $n^2$.
Decide whether to use a subproduct tree by counting multiplications from point counts and degrees as in the table.
Interpolation requires distinct nodes; repeats are detected as `InvalidInput`.
