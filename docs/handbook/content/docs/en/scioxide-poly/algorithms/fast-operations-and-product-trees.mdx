Polynomial coefficient operations include products, composition $p(q(x))$, Taylor shifts $p(x+s)$, and division.
For floating-point coefficients, direct sums and FFT give different rounding behavior for the same product; coefficient cancellation determines accuracy in composition and Taylor shifts.
Repeated same-shape operations can prepare transform tables and workspace once to avoid allocation on every execution.
This page derives their costs and rounding behavior and explains which algorithms `scioxide-poly` selects under which conditions.
Exact-coefficient Karatsuba and product-tree multipoint evaluation and interpolation are covered in [Exact-coefficient operations and product trees](../exact-arithmetic-and-product-trees/).

Coefficients use ascending powers; $a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1})$ denotes $a(x)=\sum_ia_ix^i$.
Let $M(n)$ count coefficient multiplications for a product of two polynomials each with $n$ coefficients.
Here $u$ is unit roundoff and $\varepsilon=2u$ machine epsilon (`EPSILON`); other notation follows [Shared conventions](oxref:poly-notation).

## Direct convolution and FFT

A product $c=ab$ of polynomials with $m$ and $n$ coefficients is coefficient convolution,

$$
c_k=\sum_{i+j=k}a_ib_j,\qquad k=0,\ldots,m+n-2
$$

with $m+n-1$ coefficients.
Direct schoolbook summation needs $mn$ multiplications.

The discrete Fourier transform converts circular convolution into componentwise products.
Padding both coefficient sequences with zeros to length $N\ge m+n-1$ makes circular convolution equal the desired linear convolution, costing $O(N\log N)$.
Insufficient padding wraps high-order coefficients into low orders, producing a different polynomial product.
`scioxide-poly` chooses $N$ as the smallest power of 2 at least $m+n-1$, using real transforms for real coefficients and complex transforms for complex coefficients.

The methods differ in rounding behavior.
For direct sums, each coefficient satisfies

$$
|\hat c_k-c_k|\le\gamma_L\,(|a|*|b|)_k,\qquad \gamma_L=\frac{Lu}{1-Lu}
$$

where $L\le\min(m,n)$ counts summands and $|a|*|b|$ convolves absolute coefficients (a dot-product error bound; Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed., §3.1).
SciOxide combines four partial sums; changing summation order retains the same bound.
Error scales with each coefficient's sum $(|a|*|b|)_k$, allowing relative accuracy even for small coefficients.
FFT error depends on the norm of the entire transform; each coefficient's absolute error is roughly $u\,\|a\|_2\|b\|_2$ times a factor of order $\log_2N$ (ibid., §24.1).
Coefficients many orders of magnitude smaller than others thus lose relative accuracy under the same absolute error magnitude.

`PolynomialOperationPlan` uses FFT only when both conditions below hold; otherwise it uses direct sums.

- The shorter factor has at least 32 real or 16 complex coefficients.
- Every nonzero coefficient (each nonzero real and imaginary component for complex coefficients) has magnitude at least $\sqrt\varepsilon$ times its factor's largest magnitude.

The first condition is an implementation crossover, neither a mathematical threshold nor a speed guarantee.
The second depends on coefficient values, so the path may change per call even for the same shape.
An all-zero factor fails the second condition and uses direct sums.
Each factor is power-of-two scaled before FFT and the result restored; scaling itself introduces no rounding within representable range.
Direct sums use ordinary floating-point arithmetic, retrying only coefficients with intermediate range loss in extended exponent range.
The actual path is returned as `Schoolbook` or `Fft` of `PolynomialProductAlgorithm`.

`polynomial_product_condition` computes $\kappa=\max(1,\|a\|_1\|b\|_1/\|ab\|_1)$ from the completed product to indicate cancellation.
Since $\||a|*|b|\|_1=\|a\|_1\|b\|_1$, $\kappa$ also measures the 1-norm ratio of the direct-sum error bound to result magnitude.
Reported backward error replaces $u$ in $\gamma_L$ with machine epsilon $\varepsilon$ and $L$ with product coefficient count, giving $\gamma_{m+n-1}=(m+n-1)\varepsilon/(1-(m+n-1)\varepsilon)$; forward-error estimates multiply this by $\kappa$.
If all coefficients cancel to a zero product, $\kappa$ is positive infinity (a zero factor also makes the product zero, but gives $\kappa=1$).
Empty factors give `InvalidInput` (`Empty`), product length other than $m+n-1$ gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`), and non-finite coefficients give `NonFinite`.
Cost is proportional to coefficient count, without allocation.
Both estimates are first-order rounding-model indicators, not upper bounds on computed-product forward error.

## Input shapes and storage

`PolynomialOperationRequest` declares exact input lengths per operation.

- `Multiply { left_len, right_len }` declares products, treating exchanged factors as the same shape.
- `Compose { outer_len, inner_len }` declares composition $p(q(x))$.
- `TaylorShift { len }` declares Taylor shifts.
- `Divide { dividend_len, divisor_len }` declares division `div_rem_into`; quotient and remainder conventions appear in [Long-division backward error](oxref:poly-division-reconstruction-residual).

All lengths must be positive; 0 gives `InvalidInput` (`Empty`) at preparation.
A larger declared shape does not prepare smaller shapes; declare every shape used.
`PolynomialOperationPlan::new(&requests)` deduplicates shapes and builds FFT tables only for lengths reachable from declarations.
`build(&requests, options, context)` prepares while retaining `PolynomialExecutionOptions` with execution policy and worker limit.
Defaults are sequential execution and limit 64; a larger limit gives `InvalidInput` during preparation.
The preparation `ExecutionContext` is not retained; pass `ExecutionContext` last on every execution.

`workspace_requirements(&requests)` returns memory requirements for a subset of declared shapes in `PolynomialWorkspaceRequirements`.
It counts coefficients `coefficient_len()`, FFT complex values `fft_len()`, real samples `real_len()`, and extended-exponent values `scaled_len()`; `required_bytes()` returns total bytes.
`scaled_len()` is dividend plus divisor lengths for division, twice output length for composition and Taylor-shift [retries below](#composition-and-taylor-shifts), and 0 for products.
Operations execute individually, so requirements across shapes use per-kind maxima rather than sums.
Querying undeclared shapes gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).

Workspace `PolynomialWorkspace` is caller-owned.
`PolynomialWorkspace::new()` allocates nothing; `try_reserve(&requirements)` grows only insufficient storage; `check(&requirements)` returns `Sufficient` or `NeedsGrowth` without allocation.
Reservations never shrink, so workspace for the largest Plan directly serves smaller Plans.
`plan.workspace(&requests)` creates and reserves a new workspace.

Execution methods `multiply_into`, `compose_into`, `taylor_shift_into`, and `div_rem_into` do not grow workspace.
Insufficient workspace or wrong output length gives `DimensionMismatch`; undeclared shapes `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`); non-finite coefficients `NonFinite`; result coefficient overflow `NumericalInstability`; cancellation or deadline `ExecutionStopped`.
Results are built in workspace and copied only after every check passes, preserving output on failure.
Workspace contents may change on success or failure.

## Checking product paths and errors

The next cell multiplies two factors with coefficient counts $n=16,31,32,64$ using one `PolynomialOperationPlan`, tabulating actual paths and coefficientwise errors.
It declares four shapes in one Plan and reuses one workspace reserved to their maximum requirements.
Coefficients are nonzero integers from $-8$ to $8$; direct integer convolution in `i64` gives exact products.
Replacing $a_0$ by $2^{-k}$ through the control makes $c_0=2^{-k}b_0$ a coefficient consisting only of a small product.
$2^{-k}$ times integers is exact in `f64`, allowing independent exact products after replacement as well.
The table's coefficientwise relative error is the maximum $|\hat c_k-c_k|/(|a|*|b|)_k$, compared with direct-sum error bound $\gamma_L$.

```rust
//| id: poly-prepared-product-tree
//| ref: poly-prepared-product-tree
//| title: "Product-path selection and coefficientwise error"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   tiny_exponent: {"type":"select","label":"Exponent k in a₀ = 2⁻ᵏ","options":["No replacement","20","30"],"value":"No replacement"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"tiny-20","label":"a₀ = 2⁻²⁰","description":"The largest-coefficient ratio 2⁻²³ is at least √ε, retaining FFT and increasing relative error in c₀.","inputs":{"tiny_exponent":"20"}}
//|   - {"id":"tiny-30","label":"a₀ = 2⁻³⁰","description":"Ratio 2⁻³³ is below √ε, switching back to direct sums even for n ≥ 32.","inputs":{"tiny_exponent":"30"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"Schoolbook"}
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":"Fft"}
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/3/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"min":1,"max":2}}
//|   - case: tiny-20
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":"Fft"}
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"min":1e-10,"max":1e-6}}
//|   - case: tiny-30
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":"Schoolbook"}
//|       - {"artifact":"paths","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-16}}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_poly::{
    PolynomialOperationPlan, PolynomialOperationRequest, PolynomialProductAlgorithm,
    PolynomialWorkspaceRequirements, polynomial_product_condition,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });

// Prepare lengths on both sides of the FFT crossover (shorter side 32) in one Plan.
let lengths = [16_usize, 31, 32, 64];
let requests = lengths.map(|n| PolynomialOperationRequest::Multiply {
    left_len: n,
    right_len: n,
});
let plan = PolynomialOperationPlan::<f64>::new(&requests).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let requirements: PolynomialWorkspaceRequirements<f64> = plan
    .workspace_requirements(plan.requests())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// One workspace meets the maximum requirements of all four shapes.
let mut workspace = plan.workspace(plan.requests()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tiny = match tiny_exponent.as_str() {
    "No replacement" => None,
    k => Some(1.0 / (1_u64 << k.parse::<u32>().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?) as f64),
};
// Deterministically construct nonzero integer coefficients in -8..=8.
let integer = |i: usize, salt: usize| {
    let value = ((i * 37 + salt * 11 + 5) % 16) as i64 - 8;
    if value == 0 { 1 } else { value }
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for n in lengths {
    let mut a_int: Vec<i64> = (0..n).map(|i| integer(i, 1)).collect();
    let b_int: Vec<i64> = (0..n).map(|i| integer(i, 2)).collect();
    let mut a: Vec<f64> = a_int.iter().map(|&v| v as f64).collect();
    let b: Vec<f64> = b_int.iter().map(|&v| v as f64).collect();
    if let Some(t) = tiny {
        // Replace a₀ by 2⁻ᵏ and omit it from integer-part convolution.
        a[0] = t;
        a_int[0] = 0;
    }
    let mut product = vec![0.0; 2 * n - 1];
    let algorithm: PolynomialProductAlgorithm = plan
        .multiply_into(
            &a,
            &b,
            &mut product,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Independent check: integer-part direct convolution in i64 and contribution 2⁻ᵏ b_k from a₀ = 2⁻ᵏ (both exact in f64).
    let (mut worst, mut first) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for k in 0..2 * n - 1 {
        let (mut exact, mut magnitude) = (0_i64, 0_i64);
        for i in k.saturating_sub(n - 1)..=k.min(n - 1) {
            exact += a_int[i] * b_int[k - i];
            magnitude += (a_int[i] * b_int[k - i]).abs();
        }
        let small = tiny.map_or(0.0, |t| if k < n { t * b[k] } else { 0.0 });
        let error =
            (product[k] - (exact as f64 + small)).abs() / (magnitude as f64 + small.abs());
        worst = worst.max(error);
        if k == 0 {
            first = error;
        }
    }
    let report = polynomial_product_condition(&a, &b, &product).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        n,
        format!("{algorithm:?}"),
        worst,
        first,
        report.condition_estimate(),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Coefficient count n [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Actual path" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum coefficientwise relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative error in c₀ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Condition estimate κ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "paths" })
);
emit_text!(&format!(
    "Workspace requirements: {} coefficients, {} FFT complex values, {} real values.",
    requirements.coefficient_len(),
    requirements.fft_len(),
    requirements.real_len()
));
```

With default inputs, $n=16,31$ uses direct sums with zero error; $n=32,64$ uses FFT with maximum coefficientwise relative errors $1.4\times10^{-14}$ and $4.7\times10^{-15}$.
Direct-sum error is 0 because every integer product and sum is exact in `f64`; general coefficients retain error around $\gamma_L$.
At $k=20$, ratio $2^{-23}$ to largest coefficient 8 is at least $\sqrt\varepsilon=2^{-26}$, using FFT; relative error in $c_0$ rises to $2.5\times10^{-9}$ ($n=32$) and $1.5\times10^{-8}$ ($n=64$).
At $k=30$, ratio $2^{-33}$ falls below $\sqrt\varepsilon$, returning to direct sums even for $n\ge32$ and restoring zero error.
The condition estimate $\kappa$ stays below 2 in all cases, indicating little cancellation.
The increased error at $k=20$ comes from normwise FFT error large relative to small coefficient $c_0$, rather than cancellation.
Workspace requirements are $2\cdot64-1=127$ coefficients for the largest shape and a length-128 transform (259 complex and 128 real values), not a sum over four shapes.

## Composition and Taylor shifts

For $p$ of degree $d$ and $q$ of degree $e$, composition $p(q(x))$ has degree at most $de$ and output includes $de+1$ coefficients, retaining high-order zeros.
Direct polynomial Horner $p(q)=(\cdots(p_dq+p_{d-1})q+\cdots)q+p_0$ repeats products of growing polynomials with $q$ $d$ times, requiring $O(d^2e^2)$ multiplications.

Divide-and-conquer splits $p=p_{\mathrm{low}}+x^mp_{\mathrm{high}}$ and computes

$$
p(q)=p_{\mathrm{low}}(q)+q^m\,p_{\mathrm{high}}(q)
$$

recursively.
Approximate Plans choose $m$ as the largest power of 2 below the coefficient count of $p$, preparing $q^2,q^4,q^8,\ldots$ once and sharing them throughout recursion.
Fast products give cost $O(M(de)\log d)$.
`compose_into` chooses the [product path above](#direct-convolution-and-fft) (direct sums or FFT) for powers of $q$ and products at each recursive node.

When intermediate product coefficients become 0, subnormal, or out of range within product support (degrees where nonzero coefficients can occur), `compose_into` retries the entire composition.
A structural zero cannot be distinguished from a term lost to rounding, and later products could amplify that lost term.
Inner polynomials such as $q=x^2+1$ with zero coefficients within the support of $q^2$ always trigger this retry.
The retry performs coefficient Horner $c\leftarrow c\,q+p_i$ in extended exponent range and rounds final coefficients only once.
This path costs $O(d\,N\,(e+1))$ time ($N=de+1$ is output coefficient count) and twice output length in extended-exponent values.
That memory is included in Plan requirements `scaled_len()`, so workspace need not grow.
The return value does not identify which path was taken.

Taylor shift $p(x+s)$ is composition with $q(x)=s+x$.
The textbook expression $p(x+s)=\sum_kp^{(k)}(s)x^k/k!$ overflows factorials at high order ($171!$ exceeds `f64` range) and incurs cancellation in derivative sums.
`taylor_shift_into` computes composition without factorials.
Taylor shifting translates the variable within the same monomial basis; it is not basis conversion.

`polynomial_composition_condition` returns the ratio of $\sum_i|p_i|\,\|q\|_1^i$, Horner evaluation of absolute coefficients at $\|q\|_1$, to the composed result's 1-norm as $\kappa$ (raised to 1 if smaller).
Since $\|q^i\|_1\le\|q\|_1^i$, the sum of $\|q\|_1^i$ bounds the uncancelled result's 1-norm, making the ratio an indicator of shrinkage through cancellation.
Relative coefficient perturbations of $p$ at most $u$ change resulting coefficients by at most $u\sum_i|p_i|\,\|q\|_1^i$ in 1-norm, so $u\kappa$ also bounds relative result changes.
For Taylor shifts, $\|q\|_1=1+|s|$, giving indicator $\sum_i|p_i|(1+|s|)^i/\|p(x+s)\|_1$, which grows with $|s|$.
The forward-error estimate is $\gamma_N\kappa$ ($N$ is composed coefficient count).
This ratio is also a first-order indicator, not an error guarantee.
Cost is proportional to coefficient count, without allocation.

The next cell Taylor-shifts $(x-3)^n/3$ and $(x+3)^n/3$ by $s=3$ and compares coefficient errors with $\kappa$ ($n=4,8,\ldots,24$).
Inputs round coefficients $\binom{n}{i}(\mp3)^{n-i}/3$ to `f64`; the reference is the shift of those rounded inputs themselves.
The first shift is close to $x^n/3$; terms of magnitude about $\binom{n}{i}3^{n-i}4^i/3$ cancel to a small result, giving $\kappa\approx\sum_i\binom{n}{i}3^{n-i}4^i=7^n$.
The second is close to $(x+6)^n/3$ with no cancellation, giving $\kappa=1$.
The independent check uses that every input coefficient is at least $1/3$ and becomes an integer when multiplied by $2^{54}$.
Compute shifted coefficients $c_k=\sum_{i\ge k}p_i\binom{i}{k}3^{i-k}$ exactly in `i128` as integers scaled by $2^{54}$, then round to `f64` once.
Observed error is $\|\hat c-c\|_1/\|c\|_1$; because the reference is rounded once, differences around $u$ lie within the check's resolution.
One Plan declares six degree shapes, computing them sequentially with one workspace.

```rust
//| id: poly-taylor-shift-condition
//| ref: poly-taylor-shift-condition
//| title: "Cancellation and condition estimates in Taylor shifts"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   polynomial: {"type":"select","label":"Polynomial to shift (s = 3)","options":["(x − 3)ⁿ/3 (result ≈ xⁿ/3)","(x + 3)ⁿ/3 (result ≈ (x + 6)ⁿ/3)"],"value":"(x − 3)ⁿ/3 (result ≈ xⁿ/3)"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"no-cancellation","label":"(x + 3)ⁿ/3","description":"No cancellation gives κ = 1, keeping error at unit-roundoff level.","inputs":{"polynomial":"(x + 3)ⁿ/3 (result ≈ (x + 6)ⁿ/3)"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shift","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2401,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shift","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shift","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"min":1e-12}}
//|   - case: no-cancellation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shift","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":1,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shift","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_poly::{
    PolynomialOperationPlan, PolynomialOperationRequest, polynomial_composition_condition,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sign: i128 = if polynomial.starts_with("(x − 3)") {
    -1
} else {
    1
};
let degrees = [4_usize, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24];
let requests = degrees.map(|n| PolynomialOperationRequest::TaylorShift { len: n + 1 });
let plan = PolynomialOperationPlan::<f64>::new(&requests).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = plan.workspace(plan.requests()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Construct Pascal's triangle C(i, k) (i ≤ 24) with integers.
let mut binomial: Vec<Vec<i128>> = vec![vec![1]];
for i in 1..=24 {
    let previous = &binomial[i - 1];
    let row = (0..=i)
        .map(|k| {
            if k == 0 || k == i {
                1
            } else {
                previous[k - 1] + previous[k]
            }
        })
        .collect();
    binomial.push(row);
}
// Multiplying by 2⁵⁴ makes every f64 of magnitude at least 1/4 an integer.
let frame = (1_i128 << 54) as f64;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for n in degrees {
    // Use coefficients C(n, i)(∓3)ⁿ⁻ⁱ/3 rounded to f64 as input (all at least 1/3).
    let p: Vec<f64> = (0..=n)
        .map(|i| (binomial[n][i] * (3 * sign).pow((n - i) as u32)) as f64 / 3.0)
        .collect();
    let mut shifted = vec![0.0; n + 1];
    let context = ExecutionContext::sequential();
    plan.taylor_shift_into(&p, 3.0, &mut shifted, &mut workspace, context)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let report = polynomial_composition_condition(&p, &[3.0, 1.0], &shifted)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Independent check: compute shifted rounded inputs pᵢ, cₖ = Σᵢ pᵢ C(i, k) 3ⁱ⁻ᵏ,
    // exactly in i128 as integers scaled by 2⁵⁴, then round to f64 once.
    let scaled: Vec<i128> = p.iter().map(|&c| (c * frame) as i128).collect();
    let exact: Vec<f64> = (0..=n)
        .map(|k| {
            let sum: i128 = (k..=n)
                .map(|i| scaled[i] * binomial[i][k] * 3_i128.pow((i - k) as u32))
                .sum();
            sum as f64 / frame
        })
        .collect();
    let error: f64 = shifted.iter().zip(&exact).map(|(c, e)| (c - e).abs()).sum();
    let norm: f64 = exact.iter().map(|e| e.abs()).sum();
    rows.push((
        n,
        report.condition_estimate(),
        report.forward_error_estimate(),
        error / norm,
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Degree n [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Condition estimate κ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Forward-error estimate γκ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Observed relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "shift" })
);
```

For default $(x-3)^n/3$, $\kappa$ matches $7^n$ (2401 at $n=4$, $1.9\times10^{20}$ at $n=24$); forward estimate $\gamma_{n+1}\kappa$ is $0.13$ at $n=16$ and $1.1\times10^{6}$ at $n=24$.
Observed relative error also grows with $n$, reaching $2.4\times10^{-7}$ at $n=16$ and $1.5\times10^{-2}$ at $n=24$.
At every $n$, observed error is roughly $u\,4^n/2$, many orders of magnitude below the estimate.
$u\kappa$ is worst-case amplification of input coefficient perturbations; actual rounding errors need not align in that worst direction.
Even so, at $n=24$ only about two significant digits remain in result coefficients.
For $(x+3)^n/3$, $\kappa=1$; observed error is 0 for $n\le20$ and only $4\times10^{-18}$ at $n=24$.
When $\kappa u$ is near 1, evaluate $p(x+s)$ directly without forming shifted coefficients, or use coefficients expressed in powers of $x-s$ from the start.

## Summary

`PolynomialOperationPlan` uses FFT only for products with at least 32 real (16 complex) coefficients on the shorter side and every nonzero coefficient at least $\sqrt\varepsilon$ times its factor's largest; returns the path through `PolynomialProductAlgorithm`.
Products needing coefficientwise relative accuracy switch to direct sums when small coefficients are present; read the path alongside `polynomial_product_condition` estimates.
For composition and Taylor shifts, assess cancellation through $\kappa$ of `polynomial_composition_condition`; do not trust result coefficients when $\kappa u$ approaches 1.
Declare every repeated-operation shape in `PolynomialOperationRequest` and reuse `PolynomialWorkspace` preallocated for maximum requirements to avoid execution allocation (including extended-exponent composition retries).
