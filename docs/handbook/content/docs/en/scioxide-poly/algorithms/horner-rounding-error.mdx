Errors in floating-point polynomial values can be interpreted as small coefficient perturbations.
This page defines the evaluation condition number, derives Horner backward- and forward-error bounds, and checks that SciOxide's arithmetic paths satisfy their assumptions.
It then distinguishes estimates reported by evaluation results from backward errors in checked evaluations of root candidates, comparing them under cancellation near a root.
Horner's derivation and stored-coordinate conventions are covered in [Polynomial representations and Horner's method](../representation-and-horner/).

## Backward and forward errors

When a computed value $\widehat p(x)$ can be interpreted as the exact value of a polynomial with coefficients changed to $a_k(1+\theta_k)$, the smallest $\max_k|\theta_k|$ among such $\theta$ is called the componentwise backward error.
By contrast, $|\widehat p(x)-p(x)|/|p(x)|$ is called relative forward error.

Adding componentwise relative perturbations $|\Delta a_k|\le\eta|a_k|$ to the coefficients bounds the value change by

$$
\Bigl|\sum_k\Delta a_kx^k\Bigr|\le\eta\sum_k|a_k||x|^k
$$

with equality for the choice $\Delta a_k=\eta\,|a_k|\,\mathrm{sign}(a_kx^k)\cdot\mathrm{sign}(p(x))$.
The relative condition number of the value under this perturbation model is therefore:

$$
\kappa(p,x)=\frac{\sum_k|a_k||x|^k}{|p(x)|}
$$

The numerator $\sum_k|a_k||x|^k$ is called the evaluation envelope.
Reversing the same argument gives componentwise backward error $|y-p(x)|/\sum_k|a_k||x|^k$ for any approximate value $y$ (Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, Chapter 5).
When $p(x)=0$, relative error is unavailable as a measure; inspect absolute error and residual instead.
A large condition number indicates sensitivity to input perturbations, not that a particular computed result must be inaccurate.

### Rounding-error bounds

In the standard rounding model, basic arithmetic operations without overflow or underflow satisfy $\mathrm{fl}(a\circ b)=(a\circ b)(1+\delta)$ and $|\delta|\le u$.
Here $u$ is unit roundoff, equal to $u=2^{-53}=\varepsilon/2$ for binary64 with round-to-nearest.
Horner's method with separately rounded multiplication and addition gives

$$
\widehat b_k=\bigl(x\,\widehat b_{k+1}(1+\delta_k)+a_k\bigr)(1+\delta_k')
$$

so $a_kx^k$ receives $2k+1$ factors $(1+\delta)$ when $k<n$, and $a_nx^n$ receives $2n$ factors.
A product of $m$ such factors can be combined into $1+\theta_m$ satisfying $|\theta_m|\le\gamma_m$ when $mu<1$.
Consequently,

$$
\widehat p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k(1+\theta^{(k)})x^k,\quad |\theta^{(k)}|\le\gamma_{2n},
\qquad
|\widehat p(x)-p(x)|\le\gamma_{2n}\sum_k|a_k||x|^k,
\qquad \gamma_j=\frac{ju}{1-ju}
$$

follows.
Thus Horner's componentwise backward error is at most $\gamma_{2n}(u)$, and its relative forward error is at most $\gamma_{2n}(u)\,\kappa(p,x)$.
Using fused multiply-add (FMA), which rounds a product and sum only once, reduces $\gamma_{2n}$ to $\gamma_n$ by the same argument.
This proves a bound for arithmetic satisfying the assumptions, treating coefficients and the evaluation point as exact values.

### SciOxide arithmetic paths and theorem assumptions

SciOxide's monomial evaluation uses both the textbook single-stage recurrence and rearranged recurrences that shorten arithmetic dependency chains.
For binary64, when each component of the evaluation point is zero or has magnitude between $2^{-16}$ and $2^{16}$ inclusive, it uses the quadratic Horner recurrence grouping coefficients in pairs:

$$
v\leftarrow v\,x^2+(a_{k+1}x+a_k)
$$

In addition, `evaluate` and `evaluate_with_derivative` compute both recurrences of the even–odd decomposition $p(x)=E(x^2)+x\,O(x^2)$ simultaneously when all real binary64 coefficients are zero or have magnitude between $2^{-64}$ and $2^{16}$ inclusive, the coefficient count is even and at least 4, and $|x|$ is zero or between $2^{-8}$ and $2^{8}$ inclusive.
These ranges are implementation values; points outside them and binary32 use the single-stage recurrence.
Products and sums are rounded once on platforms with fused multiply-add instructions (AArch64, and x86 and x86_64 with feature `fma` enabled), and twice, separately for multiplication and addition, elsewhere (including wasm32 and default x86_64).
Neither compensated summation nor double-precision intermediate values are used.

Rearranged paths yield bounds of the same form by counting rounding operations applied to each term $a_kx^k$.
Counting the rounding of $x^2$ once, the highest-degree term in the quadratic recurrence without FMA receives the most roundings, $\lfloor 3n/2\rfloor+1$; even–odd decomposition receives no more, while both use $n$ with FMA.
No path exceeds $2n$, so the bound using $\gamma_{2n}(u)$ above still holds at real points.

The absence of overflow and underflow is checked during evaluation; an intermediate value outside the allowed range triggers a complete reevaluation.
For binary64 with all coefficients in the range above (zero or magnitude between $2^{-64}$ and $2^{16}$ inclusive) and a real evaluation point zero or of magnitude between $2^{-16}$ and $2^{16}$ inclusive, evaluation is first retried with ordinary arithmetic rescaled by powers of two; further range failures use extended-exponent arithmetic.
Other cases retry immediately with extended-exponent arithmetic.
Rescaling by powers of two introduces no rounding.
Extended-exponent arithmetic rounds multiplication and addition separately, corresponding to the non-FMA counts.
The assumptions are maintained except when the final conversion to the ordinary type yields a subnormal number.
At complex points, a complex multiplication involves multiple roundings, so this real bound cannot be applied directly.

### Reported estimates

`backward_error()` in the `PolynomialConditionReport` returned by `evaluate_with_derivative` is $\gamma_N(\varepsilon)=N\varepsilon/(1-N\varepsilon)$ based on coordinate count $N$ ($N=n+1$ for monomials) and `EPSILON`.
Because $\varepsilon=2u$, this equals $\gamma_{2n+2}(u)$, slightly larger than the theorem's $\gamma_{2n}(u)$ above.
`condition_estimate()` divides the computed envelope by computed value $|\widehat p(x)|$, raising results below 1 to 1.
A zero envelope gives 1; a positive envelope with a zero value gives positive infinity.
`forward_error_estimate()` is the product of $\gamma_N(\varepsilon)$ and `condition_estimate()` and indicates relative forward error.
`backward_error()` itself is not multiplied by the condition number.

Because the denominator is the computed value, cancellation that destroys digits in $\widehat p(x)$ itself can lead to underestimation of the true relative error.
None of the three quantities is a certified upper bound.
`coefficient_scale()` is the largest absolute stored coordinate, not the absolute error in the value.
Positive infinity indicates unbounded relative diagnostics; it does not permit nonfinite values or derivatives.
Other operations returning the same type use different scales.
Basis conversion estimates the condition number from the ratio of largest absolute stored coordinates, while division reports a measured reconstruction residual $\|a-(qb+r)\|_1/(\|a\|_1+\|q\|_1\|b\|_1+\|r\|_1)$ as backward error rather than a model.

## Checked evaluation and backward error of root candidates

Root-finding iterations must determine whether an evaluated value can be distinguished from zero.
`checked_basis_evaluation(coefficients, z, basis)` accepts real or complex coefficients in the monomial basis (`Monomial`) or a prepared orthogonal basis (`Orthogonal`) specified by `PolynomialRootBasisRef`.
It computes the value at complex point $z$ and first and second derivatives with respect to physical variable $x$ using $O(N)$ arithmetic.
Monomials use Horner's method and orthogonal expansions use Clenshaw's algorithm; overflow or underflow in ordinary arithmetic triggers complete reevaluation with extended-exponent arithmetic.
It performs no allocation.

The resulting `CheckedBasisEvaluation` distinguishes values rounded to the ordinary type (`value()`, `derivative()`, `second_derivative()`) from values retaining extended exponents (such as `scaled_value()`).
The former return `NumericalInstability` if the value does not fit the ordinary type.
`newton_correction()` forms Newton correction $p(z)/p'(z)$ with extended exponents, returning a finite correction even when the value and derivative do not fit the ordinary type; it returns `None` when $p'(z)=0$.
`root_condition()` returns the absolute condition number $e(z)/|p'(z)|$ and relative condition number $e(z)/(|z||p'(z)|)$ of $z$ as a root ($e(z)$ is the envelope below).

`backward_error_model()` identifies the backward-error perturbation model through `PolynomialBackwardErrorModel`.
For monomials it is `Componentwise` (move each coefficient $a_k$ by a multiple of $|a_k|$, leaving zero coefficients unchanged); for orthogonal bases it is `Normwise` (move each coefficient by a multiple of $\max_k|a_k|$).
`normalized_residual()` is

$$
\eta(z)=\frac{|\widehat p(z)|}{e(z)},\qquad
e(z)=\begin{cases}\sum_k|a_k||z|^k & (\texttt{Componentwise})\\ \max_k|a_k|\sum_k|\phi_k(z)| & (\texttt{Normwise})\end{cases}
$$

and represents the smallest relative coefficient perturbation making $z$ an exact root under each model: the root candidate's backward error.
This is the previous section's backward-error formula with approximate value $y=0$.
For the zero polynomial, $e(z)=0$, so $|\widehat p(z)|$ itself is returned.

`roundoff_limited()` reports whether $\eta$ is at most the evaluation's own rounding allowance, meaning floating-point arithmetic cannot distinguish $p(z)$ from zero.
The allowance is $\gamma_{8N}(\varepsilon)$ for monomials and $\gamma_{32N}(\varepsilon)$ for orthogonal bases.
The 8 counts real roundings in a complex Horner stage (six in multiplication and two in addition); 32 for orthogonal bases allows extra recurrence and envelope stages.
Extended-exponent evaluations add $8N\varepsilon_{\min}/e(z)$ ($32N\varepsilon_{\min}/e(z)$ for orthogonal bases, where $\varepsilon_{\min}$ is the smallest subnormal) to the allowance for gradual underflow.
`residual_with_roundoff()` is the sum of $\eta$ and the allowance, a quantity for conservatively comparing root candidates.
$\eta$ is a measured quantity distinct from the evaluation result's `backward_error()` (the model $\gamma_N(\varepsilon)$).
All are estimates, not proofs that a root exists.

Empty coefficient sequences yield `InvalidInput` (`Empty`), nonfinite coefficients or points yield `NonFinite`, and more orthogonal coefficients than the prepared degree permits yield `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).
Degrees for which $8N\varepsilon\ge1$ ($32N\varepsilon\ge1$ for orthogonal bases) prevents forming the allowance yield `NumericalInstability` (`IllConditioned`).

## Cancellation near roots

Store $p(x)=(x-1)^m$ as monomial coefficients $a_k=\binom mk(-1)^{m-k}$ and evaluate near root $x=1$.
Near the root, true value $|x-1|^m$ decreases while envelope $\sum_k|a_k||x|^k=(1+|x|)^m\approx2^m$ barely changes, making condition number $\kappa(p,x)=(1+|x|)^m/|x-1|^m$ large.
Independent checks use the factored value $(x-1)^m$ and this envelope's closed form.
For $|x-1|\le1/2$, Sterbenz's lemma makes subtraction $x-1$ exact, so the factored reference's relative error is at most $\gamma_{m-1}(u)$, much smaller than the observed errors.

The following definitions block supplies coefficients and reference functions shared by two cells.
`shifted_power_coefficients(m)` returns $a_k$, and `power(base, m)` returns $\mathrm{base}^m$ using $m$ f64 multiplications (reference values rather than SciOxide functions).

```rust
//| id: poly-horner-binomial-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Coefficients and reference values for (x − 1)^m"
//| crates: []
/// Ascending-power coefficients a_k = C(m, k)(−1)^(m−k) of (x − 1)^m. Exactly representable as integers for m ≤ 16.
pub(crate) fn shifted_power_coefficients(m: usize) -> Vec<f64> {
    let mut coefficients = Vec::with_capacity(m + 1);
    let mut coefficient = if m % 2 == 0 { 1.0_f64 } else { -1.0 };
    for k in 0..=m {
        coefficients.push(coefficient);
        // Multiplication before division keeps each stage exactly equal to an integer binomial coefficient.
        coefficient = -coefficient * (m - k) as f64 / (k + 1) as f64;
    }
    coefficients
}

/// Reference base^m from m multiplications (f64 arithmetic).
pub(crate) fn power(base: f64, m: usize) -> f64 {
    (0..m).fold(1.0_f64, |acc, _| acc * base)
}
```

### Observed errors and bounds over an interval

The first cell calls `evaluate` at 400 points in an interval of half-width $\delta$ centered on the root, comparing observed errors $|\widehat p(x)-p(x)|$ against theoretical bound $\gamma_{2m}(u)(1+|x|)^m$.
Inputs are degree $m$ and half-width $\delta$.
Evaluation points are chosen to avoid short binary fractions.
At short binary fractions such as $x=1+2^{-6}$, every expanded Horner stage may be exact with zero error, preventing observation of cancellation.
The table shows the largest observed-error-to-bound ratio and the count of points with relative error at least 1.
The chart plots observed errors (markers), theoretical bounds (dashed), and true-value magnitudes $|x-1|^m$ (dotted) on a logarithmic axis; points with exactly zero observed error are omitted and their count displayed in the table.

```rust
//| id: poly-horner-backward-error
//| ref: poly-horner-backward-error
//| title: "Cancellation near roots and error bounds"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| uses: [poly-horner-binomial-model]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree m (p(x) = (x − 1)^m)","description":"Larger values make the value smaller near the root.","min":2,"max":16,"step":1,"value":6}
//|   half_width: {"type":"select","label":"Evaluation-interval half-width δ [-]","options":["0.1","0.01","0.001"],"value":"0.01"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"high-degree","label":"m = 16","description":"All 400 points have relative errors at least 1, but the bound holds.","inputs":{"degree":16}}
//|   - {"id":"wide-interval","label":"δ = 0.1","description":"Points farther from the root have small relative errors, and some have zero error.","inputs":{"half_width":"0.1"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":100,"max":160}}
//|   - case: high-degree
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":400}
//|   - case: wide-interval
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":40}}
use scioxide_poly::ApproxPolynomial;

let m = degree.round() as usize;
let delta: f64 = half_width.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot interpret half-width")?;
let p = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(shifted_power_coefficients(m)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Theoretical-bound coefficient γ_(2m)(u). u = ε/2 is binary64 unit roundoff.
let u = f64::EPSILON / 2.0;
let gamma = 2.0 * m as f64 * u / (1.0 - 2.0 * m as f64 * u);
let (mut errors, mut bounds, mut values) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut worst_ratio, mut lost, mut exact_points) = (0.0_f64, 0_u32, 0_u32);
for j in 0..400 {
    // 400 symmetric points excluding zero. Sterbenz's lemma makes x − 1 exact.
    let x = 1.0 + delta * ((2 * j + 1) as f64 / 400.0 - 1.0);
    let computed = p.evaluate(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Independent check: factored form (x − 1)^m and envelope Σ|a_k||x|^k = (1 + |x|)^m.
    let reference = power(x - 1.0, m);
    let bound = gamma * power(1.0 + x.abs(), m);
    let error = (computed - reference).abs();
    worst_ratio = worst_ratio.max(error / bound);
    lost += u32::from(error >= reference.abs());
    if error > 0.0 {
        errors.push([x, error]);
    } else {
        exact_points += 1;
    }
    bounds.push([x, bound]);
    values.push([x, reference.abs()]);
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Evaluation points [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum observed error / bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Points with relative error ≥ 1 [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Zero-error points omitted from plot [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(400_u32, worst_ratio, lost, exact_points)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Evaluation point x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute value [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "layers": [
            { "id": "error", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Observed error |p̂(x) − p(x)|", "points": errors },
            {
                "id": "bound",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Bound γ_(2m)(u) Σ|a_k||x|^k",
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed" },
                "points": bounds
            },
            {
                "id": "value",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "|p(x)| = |x − 1|^m",
                "style": { "lineType": "dotted" },
                "points": values
            }
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "cancellation" })
);
```

At defaults $m=6$ and $\delta=0.01$, observed errors are at most approximately 5% of the bound.
Yet at 128 points where $|x-1|$ is approximately $3.2\times10^{-3}$ or smaller, observed errors are at least $|p(x)|$, so not even one relative digit is correct.
The bound is proportional to envelope $(1+|x|)^m\approx2^m$, barely changing near the root, while only the true value shrinks as the $m$th power.
With $m=16$, every one of the 400 points has relative error at least 1, but the error-to-bound ratio is 0.014, consistent with the theorem.
At $\delta=0.1$, only 12 points have relative error at least 1, and four have exactly zero error.
This experiment observes selected points; the theorem provides the bound for all inputs.

### Three diagnostics at a point near a root

The second cell displays SciOxide diagnostics alongside independent formulas at a point $x=1+\delta$ at distance $\delta$ from the root.
Inputs are degree $m$ and distance from the root $\delta$.
It computes `backward_error()` and `forward_error_estimate()` from `evaluate_with_derivative`, and `normalized_residual()` and the rounding allowance (the difference between `residual_with_roundoff()` and `normalized_residual()`) from `checked_basis_evaluation`.
The independent forward-error-estimate formula is $\gamma_{m+1}(\varepsilon)(1+|x|)^m/|x-1|^m$, whose denominator uses true value $|x-1|^m$ rather than the computed value.

```rust
//| id: poly-horner-root-diagnostics
//| ref: poly-horner-root-diagnostics
//| title: "Compare evaluation diagnostics and root-candidate backward error at a point near a root"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| uses: [poly-horner-binomial-model]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree m (p(x) = (x − 1)^m)","min":2,"max":16,"step":1,"value":6}
//|   offset: {"type":"select","label":"Distance from root δ [-]","options":["0.1","0.01","0.001"],"value":"0.01"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"closer-point","label":"δ = 0.001","description":"The forward-error estimate with computed-value denominator severely underestimates the true value, and the residual falls below the rounding allowance.","inputs":{"offset":"0.001"}}

//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":1.5167e-14,"rtol":0.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":1.2434497875801908e-14,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":1.2434497875801908e-14,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: closer-point
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":1,"max":1000}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":1.0e5,"rtol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: high-degree

//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":3.7747582837255464e-15,"rtol":1e-12}}

use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, PolynomialRootBasisRef, checked_basis_evaluation};

let m = degree.round() as usize;
let delta: f64 = offset.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot interpret distance")?;
let coefficients = shifted_power_coefficients(m);
let p = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(coefficients.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let x = 1.0 + delta;
let evaluation = p.evaluate_with_derivative(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let checked = checked_basis_evaluation(
    &coefficients,
    Complex::new(x, 0.0),
    PolynomialRootBasisRef::Monomial,
)

let checked_value = checked.value().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.re;

let gamma = |j: f64| j * f64::EPSILON / (1.0 - j * f64::EPSILON);
let model = gamma((m + 1) as f64);

let allowance = gamma(8.0 * (m + 1) as f64);

let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Diagnostics at x = 1 + δ" },

    { "key": "2", "label": "Independent formula [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 }
]);

    columns,
    vec![
        (
            "backward_error() and γ_(m+1)(ε)",
            evaluation.condition().backward_error(),
            model
        ),
        (
            "forward_error_estimate() and γ_(m+1)(ε) Σ|a_k||x|^k / |x − 1|^m",
            evaluation.condition().forward_error_estimate(),
            model * envelope / power(x - 1.0, m),
        ),
        (
            "normalized_residual() and |p̂(x)| / Σ|a_k||x|^k",
            checked.normalized_residual(),
            checked_value.abs() / envelope,
        ),
        (
            "residual_with_roundoff() − normalized_residual() and γ_(8N)(ε)",
            checked.residual_with_roundoff() - checked.normalized_residual(),

    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "diagnostics" })
```

In the first row, `backward_error()` equals independent $\gamma_{m+1}(\varepsilon)$, regardless of the evaluation point or cancellation associated with the degree.
The second row's `forward_error_estimate()` uses computed $|\widehat p(x)|$ as denominator, while the independent formula uses true $|x-1|^m$.
At $m=6$ and $\delta=0.01$, both approximately agree around $0.1025$.
With the same $m=6$ but $\delta=0.001$, SciOxide estimates approximately $35$ while the independent formula gives $1.0\times10^5$, showing that a computed value that has lost digits cannot estimate the true relative error.
At $m=16$ and $\delta=0.01$, the estimate is $305$ while the independent formula gives $2.7\times10^{22}$.
The third row's `normalized_residual()` is exactly $|\widehat p(x)|/\sum_k|a_k||x|^k$, while the fourth is the checked evaluation's rounding allowance $\gamma_{8N}(\varepsilon)$ ($N=m+1$).
At $m=6$ and $\delta=0.01$, $\eta\approx1.5\times10^{-14}$ slightly exceeds allowance $1.24\times10^{-14}$, so $p(x)$ is distinguishable from zero.
At $\delta=0.001$, it drops to $\eta\approx4.5\times10^{-17}$, below the allowance, and `roundoff_limited()` becomes true.
The value is then buried in rounding, so continuing Newton iteration does not improve the root candidate.

## Summary

At real points, Horner's error is bounded by $\gamma_{2n}(u)\sum_k|a_k||x|^k$ and its componentwise backward error is at most $\gamma_{2n}(u)$.
Including SciOxide's rearranged recurrences and the presence or absence of FMA, no term receives more than $2n$ roundings, so this bound still applies at real points.
Relative forward error can grow to the componentwise backward-error bound times condition number $\kappa(p,x)$; near roots, digits can therefore be lost even with small backward error.
`backward_error()` is the model $\gamma_{N}(\varepsilon)$, `forward_error_estimate()` an estimate with a computed-value denominator, and `normalized_residual()` a root candidate's backward error; none is a certified upper bound.
Use `roundoff_limited()` to stop root-candidate iterations; if relative accuracy near a root is needed, store an alternative representation such as a factored form.
