Orthogonal polynomial zeros serve as Gaussian quadrature and interpolation nodes; families without closed forms require numerical computation.
The three-term recurrence gives a symmetric tridiagonal matrix (Jacobi matrix) whose eigenvalues are the zeros.
This page derives zero properties through Jacobi matrices, explains contracts and error estimates for `jacobi_recurrence` and `zeros`, and checks four families' zeros in three ways in a cell.
Three-term coefficients $a_n,b_n,c_n$ and monic coefficients $d_n,e_n$ were defined in [Orthogonal polynomial families](oxref:poly-three-term-recurrence-favard).

## Reduction to a symmetric tridiagonal Jacobi matrix

Orthonormal polynomials $p_k=\pi_k/\|\pi_k\|_w$ obtained by normalizing monic polynomials satisfy

$$
xp_k(x)=e_{k+1}p_{k+1}(x)+d_kp_k(x)+e_kp_{k-1}(x),\qquad e_k=\sqrt{\frac{c_k}{a_{k-1}a_k}}>0
$$


Setting $\mathbf p(x)=(p_0(x),\dots,p_{n-1}(x))^{\mathsf T}$ rewrites this as

$$
\mathbf J_n\mathbf p(x)=x\,\mathbf p(x)-e_np_n(x)\,\mathbf u_n,\qquad
\mathbf J_n=\begin{bmatrix}
d_0&e_1&&\\
e_1&d_1&\ddots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&e_{n-1}\\
&&e_{n-1}&d_{n-1}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $\mathbf u_n$ is the unit vector with only component $n$ equal to 1.
If $p_n(x)=0$, then $x$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathbf J_n$ with eigenvector $\mathbf p(x)$.
Since $\mathbf J_n$ is real symmetric with positive subdiagonal entries, its eigenvalues are real and distinct.
Thus $p_n$ has $n$ distinct real zeros.
$\mathbf J_n$ is the leading principal submatrix of $\mathbf J_{n+1}$, so Cauchy's interlacing theorem makes zeros of degrees $n$ and $n+1$ interlace.
All zeros lie inside the convex hull of weight support ($(-1,1)$ for $[-1,1]$ families, $(0,\infty)$ for Laguerre).
A diagonal similarity making off-diagonal entries positive absorbs sign-convention differences such as Laguerre's.
Eigenvalues sum to the trace, so zeros sum to $\sum_{k<n}d_k$.

## Jacobi coefficients and the final entry

`jacobi_recurrence(order)` computes $d_0,\dots,d_{\mathrm{order}-1}$ and $e_1,\dots,e_{\mathrm{order}-1}$, returning owned `JacobiRecurrence`.
Construction costs $O(\mathrm{order})$ time and memory and is independent of polynomial normalization.
`diagonal()` and `off_diagonal()` borrow slices of lengths `order` and `order - 1`; `into_parts()` supplies two `Vec` without cloning.
Zero `order` gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Zero leading recurrence coefficient $a_k$ or nonpositive $e_k^2$ gives `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`); $e_k$ underflowing to 0 gives `NumericalInstability` (`Underflow`).
For classical families with valid parameters, $e_k^2>0$ holds theoretically, so these occur only with parameters extreme enough to make coefficients unrepresentable.

`last_member_scaled(x)` evaluates the degree-`order - 1` polynomial orthonormal under probability measure $w/\mu_0$ with positive leading coefficient.
It differs from classically normalized $P_{\mathrm{order}-1}$ only by constant factor $\pm\sqrt{\mu_0/h_{\mathrm{order}-1}}$.
Computation costs $O(\mathrm{order})$ without allocation and returns an extended-exponent `Scaled`, avoiding intermediate range loss.
Project with `round()` if a binary64 value is needed; unrepresentable values fail there.

## Zeros as eigenvalues and their errors

`zeros(n)` returns the zeros of the degree-$n$ polynomial in ascending order in a `Vec`.
`ChebyshevT` and `ChebyshevU` use sine closed forms and mirror symmetric pairs, giving exact symmetry and exact central 0 for odd degrees.
Other families compute only eigenvalues of $\mathbf J_n$ with default symmetric tridiagonal eigensolver settings from `scioxide-linalg`, testing deflation with a relative criterion based on machine epsilon.
This threshold does not bound individual zero errors.
Symmetric eigenvalues are well-conditioned under perturbation; Weyl's theorem bounds each eigenvalue change from $\Delta\mathbf J$ by $\|\Delta\mathbf J\|_2$.
Backward-stable solvers thus give absolute errors proportional to $\|\mathbf J_n\|_2$; zeros far smaller than $\|\mathbf J_n\|_2$, such as near-zero Laguerre zeros, can have large relative errors.
Pass the coefficients from `jacobi_recurrence` to an eigensolver for other settings.
$n=0$ gives `InvalidInput`; failure of eigensolver convergence propagates.

Gaussian quadrature nodes are these zeros; weights follow from the first component of each eigenvector and $\mu_0$ (Golub–Welsch; G. H. Golub and J. H. Welsch, *Math. Comp.* 23, 1969, 221–230).
`scioxide-poly` does not return weights.
Weights and degree-$2n-1$ exactness are derived in [integrate's Gaussian quadrature and Clenshaw–Curtis rules](../../../scioxide-integrate/algorithms/gaussian-and-clenshaw-curtis/); zero sums and interlacing are checked in the [Hermite example](../../examples/gauss-nodes-as-zeros/).

## Checking zeros with Jacobi matrices

The next cell checks `zeros(n)` zeros of four families without closed forms in three ways.

- Newton correction $\delta_k=P_n(z_k)/P_n'(z_k)$: compute with `evaluate` and `evaluate_derivative` as a first-order error estimate for computed zero $z_k$.
  It includes rounding in evaluating $P_n$ itself and is not a guaranteed error upper bound.
- Trace: compare the zero sum with diagonal sum $\sum_kd_k$ of `jacobi_recurrence(n)`.
- Interlacing: count whether `zeros(n + 1)` zeros bracket each degree-$n$ zero individually.

For scale, tabulate Gershgorin upper bound $\|\mathbf J_n\|_2\le\max_k(|d_k|+e_k+e_{k+1})$ multiplied by machine epsilon $\varepsilon$.
Choose Legendre, Laguerre, Jacobi ($\alpha=1/2$, $\beta=-1/2$), or Gegenbauer ($\lambda=2$).
The plot shows $|\delta_k|$ against zero position $z_k$ on a logarithmic vertical axis; omit $\delta_k=0$ points and count them in the table.

```rust
//| id: poly-jacobi-zeros
//| ref: poly-jacobi-zeros
//| title: "Check zeros computed as Jacobi eigenvalues"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   family_name: {"type":"select","label":"Family","options":["Legendre","Laguerre","Jacobi (α = 1/2, β = −1/2)","Gegenbauer (λ = 2)"],"value":"Legendre"}
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree n","description":"Number of zeros.","min":2,"max":60,"step":1,"value":20}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"laguerre","label":"Laguerre","description":"Zeros span roughly (0, 4n); matrix-norm-proportional absolute errors are evident at large zeros.","inputs":{"family_name":"Laguerre"}}
//|   - {"id":"jacobi-60","label":"Jacobi, n = 60","description":"Even with asymmetric weights, zeros interlace and agree with the trace.","inputs":{"family_name":"Jacobi (α = 1/2, β = −1/2)","degree":60}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.9931285991850949,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":0}
//|   - case: laguerre
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":0.07053988969198875,"rtol":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":66.52441652561575,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":0}
//|   - case: jacobi-60
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":0}
use scioxide_poly::OrthogonalFamily;

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let n = degree.round() as usize;
let family: OrthogonalFamily<f64> = match family_name.as_str() {
    "Laguerre" => OrthogonalFamily::Laguerre,
    "Jacobi (α = 1/2, β = −1/2)" => OrthogonalFamily::Jacobi {
        alpha: 0.5,
        beta: -0.5,
    },
    "Gegenbauer (λ = 2)" => OrthogonalFamily::Gegenbauer { lambda: 2.0 },
    _ => OrthogonalFamily::Legendre,
};
let zeros = family.zeros(n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let next = family.zeros(n + 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let recurrence = family.jacobi_recurrence(n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (diagonal, off) = (recurrence.diagonal(), recurrence.off_diagonal());
// Gershgorin bound max_k(|d_k| + e_k + e_{k+1}) ≥ ‖J_n‖₂
let mut gershgorin = 0.0_f64;
for (k, &d) in diagonal.iter().enumerate() {
    let below = if k > 0 { off[k - 1] } else { 0.0 };
    let above = off.get(k).copied().unwrap_or(0.0);
    gershgorin = gershgorin.max(d.abs() + below + above);
}
// Newton correction δ_k = P_n(z_k)/P_n'(z_k) (first-order zero-error estimate)
let (mut corrections, mut largest, mut exact) = (Vec::new(), 0.0_f64, 0_usize);
for &z in &zeros {
    let value = family.evaluate(n, z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let slope = family
        .evaluate_derivative(n, z)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let delta = (value / slope).abs();
    largest = largest.max(delta);
    if delta > 0.0 {
        corrections.push([z, delta]);
    } else {
        exact += 1;
    }
}
// Interlacing: count k violating next[k] < zeros[k] < next[k + 1]
let violations = (0..n)
    .filter(|&k| !(next[k] < zeros[k] && zeros[k] < next[k + 1]))
    .count();
let trace_gap = (zeros.iter().sum::<f64>() - diagonal.iter().sum::<f64>()).abs();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Smallest zero [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Largest zero [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum |δ_k| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "ε × Gershgorin bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Zero-sum minus trace [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Interlacing violations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Zeros omitted for δ_k = 0 [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let row = (
    zeros[0],
    zeros[n - 1],
    largest,
    f64::EPSILON * gershgorin,
    trace_gap,
    violations,
    exact,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "zeros" })
);
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "|δ_k|", "points": corrections })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Zero z_k [-]",
        "yLabel": "Newton correction |P_n(z_k)/P_n′(z_k)| [-]",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
```

With default Legendre $n=20$, the largest zero $0.9931285991850948$ agrees within 1 ulp with literature value $0.99312859918509492$.
Maximum Newton correction $5.5\times10^{-16}$ is comparable to $2.4\times10^{-16}$, the product of $\varepsilon$ and Gershgorin's bound; the difference between the sum of zeros and the trace is $7.8\times10^{-16}$ and there are no interlacing violations.
For Laguerre $n=20$, zeros span $0.0705$ to $66.52$, and the matrix norm also grows.
Smallest and largest zeros agree with literature values $0.070539889691988753$ and $66.524416525615754$ within relative error $10^{-14}$.
The smallest zero's relative error $3.7\times10^{-15}$ exceeds the largest zero's $5\times10^{-16}$, but the largest zero has about 100 times greater absolute error.
Maximum Newton correction $8.1\times10^{-14}$ occurs at large zeros; the plot shows $|\delta_k|$ growing with $z_k$.
This matches Weyl's estimate of absolute error proportional to $\|\mathbf J_n\|_2$; the ratio of about 5 to $\varepsilon$ times Gershgorin's bound includes a mildly degree-dependent backward-error factor and rounding in $L_n$ evaluation itself.
Even at $n=60$ for asymmetric Jacobi weights ($\alpha=1/2$, $\beta=-1/2$), Newton correction is $5.5\times10^{-16}$ and interlacing violations remain 0.
Zeros with exactly zero $\delta_k$ are omitted from the plot and counted in the table.

## Summary

Three-term recurrences give symmetric Jacobi matrices with zeros as eigenvalues, also showing that zeros are distinct and real and interlace adjacent-degree zeros.
`jacobi_recurrence(n)` returns normalization-independent diagonals and off-diagonals in $O(n)$; pass these to eigensolvers with other settings.
`zeros` returns numerical solutions from the default eigensolver; estimate absolute errors proportional to $\|\mathbf J_n\|_2$ and check zeros individually with Newton corrections $P_n(z)/P_n'(z)$.
For zeros far smaller than $\|\mathbf J_n\|_2$, such as near-zero Laguerre zeros, assess relative error through Newton correction magnitudes before use.
`scioxide-poly` returns no Gaussian weights; use Gauss rules in `scioxide-integrate` for quadrature.
