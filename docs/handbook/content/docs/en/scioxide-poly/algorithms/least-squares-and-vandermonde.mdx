Noisy measurements do not necessarily call for high-degree interpolation through every sample.
Approximate $m$ samples $(x_i,y_i)$ with degree-$n$ polynomial $p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n c_k\phi_k(x)$.
Choose basis and degree and inspect residuals and rank to avoid coefficients overly sensitive to observation changes.
This page explains the objective and weights, sample-matrix conditioning, SciOxide solvers and APIs, and two experiments.

## Sample matrix and objective

The $m\times(n+1)$ matrix $V_{ik}=\phi_k(x_i)$ evaluating basis functions $\phi_0,\dots,\phi_n$ at nodes is the sample matrix (design matrix).
For monomials $\phi_k(x)=x^k$, $V$ is a Vandermonde matrix.
The objective minimized is

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}w_i|p(x_i)-y_i|^2
=\|W^{1/2}(Vc-y)\|_2^2,\qquad W=\operatorname{diag}(w_i)
$$

where $c=(c_0,\dots,c_n)^{\mathsf T}$ are coefficients and $w_i\ge0$ weights.
For $m=n+1$ with distinct nodes and positive weights, residuals vanish and the result equals interpolation.

Weights multiply squared residuals of individual samples.
If observation errors are independent, mean zero, and have known standard deviations $\sigma_i$, $w_i=1/\sigma_i^2$ is a candidate.
Scaling each row by $1/\sigma_i$ then makes errors homoscedastic; Gauss–Markov gives the best linear unbiased estimator (Aitken's theorem).
Square weights from conventions multiplying residuals themselves before passing them.
Passing them unchanged solves a different problem.
Zero weights exclude samples, but all-zero or negative weights are rejected with `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).

`Vandermonde::monomial(n)` specifies columns $1,x,\ldots,x^n$, while `Vandermonde::orthogonal(plan)` specifies every column of a prepared orthogonal basis.
For a single monomial matrix, `vandermonde(points, n)` returns the same matrix.
Rows follow input point order and columns ascending degree; points may be real or complex.
Each row is filled by the basis recurrence in $O(n)$, giving cost proportional to point count times column count without monomial expansion of orthogonal bases.
`matrix` returns an owned matrix; empty points give zero rows.
`matrix_into` borrows output (including strided views) and caller scratch, taking `ExecutionContext` last.
`workspace_requirements(point_count)` gives scratch length; the whole matrix is built there before copying to output, preserving output on failure without allocation.
Least-squares Plans, however, reject empty observations as invalid input.

## Conditioning of sample matrices

### Normal equations and squared conditioning

Setting the derivative of the objective with respect to $c$ to 0 gives normal equations $V^*WVc=V^*Wy$.
Writing $A=W^{1/2}V$ gives $\kappa_2(A^*A)=\kappa_2(A)^2$, so forming normal equations already squares the condition number.
Let $\varepsilon$ denote relative perturbations in $A$ and the right-hand side, including rounding error, with least-squares residual $r$ and $\sin\theta=\|r\|_2/\|W^{1/2}y\|_2$; through first order, relative solution error is bounded by

$$
\frac{\|\hat c-c\|_2}{\|c\|_2}\lesssim\varepsilon\Bigl(\frac{2\kappa_2(A)}{\cos\theta}+\kappa_2(A)^2\tan\theta\Bigr)
$$

(Golub and Van Loan, §5.3).
Orthogonal-transform solvers such as QR and SVD attain accuracy matching this bound; for small residuals, error is proportional to $\kappa_2(A)$.
Normal-equation solvers can incur errors around $\kappa_2(A)^2u$ regardless of residual size ($u$ is unit roundoff).

### Ill-conditioning of Vandermonde matrices

For monomial samples, weighted normal-equation matrix $V^*WV$ has entries $\sum_iw_ix_i^{j+k}$ and is positive-definite Hankel.
Beckermann (2000) showed that order-$n+1$ positive-definite Hankel matrices have 2-norm condition numbers at least $\gamma^{n}/(16(n+1))$ ($\gamma=e^{4G/\pi}\approx3.21$; $G$ is Catalan's constant).
Since $\kappa_2(A)^2=\kappa_2(V^*WV)$, every choice of real nodes and weights makes the condition number of $A$ grow at least exponentially with column count at ratio $\sqrt{3.21}\approx1.79$.
This lower bound concerns $A$ before column scaling.
Column scaling by diagonal matrix $D$ gives $AD$ satisfying $\kappa_2(AD)\ge\kappa_2(A)/\kappa_2(D)$.
For nodes spread over $[-1,1]$, the norm ratio $\kappa_2(D)$ of columns $x^k$ grows only polynomially with degree, so the condition number after scaling still grows exponentially at the same ratio.
Column scaling removes magnitude differences, but cannot remove near-parallel columns.

Orthogonal polynomial bases change the situation.
At $m$ first-kind Chebyshev nodes $x_i=\cos((2i+1)\pi/(2m))$, for $j\ne k$ and $j,k<m$, $\sum_iT_j(x_i)T_k(x_i)=0$ holds (discrete orthogonality).
Thus the sample matrix of $T_0,\dots,T_n$ ($n<m$) has orthogonal columns; unit-norm scaling gives $\kappa_2=1$.
Even at equally spaced points, the Chebyshev-basis condition number stays small if degree $n$ is below the order of $\sqrt m$ (Rakhmanov, 2007).
The condition number grows as degree approaches $m$; $n=m-1$ gives equally spaced interpolation itself.
Continuous orthogonality need not make $V$ columns orthogonal on arbitrary discrete samples; inspect reported conditioning.

## Column scaling and truncated SVD

SciOxide's `PolynomialFitPlan` prepares without normal equations as follows (Björck, 1996).

1. Construct sample matrix $V$ with `Vandermonde` and multiply row $i$ by $\sqrt{w_i}$ to form $A=W^{1/2}V$.
2. Divide each column by its 2-norm $s_k$ to form $\widetilde A_{ik}=A_{ik}/s_k$.
   Zero columns use unit scale; row weighting and column norms use scaling to avoid overflow or underflow.
3. Compute thin SVD $\widetilde A=U\Sigma Z^*$ of $\widetilde A$ through `scioxide-linalg`.

Setting $z_k=s_kc_k$ turns the problem into least squares for $\widetilde A z$, with solution

$$
z=\sum_{\sigma_j>\tau}Z_j\frac{U_j^*W^{1/2}y}{\sigma_j}
$$

where $U_j$ and $Z_j$ are singular vectors and $\tau$ the rank threshold.
The rank policy (`RankPolicy`) in `PolynomialApproximationPolicy` sets the threshold.
Default `RankPolicy::DimensionScaledEpsilon` retains only singular values exceeding $\tau=\min(\max(m,n+1)\,\varepsilon,1/2)\,\sigma_{\max}$ ($\varepsilon$ is machine epsilon).
`RankPolicy::Exact` excludes only exactly zero singular values; `RankPolicy::Threshold` uses absolute and relative threshold $\tau=\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\,\sigma_{\max}$.
No policy applies regularization.

Rank deficiency or more coefficients than observations still returns the minimum-norm solution in the retained subspace.
The minimized norm is that of scaled-column coordinates $z$, not necessarily original $c$.
Reported rank and conditioning concern $\widetilde A$.
Unit-norm column scaling gives a condition number within a factor $\sqrt{n+1}$ of the optimum among all diagonal column scalings (van der Sluis, 1969).

Preparation costs $O(m(n+1)\min(m,n+1))$, or $O(m(n+1)^2)$ for ordinary overdetermined systems $m\ge n+1$.
The Plan retains sample matrices and decomposition; changing only values with the same nodes, degree, and weights costs $O((m+n)(n+1))$ per fit.
More samples do not always help; clustered nodes, excessive degrees, and extrapolation can remain ill-conditioned.

## Preparation, results, and workspaces

| Item | Role and interpretation |
|---|---|
| `PolynomialFitPlan` | Retains nodes, weights, decomposition, and basis preparation. `monomial` and `orthogonal` determine result basis |
| `PolynomialFit` | Owns fitted polynomial and report. `polynomial()` borrows without conversion; `into_polynomial()` extracts the polynomial |
| `PolynomialFitReport` | Returns column-scaled rank (`rank()`), reciprocal conditioning from all singular values (`condition()`), weighted residual norm in original units (`residual_norm()`), and policy (`policy()`) |
| `PolynomialFitRequirements` | Workspace requirements returned by `workspace_requirements(())` |
| `PolynomialFitWorkspace` | Create with `workspace(())` or reserve with `try_reserve`, check capacity through `check`, and pass to `fit_into` |

`PolynomialFitPlan::monomial(points, degree, weights, policy)` creates a monomial Plan; `PolynomialFitPlan::orthogonal(points, basis, weights, policy)` uses every column of prepared orthogonal basis `basis`.
Passing `None` to `weights` gives unit weights.
Empty points, negative weights, or all-zero weights give `InvalidInput`; weight/point length mismatch gives `DimensionMismatch`; non-finite points or weights give `NonFinite`.
Basis-value overflow gives `NumericalInstability`; SVD nonconvergence propagates errors from `scioxide-linalg` (such as `ConvergenceFailure`).

Use `ApproxPolynomial::fit_monomial(points, values, degree)` and `ApproxPolynomial::fit_orthogonal(points, values, degree, spec)` for a single fit without weights or reports.
Both prepare Plans with unit weights and default policies, returning only fitted polynomials.

`fit` allocates workspace and calls `fit_into`.
`fit_into` borrows exact-length (degree + 1) coefficient output and workspace, taking `ExecutionContext` last.
Sufficiently reserved workspace gives allocation-free execution.
Failure preserves coefficient output, but workspace contents may change.
Orthogonal results retain preparation including normalization, coordinate mapping, maximum degree, and execution settings.

Small residuals do not guarantee accurate coefficients; inspect sample count, rank, and extrapolation-point sensitivity as well.
No coefficient covariance or confidence intervals are returned, so reports alone do not supply statistical uncertainty.

## Quadratic fitting on symmetric samples

Fit a weighted quadratic to dimensionless nodes $-1,-1/2,0,1/2,1$ with quadratic function $1+2x+x^2$ plus systematic quartic deviation $\eta x^4$.
Control $\eta$ sets deviation magnitude; $w$ weights the two endpoint samples (the middle three have weight 1).
Outputs are coefficient and Plan-size/rank/residual tables and a plot of individual sample residuals.

The independent check obtains constant and quadratic coefficients from symmetry-reduced two-variable normal equations.
Symmetric nodes and weights make odd column $x$ weighted-orthogonal to even columns $1,x^2$, leaving the linear coefficient independently 2.
Setting $s_k=\sum_iw_ix_i^k$ gives even-part normal equations

$$
\begin{pmatrix}s_0&s_2\\s_2&s_4\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}c_0\\c_2\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}s_0+s_2+\eta s_4\\s_2+s_4+\eta s_6\end{pmatrix}
$$

This checking formula is a closed form for this example, not a recommendation to use normal equations generally.
Moments $s_k$ are references constructed with f64 `powi`.

```rust
//| id: poly-weighted-fit
//| ref: poly-weighted-fit
//| title: "Weights and quadratic-fit residuals"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   deviation: {"type":"range","label":"Quartic deviation η [-]","min":0,"max":1,"step":0.1,"value":0.3}
//|   endpoint_weight: {"type":"range","label":"Endpoint squared-residual weight w [-]","min":0,"max":4,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"exact-quadratic","label":"η = 0","description":"Samples exactly follow a quadratic, recovering coefficients to rounding accuracy.","inputs":{"deviation":0}}
//|   - {"id":"middle-only","label":"η = 0.5, w = 0","description":"Removing endpoints gives interpolation of the middle three points and quadratic coefficient 1 + η/4.","inputs":{"deviation":0.5,"endpoint_weight":0}}
//|   - {"id":"heavy-ends","label":"w = 4","description":"Emphasizing endpoints reduces their residuals and increases central residuals.","inputs":{"endpoint_weight":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.9614285714285714,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1.3321428571428573,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.05378,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: exact-quadratic
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: middle-only
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1.125,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: heavy-ends
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_poly::{PolynomialBasis, PolynomialFitPlan};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let points = [-1.0_f64, -0.5, 0.0, 0.5, 1.0];
let values: Vec<f64> = points
    .iter()
    .map(|&x| 1.0 + 2.0 * x + x * x + deviation * (x * x) * (x * x))
    .collect();
let weights = [endpoint_weight, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0, endpoint_weight];
let plan = PolynomialFitPlan::monomial(&points, 2, Some(&weights), Default::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fit = plan.fit(&values).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let coefficients = fit.polynomial().basis().coefficients();
// Independent check: even-part 2×2 normal equations (reference moments use f64 powi).
let moment = |k: i32| {
    points
        .iter()
        .zip(&weights)
        .map(|(&x, &w)| w * x.powi(k))
        .sum::<f64>()
};
let (s0, s2, s4, s6) = (moment(0), moment(2), moment(4), moment(6));
let determinant = s0 * s4 - s2 * s2;
let expected = [
    1.0 + deviation * (s4 * s4 - s2 * s6) / determinant,
    2.0,
    1.0 + deviation * (s0 * s6 - s2 * s4) / determinant,
];
let rows: Vec<_> = coefficients
    .iter()
    .zip(expected)
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, (&actual, reference))| (k, actual, reference, (actual - reference).abs()))
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Degree k [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Coefficient c_k [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Symmetry-based check [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Absolute discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "coefficients" })
);
let report = fit.report();
let summary = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Samples m [points]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Coefficients n + 1 [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Retained rank [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Weighted residual norm [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    summary,
    vec![(
        points.len(),
        coefficients.len(),
        report.rank().rank(),
        report.residual_norm()
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
let mut residuals = Vec::new();
for (&x, &y) in points.iter().zip(&values) {
    let value = fit.polynomial().evaluate(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    residuals.push([x, value - y]);
}
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Sample residuals", "points": residuals })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Position x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Fitted value − sample value [-]"
    })
);
```

With default $\eta=0.3$ and $w=1$, constant coefficient $0.961429$ and quadratic coefficient $1.332143$ match symmetry checks to about $10^{-15}$, with weighted residual norm $0.0538$.
For $\eta=0$, the quadratic is recovered to rounding accuracy.
Zero endpoint weights leave the middle three points to determine the quadratic; endpoint residuals do not enter the objective.
For example, $\eta=0.5$ and $w=0$ give quadratic coefficient $1+\eta/4=1.125$, matching middle-three-point interpolation.
At $w=4$, smaller endpoint residuals accompany larger central residuals, changing the residual distribution.
Residual norms measure different objectives when weights change; magnitude alone does not rank fit quality.

## Conditioning of monomial and Chebyshev bases

The next cell prepares degree-$d=3,\dots,\min(30,m-1)$ monomial and Chebyshev-basis (`OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevT`) Plans on $m$ samples over $[-1,1]$, comparing reported condition numbers $\kappa_2$.
Choose equally spaced samples or `chebyshev_points` first-kind Chebyshev nodes.
Observations are $y_i=T_3(x_i)=4x_i^3-3x_i$, evaluated with SciOxide's `OrthogonalFamily::evaluate`.
For $d\ge3$, this polynomial is exactly representable, giving independent exact coefficients ($(0,-3,0,4,0,\dots)$ for monomials, $(0,0,0,1,0,\dots)$ for Chebyshev).
The table shows maximum coefficient error and weighted residual norm; the plot draws $\kappa_2$ logarithmically across degrees.
Chebyshev-basis $\kappa_2=1$ at first-kind nodes checks discrete orthogonality.

```rust

//| ref: poly-vandermonde-condition
//| title: "Condition numbers of monomial and Chebyshev sample matrices"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   samples: {"type":"range","label":"Samples m [points]","description":"Sample count on [−1, 1].","min":10,"max":200,"step":10,"value":50}
//|   layout: {"type":"select","label":"Sample placement","options":["Equally spaced","Chebyshev first-kind nodes"],"value":"Equally spaced"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"chebyshev-points","label":"Chebyshev first-kind nodes","description":"Chebyshev columns are orthogonal, giving condition number exactly 1.","inputs":{"layout":"Chebyshev first-kind nodes"}}
//|   - {"id":"few-samples","label":"m = 20","description":"Few equally spaced samples make Chebyshev conditioning grow with degree as well.","inputs":{"samples":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/0","equals":30}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/1","range":{"min":1e10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/2","approx":{"value":344.10,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/3","range":{"min":1e-9,"max":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/5","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/6","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: chebyshev-points
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/2","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/1","range":{"min":1e10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/6/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: few-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/4/0","equals":19}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":4281.2,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"condition","path":"/rows/4/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_poly::backend::barycentric::{ChebyshevPoints, chebyshev_points};
use scioxide_poly::{
    OrthogonalBasisSpec, OrthogonalFamily, PolynomialBasis, PolynomialFit, PolynomialFitPlan,
};

// κ₂ = 1/rcond (unit-norm-column sample matrix), maximum exact-coefficient discrepancy, and residual norm
fn diagnose<B: PolynomialBasis<f64>>(fit: &PolynomialFit<f64, B>, exact: [f64; 4]) -> [f64; 3] {
    let report = fit.report();
    let mut error = 0.0_f64;
    for (k, &c) in fit.polynomial().basis().coefficients().iter().enumerate() {
        error = error.max((c - exact.get(k).copied().unwrap_or(0.0)).abs());
    }
    let kappa = 1.0 / report.condition().reciprocal().value();
    [kappa, error, report.residual_norm()]
}

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let m = samples.round() as usize;
let points: Vec<f64> = if layout == "Equally spaced" {
    (0..m)
        .map(|i| -1.0 + 2.0 * i as f64 / (m - 1) as f64)
        .collect()
} else {
    chebyshev_points::<f64>(m, ChebyshevPoints::Roots)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .0
};
// Observations y = T₃(x) = 4x³ − 3x (SciOxide recurrence). Exact coefficients are known.
let family = OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevT;
let mut values = Vec::with_capacity(m);
for &x in &points {
    values.push(family.evaluate(3, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}
let spec = OrthogonalBasisSpec::classical(family).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut rows, mut monomial_curve, mut chebyshev_curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for degree in 3..=30.min(m - 1) {
    let monomial = PolynomialFitPlan::monomial(&points, degree, None, Default::default())
        .and_then(|plan| plan.fit(&values))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let basis = spec
        .prepare(degree, Default::default())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let chebyshev = PolynomialFitPlan::orthogonal(&points, basis, None, Default::default())
        .and_then(|plan| plan.fit(&values))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Independent reference: monomial coefficients (0, −3, 0, 4, 0, …), Chebyshev coefficients (0, 0, 0, 1, 0, …)
    let [kappa_m, error_m, residual_m] = diagnose(&monomial, [0.0, -3.0, 0.0, 4.0]);
    let [kappa_c, error_c, residual_c] = diagnose(&chebyshev, [0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0]);
    monomial_curve.push([degree as f64, kappa_m]);
    chebyshev_curve.push([degree as f64, kappa_c]);
    if degree % 5 == 0 || degree == 3 || degree == 30.min(m - 1) {
        rows.push((
            degree, kappa_m, kappa_c, error_m, error_c, residual_m, residual_c,
        ));
    }
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Degree d [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Monomial κ₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Chebyshev κ₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Monomial coefficient error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Chebyshev coefficient error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Monomial residual norm [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Chebyshev residual norm [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "condition" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Monomial", "points": monomial_curve }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Chebyshev basis", "points": chebyshev_curve }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Degree d [-]",
        "yLabel": "Sample-matrix condition number κ₂ [-]",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
```

With the default 50 equally spaced points, monomial $\kappa_2$ reaches $1.7\times10^{3}$ at $d=10$, $1.4\times10^{7}$ at $d=20$, and $2.5\times10^{11}$ at $d=30$, growing about 2.5-fold per degree.
This exponential growth exceeds ratio 1.79 from Beckermann's lower bound.
Chebyshev $\kappa_2$ on the same points is 4.5 at $d=20$ and only $3.4\times10^{2}$ even at $d=30$.
With first-kind Chebyshev nodes, Chebyshev $\kappa_2$ matches $1$ to rounding accuracy at every degree, while monomials worsen as with equal spacing ($1.2\times10^{11}$ at $d=30$).

Coefficient errors reflect conditioning differences directly.
For $d=30$ with 50 equally spaced points, monomial coefficient error reaches $3.7\times10^{-5}$, versus Chebyshev error $6.2\times10^{-15}$.
Monomial error is of the same order as $u\kappa_2\approx2.8\times10^{-5}$, consistent with perturbation bound $2\varepsilon\kappa_2$ above for zero-residual problems.
This value is sensitive to rounding details and can vary by over an order of magnitude between environments.
Weighted residual norms remain about $10^{-14}$ for both bases; residuals alone conceal monomial ill-conditioning.
Orthogonal-transform solvers return small-residual solutions, but conditioning determines coefficient accuracy.

With 20 equally spaced points (`few-samples`) and $d=19$, the Plan equals 20-point interpolation; even Chebyshev $\kappa_2$ grows to $4.3\times10^{3}$.
This is the same phenomenon as exponential Lebesgue growth for equally spaced [interpolation](../interpolation-and-chebyshev/); changing bases leaves node-placement ill-conditioning.
Rank threshold is $\max(m,d+1)\,\varepsilon\,\sigma_{\max}$, about $1.1\times10^{-14}$ times $\sigma_{\max}$ at $m=50$.
The experiment's $\kappa_2$ is at most $2.5\times10^{11}$, so the least singular value exceeds threshold and every retained rank is $d+1$.
Monomial singular values reach the threshold only at still higher degrees.

## Summary

Sample-matrix rows follow nodes and columns degrees; weights multiply squared residuals (square residual-multiplying weights before passing).
Real-node monomial matrices worsen exponentially with degree for any node choice; above degree 10, use orthogonal bases such as `Vandermonde::orthogonal` or `PolynomialFitPlan::orthogonal`.
SciOxide avoids normal equations and solves a column-scaled thin SVD, so for small residuals error scales with $\kappa_2$ rather than $\kappa_2^2$.
Reported rank below column count means a minimum-norm solution; reduce degree or change basis.
Reuse prepared Plans when only observations change, calling `fit_into` with preallocated workspace for allocation-free repetition.
