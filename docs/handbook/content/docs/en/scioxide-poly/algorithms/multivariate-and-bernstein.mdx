Bernstein bases let control points indicate value ranges when representing curves or surfaces.
A control-point array alone does not determine meaning; the parameter interval must also be specified.
This page explains univariate Bernstein evaluation and subdivision, rounding and conditioning, and monomial conversion.
Tensor-product surfaces and convex-hull range estimates are covered in [Tensor-product Bernstein and range estimates](../tensor-bernstein-bounds/).
Sparse multivariate polynomials are covered in [Sparse multivariate polynomials](../sparse-multivariate/).

## Bernstein basis and de Casteljau

Define the degree-$n$ Bernstein basis on $t\in[0,1]$ and polynomial with control points $b_i$ by

$$
B_i^n(t)=\binom ni t^i(1-t)^{n-i},\qquad p(t)=\sum_{i=0}^nb_iB_i^n(t)
$$


The binomial theorem gives $\sum_iB_i^n(t)=(t+(1-t))^n=1$, and every $B_i^n$ is nonnegative for $[0,1]$.
For real $t\in[0,1]$, $p(t)$ is therefore a convex combination of control points, between their minimum and maximum (convex hull property).
This exact-arithmetic property holds for real $t\in[0,1]$, excluding extrapolation and complex parameters.
Floating-point guarantees are discussed in [Convex hull property and range tightening by subdivision](oxref:poly-bernstein-subdivision-bounds).
Endpoints satisfy $p(0)=b_0$ and $p(1)=b_n$, but Bernstein is not a nodal interpolation basis; curves need not pass through interior control points.

The basis satisfies recurrence $B_i^n(t)=(1-t)B_i^{n-1}(t)+tB_{i-1}^{n-1}(t)$.
Substituting into the sum for $p$ and lowering degree successively gives, from $b_i^{(0)}=b_i$,

$$
b_i^{(r)}=(1-t)\,b_i^{(r-1)}+t\,b_{i+1}^{(r-1)},\qquad r=1,\ldots,n
$$

de Casteljau's shrinking stages, with $p(t)=b_0^{(n)}$.
This uses $n(n+1)/2$ convex combinations ($O(n^2)$) and $O(n)$ memory.
For $t\in[0,1]$, every stage is a convex combination, so exact intermediates also remain within the control-point range.
The two penultimate-stage values simultaneously give derivative $p'(t)=n\,(b_1^{(n-1)}-b_0^{(n-1)})$.
Triangle edges $b_0^{(r)}$ and $b_{n-r}^{(r)}$ ($r=0,\ldots,n$) become control points of intervals $[0,t]$ and $[t,1]$ split at $t$.

## Rounding error and conditioning

Each de Casteljau stage computes $\mathrm{fl}(\mathrm{fl}(a\,\hat s)+\mathrm{fl}(b\,t))$ where $\hat s=\mathrm{fl}(1-t)$.
Under standard rounding model $\mathrm{fl}(a\circ b)=(a\circ b)(1+\eta)$ and $|\eta|\le u$, paths through $1-t$ receive at most 3 factors of $(1+\eta)$ per stage; paths through $t$ receive at most 2.
The path from control point $b_i$ to $p(t)$ has $n$ stages, so with $\gamma_k=ku/(1-ku)$, computed value $\hat p(t)$ for real $t\in[0,1]$ satisfies

$$
|\hat p(t)-p(t)|\le\gamma_{3n}\sum_{i=0}^n|b_i|\,B_i^n(t)\le\gamma_{3n}\max_i|b_i|
$$


For $t\in[1/2,1]$, Sterbenz's lemma makes $1-t$ exact, reducing the constant to $\gamma_{2n}$.
Rounding in forming $t$ from physical point $x$ appears separately as a perturbed evaluation point.
Dividing the right-hand sum by $|p(t)|$ gives the condition number of evaluation in the Bernstein basis; relative error is bounded by this condition number times $\gamma_{3n}$.
Monomial Horner uses $\sum_k|a_k||t|^k$ for the same role ([Rounding error and diagnostics for Horner evaluation](../horner-rounding-error/)).
For $[0,1]$, the condition number in the Bernstein basis never exceeds that in the monomial basis for the same polynomial (R. T. Farouki and V. T. Rajan, "On the numerical condition of polynomials in Bernstein form", *Comput. Aided Geom. Design* 4, 1987).
Furthermore, no basis nonnegative on $[0,1]$ systematically gives smaller condition numbers than Bernstein (R. T. Farouki and T. N. T. Goodman, "On the optimal stability of the Bernstein basis", *Math. Comp.* 65, 1996).

Consider $p(t)=(t-1/2)^n$.
Bernstein coefficients are $b_i=(-1)^{n-i}2^{-n}$, giving $\sum_i|b_i|B_i^n(t)=2^{-n}$.
Monomial coefficients are $a_k=\binom nk(-1/2)^{n-k}$, with $\sum_k|a_k|t^k=(t+1/2)^n$ around 1 near $t=1/2$.
Near root $t=1/2$, both bases have the same $|p|$, giving condition-number ratio about $2^n$.

The conditioning report of `evaluate_with_derivative` uses $\max_i|b_i|$ for $t\in[0,1]$, and $\sum_i|B_i^n(t)|=(|t|+|1-t|)^n$ times $\max_i|b_i|$ outside the interval or at complex $t$, as a bound on value magnitude.
Reported `forward_error_estimate()` multiplies this bound divided by $|p|$ by $(n+1)\varepsilon/(1-(n+1)\varepsilon)$ ($\varepsilon=2u$); its constant differs from the worst-case $\gamma_{3n}$ bound above.
Because the bound is $\max_i|b_i|$ rather than $\sum_i|b_i|B_i^n(t)$, it is sharp for comparable control-point magnitudes as here and more conservative otherwise.

The next cell constructs a Bernstein representation of $(t-1/2)^n$ and converts to monomial coefficients with `to_monomial`.
Integer and power-of-two coefficients make this conversion exact.
Evaluate both at $t=1/2+\delta$ with `evaluate_with_derivative` and tabulate relative errors and reported `forward_error_estimate()`.
For reference $\delta^n$, form $\delta=t-1/2$ exactly by Sterbenz's lemma and raise it using `f64` intrinsic `powi`, without SciOxide functions (relative error about $nu$).
The second table compares conversion report `condition_estimate()` (ratio of maximum coefficient magnitudes) with closed form $\max_k\binom nk2^k$.

```rust
//| id: poly-bernstein-conditioning
//| ref: poly-bernstein-conditioning
//| title: "de Casteljau and Horner accuracy near a root"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree n [-]","description":"Degree of p(t) = (t − 1/2)ⁿ. The basis condition-number ratio is about 2ⁿ.","min":4,"max":20,"step":2,"value":10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"low-degree","label":"n = 4","description":"Condition-number ratio is about 16; both bases give over 10 digits at δ = 0.01.","inputs":{"degree":4}}
//|   - {"id":"high-degree","label":"n = 20","description":"Monomial Horner loses significant digits even at δ = 0.05, while de Casteljau retains small relative error.","inputs":{"degree":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/4/4","range":{"min":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"conversion","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":15360,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"conversion","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":15360,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: low-degree
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/4/4","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"conversion","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":32,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: high-degree
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"conversion","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":635043840,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_poly::ApproxPolynomial;

let n = degree as usize;
// Bernstein coefficients bᵢ = (−1)ⁿ⁻ⁱ 2⁻ⁿ of (t − 1/2)ⁿ. All are exact powers of 2.
let scale = 0.5_f64.powi(n as i32);
let controls: Vec<f64> = (0..=n)
    .map(|i| if (n - i) % 2 == 0 { scale } else { -scale })
    .collect();
let bernstein = ApproxPolynomial::bernstein(controls, 0.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Convert to monomial coefficients a_k = C(n, k)(−1/2)ⁿ⁻ᵏ.
let conversion = bernstein.to_monomial().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let monomial = conversion.polynomial();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for offset in [0.2_f64, 0.1, 0.05, 0.02, 0.01] {
    let t = 0.5 + offset;
    // Reference: δ = t − 1/2 is exact by Sterbenz's lemma. Reference computation uses f64 powi.
    let delta = t - 0.5;
    let reference = delta.powi(n as i32);
    let b = bernstein
        .evaluate_with_derivative(t)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let m = monomial
        .evaluate_with_derivative(t)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        delta,
        reference,
        (b.value() - reference).abs() / reference.abs(),
        b.condition().forward_error_estimate(),
        (m.value() - reference).abs() / reference.abs(),
        m.condition().forward_error_estimate(),
    ));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "δ = t − 1/2 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Reference δⁿ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "de Casteljau relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "de Casteljau estimate [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Horner relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Horner estimate [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "accuracy" })
);
// Independent check: maximum coefficient-magnitude ratio is max_k C(n, k) 2ᵏ.
let mut binomial = 1.0_f64;
let mut growth = 1.0_f64;
for k in 1..=n {
    binomial *= (n + 1 - k) as f64 / k as f64;
    growth = growth.max(binomial * 2.0_f64.powi(k as i32));
}
let summary_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Conversion condition_estimate [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "max C(n, k) 2ᵏ [-]", "type": "number" }
]);

emit_table_with_columns!(
    summary_columns,
    summary,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "conversion" })
);
```

At default $n=10$, Horner relative error at $\delta=0.01$ (reference $10^{-20}$) is $3.9\times10^3$, leaving no correct digits.
The monomial-basis condition number $(t+1/2)^n/\delta^n$ is about $10^{20}$ here, making relative error around $10^4$, its $u$ multiple.
de Casteljau relative error at the same point is $4.8\times10^{-15}$.
At $n=20$, Horner has relative error $1.3\times10^{10}$ even at $\delta=0.05$, whereas de Casteljau stays at $8.6\times10^{-15}$ even at $\delta=0.01$.
At $n=4$, the condition numbers differ less; Horner retains relative error $1.6\times10^{-9}$ at $\delta=0.01$.

de Casteljau error is far below condition-based bound $\gamma_{3n}2^{-n}/\delta^n$ (about $3\times10^{2}$ for $n=10$ and $\delta=0.01$).
Stage-$r$ values here are $b_i^{(r)}=(-1)^{n-i-r}2^{-n}(2\delta)^r$, with each stage's cancellation relative to that stage's magnitude.
Same-stage values have equal magnitude and alternating signs, so their rounding errors follow the same alternation and shrink by the same factor $2\delta$ at the next stage.
Each stage rounds by relative error about $u(1+1/(2\delta))$ of its result, giving total relative error about $nu(1+1/(2\delta))$.
Conditioning bounds concern worst-case arbitrary coefficient perturbations and overestimate actual errors for structured coefficients such as these.

The de Casteljau report estimate is a conservative 238 at $\delta=0.01$, near the worst-case bound above.
Horner's estimate at $\delta=0.01$ is 69.5, below actual relative error $3.9\times10^3$.
Estimates divide by computed $|\hat p|$, so when rounding obscures that value, they can underestimate actual relative error.
Estimates are not upper bounds; values above 1 indicate no significant digits in the computed value.
Conversion `condition_estimate()` matches closed form $\max_k\binom nk2^k$, growing through 32, 15360, and $6.35\times10^8$ for $n=4,10,20$.

## Intervals and basis conversion

On physical interval $[a,b]$, set $t=(x-a)/(b-a)$; derivatives multiply by $1/(b-a)$.
`BernsteinBasis` retains interval and control points, including trailing zeros as degree-determining coordinates.
`ApproxPolynomial::bernstein(controls, lower, upper)` rejects empty control points or $\mathrm{lower}\ge\mathrm{upper}$ with `InvalidInput`, and non-finite control points or endpoints with `NonFinite`.
Use `bernstein_with_options(controls, lower, upper, policy)` for a nondefault policy.
Evaluation at an endpoint returns its control point unchanged without convex combinations.
Only convex combinations with intermediates outside square-root range (about $2^{-511}$ to $2^{510}$ for `f64`) retry in extended exponent range.

Single-point evaluation allocates a temporary vector of control-point count per call.
Allocation-free `evaluate_batch_into` uses scratch of point count plus control-point count for $m$ points; `workspace_requirements(m)` returns $2m+(n+1)$, also including derivative-batch storage.
Evaluation outside intervals or at complex points is supported without convex hull properties; reports use the preceding $(|t|+|1-t|)^n$ bound.
`subdivide(point)` splits into two Bernstein representations at a strictly interior point; endpoints or exterior points give `InvalidInput`, non-finite points `NonFinite`.
`elevate_degree(degree)` raises degree through adjacent-control convex combinations without changing the function; degree lowering gives `InvalidInput`.
`derivative`, `antiderivative(anchor, value)`, and `integral(lower, upper)` also stay in Bernstein form.
Derivative control points are $n(b_{i+1}-b_i)/(b-a)$; antiderivative controls are $d_0=0$ and $d_{i+1}=d_i+(b-a)b_i/(n+1)$ plus a constant.

Monomial conversion is explicit.
`to_monomial` forms monomial coefficients $\binom nr\Delta^rb_0$ in $[0,1]$ from forward differences $\Delta^rb_0$ and substitutes the affine interval mapping.
Low-level `backend::polynomial_basis::bernstein_to_power_into` handles these differences and binomial recurrence $\binom n{r+1}=\binom nr(n-r)/(r+1)$.
Monomial `to_bernstein(lower, upper)` forms $b_i=\sum_{j\le i}\binom ij\binom nj^{-1}\hat a_j$ from coefficients $\hat a_j$ after substitution of the interval mapping.
Both cost $O(n^2)$, retain intermediates in extended exponent range, and round only final coefficients to the ordinary type.
Final coefficient overflow or nonzero coefficients underflowing to zero give `NumericalInstability`.
For example, interval $[-10^{308},10^{308}]$ controls $(0,1)$ convert to monomial coefficient $0.5$ and subnormal $5\times10^{-309}$; inverse conversion restores $(0,1)$.
Conversion report `condition_estimate()` is the ratio of maximum coefficient magnitudes before and after, excluding digits lost through cancellation within sums.
For $n=10$ in the cell, the ratio is 15360 and grows exponentially with degree as sum $\sum_k\binom nk2^k=3^n$ shows.

## Summary

Bernstein form retains interval and control points and uses only convex combinations for de Casteljau evaluation and subdivision in $O(n^2)$ operations.
For $[0,1]$, evaluation error is bounded by $\gamma_{3n}\sum_i|b_i|B_i^n(t)\le\gamma_{3n}\max_i|b_i|$ and the condition number near roots is smaller than for monomial Horner evaluation; retain Bernstein form for interval evaluation or root isolation.
Reported `forward_error_estimate()` divides by computed values; above 1 means no significant digits, and below 1 still provides no guarantee.
Monomial conversion is ill-conditioned; interpret reported `condition_estimate()` as a lower indicator excluding cancellation.
