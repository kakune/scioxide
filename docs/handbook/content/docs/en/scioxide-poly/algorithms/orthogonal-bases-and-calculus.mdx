Evaluating one orthogonal polynomial and evaluating a finite expansion in that family are different computations.
An expansion $p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n\gamma_k\phi_k(x)$ requires retaining family, normalization, and coordinate mapping alongside coefficients $\gamma_k$.
In `scioxide-poly`, `OrthogonalBasisSpec` specifies all three, `OrthogonalBasisPlan` constructed by `prepare` prepares recurrences, and `OrthogonalBasis` inside `ApproxPolynomial` owns coefficients and Plan.
Evaluation and conversion between orthogonal bases use prepared recurrences without monomial coefficients.
This page explains basis specification and preparation, Clenshaw evaluation and rounding, and conversion operators; the final cell examines growth when converting Chebyshev to monomials.
Family intervals, weights, and recurrences appear in [Orthogonal polynomial families](../orthogonal-families/); same-basis derivatives and antiderivatives in [Differentiation and integration of orthogonal expansions](../orthogonal-calculus/).

## Physical coordinates and normalization

`OrthogonalBasisSpec::new(family, normalization, shift, scale)` connects physical coordinate $x$ to family reference coordinate $t$ through

$$
t=\frac{x-\mathrm{shift}}{\mathrm{scale}},\qquad \mathrm{scale}>0
$$


Mapping finite interval $[x_{\rm a},x_{\rm b}]$ to $[-1,1]$ uses `shift` $=(x_{\rm a}+x_{\rm b})/2$ and `scale` $=(x_{\rm b}-x_{\rm a})/2$.
Do not mistake the last two arguments for interval endpoints.
`new` also validates family parameters; out-of-range parameters or $\mathrm{scale}\le0$ give `DomainError`, non-finite values `NonFinite`.

Use `OrthogonalBasisSpec::on_interval(family, lower, upper)` to construct from endpoints.
It computes `shift` and `scale` with one rounding each while avoiding intermediate overflow, producing a `Classical` specification.
Non-finite endpoints give `NonFinite`; $\mathrm{lower}\ge\mathrm{upper}$ or a half-width rounded to zero gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`); Laguerre and Hermite families, whose support is not $[-1,1]$, give `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).
`OrthogonalBasisSpec::classical(family)` returns a classical specification with `shift` $=0$ and `scale` $=1$; `with_normalization(normalization)` returns a specification with only the normalization changed.
`new` applies the same transform to semi-infinite and infinite families; for example, `Laguerre` has support $x\ge\mathrm{shift}$ and `HermiteH` has weight $e^{-((x-\mathrm{shift})/\mathrm{scale})^2}$.
Read settings through `family()`, `normalization()`, `shift()`, and `scale()`.

`BasisNormalization` selects constant scalings of basis elements.
Let reference classical polynomials be $P_k(t)$, leading coefficients $\ell_k$, squared norms $h_k=\int P_k(t)^2w(t)\,dt$, and scale $s=\mathrm{scale}$; the three normalizations represent these bases.

| Variant | Basis $\phi_k(x)$ | Property |
|---|---|---|
| `Classical` | $P_k(t)$ | Conventional family normalization (such as $T_k(1)=1$) |
| `Monic` | $s^kP_k(t)/\ell_k$ | Leading coefficient 1 in physical coordinate $x$ |
| `Orthonormal` | $P_k(t)/\sqrt{s\,h_k}$ | $\int\phi_j\phi_k\,w(t)\,dx=\delta_{jk}$ |

`Orthonormal` gives unit squared norm under physical measure $w((x-\mathrm{shift})/\mathrm{scale})\,dx$, including the $s$ factor from $dx=s\,dt$.
Signs follow classical families, so some elements, such as odd Laguerre degrees, have negative leading coefficients.
Monic forms use $d_k,e_k$ from symmetric tridiagonalization in [Jacobi matrices and zeros](oxref:poly-jacobi-matrix-reduction) and satisfy

$$
\phi_{k+1}(x)=\bigl(x-\mathrm{shift}-s\,d_k\bigr)\phi_k(x)-s^2e_k^2\,\phi_{k-1}(x)
$$


Preparation converts each normalized recurrence to physical coordinates and stores it.
Changing normalization changes coefficients of the same function by inverse basis scale factors, also changing coefficient norms and perturbation models.

## Evaluating prepared expansions

`spec.prepare(degree, options)` allocates `degree + 1` recurrence-coefficient sets through degree `degree`, returning `OrthogonalBasisPlan`.
Preparation costs $O(n)$ time and memory; for `Orthonormal`, it also determines initial element $\phi_0=1/\sqrt{\mu_0s}$ ($\mu_0=\int w(t)\,dt$).
Unrepresentable coefficients or $\phi_0$, or rounding to zero, gives `NumericalInstability`; `options` permitting more than 64 workers gives `InvalidInput`.
The Plan is immutable; inspect preparation through `degree()`, `spec()`, and `options()`.

`ApproxPolynomial::orthogonal(coefficients, spec)` derives degree from coefficient count, prepares a Plan with default execution settings, and constructs a default-policy polynomial.
Use `ApproxPolynomial::orthogonal_with_plan(coefficients, plan, policy)` to choose Plan degree, execution settings, or policy.
Both polynomials own coefficients in ascending degree order and the Plan.
Coefficients must be nonempty and finite; for `orthogonal_with_plan`, their count must be at most the Plan's `degree() + 1`.
Empty coefficients give `InvalidInput` (`Empty`), excessive coefficients `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`), and non-finite coefficients `NonFinite`.
Shorter expansions retain maximum Plan degree and execution settings unchanged.
The inner basis `OrthogonalBasis` borrows its Plan with `basis().plan()`; coefficients are read with `PolynomialBasis::coefficients`.
Trailing zeros remain structural coefficients; no hidden mutable cache exists.

### Clenshaw's algorithm

Expansion values use Clenshaw's algorithm.
For basis recurrence $\phi_{k+1}=(a_kx+b_k)\phi_k-c_k\phi_{k-1}$ ($\phi_{-1}=0$), start from $B_{n+1}=B_{n+2}=0$ and update

$$
B_k=\gamma_k+(a_kx+b_k)B_{k+1}-c_{k+1}B_{k+2}\quad(k=n,\dots,0),\qquad p(x)=\phi_0B_0
$$

backward.
Substitute $\gamma_k=B_k-(a_kx+b_k)B_{k+1}+c_{k+1}B_{k+2}$ into $\sum_k\gamma_k\phi_k$ and collect coefficients of $B_{k+1}$ using $\phi_{k+1}-(a_kx+b_k)\phi_k+c_k\phi_{k-1}=0$; only $\phi_0B_0$ remains, deriving this formula.
No individual basis value $\phi_k(x)$ is formed, giving single-point cost $O(n)$ and constant extra memory.
For Chebyshev first kind in reference coordinate $t$ ($a_0=1$, $a_k=2$, $b_k=0$, $c_k=1$), $p=\gamma_0+tB_1-B_2$.
Some conventions multiply $\gamma_0$ by $1/2$; SciOxide uses every coefficient unchanged.

Rounding can be interpreted as backward error.
Error $\delta_k$ introduced when computing $B_k$ gives the same result as exact computation with coefficient $\gamma_k+\delta_k$, because later updates are linear in $B_k$.
The computed value is therefore exact for coefficient perturbations $|\delta_k|\lesssim cu\bigl(|\gamma_k|+|a_kx+b_k||B_{k+1}|+|c_{k+1}||B_{k+2}|\bigr)$ ($u$ is unit roundoff and $c$ a small constant), with error bounded by $\bigl|\sum_k\delta_k\phi_k(x)\bigr|\le\max_k|\delta_k|\sum_k|\phi_k(x)|$ (A. Smoktunowicz, "Backward stability of Clenshaw's algorithm", *BIT* 42, 2002, 600–610).
Perturbation magnitude depends on intermediate $|B_k|$ rather than $|\gamma_k|$.
For Chebyshev, $k\ge1$ gives $B_k=\sum_{j\ge k}\gamma_jU_{j-k}(t)$ ($U_m$ is second kind), and the bound on $|U_m(t)|\le m+1$ is attained at $t=\pm1$.
Near endpoints, $|B_k|$ can thus grow to $O(n)$ times $\sum_j|\gamma_j|$.
Since $|T_k(t)|\le1$, error is at most $\sum_k|\delta_k|$; this bound can be $O(n^2)$ times $u\sum_j|\gamma_j|$.
Inside the interval, $|U_m(t)|\le1/\sqrt{1-t^2}$ makes amplification smaller.

The same pass computes derivatives and envelope $\sum_k|\gamma_k||\phi_k(x)|$; its ratio to $|p(x)|$ supplies the condition estimate in `PolynomialEvaluation::condition()`.
This models perturbations proportional to coefficient magnitudes, excluding intermediate $|B_k|$ growth, so it is not an error upper bound.
If ordinary intermediates approach range loss, evaluation retries in extended-exponent arithmetic, avoiding failure from intermediate overflow while final values remain representable.
Outside the interval the basis itself can grow rapidly; choosing orthogonal bases alone does not preserve extrapolation accuracy.

Evaluation APIs are as follows.

- `polynomial.evaluate(point)` returns the owned polynomial's value; `polynomial.evaluate_with_derivative(point)` returns `PolynomialEvaluation` with value, derivative, and condition estimate.
  Neither allocates for orthogonal bases.
- `plan.evaluate_with_derivative(&coefficients, point)` borrows coefficients and returns the same value, derivative, and condition estimate, avoiding a newly owned polynomial per call.
- `polynomial.evaluate_many_with_derivative(&points)` is an allocation-free iterator returning `evaluate_with_derivative` results sequentially.
  It shares coefficient loads across 4 points with at most 32 coefficients or 8 points otherwise, making it faster than separate point calls.
  Failure at one point does not prevent later evaluations.
- `plan.evaluate_batch_into(&coefficients, &points, &mut output, &mut scratch, context)` writes only values into `output`, distributing points among workers permitted by Plan settings and `context`.
  Caller scratch `scratch` needs length `workspace_requirements(points.len())` (point count); insufficient length gives `DimensionMismatch`.
  Different `output` and `points` lengths also give `DimensionMismatch`; stops give `ExecutionStopped`.
  `output` is updated only after all points succeed; on failure only `scratch` may change.

## Basis-conversion operators

`to_orthogonal(spec)` converts monomial, Bernstein, or orthogonal polynomials to another orthogonal basis; `to_monomial()` converts to monomials.
Both return `PolynomialConversion` with converted polynomials and conditioning reports, exposing coefficient growth and backward-error estimates together.
Orthogonal-source `to_orthogonal` retains original execution settings; monomial and Bernstein sources use defaults, preparing target Plans through structural expansion degree.
Orthogonal-to-orthogonal conversion applies Clenshaw in coefficient space, expressing multiplication by $x$ through the target three-term recurrence (a tridiagonal operator), without monomials.
Monomial conversion uses the same operator for Horner in coefficient space.
Both cost $O(n^2)$ time and $O(n)$ memory.
Bernstein conversion passes through monomials and inherits their high-degree coefficient ill-conditioning.
Conversion to the same specification copies coefficients unchanged.
The condition estimate in `PolynomialConversion` is the target-to-source maximum coefficient magnitude ratio (at least 1), not a proof of conversion error under cancellation.

For repeated conversions, reuse a matrix-prepared `OrthogonalTransform`.
`plan.conversion_operator(&target)` prepares between orthogonal bases; `from_monomial_operator()` from monomials; `monomial_operator()` to monomials.
Column $j$ contains target expansion coefficients of the source degree-$j$ element; advancing the source recurrence in target coefficient space builds columns successively.
Degree does not increase, making the matrix upper triangular.
`conversion_operator` requires target maximum degree at least source degree; otherwise `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).
Preparation and storage cost $O(n^2)$ for a dense $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ matrix; `entries()` borrows entries column-major.
Output coefficients are in ascending degree order.
Query output and scratch lengths with `rows`, `cols`, and `apply_workspace_len` of `LinearOperator`; `apply_into` costs $O(n^2)$ and writes only on success.
`apply` is a convenience API allocating output and scratch.
Polynomial methods `to_orthogonal_with_operator` and `to_monomial_with_operator` check source specification against polynomial basis and column count against coefficient count, then return `PolynomialConversion` without rebuilding the matrix.
Column mismatches give `DimensionMismatch`; source or target mismatches give `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).
Unlike evaluation, shorter expansions cannot directly use longer operators.

## Monomial conversion and coefficient growth

Writing $T_m$ in monomials gives coefficients $c_k$ at alternating degrees with alternating signs.
Thus $T_m(\mathrm{i}x)=\mathrm{i}^m\sum_k|c_k|x^k$; setting $x=1$ gives the sum of absolute coefficients

$$
\sum_k|c_k|=|T_m(\mathrm{i})|=\frac{(1+\sqrt2)^m+(1-\sqrt2)^m}{2}\approx\frac{(1+\sqrt2)^m}{2}
$$


A function of size $|T_m|\le1$ on $[-1,1]$ is thus represented by cancellation of coefficients with absolute sum around $(1+\sqrt2)^m/2$.
Relative perturbations $u$ of each $c_k$ can change the function over $[-1,1]$ by up to about $u\sum_k|c_k|$, without cancellation of these changes.
Rounding monomial coefficients and converting back can therefore grow errors to about $u\sum_k|c_k|$.
This indicator increases by $\log_{10}(1+\sqrt2)\approx0.38$ digits per degree $m$.

The next cell prepares a maximum-degree-$n$ Chebyshev basis and builds `monomial_operator()` and `from_monomial_operator()` matrices once.
For $m=0,\dots,n$, convert coefficients of $\gamma T_m$ ($\gamma=0.1$) to monomials, divide absolute coefficient sums by $\gamma$, and compare with the closed form above.
Choosing $\gamma$ that cannot be represented exactly in binary exposes rounding propagation; integer coefficients alone would give exact products.
The closed form is a reference using f64 intrinsic `powi`.
Convert monomial coefficients back to Chebyshev; divide the maximum original-coefficient discrepancy by $\gamma$ for relative round-trip error.
The table's condition estimate is the value from `PolynomialConversion` when converting polynomial $\gamma T_n$ through `to_monomial_with_operator`: the target-to-source maximum absolute coefficient ratio, equal to the maximum monomial coefficient magnitude of $T_n$.
The input is maximum degree $n$; coordinates and coefficients are dimensionless.

```rust
//| id: poly-chebyshev-monomial-growth
//| ref: poly-chebyshev-monomial-growth
//| title: "Digits lost in Chebyshev–monomial round trips"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Maximum degree n","min":4,"max":48,"step":4,"value":24}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"low","label":"n = 8","description":"The absolute coefficient sum is 577; relative round-trip error stays around 10⁻¹⁴.","inputs":{"degree":8}}
//|   - {"id":"high","label":"n = 48","description":"The absolute coefficient sum exceeds 10¹⁸ and relative round-trip error exceeds 1, leaving no correct coefficient digit.","inputs":{"degree":48}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"growth","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":768398401,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"growth","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"growth","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":1e-10,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"growth","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":199229440,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: low
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"growth","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":577,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"growth","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: high
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"growth","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.1808722053187136e18,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"growth","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0.5}}
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, OrthogonalBasisSpec, OrthogonalFamily};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let n = degree as usize;
let spec =
    OrthogonalBasisSpec::classical(OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevT).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plan = spec
    .prepare(n, Default::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Prepare matrices once and apply them to unit vectors for each degree.
let to_power = plan.monomial_operator().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let from_power = plan.from_monomial_operator().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut reference, mut errors, mut exact) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), 0_usize);
let (mut last_sum, mut worst_relative, mut worst_error) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
// Multiply T_m by non-binary-representable coefficient γ (integer coefficients alone give no rounding).
let gamma = 0.1_f64;
let mut unit = vec![0.0_f64; n + 1];
for m in 0..=n {
    unit.fill(0.0);
    unit[m] = gamma;
    // Monomial coefficients of γT_m and coefficients after converting them back to Chebyshev
    let power = to_power.apply(&unit).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let back = from_power.apply(&power).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    last_sum = power.iter().map(|c| c.abs()).sum::<f64>() / gamma;
    // Independent check: Σ|c_k| = |T_m(i)| = ((1 + √2)^m + (1 − √2)^m)/2 (reference using f64 powi)
    let root = std::f64::consts::SQRT_2;
    let closed = ((1.0 + root).powi(m as i32) + (1.0 - root).powi(m as i32)) / 2.0;
    worst_relative = worst_relative.max((last_sum - closed).abs() / closed);
    reference.push([m as f64, f64::EPSILON * closed]);
    let error = back
        .iter()
        .zip(&unit)
        .map(|(b, u)| (b - u).abs() / gamma)
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
    worst_error = worst_error.max(error);
    // Count zero-error degrees that cannot be plotted logarithmically and show them in the table.
    if error > 0.0 {
        errors.push([m as f64, error]);
    } else {
        exact += 1;
    }
}
// Convert polynomial T_n to monomials with the prepared matrix and inspect conditioning.
let polynomial = ApproxPolynomial::orthogonal_with_plan(unit, plan, Default::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let conversion = polynomial
    .to_monomial_with_operator(&to_power)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum degree n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Σ|c_k| of T_n [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum relative closed-form discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum relative round-trip error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Condition estimate for T_n conversion [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Degrees with relative error 0 [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let row = (
    n,
    last_sum,
    worst_relative,
    worst_error,
    conversion.condition().condition_estimate(),
    exact,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "growth" })
);
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "xLabel": "Degree m",
    "yLabel": "Relative round-trip error [-]",
    "yType": "log",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "layers": [
        {
            "id": "reference",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "ε · ((1+√2)^m + (1−√2)^m)/2",
            "points": reference,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
        },
        { "id": "round-trip", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Relative round-trip error (SciOxide)", "points": errors }
    ]
}));
```

At default $n=24$, $T_{24}$ has absolute coefficient sum $7.68\times10^8$ with relative closed-form discrepancy $9.6\times10^{-16}$.
Maximum relative round-trip error is $6.7\times10^{-9}$, losing about 8 of the coefficients' 16 digits.
Observed points grow at the same slope as dashed $\varepsilon\sum_k|c_k|$, lying 1–2 orders of magnitude below it.
At $n=8$, the sum is 577 and relative round-trip error only $8.9\times10^{-15}$.
At $n=48$, the sum reaches $1.18\times10^{18}$ with relative round-trip error 5, leaving no correct digit in recovered coefficients.
Exactly zero-error degrees are omitted from logarithmic plots and counted in the table.
The condition estimate $2.0\times10^8$ at $n=24$ indicates the trend in coefficient growth, not round-trip error itself.
Retaining orthogonal bases for evaluation and conversion avoids this growth; do not store high-degree expansions as monomial coefficients.

## Summary

Retain coefficients with their prepared basis; specify family, normalization, and affine coordinates once through `OrthogonalBasisSpec`.
`on_interval` forms center and scale from endpoints, preventing confusion with the final two arguments of `new`.
Clenshaw rounding is a coefficient perturbation depending on intermediate $|B_k|$; Chebyshev error bounds near endpoints can grow to $O(n^2)$ times $u\sum_j|\gamma_j|$.
Orthogonal-to-orthogonal conversions avoid monomials; monomial conversions grow absolute coefficient sums at rate $(1+\sqrt2)^n$, and round-trip errors grow proportionally.
Prepare and reuse `OrthogonalTransform` for repeated conversions, querying scratch length through `apply_workspace_len`.
