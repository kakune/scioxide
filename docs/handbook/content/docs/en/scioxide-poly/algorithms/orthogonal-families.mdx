Orthogonal polynomials form a sequence whose different degrees are orthogonal under an inner product specified by an interval and weight.
Orthogonality simplifies continuous least-squares projection, but does not make matrices from arbitrary discrete samples orthogonal.
This page explains classical-family conventions (intervals, weights, normalizations) and single-point recurrence evaluation, then checks Chebyshev equioscillation and zeros in a cell.
Jacobi matrices with zeros as eigenvalues are covered in [Jacobi matrices and zeros](../jacobi-matrix-and-zeros/); expansion bases and conversions in [Preparing, evaluating, and converting orthogonal bases](../orthogonal-bases-and-calculus/).

## Weighted inner products and orthogonal polynomials

For weight $w(x)>0$ on interval $I\subseteq\mathbb{R}$, define the inner product by

$$
\langle f,g\rangle_w=\int_I f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,w(x)\,dx
$$


Assume moments $\int_I x^kw(x)\,dx$ through the required degree are finite and every nonzero polynomial has positive squared norm.

A polynomial sequence $\{P_n\}_{n\ge0}$ ($\deg P_n=n$) satisfying

$$
\langle P_j,P_k\rangle_w=h_k\,\delta_{jk},\qquad h_k=\|P_k\|_w^2>0
$$

is an orthogonal polynomial system for weight $w$.
Call $h_0=\int_Iw(x)\,dx$ the weight's mass, also denoted $\mu_0$.

To approximate a square-integrable function $f$ by polynomial $f_n=\sum_{k=0}^na_kP_k$ of degree at most $n$, coefficients minimizing weighted squared error $\|f-f_n\|_w^2$ are

$$
a_k=\frac{\langle f,P_k\rangle_w}{h_k}=\frac1{h_k}\int_If(x)P_k(x)w(x)\,dx
$$


An orthogonal basis makes the normal-equation matrix diagonal, allowing independent computation of each coefficient.
Increasing $n$ leaves existing coefficients unchanged.

## Three-term recurrences and Favard's theorem

$xP_n$ has degree $n+1$ and is therefore a linear combination of $P_0,\dots,P_{n+1}$.
For $k\le n-2$, $\langle xP_n,P_k\rangle_w=\langle P_n,xP_k\rangle_w$, and since $xP_k$ has degree at most $n-1$, this inner product vanishes.
Thus only $P_{n+1}$, $P_n$, and $P_{n-1}$ remain in the expansion of $xP_n$, giving

$$
P_{n+1}(x)=(a_nx+b_n)P_n(x)-c_nP_{n-1}(x)\qquad(n\ge0,\ P_{-1}=0,\ P_0=1)
$$

the three-term recurrence.
Coefficients $a_n,b_n,c_n$ depend on constant scaling (normalization) of each $P_n$.

Writing leading coefficient $\ell_n$ of $P_n$ gives $\ell_{n+1}=a_n\ell_n$; monic polynomials $\pi_n=P_n/\ell_n$ satisfy

$$
\pi_{n+1}(x)=(x-d_n)\pi_n(x)-e_n^2\pi_{n-1}(x),\qquad
d_n=-\frac{b_n}{a_n},\quad e_n^2=\frac{c_n}{a_na_{n-1}}
$$


Taking its inner product with $\pi_{n-1}$ gives $e_n^2=\|\pi_n\|_w^2/\|\pi_{n-1}\|_w^2>0$.
Conversely, a real sequence following a three-term recurrence with $a_n\ne0$ and $c_n/(a_na_{n-1})>0$ ($n\ge1$) admits a positive measure making the polynomials orthogonal (Favard's theorem).
That measure need not have a density function $w(x)$.
SciOxide checks positivity when constructing [Jacobi matrices](oxref:poly-jacobi-matrix-coefficients), returning $a_n=0$ or $e_n^2\le0$ as `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).

Recurrence computation of $P_n(x)$ avoids monomial coefficients and uses $O(n)$ operations with constant extra memory.
Besides $P_n$, the three-term recurrence has a second-kind function solution satisfying the same recurrence.
Outside the support, $P_n$ is dominant (grows faster than the other solution); on the support, both solutions oscillate with comparable magnitudes (Gautschi, 2004).
Forward computation is stable in both cases, without the exponential error amplification of forward computation of a recessive solution.
Outside the support, however, $P_n$ itself grows rapidly with degree; near zeros, cancellation can increase relative error.

## Classical orthogonal polynomial families

`OrthogonalFamily` represents nine families.
Parameters and single-point evaluation arguments of `OrthogonalFamily` are real.
Expansions in [prepared bases](../orthogonal-bases-and-calculus/) also accept complex points and coefficients.

| Variant | Polynomial | Interval $I$ | Weight $w(x)$ | Squared norm $h_n$ | Parameters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ChebyshevT` | Chebyshev first kind $T_n$ | $[-1,1]$ | $(1-x^2)^{-1/2}$ | $h_0=\pi$, $h_n=\pi/2$ | None |
| `ChebyshevU` | Chebyshev second kind $U_n$ | $[-1,1]$ | $(1-x^2)^{1/2}$ | $\pi/2$ | None |
| `Legendre` | Legendre $P_n$ | $[-1,1]$ | $1$ | $2/(2n+1)$ | None |
| `Jacobi { alpha, beta }` | Jacobi $P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}$ | $[-1,1]$ | $(1-x)^\alpha(1+x)^\beta$ | $\dfrac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\,n!}$ | $\alpha,\beta>-1$ |
| `Gegenbauer { lambda }` | Gegenbauer $C_n^{(\lambda)}$ | $[-1,1]$ | $(1-x^2)^{\lambda-1/2}$ | $\dfrac{2^{1-2\lambda}\pi\,\Gamma(n+2\lambda)}{(n+\lambda)\Gamma(\lambda)^2\,n!}$ | $\lambda>-1/2$, $\lambda\ne0$ |
| `Laguerre` | Laguerre $L_n$ | $[0,\infty)$ | $e^{-x}$ | $1$ | None |
| `GeneralizedLaguerre { alpha }` | Generalized Laguerre $L_n^{(\alpha)}$ | $[0,\infty)$ | $x^\alpha e^{-x}$ | $\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)/n!$ | $\alpha>-1$ |
| `HermiteH` | Physicists' Hermite $H_n$ | $(-\infty,\infty)$ | $e^{-x^2}$ | $\sqrt\pi\,2^nn!$ | None |
| `HermiteHe` | Probabilists' Hermite $He_n$ | $(-\infty,\infty)$ | $e^{-x^2/2}$ | $\sqrt{2\pi}\,n!$ | None |

Weight mass is $\mu_0=h_0$: $2^{\alpha+\beta+1}B(\alpha+1,\beta+1)$ for Jacobi, $B(1/2,\lambda+1/2)$ for Gegenbauer, and $\Gamma(\alpha+1)$ for generalized Laguerre.
Classical normalizations and recurrence coefficients are as follows.

| Variant | $a_n$ | $b_n$ | $c_n$ | $P_1$ | Normalization |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ChebyshevT` | $a_0=1$, $a_n=2$ | $0$ | $1$ | $x$ | $T_n(1)=1$ |
| `ChebyshevU` | $2$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2x$ | $U_n(1)=n+1$ |
| `Legendre` | $\frac{2n+1}{n+1}$ | $0$ | $\frac{n}{n+1}$ | $x$ | $P_n(1)=1$ |
| `Jacobi` | $A_n$ below | $B_n$ below | $C_n$ below | $\frac{(\alpha-\beta)+(\alpha+\beta+2)x}{2}$ | $P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}(1)=\binom{n+\alpha}{n}$ |
| `Gegenbauer` | $\frac{2(n+\lambda)}{n+1}$ | $0$ | $\frac{n+2\lambda-1}{n+1}$ | $2\lambda x$ | $C_n^{(\lambda)}(1)=\binom{n+2\lambda-1}{n}$ |
| `Laguerre` | $-\frac1{n+1}$ | $\frac{2n+1}{n+1}$ | $\frac{n}{n+1}$ | $1-x$ | $L_n(0)=1$ |
| `GeneralizedLaguerre` | $-\frac1{n+1}$ | $\frac{2n+\alpha+1}{n+1}$ | $\frac{n+\alpha}{n+1}$ | $1+\alpha-x$ | $L_n^{(\alpha)}(0)=\binom{n+\alpha}{n}$ |
| `HermiteH` | $2$ | $0$ | $2n$ | $2x$ | Leading coefficient $2^n$ |
| `HermiteHe` | $1$ | $0$ | $n$ | $x$ | Monic |

For Jacobi coefficients, setting $s=\alpha+\beta$ gives, for $n\ge1$,

$$
A_n=\frac{(2n+s+1)(2n+s+2)}{2(n+1)(n+s+1)},\qquad
B_n=\frac{(2n+s+1)(\alpha^2-\beta^2)}{2(n+1)(n+s+1)(2n+s)},
$$

$$
C_n=\frac{(n+\alpha)(n+\beta)(2n+s+2)}{(n+1)(n+s+1)(2n+s)}
$$

(DLMF 18.9.2).
At $n=0$, the general expression includes $0/0$ for $s=0$ or $s=-1$, so use $a_0=(s+2)/2$ and $b_0=(\alpha-\beta)/2$ separately.
`recurrence_coefficients(n)` returns the $(a_n,b_n,c_n)$ above with these conventions unchanged.
$c_0$ multiplies $P_{-1}=0$ and has no meaningful value; some families return nonzero values (1 for `ChebyshevT` and `ChebyshevU`, $\alpha$ for `GeneralizedLaguerre`).
Laguerre has $a_n<0$, making leading coefficients alternate in sign with degree, but $c_n/(a_na_{n-1})=n^2>0$ and Favard's condition holds.

### Family parameters and relationships

`evaluate`, `evaluate_derivative`, `weight`, `zeros`, `recurrence_coefficients`, `jacobi_recurrence`, and `log_weight_mass` of `OrthogonalFamily` check family parameters on every call.
`OrthogonalBasisSpec::new` performs the same checks before preparation.
Non-finite parameters give `NonFinite` (`Input`); out-of-range parameters give `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
The latter identifies which argument `alpha`, `beta`, or `lambda` is out of range.

The Jacobi and generalized Laguerre condition $\alpha,\beta>-1$ ensures endpoint weight integrability.
Gegenbauer weight $(1-x^2)^{\lambda-1/2}$ requires $\lambda>-1/2$ for the same reason.
$\lambda=0$ is excluded because, under this normalization, $C_n^{(\lambda)}$ for $n\ge1$ tends to zero as $\lambda\to0$.
The limiting family is $\lim_{\lambda\to0}C_n^{(\lambda)}/\lambda=(2/n)T_n$; use `ChebyshevT`.

Families have these relationships.

- Gegenbauer becomes Legendre at $\lambda=1/2$ and Chebyshev second kind at $\lambda=1$.
- Jacobi becomes Legendre at $\alpha=\beta=0$ and a constant multiple of Gegenbauer at $\alpha=\beta=\lambda-1/2$.
- $T_n'=nU_{n-1}$ connects Chebyshev first and second kinds.
- The Hermite conventions satisfy $H_n(x)=2^{n/2}He_n(\sqrt2\,x)$; zeros of $H_n$ are $1/\sqrt2$ times those of $He_n$.

Hermite zero scales, weights, and norms all differ; merely changing the name does not yield the same computation.

## Single-point evaluation and weights

`evaluate(n, x)` returns the value of the degree-$n$ polynomial; `evaluate_derivative(n, x)` returns the first derivative.
Point $x$ may be any finite real number, including outside weight support.
Evaluation paths differ by family.

- Values of `Legendre` are delegated to `scioxide_special::legendre_p` under its accuracy, degree, and default-work contracts.
  scioxide-special's tested accuracy is within 64 ulp for f64; near zeros, this becomes an absolute rather than relative error bound.
  Reaching its work limit propagates that evaluator's error unchanged.
- `ChebyshevT`, `ChebyshevU`, `HermiteH`, and `HermiteHe` use integer-coefficient forward recurrences.
  Hermite uses forward recurrence like other families, without a separate parity-based path omitting terms.
- Other families (including the derivative of `Legendre`) use the general forward recurrence.
  Intermediate states are checked at fixed stage intervals and power-of-two scaled; exceeding ordinary range retries in extended-exponent form.
- Derivatives advance the differentiated recurrence alongside values, costing $O(n)$ for every family.

Representable final values are returned even if intermediates exceed ordinary floating-point range.
Unrepresentable final values give errors rather than infinity.
For example, $H_{300}(0)=300!/150!\approx5\times10^{351}$ exceeds binary64 maximum, so `HermiteH.evaluate(300, 0.0)` gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`), while odd-degree $H_{301}(0)=0$ returns a value.
Invalid parameters, non-finite points, or degrees not exactly representable in the scalar also give errors.

`weight(x)` returns orthogonality weight values; points outside support give `DomainError`.
Weights with finite endpoint limits return them (`ChebyshevU` is 0 at $\pm1$; `Jacobi { alpha: 0.0, beta: 1.0 }` is $2$ at $x=1$).
Divergent-weight endpoints, such as $\pm1$ for `ChebyshevT`, also give `DomainError`.
Very small weights may round to zero.

`log_weight_mass()` returns $\log\mu_0$.
For large parameters, $\mu_0$ itself exceeds range; compute logarithmically with `ln_beta` or `ln_gamma`.
This value is independent of polynomial normalization.

## Chebyshev equioscillation and zeros

The next cell evaluates $T_n(x)$ with `evaluate` of `ChebyshevT` at 241 points on $[-1,1]$, independently checking against $\cos(n\arccos x)$.
Compare zeros returned by `zeros` with closed form $\cos((2k-1)\pi/(2n))$, and `evaluate_derivative` values with $nU_{n-1}(x)$ using `ChebyshevU`.
Reference trigonometric functions use SciOxide's `RealScalar::cos` and `RealScalar::acos`.
The input is degree $n$; coordinates and values are dimensionless.

```rust
//| id: poly-chebyshev-family
//| ref: poly-chebyshev-family
//| title: "Vary Chebyshev degree and equioscillation"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree n","description":"Change zero and oscillation counts within the interval.","min":1,"max":20,"step":1,"value":6}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"degree-20","label":"n = 20","description":"Even at higher degree, recurrence–reference discrepancies stay below 10⁻¹⁴.","inputs":{"degree":20}}
//|   - {"id":"degree-1","label":"n = 1","description":"T₁(x) = x has only the exact central zero.","inputs":{"degree":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":6}
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: degree-20
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":20}
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: degree-1
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"chebyshev","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_poly::OrthogonalFamily;

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let n = degree.round() as usize;
let mut curve = Vec::new();
let mut recurrence_gap = 0.0_f64;
let mut derivative_gap = 0.0_f64;
for index in 0..=240 {
    let x = -1.0 + 2.0 * index as f64 / 240.0;
    // T_n(x) and T_n'(x) from SciOxide's three-term recurrence
    let value = OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevT
        .evaluate(n, x)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let slope = OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevT
        .evaluate_derivative(n, x)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Independent check 1: trigonometric T_n(x) = cos(n arccos x)
    let reference = RealScalar::cos(n as f64 * RealScalar::acos(x));
    recurrence_gap = recurrence_gap.max((value - reference).abs());
    // Independent check 2: second-kind relation T_n' = n U_{n-1} (compare after division by |T_n'| ≤ n²)
    let second_kind = OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevU
        .evaluate(n - 1, x)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    derivative_gap =
        derivative_gap.max((slope - n as f64 * second_kind).abs() / (n * n) as f64);
    curve.push([x, value]);
}
// Compare ascending zeros with cos((2k − 1)π/(2n)). Ascending index j corresponds to k = n − j.
let zeros: Vec<f64> = OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevT
    .zeros(n)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let zero_gap = zeros
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, &z)| {
        let angle = (2 * (n - j) - 1) as f64 * std::f64::consts::PI / (2 * n) as f64;
        (z - RealScalar::cos(angle)).abs()
    })
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let maximum_absolute = curve.iter().map(|p| p[1].abs()).fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Zeros [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum zero discrepancy from closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum recurrence discrepancy from cos(n arccos x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum |T_n′ − nU_(n−1)| / n² [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    {
        "key": "4",
        "label": "Maximum sampled |T_n(x)| [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": { "fractionDigits": 6 }
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        zeros.len(),
        zero_gap,
        recurrence_gap,
        derivative_gap,
        maximum_absolute
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "chebyshev" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "T_n(x)", "points": curve })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Position x [-]",
        "yLabel": "T_n(x) [-]",
        "yMin": -1.05,
        "yMax": 1.05
    })
);
```

### Reading equioscillation and zeros

At default $n=6$, the maximum discrepancy of the 6 zeros from closed form is $1.1\times10^{-16}$, and recurrence discrepancy from $\cos(n\arccos x)$ is about $10^{-15}$.
This includes rounding in reference $\cos(n\arccos x)$ itself.
Absolute error in $\theta=\arccos x$ is about $\pi u$ ($u$ is unit roundoff); $\cos(n\theta)$ amplifies it by at most $n$, giving reference-error indicator $n\pi u$.
At $n=20$, discrepancy $6.8\times10^{-15}$ is comparable to indicator $7\times10^{-15}$, suggesting forward-recurrence error no greater than this.
The derivative's discrepancy from $nU_{n-1}$, divided by the maximum $n^2$ of $|T_n'|$, is also at most a few times machine epsilon $\varepsilon\approx2.2\times10^{-16}$.

At $n=1$, `zeros` returns exact 0; table discrepancy $6.1\times10^{-17}$ comes from rounding in reference $\cos(\pi/2)$.

Changing degree still gives $n$ zeros, with all extrema in the interval of magnitude 1.
Equioscillation makes $2^{1-n}T_n$ minimize the maximum absolute value on $[-1,1]$ among monic degree-$n$ polynomials (minimum $2^{1-n}$).
Hence choosing $T_{n+1}$ zeros as interpolation nodes minimizes the maximum magnitude of nodal polynomial $\prod_j(x-x_j)=2^{-n}T_{n+1}(x)$.
Effects on interpolation error and Lebesgue constants are covered in [Interpolation and Chebyshev nodes](../interpolation-and-chebyshev/).

## Summary

Orthogonal families are determined by interval, weight, and normalization; `OrthogonalFamily` variants correspond to the table conventions.
`recurrence_coefficients(n)` returns the coefficients $(a_n,b_n,c_n)$ from the table unchanged; `evaluate` and `evaluate_derivative` advance this recurrence to obtain values and derivatives in $O(n)$.
Representable final values are returned despite intermediate range loss; unrepresentable final values give errors, not infinity.
Hermite conventions differ in variable scales, weights, and norms; check `HermiteH` versus `HermiteHe` before reusing zeros or coefficients.
