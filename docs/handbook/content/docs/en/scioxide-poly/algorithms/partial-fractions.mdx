Partial fractions allow termwise formulas for operations such as inverse Laplace transforms and integration.
SciOxide takes denominator factors and multiplicities from the caller, without searching for factors.
This page covers existence and uniqueness, floating-point coefficient systems and conditioning, reusable decomposition workspaces, and coefficient errors near close poles.
Exact-field coefficients are covered in [Exact partial-fraction decomposition](../exact-partial-fractions/).
The symbol $u$ denotes unit roundoff ($2^{-53}$ for `f64`); polynomial coefficients use ascending powers.

## Decomposition form and uniqueness

Given denominator $D(x) = \prod_{i=1}^r F_i(x)^{m_i}$ as a product of powers of coprime monic factors, rational function $N/D$ has a unique decomposition

$$
\frac{N(x)}{D(x)} = Q(x) + \sum_{i=1}^r \sum_{j=1}^{m_i} \frac{A_{ij}(x)}{F_i(x)^j} \quad (\deg A_{ij} < \deg F_i)
$$

where $r$ counts factors and $Q$ is the quotient (polynomial part) of $N$ divided by $D$.
Existence and uniqueness follow from the Chinese remainder theorem.
Expressing remainder $R = N - QD$ through cofactors $C_i = D/F_i^{m_i}$, $C_i$ is invertible modulo $F_i^{m_i}$, and $A_i = R\, C_i^{-1} \bmod F_i^{m_i}$ is the unique tuple satisfying $R/D = \sum_i A_i / F_i^{m_i}$.
Expanding $A_i$ in base $F_i$ as $A_i = \sum_{k=0}^{m_i - 1} a_k F_i^k$ ($\deg a_k < \deg F_i$), $A_i/F_i^{m_i} = \sum_k a_k / F_i^{m_i - k}$ supplies each power term.

For linear factor $F_i = x - a$, $A_{ij}$ are principal-part coefficients of the Laurent expansion at pole $a$, given by

$$
A_{ij} = \frac{1}{(m_i - j)!} \left.\frac{d^{\,m_i - j}}{dx^{\,m_i - j}} \Big[(x - a)^{m_i} \frac{N(x)}{D(x)}\Big]\right|_{x = a}
$$


$A_{i1}$ is the residue at pole $a$.
A simple pole ($m_i = 1$) gives $A_{i1} = N(a)/D'(a)$.
As simple poles $a, b$ approach, both $D'(a)$ and $D'(b)$ decrease proportionally to $a - b$; residues grow proportionally to $1/(a - b)$ and cancel to reconstruct $N/D$.

## Coefficient equations including repeated poles

After division $N = QD + R$ ($\deg R < n = \deg D$), multiplying both sides by $D$ gives an identity linear in unknown coefficients $A_{ij}$.

$$
R(x) = \sum_{i=1}^r \sum_{j=1}^{m_i} A_{ij}(x)\, F_i(x)^{m_i - j} \prod_{l \ne i} F_l(x)^{m_l}
$$

There are $\sum_i m_i \deg F_i = n$ unknowns and $n$ equations from coefficients of $x^0, \dots, x^{n-1}$, giving an $n \times n$ linear system.
Nonsingularity is equivalent to coprime factors (Chinese-remainder isomorphism $F[x]/(D) \cong \prod_i F[x]/(F_i^{m_i})$).
Nearby poles correspond to coprime but nearly noncoprime factors, making the coefficient matrix nearly singular.

`ApproxPartialFractionPlan::new(&denominator, &[(&factor, multiplicity), ...])` prepares as follows.

1. Check a monic denominator, monic nonconstant factors, positive multiplicities with degree sum equal to denominator degree, and matching policies.
   Violations give `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`); degree sums exceeding `usize` give `SizeOverflow`.
2. Form factor product $P = \prod F_i^{m_i}$ and check that relative coefficient error `factorization_error` $= \max_k |D_k - P_k| / \max(\max_k |D_k|, \max_k |P_k|)$ is at most policy tolerance $\tau_{\text{abs}} + \tau_{\text{rel}}$.
   Exceeding it gives `NumericalInstability` (`IllConditioned`).
3. Build the identity's coefficient matrix using polynomial products rather than finding roots.
   The column for the $x^s$ coefficient of $A_{ij}$ shifts cofactor $F_i^{m_i - j} \prod_{l \ne i} F_l^{m_l}$ coefficients by $s$.
4. Equilibrate columns then rows by their maximum absolute values and perform Householder QR with column pivoting.
5. Determine numerical rank with the rank policy; reject non-full rank as noncoprime or numerically inseparable factors with `NumericalInstability` (`IllConditioned`).

Factors need not be irreducible (such as real-coefficient $x^2 + 1$); the Plan neither searches for factors nor merges nearby poles.
Coefficients may be real or complex.
Preparation uses $O(n^2)$ memory and $O(n^3)$ work.

`decompose(numerator)` divides numerator by factor product $P$ for polynomial part and remainder, solves coefficients through QR, then reverses equilibration.
Division by $P$ rather than the caller's denominator keeps polynomial part and remainder consistent with the factorization defining the equations.
Finally, reconstruct numerator from polynomial part and terms; divide maximum coefficientwise error by the maximum coefficientwise scale (sum of original magnitude and contributing term magnitudes) to obtain `reconstruction_error`.
If this exceeds tolerance $\tau_{\text{abs}} + \tau_{\text{rel}}$, or QR solving does not accept full rank, reject with `NumericalInstability` (`IllConditioned`).
The scale includes contribution magnitudes, so reconstruction by cancellation of large coefficients is also reflected as error.

Near close poles, coefficient equations approach singularity and individual coefficient $A_{ij}$ errors can grow to roughly unit roundoff divided by the reciprocal condition number.
Read this ill-conditioning through `PartialFractionReport`.
`factorization_error()` is the factor-product relative error above; `reconstruction_error()` numerator reconstruction backward error; `rank()` numerical rank of coefficient equations (always $n$ because Plans accept full rank only); `reciprocal_condition()` estimated 1-norm reciprocal condition number of equilibrated equations (Hager–Higham).
A constant denominator has no equations, so `reciprocal_condition()` returns `None`.
All are estimates, not coefficient-error guarantees.

Plan `denominator()` is the denominator; `factors()` is an input-ordered sequence of `PartialFractionFactor`, each with factor `polynomial()`, multiplicity `multiplicity()`, and pole sensitivity `pole_condition()` for linear factors.
`pole_condition()` is a `RootConditionReport` treating pole $a$ as a root of original denominator $D$; factors of degree at least two give `None`.
Repeated poles satisfy $D'(a) = 0$, making absolute sensitivity `absolute()` `Singular`.
Plan `terms()` is a sequence of `PartialFractionTerm` with factor index `factor_index()`, denominator power `power()`, and packed coefficient range `coefficients()`, ordered by input factor then ascending power.

## Plan, workspace, and output

Preallocate memory for repeated decomposition with the same denominator and changing numerators.
`PartialFractionRequirements` returned by `workspace_requirements(numerator_len)` supports numerators with at most `numerator_len` coefficients, describing quotient/long-division storage, extended-exponent scratch, coefficient-system right-hand side and solution, and Householder solve storage.
Quotient requirements use numerator length; remainder requirements use the larger of numerator length and denominator degree, making requirements monotonic in both Plan degree and numerator length.
`degree()` returns denominator degree (packed coefficient count); `required_bytes()` returns workspace allocation bytes (excluding output capacity and `Vec` bookkeeping).
Zero `numerator_len` gives `InvalidInput` (`Empty`).

`PartialFractionWorkspace` is caller-owned workspace, constructed with `plan.workspace(numerator_len)` or allocated from `PartialFractionWorkspace::new()` using `try_reserve(&requirements)`.
Reservations only grow; `check(&requirements)` checks retained capacity, so workspace for the largest Plan and numerator also serves smaller Plans, degrees, and numerators.
Reserve output `ApproxPartialFractions` with `try_reserve` using the same requirements; `polynomial()` returns polynomial part, `residues()` coefficients packed in Plan term order, and `report()` the report (`None` until success).
`decompose_into(numerator, &mut output, &mut workspace, context)` allocates nothing and returns `DimensionMismatch` for insufficient workspace or output capacity.
Empty numerators give `InvalidInput` (`Empty`), non-finite coefficients `NonFinite`, and cancellation or deadline `ExecutionStopped`.
Failure preserves output; workspace remains reusable after success or failure.
After preparation, computation costs $O(n^2 + N(n+1))$ ($N$ is numerator coefficient count).

`decompose` is a convenience form using storage allocated to requirements and a `ExecutionContext::sequential_native()` context.
That context can select processor-specific arithmetic kernels, so final digits may differ from `decompose_into` given `ExecutionContext::sequential()`.
Check reused-storage results against analytical values or reconstruction errors with tolerances.

Partial-fraction Plans retain no execution policy and compute sequentially on the supplied context.
`PolynomialExecutionOptions`, expressing parallel-execution intent and worker limit (default sequential, limit 64), is retained by operation Plan `PolynomialOperationPlan`, orthogonal-basis Plans, tensor Bernstein, and sparse multivariate evaluation Plans, not partial fractions.
Handbook cells run sequentially in wasm.

## Decomposition and reconstruction of repeated poles

The cells below use denominator $D = (x - 1)^2 F_1$ with known factors $(x-1)^2$ and $F_1 = (x-1)^2 + s^2$ (poles $1 \pm is$).
Numerator $N$ combines partial fractions $a/(x-1) + 2/(x-1)^2 + (3 + 4x)/F_1$ over a common denominator as $(a(x - 1) + 2)F_1 + (3 + 4x)(x - 1)^2$, constructed with SciOxide polynomial products.
True decomposition coefficients are thus $a, 2, 3, 4$, making comparison with construction values an independent check.
Definition `PartialFractionModel::new(a, s)` forms factors, denominator, and numerator; `plan()` prepares a Plan with multiplicity 2 for $x - 1$ and 1 for $F_1$.

```rust
//| id: poly-partial-fraction-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Rational function with repeated pole x = 1 and conjugate pair 1 ± is"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPartialFractionPlan, ApproxPolynomial, MonomialBasis};

pub(crate) type Monomial = ApproxPolynomial<f64, MonomialBasis<f64>>;

/// Factors of denominator D = (x − 1)² F₁, with F₁ = (x − 1)² + s², and
/// numerator N combining a/(x − 1) + 2/(x − 1)² + (3 + 4x)/F₁ over a common denominator
pub(crate) struct PartialFractionModel {
    pub(crate) linear: Monomial,
    pub(crate) pair: Monomial,
    pub(crate) denominator: Monomial,
    pub(crate) numerator: Monomial,
}

impl PartialFractionModel {
    pub(crate) fn new(residue: f64, s: f64) -> Result<Self, SciOxideError> {
        let linear = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![-1.0_f64, 1.0])?;
        let square = linear.try_mul(&linear)?;
        // F₁ = x² − 2x + 1 + s²
        let pair = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![1.0 + s * s, -2.0, 1.0])?;
        let denominator = square.try_mul(&pair)?;
        // N = (a(x − 1) + 2)F₁ + (3 + 4x)(x − 1)²
        let head = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![2.0 - residue, residue])?;
        let tail = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![3.0, 4.0])?;
        let numerator = head.try_mul(&pair)?.try_add(&tail.try_mul(&square)?)?;
        Ok(Self {
            linear,
            pair,
            denominator,
            numerator,
        })
    }

    /// Plan with multiplicity 2 for factor (x − 1) and 1 for F₁
    pub(crate) fn plan(&self) -> Result<ApproxPartialFractionPlan<f64>, SciOxideError> {
        ApproxPartialFractionPlan::new(&self.denominator, &[(&self.linear, 2), (&self.pair, 1)])
    }
}
```

The input is simple-pole coefficient $a$.

### Coefficient errors and reconstruction at nearby poles

The next cell fixes $a = 1$, approaches repeated pole $x = 1$ with a conjugate pole pair at $s = 2^{-k}$ ($k = 0, 2, \dots, 24$), and decomposes using `decompose`.
For each $k$, the table shows the reciprocal condition number, maximum coefficient discrepancy, maximum relative discrepancy of the computed partial-fraction sum from $N/D$ at 4 points, and reported reconstruction error.
The 4-point discrepancy evaluates $\hat a/(x-1) + \hat A_2/(x-1)^2 + (\hat b_0 + \hat b_1 x)/F_1$ with computed coefficients $[\hat a, \hat A_2, \hat b_0, \hat b_1]$ in `f64` and compares against SciOxide-evaluated $N(x)/D(x)$ using $x = -2, 0, 2, 4$ (independent check).
For $k$ rejected during preparation, show only the error classification.
The plot shows the same quantities logarithmically against $s$, adding dashed coefficient-error indicator $u/\text{reciprocal condition number}$.

```rust
//| id: poly-partial-fraction-near-poles
//| ref: poly-partial-fraction-near-poles
//| title: "Coefficient errors and function reconstruction at nearby poles"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-partial-fraction-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"near-poles","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.020202020202020204,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-poles","path":"/rows/6/3","range":{"min":1e-10,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-poles","path":"/rows/6/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-poles","path":"/rows/10/3","range":{"min":1e-4,"max":1e-1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-poles","path":"/rows/10/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"near-poles","path":"/rows/12/6","equals":"NumericalInstability"}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_poly::PolynomialBasis;

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let unit_roundoff = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -53);
let (mut rows, mut conditions, mut errors) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut predicted, mut pointwise, mut exact_points) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), 0);
for k in (0..=24).step_by(2) {
    let s = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -k);
    let model = PartialFractionModel::new(1.0, s).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Preparation is rejected if factors cannot be separated numerically
    let plan = match model.plan() {
        Ok(plan) => plan,
        Err(error) => {
            let kind = format!("{:?}", error.kind());
            rows.push((k, s, None, None, None, None, kind));
            continue;
        }
    };
    let fractions = plan
        .decompose(model.numerator.basis().coefficients())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let report = fractions.report().ok_or("No decomposition report")?;
    // Coefficients in factor input order and ascending power: [a, 2, b₀, b₁] (the F₁ numerator is b₀ + b₁x)
    let c = fractions.residues();
    // Independent check 1: maximum discrepancy from construction values [1, 2, 3, 4]
    let coefficient_error = c
        .iter()
        .zip([1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0])
        .map(|(c, r)| (c - r).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max);
    // Independent check 2: evaluate the computed partial-fraction sum in f64 and compare with SciOxide-evaluated N/D
    let mut difference = 0.0_f64;
    for x in [-2.0_f64, 0.0, 2.0, 4.0] {
        let n = model.numerator.evaluate(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let d = model.denominator.evaluate(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let t = x - 1.0;
        let sum = c[0] / t + c[1] / (t * t) + (c[2] + c[3] * x) / (t * t + s * s);
        difference = difference.max(((sum - n / d) / (n / d)).abs());
    }
    let rcond = report.reciprocal_condition().ok_or("No reciprocal condition number")?;
    conditions.push([s, rcond]);
    errors.push([s, coefficient_error]);
    predicted.push([s, unit_roundoff / rcond]);
    // Omit and count zero-discrepancy points on logarithmic axes
    if difference > 0.0 {
        pointwise.push([s, difference]);
    } else {
        exact_points += 1;
    }
    rows.push((
        k,
        s,
        Some(rcond),
        Some(coefficient_error),
        Some(difference),
        Some(report.reconstruction_error()),
        "Returns a value".to_string(),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "k [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Pole-pair distance s [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Reciprocal condition number [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum coefficient discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative discrepancy at 4 points [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Reconstruction error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Result" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "near-poles" })
);
let pointwise_name = format!("Maximum relative discrepancy at 4 points (excluding {exact_points} zero points)");
let series = vec![
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Reciprocal condition number", "points": conditions }),
    serde_json::json!({ "name": "Maximum coefficient discrepancy", "points": errors }),
    serde_json::json!({ "name": pointwise_name, "points": pointwise }),

        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
```

The reciprocal condition number decreases proportionally to $s^2$, giving $3.3 \times 10^{-7}$, $1.3 \times 10^{-9}$, $5.0 \times 10^{-12}$, and $1.9 \times 10^{-14}$ at $k = 8, 12, 16, 20$.
Maximum coefficient discrepancies grow at roughly dashed $u/\text{reciprocal condition number}$ magnitude, giving $1.2 \times 10^{-10}$, $8.7 \times 10^{-8}$, $1.1 \times 10^{-5}$, and $6.4 \times 10^{-3}$ at the same $k$.
Yet 4-point relative discrepancies stay about $10^{-15}$ and reconstruction errors about $10^{-16}$.
Individual coefficient errors align so they cancel each other.
Writing coefficient errors as $\delta a, \delta A_2, \delta b_0, \delta b_1$, sum error is

$$
\frac{\delta a}{x - 1} + \frac{\delta A_2}{(x - 1)^2} + \frac{\delta b_0 + \delta b_1 x}{F_1}
= \frac{(x - 1)^2 \big((\delta a + \delta b_1) x + \delta A_2 + \delta b_0 - \delta a\big) + s^2 \big(\delta a (x - 1) + \delta A_2\big)}{D(x)}
$$


If $\delta b_1 = -\delta a$ and $\delta b_0 = \delta a - \delta A_2$, the first term vanishes and the second is scaled by $s^2$, making even coefficient errors around $u/s^2$ give sum errors around $u$.
Coefficient errors at $k = 12$ ($\delta a = -8.7 \times 10^{-8}$, $\delta A_2 = -5.9 \times 10^{-8}$, $\delta b_0 = -2.8 \times 10^{-8}$, $\delta b_1 = 8.7 \times 10^{-8}$) satisfy this relationship.
At $k = 24$, numerical rank drops and preparation is rejected with `NumericalInstability` (`IllConditioned`).
Coefficient magnitudes and errors are distinct from reconstruction-error diagnostics.
Also distinguish supplying known repeated factors from automatically inferring multiplicities from floating-point coefficients (unsupported).

## Summary

Callers supply denominator factors and multiplicities; decomposition follows Plan checks of factor-product agreement and coprimality.
Read approximate reports' reciprocal conditioning and reconstruction errors separately: even when close poles amplify coefficient errors to roughly unit roundoff divided by reciprocal conditioning, reconstruction error checks whether the term sum recovers the function.
For the same denominator with changing numerators, allocate workspace and output from requirements and repeat `decompose_into` without allocation.
Check reused results against analytical values or reconstruction errors, rather than bitwise equality with `decompose`.
Exactly representable coefficients such as rationals allow rounding-free [exact partial fractions](../exact-partial-fractions/).
Automatic multiplicity detection from floating-point coefficients is not provided.
