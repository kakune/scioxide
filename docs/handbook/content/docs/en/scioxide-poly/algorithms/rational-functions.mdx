Rational functions $r(x) = p(x)/q(x)$ represent functions with poles and asymptotes that are difficult to express as polynomials, and arise in Padé approximations, transfer functions, and saturation models.
With floating-point coefficients, results depend on how far denominator values near poles can be trusted and whether nearby numerator and denominator factors should be cancelled.
This page covers SciOxide normal forms and arithmetic, near-pole evaluation criteria, derivatives, approximate cancellation, and exact-coefficient rational functions.
Approximate GCDs for cancellation are covered in [Polynomial GCDs and approximate GCDs](../approximate-gcd/); decomposition from denominator factors in [Partial-fraction decomposition](../partial-fractions/).
The symbol $\varepsilon$ is machine epsilon ($2^{-52}$ for `f64`); polynomial coefficients use ascending powers.

## Rational normal forms and poles

Rational function $r(x) = p(x)/q(x)$ is the ratio of numerator $p$ (degree $m$) to nonzero denominator $q$ (degree $n$).
Roots of $p$ are zeros; roots of $q$ not shared with $p$ are poles.
An improper fraction with $m \ge n$ uniquely separates by division $p = sq + r_{\text{rem}}$ into polynomial part $s$ and proper fraction $r_{\text{rem}}/q$ ($\deg r_{\text{rem}} < n$).

### Normal form and operations

`ApproxRationalFunction::new(numerator, denominator)` makes the denominator monic (leading coefficient 1) and divides the numerator by the same leading coefficient (division uses extended-exponent arithmetic and rounds each coefficient once).
A zero numerator is represented as $0/1$.
Common factors remain uncancelled; perform explicit cancellation through `simplify` below.
A zero denominator gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`); different numerator/denominator policies give `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).

`try_add`, `try_sub`, `try_mul`, `try_div`, and `reciprocal` form new numerators and denominators through products, as in $a/b + c/d = (ad + cb)/(bd)$, without cancelling common factors.
`try_div` by the zero function and `reciprocal` of the zero function give a zero denominator and return `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Repeated arithmetic raises degrees and retains factors that cancel only approximately; when needed, inspect `simplify` diagnostics and cancel at the end.
`numerator()` and `denominator()` borrow normalized numerator and denominator.

### Evaluation near poles

`evaluate(x)` evaluates numerator and denominator separately by Horner and returns the rational value.
`evaluate_parts(x)` returns `ApproximateRationalEvaluation` containing value plus numerator and denominator evaluations, using the same pole criterion as `evaluate`.
`value()` is the rational value; `numerator()` and `denominator()` are separate `PolynomialEvaluation` containing values, derivatives, and conditioning reports.
If the computed denominator is 0 or its estimated relative forward error $\gamma_N \max(1, \kappa_q(x))$ is at least 1, evaluation treats $x$ as a pole and returns `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
Here $\gamma_N = N\varepsilon/(1 - N\varepsilon)$, $N$ is denominator coefficient count, and $\kappa_q(x) = \sum_k |q_k||x|^k / |q(x)|$ is the condition number of denominator evaluation ([Horner rounding error](../horner-rounding-error/)).
An estimate at least 1 means the denominator is smaller than its estimated error, leaving sign and magnitude undetermined.
Near simple pole $a$, $|q(x)| \approx |q'(a)||x - a|$, so the interval triggering this criterion has width about $\gamma_N \sum_k |q_k||a|^k / |q'(a)|$.
A small but accurately computed denominator returns a value independently of coefficient tolerances.
Division uses extended-exponent arithmetic followed by one rounding, avoiding intermediate overflow or underflow; only a rounded quotient unrepresentable in `f64` gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
Non-finite points are rejected with `NonFinite`.

The cells below use saturation model $v(c)/V_{\max} = c/(K + c)$ ($c$ is substrate concentration and $K$ the half-saturation constant, both mmol/L) and its reciprocal $(K + c)/c$.
At the first function's pole $c = -K$, denominator $K + c$ becomes small through cancellation; at the reciprocal's pole $c = 0$, denominator $c$ becomes small without cancellation.
In the definitions block, `saturation(k)` constructs the rational model; `denominator_estimate(f, c)` returns the denominator relative forward-error estimate at point $c$.
Denominator evaluation exposes the estimate even where rational evaluation rejects the point; at an exactly zero denominator, infinity is returned as `None`.

```rust
//| id: poly-rational-pole-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Saturation model c/(K + c) and denominator error estimates"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, ApproxRationalFunction};

/// Saturation model v/Vmax = c/(K + c). Numerator [0, 1], denominator [K, 1] (ascending powers). c and K have units mmol/L
pub(crate) fn saturation(k: f64) -> Result<ApproxRationalFunction<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let numerator = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![0.0_f64, 1.0])?;
    let denominator = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![k, 1.0])?;
    ApproxRationalFunction::new(numerator, denominator)
}

/// Estimated denominator relative forward error γ_N max(1, κ_q(c)) at c.
/// Readable from denominator evaluation even where rational evaluation rejects a point. Exactly zero denominators have infinite estimates, returned as None
pub(crate) fn denominator_estimate(
    f: &ApproxRationalFunction<f64>,
    c: f64,
) -> Result<Option<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let estimate = f
        .denominator()
        .evaluate_with_derivative(c)?
        .condition()
        .forward_error_estimate();
    Ok(Some(estimate).filter(|x| x.is_finite()))
}
```

The next cell selects relative pole distance $h = 2^{-j}$, evaluating $v$ at $c = -K(1 - h)$ and $1/v$ at $c = K h$ through `evaluate_parts`.
Inputs are half-saturation constant $K$ and $j$; the table shows points, estimated denominator relative forward error, values, relative closed-form discrepancies, and evaluation results.
Independent checks use closed forms $v = 1 - 1/h$ and $1/v = 1 + 1/h$.
For integer $K$, $c$ is exact; otherwise rounding of $c$ enters the denominator.

```rust
//| id: poly-rational-saturation-scale
//| ref: poly-rational-saturation-scale
//| title: "Evaluating a saturation model near a pole"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-rational-pole-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   half_saturation: {"type":"range","label":"Half-saturation constant K [mmol/L]","description":"Integer values make near-pole points exactly binary-representable.","min":0.2,"max":5,"step":0.2,"value":1}
//|   distance_power: {"type":"range","label":"j in relative pole distance h=2⁻ʲ","description":"Choose evaluation points c = −K(1 − h) and c = Kh.","min":2,"max":60,"step":2,"value":40}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"inexact-constant","label":"K = 0.2 mmol/L","description":"Rounding c affects K + c, producing closed-form discrepancies.","inputs":{"half_saturation":0.2}}
//|   - {"id":"at-pole","label":"j = 52","description":"The estimate exceeds 1, treating the point as a pole and giving DomainError.","inputs":{"distance_power":52}}

//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"evaluations","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":"Returns a value"}
//|       - {"artifact":"evaluations","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"evaluations","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.0e-3,"rtol":0.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"evaluations","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":4.440892098500626e-16,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: inexact-constant
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"evaluations","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":1e-6,"max":1e-3}}
//|   - case: at-pole
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"evaluations","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":"DomainError（OutsideDomain）"}
//|       - {"artifact":"evaluations","path":"/rows/1/6","equals":"Returns a value"}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideErrorKind, RealScalar};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let (sig12, sig17) = (
    serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 12 }),
    serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 17 }),
);
let k = half_saturation;
let model = saturation(k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Reciprocal (K + c)/c has pole c = 0, without cancellation in denominator evaluation
let inverse = model.reciprocal().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let h = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -(distance_power as i32));
let mut rows = Vec::new();
// Independent check: closed forms v = 1 − 1/h and 1/v = 1 + 1/h (f64 arithmetic)
for (name, f, c, exact) in [
    ("v(c), c = −K(1 − h)", &model, -k * (1.0 - h), 1.0 - 1.0 / h),
    ("1/v(c), c = Kh", &inverse, k * h, 1.0 + 1.0 / h),
] {
    let (value, outcome) = match f.evaluate_parts(c) {
        Ok(e) => (Some(e.value()), "Returns a value".to_string()),
        Err(error) if error.kind() == SciOxideErrorKind::DomainError => {
            (None, "DomainError（OutsideDomain）".to_string())
        }
        Err(error) => return Err(error.to_string()),
    };
    let difference = value.map(|v| ((v - exact) / exact).abs());
    let estimate = denominator_estimate(f, c).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((name, c, estimate, value, exact, difference, outcome));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Function and point" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Point c [mmol/L]", "type": "number", "format": sig17 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Estimated denominator relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "∞" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig12, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig12 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative closed-form discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Evaluation result" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "evaluations" })
);
```

At $c = -K(1 - h)$, the denominator condition number is about $2/h$ and estimated relative forward error is $\gamma_2 \cdot 2/h \approx 8.9 \times 10^{-16}/h$.
Defaults ($K = 1$, $h = 2^{-40}$) give estimate $9.8 \times 10^{-4}$ and return a value with zero relative closed-form discrepancy.
At $j = 52$, the estimate is 4.0 and `evaluate_parts` returns `DomainError`.
For $1/v(Kh)$ approaching reciprocal pole $c = 0$, denominator $c$ has no cancellation, so the estimate stays $\gamma_2 = 4.4 \times 10^{-16}$ and evaluation returns value $1 + 1/h$.
For values not exactly representable in binary, such as $K = 0.2$, rounding of $c$ enters $K + c$, giving relative closed-form discrepancy $6.1 \times 10^{-5}$ at $h = 2^{-40}$.

The next cell computes the same estimates at 30 points $j = 2, 4, \dots, 60$, plotting logarithmically against $h$.
For $c = -K(1 - h)$, it also compares values of `evaluate` with the closed form.
The table shows the first $j$ where `evaluate` returns `DomainError`, counts of exactly zero denominators and zero closed-form discrepancies (both omitted from plots), and the maximum relative discrepancy.
The only input is half-saturation constant $K$.

```rust

//| inputs:

//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"inexact-constant","label":"K = 0.2 mmol/L","description":"Rounding c makes relative closed-form discrepancy grow proportionally to 1/h.","inputs":{"half_saturation":0.2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":50}

//|   - case: inexact-constant
//|     expect:

//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":1e-3,"max":0.1}}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideErrorKind, RealScalar};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });

let inverse = model.reciprocal().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Omit and count infinite estimates (zero denominators) and zero relative discrepancies on logarithmic axes
let (mut cancelling, mut plain, mut observed) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut zero_denominator, mut exact_values, mut first_pole) = (0, 0, None);
for j in (2..=60).step_by(2) {
    let h = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -j);
    let c = -half_saturation * (1.0 - h);
    match denominator_estimate(&model, c).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? {
        Some(value) => cancelling.push([h, value]),
        None => zero_denominator += 1,
    }
    let plain_estimate = denominator_estimate(&inverse, half_saturation * h);
    if let Some(value) = plain_estimate.map_err(|e| e.to_string())? {
        plain.push([h, value]);
    }
    // Independent check: compare evaluable points with 1 − 1/h. Record the first rejected j

        Ok(value) => {
            let difference = ((value - (1.0 - 1.0 / h)) / (1.0 - 1.0 / h)).abs();

                observed.push([h, difference]);
            } else {
                exact_values += 1;
            }
        }
        Err(error) if error.kind() == SciOxideErrorKind::DomainError => {
            first_pole = first_pole.or(Some(j));
        }
        Err(error) => return Err(error.to_string()),
    }

let largest = observed.iter().map(|point| point[1]).reduce(f64::max);
let columns = serde_json::json!([

    { "key": "1", "label": "Points with denominator 0 [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Points with zero closed-form discrepancy [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum relative discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "0" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(first_pole, zero_denominator, exact_values, largest)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sweep" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Estimate: K + c (pole c = −K)",
            "points": cancelling,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Estimate: c (pole c = 0)",
            "points": plain,
            "style": { "lineType": "dotted" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Relative discrepancy from 1 − 1/h",
            "points": observed,
            "style": { "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true }

        "yLabel": "Relative error [-]",
```

The dashed estimate grows inversely with $h$ and reaches 1 at $h = 2^{-50}$, so at $j \ge 50$, `evaluate` returns `DomainError`.
For $h \le 2^{-54}$, $c$ rounds to $-K$, making the denominator exactly 0 and estimate infinite (4 points $j = 54, \dots, 60$ for every $K$ in the input range, omitted from the plot).
The dotted reciprocal-denominator estimate stays $\gamma_2$ independently of $h$ because $c$ has no cancellation; evaluation returns values even at $h = 2^{-60}$.
Pole detection depends on denominator-to-estimated-error ratio, not denominator magnitude alone.

For integers such as $K = 1$, both $c$ and $K + c$ are exact; all 24 evaluable points have zero closed-form discrepancy (omitted from plots).
At $K = 0.2$, relative closed-form discrepancy grows proportionally to $1/h$ at about one order of magnitude below the estimate, reaching maximum $1.5 \times 10^{-2}$ at $j = 48$.
The estimate measures denominator changes under coefficient and point rounding of this size; detection uses the estimate even if actual error is smaller.

### Numerator and denominator derivatives

No function directly returns a rational derivative.
The numerator and denominator evaluations in `evaluate_parts` contain derivatives, allowing the quotient rule

$$
r'(x) = \frac{p'(x)\, q(x) - p(x)\, q'(x)}{q(x)^2}
$$


Away from poles, numerator and denominator values and derivatives have relative error $O(\varepsilon)$, and this formula adds only a few arithmetic roundings, so relative error in $r'$ remains $O(\varepsilon)$.
Near poles, $q^2$ decreases and numerator cancellation can also occur; check $r'$ accuracy separately even at points passing pole detection.

The next cell computes saturation-model derivatives at 4 points $c/K = 0, 1/2, 1, 4$, checking against closed form $K/(K + c)^2$ independently.
At $c = 0$, the value is origin slope $1/K$.

```rust
//| id: poly-rational-quotient-derivative
//| ref: poly-rational-quotient-derivative
//| title: "Rational derivatives from numerator and denominator derivatives"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-rational-pole-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   half_saturation: {"type":"range","label":"Half-saturation constant K [mmol/L]","description":"The derivative's closed form is K/(K + c)².","min":0.2,"max":5,"step":0.2,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"inexact-constant","label":"K = 0.2 mmol/L","description":"Even non-binary-representable K gives derivative agreement with the closed form to rounding accuracy.","inputs":{"half_saturation":0.2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"derivative","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1,"rtol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"derivative","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: inexact-constant
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"derivative","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":5,"rtol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"derivative","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig15 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let k = half_saturation;
let model = saturation(k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
// Points c/K = 0, 1/2, 1, 4. At c = 0, the value is origin slope 1/K
for ratio in [0.0_f64, 0.5, 1.0, 4.0] {
    let c = ratio * k;
    let parts = model.evaluate_parts(c).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (p, q) = (parts.numerator(), parts.denominator());
    // Quotient rule r′ = (p′q − pq′)/q²
    let derivative =
        (p.derivative() * q.value() - p.value() * q.derivative()) / (q.value() * q.value());
    // Independent check: d/dc [c/(K + c)] = K/(K + c)² (f64 arithmetic)
    let exact = k / ((k + c) * (k + c));
    rows.push((c, derivative, exact, ((derivative - exact) / exact).abs()));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Point c [mmol/L]", "type": "number", "format": sig15 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Quotient-rule r′(c) [L/mmol]", "type": "number", "format": sig15 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form K/(K + c)² [L/mmol]", "type": "number", "format": sig15 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "derivative" })
);
```

At default $K = 1$, all 4 relative discrepancies are 0; the value at $c = 0$ matches origin slope $1/K = 1$.
Even for $K = 0.2$, relative discrepancies stay at most $2.8 \times 10^{-16}$, on the order of $\varepsilon$ including rounding in $K$ and $c$.

### Approximate cancellation

`ApproxRationalFunction::simplify()` applies `approximate_gcd` to numerator and denominator, constructing a new rational function from retained cofactors only when both remainders meet policy tolerances.
It reuses cofactors from GCD division without repeating division.
Each remainder's maximum absolute coefficient must be at most $\tau_{\text{abs}} + \tau_{\text{rel}} \max_i |\text{input}_i|$ ([GCD and simplification reports](oxref:poly-gcd-simplification-report)).
If either remainder exceeds tolerance, return `NumericalInstability` (`IllConditioned`) without partial results.
On success, `ApproximateRationalSimplification` returns cancelled rational function `rational()`, supporting `gcd_report()`, and normalized remainders `numerator_error()` and `denominator_error()` at original numerator and denominator scales.
Degree-0 GCDs leave the normal form unchanged.

Factors shared only approximately make cancellation change the original function.
Rank thresholds determine common-factor degree; tolerances determine acceptance of cancellation.
Select them as separate policy components.
The [rational saturation example](../../examples/rational-saturation/) checks changes in values and preservation of model properties such as zeros after cancellation.

## Exact-coefficient rational functions

For exact coefficients (`ExactField`), `ExactRationalFunction::new` forms an exact-GCD-reduced expression with monic denominator and reduces after every arithmetic operation.
Uniqueness of reduced form makes equal rational functions compare equal under `==`.
`reciprocal` merely swaps reduced numerator and denominator without GCD computation; the zero function gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
`ExactRationalFunction::evaluate` rejects exactly zero-denominator points with `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Coefficient-type division violating $(a/b) \cdot b = a$ gives `NumericalInstability` (`Breakdown`).

## Summary

`ApproxRationalFunction` retains monic-denominator normal form without cancelling factors in arithmetic; cancel explicitly with `simplify`.
Poles are detected when estimated relative forward error $\gamma_N \max(1, \kappa_q)$ reaches 1, rather than from denominator magnitude; cancellation-free denominators allow evaluation very near 0.
Treat points returning `DomainError` as poles; read estimates from denominator evaluation's `condition()`.
Construct derivatives from numerator and denominator derivatives in `evaluate_parts` using the quotient rule, checking near-pole accuracy separately.
With exact coefficients, `ExactRationalFunction` reduces after every operation and rejects only exactly zero-denominator points.
