Even for the same polynomial, rounding propagates differently depending on the stored input data.
Monomials suit coefficient operations, barycentric form preserves samples in interpolation, and orthogonal expansions suit interval approximation.
This page defines stored-coordinate conventions, derives monomial Horner evaluation, and explains basis-conversion and evaluation scratch contracts.
Evaluation rounding bounds and meanings of reported estimates appear in [Rounding error and diagnostics for Horner evaluation](../horner-rounding-error/).

## Stored coordinates and degree

This chapter calls the numerical sequence a type retains to represent a polynomial its stored coordinates.
These are coefficients, node values, or control points depending on the basis.
Monomial stored coordinates are ascending-power coefficients.

$$
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_kx^k,\qquad [a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_n].
$$

`ApproxPolynomial::monomial` owns coefficients and removes only exact trailing zeros.
The zero polynomial is one coefficient $[0]$.
Empty coefficient sequences and non-finite coefficients give errors.
Small finite coefficients are not discarded by tolerance.
For example, the leading term of $10^{-20}x^{10}+1$ is as large as the constant at $x=100$.
Stored degree must be distinguished from judgments of smallness at a particular scale.

`PolynomialApproximationPolicy` holds the decision rules.
With default policy and maximum absolute stored coordinate $s$, coefficients of magnitude at most $\tau=\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\cdot s$ (default $\mathrm{atol}=\mathrm{rtol}=\sqrt\varepsilon$) are treated as zero.
`effective_degree()` of `PolynomialCoefficientReport` returned by `classify_coefficients` is the index of the last coefficient exceeding $\tau$.
`effective_rank()` counts stored coordinates exceeding the numerical-rank threshold (default $s\,N\varepsilon$, where $N$ is coordinate count); it is neither degree nor matrix rank.
Neither classification changes stored coordinates.
Construction and evaluation do not perform this classification.

Newton form retains trailing zero coefficients and their nodes.
Barycentric coordinates are node values and Bernstein coordinates are control points, rather than monomial coefficients.
Effective-degree classification thus forms Newton divided differences of node values for barycentric form and power coefficients of $t=(x-a)/(b-a)$ for Bernstein, also setting $s$ to their maximum absolute coefficient.
Both use extended-exponent arithmetic with $O(n^2)$ operations and $O(n)$ temporary allocation; other bases inspect stored coordinates directly in $O(n)$.

`PolynomialBasis` is their shared sealed trait; external types cannot implement it.
The basis of `ApproxPolynomial` is `MonomialBasis`, `NewtonBasis`, `BarycentricBasis`, `BernsteinBasis`, or `OrthogonalBasis`.
Importing the trait through `use` permits borrowing stored coordinates with `basis().coefficients()` in any basis.
This operation alone does not convert the basis.

## Horner and differentiation

Evaluating nested parentheses obtains values without forming individual powers.

$$
p(x)=a_0+x\bigl(a_1+x(\cdots+x\,a_n)\bigr).
$$

Set $b_n=a_n$ and $c_n=0$; for $k=n-1,\ldots,0$ update

$$
c_k=b_{k+1}+x\,c_{k+1},\qquad b_k=a_k+x\,b_{k+1}
$$

to obtain $b_0=p(x)$ and $c_0=p'(x)$.
$b_k$ are also coefficients of quotient $q(t)=\sum_{k\ge1}b_kt^{k-1}$ from dividing $p$ by $t-x$ (synthetic division), giving $p(t)=b_0+(t-x)q(t)$.
Differentiating with respect to $t$ and setting $t=x$ gives $p'(x)=q(x)$.
The second recurrence evaluates this $q(x)$ with the same Horner method.
The update of $c_k$ uses $b_{k+1}$ before its update in the same stage.

Value-only evaluation performs $n$ multiplications and additions each, costing $O(n)$ time and $O(1)$ extra memory.
Adding derivatives roughly doubles operations.
Rebuilding every power $x^k$ requires $n(n+1)/2$ multiplications; direct summation updating powers successively as $x^k=x\cdot x^{k-1}$ needs only $O(n)$.
Horner offers more than fewer operations: small backward error is proved (Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, Chapter 5).

`evaluate` returns only the value; `evaluate_with_derivative` returns `PolynomialEvaluation` combining value, derivative, and `PolynomialConditionReport`.
Both share computation, giving bit-identical values.
Non-finite points or unrepresentable values, derivatives, or envelopes give errors (`NonFinite`, `NumericalInstability`).
Unbounded relative diagnostics return positive infinity rather than errors.
`derivative` returns a newly owned derivative polynomial, unlike reading a derivative at one point.
In monomial form, replace $a_k$ with $ka_k$ ($k\ge1$); orthogonal calculus follows [Differentiation and integration of orthogonal expansions](../orthogonal-calculus/).

## Basis conversion and scratch space

`PolynomialConversion` owns the converted polynomial and conditioning report `PolynomialConditionReport`.
`to_monomial` accepts every basis; `to_newton`, `to_barycentric`, and `to_bernstein` convert from monomials, while `to_orthogonal` converts monomial, Bernstein, or orthogonal expansions.
Bernstein `to_orthogonal` passes through monomials and reports the monomial-to-orthogonal step.
Conversion conditioning is estimated by the target-to-source maximum absolute stored-coordinate ratio (at least 1).
The backward-error model is $\gamma_N(\varepsilon)$ using the larger coordinate count $N$ (see [Reported estimates](oxref:poly-condition-report-estimates) for notation).
Intermediate cancellation can lose more digits than this ratio indicates.
`BasisNormalization` specifies orthogonal normalization (`Classical`, `Monic`, `Orthonormal`).
Conversion cannot recover information already rounded in the input.

Single-point `evaluate` and `evaluate_with_derivative` take no scratch argument.
Bernstein uses de Casteljau with $O(n^2)$ operations, allocating a temporary vector of control-point count per call (allocation failure gives `AllocationFailure`).
Other bases cost $O(n)$ without allocation.
`evaluate_batch(&points)` returns multipoint values in a new vector.
Avoid allocation by passing scratch to value-writing `evaluate_batch_into` or derivative-writing `evaluate_batch_with_derivative_into`.
Query length from `PolynomialScratchRequirements` returned by `workspace_requirements(m)` ($m$ is point count).
Values alone need $m+s$ entries, values and derivatives $2m+s$ ($s$ is Bernstein control-point count, 0 for other bases); returned length $2m+s$ suffices for both.
Short scratch or output length differing from point count gives `DimensionMismatch` before numerical computation.
`_into` forms allocate nothing, storing values in scratch before a final output copy and preserving output on failure.
They also take an execution context (`ExecutionContext`), checking cancellation and deadlines before each point and before output copying; stopping gives `ExecutionStopped`.
Each value agrees bit for bit with single-point `evaluate`.
`PolynomialWorkspace` for repeated coefficient operations and its requirements are covered in [Fast products, composition, and Taylor shifts](../fast-operations-and-product-trees/).

## Comparing evaluation and operation counts

The next cell constructs degree-$n$ Taylor polynomial $p_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n x^k/k!$ of the exponential, obtaining values, derivatives, and diagnostics through SciOxide evaluation.
Inputs are degree $n$ and dimensionless point $x$.
There are three independent checks.
First, a naive direct sum rebuilding each power compares values and multiplication counts.
Second, Taylor identity $p_n'(x)=p_{n-1}(x)=p_n(x)-x^n/n!$ checks derivatives, using f64 `powi` for $x^n$ (a reference, not a SciOxide function).
Third, use the ratio of the envelope $\sum_k|a_kx^k|$ to $|p_n(x)|$ and the formula for $\gamma_N(\varepsilon)$.
Conditioning and backward-error models are derived in [Rounding error and diagnostics for Horner evaluation](../horner-rounding-error/).

```rust
//| id: poly-horner-operation-growth
//| ref: poly-horner-operation-growth
//| title: "Taylor polynomial Horner evaluation versus a naive direct sum"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Polynomial degree n","description":"Change the Taylor polynomial degree.","min":2,"max":80,"step":1,"value":20}
//|   point: {"type":"range","label":"Evaluation point x [-]","description":"Negative values alternate term signs, causing cancellation.","min":-8,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":-4}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"strong-cancellation","label":"n = 80, x = −8","description":"Strong cancellation raises condition estimates to e^16 ≈ 8.9 × 10^6.","inputs":{"degree":80,"point":-8}}
//|   - {"id":"positive-point","label":"n = 20, x = 4","description":"All terms are positive, without cancellation, giving condition estimate 1.","inputs":{"degree":20,"point":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":2981.0,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":2981.0,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":4.6629367034256575e-15,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"operations","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":210}
//|       - {"artifact":"operations","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":20}
//|   - case: strong-cancellation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":8886110.52,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"operations","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":3240}
//|   - case: positive-point
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"comparison","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":54.5981499281488,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_poly::ApproxPolynomial;

let n = degree.round() as usize;
// Ascending coefficients a_k = 1/k! of the degree-n Taylor polynomial for e^x.
let mut coefficients = vec![1.0_f64];
for k in 1..=n {
    coefficients.push(coefficients[k - 1] / k as f64);
}
let polynomial = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(coefficients.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Main computation: obtain value, derivative, and diagnostics together through SciOxide Horner.
let evaluation = polynomial
    .evaluate_with_derivative(point)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let condition = evaluation.condition();

// Independent check: naive direct sum rebuilding each x^k (k − 1 multiplications for x^k, 1 for its coefficient).
let mut naive = coefficients[0];
let mut envelope = coefficients[0].abs();
let mut naive_multiplications = 0_u64;
for (k, &a) in coefficients.iter().enumerate().skip(1) {
    let mut power = point;
    for _ in 1..k {
        power *= point;
        naive_multiplications += 1;
    }
    naive += a * power;
    envelope += (a * power).abs();
    naive_multiplications += 1;
}
// Independent check: Taylor identity p′(x) = p(x) − x^n/n! (reference x^n uses f64 powi).
let slope = evaluation.value() - coefficients[n] * point.powi(n as i32);
// Independent check: γ_N(ε) from coordinate count N = n + 1 and EPSILON.
let count = (n + 1) as f64;
let gamma = count * f64::EPSILON / (1.0 - count * f64::EPSILON);
let ratio = (naive != 0.0).then(|| envelope / naive.abs());
let digits = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "SciOxide [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Independent check [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits, "nullText": "+∞" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" }
]);
let row = |name: &'static str, scioxide: f64, check: Option<f64>| {
    (name, scioxide, check, check.map(|c| scioxide - c))
};
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![
        row("Value p(x)", evaluation.value(), Some(naive)),
        row("Derivative p′(x)", evaluation.derivative(), Some(slope)),
        row("Condition estimate", condition.condition_estimate(), ratio),
        row(
            "Backward-error model γ_N(ε)",
            condition.backward_error(),
            Some(gamma)
        ),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "comparison" })
);
let operation_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Degree n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Naive direct-sum multiplications [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Horner multiplications [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    operation_columns,
    vec![(n as u64, naive_multiplications, n as u64)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "operations" })
);
// Theoretical multiplication counts: naive direct sum n(n + 1)/2, Horner n.
let naive_curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (1..=80)
    .map(|d| [d as f64, (d * (d + 1) / 2) as f64])
    .collect();
let horner_curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (1..=80).map(|d| [d as f64, d as f64]).collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Direct sum rebuilding powers n(n + 1)/2",
            "points": naive_curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Horner n",
            "points": horner_curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Degree n [-]",
        "yLabel": "Multiplication count [count]",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": n as f64, "label": "Selected n" }
        ]
    })
);
```

The first table subtracts independent checks from SciOxide values; condition estimates are blank only when the naive sum is exactly 0.
The second table and plot show theoretical multiplication counts, not measured execution time.
SciOxide also uses recurrences grouping coefficient pairs, but value multiplications remain about $n$, comparable to Horner (see [SciOxide execution paths and theorem assumptions](oxref:poly-horner-arithmetic-paths)).
SciOxide simultaneously computes derivatives and envelopes, so total operations exceed the $n$ value-only multiplications.

With default $n=20$ and $x=-4$, condition estimate $2.98\times10^3$ matches independent check $\sum_k|a_kx^k|/|p_n(x)|$.
This is close to $e^{|x|}/e^{x}=e^{8}\approx2981$.
The envelope is near $e^{|x|}$ and value near $e^{x}$, so negative $x$ can amplify relative error by about $e^{2|x|}$ through cancellation.
At $n=80$ and $x=-8$, the condition estimate grows to $8.9\times10^6\approx e^{16}$ and the naive-sum discrepancy to $1.6\times10^{-14}$ (about $5\times10^{-11}$ relative to the value).
At $x=4$, all terms are positive and condition estimate is 1; the two sums differ by only a few rounding units.
The difference between a finite Taylor polynomial and the exponential is truncation error, separate from differences between evaluations of the same polynomial.

## Summary

Stored coordinates are basis-specific coefficients, node values, or control points; effective-degree classification and basis conversion are explicit operations that do not modify the original stored coordinates.
Horner uses synthetic division for values and derivatives in $O(n)$ operations and $O(1)$ extra memory, avoiding the $n(n+1)/2$ multiplications of rebuilding powers.
Conversion reports estimate conditioning from growth of maximum absolute stored coordinates, without recovering rounded input information.
Single-point evaluation takes no scratch; query scratch length for allocation-free multipoint `evaluate_batch_into` with `workspace_requirements(m)`.
Cancellation points, as in alternating series, give large condition estimates; read `condition()` alongside values.
