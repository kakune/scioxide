Whether two polynomials share roots can be determined from coefficients without computing and matching every root.
The tool is the resultant, the determinant of a matrix built from coefficients.
Resultants also eliminate variables in algebraic systems; discriminants indicate multiple roots, and subresultants indicate common-factor degree.
This page derives definitions and identities, checks conventions numerically, and explains exact-field remainder-sequence computation.
Diagnostics for small floating-point values and reuse of fixed-degree elimination are covered in [Floating-point resultants and numerical rank](../approximate-elimination/).

## Sylvester matrices and resultants

Over field $F$, consider degree-$m$ polynomial $f(x) = \sum_{i=0}^m a_i x^i$ ($a_m \ne 0$) and degree-$n$ polynomial $g(x) = \sum_{j=0}^n b_j x^j$ ($b_n \ne 0$).

### Definitions of Sylvester matrices and resultants

Sylvester matrix $\mathbf{S}(f, g)$ has size $(m+n) \times (m+n)$; its first $n$ rows contain descending-power coefficients of $f$ shifted one place per row, and its last $m$ rows similarly contain the coefficients of $g$.

$$
\mathbf{S}(f, g) = \begin{bmatrix}
a_m & a_{m-1} & \cdots & a_0 & & \\
 & \ddots & \ddots & & \ddots & \\
 & & a_m & a_{m-1} & \cdots & a_0 \\
b_n & b_{n-1} & \cdots & b_0 & & \\
 & \ddots & \ddots & & \ddots & \\
 & & b_n & b_{n-1} & \cdots & b_0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Blank entries are 0.
Define the resultant as $\operatorname{Res}(f, g) = \det \mathbf{S}(f, g)$.

Each row gives coefficients of polynomials $x^{n-1} f, \dots, x f, f, x^{m-1} g, \dots, g$ in basis $x^{m+n-1}, \dots, 1$.
Thus $\det \mathbf{S}(f,g) = 0$ is equivalent to a pair $u, v$, not both zero, with $\deg u < n$ and $\deg v < m$ satisfying $uf + vg = 0$.
Then $f$ divides $vg$, but $\deg v < m$, so $f$ and $g$ have a nonconstant common factor.
Conversely, common factor $h$ gives $u = g/h$ and $v = -f/h$ satisfying this relation.
Under $a_m b_n \ne 0$, therefore, $\operatorname{Res}(f, g) = 0$ is equivalent to $f$ and $g$ sharing a root in the algebraic closure.

### Root-product representation and basic properties

If $f$ has roots $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_m$ and $g$ roots $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_n$, the resultant is a product over roots (Poisson's formula).

$$
\operatorname{Res}(f, g) = a_m^n b_n^m \prod_{i=1}^m \prod_{j=1}^n (\alpha_i - \beta_j) = a_m^n \prod_{i=1}^m g(\alpha_i) = (-1)^{mn} b_n^m \prod_{j=1}^n f(\beta_j)
$$

These properties follow immediately.

- Exchanging arguments gives $\operatorname{Res}(g, f) = (-1)^{mn} \operatorname{Res}(f, g)$.
- Scaling gives $\operatorname{Res}(c f, g) = c^n \operatorname{Res}(f, g)$ and $\operatorname{Res}(f, c g) = c^m \operatorname{Res}(f, g)$.
- For nonzero constant $c$ ($n = 0$), $\operatorname{Res}(f, c) = c^m$; two constants give 1 as the determinant of an $0 \times 0$ matrix.

Sylvester matrices use declared degrees, so a vanishing leading coefficient changes their shape.
SciOxide uses actual degrees after removing only exact trailing zeros (`EliminationReport::degrees`); small coefficients do not lower degree.
Parameterized elimination requires separate examination of special values where leading coefficients vanish.

## Discriminants and multiple roots

Let $p(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k x^k$ ($a_n \ne 0$, $n \ge 2$) have roots $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n$.
$p$ has a multiple root exactly when $p$ and $p'$ share a root.
Substituting $p'(\alpha_i) = a_n \prod_{j \ne i} (\alpha_i - \alpha_j)$ into root-product formula $\operatorname{Res}(p, p') = a_n^{n-1} \prod_i p'(\alpha_i)$ gives

$$
\operatorname{Res}(p, p') = a_n^{2n-1} \prod_{i \ne j} (\alpha_i - \alpha_j) = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2} a_n^{2n-1} \prod_{i<j} (\alpha_i - \alpha_j)^2
$$

The second equality uses $n(n-1)/2$ factors equal to $(\alpha_i - \alpha_j)(\alpha_j - \alpha_i) = -(\alpha_i - \alpha_j)^2$, one for each pair $i<j$.
Define the discriminant by removing this sign and leading coefficient.

$$
D(p) = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2} \frac{1}{a_n} \operatorname{Res}(p, p') = a_n^{2n-2} \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} (\alpha_i - \alpha_j)^2
$$

Quadratic $ax^2 + bx + c$ gives $D = b^2 - 4ac$; cubic $x^3 + px + q$ gives $D = -4p^3 - 27q^2$.
For real coefficients with distinct roots, $D$ has sign $(-1)^s$ ($s$ counts conjugate complex-root pairs).
Both exact and approximate SciOxide versions return $D = 1$ for nonzero polynomials below degree 2.
The zero polynomial's discriminant is undefined: exact versions reject it with `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), approximate versions with `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).

## Checking identities for nearby roots

The next cell uses polynomial $p$ with leading coefficient $a_n = 3$ and the first $n$ roots from $1, 1+\delta, -1, 2, -2, 3$, and linear $g = x - (1 + \delta/2)$ rooted at the midpoint of the nearby pair.
Inputs are degree $n$ and $k$ in nearby-root separation $\delta = 2^{-k}$.
Compute $\operatorname{Res}(p, g)$, $\operatorname{Res}(g, p)$, and $\operatorname{Res}(p, p')$ with `approximate_resultant`, and $D(p)$ with `SylvesterDiscriminant`.
Independent checks use root products without Sylvester matrices, checking exchange sign $(-1)^{n}$ and discriminant convention $(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}/a_n$.
Roots and $\delta$ are exactly binary-representable, so coefficients of $p$ and $g$ incur no rounding.
Root-product references contain only rounding of order $u$ from a few multiplications, so relative discrepancies mainly reflect SciOxide computation errors.
Return type `ApproximateElimination` and reciprocal matrix conditioning are explained in [Floating-point resultants and numerical rank](oxref:poly-subresultants-approximate-elimination).

```rust

//| ref: poly-resultant-discriminant-identities
//| title: "Comparing resultant and discriminant conventions with root products"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree n of p","description":"Use the first n roots from 1, 1+δ, −1, 2, −2, 3.","min":2,"max":6,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   separation_power: {"type":"range","label":"k in root separation δ=2⁻ᵏ","description":"Bring roots 1 and 1+δ closer.","min":2,"max":20,"step":1,"value":6}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"even-degree","label":"n = 4","description":"Even n gives Res(g, p) the same sign as Res(p, g); even n(n−1)/2 = 6 also gives Res(p, p′) and D(p) the same sign.","inputs":{"degree":4}}
//|   - {"id":"close-roots","label":"δ = 2⁻²⁰","description":"Nearby roots make the Sylvester matrix nearly singular, worsening reciprocal conditioning and relative discrepancies.","inputs":{"separation_power":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":3.676414489746094e-4,"rtol":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":-3.676414489746094e-4,"rtol":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: even-degree
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":3.647692501544952e-4,"rtol":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":25.223811062111054,"rtol":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: close-roots
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":1e-12,"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"min":1e-15,"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_poly::{
    ApproxPolynomial, ApproximateElimination, PolynomialApproximationPolicy, PolynomialBasis,
    SylvesterDiscriminant, approximate_resultant,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sci6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 6 });

let n = degree as usize;
let delta = RealScalar::powi(2.0_f64, -(separation_power as i32));
let lead = 3.0_f64;
let roots = [1.0, 1.0 + delta, -1.0, 2.0, -2.0, 3.0][..n].to_vec();
// p = 3 ∏(x − αᵢ), g = x − β (β is the nearby-root midpoint). Coefficients use ascending powers
let scale = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![lead]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let p = ApproxPolynomial::from_roots(&roots)
    .and_then(|monic| monic.try_mul(&scale))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let derivative = p.derivative().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let beta = 1.0 + delta / 2.0;
let g = [-beta, 1.0];
let (pc, dc) = (p.basis().coefficients(), derivative.basis().coefficients());

let res_pg = approximate_resultant(pc, &g).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let res_gp = approximate_resultant(&g, pc).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let res_pd = approximate_resultant(pc, dc).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let discriminant = SylvesterDiscriminant::new(n, PolynomialApproximationPolicy::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let disc = discriminant.compute(pc).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check: root-product formulas (without Sylvester matrices)
let product_pg = lead * roots.iter().map(|&a| a - beta).product::<f64>();
let mut squared = 1.0;
for i in 0..n {
    for j in i + 1..n {
        squared *= (roots[i] - roots[j]) * (roots[i] - roots[j]);
    }
}
let disc_product = (0..2 * n - 2).map(|_| lead).product::<f64>() * squared;
let swap_sign = if n % 2 == 1 { -1.0 } else { 1.0 };
let disc_sign = if (n * (n - 1) / 2) % 2 == 1 {
    -1.0
} else {
    1.0
};

let value = |e: ApproximateElimination<f64>| e.value().try_value().map_err(|e| e.to_string());
let entries = [
    ("Res(p, g)", value(res_pg)?, product_pg, res_pg),
    ("Res(g, p)", value(res_gp)?, swap_sign * product_pg, res_gp),
    (
        "D(p)（SylvesterDiscriminant）",
        value(disc)?,
        disc_product,
        disc,
    ),
    (
        "(−1)^{n(n−1)/2} Res(p, p′)/aₙ",
        disc_sign * value(res_pd)? / lead,
        disc_product,
        res_pd,
    ),
];
let rows: Vec<_> = entries
    .iter()
    .map(|(name, computed, reference, elimination)| {
        (
            *name,
            *computed,
            *reference,
            ((computed - reference) / reference).abs(),
            elimination.report().reciprocal_condition(),
        )
    })
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "SciOxide [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Root-product formula [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Matrix reciprocal condition number [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "identities" })
);
emit_text!(&format!(
    "δ = {delta:e}, exchange sign (−1)^n = {swap_sign}, discriminant sign (−1)^(n(n−1)/2) = {disc_sign}"
));
```

### Reading the results

With defaults $n = 3$ and $k = 6$, all four quantities agree with root products within relative discrepancy $5 \times 10^{-13}$.
Odd $n$ makes $\operatorname{Res}(g, p)$ and $\operatorname{Res}(p, g)$ opposite in sign; even values give the same sign.
When $n(n-1)/2$ is odd, as for $n = 2, 3, 6$, $\operatorname{Res}(p, p')$ and $D(p)$ have opposite signs.
Omitting division by leading coefficient $a_n = 3$ in row four gives three times $D(p)$.

Increasing $k$ decreases the matrix's reciprocal condition number roughly proportionally to $\delta^2$; at $n = 3$ it drops from about $2 \times 10^{-3}$ for $k = 2$ to $5 \times 10^{-14}$ for $k = 20$.
For nearby-root distance $\delta$, both $p$/$g$ and $p$/$p'$ become common-root pairs under perturbations $O(\delta^2)$.
Relative discrepancies also increase: at $n = 3$ and $k = 20$, $\operatorname{Res}(p, g)$ gives $2 \times 10^{-7}$ and the two discriminant computations give $4 \times 10^{-5}$ and $8 \times 10^{-5}$.
For defaults, $n = 2, 4, 6$, and $k = 2, 20$ alike, relative discrepancies stay below unit roundoff $u = 2^{-53}$ divided by the reciprocal condition number (about $2 \times 10^{-3}$ at $n = 3$ and $k = 20$).
$u$/reciprocal condition number indicates determinant sensitivity near close roots ([Determinant error indicators](oxref:poly-subresultants-approximate-elimination)); it is not an error bound.
Users judge whether a small resultant indicates a common root by comparing magnitude and reciprocal conditioning with tolerances; the resultant itself is no proof.

## Subresultants and remainder sequences

### Definition of subresultants

For $0 \le j < \min(m, n)$, consider an $(m+n-2j) \times (m+n-j)$ matrix with $n - j$ rows from $f$ and $m - j$ rows from $g$.
Let $\mathbf{M}_{j,k}$ be the square matrix selecting the first $m+n-2j-1$ columns and the column for $x^k$; define the following polynomial as subresultant $j$.

$$
S_j(f, g)(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{j} \det \mathbf{M}_{j,k} \, x^k, \qquad s_j = \det \mathbf{M}_{j,j}
$$

$s_j$ is the principal subresultant coefficient.
For $j = 0$, the matrix is Sylvester and $S_0 = s_0 = \operatorname{Res}(f, g)$.

The fundamental subresultant theorem characterizes GCD degree through principal coefficients.
$\deg \gcd(f, g) = d$ is equivalent to $s_0 = \dots = s_{d-1} = 0$ and $s_d \ne 0$; then $S_d$ is a nonzero constant multiple of $\gcd(f, g)$ (von zur Gathen and Gerhard, *Modern Computer Algebra*, Chapter 6).
Numerically, nearby common roots make $s_0, \dots, s_{d-1}$ relatively small; roots of $S_d$ approximate those nearby common roots.
The approximate GCD in [Polynomial GCDs and approximate GCDs](../approximate-gcd/) likewise selects degree from Sylvester singular values and initializes cofactors from singular vectors of the matrix corresponding to $S_d$ to recover factors.

### Euclidean remainder sequences and Brown normalization

Euclid over exact fields tends to grow remainder denominators and digit counts at each stage.
Avoiding fractions with pseudo-remainders $\operatorname{prem}(a, b) = \operatorname{lc}(b)^{\deg a - \deg b + 1} (a \bmod b)$ instead causes exponential coefficient growth.
A subresultant polynomial remainder sequence (PRS) divides each pseudo-remainder by known factors, normalizing elements to actual subresultants (determinants).
Their coefficients are determinants formed from input coefficients, so magnitude (digit count) grows only polynomially with stage count.

`exact_subresultants(left, right)` constructs this sequence with Brown's (1978) recurrence, returning `ExactSubresultants`.
`polynomials()` contains the two inputs in descending degree order and nonzero remainders.
The same recurrence handles defective stages dropping at least two degrees; sequences need not contain every degree.
The last element is a nonzero constant multiple of the input GCD, whose degree is common-factor degree.
`scalar_subresultants()` contains scalar subresultants corresponding to each element; the first is 1 by convention and the second is $\operatorname{lc}(b)^{m-n}$ for lower-degree input $b$ ($m \ge n$ are input degrees).
`resultant()` is the resultant in original input order, including exchange sign $(-1)^{mn}$.
One zero-polynomial input gives only the nonzero input (empty if both are zero), with resultant 0.
Ordinary dense division costs $O(d^2)$ field operations and retains $O(d^2)$ coefficients ($d = \max(m, n)$).
Users' coefficient types determine per-operation cost and coefficient bit-length growth.

`exact_discriminant(p)` computes $D(p)$ over an exact field, returns 1 for nonzero constants and linear polynomials, and rejects zero polynomials with `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Positive characteristic may lower the degree of $p'$ below $n-1$; the identity retains normalization for formal degree $n-1$.
If $p' = 0$ (for example $p(x) = x^P$ in characteristic $P$), the discriminant is 0.
Computation uses $\operatorname{Res}(p, p')$ through `exact_subresultants(p, p')` and costs $O(n^2)$ field operations.

Sturm sequences in the curve-intersection example are also remainder sequences, but reverse each remainder's sign to preserve sign variations for counting real roots.
Subresultant PRS elements differ from corresponding Sturm elements by positive or negative constants; sign-variation counts generally differ, so they cannot directly count real roots.
[Two-curve intersections](oxref:poly-exact-sturm-count) provides exact remainder-sequence and discriminant examples.

## Summary

Resultants examine common roots and discriminants multiple roots using coefficients alone, linked by convention $D(p) = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2} \operatorname{Res}(p, p')/a_n$.
Exchanging arguments introduces sign $(-1)^{mn}$; keep argument order consistent when using signs.
The first nonzero principal subresultant index gives GCD degree; over exact fields read it as the degree of the last remainder in `exact_subresultants`.
Floating-point resultant values can be small from rounding; use reciprocal conditioning and numerical rank from [Floating-point resultants and numerical rank](../approximate-elimination/) together to assess common roots.
