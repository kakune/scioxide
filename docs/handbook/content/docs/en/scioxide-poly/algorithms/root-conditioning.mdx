Reducing polynomial roots to eigenvalues and assessing candidate accuracy are separate tasks.
`scioxide-poly` constructs matrices with roots as eigenvalues (monomial companion matrices and orthogonal colleague or comrade matrices), computing candidate sensitivities and disks estimated to contain roots.
Compute eigenvalues with `scioxide-linalg`; simultaneous root iteration and stopping are covered in [rootfind's polynomial roots](../../../scioxide-rootfind/algorithms/polynomial-root-pipeline/).

## Monomial companion matrices

For $p(x)=a_nx^n+\cdots+a_0$ and $a_n\ne0$, matrix

$$
C=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&\cdots&0&-a_0/a_n\\
1&0&\cdots&0&-a_1/a_n\\
0&1&\cdots&0&-a_2/a_n\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&-a_{n-1}/a_n
\end{pmatrix}
$$

has characteristic polynomial $p/a_n$.
$C$ represents multiplication by $x$ in quotient ring $\mathbb C[x]/(p)$ with basis $1,x,\dots,x^{n-1}$; only its final column eliminates $x^n$ using $p$.
This is the Frobenius companion matrix.
Monomial polynomial `root_matrix()` returns it.
Entries are leading-coefficient ratios $a_i/a_n$, so common scaling of coefficients leaves the matrix mathematically unchanged.
This differs from variable scaling ($x\mapsto\sigma x$), which changes both roots and matrix.

Backward-stable QR gives exact eigenvalues of a matrix perturbed by $O(u)$ times $\|C\|$ ($u$ is unit roundoff, $2^{-53}\approx1.1\times10^{-16}$ in binary64).
The perturbation need not preserve companion structure; converting to coefficient perturbations can give coefficient backward error far larger than $u$ for unequal coefficient magnitudes (A. Edelman and H. Murakami, *Math. Comp.* 64, 1995).
Pre-eigenvalue balancing (diagonal similarity aligning row and column norms) often reduces this discrepancy.
Assess each eigenvalue candidate using backward errors and condition numbers below.

## Matrices with orthogonal-basis roots as eigenvalues

For basis recurrence $P_{k+1}=(a_kx+b_k)P_k-c_kP_{k-1}$,

$$
xP_k=\frac{P_{k+1}-b_kP_k+c_kP_{k-1}}{a_k}
$$


For expansion $p=\sum_{k=0}^n\gamma_kP_k$ ($\gamma_n\ne0$), place these three terms in columns $k\le n-2$ and the elimination of $P_n$ through $P_n=-\sum_{i<n}(\gamma_i/\gamma_n)P_i$ in the final column, representing multiplication by $x$ in coordinates $P_0,\dots,P_{n-1}$.
Its eigenvalues are also roots of $p$; it is tridiagonal plus a final-column correction.
This is a comrade matrix for general orthogonal bases and a colleague matrix for Chebyshev.
Orthogonal polynomial `root_matrix()` returns this matrix for its prepared basis.
Prepared recurrences include normalization and physical-coordinate mapping, giving physical roots $x$ without monomial expansion.

Construct matrices through these APIs.

- `ApproxPolynomial::root_matrix()` constructs and returns an owned `scioxide_linalg::Matrix` of size $n\times n$ from monomial or orthogonal polynomials.
  $n$ is structural degree, stored coefficient count minus 1.
  Matrices use the polynomial's variable coordinates; orthogonal bases on interval $[a,b]$ give eigenvalues in the same coordinates as $a,b$.
- `polynomial_root_matrix_into(coefficients, basis, &mut matrix, context)` writes the same entries into a caller-owned $n\times n$ matrix without allocation.
  Every fallible entry is computed before writing, preserving `matrix` on failure.
  Execution-context stops are checked at API entry and immediately before writing, returning `ExecutionStopped`.
- Pass `basis` either `PolynomialRootBasisRef::Monomial` or Plan-borrowing `PolynomialRootBasisRef::Orthogonal(&plan)`.
  Obtain it from polynomials with `PolynomialRootBasis::root_basis()` and distinguish variants with `is_monomial()`.
  Only monomial and orthogonal bases implement `PolynomialRootBasis`.

Empty coefficients give `InvalidInput` (`Empty`), degree-0 polynomials or zero leading coefficients `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), orthogonal coefficients exceeding Plan degree `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`), matrix shape mismatch `DimensionMismatch`, and non-finite coefficients `NonFinite`.
Real ratios use one correctly rounded division for normal or zero quotients; otherwise they use extended exponent range and round once.
Unrepresentable entries give `NumericalInstability`.
No diagonal similarity is applied; eigenvalue computation handles balancing (`scioxide_linalg` defaults to `GeneralEigenBalancing::PermuteAndScale`).
Matrix construction costs $O(n^2)$ time and memory; general eigenvalues cost $O(n^3)$ time, with allocations separate from the prepared basis's $O(n)$ recurrence.

## First-order sensitivity of simple roots

For simple root $p(r)=0$ with $p'(r)\ne0$, changing coefficients by $a_k+\Delta a_k$ gives first-order root shift $\Delta r$ satisfying

$$
p'(r)\Delta r+\sum_k\Delta a_kr^k\approx0
$$


For componentwise relative perturbations $|\Delta a_k|\le\eta|a_k|$, absolute and relative condition numbers giving $|\Delta r|\lesssim\eta\,\kappa_{\rm abs}(r)$ are

$$
\kappa_{\rm abs}(r)=\frac{\sum_k|a_k||r|^k}{|p'(r)|},\qquad
\kappa_{\rm rel}(r)=\frac{\kappa_{\rm abs}(r)}{|r|}
$$


$\kappa_{\rm rel}$ is undefined at $r=0$.
Changing only coefficient $a_k$ by absolute $\delta$ gives $\Delta r\approx-\delta r^k/p'(r)$; Wilkinson used this to show large movements even of separated integer roots when $|r|^k$ is large relative to $|p'(r)|$.

Multiple roots do not admit this linearization.
For multiplicity-$m$ root $r$, $p(r+\Delta)\approx p^{(m)}(r)\Delta^m/m!$; if coefficient perturbation changes $p$ by $\Delta p$,

$$
\Delta\approx\Bigl(-\frac{m!\,\Delta p(r)}{p^{(m)}(r)}\Bigr)^{1/m}
$$

so the root splits into $m$ roots moving on scale $\eta^{1/m}$ for relative perturbation $\eta$.
As simple roots approach within distance $2\varepsilon$, $|p'(r)|$ decreases proportionally to $\varepsilon$ and $\kappa_{\rm abs}$ grows proportionally to $1/\varepsilon$.
When movement approaches root separation, first-order prediction alone cannot track corresponding roots.

Backward error measures how close candidate $z$ is to a root.
In the componentwise model, the smallest relative perturbation making $z$ exact is

$$
\eta(z)=\frac{|p(z)|}{\sum_k|a_k||z|^k}
$$

with forward-error indicator $|z-r|\approx\kappa_{\rm abs}(r)\,\eta(z)$ (N. J. Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2002, §5.1).
Small backward error means only an exact root of a nearby problem; ill-conditioned roots can retain large forward errors.

`scioxide-poly` returns three quantities corresponding to $\eta$, $\kappa_{\rm abs}$, and $\kappa_{\rm rel}$ as `RootConditionReport`.
Each is a `RootEstimate`, with `Singular` for a zero denominator and `Inconclusive(ScalarRange)` outside scalar range.

| Accessor | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `coefficient()` | $B(z)/\lvert p(z)\rvert$ | Reciprocal of backward error $\eta(z)$. `Singular` when $p(z)=0$ |
| `absolute()` | $B(z)/\lvert p'(z)\rvert$ | Absolute conditioning $\kappa_{\rm abs}$. `Singular` when $p'(z)=0$ |
| `relative()` | $B(z)/(\lvert z\rvert\lvert p'(z)\rvert)$ | Relative conditioning. `Unavailable` when $z=0$ |

Envelope $B(z)$ describes coefficient perturbations: monomials use componentwise $B(z)=\sum_k|a_k||z|^k$, orthogonal bases use $B(z)=\max_i|\gamma_i|\sum_i|P_i(z)|$ with all coefficients allowed to move up to $\max_i|\gamma_i|$.
Different models preclude comparing only numerical condition values between bases.
Basis conversion also changes the input-error model.

`ApproxPolynomial::root_condition(point)` reports in the polynomial's own basis without monomials.
Monomial and orthogonal bases use these envelopes; other bases use componentwise evaluation envelopes.
Real-coefficient representations accept only real points; complex points require complex-coefficient polynomials.
The result of `checked_basis_evaluation(coefficients, z, basis)` also exposes the same report through `root_condition()`, with `normalized_residual()` corresponding to $\eta(z)$.
`PolynomialConditionReport` reports evaluation and basis-conversion conditioning and differs from root-sensitivity types.
The [ill-conditioned roots example](../../examples/wilkinson-root-sensitivity/) examines large movements of Wilkinson roots.

## Candidate roots and estimated disks

Given all distinct candidate roots $z_1,\dots,z_n$, define Weierstrass corrections by

$$
w_i=\frac{p(z_i)}{a_n\prod_{j\ne i}(z_i-z_j)}
$$


Update $z_i\leftarrow z_i-w_i$ is one Durand–Kerner (Weierstrass) step.
For diagonal $D=\mathrm{diag}(z_i)$ and $\mathbf w=(w_i)$, the characteristic polynomial of $A=D-\mathbf w\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}$ is monic of degree $n$ and agrees with $p(z_k)/a_n$ at $z=z_k$, hence equals $p/a_n$.
Eigenvalues of $A$ are roots of $p$; Gershgorin places all roots in the union of disks centered at $z_i-w_i$ with radii $(n-1)|w_i|$.
An isolated connected component of $k$ disks contains exactly $k$ roots (Braess–Hadeler and Carstensen inclusion theorems).
This theorem uses exact arithmetic.

`polynomial_root_diagnostics(coefficients, candidates)` estimates these disks from monomial coefficients (real or complex) and all candidates.
Exact high-order coefficient zeros are removed; candidate count must equal the remaining degree, otherwise `DimensionMismatch`.
Empty coefficients or the zero polynomial give `InvalidInput`; non-finite coefficients or candidates give `NonFinite`.
For each candidate compute `RootConditionReport`, nearest-other-candidate distance, and Weierstrass correction.
Correction computation is shared with `backend::polynomial_iteration::weierstrass_correction`.
It forms corrections from $p(z_i)/a_n$ and other candidates; returned `WeierstrassCorrection` distinguishes ordinary `Defined` corrections from undefined `Coincident` for floating-point-equal candidates.
Products and quotients first use ordinary floating-point arithmetic; intermediate overflow, underflow, or subnormals retry the whole computation in extended exponent range.
A zero real or imaginary correction component is accepted only when confirmed not lost to rounding (a real divisor and a zero corresponding numerator component); otherwise extended-range computation is retried as well.
Products follow candidates sorted lexicographically by real and imaginary parts, making results independent of input order.

Ordinary floating-point computation adds a radius term allowing for rounding in $p(z_i)$.
Let $\varepsilon$ be machine epsilon ($2^{-52}$ in binary64) and $B(z_i)=\sum_k|a_k||z_i|^k$; the radius is

$$
\rho_i=(n-1)|w_i|+n\cdot\frac{8(n+1)\varepsilon B(z_i)}{|a_n|\prod_{j\ne i}|z_i-z_j|}
$$


The second term divides Horner rounding model $8(n+1)\varepsilon B(z_i)$ by the correction's denominator, preventing a zero radius when the residual happens to be zero.
This is an error model rather than directed rounding; disks and cluster counts are not guaranteed enclosures.
For proofs use [validated's polynomial root counts](oxref:validated-api-roots-polynomial) (`PolynomialRootPlan`).
Combine overlapping disks into `RootCluster` by connected component.
Cost is $O(n^2)$ time and $O(n)$ memory.

Result items are as follows.

| Item | Role and status meaning |
|---|---|
| `RootDiagnostics` | Overall result. `roots()` sorts candidates lexicographically by real and imaginary parts; includes `clusters()`, `status()`, and `radii()` |
| `RootDiagnostic` | Per-candidate result. `input_index()` is the caller-array index, `candidate()` the candidate, `condition()` monomial `RootConditionReport`, `disk()` the disk, `candidate_separation()` nearest-other-candidate distance (not true root separation), and `cluster()` its cluster index |
| `RootDiskEstimate` | Estimated disk `center()` ($z_i-w_i$) and `radius()` ($\rho_i$) |
| `RootCluster` | Connected component of overlapping disks. `disk()` expands from the first candidate center to cover all component disks (not a minimum disk); `candidate_count()` counts disks; `separation()` is the gap to other cluster disks (0 if overlapping, `Unavailable` for one cluster). Candidate counts are not proved root counts |
| `RootEstimate` | Distinguishes `Finite`, `Singular` (zero first-order-model denominator), `Unavailable` (undefined quantity), and `Inconclusive` (undetermined by arithmetic or candidate arrangement). Do not replace missing values with zero |
| `RootEstimateFailure` | Unresolved reason. `ScalarRange` means outside scalar range (including a nonzero value rounded to zero); `CoincidentCandidates` means two floating-point-equal candidates, making correction undefined |
| `RootDiagnosticsStatus` | `Estimated` means disks were estimated for all candidates, not proved. `Inconclusive(_)` discards local disks and combines all candidates in one origin-centered cluster with Cauchy outer radius |
| `RootRadiusReport` / `polynomial_root_radii` | Outer radius `outer()` from Cauchy bound $\lvert z\rvert<1+\max_{i<n}\lvert a_i\rvert/\lvert a_n\rvert$; nonzero-root inner radius `inner_nonzero()` applying the same bound to the reversed polynomial after removing exact low-order zeros $a_0=\cdots=a_{m-1}=0$; origin-root count `zero_roots()` equal to zero coefficient count $m$; and degree `degree()`. Floating-point bounds costing $O(n)$ without allocation |
| `RootDiagnosticsRequirements` / `RootDiagnosticsWorkspace` | Output/workspace requirements by candidate count and reusable workspace. `RootDiagnosticsWorkspace::workspace_requirements(degree)` returns requirements (`degree()`, `required_bytes()`); `RootDiagnostics::try_reserve` and `RootDiagnosticsWorkspace::try_reserve` preallocate output and workspace. `RootDiagnosticsWorkspace::workspace(degree)` creates preallocated workspace; `check` returns sufficiency as `Capacity` without allocation |

Nearby candidates differ from candidates equal under floating-point comparison.
The latter make corrections undefined and give status `Inconclusive(CoincidentCandidates)`.
Unrepresentable disk centers or radii or cluster-covering radii give `Inconclusive(ScalarRange)`.
`polynomial_root_diagnostics` allocates output and workspace per call.
For repeated degrees, pass preallocated `RootDiagnostics` and `RootDiagnosticsWorkspace` to `polynomial_root_diagnostics_into(coefficients, candidates, &mut output, &mut workspace, context)`.
This form allocates nothing, returns `DimensionMismatch` before computation if capacity is insufficient, and swaps output only on success.
Execution-context stops are checked per candidate, returning `ExecutionStopped`.

## Conditioning and disks of approaching roots

$p(x)=(x-1)^2-\varepsilon^2=x^2-2x+(1-\varepsilon^2)$ has roots $1\pm\varepsilon$.
For $0<\varepsilon\le1$, $|a_0|=1-\varepsilon^2$, giving $\sum_k|a_k||r|^k=(1+r)^2-\varepsilon^2=4r$ and $|p'(r)|=2\varepsilon$ at $r=1\pm\varepsilon$.
Thus $\kappa_{\rm abs}(r)=2r/\varepsilon$ grows as the roots approach.
The next cell uses companion eigenvalues as candidates; its first table lists distance to closed-form roots, backward error $\eta$, SciOxide and closed-form condition estimates, disk radii, and cluster indices.
The second summarizes companion degree, cluster count, largest candidate count per cluster, and Cauchy radii, then emits diagnostic status as the outcome.
The input is exponent $q$ of $\varepsilon=10^{-q}$; coordinates are dimensionless.
Status `Estimated` gives satisfied; `Inconclusive(_)` gives partial, indicating replacement of local disks with a Cauchy disk.
The following definitions block provides coefficients and closed-form checks.
`closed_form_check(z, epsilon)` returns the distance from candidate $z$ to nearest closed-form root $r=1\pm\varepsilon$ and that root's $\kappa_{\rm abs}=2r/\varepsilon$.

```rust
//| id: poly-root-two-roots-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Coefficients and closed-form checks for approaching roots"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::Complex;

/// Ascending-power coefficients of p(x) = (x − 1)² − ε². Round 1 − ε² once here.
pub(crate) fn two_root_coefficients(epsilon: f64) -> [f64; 3] {
    [1.0 - epsilon * epsilon, -2.0, 1.0]
}

/// Independent check: distance to the closed-form root r = 1 ± ε nearest z, and κ_abs = 2r/ε (0 < ε ≤ 1).
pub(crate) fn closed_form_check(z: Complex<f64>, epsilon: f64) -> (f64, f64) {
    let (distance, nearest) = [1.0 - epsilon, 1.0 + epsilon]
        .iter()
        .map(|&r| ((z - Complex::new(r, 0.0)).norm(), r))
        .fold((f64::INFINITY, 0.0), |best, item| {
            if item.0 < best.0 { item } else { best }
        });
    (distance, 2.0 * nearest / epsilon)
}
```

At default $q=3$, distances to closed-form roots are $1.4\times10^{-14}$ and $1.5\times10^{-14}$; candidate $p(z)$ rounds to exactly 0, giving backward error $\eta$ equal to 0.
SciOxide condition estimates $1998.0$ and $2002.0$ agree with closed form $2r/\varepsilon$ within relative discrepancy $10^{-10}$.
Forward-error indicator $\kappa_{\rm abs}u\approx2\times10^{-13}$ for coefficient rounding $u\approx1.1\times10^{-16}$ exceeds actual root distances.
Since $p(z_i)=0$, Weierstrass corrections are also 0; radius $2.1\times10^{-11}$ comes entirely from rounding allowances.
Substituting $n=2$ and $B(z_i)\approx4$ gives term $48\varepsilon\cdot4/|z_1-z_2|\approx4.3\times10^{-14}/(2\times10^{-3})$.
Radii are far smaller than root separation $2\times10^{-3}$, giving separate candidates in two clusters.
Cauchy outer radius 3 and inner radius (about $1/3$) estimate root-magnitude ranges through $O(n)$ arithmetic.

Increasing $q$ raises conditioning proportionally to $1/\varepsilon$; closed-form-root distances grow at nearly the same rate ($4.1\times10^{-13}$ at $q=5$).
Rounding allowances are inverse to candidate separation $2\varepsilon$, so disks overlap at $2\varepsilon\lesssim\sqrt{2\cdot4.3\times10^{-14}}\approx2.9\times10^{-7}$.
At $q=7$, radius $2.1\times10^{-7}$ exceeds separation $2\times10^{-7}$, forming one cluster of 2 candidates.
This estimates inability to separate two roots at this precision; it does not prove multiplicity.
At $q=8$, storing $1-10^{-16}$ rounds it to $1-2^{-53}$, giving stored polynomial roots $1\pm1.05\times10^{-8}$.
Eigenvalues are $1\pm1.49\times10^{-8}$; of closed-form distance $4.9\times10^{-9}$, $5\times10^{-10}$ comes from input rounding and the rest from eigensolver error.
The latter also lies within $\kappa_{\rm abs}u\approx2\times10^{-8}$.
Condition estimate $1.34\times10^8$ is evaluated at candidates rather than true roots and therefore differs from closed-form $2\times10^8$.
At $q=9$, $1-10^{-18}$ rounds to 1, giving a stored double root 1.
Both eigenvalues are $1-2^{-53}$ and floating-point equal, giving status `Inconclusive(CoincidentCandidates)`.
Candidates lie $2^{-53}$ from the multiple root, so $p'(z)\ne0$; conditioning is finite but large, $3.6\times10^{16}$, showing that the first-order model has lost meaning.
Local disks become “None”; all candidates form one origin-centered cluster of radius 3, giving partial.
At $q=0$, one root is the origin ($a_0=0$).
The componentwise model preserves zero coefficients, so this root's $\kappa_{\rm abs}$ and disk radius are 0; conditioning of the other root 2 matches closed form 4.
Removing low-order zero $a_0=0$ leaves $-2+x$, giving inner radius $2/3$.

## Summary

Monomials give companion matrices and orthogonal bases recurrence-based comrade matrices; construct both through `root_matrix()` without monomial conversion.
Read candidate backward error $\eta(z)$ separately from conditioning $\kappa_{\rm abs}$, using their product to indicate forward error.
Small backward error alone does not establish root-position accuracy; conditioning grows as $1/\varepsilon$ when roots approach.
Weierstrass disks and clusters are floating-point estimates including rounding allowances; status `Inconclusive(_)` leaves only a Cauchy disk.
Use validated for root-count proofs.
