Multivariate polynomials can have far fewer nonzero terms than the number of monomials allowed by degree.
Sparse representations retain only exponents and coefficients of nonzero terms.
Coefficient arrays alone are insufficient; specify variable count and exponent ordering (monomial order).
This page explains canonical forms and arithmetic, reusable evaluation Plans and rounding, and limits on intermediate substitution terms.

## Sparse multi-indices

Write a monomial in $d$ variables as $x^\alpha=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}x_j^{\alpha_j}$.
Here $\alpha$ is a nonnegative integer exponent vector (multi-index) of total degree $|\alpha|=\sum_j\alpha_j$.
There are $\binom{n+d}{d}$ monomials of total degree at most $n$, so dense coefficient arrays grow rapidly with $d$.
For example, $d=6$ and $n=10$ give 8008.
Sparse form stores only $s$ nonzero terms as $s$-row, $d$-column exponents and $s$ coefficients.

`Monomial` borrows an exponent vector, computing and retaining total degree once.
`Monomial::new` returns `SizeOverflow` if total degree does not fit `usize`; an empty exponent vector represents a constant without variables.

`MonomialOrder` specifies monomial ordering.
For equal-length vectors $\alpha,\beta$, each order determines $x^\alpha>x^\beta$ as follows.

- `Lexicographic` (lexicographic) gives $x^\alpha>x^\beta$ if the first nonzero component of $\alpha-\beta$ is positive.
  Variable 0 has greatest priority.
- `GradedLexicographic` (graded lexicographic) compares total degrees, then lexicographic order for ties.
- `GradedReverseLexicographic` (graded reverse lexicographic) compares total degrees and, if they are equal, gives $x^\alpha>x^\beta$ if the last nonzero component of $\alpha-\beta$ is negative.

For example, $x_0^2x_2$ and $x_0x_1^2$ both have total degree 3.
Graded lexicographic order makes $x_0^2x_2$ larger because its $x_0$ exponent is larger.
Graded reverse lexicographic makes $x_0x_1^2$ larger because difference $(1,-2,1)$ has positive final component.
Terms are stored ascending in the selected order; the final term is the leading term.
The leading term of the same polynomial depends on the order.
`MonomialOrder::compare_monomials` compares monomials, returning `DimensionMismatch` for different variable counts.

`ApproxSparsePolynomial::new(dimension, order, exponents, coefficients)` takes flat exponents with `dimension` entries per term and coefficients, producing canonical form.
Canonicalization sorts terms ascending, adds duplicate-exponent coefficients in input order, and removes only exact zero coefficients.
Small coefficients are never discarded by tolerance.
Equal polynomials in canonical form with matching orders have identical term sequences.
Exponent-array length other than `dimension` times term count gives `DimensionMismatch`, non-finite coefficients `NonFinite`, and duplicate-coefficient sum overflow `NumericalInstability`.
`from_terms` builds the same canonical form from an iterator of `(exponents, coefficient)`, rejecting terms with exponent length other than `dimension` as `DimensionMismatch`.

`ExactSparsePolynomial` is an exact sparse polynomial using the same canonicalization with user coefficient types implementing `ExactCoefficient` (integers, rationals, finite fields, and so on).
SciOxide implements `ExactCoefficient` for no types.
Orphan rules prevent user crates implementing it for `f32` or `f64`, excluding floating-point coefficients.
Exact coefficient arithmetic has no non-finite or overflow checks.

`SparseTerms` returned by `terms()` borrows stored terms and provides `len`, `is_empty`, and `iter`.
`SparseTermIter` returns `(Monomial, &coefficient)` in canonical order, supports both ends, and allocates nothing.
`exponents()` and `coefficients()` borrow flat arrays unchanged; `degree()` returns maximum total term degree (0 for zero polynomials).

## Merging sums and products

Addition/subtraction `try_add` and `try_sub` merge two ascending sequences in one pass, removing exact cancellations.
Term counts $s,t$ require $O(s+t)$ comparisons.

Multiplication `try_mul` merges $st$ term products in ascending order.
For each shorter-side term, its products with the longer side form a sorted sequence, reducing the problem to merging $\min(s,t)$ sorted sequences.
Put sequence heads in a binary heap of $\min(s,t)$ entries; pop the minimum product and insert its sequence's next product (S. C. Johnson, "Sparse polynomial arithmetic", *ACM SIGSAM Bulletin* 8(3), 1974).
Equal-monomial products appear consecutively and have coefficients added immediately.
There are $O(st\log\min(s,t))$ comparisons and $st$ coefficient products; scratch memory is only the heap and one exponent vector.
Result storage is reserved up to the smaller of product-pair count and exponent-box size $\prod_j(\max\alpha_j+\max\beta_j+1)$.

Binary operations require matching variable counts and orders; different counts give `DimensionMismatch`, different orders `InvalidInput`.
Floating-point coefficient sum or product overflow gives `NumericalInstability`; complex products use extended exponent range near component range boundaries.
`try_mul` has no term-count limit.

`derivative(axis)` returns partial derivatives, rejecting out-of-range variable axes with `OutOfBounds`.
`permute_variables` renumbers variables, rejecting non-permutation arrays with `InvalidInput`.
`with_order` rebuilds canonical form for another ordering of the same polynomial.
`from_tensor` and `to_tensor_into` of `ApproxSparsePolynomial` convert to and from dense monomial-coefficient tensors.
`to_tensor_into` checks every exponent against tensor size before writing; failure gives `DimensionMismatch`.

## Sparse-evaluation Plans

Rebuilding powers per term when evaluating $p(x)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha x^\alpha$ repeatedly forms the same $x_j^k$.
`SparseEvaluationPlan` forms table $x_j,x_j^2,x_j^4,\ldots,x_j^{2^{b_j-1}}$ once per point, with $b_j$ the bit count of maximum exponent on axis $j$.
Each term starts from its coefficient, multiplying table entries for set bits in $\alpha_j$; terms are summed in canonical order.
Single-point table construction needs $\sum_j(b_j-1)$ squarings, each term $\sum_j\mathrm{popcount}(\alpha_j)$ multiplications, and summation $s$ additions.
Cost depends on exponent magnitudes as well as term count.

### Rounding error and conditioning

For real coefficients and points, use standard model $\mathrm{fl}(a\circ b)=(a\circ b)(1+\eta)$ and $|\eta|\le u$ ($u=2^{-53}$ for `f64`).
Table value $x_j^{2^k}$ uses $k$ squarings, equaling $x_j^{2^k}$ times $2^k-1$ factors of $(1+\eta)$.
Term $c_\alpha x^\alpha$ multiplies coefficient by table values in sequence; including table construction gives exactly $|\alpha|$ rounding factors.
Summation from the start adds at most $s-1$ factors per term.
With maximum total degree $N$ and $\gamma_k=ku/(1-ku)$, computed value $\hat p(x)$ satisfies

$$
|\hat p(x)-p(x)|\le\gamma_{N+s-1}\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha|\,|x^\alpha|
$$

(N. J. Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed., SIAM, 2002, §§3.1, 5.1).
The right-hand sum divided by $|p(x)|$ is the condition number of evaluation; relative error is bounded by that condition number times $\gamma_{N+s-1}$.
Cancellation points have large condition numbers; expanded evaluation can be less accurate than factored evaluation of the same polynomial.
Plans return no condition number; if needed, evaluate the polynomial with absolute-valued coefficients at $|x|$ to compute the sum on the right.

### Range loss and extended exponents

Evaluation starts in ordinary floating point; any power, product, or partial sum outside the safe range (components 0 or, for `f64`, magnitude roughly at least $2^{-511}$ and below $2^{510}$) retries that point from the start in extended exponent range, rounding once at the end.
Out-of-range coefficients are recognized during Plan construction and use extended range from the start.
Significand rounding remains the same in extended range, so the analysis above still holds.
Final overflow gives `NumericalInstability`; underflow rounds to subnormals or zero.
Wrong point dimension or short scratch gives `DimensionMismatch`; non-finite coordinates give `NonFinite`.

### Scratch space and lifetimes

Caller scratch length for one point is $1+\sum_jb_j$, queried with `workspace_requirements(1).scratch_len()`.
Axes with all-zero exponents need no table or scratch.
For batch evaluation `evaluate_batch_into` at $m$ points, `workspace_requirements(m)` returns the combined length for $m$ per-point values and $\sum_jb_j$ table entries per active worker.
Default sequential settings use one worker.
`evaluate_batch_into` evaluates point arrays of shape `[points, variables]`; mismatches give `InvalidInput` and failure preserves output.

`ApproxSparsePolynomial::evaluation_plan(options)` borrows terms and table layout without cloning coefficients, so construction costs little.
`SparseEvaluationPlan::new(dimension, order, exponents, coefficients, options)` borrows caller flat arrays, allocating table layout once.
Unlike owning constructors, it rejects zero coefficients, duplicate exponents, and disordered terms with `InvalidInput` instead of canonicalizing them.
Both Plans last only as long as borrowed arrays; evaluation allocates nothing.
`ApproxSparsePolynomial::evaluate(point)` evaluates one point identically, allocating power-table scratch per call.

`ExactSparseView` borrows exact-coefficient terms, constructed through `ExactSparsePolynomial::as_view()` or canonical-validating `ExactSparseView::new(dimension, order, exponents, coefficients)`.
It forms powers by repeated squaring per term without sharing per-axis tables.
One point needs no scratch; `evaluate_batch_into` uses one coefficient per point as scratch and preserves output on failure.
Coefficient cloning and arithmetic allocations follow the coefficient type's contract.
No exact-coefficient evaluation Plan corresponding to `SparseEvaluationPlan` is provided; use `ExactSparseView` for many points.

## Substitution and expansion limits

`substitute(replacements, target_dimension)` replaces each variable $x_i$ by a polynomial $r_i$ in `target_dimension` variables, producing $p(r_0,\ldots,r_{d-1})$.
Each term $c_\alpha x^\alpha$ expands to $c_\alpha\prod_ir_i^{\alpha_i}$.
Powers of $r_i$ form only needed $r_i^2,r_i^4,\ldots$ by repeated squaring and reuse them in later terms.
Term expansion starts from constant $c_\alpha$, multiplying $r_i^{2^k}$ for each set bit of $\alpha_i$.
Combine all term expansions into canonical form at the end.
Wrong substitution count, variable counts differing from `target_dimension`, or mismatched orders give `InvalidInput`.

Intermediates may have many terms even if the final result has few.
For example, substituting $x\to x+y$ and $y\to x-y$ into $x^my^m$ gives $(x^2-y^2)^m$ with $m+1$ terms.
However, intermediate $(x+y)^m(x-y)^j$ ($j$ is a partial sum from low bits of $m$) can have up to $m+j+1$ monomials of total degree $m+j$.

`substitute` does not limit intermediate terms.
`SparseExpansionLimits::new(max_terms)` passed to `substitute_with_options(replacements, target_dimension, limits)` limits terms in all these intermediate polynomials.

- Substitutions $r_i$ themselves on positive-exponent axes and their repeated squares $r_i^{2^k}$.
- Products while expanding each term.
- Partial sums of sequential term expansions.

Counts are canonical after merging duplicate exponents and removing exact cancellations.
Products check before adding new terms; product pairs and heap memory do not count as terms.
Exceeding the limit returns `ResourceLimit` without partial results.
`SparseExpansionLimits::default()` uses limit `usize::MAX`, meaning unlimited.
Setting `max_terms` to 0 allows only substitution into the zero polynomial.
The limit restricts only terms, not computation time or coefficient-type allocations.
`substitute_into(replacements, target_dimension, limits, output, context)` performs the same substitution as `substitute_with_options` while checking cancellation and deadlines through `ExecutionContext`, replacing output only on success.

The next cell applies the substitution above to $x^my^m$, tabulating results by term-count limit $L=m+1,\ldots,2m+1$.
The first table shows termination and result term count per limit; rows exceeding limits show “—” for term counts.
The second table provides independent checks.
Compare successful coefficients with binomial coefficients $(-1)^j\binom mj$, evaluate the result at point $(3,2)$ through a Plan, and compare with $(3^2-2^2)^m=5^m$.
For $m\le12$, all intermediates are integers below $2^{53}$, so evaluation should agree exactly with $5^m$.
The cell passes exponent $m$ and result exponents and coefficients to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: poly-sparse-substitution
//| ref: poly-sparse-substitution
//| title: "Intermediate substitution terms and sparse-evaluation scratch"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| exports: [power, exponents, coefficients]
//| inputs:
//|   power: {"type":"range","label":"Exponent m [-]","description":"Exponent of p = xᵐyᵐ. Intermediate expansion depends on the binary form of m.","min":2,"max":12,"step":1,"value":6}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"power-of-two","label":"m = 8","description":"A power-of-two m has maximum intermediate term count equal to final count m + 1.","inputs":{"power":8}}
//|   - {"id":"largest","label":"m = 12","description":"The largest intermediate is the 17-term (x + y)¹²(x − y)⁴, first succeeding at L = 17.","inputs":{"power":12}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"ResourceLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":"Success"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":7}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":15625}
//|   - case: power-of-two
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Success"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":390625}
//|   - case: largest
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":"ResourceLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"limits","path":"/rows/4/2","equals":13}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":11}
//|       - {"artifact":"check","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":244140625}
use scioxide_poly::{
    ApproxSparsePolynomial, MonomialOrder, SparseEvaluationPlan, SparseExpansionLimits,
};

let m = power as usize;
let order = MonomialOrder::GradedLexicographic;
// p(x, y) = xᵐyᵐ and substitutions x → x + y, y → x − y (two exponents per term).
let p = ApproxSparsePolynomial::new(2, order, vec![m, m], vec![1.0_f64])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sum = ApproxSparsePolynomial::new(2, order, vec![1, 0, 0, 1], vec![1.0, 1.0])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let difference = ApproxSparsePolynomial::new(2, order, vec![1, 0, 0, 1], vec![1.0, -1.0])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let replacements = [sum, difference];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut result = None;
for limit in m + 1..=2 * m + 1 {
    let limits = SparseExpansionLimits::new(limit);
    match p.substitute_with_options(&replacements, 2, limits) {
        Ok(q) => {
            rows.push((limit, "Success".to_string(), Some(q.terms().len())));
            result.get_or_insert(q);
        }
        Err(e) => rows.push((limit, format!("{:?}", e.kind()), None)),
    }
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Intermediate term limit L [terms]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Termination" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Result terms [terms]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "limits" })
);

let q = result.ok_or("Substitution failed at every limit")?;
// Independent check: coefficient of x^(2m−2j) y^(2j) in (x² − y²)ᵐ is (−1)ʲ C(m, j).
let mut coefficient_error = 0.0_f64;
for (monomial, &c) in q.terms() {
    let j = monomial.exponents()[1] / 2;
    let binomial = (0..j).fold(1.0, |b, i| b * (m - i) as f64 / (i + 1) as f64);
    let expected = if j % 2 == 0 { binomial } else { -binomial };
    coefficient_error = coefficient_error.max((c - expected).abs());
}
// Borrow flat result exponents and coefficients, validate canonical form, and construct a Plan.
let plan = SparseEvaluationPlan::new(
    q.dimension(),
    q.order(),
    q.exponents(),
    q.coefficients(),
    Default::default(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let scratch_len = plan
    .workspace_requirements(1)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .scratch_len();
let mut scratch = vec![0.0; scratch_len];
let value = plan
    .evaluate(&[3.0, 2.0], &mut scratch)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let leading = q.terms().iter().next_back().ok_or("Result is the zero polynomial")?.0;
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Leading-term exponent of x", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum binomial-coefficient discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Scratch [entries]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "p(3, 2) from Plan [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "5ᵐ [-]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let check = vec![(
    leading.exponents()[0],
    coefficient_error,
    scratch_len,
    value,
    5_u64.pow(m as u32),
)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    check,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "check" })
);
export!(power, m);
export!(exponents, q.exponents());
export!(coefficients, q.coefficients());
```

At default $m=6$, $L=7,8$ gives `ResourceLimit`; success starts at $L=9$ and produces 7 terms.
Since $6=110_2$, multiplying intermediate $(x+y)^6$ by $(x-y)^2$ gives a polynomial of total degree 8 with 9 terms.
The first successful term-count limit is $m+1=9$ for $m=8=1000_2$, where intermediate and final maxima agree; for $m=12=1100_2$ it is set by the 17 terms of $(x+y)^{12}(x-y)^4$.
Coefficients match binomial values, and the leading term is graded-lexicographic maximum $x^{2m}$.
Scratch has $1+2b$ entries ($b$ counts bits of $2m$), giving 9 at $m=6$ and 11 at $m=12$.
$p(3,2)$ matches $5^{12}=244140625$ even at $m=12$.

## Expanded-form evaluation and cancellation

The error analysis above makes expanded-form accuracy depend on condition number $\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha||x^\alpha|/|p(x)|$.
In expanded $(x^2-y^2)^m$ at positive points, $\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha||x^\alpha|=(x^2+y^2)^m$, giving the condition number

$$
\kappa(x,y)=\left(\frac{x^2+y^2}{|x^2-y^2|}\right)^m
$$


Near diagonal $x=y$, values shrink to order $(2\delta)^m$ ($\delta=x-y$) while term magnitudes remain around $2^m$, making $\kappa$ grow as $\delta^{-m}$.

The next cell borrows exponent $m$ and result exponents and coefficients from the preceding cell, builds a Plan through `SparseEvaluationPlan::new`, and evaluates at $x=1+\delta$ and $y=1$.
Evaluate the polynomial with the same exponents and absolute coefficients at those points to compute right-hand sum $\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha||x^\alpha|$.
Reference factored form $((x-y)(x+y))^m$ computes $x-y$ exactly by Sterbenz's lemma; remaining relative rounding is about $3mu$.
References use `f64` intrinsic `powi`, without SciOxide functions.
The last column divides actual relative error by bound $\gamma_{N+s-1}\kappa$ ($N=2m$, $s=m+1$); a ratio at most 1 satisfies the bound.

```rust
//| id: poly-sparse-cancellation
//| ref: poly-sparse-cancellation
//| title: "Expanded-form evaluation and conditioning"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| dependsOn:
//|   solve: poly-sparse-substitution
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"cancellation","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1358431.9067819,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cancellation","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cancellation","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"cancellation","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"min":1}}
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxSparsePolynomial, MonomialOrder, SparseEvaluationPlan};

let m = solve["power"].as_u64().ok_or("No exponent")? as usize;
let exponents: Vec<usize> =
    serde_json::from_value(solve["exponents"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let coefficients: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(solve["coefficients"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let order = MonomialOrder::GradedLexicographic;
// Borrow canonical terms and validate them without sorting to construct a Plan.
let plan = SparseEvaluationPlan::new(2, order, &exponents, &coefficients, Default::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Absolute-coefficient polynomial. At positive points its value is error-bound sum Σ|c_α| x^α.
let magnitudes: Vec<f64> = coefficients.iter().map(|c| c.abs()).collect();
let envelope = ApproxSparsePolynomial::new(2, order, exponents.clone(), magnitudes)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let envelope_plan = envelope
    .evaluation_plan(Default::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let scratch_len = plan
    .workspace_requirements(1)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .scratch_len();
let mut scratch = vec![0.0; scratch_len];
// γ_k = k u / (1 − k u), k = N + s − 1 (N = 2m is maximum total degree; s is term count).
let unit = f64::EPSILON / 2.0;
let k = (2 * m + coefficients.len() - 1) as f64;
let gamma = k * unit / (1.0 - k * unit);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for e in 1..=6 {
    let (x, y) = (1.0 + 10.0_f64.powi(-e), 1.0);
    let value = plan
        .evaluate(&[x, y], &mut scratch)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let absolute = envelope_plan
        .evaluate(&[x, y], &mut scratch)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Reference: factored form ((x − y)(x + y))ᵐ, using f64 powi.
    let reference = ((x - y) * (x + y)).powi(m as i32);
    let relative = (value - reference).abs() / reference.abs();
    let condition = absolute / reference.abs();
    rows.push((
        x - y,
        reference,
        relative,
        condition,
        relative / (gamma * condition),
    ));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "δ = x − y [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Reference (x² − y²)ᵐ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Expanded-form relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Condition number κ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative error / (γ κ) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "cancellation" })
);
```

At default $m=6$ and $\delta=10^{-1}$, the condition number is $\kappa\approx1.36\times10^6$ and relative error $3.8\times10^{-11}$.
At $\delta=10^{-2}$, $\kappa\approx1.0\times10^{12}$, and relative error rises to $3.0\times10^{-6}$.
At $\delta=10^{-3}$, 7 terms cancel exactly to computed 0, giving relative error 1.
For smaller $\delta$, computed values have unrelated rounding-error magnitude (absolute size about $10^{-15}$), making relative error far above 1.
Every row has ratio below 0.05, satisfying the bound, which is 20–2000 times actual error.
The condition-number column matches $((x^2+y^2)/(x^2-y^2))^m$, growing about $10^m$-fold per tenfold decrease in $\delta$.
Factored $((x-y)(x+y))^m$ retains relative accuracy around $3mu$ at the same points; cancellation belongs to the expanded representation, not the polynomial itself.

## Summary

Construct sparse polynomials with matching variable counts and monomial orders, reading the final term as the leading term.
Products merge through a heap of $\min(s,t)$ entries and require $O(st\log\min(s,t))$ comparisons; the product of term counts indicates cost.
For repeated evaluation, construct `SparseEvaluationPlan` by borrowing the polynomial and prepare $1+\sum_jb_j$ scratch entries once for allocation-free evaluation.
Relative evaluation error is bounded by the condition number $\sum_\alpha|c_\alpha||x^\alpha|/|p(x)|$ times $\gamma_{N+s-1}$; consider factored forms or other bases at cancellation points.
Intermediate substitution expansions can exceed final size; supply intermediate term limits to `SparseExpansionLimits` and reconsider limits or expression form after `ResourceLimit`.
