Products of per-axis Bernstein bases represent surfaces and multivariate polynomials whose ranges can be estimated from control-point minima and maxima.
A control-point array alone is insufficient; specify axis order, each axis interval, and output-component layout.
This page explains control-net storage and evaluation, guarantees of convex-hull range estimates, and how quickly subdivision tightens ranges.
Univariate bases, de Casteljau, and rounding are covered in [Bernstein representations and de Casteljau's algorithm](../multivariate-and-bernstein/).

## Tensor-product control nets

`TensorBernstein` represents surfaces and other functions by products of per-axis Bernstein bases,

$$
p(t_0,\ldots,t_{d-1})=\sum_{i_0,\ldots,i_{d-1}}b_{i_0\cdots i_{d-1}}\prod_jB_{i_j}^{n_j}(t_j)
$$


Degrees $(n_0,\ldots,n_{d-1})$ give $N=\prod_j(n_j+1)$ control points, each holding output components.
Storage is row-major with the last axis fastest, followed by contiguous output components per control point.
For two axes and three components, store the three components of $(i,j)$, then advance $j$; after traversing $j$, advance $i$.
One component represents a multivariate polynomial, multiple real components Bézier curves or surfaces, and zero axes a single point.

`TensorBernstein::new(degrees, domains, components, coefficients)` rejects zero components, mismatched axis/domain counts, intervals with $\mathrm{lower}\ge\mathrm{upper}$, and wrong control-point counts with `InvalidInput`, and non-finite control points with `NonFinite`.
`TensorBernstein::with_options` performs the same checks and takes batch execution settings `PolynomialExecutionOptions`, returning `InvalidInput` for worker limits above 64.
Evaluation contracts one axis at a time with de Casteljau, starting with the last.
Last-axis contraction uses $O(n_{d-1}^2)$ operations for each of $N/(n_{d-1}+1)$ sequences, giving total $O(N\max_j(n_j+1))$ per point and component.
Per-axis rounding accumulates, bounded on real boxes by $\gamma_{3\sum_jn_j}\max|b|$ ($\gamma_k=ku/(1-ku)$; $u$ is unit roundoff; see univariate [Rounding error and conditioning](oxref:poly-bernstein-rounding-condition)).

`evaluate(point)` allocates output and scratch, returning all components at one point as a vector.
`as_view()` returns a control-net-borrowing `TensorBernsteinView` without cloning coefficients or retaining caches.
`TensorBernsteinView::new(extents, domains, components, coefficients, options)` borrows caller arrays and takes per-axis control-point counts (degree + 1) through `extents`, rather than degrees; the same checks include zero counts.
Pass `evaluate_into(point, output, scratch, context)` a point, output, caller scratch, and execution context.
Query scratch through `workspace_requirements(1).scratch_len()`; default sequential settings need component count $k$ plus control-point count $N$.
Parallel settings add $N$ entries per worker.
Real and complex parameters and extrapolation are accepted; mismatched shapes or short scratch give `DimensionMismatch`, preserving output on failure.
`evaluate_batch_into` evaluates points of shape `[points, axes]` and writes their $k$ components in order.
`derivative(axis)`, `elevate_degree(axis, degree)`, and `subdivide(axis, point)` act along one axis, rejecting out-of-range axes with `InvalidInput`.

## Convex hull property and range tightening by subdivision

For real control nets, `control_bounds()` returns `BernsteinBounds`; `coordinates()` gives intervals of componentwise control minima and maxima.
Univariate real Bernstein representations have the same `control_bounds()`.
Distinguish exact from floating-point arithmetic when interpreting this interval's guarantees.

- In exact arithmetic, every component's values over a real parameter box lie in this interval.
  `control_bounds()` extracts stored minima and maxima without rounding, so containment is exact for the polynomial defined by those stored control points.
- Stored control points have undergone construction, conversion, subdivision, and degree-elevation rounding.
  Subdivision computes child control points from the parent control points with rounding and also rounds the split normalization $t=(x-a)/(b-a)$; the union of child intervals can miss the parent polynomial's range by rounding amounts.
- Floating-point values $\hat p$ can leave the interval by evaluation rounding error (at most $\gamma_{3\sum_jn_j}\max|b|$ on real boxes).
- Thus `BernsteinBounds` is an estimate subject to rounding shifts, not an enclosure guaranteed by outward rounding.
  Use scioxide-validated interval arithmetic for proved ranges.

Control-point ranges are often wider than actual ranges but approach them under subdivision.
Represent quadratic $(u-c)^2$ on width-$h$ interval $[c+e,c+e+h]$: in local variable $s\in[0,1]$, $(e+hs)^2=e^2+2ehs+h^2s^2$, giving control points $e^2$, $e^2+eh$, and $(e+h)^2$.
Endpoint controls equal endpoint values; the central control is $h^2/4$ below midpoint value $(e+h/2)^2$.
Thus the control range extends below the actual range by at most $h^2/4$, with no upper excess.
The control net for $z(u,v)=(u-c_u)^2+\kappa(v-c_v)^2$ sums per-axis controls $f_i+\kappa g_j$; splitting $[0,1]^2$ into $k\times k$ equal pieces bounds excess of the union of control ranges by $(1+|\kappa|)/(4k^2)$.
For general degree, subdivided control polygons approach the polynomial at rate $O(h^2)$ (G. Farin, *Curves and Surfaces for CAGD*, 5th ed., Morgan Kaufmann, 2002).

The next cell represents dimensionless surface $z(u,v)=(u-a)^2+\kappa(v-0.6)^2$ by a biquadratic net on $[0,1]^2$.
Inputs are extremum location $a$ in direction $u$ and coefficient $\kappa$ in direction $v$.
For $\kappa<0$, the surface has a saddle point.
For each subdivision count $k=1,2,4,8,16$, form $k\times k$ pieces and compare their control-range union with closed-form range $[\min z,\max z]$.
Evaluate $5\times5$ grid points per piece with `TensorBernsteinView::evaluate_into`, measuring how far values fall outside each control range and differ from the closed form.
Finite-grid checks do not replace proof of the convex hull property.
The following definitions block constructs control nets and equal subdivisions.
`surface(center, kappa)` computes axis controls with `to_bernstein` to form the net; `split_uniform(patch, k)` calls `subdivide` $k-1$ times per axis to return $k^2$ pieces.

```rust
//| id: poly-bernstein-surface-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Biquadratic control net and equal subdivisions"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
use scioxide_core::Interval;
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, PolynomialBasis, TensorBernstein};

/// Extremum location b in the v direction.
pub(crate) const V_CENTER: f64 = 0.6;

/// Use to_bernstein for controls of (s − c)² = c² − 2c s + s² on [0, 1].
pub(crate) fn quadratic_controls(c: f64) -> Result<Vec<f64>, String> {
    let monomial =
        ApproxPolynomial::monomial(vec![c * c, -2.0 * c, 1.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let conversion = monomial.to_bernstein(0.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(conversion.polynomial().basis().coefficients().to_vec())
}

/// Control net of z(u, v) = (u − a)² + κ(v − b)² on [0, 1]². Store f_i + κ g_j row-major (v fastest).
pub(crate) fn surface(center: f64, kappa: f64) -> Result<TensorBernstein<f64>, String> {
    let (f, g) = (quadratic_controls(center)?, quadratic_controls(V_CENTER)?);
    let net: Vec<f64> = f
        .iter()
        .flat_map(|&fi| g.iter().map(move |&gj| fi + kappa * gj))
        .collect();
    let unit = Interval::new(0.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    TensorBernstein::new(&[2, 2], vec![unit; 2], 1, net).map_err(|e| e.to_string())
}

/// k² pieces dividing each axis equally into k parts. Split points j/k lie strictly inside the remaining interval.
pub(crate) fn split_uniform(
    surface: &TensorBernstein<f64>,
    k: usize,
) -> Result<Vec<TensorBernstein<f64>>, String> {
    let mut patches = vec![surface.clone()];
    for axis in 0..2 {
        let mut next = Vec::new();
        for patch in patches {
            let mut rest = patch;
            for j in 1..k {
                let (left, right) = rest
                    .subdivide(axis, j as f64 / k as f64)
                    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
                next.push(left);
                rest = right;
            }
            next.push(rest);
        }
        patches = next;
    }
    Ok(patches)
}
```

```rust
//| id: poly-bernstein-bounds
//| ref: poly-bernstein-bounds
//| title: "Control-point ranges and subdivision tightening"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| uses: [poly-bernstein-surface-model]
//| inputs:
//|   center: {"type":"range","label":"Extremum location a in the u direction [-]","description":"Minimum location of (u − a)². Moving it to an endpoint puts the minimum at a corner.","min":0,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.35}
//|   kappa: {"type":"range","label":"v-direction coefficient κ [-]","description":"Negative values make the v direction concave, giving a saddle surface.","min":-2,"max":2,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"saddle","label":"κ = −1.5","description":"Upper excess also appears; excess × k² is bounded by (1 + 1.5)/4 = 0.625.","inputs":{"kappa":-1.5}}
//|   - {"id":"corner-extrema","label":"a = 0, κ = 0","description":"Both extrema occur at corners, so control and actual ranges agree before subdivision.","inputs":{"center":0,"kappa":0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.4675,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/4/4","approx":{"value":0.001875,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/4/5","range":{"max":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/4/6","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/4/7","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"range","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.7825,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: saddle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.7825,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/4/5","range":{"max":0.625}}
//|       - {"artifact":"range","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":-0.54,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: corner-extrema
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bounds","path":"/rows/4/4","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_poly::{BernsteinBounds, TensorBernsteinView};

let b = V_CENTER;
let net = surface(center, kappa)?;
// Independent check: separability makes the range the sum of per-axis quadratic ranges (closed form).
let f_max = (center * center).max((1.0 - center) * (1.0 - center));
let g_max = kappa * (b * b).max((1.0 - b) * (1.0 - b));
let (exact_low, exact_high) = (g_max.min(0.0), f_max + g_max.max(0.0));
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for k in [1_usize, 2, 4, 8, 16] {
    let patches = split_uniform(&net, k)?;
    let (mut low, mut high) = (f64::INFINITY, f64::NEG_INFINITY);
    let (mut overshoot, mut error) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for patch in &patches {
        let bounds: BernsteinBounds<f64> = patch.control_bounds().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let range = bounds.coordinates()[0];
        low = low.min(range.lower());
        high = high.max(range.upper());
        let view: TensorBernsteinView<'_, f64> = patch.as_view();
        let requirements = view.workspace_requirements(1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let mut scratch = vec![0.0; requirements.scratch_len()];
        let mut output = [0.0];
        let (du, dv) = (patch.domains()[0], patch.domains()[1]);
        for i in 0..5 {
            for j in 0..5 {
                let u = du.lower() + (du.upper() - du.lower()) * i as f64 / 4.0;
                let v = dv.lower() + (dv.upper() - dv.lower()) * j as f64 / 4.0;
                view.evaluate_into(
                    &[u, v],
                    &mut output,
                    &mut scratch,
                    ExecutionContext::sequential(),
                )
                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
                let z = output[0];
                overshoot = overshoot.max(range.lower() - z).max(z - range.upper());
                let closed = (u - center) * (u - center) + kappa * (v - b) * (v - b);
                error = error.max((z - closed).abs());
            }
        }
    }
    let excess = (exact_low - low) + (high - exact_high);
    rows.push((
        k,
        patches.len(),
        low,
        high,
        excess,
        excess * (k * k) as f64,
        overshoot,
        error,
    ));
}
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Subdivision count k [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Pieces [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Minimum control point [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum control point [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Excess beyond actual range [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Excess × k² [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Grid-value range violation [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Maximum closed-form discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "bounds" })
);
let summary_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Closed-form minimum [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Closed-form maximum [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Bound (1 + |κ|)/4 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 }
]);
let summary = vec![(exact_low, exact_high, (1.0 + kappa.abs()) / 4.0)];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    summary_columns,
    summary,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "range" })
);
```

With defaults, excess falls from $0.4675$ at $k=1$ to $0.001875$ at $k=16$; excess × $k^2$ stays between 0.32 and 0.48, below bound 0.5.
Minimum control point $-0.4675$ at $k=1$ equals the sum of central per-axis controls $a^2-a=-0.2275$ and $b^2-b=-0.24$.
For $\kappa>0$, the surface is convex; the upper endpoint of its value range occurs at a corner control, giving zero upper excess.
For $\kappa=-1.5$, direction $v$ is concave; central control $0.36$ of $\kappa g$ pushes range endpoint $0.4225$ upward, adding upper excess $0.36$.
Lower excess $0.2275$ then comes only from the $u$ term.
Excess × $k^2$ lies between 0.40 and 0.60, below bound $(1+1.5)/4=0.625$.
For $a=0,\kappa=0$, both extrema are at corners, so excess is 0 even before subdivision.
For tried inputs (defaults, two cases, and each input's endpoints), grid range violations were 0 and closed-form discrepancies at most $7\times10^{-16}$.
When each axis is linear, as in bilinear surfaces, controls are corner values; range endpoints occur at corners and control ranges equal actual ranges.
At degree at least two, control ranges are wider, as here, and tighten under subdivision.

## Summary

Construct tensor surfaces with consistent axis order and intervals; store controls with the last axis fastest and each control's components contiguous.
Contract by de Casteljau from the last axis; avoid allocation by passing `workspace_requirements(1)`-length scratch to `TensorBernsteinView::evaluate_into`.
Ranges from `control_bounds()` exactly enclose the polynomial defined by stored controls, but construction and subdivision rounding make them estimates; use scioxide-validated for proved ranges.
Degree-two or higher control nets have ranges wider than actual ranges; dividing each axis into $k$ equal parts approaches the true range at rate $O(1/k^2)$, allowing subdivision counts to be estimated from accuracy needs.
