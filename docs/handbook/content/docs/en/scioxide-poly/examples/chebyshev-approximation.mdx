Real-axis plots alone do not reveal how readily high-degree polynomials approximate a function.
Runge function $1/(1+25x^2)$ is infinitely differentiable on the real axis but has poles at $\pm i/5$ in the complex plane.
Exponential $e^x$ has no finite complex singularities, while absolute value $|x|$ is not differentiable at the origin.
Compare Chebyshev coefficient decay for these three functions to identify which properties determine its rate.
All coordinates and values are dimensionless.

## Expansion conventions and complex singularities

Expand a function on $[-1,1]$ as $f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty c_kT_k(x)$, multiplying $c_0$ by the same $T_0=1$ as other coefficients.
Setting $x=\cos\theta$ turns Chebyshev expansion into a cosine series of $f(\cos\theta)$, with

$$
c_k=\frac{2-\delta_{k0}}{\pi}\int_0^\pi f(\cos\theta)\cos k\theta\,d\theta
$$


For analytic functions, deforming this integral to a complex contour bounds geometric coefficient decay.

Mapping circle $|w|=\rho>1$ through $x=(w+w^{-1})/2$ gives the Bernstein ellipse $E_\rho$ with foci $\pm1$.
If $f$ is analytic inside $E_\rho$ and satisfies $|f|\le M$ there, coefficients for $k\ge1$ satisfy $|c_k|\le2M\rho^{-k}$ (Trefethen, ATAP, Theorem 8.1).
The ellipse through $ia$ ($a>0$) follows from $w=i(a+\sqrt{a^2+1})$ as

$$
\rho=a+\sqrt{a^2+1}
$$

For Runge poles $\pm i/5$ ($a=1/5$), this gives $\rho=1/5+\sqrt{1+1/25}\approx1.2198$ and $\ln\rho\approx0.1987$.
$f$ is analytic but unbounded inside the pole-touching ellipse, so the theorem's bound needs a slightly smaller $\rho$.
The decay rate itself is nevertheless determined by this $\rho$.
Runge coefficients have closed forms from partial fractions and Cauchy-kernel expansion $1/(z-x)=(z^2-1)^{-1/2}\bigl(1+2\sum_{k\ge1}w^{-k}T_k(x)\bigr)$ ($z=(w+w^{-1})/2$, $|w|>1$).
Setting $s=\sqrt{1+a^2}$ gives

$$
c_0=\frac as,\qquad c_{2j}=(-1)^j\frac{2a}{s}\rho^{-2j},\qquad c_{2j+1}=0
$$

so even-degree coefficients form an exact geometric sequence of ratio $-\rho^{-2}$.
On a semilog plot, they lie on a line with slope $-\ln\rho$.

The exponential is analytic inside every ellipse with $\rho$, so coefficients decrease faster than any geometric sequence.
Indeed, $e^x=I_0(1)+2\sum_{k\ge1}I_k(1)T_k(x)$ ($I_k$ is a modified Bessel function of the first kind), and $c_k\approx2^{1-k}/k!$.
Its semilog curve steepens progressively rather than forming one straight line.

For absolute value,

$$
c_0=\frac2\pi,\qquad c_{2j}=\frac{4(-1)^{j-1}}{\pi(4j^2-1)},\qquad c_{2j+1}=0
$$

giving algebraic decay $|c_k|=O(k^{-2})$.
This corresponds to $|x|$ being absolutely continuous, with first derivative $\operatorname{sign}x$ discontinuous at $x=0$ but of bounded variation (total variation $V=2$).
ATAP Theorem 7.1 with $\nu=1$ gives $|c_k|\le2V/(\pi k(k-1))=4/(\pi k(k-1))$; actual $4/(\pi(k^2-1))$ lies just inside this bound.
On logarithmic axes, coefficients lie on a line of slope $-2$.
Runge and $|x|$ are even, so odd coefficients vanish.

Truncation at degree $n$ has error bounded by the tail sum

$$
S_n=\sum_{k>n}|c_k|
$$

giving $\max_{[-1,1]}|f-\sum_{k\le n}c_kT_k|\le S_n$.
For Runge, $S_n=(2a/s)\rho^{-(n+2)}/(1-\rho^{-2})=O(\rho^{-n})$ (even $n$).
For absolute value, partial fractions $1/(4j^2-1)=\tfrac12\bigl(1/(2j-1)-1/(2j+1)\bigr)$ telescope to $S_n=2/(\pi(n+1))=O(1/n)$.
Coefficient decay and truncation theory appear in Trefethen, *Approximation Theory and Approximation Practice* (ATAP), Chapters 7 and 8, and [DLMF §3.11(ii)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/3.11.ii).

## Sampled coefficients and aliasing

The cells take $n+1$ samples at zeros $x_j=\cos((j+1/2)\pi/(n+1))$ ($j=0,\dots,n$) of $T_{n+1}$ (first-kind Chebyshev nodes) and obtain degree-$n$ coefficients $\tilde c_k$ through `PolynomialFitPlan::orthogonal`.
The square full-rank sample matrix makes this fitting interpolation.
Computed $\tilde c_k$ are not the infinite-series coefficients $c_k$ themselves.
Since $T_{2m(n+1)\pm k}(x_j)=(-1)^mT_k(x_j)$, high-degree components fold into lower degrees,

$$
\tilde c_k=c_k+\sum_{m\ge1}(-1)^m\bigl(c_{2m(n+1)-k}+c_{2m(n+1)+k}\bigr)\qquad(1\le k\le n)
$$

which is aliasing.
For $k=0$, both terms have the same $c_{2m(n+1)}$, so count it only once.
For example, at $k=n$ the leading term is $-c_{n+2}$; Runge has $c_{n+2}=-\rho^{-2}c_n$, giving $\tilde c_n\approx(1+\rho^{-2})c_n\approx1.67\,c_n$.
As $k$ approaches $n$, aliasing is comparable to $c_k$; restrict closed-form comparisons and regression to $k\le n/2$.

The aliasing formula also bounds interpolation error by tail sums.
Each $T_k$ ($k>n$) becomes a lower-degree $\pm T_{k'}$ at samples, giving $|f-p_n|\le\sum_{k>n}|c_k|\,|T_k-(\pm T_{k'})|\le2S_n$ (when $T_k$ vanishes at sample points for degree $k$, only $|T_k|\le1$ remains).
ATAP Theorem 4.2 gives the same error-expansion form for second-kind nodes.
Fitting adds rounding; interpret coefficients below about $\varepsilon|c_0|$ as affected by rounding ($\varepsilon=2^{-52}$).

The following block defines helpers shared by two cells.
`Subject` represents the three functions; `value` corresponds to $f(x)$, `coefficient` to closed-form infinite-series coefficients $c_k$, and `tail` to tail sum $S_n$.
`bernstein_rho` returns $\rho$ of the ellipse through pole $i/5$.
Exponential coefficients are computed as $2I_k(1)$ with `bessel_i` from `scioxide-special`.
Closed forms are checks independent of fitting.

```rust
//| id: poly-chebyshev-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Three functions and closed-form infinite-series coefficients"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use std::f64::consts::PI;

/// a in Runge poles ±ia for 1/(1 + 25x²).
pub(crate) const RUNGE_POLE: f64 = 0.2;

/// Bernstein ellipse through ia: ρ = a + √(a² + 1).
pub(crate) fn bernstein_rho() -> f64 {
    RUNGE_POLE + RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 + RUNGE_POLE * RUNGE_POLE)
}

/// Three functions whose coefficient decay is compared.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, PartialEq)]
pub(crate) enum Subject {
    Runge,
    Exponential,
    Absolute,
}

impl Subject {
    /// Select a function from cell option strings.
    pub(crate) fn from_label(label: &str) -> Self {
        match label {
            "Runge function 1/(1+25x²)" => Self::Runge,
            "Exponential eˣ" => Self::Exponential,
            _ => Self::Absolute,
        }
    }

    /// Even functions have zero odd-degree coefficients.
    pub(crate) fn is_even(self) -> bool {
        self != Self::Exponential
    }

    /// f(x).
    pub(crate) fn value(self, x: f64) -> f64 {
        match self {
            Self::Runge => 1.0 / (1.0 + 25.0 * x * x),
            Self::Exponential => RealScalar::exp(x),
            Self::Absolute => x.abs(),
        }
    }

    /// Closed-form infinite-series c_k. Exponential uses 2 I_k(1) (I_0(1) at k = 0).
    pub(crate) fn coefficient(self, k: usize) -> Result<f64, String> {
        let scale = if k == 0 { 1.0 } else { 2.0 };
        let sign = if (k / 2) % 2 == 0 { 1.0 } else { -1.0 };
        let (a, s) = (RUNGE_POLE, RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 + RUNGE_POLE * RUNGE_POLE));
        Ok(match self {
            Self::Exponential => {
                scale * scioxide_special::bessel_i(k as f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            }
            _ if k % 2 == 1 => 0.0,
            Self::Runge => scale * a / s * sign * RealScalar::powi(bernstein_rho(), -(k as i32)),
            Self::Absolute if k == 0 => 2.0 / PI,
            Self::Absolute => -4.0 * sign / (PI * (k * k - 1) as f64),
        })
    }

    /// Tail sum S_n = Σ_{k>n} |c_k| (even n).
    pub(crate) fn tail(self, n: usize) -> Result<f64, String> {
        let (a, s) = (RUNGE_POLE, RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 + RUNGE_POLE * RUNGE_POLE));
        let rho = bernstein_rho();
        Ok(match self {
            // Geometric series Σ_{j≥0} (2a/s) ρ^{−(n+2+2j)} with ratio ρ⁻²
            Self::Runge => {
                2.0 * a / s * RealScalar::powi(rho, -((n + 2) as i32))
                    / (1.0 - RealScalar::powi(rho, -2))
            }
            // 2 I_k(1) through k = n + 30 suffices for binary64 precision
            Self::Exponential => (n + 1..=n + 30)
                .map(|k| self.coefficient(k))
                .sum::<Result<f64, String>>()?,
            // (4/π) Σ_{j>n/2} 1/(4j² − 1) = 2/(π(n + 1)) (telescoping partial fractions)
            Self::Absolute => 2.0 / (PI * (n + 1) as f64),
        })
    }
}
```

## Plotting coefficient decay

The next cell selects function and degree $n$, plotting sampled $|\tilde c_k|$ (symbols) and infinite-series $|c_k|$ (dashed) on semilog axes.
The dotted line is rounding level $\varepsilon|c_0|$.
For even functions, omit theoretically zero odd degrees.
Exactly zero sampled coefficients cannot be plotted logarithmically; omit them and count them in the next cell's table (none occurred in this input range).
The vertical axis is fixed at $[10^{-20},10]$; smaller closed-form values fall below the plot.
Pass coefficients and function selection to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: poly-chebyshev-coefficient-decay
//| ref: poly-chebyshev-coefficient-decay
//| title: "Analyticity and coefficient decay"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-chebyshev-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| exports: [function, degree, coefficients, rank, omitted]
//| inputs:
//|   function: {"type":"select","label":"Function","options":["Runge function 1/(1+25x²)","Exponential eˣ","Absolute value |x|"],"value":"Runge function 1/(1+25x²)"}
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree n [-]","description":"Change sample count n + 1 and fitted coefficient count.","min":16,"max":64,"step":8,"value":32}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"exponential","label":"eˣ","description":"Coefficients decay faster than geometrically, reaching rounding level near k ≈ 15.","inputs":{"function":"Exponential eˣ"}}
//|   - {"id":"absolute","label":"|x|","description":"Coefficients decrease algebraically as O(k⁻²), retaining aliasing even at k ≤ n/2.","inputs":{"function":"Absolute value |x|"}}

//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/spec/layers/1/points/10/1","approx":{"value":5.5059e-10,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: absolute
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/spec/layers/1/points/1/1","approx":{"value":0.42441318157838759,"atol":2e-3}}
//|   - case: runge-64
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coefficients","path":"/spec/layers/1/points/16/1","approx":{"value":6.796e-4,"rtol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_poly::{
    BasisNormalization, OrthogonalBasisSpec, OrthogonalFamily, PolynomialBasis,
    PolynomialFitPlan,
};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let subject = Subject::from_label(&function);
// Degree n is a multiple of 8 from 16 to 64 (and is therefore even).
let n = degree as usize;
// Take n + 1 samples at first-kind Chebyshev nodes (zeros of T_(n+1)) and fit degree-n coefficients.
let nodes: Vec<f64> = (0..=n)
    .map(|j| RealScalar::cos(PI * (j as f64 + 0.5) / (n + 1) as f64))
    .collect();
let values: Vec<f64> = nodes.iter().map(|&x| subject.value(x)).collect();
let spec = OrthogonalBasisSpec::new(
    OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevT,
    BasisNormalization::Classical,
    0.0_f64,
    1.0,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let basis = spec
    .prepare(n, Default::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plan = PolynomialFitPlan::orthogonal(&nodes, basis, None, Default::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fit = plan.fit(&values).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let coefficients = fit.polynomial().basis().coefficients().to_vec();

// Omit theoretically zero odd degrees for even functions; count exactly zero coefficients, which cannot be plotted logarithmically.
let mut fitted = Vec::new();
let mut reference = Vec::new();
let mut omitted = 0_usize;
for (k, &coefficient) in coefficients.iter().enumerate() {
    if subject.is_even() && k % 2 == 1 {
        continue;
    }
    if coefficient == 0.0 {
        omitted += 1;
    } else {
        fitted.push([k as f64, coefficient.abs()]);
    }
    // Independent check: closed-form infinite-series coefficients
    reference.push([k as f64, subject.coefficient(k)?.abs()]);
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,

        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 64.0,
        "yMin": 1e-20,
        "yMax": 10.0,
        "layers": [
            {
                "id": "series",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Infinite-series |c_k| (closed form)",
                "points": reference,
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
            },

        }]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "coefficients" })

export!(function, function);
export!(degree, n);

```

For default Runge ($n=32$), even-degree symbols nearly form a straight line along the dashed curve.
For $k\le n/2$, symbol-to-dashed ratios stay within 1.001, rising above it to 1.30 at $k=30$ and 1.67 at $k=32$.
Ratio 1.67 at $k=n$ matches leading-alias prediction $1+\rho^{-2}$.
Setting $n=64$ restores the same $k=32$ ratio to 1.000, shifting divergence to $k=62$ and $64$.
For $n=16$, ratio 1.06 even at $k=10$ puts aliasing inside the regression range.

Exponential coefficients reach rounding level $\varepsilon|c_0|\approx2.8\times10^{-16}$ at $k\approx15$, then only fluctuate around $10^{-16}$.
This plateau does not represent analyticity; do not infer decay from it.
Closed-form coefficients fall below plot minimum $10^{-20}$ at $k\ge18$ and leave the visible range.

Absolute-value symbols decay algebraically along the dashed curve but have ratio 1.08 even at $k=10$ and 2.3 at $k=n=32$, departing earlier than Runge.
Since coefficients decrease only as $O(k^{-2})$, aliased higher coefficients remain of the same order.

## Checking decay rates and truncation error

The next cell receives coefficients and tabulates three independent checks.
First, maximum discrepancy from closed form $c_k$ over $k\le n/2$.
Second, least-squares slopes over even $2\le k\le n/2$: use pairs $(k,\ln|\tilde c_k|)$ for Runge and $(\ln k,\ln|\tilde c_k|)$ for absolute value, comparing predictions $-\ln\rho$ and $-2$.
The exponential is not linear on this plot, so no slope is computed.
Third, compare maximum error $E_n$ from `ApproxPolynomial::orthogonal` evaluation of the fitted expansion on 801 grid points with tail sum $S_n$.
Theory gives $E_n/S_n\le2$.
If $S_n$ is below $10^3\varepsilon$, rounding determines $E_n$ and no ratio is shown.

```rust
//| id: poly-chebyshev-decay-checks
//| ref: poly-chebyshev-decay-checks
//| title: "Decay-rate regression and truncation-error checks"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-chebyshev-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| dependsOn:
//|   fit: poly-chebyshev-coefficient-decay
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":-0.19869011034924142,"atol":2e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":3e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0.5,"max":2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":33}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, OrthogonalBasisSpec, OrthogonalFamily};

let subject = Subject::from_label(fit["function"].as_str().ok_or("No function")?);
let n = fit["degree"].as_u64().ok_or("No degree")? as usize;
let coefficients: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(fit["coefficients"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Compare coefficients at k ≤ n/2 with closed forms and regress decay (higher degrees have stronger aliasing).
let mut deviation = 0.0_f64;
let mut regression = Vec::new();
for (k, &coefficient) in coefficients.iter().enumerate().take(n / 2 + 1) {
    deviation = deviation.max((coefficient - subject.coefficient(k)?).abs());
    if k >= 2 && !(subject.is_even() && k % 2 == 1) && coefficient != 0.0 {
        // Use ln|c̃_k| versus k for Runge and versus ln k for absolute value.
        let x = match subject {
            Subject::Absolute => RealScalar::ln(k as f64),
            _ => k as f64,
        };
        regression.push((x, RealScalar::ln(coefficient.abs())));
    }
}
// Least-squares slope. Exponential decay is faster than geometric and not a straight line, so no slope is computed.
let m = regression.len() as f64;
let (mx, my) = regression
    .iter()
    .fold((0.0, 0.0), |(sx, sy), &(x, y)| (sx + x / m, sy + y / m));
let (sxy, sxx) = regression.iter().fold((0.0, 0.0), |(sxy, sxx), &(x, y)| {
    (sxy + (x - mx) * (y - my), sxx + (x - mx) * (x - mx))
});
let (slope, predicted) = match subject {
    Subject::Runge => (Some(sxy / sxx), Some(-RealScalar::ln(bernstein_rho()))),
    Subject::Exponential => (None, None),
    Subject::Absolute => (Some(sxy / sxx), Some(-2.0)),
};

// Evaluate the fitted expansion on 801 points and measure maximum function error.
let spec =
    OrthogonalBasisSpec::classical(OrthogonalFamily::ChebyshevT).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let expansion = ApproxPolynomial::orthogonal(coefficients, spec).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut max_error = 0.0_f64;
for j in 0..=800 {
    let x = -1.0 + j as f64 / 400.0;
    let value = expansion.evaluate(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    max_error = max_error.max((value - subject.value(x)).abs());
}
let tail = subject.tail(n)?;
// When the tail sum is below rounding level, rounding sets maximum error and no ratio is shown.
let ratio = (tail > 1e3 * f64::EPSILON).then(|| max_error / tail);

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Regression slope [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "Not applicable" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Predicted slope [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed-form discrepancy at k ≤ n/2 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Grid maximum error E_n [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Tail sum S_n [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "E_n/S_n [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2, "nullText": "Rounding level" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Sample-matrix rank", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Zero coefficients omitted from logarithmic axes", "type": "integer" }
]);
let row = (
    slope,
    predicted,
    deviation,
    max_error,
    tail,
    ratio,
    fit["rank"].as_u64().ok_or("No rank")?,
    fit["omitted"].as_u64().ok_or("No zero-coefficient count")?,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
```

For default Runge ($n=32$), fitted slope $-0.1986$ agrees with prediction $-\ln\rho=-0.1987$ to 3 digits.
Maximum discrepancy $1.9\times10^{-5}$ over $k\le n/2$ has the same magnitude as leading alias term $|c_{2(n+1)-k}|=|c_{50}|\approx1.9\times10^{-5}$ ($k=16$).
Grid maximum $E_{32}=1.4\times10^{-3}$ nearly equals tail sum $S_{32}=1.4\times10^{-3}$ (ratio 1.01) and lies within bound $2S_n$.
At $n=64$, $E_{64}=2.5\times10^{-6}$ and $S_{64}=2.4\times10^{-6}$; slope $-0.19869007$ matches prediction within relative discrepancy $2\times10^{-7}$.
For $n=16$, the short regression range also contains aliasing, giving slope $-0.195$.
Each degree increase of 32 multiplies error by $\rho^{-32}\approx1/580$.

For the exponential, closed-form discrepancy over $n=32$ is $1.8\times10^{-15}$ and grid maximum error $5.3\times10^{-15}$, both at rounding level.
Tail sum $S_{32}=2.7\times10^{-47}$ makes all error rounding-related, so no ratio is displayed.
At $n=64$, maximum error increases to $1.6\times10^{-14}$.
Excess degree only adds fitting and evaluation rounding.

For absolute value, fitted slopes are $-2.03$ at $n=32$ and $-2.0004$ at $n=64$.
Aliasing has the same $O(k^{-2})$ magnitude as coefficients, leaving closed-form discrepancy $1.1\times10^{-3}$ ($n=32$) even at $k\le n/2$.
Grid maxima are $1.8\times10^{-2}$ at $n=32$ and $9.2\times10^{-3}$ at $n=64$, following $S_n=2/(\pi(n+1))$ at ratio 0.94 and halving at $O(1/n)$.
Before mechanically discarding small coefficients, assess rounding level, aliasing, and required value accuracy.

## Summary

Chebyshev decay depends on complex singularity locations as well as real smoothness; $\rho$ of the nearest-singularity Bernstein ellipse determines geometric rate $\rho^{-k}$.
Singularity-free functions decay faster than geometrically; functions with discontinuous first derivatives decrease algebraically as $O(k^{-2})$.
Interpolation error is at most twice tail sum $S_n=\sum_{k>n}|c_k|$; for Runge and $|x|$ it is actually close in magnitude to $S_n$.
Sampled coefficients add aliasing and rounding to infinite-series coefficients; infer decay from $k\le n/2$ and coefficients above rounding level $\varepsilon|c_0|$.
Increasing degree after coefficients reach rounding level does not reduce error and only adds rounding.
