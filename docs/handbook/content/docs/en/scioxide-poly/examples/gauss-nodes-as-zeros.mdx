The $n$th excited state of a one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator uses mass $m$, angular frequency $\omega$, length scale $\ell=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$, and dimensionless position $\xi=x/\ell$ to give:

$$
\psi_n(\xi)=\bigl(2^nn!\sqrt\pi\bigr)^{-1/2}H_n(\xi)\,e^{-\xi^2/2}
$$

Since $e^{-\xi^2/2}$ never vanishes, locations where probability density $|\psi_n|^2$ is zero (wavefunction nodes) are exactly the physicists' Hermite polynomial $H_n$ zeros.
The same zeros are nodes of $n$-point Gauss–Hermite quadrature with weight $e^{-\xi^2}$.
This page covers only zeros and the three-term recurrence of $H_n$.
Expectations and Gauss–Hermite exactness degree are covered in [Quantum harmonic-oscillator expectations in integrate](../../../scioxide-integrate/examples/quantum-oscillator-expectations/) and [Gaussian quadrature and Clenshaw–Curtis rules](../../../scioxide-integrate/algorithms/gaussian-and-clenshaw-curtis/).

As a concrete example, approximate stretching vibrations of carbon monoxide ¹²C¹⁶O by a harmonic oscillator.
Reduced mass $\mu=6.8562$ u and harmonic frequency $\tilde\nu=2169.81$ cm⁻¹ ($\omega=2\pi c\tilde\nu$) give length scale $\ell=\sqrt{\hbar/(\mu\omega)}\approx4.761$ pm.
Node locations $x=\ell\,r_j$ are displacements from equilibrium internuclear separation 112.8 pm.
Actual CO vibrations are anharmonic, so this conversion is a guide for low levels.

## From three-term recurrences to matrices

The physicists' convention is $H_0=1$, $H_1=2\xi$,

$$
H_{k+1}=2\xi H_k-2kH_{k-1}
$$

with orthogonality weight $e^{-\xi^2}$.
Taking $a_k=2$, $b_k=0$, and $c_k=2k$ in recurrence $p_{k+1}=(a_kx+b_k)p_k-c_kp_{k-1}$ from [Orthogonal polynomial families](oxref:poly-jacobi-matrix-reduction) represents multiplication by $\xi$ in the orthonormal basis as symmetric tridiagonal $\mathbf J$ with diagonal $d_k=0$ and subdiagonal $e_k=\sqrt{c_k/(a_{k-1}a_k)}=\sqrt{k/2}$.
Eigenvalues of its leading $n\times n$ principal submatrix $\mathbf J_n$ are zeros of $H_n$ (Golub–Welsch method).
`OrthogonalFamily::jacobi_recurrence(n)` returns owned diagonal and subdiagonal entries without requiring monomial expansion.
`OrthogonalFamily::HermiteH.zeros(n)` computes eigenvalues of the same matrix using `scioxide-linalg`'s symmetric tridiagonal eigensolver, returning them in ascending order.
Computing eigenvalues alone costs $O(n^2)$.

Because $\mathbf J_n$ is real symmetric with positive subdiagonal entries, all eigenvalues are real and simple.
Since $\mathbf J_n$ is a principal submatrix of $\mathbf J_{n+1}$, Cauchy interlacing gives $r_{n+1,j}\le r_{n,j}\le r_{n+1,j+1}$ for ascending zeros $r_{n,j}$ and $r_{n+1,j}$ of degrees $n$ and $n+1$.
Equality cannot hold.
If $H_n$ and $H_{n+1}$ shared a zero, following the recurrence downward would make $H_0=1$ zero too.
Consequently, the zeros strictly interlace:

$$
r_{n+1,j}<r_{n,j}<r_{n+1,j+1}\qquad(j=1,\dots,n)
$$

Computing zeros of both degrees independently checks this numerically.
Physically, each excitation adds one node, and nodes of adjacent states interlace.

## Sums, sum of squaress, and conventions

Eigenvalues sum to the trace.
The diagonal of $\mathbf J_n$ is zero, giving $\sum_jr_j=0$.
Symmetry of zeros about the origin also follows from parity $H_n(-\xi)=(-1)^nH_n(\xi)$.
The sum of squares is $\mathrm{tr}\,\mathbf J_n^2$, counting every squared subdiagonal entry twice, so:

$$
\sum_jr_j^2=2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}e_k^2=2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac k2=\frac{n(n-1)}2
$$

Under the probabilists' convention $He_{k+1}=xHe_k-kHe_{k-1}$ (weight $e^{-x^2/2}$), subdiagonal entries become $\sqrt k$ and the sum of squares becomes $n(n-1)$.
The relation $H_n(\xi)=2^{n/2}He_n(\sqrt2\,\xi)$ means zeros of $H_n$ are $1/\sqrt2$ times zeros of $He_n$.
Confusing conventions moves all zeros by a factor $\sqrt2$ or $1/\sqrt2$.
At $n=2$, zeros $\pm1/\sqrt2$ of $H_2=4\xi^2-2$ and sum of squares 1 verify both formulas.

## Computing zeros and identities

The following cell computes degree-$n$ and degree-$n+1$ zeros with `OrthogonalFamily::HermiteH.zeros`.
The input is degree $n$ (2–20), and coordinates $\xi$ are dimensionless.
The first table shows each zero, its neighboring degree-$n+1$ zeros, CO node positions $\ell r_j$ [pm], and Newton corrections $|H_n/H_n'|$.
Values of $H_n$ grow rapidly with degree; instead of residuals at zeros, inspect Newton corrections, first-order estimates of distance to a zero.
The second table gives two independent checks.

- The trace identities above (sum 0 and sum of squares $n(n-1)/2$), and $2\sum e_k^2$ computed from `JacobiRecurrence`'s subdiagonal.
- Interlacing with adjacent degrees.
  The minimum interlacing gap is $\min_j\min(r_{n,j}-r_{n+1,j},\ r_{n+1,j+1}-r_{n,j})$; positivity establishes interlacing.

The chart places zeros of both degrees horizontally, using degree as the vertical axis.
The cell passes the degree and zeros to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: poly-hermite-gauss-zeros
//| ref: poly-hermite-gauss-zeros
//| title: "Hermite zeros and interlacing of adjacent degrees"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| exports: [degree, roots]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Degree n [-]","description":"Changes the zero count (vibrational quantum number).","min":2,"max":20,"step":1,"value":8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"degree-2","label":"n = 2","description":"Zeros have closed form ±1/√2 and sum of squares 1.","inputs":{"degree":2}}
//|   - {"id":"degree-20","label":"n = 20","description":"As zero counts rise, sum, sum-of-squares, and interlacing checks still hold to rounding level.","inputs":{"degree":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/7/2","approx":{"value":2.930637420257244,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.2603557815242863,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: degree-2
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.7071067811865476,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-14}}
//|   - case: degree-20
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"zeros","path":"/rows/19/2","approx":{"value":5.387480890011233,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identities","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.16287098325344118,"rtol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_poly::OrthogonalFamily;

let n = degree as usize;
let family = OrthogonalFamily::<f64>::HermiteH;
// Zeros as eigenvalues of Jacobi matrices J_n and J_(n+1) (ascending)
let roots = family.zeros(n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let next = family.zeros(n + 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let recurrence = family.jacobi_recurrence(n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// CO stretching length scale ℓ = √(ħ/(μω)) [pm]. μ = 6.8562 u, ω = 2πc·2169.81 cm⁻¹.
let hbar = 1.054_571_817e-34;
let mu = 6.8562 * 1.660_539_067e-27;
let omega = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * 2.997_924_58e10 * 2169.81;
let length = RealScalar::sqrt(hbar / (mu * omega)) * 1e12;

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (j, &x) in roots.iter().enumerate() {
    let value = family.evaluate(n, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let derivative = family
        .evaluate_derivative(n, x)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Newton correction |H_n/H_n′| estimates distance to a zero to first order
    let correction = (value / derivative).abs();
    rows.push((j + 1, next[j], x, next[j + 1], x * length, correction));
}
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Index j", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Left degree-n+1 zero [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Degree-n zero r_j [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Right degree-n+1 zero [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "CO node position [pm]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "|H_n/H_n′| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "zeros" })
);

// Trace-identity and interlacing checks
let sum = roots.iter().sum::<f64>();
let squares = roots.iter().map(|x| x * x).sum::<f64>();
let trace = 2.0 * recurrence.off_diagonal().iter().map(|e| e * e).sum::<f64>();
let expected = (n * (n - 1)) as f64 / 2.0;
let gap = roots
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, &x)| (x - next[j]).min(next[j + 1] - x))
    .fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
let checks = vec![
    ("Zero sum Σr_j", sum, Some(0.0)),
    ("Sum of squared zeros Σr_j²", squares, Some(expected)),
    ("2Σe_k² from subdiagonal", trace, Some(expected)),
    ("Minimum interlacing gap", gap, None),
];
let rows: Vec<_> = checks
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, value, exact)| (name, value, exact, exact.map(|e| (value - e).abs())))
    .collect();
let general = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 15 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Computed [-]", "type": "number", "format": general },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Analytic [-]", "type": "number", "format": general, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "identities" })
);

let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = roots.iter().map(|&x| [x, n as f64]).collect();
let next_points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = next.iter().map(|&x| [x, (n + 1) as f64]).collect();
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "H_n zeros", "points": points }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "H_(n+1) zeros", "points": next_points })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Zero ξ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Degree [-]",
        "xMin": -7.0,
        "xMax": 7.0,
        "yMin": 1.0,
        "yMax": 22.0
    })
);
export!(degree, n);
export!(roots, roots);
```

At default $n=8$, the zero sum is $-1.8\times10^{-15}$ and the sum of squares differs from analytic value 28 by less than $10^{-13}$.
Subdiagonal-derived $2\sum e_k^2$ is also 28, checking the trace identity from matrix data.
The minimum interlacing gap is positive at 0.26, so every displayed zero lies between adjacent-degree zeros.
Against the eight-point Gauss–Hermite nodes in Abramowitz–Stegun Table 25.10 (15 decimal digits), the near-central zero 0.381187 agrees to table precision; the largest zero 2.930637 differs from tabulated 2.930637420257244 by $5\times10^{-15}$.
Newton corrections $|H_n/H_n'|$ are at most $5\times10^{-15}$, indicating rounding-level zero errors.
CO nodes lie within $\pm13.95$ pm of equilibrium, inside classical turning points $\pm\ell\sqrt{2n+1}=\pm19.6$ pm.

At $n=2$, zeros $\pm0.707107$ agree with closed form $\pm1/\sqrt2$, with sum of squares 1.
At $n=20$, the sum of squares differs from 190 by $1.7\times10^{-13}$ and the minimum interlacing gap is 0.16.
The largest zero is 5.39 (25.6 pm for CO); wavefunction nodes remain inside classically allowed region $|\xi|<\sqrt{2n+1}\approx6.40$.
Neighboring zeros are closest near the center (0.49 at $n=20$) and spread toward the edges (0.78).

## Checks using another algorithm and convention

The following cell receives the previous cell's zeros and recomputes them in two other ways.

- Zeros from another algorithm.
  Represent $H_n$ as a Hermite expansion with coefficients $(0,\dots,0,1)$, construct a comrade matrix (a companion matrix in an orthogonal basis) with `root_matrix()`, and solve using general eigenvalue computation `GeneralEigenvaluesResult::compute` in `scioxide-linalg`.
  The comrade matrix is nonsymmetric, so eigenvalues return as complex numbers.
- Zeros of probabilists' $He_n$ divided by $\sqrt2$.

The first table gives discrepancies per zero; the second gives maximum discrepancies and maximum comrade-eigenvalue imaginary part.

```rust
//| id: poly-hermite-zero-crosscheck
//| ref: poly-hermite-zero-crosscheck
//| title: "Check zeros with comrade matrices and the probabilists' convention"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-linalg, scioxide-poly]
//| dependsOn:
//|   zeros: poly-hermite-gauss-zeros
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_linalg::GeneralEigenvaluesResult;
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, OrthogonalBasisSpec, OrthogonalFamily};
use std::f64::consts::SQRT_2;

let n = zeros["degree"].as_u64().ok_or("Missing degree")? as usize;
let roots: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(zeros["roots"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Alternative algorithm: use Hermite-expansion coefficients (0, …, 0, 1) for H_n and solve its comrade matrix with general eigendecomposition.
let family = OrthogonalFamily::<f64>::HermiteH;
let spec = OrthogonalBasisSpec::classical(family).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut unit = vec![0.0_f64; n + 1];
unit[n] = 1.0;
let hermite = ApproxPolynomial::orthogonal(unit, spec).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let matrix = hermite.root_matrix().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let eigen = GeneralEigenvaluesResult::compute(&matrix).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let imaginary = eigen
    .eigenvalues()
    .iter()
    .map(|z| z.im.abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let mut comrade: Vec<f64> = eigen.eigenvalues().iter().map(|z| z.re).collect();
comrade.sort_by(f64::total_cmp);

// Alternative convention: divide probabilists' He_n zeros by √2 to obtain H_n zeros.
let probabilists = OrthogonalFamily::<f64>::HermiteHe
    .zeros(n)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rows: Vec<(usize, f64, f64, f64)> = roots
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, &x)| (j + 1, x, comrade[j] - x, probabilists[j] / SQRT_2 - x))
    .collect();
let max_gap = |column: fn(&(usize, f64, f64, f64)) -> f64| {
    rows.iter()
        .map(column)
        .map(f64::abs)
        .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max)
};
let summary = vec![(max_gap(|row| row.2), imaginary, max_gap(|row| row.3))];

let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Index j", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Zero r_j (Jacobi matrix) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Comrade matrix − r_j [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "He_n zero/√2 − r_j [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "comparison" })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum comrade discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum comrade-eigenvalue imaginary part [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum discrepancy from He_n zeros/√2 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    summary,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
```

At default $n=8$, all comrade eigenvalues are real (maximum imaginary part 0), differing from `zeros` values by at most $7.5\times10^{-15}$.
Discrepancies from $He_n$ zeros divided by $\sqrt2$ are also at most $6.7\times10^{-15}$, numerically confirming the conventions' scale relationship.
These are finite-precision numerical agreements, not exact enclosures of zeros.

## Summary

Hermite zeros are eigenvalues of symmetric tridiagonal Jacobi matrices built from three-term recurrences, and locations of quantum harmonic-oscillator wavefunction nodes.
Sums and sum of squaress follow from traces, interlacing from principal-submatrix eigenvalue properties; each provides an independent check.
Comrade matrices and probabilists' conventions recompute the same zeros through another algorithm and another convention.
The physicists' and probabilists' conventions give zero scales differing by a factor $\sqrt2$ and sums of squared zeros $n(n-1)/2$ and $n(n-1)$; check which convention is used before using them as quadrature nodes or in expectations.
To restore physical lengths, multiply dimensionless zeros by $\ell=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$.
