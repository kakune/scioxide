Exponential $e^x$ has no finite poles, but rational approximation introduces poles at denominator zeros.
These belong to the approximation rather than the original function; their locations also relate to time-integration stability functions.
Construct Padé approximations of $e^x$ from closed-form coefficients, evaluate through `ApproxRationalFunction`, and examine leading errors near the origin separately from pole locations.

## Matching at the origin and closed forms

Among rational functions $R_{m,n} = P_m/Q_n$ ($Q_n(0) = 1$) with numerator degree $m$ and denominator degree $n$, those satisfying

$$
Q_n(x)e^x - P_m(x) = O(x^{m+n+1})
$$

are called $[m/n]$ Padé approximants.
Unknowns are $m+1$ coefficients of $P_m$ and $n$ of $Q_n$, determined by $m+n+1$ linear equations zeroing coefficients of $x^0, \dots, x^{m+n}$.
For the exponential, these equations have closed-form solutions.

$$
P_m(x) = \sum_{k=0}^m \frac{(m+n-k)!\,m!}{(m+n)!\,(m-k)!\,k!} x^k,
\qquad
Q_n(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n \frac{(m+n-k)!\,n!}{(m+n)!\,(n-k)!\,k!} (-x)^k
$$

If $n = 0$, $P_m$ is a Taylor polynomial of $e^x$; if $m = 0$, $Q_n$ is a Taylor polynomial of $e^{-x}$ and $R_{0,n} = 1/Q_n$ its reciprocal.
$[1/1] = (1 + x/2)/(1 - x/2)$ is the trapezoidal stability function and $[0/1] = 1/(1-x)$ backward Euler's ([Error, order, and stability](../../../scioxide-ode/algorithms/error-order-and-stability/)).

`scioxide-poly` does not construct Padé approximations from Taylor coefficients.
This page supplies closed-form coefficients to `ApproxRationalFunction` for evaluation only.
For constructing $[m/n]$ from Taylor coefficients, use `PadeApproximant` in [interp's Padé approximations](oxref:interp-pade); for sample-based rational approximation, use AAA in [interp's rational approximation](../../../scioxide-interp/algorithms/rational-approximation/).

The following definitions block implements the closed forms as Rust functions shared by two cells.
`pade_coefficients(m, n)` returns ascending-power coefficients of $P_m$ and $Q_n$; $Q_n$ includes signs from expanding $(-x)^k$.
Input range $m,n\le5$ uses factorials only through $11!$, exactly representable in `f64`, so coefficient errors come only from division rounding.

```rust
//| id: poly-pade-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Closed-form coefficients for exponential Padé approximation"
//| crates: []
/// Return k! using f64 products. Factorials through 22! are exactly representable.
pub(crate) fn factorial(k: usize) -> f64 {
    (1..=k).map(|j| j as f64).product()
}

/// Ascending coefficients of numerator P_m and denominator Q_n of the [m/n] approximant to e^x, with Q_n(0) = 1.
pub(crate) fn pade_coefficients(m: usize, n: usize) -> (Vec<f64>, Vec<f64>) {
    // (m+n−k)! d! / ((m+n)! (d−k)! k!). d is m for the numerator and n for the denominator.
    let coefficient = |degree: usize, k: usize| {
        factorial(m + n - k) * factorial(degree)
            / (factorial(m + n) * factorial(degree - k) * factorial(k))
    };
    let numerator = (0..=m).map(|k| coefficient(m, k)).collect();
    // Q_n sums (−x)^k, so reverse signs of odd-degree coefficients.
    let denominator = (0..=n)
        .map(|k| if k % 2 == 0 { 1.0 } else { -1.0 } * coefficient(n, k))
        .collect();
    (numerator, denominator)
}
```

## Leading error and its sign

Write the first nonzero term on the left-hand side of the matching condition as $C_{m,n}x^{m+n+1}$; since $Q_n(0)=1$,

$$
e^x - R_{m,n}(x) = \frac{Q_n(x)e^x - P_m(x)}{Q_n(x)} = C_{m,n}\,x^{m+n+1} + O(x^{m+n+2})
$$


The exponential's closed-form coefficients give this leading error near the origin.

$$
e^x - R_{m,n}(x) = \frac{(-1)^n\, m!\, n!}{(m+n)!\,(m+n+1)!}\, x^{m+n+1} + O(x^{m+n+2})
$$

For $n = 0$, it equals the Taylor remainder's leading term $x^{m+1}/(m+1)!$.
Defining error as $R - e^x$ reverses the sign; keep orientation consistent in comparisons.
The leading term is asymptotic as $x \to 0$, not a bound at distant points.
Large $|x|$ brings higher-order effects; overly small $|x|$ hides differences in evaluation rounding.

## Checking the leading error term

The next cell evaluates $[m/n]$ at $x = -2^{-j}$ ($j = 0, \dots, 12$), comparing $e^x - R(x)$ with the leading term.
On the negative real axis, every term $c_k(-x)^k$ of $Q_n$ is positive, giving $Q_n > 0$; no $[m/n]$ has a pole there.
Inputs are numerator and denominator degrees from zero to five.
Reference $e^x$ uses `f64` intrinsic `exp` as a check independent of SciOxide evaluation.
`ApproximateRationalEvaluation` returned by `evaluate_parts` contains value and numerator/denominator evaluation conditioning reports.
Sum their relative forward-error estimates (first-order rounding-model $\gamma_k\kappa$ estimates, not bounds) and multiply by $|R|$ for the table's evaluation-error indicator; observed differences below it are treated as rounding-level.
Exactly zero differences have no ratio, appear as “Zero error,” and are omitted from logarithmic plots.

```rust
//| id: poly-pade-exponential
//| ref: poly-pade-exponential
//| title: "Padé errors and leading terms"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-pade-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   numerator_degree: {"type":"range","label":"Numerator degree m [-]","min":0,"max":5,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   denominator_degree: {"type":"range","label":"Denominator degree n [-]","min":0,"max":5,"step":1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"taylor","label":"[4/0] (Taylor polynomial)","description":"Error follows leading Taylor remainder x⁵/5!, with positive ratio approaching 1.","inputs":{"numerator_degree":4,"denominator_degree":0}}
//|   - {"id":"diagonal-5","label":"[5/5]","description":"The leading term is around 10⁻¹⁰ even at x = −1; most points with |x| ≤ 1/4 reach rounding level.","inputs":{"numerator_degree":5,"denominator_degree":5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"leading","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1.3888888888888889e-3,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.39,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/3","approx":{"value":0.992,"atol":2e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/12/3","equals":null}
//|   - case: taylor
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"leading","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":8.333333333333333e-3,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/7/3","approx":{"value":0.998,"atol":2e-3}}
//|   - case: diagonal-5
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":9.94e-11,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.377,"atol":0.01}}
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, ApproxRationalFunction};

let (m, n) = (numerator_degree as usize, denominator_degree as usize);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let (p, q) = pade_coefficients(m, n);
let numerator = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let denominator = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rational =
    ApproxRationalFunction::new(numerator, denominator).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Leading coefficient (−1)^n m! n! / ((m+n)! (m+n+1)!)
let sign = if n % 2 == 0 { 1.0 } else { -1.0 };
let leading = sign * factorial(m) * factorial(n) / (factorial(m + n) * factorial(m + n + 1));

let (mut rows, mut observed, mut predicted, mut floor) =
    (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for j in 0..=12 {
    let x = -1.0 / (1_u32 << j) as f64;
    let evaluation = rational.evaluate_parts(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let value = evaluation.value();
    // Evaluation-error indicator: sum numerator/denominator relative forward-error estimates and multiply by |R|
    let numerator_error = evaluation.numerator().condition().forward_error_estimate();
    let denominator_error = evaluation
        .denominator()
        .condition()
        .forward_error_estimate();
    let estimate = (numerator_error + denominator_error) * value.abs();
    // Independent check: reference f64 exp intrinsic (without SciOxide), closed-form leading term
    let error = x.exp() - value;
    let term = leading * x.powi((m + n + 1) as i32);
    // Zero errors have no defined ratio.
    let ratio = (error != 0.0).then_some(error / term);
    rows.push((x, error, term, ratio, estimate));
    // Omit zero-error points from logarithmic plots and count them in the table.
    if error != 0.0 {
        observed.push([-x, error.abs()]);
    }
    predicted.push([-x, term.abs()]);
    floor.push([-x, estimate]);
}
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "x [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "1", "label": "e^x − R(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Leading term [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Ratio [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4, "nullText": "Zero error" },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Evaluation-error indicator [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Leading coefficient [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Degree m+n+1", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Zero-error points omitted from plot", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    vec![(leading, m + n + 1, 13 - observed.len())],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "leading" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "|e^x − R(x)|",
            "points": observed,
            "style": { "symbol": "circle", "showSymbol": true }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Leading-term magnitude",
            "points": predicted,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Evaluation-error indicator",
            "points": floor,
            "style": { "lineType": "dotted" }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Distance from origin |x| (x < 0) [-]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
```

Default $[2/2]$ has leading-error coefficient $1/720 \approx 1.39 \times 10^{-3}$; ratios approach 1 from $0.39$ at $x = -1$ to $0.992$ at $x = -2^{-7}$.
Deviations from 1 ($0.61$, $0.38$, $0.22$, $0.12$, …) nearly halve with each halving of $|x|$, reflecting the next $O(x^{m+n+2})$ term.
The evaluation-error indicator is about $1.3 \times 10^{-15}$; at $x = -2^{-8}$, observed difference $1.2 \times 10^{-15}$ falls below it and ratio $0.967$ departs from 1.
At the 4 points nearer the origin than $x = -2^{-9}$, differences are 0; the remaining region is at rounding level.
Observed points follow the dashed slope-$m+n+1 = 5$ line, departing near the dotted rounding level.
Leading signs are the product of signs of $(-1)^n$ and $x^{m+n+1}$; above rounding level, observed differences share that sign, so ratios stay positive and approach 1 for all $m, n$.

$[4/0]$ is a Taylor polynomial with leading remainder $x^5/5!$; ratios approach 1 from $0.85$ at $x=-1$ to $0.999$ at $x=-2^{-7}$.
For large $m+n$, as in $[5/5]$, the leading term is only $9.9 \times 10^{-11}$ even at $x = -1$ and ratio remains $0.38$; the leading term alone cannot describe error.
At $x = -1/4$, the difference is already 0; nearer the origin, ratios are undefined or exceed $10^{3}$ as rounding-level differences divide by tiny leading terms.

## Pole locations and magnitudes on the imaginary axis

### Pole half-planes and Vieta relations

Poles of $R_{m,n}$ are zeros of $Q_n$.
Saff and Varga (*Numer. Math.* 25, 1975) showed all poles of the $[m/n]$ Padé approximant to $e^z$ lie in half-plane $\operatorname{Re} z > m - n$.
The second cell checks this bound over inputs $m \le 5$ and $1 \le n \le 5$.
For $m \ge n$, all poles lie in the right half-plane; $[1/1]$ has pole $z = 2$ and $[2/2]$ poles $3 \pm i\sqrt{3}$.
For $m < n$, poles can enter the left half-plane; $[0/5]$ has a conjugate pair with real part $-0.24$.
Padé approximation of $e^{-x}$ replaces $x$ by $-x$, reflecting poles into half-plane $\operatorname{Re} z < n - m$.
Statements that exponential-approximation poles lie in the left half-plane refer to this $e^{-x}$ case; distinguish it from $e^x$.

Closed-form coefficients also give pole sums and products.
Substitute ratios of $x^n$, $x^{n-1}$, and constant coefficients of $Q_n$ into Vieta relations to obtain

$$
\sum_{i} z_i = (m+1)\,n, \qquad \prod_i z_i = \frac{(m+n)!}{m!}
$$

These provide checks independent of eigenvalue computation.

### Diagonal approximation and A-stability

The diagonal approximant with $m = n$ satisfies $P_n(z) = Q_n(-z)$.
Real coefficients give $Q_n(-iy) = \overline{Q_n(iy)}$ at $z = iy$; with nonzero denominator, $|R_{n,n}(iy)| = 1$.
This means magnitude exactly 1 on the imaginary axis, not equality of phase $\arg R_{n,n}(iy)$ with $y$.

In time integration, using $R$ as a stability function, A-stability means $|R(z)| \le 1$ throughout the left half-plane.
$[m/n]$ is A-stable exactly when $m \le n \le m + 2$ (Ehle's conjecture, proved by Wanner, Hairer, and Nørsett in 1978).
For $n \ge m + 3$, even $[0/3]$ with all poles in the right half-plane has $|R(iy)|^2 = 1/(1 - y^4/12 + y^6/36)$ exceeding 1 at small $y$, giving $|R(iy)| > 1$ on the imaginary axis.

The next cell computes poles as eigenvalues of a matrix whose eigenvalues are denominator roots, checking Vieta relations and half-plane $\operatorname{Re} z > m - n$.
Matrix construction is covered in [Matrices with roots as eigenvalues and root sensitivity](../../algorithms/root-conditioning/); scioxide-linalg computes eigenvalues.
`ApproxRationalFunction::new` divides numerator and denominator by the same value to make the denominator monic; the matrix from `denominator()` thus uses $Q_n$ divided by its leading coefficient, preserving roots.
“Imaginary part” records imaginary components left by eigenvalue computation in sums and products that should be real.
Evaluate $|R(iy)|$ on the imaginary axis at $y = 0, 0.5, 1, 2$.
A constant denominator ($n = 0$) has no poles; choose denominator degree at least 1.
Nondiagonal approximants need not have unit imaginary-axis magnitude.

```rust
//| id: poly-pade-poles
//| ref: poly-pade-poles
//| title: "Padé poles and magnitudes on the imaginary axis"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-pade-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   numerator_degree: {"type":"range","label":"Numerator degree m [-]","min":0,"max":5,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   denominator_degree: {"type":"range","label":"Denominator degree n [-]","min":1,"max":5,"step":1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"order-0-3","label":"[0/3]","description":"All poles lie in the right half-plane, but n ≥ m + 3 makes |R(iy)| exceed 1 at small y.","inputs":{"numerator_degree":0,"denominator_degree":3}}
//|   - {"id":"order-0-5","label":"[0/5]","description":"A negative-real-part pole pair appears, inside bound Re z > m − n = −5.","inputs":{"numerator_degree":0,"denominator_degree":5}}
//|   - {"id":"diagonal-5","label":"[5/5]","description":"Poles lie in the right half-plane; Vieta relations hold within relative discrepancy around 10⁻¹⁵.","inputs":{"numerator_degree":5,"denominator_degree":5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"vieta","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":6.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"vieta","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":12.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"vieta","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":3.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"axis","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":0.0,"atol":1e-14}}
//|   - case: order-0-3
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"axis","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.00240,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"axis","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.83205,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"vieta","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":0.0}}
//|   - case: order-0-5
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"vieta","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":-5.0,"max":0.0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"vieta","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":120.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: diagonal-5
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"vieta","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":30.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"vieta","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":30240.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"axis","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":0.0,"atol":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_linalg::GeneralEigenvaluesResult;
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, ApproxRationalFunction};

let (m, n) = (numerator_degree as usize, denominator_degree as usize);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let digits6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
// Use complex coefficients for evaluation on the imaginary axis
let (p, q) = pade_coefficients(m, n);
let complex = |values: Vec<f64>| values.into_iter().map(|c| Complex::new(c, 0.0)).collect();
let numerator = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(complex(p)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let denominator = ApproxPolynomial::monomial(complex(q)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rational =
    ApproxRationalFunction::new(numerator, denominator).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Poles = eigenvalues of the denominator-root matrix (denominator made monic by new)
let matrix = rational
    .denominator()
    .root_matrix()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let poles = GeneralEigenvaluesResult::compute(&matrix)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .eigenvalues()
    .to_vec();
let sum = poles.iter().fold(Complex::new(0.0, 0.0), |s, &z| s + z);
let product = poles.iter().fold(Complex::new(1.0, 0.0), |s, &z| s * z);
let minimum_real = poles.iter().map(|z| z.re).fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
// Independent check: closed-form Vieta relations and the Saff–Varga bound
let rows = vec![
    ("Pole count", poles.len() as f64, n as f64, None),
    ("Pole sum (m+1)n", sum.re, ((m + 1) * n) as f64, Some(sum.im)),
    (
        "Pole product (m+n)!/m!",
        product.re,
        factorial(m + n) / factorial(m),
        Some(product.im),
    ),
    (
        "Minimum pole real part (greater than bound m − n)",
        minimum_real,
        m as f64 - n as f64,
        None,
    ),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "From eigenvalues [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form / bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Imaginary part [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "vieta" })
);
let mut axis = Vec::new();
for y in [0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0] {
    let value = rational
        .evaluate(Complex::new(0.0, y))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    axis.push((y, value.norm(), value.norm() - 1.0));
}
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Imaginary-axis y [-]", "type": "number" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "|R(iy)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 },
        { "key": "2", "label": "|R(iy)| − 1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
    ]),
    axis,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "axis" })
);
let pole_points: Vec<_> = poles.iter().map(|z| [z.re, z.im]).collect();
emit_scatter_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Poles (denominator zeros)", "points": pole_points })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Pole real part [-]",
        "yLabel": "Pole imaginary part [-]",
        "xMin": -6.0,
        "xMax": 8.0,
        "yMin": -8.0,
        "yMax": 8.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": 0.0, "label": "Imaginary axis" },
            {
                "kind": "line",
                "axis": "x",
                "value": m as f64 - n as f64,
                "label": "Re z = m − n (Saff–Varga bound)",
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
            }
        ]
    })
);
```

Default $[2/2]$ has poles $3 \pm 1.732i$; sum 6 and product 12 agree with closed forms within relative discrepancy $3 \times 10^{-16}$, and $|R(iy)| - 1$ is 0 at all four $y$.
At $[5/5]$, real parts span $4.65$ to $7.29$; sum 30 and product 30240 match closed forms within relative discrepancy $10^{-15}$.
At $[0/5]$, minimum real part $-0.240$ is in the left half-plane but above bound $m - n = -5$.
For the same $[0/5]$, $|R(iy)|$ is just below 1 at $y=0.5, 1$ and rises to $1.009$ at $y=2$; unit magnitude is confined to diagonal approximation.
Poles of $[0/3]$ are $1.596$ and $0.702 \pm 1.807i$, all in the right half-plane, but $|R(0.5i)| = 1.0024$ and $|R(i)| = 1.029$ exceed 1 before falling to $0.832$ at $y = 2$.
These values match formula $|R(iy)|^2 = 1/(1 - y^4/12 + y^6/36)$ above, numerically confirming failure of A-stability condition $n \le m+2$.
Pole locations are distinct from leading-error magnitudes; on real intervals with $x>0$, avoid real poles (such as $x = 1$ of $[0/1]$).

## Summary

The $[m/n]$ Padé approximant to $e^x$ has closed-form coefficients; evaluation through `ApproxRationalFunction` gives error following leading term $(-1)^n m!\,n!\,x^{m+n+1}/((m+n)!(m+n+1)!)$ near the origin.
Distinguish ratios approaching 1 from rounding-level differences below `evaluate_parts` error indicators; at large $m+n$, even $|x|=1$ errors need more than the leading term.
Poles lie in $\operatorname{Re} z > m - n$: the right half-plane for $m \ge n$ and possibly the left for $m < n$; reserve “left-half-plane poles” for approximations of $e^{-x}$.
Compute poles as denominator-root matrix eigenvalues and check with closed-form Vieta relations.
Diagonal $|R(iy)| = 1$ concerns imaginary-axis magnitude, not phase equality or nondiagonal cases; check $m \le n \le m+2$ when using stability functions.
