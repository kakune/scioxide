Intersections of two curves solve a two-variable algebraic system.
A resultant eliminates one variable to give univariate roots, but candidates require substitution into both original equations and special treatment of multiple roots at tangencies.
For a circle and parabola, examine transverse and tangent intersections using both floating-point candidates and exact rational counts.

## Eliminating between a circle and parabola

In dimensionless coordinates $(x, y)$, find intersections of the unit circle and a parabola shifted vertically by $h$,

$$
f(x,y) = y^2 + x^2 - 1 = 0, \qquad g(x,y) = y - x^2 + h = 0
$$


Treat $f$ and $g$ as polynomials in $y$ with polynomial coefficients in $x$; eliminating $y$ through the [resultant product formula](oxref:poly-resultant-root-formula) gives

$$
q_h(x) = \operatorname{Res}_y(f, g) = (-1)^{2 \cdot 1} \cdot 1^2 \cdot f(x, x^2 - h) = x^4 + (1-2h)x^2 + h^2 - 1
$$


The sole root of $g$ in $y$ is $y = x^2 - h$, making this equivalent to substitution.
If $q_h(x_0) = 0$, $(x_0, y_0)$ with $y_0 = x_0^2 - h$ is an intersection, giving exactly one real intersection per real $x_0$.
In general elimination, leading coefficients in $y$ may vanish at particular $x$, changing Sylvester shape and introducing extra candidates or losing solutions.
Here both $f$ and $g$ have leading coefficient 1 in $y$, so no such $x$ exists.

Use $t = x^2$ for independent checks.

$$
t^2 + (1-2h)t + h^2 - 1 = 0, \qquad t_\pm = \frac{2h - 1 \pm \sqrt{5 - 4h}}{2}
$$

Positive $t$ gives two real intersections $x = \pm\sqrt{t}$; $t = 0$ gives one $x = 0$; negative or complex $t$ gives no real intersection.
The discriminant of $q_h$ is $16(h^2 - 1)(5 - 4h)^2$ (substitute $B = 1 - 2h$ and $C = h^2 - 1$ into discriminant $16C(B^2 - 4C)^2$ of $x^4 + Bx^2 + C$), vanishing at $h = \pm 1$ and $h = 5/4$.
Distinct real intersection counts by $h$ are as follows.

| Range of $h$ | $t_\pm$ | Distinct real intersections | Tangency |
|---|---|---|---|
| $h < -1$ | Both negative | 0 | None |
| $h = -1$ | $t_+ = 0$ | 1（$(0, 1)$） | Tangent at $(0, 1)$ |
| $-1 < h < 1$ | $t_+ > 0 > t_-$ | 2 | None |
| $h = 1$ | $t_- = 0$, $t_+ = 1$ | 3（$(0,-1)$, $(\pm1, 0)$） | Tangent at $(0, -1)$ |
| $1 < h < 5/4$ | Both positive | 4 | None |
| $h = 5/4$ | $t_+ = t_- = 3/4$ | 2（$(\pm\sqrt3/2, -1/2)$） | Tangent at both |
| $h > 5/4$ | Complex | 0 | None |

Tangencies make $q_h$ have multiple roots, so multiplicity-counted roots differ from geometrically distinct intersections.

The following definitions block implements closed forms for first-cell independent checks and plotting.
`closed_form_coefficients` gives ascending coefficients of $q_h$, `closed_form_real_roots` real roots $\pm\sqrt{t_\pm}$ from $t_\pm \ge 0$ (with multiplicity), and `curves` circle/parabola plot points.
Square roots and trigonometry use `f64` intrinsics independently of SciOxide computation.

```rust
//| id: poly-resultant-closed-form
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Closed-form q_h and plotting curves"
//| crates: []
/// Ascending coefficients of q_h(x) = x⁴ + (1 − 2h)x² + h² − 1.
pub(crate) fn closed_form_coefficients(h: f64) -> [f64; 5] {
    [h * h - 1.0, 0.0, 1.0 - 2.0 * h, 0.0, 1.0]
}

/// Real roots ±√t± from nonnegative solutions t± of the quadratic in t = x² (with multiplicity; reference f64 sqrt).
pub(crate) fn closed_form_real_roots(h: f64) -> Vec<f64> {
    let discriminant = 5.0 - 4.0 * h;
    if discriminant < 0.0 {
        return Vec::new();
    }
    [1.0, -1.0]
        .iter()
        .map(|sign| (2.0 * h - 1.0 + sign * discriminant.sqrt()) / 2.0)
        .filter(|&t| t >= 0.0)
        .flat_map(|t| [t.sqrt(), -t.sqrt()])
        .collect()
}

/// Circle x² + y² = 1 (241 points) and parabola y = x² − h (201 points over x ∈ [−1.5, 1.5]) for plotting.
pub(crate) fn curves(h: f64) -> (Vec<[f64; 2]>, Vec<[f64; 2]>) {
    let circle = (0..=240)
        .map(|i| {
            let (sin, cos) = (2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * i as f64 / 240.0).sin_cos();
            [cos, sin]
        })
        .collect();
    let parabola = (0..=200)
        .map(|i| {
            let x = -1.5 + 3.0 * i as f64 / 200.0;
            [x, x * x - h]
        })
        .collect();
    (circle, parabola)
}
```

## Sampled resultants and intersection candidates

The first cell computes `approximate_resultant` at five distinct $x$ and Newton-interpolates these values as a quartic.
SciOxide resultants perform the main computation; eliminated coefficients are not entered manually.
Convert interpolation to monomials and compute roots of $q_h$ as eigenvalues of a matrix with roots as eigenvalues; small-imaginary-part roots become intersection candidates.
For candidates, use $y = x^2 - h$ to determine $y$ and inspect residuals in original circle equation $f(x, y)$.
This construction zeros the residual of $g$, so only $f$ residuals are tabulated.

There are two independent checks.
Compare interpolated coefficients with closed form $[h^2 - 1, 0, 1 - 2h, 0, 1]$ and candidates with the nearest root $\pm\sqrt{t_\pm}$ constructed from $t_\pm$.
The input is integer $j$, with $h = j/20$; subsequent exact cells use the same rational value.
The table lists candidate $x$, imaginary part, discrepancy from the nearest closed-form root, $y$, circle residual, maximum coefficient discrepancy, and candidate count.
Plot the circle, parabola, and candidates.

Interpolated coefficients match closed form, showing reconstruction through resultant sampling and interpolation to rounding accuracy.
At $j = 22$ ($h = 1.1$), four candidates match closed-form roots within $2 \times 10^{-15}$; $j = 30$ ($h = 1.5$) and $j = -30$ give none.
At tangency $j = 25$ ($h = 5/4$), eigensolver rounding splits double roots $x = \pm\sqrt{3}/2$ by about $10^{-8}$.
One pair is conjugate with imaginary parts $\pm 5 \times 10^{-9} i$; the other splits into real values $0.8660253793$ and $0.8660254283$.
With unit roundoff $u$, double-root perturbations are of order $\sqrt{u} \approx 10^{-8}$, matching the splitting predicted by root ill-conditioning.
The two real candidates lie $2.5 \times 10^{-8}$ from closed-form roots, yet circle residuals stay $2 \times 10^{-15}$.
At tangency, $q_h$ grows quadratically with root distance; rounding-level substitution residuals can thus accompany position errors of the order of the square root of the residual.
Imaginary threshold $10^{-6}$ keeps four candidates but only two distinct intersections exist.
At $j = 20$ ($h = 1$), candidates are the pair $x = \pm1$ and two overlapping copies of double root $x = 0$ at 0, totaling four.
At $j = -20$, only $x = 0$ appears, twice.
Floating-point candidate counts include multiplicity and depend on thresholds; they cannot prove intersection counts.

## Counts from exact Sturm sequences

For real-coefficient $q$, start a Sturm sequence with $p_0 = q$ and $p_1 = q'$, reverse remainder signs through $p_{k+1} = -(p_{k-1} \bmod p_k)$, and stop at zero remainder.
The final nonzero element is a constant multiple of $\gcd(q, q')$, with positive degree if $q$ has multiple roots.
Let $V(x)$ count sign changes at $x$ ignoring zeros; on interval $(a, b)$ with endpoints not roots of $q$, distinct real-root count is $V(a) - V(b)$ (Sturm's theorem).
This holds with multiple roots, counting distinct roots without multiplicity.
Positive scaling of remainders preserves sign changes; negative scaling does not, so do not interpose normalizations such as making polynomials monic.
Intersection coordinates $x$ lie on the circle and satisfy $|x| \le 1$; endpoints $\pm 2$ are not roots of $q_h$ ($q_h(\pm 2) = h^2 - 8h + 19 > 0$), so $V(-2) - V(2)$ counts distinct real intersections.

SciOxide has no dedicated Sturm API; combine `derivative` and `div_rem` of `ExactPolynomial`.
Rational coefficients are required, but SciOxide also provides no rational type.
The following definitions block defines small rational type `Q`, reducing `i128` numerators and denominators on each operation, and a function forming $q_h$ from $h = j/20$.
`Q` implements `ExactCoefficient` and `ExactField`, checking overflow and stopping rather than wrapping silently.
It is not arbitrary precision; use only small coefficients such as this page's input range.

```rust
//| id: poly-exact-rational-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Small rational type and exact coefficients of q_h"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_poly::{ExactCoefficient, ExactField, ExactPolynomial};
use std::ops::{Add, Div, Mul, Neg, Sub};

/// Reduced numerator/denominator (positive denominator). Arithmetic overflow stops with panic.
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub(crate) struct Q(pub(crate) i128, pub(crate) i128);

impl Q {
    pub(crate) fn new(numerator: i128, denominator: i128) -> Self {
        assert_ne!(denominator, 0, "Rational denominator is 0");
        let (mut a, mut b) = (numerator.abs(), denominator.abs());
        while b != 0 {
            (a, b) = (b, a % b);
        }
        let g = a * denominator.signum();
        Self(numerator / g, denominator / g)
    }

    pub(crate) fn to_f64(self) -> f64 {
        self.0 as f64 / self.1 as f64
    }
}

fn checked(value: Option<i128>) -> i128 {
    value.expect("Rational arithmetic exceeded i128 range")
}

impl Add for Q {
    type Output = Self;
    fn add(self, other: Self) -> Self {
        let left = checked(self.0.checked_mul(other.1));
        let right = checked(other.0.checked_mul(self.1));
        Self::new(
            checked(left.checked_add(right)),
            checked(self.1.checked_mul(other.1)),
        )
    }
}
impl Neg for Q {
    type Output = Self;
    fn neg(self) -> Self {
        Self(-self.0, self.1)
    }
}
impl Sub for Q {
    type Output = Self;
    fn sub(self, other: Self) -> Self {
        self + -other
    }
}
impl Mul for Q {
    type Output = Self;
    fn mul(self, other: Self) -> Self {
        Self::new(
            checked(self.0.checked_mul(other.0)),
            checked(self.1.checked_mul(other.1)),
        )
    }
}
impl Div for Q {
    type Output = Self;
    fn div(self, other: Self) -> Self {
        Self::new(
            checked(self.0.checked_mul(other.1)),
            checked(self.1.checked_mul(other.0)),
        )
    }
}
impl ExactCoefficient for Q {
    fn zero() -> Self {
        Q(0, 1)
    }
    fn one() -> Self {
        Q(1, 1)
    }
    fn from_usize(value: usize) -> Self {
        Q(value as i128, 1)
    }
}
impl ExactField for Q {}

/// Exact ascending coefficients of q_h(x) = x⁴ + (1 − 2h)x² + h² − 1.
pub(crate) fn intersection_polynomial(h: Q) -> Result<ExactPolynomial<Q>, SciOxideError> {
    let (zero, one, two) = (Q(0, 1), Q(1, 1), Q(2, 1));
    ExactPolynomial::new(vec![h * h - one, zero, one - two * h, zero, one])
}
```

```rust
//|   - case: default

let q = intersection_polynomial(Q::new(shift_index as i128, 20)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Sturm sequence: p₀ = q, p₁ = q′, p_{k+1} = −(p_{k−1} mod p_k)
let zero = ExactPolynomial::constant(Q(0, 1)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut sequence = vec![q, derivative];
loop {
    let n = sequence.len();
    let (_, remainder) = sequence[n - 2]
        .div_rem(&sequence[n - 1])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if remainder.is_zero() {
        break;
    }
    sequence.push(zero.try_sub(&remainder).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}

let variations = |x: Q| {
    let signs: Vec<_> = sequence
        .iter()
        .map(|p| sign(p, x))
        .filter(|&s| s != 0)
        .collect();
    signs.windows(2).filter(|w| w[0] != w[1]).count()
};
let (left, right) = (Q(-2, 1), Q(2, 1));
let rows: Vec<_> = sequence
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, p)| (i, p.degree(), sign(p, left), sign(p, right)))
    .collect();
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Sequence index k", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Degree of p_k", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Sign at x = −2", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Sign at x = 2", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sequence" })
);
let (v_left, v_right) = (variations(left), variations(right));
let last_degree = sequence.last().map_or(0, |p| p.degree());
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "V(−2)", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "V(2)", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Distinct real intersections", "type": "integer" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Final-element degree", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    vec![(v_left, v_right, v_left - v_right, last_degree)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "count" })
);
```

$j = 0$ gives 2, $j = 22$ gives 4, and $j = 30$ and $j = -30$ give 0, matching first-table $h$ ranges and first-cell candidate counts.
Tangency cases $j = -20, 20, 25$ give 1, 3, and 2 distinct intersections, unlike multiplicity-inclusive candidate counts 2, 4, and 4.
The Sturm sequence then ends at a constant multiple of $\gcd(q, q')$; final degrees are 1 at $j = \pm 20$ (a multiple of $x$) and 2 at $j = 25$ (a multiple of $x^2 - 3/4$).
Without tangency at $j$, the final element is a nonzero constant and $q_h$ has no multiple roots.

## Tangency detection with discriminants and subresultants

Tangency corresponds to a multiple root of $q_h$, detected by zero discriminant $D(q_h)$.
For a polynomial of degree $n$ with leading coefficient $a$, $D(q) = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\operatorname{Res}(q, q')/a$; for $n = 4$, $(-1)^6 = 1$ and $a = 1$ give $\operatorname{Res}(q_h, q_h') = D(q_h)$.
`exact_discriminant` returns the discriminant; `exact_subresultants` returns the subresultant remainder sequence and resultant of $q_h$ and $q_h'$.
Its final element is also a constant multiple of $\gcd(q, q')$, but stage signs and scaling differ from Sturm ([Resultants, discriminants, and subresultants](../../algorithms/resultants-and-elimination/)).

The next cell computes both for the same $q_h$ and tabulates rational discrepancies from closed form $16(h^2-1)(5-4h)^2$.
Another table compares final subresultant-element degree with degree of $\gcd(q_h, q_h')$ computed by `ExactPolynomial::gcd`.

```rust
//| inputs:
//|   shift_index: {"type":"range","label":"j in parabola shift h = j/20","description":"Shift parabola y = x² − h vertically.","min":-30,"max":30,"step":1,"value":0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"four-crossings","label":"j = 22（h = 1.1）","description":"The positive discriminant corresponds to either 0 or 4 real roots.","inputs":{"shift_index":22}}
//|   - {"id":"double-tangency","label":"j = 25（h = 5/4）","description":"Discriminant and resultant are 0; gcd(q, q′) has degree 2.","inputs":{"shift_index":25}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"invariants","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-400.0,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"invariants","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"invariants","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":0}
//|   - case: four-crossings
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"invariants","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"invariants","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":0}
//|   - case: double-tangency
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"invariants","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"invariants","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"gcd","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":2}
use scioxide_poly::{ExactPolynomial, exact_discriminant, exact_subresultants};

let sig8 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 8 });
let h = Q::new(shift_index as i128, 20);
let q = intersection_polynomial(h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let derivative = q.derivative().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let discriminant = exact_discriminant(&q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let subresultants = exact_subresultants(&q, &derivative).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let resultant = *subresultants.resultant();
// Independent check: D(q_h) = 16(h² − 1)(5 − 4h)². Compute discrepancies as rationals.
let factor = Q(5, 1) - Q(4, 1) * h;
let closed = Q(16, 1) * (h * h - Q(1, 1)) * factor * factor;
let rows = vec![
    (
        "D(q_h)（exact_discriminant）",
        discriminant.to_f64(),
        closed.to_f64(),
        (discriminant - closed).to_f64(),
    ),
    (
        "Res(q_h, q_h′)（exact_subresultants）",
        resultant.to_f64(),
        closed.to_f64(),
        (resultant - closed).to_f64(),
    ),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Exact computation [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig8 },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig8 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Discrepancy (computed as rational) [-]", "type": "number" }
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "invariants" })
);
let last = |list: &[ExactPolynomial<Q>]| list.last().map_or(0, |p| p.degree());
let gcd = q.gcd(&derivative).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Degree", "type": "integer" }
    ]),
    vec![
        (
            "Final subresultant remainder element",
            last(subresultants.polynomials())
        ),
        ("gcd(q_h, q_h′)（ExactPolynomial::gcd）", gcd.degree()),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "gcd" })
);
```

At every input-range $j = -30, \dots, 30$, discriminant and resultant equal closed form $16(h^2-1)(5-4h)^2$ as rationals, with discrepancy 0.
The discriminant is negative for $|h| < 1$ ($-400$ at $j = 0$), positive at $|h| > 1$ ($h \ne 5/4$).
Negative quartic discriminants mean exactly two distinct real roots; positive ones mean zero or four, consistent with Sturm counts.
At tangencies $j = -20, 20, 25$, both discriminant and resultant vanish, and the degrees of the final element of the subresultant remainder sequence and $\gcd(q_h, q_h')$ are 1, 1, and 2, matching Sturm.

## Summary

Eliminating $y$ makes the intersection $x$ coordinates roots of a univariate polynomial; substitute candidates into both original equations and compare with closed forms when available.
Tangency produces multiple roots; floating-point eigenvalues split double roots by about $\sqrt{u} \approx 10^{-8}$, so candidate counts are not intersection counts.
Rational Sturm sign variations count distinct intersections exactly; without a dedicated API, combine `derivative` and `div_rem` of `ExactPolynomial`.
Sturm requires reversed remainder signs, differing from subresultant sequence signs.
Detect tangency through `exact_discriminant` returning 0 and $\gcd(q, q')$ of positive degree.
Callers supply rational coefficient types and check overflow.
