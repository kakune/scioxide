Runge's phenomenon, demonstrated by C. Runge in 1901, is a classic case where higher polynomial interpolation degree need not improve accuracy.
Even infinitely differentiable real functions can have increasing endpoint error with equally spaced nodes when complex poles lie near the interval.
Interpolate Runge function $f(x) = 1/(1 + 25x^2)$ and variants with shifted poles at equally spaced and Chebyshev root nodes using barycentric `ApproxPolynomial::interpolate_barycentric`, comparing errors.
All quantities are dimensionless.
[Interpolation and Chebyshev nodes](../../algorithms/interpolation-and-chebyshev/) derives remainders, Lebesgue constants, and node optimality; here predict convergence and divergence rates from poles and check numerically.

## Runge's function and poles

Consider the function family with pole distance $b > 0$,

$$
f_b(x) = \frac{1}{1 + (x/b)^2} \quad (x \in [-1, 1])
$$


At $b = 1/5$, this is Runge function $1/(1 + 25x^2)$.
$f_b$ is even and infinitely differentiable on the real axis.
In the complex plane it has simple imaginary-axis poles $z = \pm \mathrm{i}b$, at distance $b$ from the interval.

## Error at equally spaced nodes

Two factors determine interpolation remainder $f(x) - p_n(x) = \dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!} \omega(x)$, $\omega(x)=\prod_{j=0}^n (x - x_j)$.

The first is the higher derivative.
Differentiate partial fractions $f_b(x)=\frac{b}{2\mathrm{i}}\bigl(\frac{1}{x-\mathrm{i}b}-\frac{1}{x+\mathrm{i}b}\bigr)$ $k$ times; since real $x$ satisfies $|x\mp\mathrm{i}b|\ge b$,

$$
\max_{x\in\mathbb R}\bigl|f_b^{(k)}(x)\bigr| \le k!\,b^{-k}
$$

with equality at $x=0$ for even $k$.
Derivatives grow as factorials times powers of inverse pole distance $1/b$.

The second factor is nodal polynomial $\omega$.
For equally spaced nodes $x_j = -1 + 2j/n$, as $n \to \infty$, $|\omega(z)|^{1/n}$ tends to $e^{u(z)}$ (on the interval this is the envelope limit of $|\omega|$, which vanishes at nodes).
Here

$$
u(z)=\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\ln|z-t|\,dt
$$

is the logarithmic potential of the uniform interval distribution.
Since $u(0) = -1$ and $u(\pm1) = \ln 2 - 1 \approx -0.3069$, endpoint-near $|\omega|$ is about $2^n$ times as large as at the center.
On the imaginary axis, $u(\mathrm{i}b) = \frac12\bigl[\ln(1 + b^2) - 2 + 2b\arctan(1/b)\bigr]$.

Combining both factors gives error changing roughly at rate $e^{n(u(x) - u(\mathrm{i}b))}$.
The region of equally spaced convergence is $u(z) < u(\mathrm{i}b)$, bounded by the contour of $u$ through pole $\pm\mathrm{i}b$ (Epperson, 1987; Trefethen, *Approximation Theory and Approximation Practice*, 2019, Chapter 13).
For Runge, $u(\pm\mathrm{i}/5)\approx-0.7057$; this contour meets the real axis at $\pm0.7267$.
For $0.7267 < |x| < 1$, errors away from nodes become unbounded with degree.
Near endpoints, error changes by a factor of about $g_{\rm eq} = e^{u(1)-u(\mathrm{i}b)}$ per degree; for Runge it grows by a factor of $g_{\rm eq} \approx 1.490$.
Critical distance satisfying $u(\mathrm{i}b) = u(1)$ is $b^* \approx 0.5255$; for $b > b^*$, $g_{\rm eq} < 1$ and equally spaced interpolation converges over the whole interval.

## Chebyshev root nodes

Zeros of first-kind Chebyshev polynomial $T_{n+1}(x)$ (Chebyshev root nodes) are

$$
x_j = \cos\left( \frac{2j + 1}{2(n + 1)} \pi \right) \quad (j = 0, 1, \dots, n)
$$


Spacing is $O(1/n)$ centrally and $O(1/n^2)$ near endpoints, becoming denser toward the ends.
The nodal polynomial is then $\omega(x) = 2^{-n} T_{n+1}(x)$, with $|\omega(x)| \le 2^{-n}$ on $[-1,1]$.
This is the smallest maximum magnitude on $[-1,1]$ among monic degree-$n+1$ polynomials.
Interpolation error for $f_b$ consequently tends to 0 at rate $O(\rho^{-n})$ determined by the pole-touching Bernstein ellipse (foci $\pm1$, semimajor plus semiminor axes $\rho$).
The ellipse through pole $z$ has $\rho$ given by $|z + \sqrt{z^2 - 1}|$; for $z = \mathrm{i}b$,

$$
\rho = b + \sqrt{1 + b^2}
$$


For Runge, $\rho \approx 1.2198$, giving error factor $1/\rho \approx 0.8198$ per degree.
$\rho > 1$ holds for every $b > 0$, so Chebyshev-root interpolation converges however close the poles are to the interval.

The following block defines helpers shared by two cells.
`runge_function` corresponds to $f_b$ and `interpolants` to the degree-$n$ interpolants (equally spaced, then Chebyshev roots).
Construct $n+1$ ascending root nodes with `Roots` of `backend::barycentric::chebyshev_points`.
`interpolate_barycentric` recomputes weights from nodes, so weights returned by `chebyshev_points` are unused.

```rust
//| id: poly-runge-nodes-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Pole-distance-b function and interpolation with two node layouts"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_poly::backend::barycentric::{ChebyshevPoints, chebyshev_points};
use scioxide_poly::{ApproxPolynomial, BarycentricBasis};

/// Barycentric interpolating polynomial.
pub(crate) type Interpolant = ApproxPolynomial<f64, BarycentricBasis<f64>>;

/// f_b(x) = 1/(1 + (x/b)²). Poles are ±ib; b = 1/5 gives Runge's function.
pub(crate) fn runge_function(pole: f64, x: f64) -> f64 {
    let scaled = x / pole;
    1.0 / (1.0 + scaled * scaled)
}

/// Return degree-n interpolants at equally spaced then Chebyshev root nodes (zeros of T_{n+1}).
pub(crate) fn interpolants(n: usize, pole: f64) -> Result<[Interpolant; 2], SciOxideError> {
    let equispaced: Vec<f64> = (0..=n).map(|j| -1.0 + 2.0 * j as f64 / n as f64).collect();
    let (chebyshev, _) = chebyshev_points::<f64>(n + 1, ChebyshevPoints::Roots)?;
    let interpolate = |nodes: Vec<f64>| {
        let values = nodes.iter().map(|&x| runge_function(pole, x)).collect();
        ApproxPolynomial::interpolate_barycentric(nodes, values)
    };
    Ok([interpolate(equispaced)?, interpolate(chebyshev)?])
}
```

## Interpolation curves and error distributions

For Runge ($b = 1/5$), the next cell varies degree $n$ and compares equally spaced and Chebyshev-root curves and absolute errors on a 201-point grid.
Error against the analytical Runge function is the independent check.
The upper plot draws the true function dashed and interpolants solid; the lower shows absolute error on semilog axes.
Both horizontal axes are linked.
The upper vertical axis is fixed at $[-1.5, 1.5]$; high-degree equally spaced endpoint oscillations leave the frame, so read their size below and in the table.
Omit zero-error evaluation points at nodes from logarithmic plots and count them in the table.

```rust
//| id: poly-runge-nodes
//| ref: poly-runge-nodes
//| title: "Comparing equally spaced and Chebyshev nodes"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-runge-nodes-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   degree: {"type":"range","label":"Interpolation degree n","description":"Node count is degree+1.","min":4,"max":14,"step":2,"value":10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"low-degree","label":"n = 4","description":"At low degree, both layouts have comparable errors.","inputs":{"degree":4}}
//|   - {"id":"high-degree","label":"n = 14","description":"Equally spaced endpoint oscillations exceed maximum error 7.","inputs":{"degree":14}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.9156430502,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.1089290399,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: low-degree
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.43818,"atol":5e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.40202,"atol":5e-5}}
//|   - case: high-degree
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":7.1920,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.04652,"atol":5e-5}}
// Runge function: pole distance b = 1/5
let pole = 0.2;
let n = degree as usize;
let models = interpolants(n, pole).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Inspect values and absolute errors on a 201-point grid. Index 0 is equally spaced, 1 Chebyshev roots.
let mut exact = Vec::new();
let mut curves = [Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
let mut errors = [Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
let mut worst = [0.0_f64; 2];
let mut zero_errors = [0_usize; 2];
for i in 0..=200 {
    let x = -1.0 + 2.0 * i as f64 / 200.0;
    let reference = runge_function(pole, x);
    exact.push([x, reference]);
    for k in 0..2 {
        let value = models[k].evaluate(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let error = (value - reference).abs();
        worst[k] = worst[k].max(error);
        curves[k].push([x, value]);
        // Omit and count zero errors at nodes on logarithmic axes
        if error > 0.0 {
            errors[k].push([x, error]);
        } else {
            zero_errors[k] += 1;
        }
    }
}

]);

        ("Equally spaced", worst[0], zero_errors[0]),

    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
let [equal_curve, chebyshev_curve] = curves;

let [equal_error, chebyshev_error] = errors;
emit_line_chart!(

        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Equally spaced interpolation", "points": equal_curve }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Chebyshev interpolation", "points": chebyshev_curve }),

    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "runge-x" },
        "xLabel": "x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Function value f(x) [-]",
        "yMin": -1.5,
        "yMax": 1.5
    })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Equally spaced absolute error", "points": equal_error }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Chebyshev absolute error", "points": chebyshev_error }),

    options = &serde_json::json!({
```

Raising degree through $n = 4, 10, 14$ improves equally spaced fitting centrally ($|x| \le 0.5$) but creates large endpoint oscillations ($|x| \ge 0.8$), increasing maximum errors through $0.438$, $1.916$, and $7.192$.
Chebyshev-root errors are nearly uniform over the interval; maximum errors decrease through $0.402$, $0.109$, and $0.0465$.
Endpoint-heavy versus interval-wide error distributions distinguish the layouts.
At default $n = 10$, zero-error evaluation points are all 11 equally spaced nodes and the single Chebyshev-root point $x = 0$, where nodes coincide with grid points.

## Maximum error as degree increases

The next cell selects pole distance $b$, varies even degrees from 2 to 30, and measures each layout's maximum error $e_n$ on a 1001-point grid.
Table ratios are per-degree error factors: square roots $(e_n/e_{n-2})^{1/2}$ of ratios at consecutive even degrees.
Above 1 means increasing error with degree; below 1 means decreasing error.
Independently compare them with theoretical $g_{\rm eq} = e^{u(1)-u(\mathrm{i}b)}$ and $1/\rho$.
Theory uses the closed forms above with f64 `sqrt`, `ln`, `atan`, and `exp` as references without SciOxide functions.
Dashed reference lines pass through observed $n=30$ values with the theoretical ratios.

```rust
//| id: poly-runge-degree-sweep
//| ref: poly-runge-degree-sweep
//| title: "Comparing maximum errors as degree increases"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [poly-runge-nodes-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-poly]
//| inputs:
//|   pole: {"type":"range","label":"Pole distance b [-]","description":"Pole locations ±ib of f(x) = 1/(1 + (x/b)²). b = 0.2 gives Runge's function.","min":0.1,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"far-pole","label":"b = 0.6","description":"Poles lie beyond critical distance 0.5255, so equally spaced interpolation also converges.","inputs":{"pole":0.6}}
//|   - {"id":"near-pole","label":"b = 0.1","description":"Nearby poles increase equally spaced errors about 1.7-fold per degree.","inputs":{"pole":0.1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"theory","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.2198039027,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/14/2","approx":{"value":2.08e-3,"rtol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/14/4","approx":{"value":0.8198,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/14/3","approx":{"value":1.452,"atol":0.002}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":null}
//|   - case: far-pole
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"theory","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.9242050973,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/14/4","approx":{"value":0.5662,"atol":0.005}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/14/1","range":{"max":0.01}}
//|   - case: near-pole
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/14/4","approx":{"value":0.905,"atol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/14/3","approx":{"value":1.674,"atol":0.002}}
// Reference (f64 sqrt, ln, atan, exp): ellipse ρ = b + √(1 + b²), and
// g_eq from logarithmic potential u(ib) = ½[ln(1 + b²) − 2 + 2b·atan(1/b)] and u(1) = ln 2 − 1
let rho = pole + (1.0 + pole * pole).sqrt();
let potential = 0.5 * ((1.0 + pole * pole).ln() - 2.0 + 2.0 * pole * (1.0 / pole).atan());
let equal_factor = (std::f64::consts::LN_2 - 1.0 - potential).exp();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut series = [Vec::new(), Vec::new()];
let mut previous: Option<[f64; 2]> = None;
for n in (2..=30).step_by(2) {
    let models = interpolants(n, pole).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut worst = [0.0_f64; 2];
    for (k, model) in models.iter().enumerate() {
        // Measure maximum error on 1001 evaluation points
        for i in 0..=1000 {
            let x = -1.0 + 2.0 * i as f64 / 1000.0;
            let value = model.evaluate(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            worst[k] = worst[k].max((value - runge_function(pole, x)).abs());
        }
        series[k].push([n as f64, worst[k]]);
    }
    // Per-degree error ratio (e_n / e_{n−2})^{1/2} (f64 sqrt). Undefined in the first row.
    let ratio = |k: usize| previous.map(|e| (worst[k] / e[k]).sqrt());
    rows.push((n, worst[0], worst[1], ratio(0), ratio(1)));
    previous = Some(worst);
}
```

For Runge ($b = 0.2$), Chebyshev-root maximum errors nearly form a straight semilog line matching the dashed slope.
From $n = 20$ to $30$, maximum error falls from $1.53 \times 10^{-2}$ to $2.06 \times 10^{-3}$ with per-degree ratios near $0.82$, close to theory $1/\rho \approx 0.8198$.
Over the same degrees, equally spaced error rises from $59.8$ to $2385$; ratios $1.431$ to $1.452$ approach theoretical $g_{\rm eq} \approx 1.490$ from below.
Ratios have not reached theory because degree-dependent polynomial factors multiply the exponential error factor and remain significant at finite $n$.

At $b = 0.6$, poles are farther than critical distance $b^* \approx 0.5255$, giving theoretical ratio $g_{\rm eq} \approx 0.924$ below 1.
Equally spaced error indeed falls to $2.85 \times 10^{-3}$ at $n = 30$, but is 5 orders larger than Chebyshev-root $2.20 \times 10^{-8}$.
At $b = 0.1$, equally spaced error reaches $8.9 \times 10^{4}$ at $n = 30$; per-degree ratio $1.674$ approaches theoretical $1.718$.
Chebyshev-root ratios lie between $0.89$ and $0.92$ around theoretical $1/\rho \approx 0.905$.
Closer poles bring $\rho$ nearer 1 and slow convergence, but error decreases for every $b$.

For equally spaced nodes at interpolation degree $n = 30$, the Lebesgue constant is about $2^{31}/(e \cdot 30 \ln 30) \approx 8 \times 10^{6}$, potentially amplifying sample rounding to about $10^{-9}$.
Truncation dominates over this $b$ range, hiding rounding effects in the table.
At still higher degrees, amplified rounding makes equally spaced error plateau even for distant poles.

## Summary

Equally spaced convergence depends on which side of the logarithmic potential contour of the node distribution contains the poles; imaginary-axis poles have critical distance $b^* \approx 0.5255$.
For Runge ($b = 0.2$), error grows indefinitely over $|x|>0.7267$; near endpoints it increases by a factor of about $1.49$ per degree.
Chebyshev-root error decreases at rate $\rho^{-n}$ from pole-touching ellipse $\rho = b + \sqrt{1 + b^2}$, checked by tabulated per-degree ratios.
For high-degree approximation, choose Chebyshev barycentric interpolation or orthogonal expansions over equally spaced interpolation.
Finite-grid maximum errors do not prove uniform-error bounds; compare finer grids and theoretical rates as well.
For noisy samples, distinguish interpolation from [least squares](../../algorithms/least-squares-and-vandermonde/).
