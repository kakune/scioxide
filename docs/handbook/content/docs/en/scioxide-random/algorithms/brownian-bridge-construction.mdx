In stochastic differential equations and the valuation of path-dependent financial products, a Wiener process path on a time grid is constructed from $n$ standard normal coordinates.
When QMC points are mapped through normal quantiles and used for these coordinates, the leading coordinates of the point sequence are more uniform, so the assignment of coordinates to parts of the path affects the error.
This page explains the assignment order used by `BrownianBridgePlan` in `scioxide-random` and why this order concentrates the path variance in the leading coordinates.
QMC point construction is covered in [Constructing Halton and Sobol sequences and measuring discrepancy](../quasi-random-and-brownian-bridges/), and transport to normal coordinates in [Transporting QMC points to distributions](oxref:random-qmc-transport).

## Sequential construction and conditional distributions

A Wiener process $W$ satisfies $W(t_0) = 0$ and has mutually independent increments on disjoint intervals, with the normal distribution $W(t) - W(s) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t - s)$.
A straightforward way to construct values at times $t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n$ from independent standard normal coordinates $z_0, \dots, z_{n-1}$ is to proceed in time order using

$$
W(t_{k+1}) = W(t_k) + \sqrt{t_{k+1} - t_k}\; z_k
$$


In this sequential construction, coordinate $z_k$ enters every value from $t_{k+1}$ onward with the same coefficient, so the path variance is spread over many coordinates rather than concentrated in the first few.

A Brownian bridge constructs the value at time $t_m$ inside an interval $[t_l, t_r]$ with known endpoint values from the conditional distribution.
The increments $X = W(t_m) - W(t_l)$ and $Y = W(t_r) - W(t_m)$ are independent, with variances $a = t_m - t_l$ and $b = t_r - t_m$, respectively.
Conditioning a normal distribution on the sum $X + Y = W(t_r) - W(t_l)$ gives a normal conditional distribution for $X$ with mean $\frac{a}{a+b}(X+Y)$ and variance $\frac{ab}{a+b}$.
Thus,

$$
W(t_m) \mid W(t_l), W(t_r) \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left( \frac{(t_r - t_m) W(t_l) + (t_m - t_l) W(t_r)}{t_r - t_l},\ \frac{(t_m - t_l)(t_r - t_m)}{t_r - t_l} \right)
$$

where the mean is the linear interpolation of the endpoint values and the variance is greatest at the center of the interval.

## Assigning coordinates with a Brownian bridge

`BrownianBridgePlan::new(&times)` validates and copies the time grid $t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n$ and precomputes the interpolation weights and conditional standard deviations at each stage.
Coordinate $z_0$ determines the endpoint $W(t_n) = \sqrt{t_n - t_0}\, z_0$; the remaining coordinates determine the midpoint $m = \lfloor (l + r)/2 \rfloor$ of each index interval $(l, r)$ whose endpoints are known, in breadth-first order from left to right.
For a grid with 8 intervals, coordinates are used in index order $8, 4, 2, 6, 1, 3, 5, 7$.
The grid may be nonuniform, and the number of intervals need not be a power of 2.
Since $W(t_0)$ is set to 0, the result has $\mathrm{Cov}(W(t_i), W(t_j)) = \min(t_i, t_j) - t_0$.

Fewer than 2 times gives `InvalidInput`, times that are not strictly increasing give `DomainError`, and nonfinite times give `NonFinite`.
The conditional standard deviation is evaluated in the order $\sqrt{t_m - t_l}\sqrt{t_r - t_m}/\sqrt{t_r - t_l}$, retaining a positive value even on grids where the product in the variance is subnormal.
If a positive standard deviation still cannot be represented, the error is `NumericalInstability`.
Construction takes $O(n)$ time and storage, and both `input_len()` and `output_len()` return $n$.

`apply(z)` returns $W(t_1), \dots, W(t_n)$ in a new `Vec` in $O(n)$ time ($W(t_0) = 0$ is not output).
`apply_into(z, out, workspace)` constructs the same path in the workspace before copying it to the caller's output, leaving the output unchanged on error.
Create the workspace with `plan.workspace(())`; reusing it avoids allocation for each path.
If the length of `z` or the output is not $n$, the error is `DimensionMismatch`; a nonfinite value in `z` gives `NonFinite`, and overflow in a path value gives `NumericalInstability`.

## Variance carried by the leading coordinates

The path is a linear map $W = A z$ of $z$ ($A$ is $n \times n$), so the contribution of coordinate $z_j$ to the total path variance $\sum_i \mathrm{Var}\,W(t_i) = \sum_i (t_i - t_0)$ is $\sum_i A_{ij}^2$.
In the sequential construction, coordinate $z_j$ contributes variance $t_{j+1} - t_j$ to each of the $n - j$ values from time $t_{j+1}$ onward, giving a contribution of $(t_{j+1} - t_j)(n - j)$.
In the bridge construction, the endpoint coordinate $z_0$ enters $W(t_i)$ through linear interpolation with coefficient $(t_i - t_0)/\sqrt{t_n - t_0}$, so its share when $t_0 = 0$ is $\sum_i t_i^2 / (t_n \sum_i t_i)$.
On the uniform grid $t_i = i/n$, this share is $(2n + 1)/(3n)$ and is at least 2/3 regardless of $n$.

In QMC integration, low-discrepancy sequences offer an advantage when most variation in the function value can be explained as a function of a small number of coordinates (when the effective dimension is low).
The bridge ordering assigns the broad shape of the path to the leading coordinates, reducing the effective dimension of quantities such as the terminal value and the path average.
However, the effective dimension depends on the integrand, and the benefit is small for quantities sensitive to fine path fluctuations.

The following cell passes unit vectors to `apply_into` to extract the columns of $A$ and compares the share of variance explained by the first $k$ coordinates with sequential construction.
The inputs are the number of intervals $n$ and the time grid ($T = t_n = 1$).
The sequential contributions $(t_{j+1} - t_j)(n - j)$, total variance $\sum_i t_i$, and endpoint coordinate share $\sum_i t_i^2 / \sum_i t_i$ are calculated by hand for an independent check.

```rust
//| id: random-bridge-variance-order
//| ref: random-bridge-variance-order
//| title: "Compare variance carried by leading coordinates in Brownian bridge and sequential constructions"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   steps: {"type":"range","label":"Number of intervals n [-]","min":8,"max":128,"step":8,"value":64}
//|   grid: {"type":"select","label":"Time grid (T = 1)","options":["Uniform t_i = i/n","Finer near the start t_i = (i/n)²","Finer near the end t_i = 1 − (1 − i/n)²"],"value":"Uniform t_i = i/n"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fine-start","label":"Grid finer near the start","description":"The share of the first 8 sequential coordinates falls to 4.2%, while the bridge retains 94.0%.","inputs":{"grid":"Finer near the start t_i = (i/n)²"}}
//|   - {"id":"short-grid","label":"n = 8","description":"With 8 intervals there are 8 coordinates, so both reach 1 at k = 8; at k = 1 the bridge carries 0.708 and sequential construction carries 0.222.","inputs":{"steps":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shares","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.671,"max":0.673}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shares","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":0.959,"max":0.961}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shares","path":"/rows/3/2","range":{"min":0.232,"max":0.234}}
//|       - {"artifact":"totals","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"totals","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: fine-start
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shares","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":0.939,"max":0.941}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shares","path":"/rows/3/2","range":{"min":0.041,"max":0.043}}
//|       - {"artifact":"totals","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: short-grid
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shares","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":0.999999,"max":1.000001}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shares","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.7083,"max":0.7084}}
use scioxide_random::BrownianBridgePlan;

let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let n = steps.round() as usize;
let times: Vec<f64> = (0..=n)
    .map(|i| {
        let s = i as f64 / n as f64;
        match grid.as_str() {
            "Finer near the start t_i = (i/n)²" => s * s,
            "Finer near the end t_i = 1 − (1 − i/n)²" => 1.0 - (1.0 - s) * (1.0 - s),
            _ => s,
        }
    })
    .collect();
let plan = BrownianBridgePlan::new(&times).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = plan.workspace(()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut unit, mut column) = (vec![0.0_f64; n], vec![0.0_f64; n]);
// Contribution Σ_i A_ij² of coordinate j (bridge). Passing unit vector e_j gives column j of A.
let mut bridge = Vec::with_capacity(n);
for j in 0..n {
    unit.fill(0.0);
    unit[j] = 1.0;
    plan.apply_into(&unit, &mut column, &mut workspace)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    bridge.push(column.iter().map(|w| w * w).sum::<f64>());
}
// Independent check: sequential contribution (t_{j+1} − t_j)(n − j), total variance Σ t_i (t_0 = 0),
// and endpoint coordinate share Σ t_i² / Σ t_i (T = 1).
let forward: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| (times[j + 1] - times[j]) * (n - j) as f64)
    .collect();
let total: f64 = times[1..].iter().sum();
let terminal = times[1..].iter().map(|t| t * t).sum::<f64>() / total;
let cumulative = |parts: &[f64]| {
    parts
        .iter()
        .scan(0.0, |acc, v| {
            *acc += v;
            Some(*acc / total)
        })
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(k, f)| [(k + 1) as f64, f])
        .collect::<Vec<[f64; 2]>>()
};
let (bridge_curve, forward_curve) = (cumulative(&bridge), cumulative(&forward));
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Brownian bridge",
            "points": bridge_curve,
            "style": { "showSymbol": true, "symbol": "circle" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Sequential construction (analytical)",
            "points": forward_curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Number of coordinates used k [count]",
        "yLabel": "Share of variance explained [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.0
    })
);
let rows: Vec<(usize, f64, f64)> = [1_usize, 2, 4, 8]
    .iter()
    .map(|&k| (k, bridge_curve[k - 1][1], forward_curve[k - 1][1]))
    .collect();
let share_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Number of leading coordinates k [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Brownian bridge [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Sequential construction [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    share_columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "shares" })
);
let gap = (bridge.iter().sum::<f64>() - total).abs();
let total_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Total variance Σ t_i [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "|Sum of bridge columns − Σ t_i| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Endpoint coordinate share (analytical) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "|First bridge coordinate − analytical value| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    total_columns,
    vec![(total, gap, terminal, (bridge[0] / total - terminal).abs())],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "totals" })
);
```

With 64 uniform intervals, the first bridge coordinate (the terminal value) carries 67.2% of the total variance and the first 8 carry 96.0%, whereas the first 8 sequential coordinates carry 23.3%.
The first coordinate share of 0.6719 agrees with the analytical value $(2n+1)/(3n) = 129/192$.
On the grid finer near the start, the share of the first 8 sequential coordinates falls to 4.2%, while the bridge retains 94.0%.
In sequential construction, the leading coordinates represent increments over short intervals, so a finer grid near the start reduces their contribution.
The final table shows that the total variance obtained from the bridge columns agrees with $\sum_i t_i = 32.5$, with a difference of 0 ($3.6 \times 10^{-15}$ on the grid finer near the start).
For functions with low effective dimension, this ordering lets the uniform leading coordinates of a Sobol sequence determine the broad shape of the path.

## Summary

A Brownian bridge uses conditional normal distributions to determine path values at the endpoint, midpoint, and successive midpoints, with construction cost $O(n)$ and per-path cost $O(n)$, producing the same covariance $\min(t_i, t_j) - t_0$ as sequential construction.
On a uniform grid, the endpoint coordinate alone carries $(2n+1)/(3n)$ of the total variance, and on the cell's grids the first 8 carry more than 90%, allowing the leading QMC coordinates to control the broad path shape.
The grid may be nonuniform and need not have a power-of-2 size; reusing `apply_into` and its workspace constructs many paths without per-path allocation.
The benefit depends on the integrand's effective dimension, so for quantities sensitive to fine path fluctuations, assess it using the standard error across randomized replicates.
