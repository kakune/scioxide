Data expressed as angles, such as wind direction, wave propagation direction, and circadian phase, are periodic.
The arithmetic mean of angles $5^\circ$ and $355^\circ$ is $180^\circ$, pointing south even though both observations point almost north.
The circular mean, which reads the direction of the average unit vector $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$, is $0^\circ$ and represents the two observations.
`scioxide-random` provides `VonMises`, `VonMisesLine`, and `WrappedCauchy` as laws taking values on the circle.
This page explains principal interval conventions for treating angles as real numbers, the evaluation and sampling algorithms of each law, and the meaning and sample bias of mean resultant length.
Laws for directions on the sphere $S^{d-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ (`UniformDirection` and `VonMisesFisher`) are covered in [Probability laws for directions on the sphere](../spherical-directional-laws/).

## Circular laws and principal intervals

Since angle laws return real values, they need a convention for which of the angles differing by $2\pi$ to return.
`scioxide-random` assigns each law a principal interval of length $2\pi$ and defines its CDF, upper-tail probability, quantiles, mean and variance, and samples on that interval.
Angles are measured in radians.

| Law | Constructor | Principal interval | Density | Mean direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `VonMises` | `new(location, concentration)`, $\kappa \ge 0$ | $[\mu - \pi, \mu + \pi]$ | $\dfrac{e^{\kappa\cos(\theta - \mu)}}{2\pi I_0(\kappa)}$, $2\pi$-periodic on all real numbers | $\mu$ |
| `VonMisesLine` | `new(location, concentration)` | $[\mu - \pi, \mu + \pi]$ | The same formula restricted to the principal interval, 0 outside | $\mu$ |
| `WrappedCauchy` | `new(location, rho)`, $0 \le \rho < 1$ | $[\mu, \mu + 2\pi]$ | $\dfrac{1 - \rho^2}{2\pi\bigl(1 + \rho^2 - 2\rho\cos(\theta - \mu)\bigr)}$, 0 outside the principal interval | $\mu$ |

$I_\nu$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind, $\kappa$ is the concentration, and both $\kappa = 0$ and $\rho = 0$ give the uniform distribution on the circle.
`new` uses the default `LawOptions`; `with_options(…, options)` takes settings after the same parameters to change accuracy and resource limits.
A location so large that adding $\pm\pi$ ($+2\pi$ for wrapped Cauchy) to `location` rounds back to `location` gives `DomainError` because the principal interval is unrepresentable.

`VonMises` and `VonMisesLine` have the same parameters, CDF, quantiles, moments, and samples; only the density domain differs.
As a circular density, `pdf` of `VonMises` returns periodic values for every real angle, giving the same value at $\theta$ and $\theta + 2\pi$.
`VonMisesLine` returns density 0 outside the principal interval, giving a probability law whose integral over the real line is 1.
Use `VonMisesLine` as a real-valued random variable in compositions such as mixtures and transformations, or in calculations that rely on the density integrating to 1.

`VonMises` and `WrappedCauchy` return circular statistical quantities through separate methods.
`mean_resultant_length()` returns the population mean resultant length $\rho = \lVert\mathbb{E}[(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)]\rVert$, `circular_variance()` returns $1 - \rho$, and `mean_direction()` returns the mean direction $\mu$.
A uniform distribution ($\kappa = 0$ or $\rho = 0$) has no mean direction, so `mean_direction()` returns `DomainError` for an undefined moment.
`winding_cdf(x)` of `VonMises` is a lifted CDF that increases by 1 per revolution and is not a probability outside the principal interval.

The `Mean` and `Variance` traits return the real-valued mean and variance on the principal interval.
`mean()` of `VonMises` is $\mu$, agreeing with the circular mean direction.
On the principal interval $[\mu, \mu + 2\pi]$ of `WrappedCauchy`, mass concentrates near both ends and the density is symmetric about $\mu + \pi$, so `mean()` returns $\mu + \pi$.
For the circular direction, use `mean_direction()` rather than `mean()`.

## Evaluating and sampling the von Mises distribution

The normalizing constant of the von Mises distribution is $I_0(\kappa)$; `new` computes $I_0$ and $I_1$ once in the form $e^{-\kappa}I_\nu(\kappa)$ to avoid overflow.
The mean resultant length is $\rho = I_1(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa)$; for $\kappa > 10^8$, the asymptotic form $1 - \rho \approx \frac{1}{2\kappa}\bigl(1 + \frac{1}{4\kappa}\bigr)$ preserves $1 - \rho$.
The exponent in the density is evaluated as $\kappa\cos(\theta - \mu) - \kappa = -2\kappa\sin^2\bigl((\theta - \mu)/2\bigr)$, avoiding subtraction of $\cos$ and 1 for large $\kappa$.

The CDF is computed by directly integrating the smaller tail.
A rational change of integration variable resolves mass concentrated near the tail's starting point for large $\kappa$ with few subdivisions; integration uses adaptive Gauss–Kronrod quadrature (a 7-point and 15-point pair).
Quantiles are solved in coordinates transformed by $y = \sqrt{\max(\kappa, 1)}\,\operatorname{atanh}\bigl((\theta - \mu)/\pi\bigr)$.
These coordinates have support on the whole real line, so a bracket can be found after finitely many expansions even when large $\kappa$ concentrates mass in a narrow region.
Tail integration and quantile search count toward the law's `work_budget`.

Samples are generated by rejection sampling of angle $\theta - \mu \in [-\pi, \pi]$ followed by adding $\mu$.

- For $\kappa = 0$, the distribution is uniform and returns $2\pi U - \pi$.
- For $0 < \kappa < 0.5$, a uniform envelope is used, accepting when $\ln V \le -2\kappa\sin^2(\theta/2)$.
  The acceptance rate is at least $e^{-2\kappa}$ and exceeds $e^{-1}$ for $\kappa < 0.5$.
- For $\kappa \ge 0.5$, the method of Best and Fisher (1979) uses a wrapped Cauchy envelope.
  The envelope parameter $r - 1 = (1 - b)^2/(2b)$ and the quantity $1 - \cos\theta$ are computed with rationalized expressions to avoid cancellation even for large $\kappa$.
  After accepting the candidate magnitude $\lvert\theta\rvert$, either sign is assigned with probability 1/2.

Both rejection methods stop after 1,024 proposals and return an error if none has been accepted.

## Wrapped Cauchy distribution

The wrapped Cauchy distribution wraps Cauchy values modulo $2\pi$; its mean resultant length is the parameter $\rho$ itself.
The CDF has a closed form; for $\theta - \mu \in [0, \pi]$, it is

$$
F(\theta) = \frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\Bigl(\frac{1 + \rho}{1 - \rho}\tan\frac{\theta - \mu}{2}\Bigr)
$$


For $\theta - \mu \in (\pi, 2\pi)$, substituting the distance $\mu + 2\pi - \theta$ from the right endpoint into the same expression gives the upper-tail probability $1 - F(\theta)$.
The implementation computes the smaller tail near an endpoint directly using `atan2` and forms the other as its complement.
The closed form also has an inverse, used for quantiles and inverse-transform sampling.

## Mean resultant length and sample bias

For an angle sample $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n$, define the mean resultant vector and its length by

$$
(\bar{C}, \bar{S}) = \Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\cos\theta_i,\ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\sin\theta_i\Bigr),\qquad
\bar{R} = \sqrt{\bar{C}^2 + \bar{S}^2} \in [0, 1]
$$

The length $\bar{R}$ is called the sample mean resultant length.
As $n \to \infty$, $\bar{R}$ converges to the population value $\rho$; for von Mises, $\rho = I_1(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa)$, while for wrapped Cauchy, $\rho$ is itself a parameter.
For von Mises, $\rho$ is 0 at $\kappa = 0$ and approaches 1 as $\kappa \to \infty$, expressing concentration as a number in $[0, 1]$.

For finite $n$, $\bar{R}$ tends to exceed $\rho$.
The expected squared mean of unit vectors $\mathbf{u}_i = (\cos\theta_i, \sin\theta_i)$ splits into the squared mean and variance, giving exactly

$$
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bar{R}^2\bigr] = \rho^2 + \frac{1 - \rho^2}{n}
$$

using $\mathbb{E}\lVert\mathbf{u}_i\rVert^2 = 1$.
Even for the uniform distribution ($\rho = 0$), $\mathbb{E}[\bar{R}^2] = 1/n$, and for $n = 10$, $\bar{R}$ is about 0.3.
When interpreting $\bar{R}$ from a small sample as evidence of concentration, account for this bias in the comparison.

For an unbiased comparison, use the component $\bar{C}_\mu = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \cos(\theta_i - \mu)$ along the mean direction $\mu$.
Since $\mathbb{E}[\cos(\theta - \mu)] = \rho$, $\bar{C}_\mu$ is an unbiased estimate of $\rho$, with standard error $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\cos(\theta - \mu))/n}$.
For von Mises, $I_0(\kappa) - I_2(\kappa) = (2/\kappa)I_1(\kappa)$ gives $\mathbb{E}[\cos 2(\theta - \mu)] = I_2/I_0 = 1 - 2\rho/\kappa$, hence

$$
\operatorname{Var}\bigl(\cos(\theta - \mu)\bigr) = \frac{1 + I_2/I_0}{2} - \rho^2 = 1 - \frac{\rho}{\kappa} - \rho^2
$$

with limiting value $1/2$ at $\kappa = 0$.
For wrapped Cauchy, $\mathbb{E}[\cos 2(\theta - \mu)] = \rho^2$, so the variance is $(1 - \rho^2)/2$.
[Wind direction samples and bias in mean resultant length](../../examples/von-mises-wind-samples/) provides an example checking the bias of $\bar{R}$ and the standard error of the mean direction using wind observations.

## Checking principal intervals and the mean cosine

The following cell plots the densities of `VonMises`, `VonMisesLine`, and `WrappedCauchy` with mean direction $\mu = 0$ over $[-\pi, 2\pi]$, showing the differences between principal intervals.
The density of `VonMises` continues periodically; `VonMisesLine` is 0 outside $[-\pi, \pi]$, and `WrappedCauchy` is 0 outside $[0, 2\pi]$.
The first table shows the minimum and maximum of $n$ samples from each of `VonMises` and `WrappedCauchy`, together with `mean()` and `mean_direction()`, checking principal interval conventions.
The second table places $z$ comparing $\bar{C}_\mu$ with `mean_resultant_length()` alongside $\bar{R}$ and the bias-inclusive magnitude $\sqrt{\rho^2 + (1 - \rho^2)/n}$.
The inputs are the von Mises concentration $\kappa$, wrapped Cauchy $\rho$, sample size $n$, and SplitMix64 seed.

```rust
//| id: random-circular-interval-moments
//| ref: random-circular-interval-moments
//| title: "Check principal intervals and the mean cosine of circular laws"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   concentration: {"type":"range","label":"von Mises concentration κ [-]","description":"At 0 the distribution is uniform and mean_direction() is undefined.","min":0,"max":10,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//|   rho: {"type":"range","label":"Wrapped Cauchy ρ [-]","description":"The mean resultant length itself; closer to 1 places more mass near the two ends of the principal interval.","min":0,"max":0.95,"step":0.05,"value":0.5}
//|   sample_count: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size n [count]","description":"Changes the standard error of z and the bias term (1 − ρ²)/n of R̄.","min":20,"max":20000,"step":20,"value":4000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"SplitMix64 seed [-]","description":"Observe sample-to-sample fluctuations in z.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":17}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"uniform","label":"κ = 0, ρ = 0","description":"Both become uniform, mean_direction() is undefined, and R̄ is of order 1/√n.","inputs":{"concentration":0,"rho":0}}
//|   - {"id":"small-uniform","label":"κ = 0, ρ = 0, n = 20","description":"The population ρ is 0, but R̄ is of order √(1/n) ≈ 0.22.","inputs":{"concentration":0,"rho":0,"sample_count":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"intervals","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":3.141592653589793,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"intervals","path":"/rows/1/4","approx":{"value":0,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"resultant","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"resultant","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|   - case: uniform
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"intervals","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"resultant","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    Density, Distribution, Mean, SeedableRng, SplitMix64, VonMises, VonMisesLine, WrappedCauchy,
};

let (kappa, n) = (concentration, sample_count.round() as usize);
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let circle = VonMises::<f64>::new(0.0, kappa).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let line = VonMisesLine::<f64>::new(0.0, kappa).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let wrapped = WrappedCauchy::<f64>::new(0.0, rho).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut periodic, mut restricted, mut cauchy) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for i in 0..=270 {
    let x = -pi + 3.0 * pi * i as f64 / 270.0;
    periodic.push([x, circle.pdf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
    restricted.push([x, line.pdf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
    cauchy.push([x, wrapped.pdf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
}
// Cosine variance: 1 − ρ/κ − ρ² for von Mises (limiting value 1/2 at κ = 0), and (1 − ρ²)/2 for wrapped Cauchy.
let von_mises_rho = circle.mean_resultant_length().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let von_mises_variance = if kappa == 0.0 {
    0.5
} else {
    1.0 - von_mises_rho / kappa - von_mises_rho * von_mises_rho
};
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let von_mises_draws = circle.sample_n(&mut rng, n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let cauchy_draws = wrapped.sample_n(&mut rng, n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let nf = n as f64;
// Sample range, mean cosine C̄_μ along the mean direction (μ = 0) and its z, R̄, and √(ρ² + (1 − ρ²)/n).
let summarize = |theta: &[f64], population: f64, variance: f64| {
    let low = theta
        .iter()
        .fold(f64::INFINITY, |a, &t| RealScalar::min(a, t));
    let high = theta
        .iter()
        .fold(f64::NEG_INFINITY, |a, &t| RealScalar::max(a, t));
    let c = theta.iter().map(|&t| RealScalar::cos(t)).sum::<f64>() / nf;
    let s = theta.iter().map(|&t| RealScalar::sin(t)).sum::<f64>() / nf;
    let z = (c - population) / RealScalar::sqrt(variance / nf);
    let biased =
        RealScalar::sqrt(population * population + (1.0 - population * population) / nf);
    (low, high, c, z, RealScalar::hypot(c, s), biased)
};
let vm = summarize(&von_mises_draws, von_mises_rho, von_mises_variance);
let wc = summarize(&cauchy_draws, rho, (1.0 - rho * rho) / 2.0);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({"name": "VonMises", "points": periodic}),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "VonMisesLine",
            "points": restricted,
            "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "WrappedCauchy",
            "points": cauchy,
            "style": {"lineType": "dotted"}
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Angle θ [rad]",
        "yLabel": "Probability density [1/rad]",
        "xMin": -pi,
        "xMax": 2.0 * pi,
        "annotations": [
            {"kind": "range", "axis": "x", "from": -pi, "to": pi, "label": "von Mises principal interval"},
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": 2.0 * pi, "label": "Wrapped Cauchy right endpoint"}
        ]
    })
);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let interval_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Law" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample minimum [rad]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Sample maximum [rad]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "mean() [rad]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    {
        "key": "4",
        "label": "mean_direction() [rad]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": fixed4,
        "nullText": "Undefined"
    }
]);
// mean() gives the real-valued mean on the principal interval; mean_direction() gives the circular mean direction (undefined for a uniform distribution).
let vm_mean = circle.mean().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let wc_mean = wrapped.mean().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (vm_direction, wc_direction) =
    (circle.mean_direction().ok(), wrapped.mean_direction().ok());
emit_table_with_columns!(
    interval_columns,
    vec![
        ("VonMises", vm.0, vm.1, vm_mean, vm_direction),
        ("WrappedCauchy", wc.0, wc.1, wc_mean, wc_direction),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "intervals" })
);
let resultant_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Law" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "mean_resultant_length() ρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Mean cosine C̄_μ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "z = (C̄_μ − ρ)/SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "R̄ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "√(ρ² + (1 − ρ²)/n) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    resultant_columns,
    vec![
        ("VonMises", von_mises_rho, vm.2, vm.3, vm.4, vm.5),
        ("WrappedCauchy", rho, wc.2, wc.3, wc.4, wc.5),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "resultant" })
);
```

With the defaults ($\kappa = 2$, $\rho = 0.5$, $n = 4\,000$, seed 17), von Mises samples lie in $[-2.983, 3.078]$ and wrapped Cauchy samples in $[9.7 \times 10^{-5}, 6.2828]$, both within their respective principal intervals.
`mean()` of `WrappedCauchy` returns $\pi \approx 3.1416$, differing by $\pi$ from the circular mean direction 0 returned by `mean_direction()`.
The mean cosine $\bar{C}_\mu$ is 0.6984 for von Mises ($\rho = I_1(2)/I_0(2) = 0.6978$, $z = 0.09$) and 0.4899 for wrapped Cauchy ($\rho = 0.5$, $z = -1.04$).
Changing the seed to 1 and 1000 gives $z$ in the range $-1.16$ to $1.64$.

At $\kappa = 0$ and $\rho = 0$, both laws become uniform and `mean_direction()` is undefined.
Although the population $\rho$ is 0, $\bar{R}$ for $n = 4\,000$ is 0.021 and 0.015, of order $\sqrt{1/n} \approx 0.016$.
At $n = 20$, $\bar{R}$ is 0.263 and 0.105, showing that even uniform samples produce values of order $\sqrt{1/20} \approx 0.22$.
Here, $z$ of $\bar{C}_\mu$ is $-0.88$ and $-0.57$, consistent with $\rho = 0$.

## Summary

`VonMises` and `VonMisesLine` represent the same von Mises distribution: the former returns a periodic circular density, and the latter a real-line density that is 0 outside the principal interval $[\mu - \pi, \mu + \pi]$.
The principal interval of `WrappedCauchy` is $[\mu, \mu + 2\pi]$, so `mean()` returns $\mu + \pi$; obtain the circular mean direction with `mean_direction()`.
The mean resultant length $\rho$ is a population value; the sample $\bar{R}$ is biased upward by $\mathbb{E}[\bar{R}^2] = \rho^2 + (1 - \rho^2)/n$, so account for this bias in small samples.
To check a generator or its parameters without bias, compare the mean cosine $\bar{C}_\mu$ along the mean direction with $\rho$ using its standard error.
