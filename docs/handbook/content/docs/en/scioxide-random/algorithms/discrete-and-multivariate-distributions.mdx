Count laws return integers; rounding floating-point samples to integers shifts their probability masses.
This page explains how `scioxide-random` generates integer samples, switches methods for binomial and Poisson distributions, and generates other count laws and weighted indices (the alias method).
Laws with finite support handled by tables, such as Poisson binomial, Boltzmann, and noncentral hypergeometric, are covered in [Count laws with finite support](../finite-support-count-laws/); vector-valued laws such as multivariate normal are in [Probability laws for multivariate values, matrices, and groups](../multivariate-and-matrix-laws/).
Rejection and word limits are collected in [Rejection and work limits](oxref:random-algorithms-rejection-limits).

## Integer samples and CountDistribution

Count laws generate samples through `CountDistribution`.
The sample type is `usize` for nonnegative counts and `i64` for signed counts such as `Skellam`, `DiscreteLaplace`, and `DiscreteUniform`.
The three forms are `sample` (one sample), `sample_n` (owned output), and `sample_n_into` (caller-owned output, `SamplingPolicy`, and workspace).
Sampling uses integer uniform words (`next_uniform_u64`, or `next_uniform_u32` for uniform integer selection with width at most $2^{32}$), independently of the law's scalar type, so `f32` and `f64` laws return identical samples for identical parameter values and seeds.
Unrepresentable counts give errors such as `NotImplemented` instead of being truncated.
Probability mass and CDF queries use `Mass`, `Cdf`, and `Survival` with exact integer arguments; `cdf(k)` includes the atom at $k$, while `sf(k)` excludes it.

Only laws requiring preparation of constants have separate samplers.
`Binomial::prepare_sampler` returns `BinomialSampler`, `Poisson::prepare_sampler` returns `PoissonSampler`, and `Skellam::prepare_sampler` returns `SkellamSampler`, the difference of two Poisson samplers.
Preparation consumes no random numbers and allocates no storage.
These three laws also implement `CountDistribution`, preparing a sampler before each draw; for repeated individual draws, prepare the sampler once and reuse it.
Other count laws complete preparation at construction and implement `CountDistribution` themselves.

## Three binomial methods

Sampling $X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, p)$ uses $p' = \min(p, 1 - p)$, reflecting to $n - X$ if $p > 1/2$.
$n - X$ follows $\mathrm{Binomial}(n, 1 - p)$, so reflection preserves the law.
Both methods use the smaller probability because the stability of BINV's recurrence and the BTPE constants assume $p \le 1/2$.
The method is selected once during preparation.

| Condition | Method | Word limit per sample |
|---|---|---|
| $n \le 16$ | Perform $n$ Bernoulli trials directly | 272 |
| $n p' < 10$ | Inverse transform (BINV) | 18 |
| Otherwise | BTPE (Kachitvichyanukul–Schmeiser transformed rejection) | 2,048 |

BINV is inverse-transform sampling that sums masses from small $k$ upward and returns the $k$ at which the sum exceeds uniform $U$.
First, a Bernoulli trial with $P(X > 0) = 1 - (1 - p')^n$ determines whether $X = 0$.
The logarithm of $(1 - p')^n = \exp(n\ln(1 - p'))$ uses `ln_1p`, and $P(X > 0)$ uses `expm1`, preventing $P(X > 0)$ from rounding to 0 even for very small $np'$.
If $X > 0$, begin at conditional probability $P(X = 1 \mid X > 0)$ and form subsequent terms with the probability-mass ratio

$$
\frac{P(k+1)}{P(k)} = \frac{n - k}{k + 1}\cdot\frac{p'}{1 - p'}
$$

so no factorials or powers are evaluated inside the loop.
For $X > 0$, the number of stages is $X$ itself, so its expectation is proportional to the mean $np'$ and is short when restricted to $np' < 10$.

BTPE proposes candidates from an envelope bounding the probability masses, consisting of a triangle around the mode, parallelograms on both sides, and exponentially decreasing tails, accepting by probability-mass ratios.
The triangular region lies below the probability masses, so candidates there are accepted without mass evaluation.
Other candidates are tested near the mode (distance $d \le 20$) using products of adjacent mass ratios, and farther away using log-masses with Loader's Stirling correction.
The normal-approximation squeeze test is used only in its valid range $20 < d < npq/2 - 1$; outside it, log-mass ratios are compared directly.
At most 1,024 candidates are allowed before acceptance; exceeding this gives an error.
Nondegenerate laws can be prepared for $n \le \min(\texttt{usize::MAX}, 2^{52} - 1)$; beyond this, the error is `NotImplemented`.

Quantile `quantile` chooses a starting point using a normal approximation with a Cornish–Fisher correction, then uses doubling and discrete bisection to return the smallest $k$ satisfying $\mathrm{CDF}(k) \ge p$.
The tests use the law's own tail probabilities, so the approximation only sets the starting point and never determines the returned count.

## Poisson and Skellam distributions

For rate $\lambda < 12$, `PoissonSampler` uses an inverse transform of the same form as BINV.
A Bernoulli trial with $P(X > 0) = 1 - e^{-\lambda}$ first determines whether the count is 0; if $X > 0$, it advances the conditional CDF from $P(X = 1 \mid X > 0)$ using ratio $P(k+1)/P(k) = \lambda/(k+1)$.
For $\lambda \ge 12$ it uses Hörmann's PTRS (transformed rejection with a squeeze).
PTRS envelope constants are valid for $\lambda \ge 10$, so the switching point 12 lies within this range.
Word limits are 18 for inverse transform and 2,048 for PTRS; rate 0 consumes no words.
Inverse-transform stages and rejection candidates both stop at 1,024.
`Poisson::new(lambda)` uses default `LawOptions`, while `Poisson::with_options(lambda, options)` accepts settings.
`prepare_sampler` returns `NotImplemented` for $\lambda > \min(\texttt{usize::MAX}/2, 2^{51})$; batched sampler generation respects the law's `max_bytes` limit.

`SkellamSampler` returns the difference $X - Y$ of two Poisson samples, sharing the law's `sample_word_budget` between them.
If the difference does not fit in `i64`, the error is `NotImplemented`.

## Signed count laws

`Skellam`, `DiscreteLaplace`, and `DiscreteUniform` are count laws allowing negative values, with `i64` samples and query arguments.
All three implement `Mass`, `LogMass`, `Cdf`, `Survival`, `LogCdf`, `LogSurvival`, `Quantile`, `InverseSurvival`, `Mean`, and `Variance` for `i64` counts; `cdf(k)` includes the atom at $k$, while `sf(k)` excludes it.
All three implement `CountDistribution` themselves; `Skellam` generates samples using a `SkellamSampler` prepared by `prepare_sampler()` on each call.
`Skellam` and `DiscreteLaplace` return `NotImplemented` for queries whose count magnitude exceeds $2^{53}$.

| Law | Constructor | Support | Probability mass | Mean | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Skellam` | `new(mu1, mu2)`, $\mu_1, \mu_2 \ge 0$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ (one-sided if one rate is 0, $\{0\}$ if both are 0) | $e^{-(\mu_1 + \mu_2)}(\mu_1/\mu_2)^{k/2} I_{\lvert k \rvert}(2\sqrt{\mu_1\mu_2})$ | $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ | $\mu_1 + \mu_2$ |
| `DiscreteLaplace` | `new(a)`, $a > 0$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ | $\tanh(a/2)\,e^{-a\lvert k \rvert}$ | 0 | $2e^{-a}/(1 - e^{-a})^2$ |
| `DiscreteUniform` | `new(low, high)`, `low < high`（`i64`） | $\{\text{low}, \dots, \text{high} - 1\}$ | $1/w$, $w = \text{high} - \text{low}$ | $(\text{low} + \text{high} - 1)/2$ | $(w^2 - 1)/12$ |

Every constructor uses default `LawOptions`; `with_options` takes settings after the same parameters to change accuracy and limits.

`Skellam` is the law of the difference $X - Y$ of independent $\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu_1)$ and $\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu_2)$; $I_\nu$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind.
Mass is evaluated with the exponentially scaled Bessel function $e^{-x}I_{\lvert k \rvert}(x)$ ($x = 2\sqrt{\mu_1\mu_2}$), combining exponential terms into $-(\sqrt{\mu_1} - \sqrt{\mu_2})^2$ to avoid overflow even at large rates.
In far tails where the Bessel value falls to 0, evaluation switches to a Poisson convolution sum.
The CDF and upper-tail probability sum a convolution over the Poisson variable $B$ with the smaller rate.
Summation proceeds outward from the mode of $B$ and stops when the unsummed mass of $B$ (an upper bound on remaining terms) is below the accumulated sum times the relative tolerance.
One zero rate is treated exactly as a Poisson distribution (or its sign reversal); both zero rates give a point mass at 0.

`DiscreteLaplace` is a symmetric discrete exponential distribution on $\mathbb{Z}$; its normalizing constant follows from $\sum_k e^{-a\lvert k \rvert} = (1 + e^{-a})/(1 - e^{-a})$ as $\tanh(a/2)$.
The tail away from 0 is a geometric series; evaluate the closed form $P(K > k) = e^{-a(k+1)}/(1 + e^{-a})$ for $k \ge 0$ and $P(K \le k) = e^{-a\lvert k \rvert}/(1 + e^{-a})$ for $k < 0$ logarithmically, forming the tail nearer 0 as its complement.
Mathematically, this is the difference of two independent identically distributed geometric variables, but the implementation does not use that construction.

`DiscreteUniform` stores width $w$ as `u64`, allowing intervals spanning the full `i64` range.
The CDF directly computes the smaller of the integer-count ratio $(k - \text{low} + 1)/w$ and its complement, avoiding subtraction for the complement.
Quantiles extract the binary64 significand and exponent of probability $p$, compute $p \cdot w$ exactly using a 128-bit integer product, and round upward, so rounding cannot shift to an adjacent integer even for $w > 2^{53}$.
Mass returns $-\ln w$ in binary64, including rounding of $w$ for $w > 2^{53}$.

Sampling differs by law.

- `SkellamSampler` draws $X$ and $Y$ from two prepared `PoissonSampler` instances and returns their difference; both share word and work limits.
- `DiscreteLaplace` inverts the CDF for open-interval uniform $U$, searching integers by doubling and bisection using closed-form tails.
  The search count is bounded by `work_budget` and increases as $\lvert k \rvert$ grows.
- `DiscreteUniform` uses Lemire's nearly divisionless rejection method to generate a uniform integer on $[0, w)$ and adds `low`.
  For $w \le 2^{32}$ it uses 32-bit uniform words, never rounding floating-point coordinates to integers.

Quantiles `quantile` and `isf` of `Skellam` and `DiscreteLaplace` use the same doubling and bisection on tail values; only `DiscreteUniform` quantiles have an integer-arithmetic closed form.

## Other count laws

The default sampler exactly inverts the lower-tail probability (upper-tail probability if $U > 1/2$) for open-interval uniform $U$.
Laws with a known upper support bound bisect $[\text{lower bound}, \text{upper bound}]$ directly; laws with no upper bound bisect a bracket expanded by doubling.
Tail evaluations and search iterations count against the law's `work_budget`.
Laws with cheaper exact constructions use the following methods.

| Law | Sampling method |
|---|---|
| `Geometric` | $K = \lfloor \ln U / \ln q \rfloor + 1$ ($\ln q$ prepared by `ln_1p(-p)`) |
| `NegativeBinomial` | Construct $\Lambda \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(r, q/p)$ using logarithm $\ln \Lambda$, then draw $\mathrm{Poisson}(\Lambda)$. If $\Lambda$ underflows, return count 0 |
| `BetaBinomial` | For at most 128 trials when either $\min(\alpha, \beta) < 1$ or $\max(\alpha, \beta) > 1024$ (including U and J shapes), use Pólya sequential conditional probabilities; otherwise form the smaller of $p$ and $1 - p$ from beta log-odds, draw a binomial, and reflect to $n - X$ when needed |
| `BetaNegativeBinomial` | Draw a negative binomial while retaining beta log-odds and the Gamma–Poisson log-rate |
| `LogSeries` | Kemp's two-uniform method (LK) |
| `YuleSimon` | Geometric distribution with success probability $e^{-E/\rho}$ ($E \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1)$) |
| `Zipf` | Devroye rejection (acceptance tests evaluated logarithmically) |
| `Zipfian` | Rejection-inversion with constant storage |
| `Hypergeometric` | Stadlober HRUA (ratio-of-uniforms with an envelope covering the full support). If the smaller of the draw count and (population − draw count) is below 10, draw without replacement one at a time. Storage is independent of support size |

## Weighted indices and the alias method

`WeightedSampler::new(weights)` builds a Walker–Vose alias table from nonnegative weights $w_1, \dots, w_n$ (at least one positive).
Weights are divided by their maximum and normalized using compensated summation, giving $q_i = n w_i / \sum_j w_j$.
The mean of $q_i$ is 1 apart from rounding; dividing by the maximum avoids sum overflow even for weights all equal to `f32::MAX`.

The table stores one retention probability $F_i$ and one alias $A_i$ per column $i$.
Vose's construction pairs a column $s$ with $q_s < 1$ and a column $l$ with $q_l \ge 1$, sets $F_s = q_s$ and $A_s = l$, and reduces the remaining mass of $l$ to $q_l \leftarrow q_l - (1 - q_s)$.
Each pairing determines one column, so construction takes $O(n)$ time and storage.
Remaining columns differ from 1 only through rounding error and are treated as $F_i = 1$.

A draw chooses column $i$ uniformly using `uniform_index` (a 32-bit uniform word for at most $2^{32}$ columns), reads one $u$ with `next_float`, and returns $i$ if $u < F_i$, otherwise $A_i$.
The probability of returning index $j$ is the sum of retention in its own column and contributions from columns with alias $j$:

$$
\frac{1}{n}\Bigl(F_j + \sum_{i : A_i = j}(1 - F_i)\Bigr) = \frac{q_j}{n} = \frac{w_j}{\sum_k w_k}
$$


Expected time is $O(1)$ with no storage allocation.
A zero-weight index has retention probability 0 and is never an alias, so it is never returned.

## Range of exactly supported counts

The magnitude limit for exact count arithmetic is $2^{53}$, also applying to signed counts and integer inversion.
The exception is `DiscreteUniform`, whose `u64` width and 128-bit integer-product quantiles support the full `i64` range.
For methods that floor continuous candidates, binomial trial counts and Poisson candidates are further restricted to values below $2^{52}$.
Counts exceeding the limit return `NotImplemented` instead of being truncated.

## Comparing binomial sampler counts with probability masses

The following cell generates $m$ samples from $\mathrm{Binomial}(n, p)$ using `BinomialSampler` and compares the relative frequency at each $k$ with `pmf(k)`.
The inputs are trial count $n$, success probability $p$, sample size $m$, and SplitMix64 seed.
Pearson's $X^2$ is computed after merging adjacent $k$ into bins with expected counts at least 5, alongside the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of chi-squared with the resulting degrees of freedom.
The table's first column names the method obtained by manually applying the preparation rules (direct for $n \le 16$, BINV for $n\min(p, 1 - p) < 10$, BTPE otherwise).
The plot band is probability mass $\pm 2$ SE ($\mathrm{SE} = \sqrt{P(k)(1 - P(k))/m}$), the dashed steps are probability masses, and the markers are relative frequencies.

```rust
//| id: random-binomial-sampler-fit
//| ref: random-binomial-sampler-fit
//| title: "Compare binomial sampler frequencies with probability masses"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   trials: {"type":"range","label":"Trial count n [trials]","description":"Switches to the direct method for n ≤ 16.","min":4,"max":200,"step":4,"value":40}
//|   success_probability: {"type":"range","label":"Success probability p [-]","description":"Switches to BINV for n min(p, 1 − p) < 10.","min":0.02,"max":0.98,"step":0.02,"value":0.3}
//|   draws: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size m [count]","description":"Changes the standard error of relative frequencies as 1/√m.","min":2000,"max":20000,"step":2000,"value":10000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"SplitMix64 seed [-]","description":"Observe sample-to-sample fluctuations in X² and standardized errors.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"binv","label":"p = 0.02（BINV）","description":"np' = 0.8 < 10, so inverse-transform BINV is used.","inputs":{"success_probability":0.02}}
//|   - {"id":"direct","label":"n = 4 (direct)","description":"n ≤ 16, so 4 Bernoulli trials are performed directly.","inputs":{"trials":4}}
//|   - {"id":"reflected","label":"n = 200, p = 0.98 (reflected BINV)","description":"p' = 0.02 and np' = 4 < 10, so BINV samples are reflected to n − X.","inputs":{"trials":200,"success_probability":0.98}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"BTPE"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0,"max":60}}
//|   - case: binv
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"BINV"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: direct
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Direct"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: reflected
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"BINV"}
//|       - {"artifact":"fit","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    Binomial, ChiSquared, CountDistribution, Mass, Quantile, SeedableRng, SplitMix64,
};

let n = trials.round() as usize;
let p = success_probability;
let m = draws.round() as usize;
let mf = m as f64;
let law = Binomial::new(n, p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sampler = law.prepare_sampler().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let samples = sampler.sample_n(&mut rng, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut counts = vec![0.0_f64; n + 1];
for &k in &samples {
    counts[k] += 1.0;
}
let pmf = (0..=n)
    .map(|k| law.pmf(k))
    .collect::<Result<Vec<f64>, _>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Merge adjacent k until expected count m·P(k) reaches 5, putting any remainder in the last bin.
let (mut bins, mut expected, mut observed) = (Vec::new(), 0.0, 0.0);
for k in 0..=n {
    expected += mf * pmf[k];
    observed += counts[k];
    if expected >= 5.0 {
        bins.push((observed, expected));
        (expected, observed) = (0.0, 0.0);
    }
}
if let Some(last) = bins.last_mut() {
    (last.0, last.1) = (last.0 + observed, last.1 + expected);
}
let chi_square: f64 = bins.iter().map(|(o, e)| (o - e) * (o - e) / e).sum();
let df = bins.len().saturating_sub(1).max(1);
let reference = ChiSquared::new(df as f64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let low = reference.quantile(0.025).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let high = reference.quantile(0.975).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Manually apply the same preparation rules to p' = min(p, 1 − p) and n.
let working = RealScalar::min(p, 1.0 - p);
let method = if n <= 16 {
    "Direct"
} else if n as f64 * working < 10.0 {
    "BINV"
} else {
    "BTPE"
};
let mean = samples.iter().sum::<usize>() as f64 / mf;
let expected_mean = n as f64 * p;
let standard_error = RealScalar::sqrt(expected_mean * (1.0 - p) / mf);
let (mut band, mut theory, mut observed_points) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for k in 0..=n {
    let x = k as f64;
    let se = RealScalar::sqrt(pmf[k] * (1.0 - pmf[k]) / mf);
    band.push([x, pmf[k], pmf[k] - 2.0 * se, pmf[k] + 2.0 * se]);
    theory.push([x, pmf[k]]);
    observed_points.push([x, counts[k] / mf]);
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Success count k [count]",
        "yLabel": "Probability / relative frequency [-]",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "layers": [
            {"id": "band", "kind": "band", "name": "Probability mass ± 2 SE", "points": band},
            {
                "id": "pmf",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Probability mass pmf(k)",
                "style": {"lineType": "dashed", "step": "middle"},
                "points": theory
            },
            {"id": "sample", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Sample relative frequency", "points": observed_points}
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "frequencies" })
);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Sampling method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample mean [count]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "np [count]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "z = (sample mean − np)/SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Pearson X² [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Degrees of freedom", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "X² 2.5% quantile [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "X² 97.5% quantile [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        method,
        mean,
        expected_mean,
        (mean - expected_mean) / standard_error,
        chi_square,
        df,
        low,
        high
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "fit" })
);
```

Relative frequencies fluctuate around $\mathrm{pmf}(k)$ with standard error of order $\sqrt{\mathrm{pmf}(k)(1 - \mathrm{pmf}(k))/m}$; each point falls outside the band with probability about 4.6%.
The defaults ($n = 40$, $p = 0.3$, $m = 10\,000$, seed 5) use BTPE, giving $X^2 = 18.26$ (19 degrees of freedom, 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles 8.91 and 32.85), sample mean $12.0612$, and standardized error $2.11$.
The probability of $|z| > 2$ is approximately 4.6%, so this single run is not evidence of bias.
Changing the seed to 1 and 1000 with the same settings gives standardized errors $1.18$ and $0.93$ and $X^2$ values $19.75$ and $17.26$.
Reducing to $p = 0.02$ switches to BINV, while $n = 4$ switches to the direct method, giving $X^2$ values $6.41$ (5 degrees of freedom) and $2.10$ (4 degrees of freedom), respectively.
For $n = 200$ and $p = 0.98$, $p' = 0.02$ gives $np' = 4 < 10$, generating BINV samples reflected to $n - X$ ($X^2 = 9.45$, 12 degrees of freedom).
All are statistical checks: judge a discrepancy by whether it recurs across seeds, rather than by one seed alone.

## Summary

Count laws generate integer samples through the three forms of `CountDistribution`, independently of the law's scalar type, and return errors for unrepresentable counts rather than truncating them.
Binomial sampling selects direct, BINV, or BTPE using $n$ and $n\min(p, 1-p)$; Poisson sampling switches between inverse transform and PTRS at rate 12, each with a word limit per sample.
For repeated individual draws, prepare a sampler once using `prepare_sampler`; inspect samples through theoretical probability masses and SE, or $X^2$ and its quantiles.
Count laws without specialized constructions sample through exact CDF inversion, allowing search iterations to be bounded using `work_budget`.
`WeightedSampler` uses a Walker–Vose table normalized after division by the maximum, drawing through a uniform column selection and a single comparison.
