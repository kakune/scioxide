Distributions of maxima and exceedances above high thresholds often have limits determined solely by the underlying distribution's tail.
This page states the limit theorems with their conditions and explains the definitions and capabilities of the limiting `GeneralizedExtremeValue` and `GeneralizedPareto` laws and laws with power-law tail weights (Pareto, Student t, Cauchy, and stable distributions).
The two limiting laws use opposite sign conventions for their shape parameters, so check this correspondence first.
The cell constructs a generalized extreme-value distribution from an extreme-value index and compares samples, return levels, and means with closed forms.
The approach of threshold exceedances to a generalized Pareto distribution is examined in [Threshold exceedances and the generalized Pareto approximation](../threshold-exceedances/).

## Extreme-value limit theorems

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be independent random variables with CDF $F$, and set $M_n = \max(X_1, \dots, X_n)$ and $x_F = \sup\{x : F(x) < 1\}$ (the right endpoint, possibly infinite).

The Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem states the following.
If constants $a_n > 0$ and $b_n$ exist such that the distribution of $(M_n - b_n)/a_n$ converges to a nondegenerate distribution $G$, then, up to location and scale, $G$ is the generalized extreme-value distribution

$$
G_\xi(x) = \exp\bigl(-(1 + \xi x)^{-1/\xi}\bigr), \qquad 1 + \xi x > 0
$$

with limiting form $\exp(-e^{-x})$ at $\xi = 0$.
In this case, $F$ is said to lie in the domain of attraction of $G_\xi$.
Such $a_n$ and $b_n$ do not exist for every $F$.
Among integer-valued laws, Poisson and geometric distributions, whose ratio $(1 - F(n))/(1 - F(n - 1))$ does not approach 1, have no nondegenerate limit.
Integer-valued laws with power-law tails (such as Zipf), however, lie in the Fréchet domain of attraction.

The Pickands–Balkema–de Haan theorem concerns the conditional distribution of the excess $X - u$ above threshold $u$.
Membership of $F$ in the domain of attraction of $G_\xi$ is equivalent to the existence of a positive function $\sigma(u)$ such that

$$
\lim_{u \to x_F}\ \sup_{0 \le y < x_F - u} \Bigl\lvert P(X - u \le y \mid X > u) - H_\xi\bigl(y/\sigma(u)\bigr) \Bigr\rvert = 0, \qquad H_\xi(y) = 1 - (1 + \xi y)^{-1/\xi}
$$


$H_\xi$ is the generalized Pareto distribution; scale $\sigma(u)$ varies with the threshold.
Only the generalized Pareto distribution appears as a threshold-exceedance limit.
Exceedances of `Pareto` agree with a generalized Pareto distribution ($\xi = 1/b$, $\sigma(u) = u/b$) at every threshold; `TruncatedPareto` is a different model with bounded support.

The sign of extreme-value index $\xi$ divides domains of attraction into three types.
A slowly varying function $L$ satisfies $L(tx)/L(x) \to 1$ ($x \to \infty$) for every $t > 0$.

| Type | $\xi$ | Tail of the underlying distribution | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fréchet type | $\xi > 0$ | $1 - F(x) = x^{-1/\xi}L(x)$. Moment of order $r$ is finite only for $r < 1/\xi$ | Pareto ($\xi = 1/b$), Student t ($\xi = 1/\nu$), Cauchy ($\xi = 1$), right tail of a stable distribution with $\alpha < 2$ and $\beta > -1$ ($\xi = 1/\alpha$), `InverseWeibull` ($\xi = 1/c$) |
| Gumbel type | $\xi = 0$ | Tail decreases faster than any power (the right endpoint may be finite or infinite) | Normal, exponential, gamma, lognormal, `WeibullMin` |
| Weibull type | $\xi < 0$ | Finite right endpoint $x_F$, with $1 - F(x_F - 1/t) = t^{1/\xi}L(t)$ | Uniform ($\xi = -1$), beta |

The definition and capabilities of `InverseWeibull` ($F(x) = \exp(-x^{-c})$) are in [Family definitions and supports](oxref:random-families-definitions).

## Shape-parameter sign conventions

`GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(c)` uses shape parameter $c = -\xi$.
The CDF is

$$
F(x) = \exp\bigl(-(1 - cx)^{1/c}\bigr), \qquad 1 - cx > 0
$$

where the support is $(-\infty, 1/c]$ for $c > 0$ (Weibull type) and $[1/c, \infty)$ for $c < 0$ (Fréchet type); $c = 0$ is the same law as `GumbelRight`.
`GeneralizedPareto::new(c)` uses the sign $c = \xi$, with upper-tail probability $S(x) = (1 + cx)^{-1/c}$.
Fréchet-type heavy tails use $c < 0$ in `GeneralizedExtremeValue` and $c > 0$ in `GeneralizedPareto`.

| Extreme-value index | Value passed to `GeneralizedExtremeValue::new` | Value passed to `GeneralizedPareto::new` |
|---|---|---|
| $\xi = 0.3$ (Fréchet type) | $-0.3$ | $0.3$ |
| $\xi = 0$ (Gumbel type) | $0$ | $0$ (unit exponential distribution) |
| $\xi = -0.3$ (Weibull type) | $0.3$ (upper support endpoint $1/0.3$) | $-0.3$ (upper support endpoint $1/0.3$) |

## Law definitions and capabilities

`Cauchy` takes location and scale in its constructor; other laws are standard forms with location 0 and scale 1, so add location and scale using `Affine::new(law, μ, σ)`.
For laws marked with `with_options` in the table, `new` uses default `LawOptions` and `with_options` takes settings after the parameters.

| Constructor | Parameter domain | Support | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(c)` | $c$ finite; $1/c$ representable when $c \ne 0$ | $(-\infty, 1/c]$ for $c > 0$, $[1/c, \infty)$ for $c < 0$ | $F(x) = \exp\bigl(-(1 - cx)^{1/c}\bigr)$ |
| `GumbelRight::new()` | None | $\mathbb{R}$ | $F(x) = \exp(-e^{-x})$. Left-skewed Gumbel is `Affine::new(law, 0.0, -1.0)` |
| `GeneralizedPareto::new(c)` | $c$ finite ($-1/c$ representable when $c < 0$) | $[0, \infty)$ for $c \ge 0$, $[0, -1/c]$ for $c < 0$ | $S(x) = (1 + cx)^{-1/c}$; $c = 0$ is $e^{-x}$ |
| `Pareto::new(b)` | $b > 0$ | $[1, \infty)$ | $S(x) = x^{-b}$. `Affine::new(law, -1.0, 1.0)` is Lomax |
| `TruncatedPareto::new(b, c)` | $b \ne 0$ finite, $c > 1$, $\lvert b \rvert \ln c$ representable | $[1, c]$ | $f(x) \propto x^{-b-1}$, $F(x) = (1 - x^{-b})/(1 - c^{-b})$ |
| `StudentT::new(nu)` (`with_options`) | $\nu > 0$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $t$ distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom. Tail probabilities decrease in proportion to $\lvert x \rvert^{-\nu}$ |
| `Cauchy::new(location, scale)` | Finite location, scale $> 0$ | $\mathbb{R}$ | $f(x) = 1/\bigl(\pi s(1 + ((x - \ell)/s)^2)\bigr)$ |
| `Kappa4::new(h, k)` (`with_options`) | $h$ and $k$ finite, finite support endpoints representable in floating point | Upper endpoint $1/k$ for $k > 0$. Lower endpoint $(1 - h^{-k})/k$ for $h > 0$ ($\ln h$ for $k = 0$), $1/k$ for $h \le 0$ and $k < 0$ | $F(x) = \bigl(1 - h(1 - kx)^{1/k}\bigr)^{1/h}$. $h = 0$ is `GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(k)`; $h = 1$ is `GeneralizedPareto::new(-k)` |
| `Gompertz::new(shape)` (`with_options`) | $c > 0$ | $[0, \infty)$ | $S(x) = \exp\bigl(-c(e^x - 1)\bigr)$. Left-skewed Gumbel conditioned on $x \ge 0$ |
| `LevyStable::new(alpha, beta)` (`with_options`) | $0 < \alpha \le 2$, $\lvert\beta\rvert \le 1$ | $\mathbb{R}$ (a half-line for $\alpha < 1$ and $\lvert\beta\rvert = 1$) | Stable distribution in Nolan's S1 parameterization. For $\alpha < 2$, probabilities decrease in proportion to $\lvert x \rvert^{-\alpha}$ in the right tail if $\beta > -1$ and the left tail if $\beta < 1$. The tail opposite $\beta = \pm 1$ is a support endpoint for $\alpha < 1$ and decreases faster than any power for $1 \le \alpha < 2$ |

`TruncatedPareto` has a power density restricted to $[1, c]$; for $b < -1$ its density increases to the right.
It is a bounded-support power-law model, used when an observational upper limit is known.
`Kappa4` is the standard form of Hosking's four-parameter kappa distribution, including generalized extreme-value, generalized Pareto, and generalized logistic ($h = -1$) in one expression.
`Gompertz` is a lifetime law obtained by restricting the left-skewed Gumbel distribution $S(x) = \exp(-e^{x - \mu})$, the limit for minima, to $x \ge 0$ and setting $c = e^{-\mu}$.
For `LevyStable`, $\alpha = 2$ is normal with variance 2 and $(\alpha, \beta) = (1, 0)$ is standard Cauchy.

Quantile quadrature in the next table integrates $\mathrm{E}[X^r] = \int_0^1 Q(u)^r\,du$ within the tolerances and work limit of `LawOptions`; `Kappa4` handles signs by splitting the integral at $F(0)$.

| Law | Mean | Variance | Sampling | Quantile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `GeneralizedExtremeValue` | $c > -1$: $(1 - \Gamma(1 + c))/c$ ($\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}$ at $c = 0$) | $c > -1/2$: $\bigl(\Gamma(1 + 2c) - \Gamma(1 + c)^2\bigr)/c^2$ | Inverse transform | Closed form |
| `GumbelRight` | $\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}$ | $\pi^2/6$ | $-\ln(-\ln U)$ | Closed form |
| `GeneralizedPareto` | $c < 1$: $1/(1 - c)$ | $c < 1/2$: $1/\bigl((1 - c)^2(1 - 2c)\bigr)$ | Inverse transform | Closed form |
| `Pareto` | $b > 1$: $b/(b - 1)$ | $b > 2$: $b/\bigl((b - 1)^2(b - 2)\bigr)$ | $e^{E/b}$ ($E$ standard exponential) | Closed form |
| `TruncatedPareto` | Not implemented | Not implemented | Inverse transform | Closed form |
| `StudentT` | $\nu > 1$: 0 | $\nu > 2$: $\nu/(\nu - 2)$ | $Z/\sqrt{\chi^2_\nu/\nu}$ | Inverse incomplete beta function. For $\nu \ge 10^6$, Cornish–Fisher expansion through $\nu^{-4}$ |
| `Cauchy` | Not implemented | Not implemented | Inverse transform | Closed form |
| `Kappa4` | Quantile quadrature. Exists only for orders $r < -1/k$ when $k < 0$ and $r < -1/(hk)$ when $h < 0 < k$ | Same | Inverse transform | Closed form |
| `Gompertz` | Quantile quadrature | Same | Inverse transform | Closed form $\ln\bigl(1 - \ln(1 - p)/c\bigr)$ |
| `LevyStable` | $\alpha > 1$: 0 | $\alpha = 2$: 2 | Chambers–Mallows–Stuck method. $\alpha = 2$, $(1, 0)$, and $(1/2, \pm 1)$ use normal, Cauchy, and inverse gamma variates | Closed forms for $\alpha = 2$, $(\alpha, \beta) = (1, 0)$, and $(1/2, \pm 1)$ (Lévy); first-order expansion near the median for $\beta = 0$ (when $\lvert p - 1/2 \rvert < 10^{-3}$ and the relative bound on omitted terms is less than 1/16 of the relative tolerance). Otherwise numerical inversion by Brent's method |

Inverse-transform sampling maps uniform $U$ on the open interval $(0, 1)$ through the quantile function.
Means and variances outside their parameter ranges give `DomainError` with reason `Undefined`.
`Cauchy` and `TruncatedPareto` implement neither `Mean` nor `Variance`, so code calling them does not compile.
Moment handling for Student t and Cauchy is also covered in [Transformations, rejection, and tail control for continuous distributions](oxref:random-continuous-heavy-tails).

## Numerical calculation of tails and quantiles

The generalized extreme-value CDF forms $t(x) = (1 - cx)^{1/c}$ through logarithm $\ln t = \ln(1 - cx)/c$, evaluating $\ln(1 - cx)$ with `ln_1p`.
It connects continuously to $\ln(1 - cx)/c \to -x$ as $c \to 0$, so no switch to the Gumbel formula is needed except at exactly $c = 0$.
For upper-tail probability $S = 1 - e^{-t}$, use $\ln S \approx \ln t$ when $\ln t < \ln\varepsilon$; otherwise evaluate $\ln(1 - e^{-t})$ with the complement formula.

For quantiles, set $\ell = \ln(-\ln p)$ and evaluate

$$
Q(p) = \frac{1 - (-\ln p)^{c}}{c} = -\ell\,\frac{e^{c\ell} - 1}{c\ell}
$$

using `exp_m1`, returning $-\ell$ when $c\ell = 0$.
Inversion from upper-tail probability $q$ (`isf`) forms $-\ln p$ as $-\ln(1 - q)$ using `ln_1p`, avoiding rounding of $1 - q$.
Generalized Pareto uses the same approach, evaluating $\ln S(x) = -\ln(1 + cx)/c$ and $Q(p) = \bigl((1 - p)^{-c} - 1\bigr)/c$ with `ln_1p` and `exp_m1`.
`Kappa4` also evaluates $t = (1 - kx)^{1/k}$ and $(1 - ht)^{1/h}$ with `ln_1p` and `exp_m1`, connecting continuously to the limiting formulas at $h = 0$ and $k = 0$ as $h$ or $k$ approaches 0.
`Gompertz` forms $e^x - 1$ using `exp_m1`, preserving tails for small $x$.

The generalized extreme-value mean $(1 - \Gamma(1 + c))/c$ is evaluated as $-\operatorname{expm1}(\ln\Gamma(1 + c))/c$ after forming $\ln\Gamma(1 + c)$.
For $\lvert c \rvert \le 1/8$, $\ln\Gamma(1 + c) = c\int_0^1 \psi(1 + ct)\,dt$ (where $\psi$ is digamma) is computed by quadrature, avoiding subtraction to form $\Gamma(1 + c) - 1$.
For $\lvert c \rvert < \sqrt{\varepsilon}$, it returns the first-order expansion $\gamma_{\mathrm{E}} - (\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}^2/2 + \pi^2/12)\,c$.
Variance is rewritten as $\Gamma(1 + c)^2\bigl(e^{\Delta} - 1\bigr)/c^2$ with $\Delta = \ln\Gamma(1 + 2c) - 2\ln\Gamma(1 + c)$; for $\lvert c \rvert \le 1/8$, the second difference $\Delta = c^2\int_0^1\int_0^1 \psi'(1 + c(s + t))\,ds\,dt$ (where $\psi'$ is trigamma) is obtained by quadrature with a positive integrand.
Neither subtracts two nearby values, preserving relative accuracy even when $c$ is near 0.

The return level $z_T$ for return period $T$ is the threshold $z_T$ exceeded by a single-period maximum with probability $1/T$, namely $z_T = Q(1 - 1/T)$.
In terms of the extreme-value index, the standard generalized extreme-value return level is

$$
z_T = \frac{\bigl(-\ln(1 - 1/T)\bigr)^{-\xi} - 1}{\xi}
$$

which becomes $-\ln(-\ln(1 - 1/T))$ as $\xi \to 0$.
`isf(1/T)` computes this value directly from the upper-tail probability.

## Generalized extreme-value samples and return levels

The following cell takes extreme-value index $\xi$ and constructs `GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(-ξ)`.
The first table shows $c$ passed to the constructor, the finite support endpoint $1/c$ (omitting the row for $c = 0$), sample minimum and maximum, and the difference between the 100-period return level `isf(0.01)` and the closed form above (independent check).
The second table gives the 95% confidence interval for the sample mean and $z$ for its difference from closed form $(\Gamma(1 - \xi) - 1)/\xi$ ($\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}$ at $\xi = 0$).
Variance is finite over the input range $\lvert\xi\rvert \le 0.4$, allowing the sample mean's standard error to be estimated by $s/\sqrt{n}$.
Samples use inverse-transform sampling, so the KS distance equals that of the uniform random numbers, as described in [Comparing CDFs and moments with samples](oxref:random-families-sample-check); this cell does not compute it.

With the defaults ($\xi = 0.3$, $n = 3\,000$, seed 101), the constructor receives $c = -0.3$ and the lower support endpoint is $1/c = -3.333$.
The sample minimum is $-1.668$ and maximum $34.57$, showing a heavy Fréchet right tail.
The return level is $z_{100} = 9.9169$ at `isf(0.01)`, differing from the closed form by $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ (1 ulp).
For closed-form mean $(\Gamma(0.7) - 1)/0.3 = 0.99352$, the sample mean is 0.99221 ($z = -0.03$).

For $\xi = -0.3$, the constructor receives $c = 0.3$, the upper support endpoint is $1/c = 3.333$, and the sample maximum $3.040$ stays below it.
Calling `new(0.3)` while intending extreme-value index $\xi = 0.3$ creates this Weibull law, with $z_{100}$ equal to $2.4948$ rather than $9.9169$.
At $\xi = 0$, the endpoint row disappears, $z_{100} = -\ln(-\ln 0.99) = 4.6001$, and the sample mean is 0.58043 ($z = 0.14$) compared with mean $\gamma_{\mathrm{E}} = 0.57722$.
For $\xi = 0.4$ and $n = 10\,000$, the sample maximum reaches $109.3$; sample mean 1.16815 differs from closed form 1.22298 by $z = -1.77$.
Heavier tails make the sample mean more sensitive to a few large values, and the distribution of $z$ has heavier tails than a normal distribution.

## Summary

The Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem restricts a nondegenerate limit of normalized maxima, when it exists, to a generalized extreme-value distribution.
Under the same condition, the Pickands–Balkema–de Haan theorem restricts the threshold-exceedance limit to a generalized Pareto distribution with scale $\sigma(u)$.
`GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(c)` uses $c = -\xi$ and `GeneralizedPareto::new(c)` uses $c = \xi$, so change the sign when constructing a law from its extreme-value index and check the convention through support endpoints.
Obtain return levels directly from upper-tail probabilities with `isf(1/T)`; they agree with closed forms within rounding error.
Generalized extreme-value and generalized Pareto means and variances exist only for $\xi < 1$ and $\xi < 1/2$, with `DomainError` outside those ranges; `Cauchy` and `TruncatedPareto` implement neither `Mean` nor `Variance`, so inspect samples using quantiles and return levels.
