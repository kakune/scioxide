F-tests in ANOVA and regression use statistics that follow an F distribution under the null hypothesis.
Under the alternative hypothesis, noncentral distributions arise with noncentrality representing a mean shift, and their tails give power.
This page explains algorithms for evaluating F and noncentral distributions and checks one-way ANOVA power against two independent methods.
It ends by describing how `scioxide-stats` uses these laws to test data and which null distributions `scioxide-random` does not provide.
Shared law contracts and tail reports `TestStatisticResult` are in [Test-statistic laws and mathematical contracts](oxref:random-tests-contracts).

## F and noncentral distributions

`FisherF` is the law of $F = (U_1/d_1)/(U_2/d_2)$ (with independent $U_i \sim \chi^2_{d_i}$).
Setting $z = d_1x/(d_1x + d_2)$ gives $P(F \le x) = I_z(d_1/2, d_2/2)$ (the regularized incomplete beta function), so $\ln z$ and $\ln(1 - z)$ are formed from softplus $\ln(1 + e^{y})$ of $\ln(d_1x/d_2)$, and the upper tail uses the reflected beta $I_{1-z}(d_2/2, d_1/2)$.
Neither side forms $1 - (\text{a number near 1})$.
Quantiles invert the incomplete beta function on the smaller tail, and samples form the ratio of two chi-squared variates logarithmically.

Noncentral distributions are the alternative-hypothesis laws of statistics constructed from shifted normal variates.
Noncentrality measures the size of the mean shift; it is not a scale parameter.

- `NoncentralChiSquared::new(ν, λ)` is the law of $\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}(Z_i + \mu_i)^2$ ($\lambda = \sum \mu_i^2 \ge 0$), equivalent to $\chi^2_{\nu + 2J}$ mixed over $J \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda/2)$.
  Its mean is $\nu + \lambda$ and variance $2(\nu + 2\lambda)$.
  Tails use the Benton–Krishnamoorthy recurrence to generate terms from one incomplete gamma function, summing positive terms in both directions from the $j$ maximizing the weighted term.
  Terms are log-concave in $j$, so the remainder is bounded by a geometric series; summation stops when that bound falls below tolerance.
  Quantiles start from Pearson's (1959) approximation matching the first three cumulants, then use safeguarded Newton iteration in logarithmic coordinates on the smaller log-tail.
  Samples use $(Z + \sqrt{\lambda})^2 + \chi^2_{\nu-1}$ for $\nu > 1$ and a Poisson mixture for $\nu \le 1$.
- `NoncentralF::new(d₁, d₂, λ)` is an F law with a noncentral numerator only; tails sum a Poisson mixture of incomplete beta $I_z(d_1/2 + j, d_2/2)$ using the same recurrence.
  Quantiles start from an F approximation whose numerator is approximated by Patnaik's scaled chi-squared matching the first two moments.
- `NoncentralStudentT::new(ν, δ)` is the law of $(Z + \delta)/\sqrt{U/\nu}$ ($U \sim \chi^2_\nu$); $t < 0$ is reflected to $t \ge 0$ using $T(\delta) = -T(-\delta)$ before applying the Benton–Krishnamoorthy beta series.
  If differencing series loses the required tolerance, as for negative noncentrality or far tails, it integrates $P(T \le x) = \mathbb{E}_S[\Phi(xS - \delta)]$ over the chi scale.
  Quantiles start from a moment-matched normal (a shifted t if no variance exists), then use Newton iteration in $\operatorname{asinh}$ coordinates.

Every noncentral distribution directly uses its central law's evaluation when noncentrality is 0.

## ANOVA power

Consider one-way ANOVA with $k$ groups of $n$ observations each, group means $\mu_i$, and common variance $\sigma^2$.
Under the null hypothesis (all means equal), the F statistic follows $F(d_1, d_2)$ with $d_1 = k - 1$ and $d_2 = k(n - 1)$.
Under the alternative it follows noncentral F with noncentrality $\lambda = n\sum_i(\mu_i - \bar\mu)^2/\sigma^2 = kn f^2$, where $f = \sqrt{\sum_i(\mu_i - \bar\mu)^2/k}/\sigma$ is Cohen's effect size.
For the level-$\alpha$ critical value $c$ ($P(F > c) = \alpha$), the power is $P(F' > c)$.

The following cell computes the critical value with `FisherF::isf` and reports power and its estimated error with `NoncentralF::evaluate_tail`.
There are two independent checks.
One directly sums the Poisson mixture $P(F' > c) = \sum_j e^{-\lambda/2}(\lambda/2)^j/j!\;P\bigl(F(d_1 + 2j, d_2) > c\,d_1/(d_1 + 2j)\bigr)$ using central `FisherF` values (terms through $j \le \lambda/2 + 10\sqrt{\lambda/2} + 20$).
The other is Monte Carlo: generate normal observations from equally spaced group means matching effect size $f$, count the fraction of F statistics exceeding the critical value, and report $z$ standardized by binomial standard error $\sqrt{p(1 - p)/R}$ (where $p$ is theoretical power and $R$ is the replicate count).
At $f = 0$, power equals $\alpha$ and the Monte Carlo fraction represents the type I error probability.

```rust
//| id: random-anova-power
//| ref: random-anova-power
//| title: "Compute one-way ANOVA power with noncentral F and compare Poisson mixtures and Monte Carlo"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-random, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   groups: {"type":"range","label":"Number of groups k [count]","min":2,"max":8,"step":1,"value":4}
//|   per_group: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size per group n [count]","min":3,"max":40,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   effect: {"type":"range","label":"Effect size f [-]","description":"Standard deviation of group means divided by the error standard deviation. Guidelines: 0.1 small, 0.25 medium, 0.4 large.","min":0,"max":0.6,"step":0.05,"value":0.25}
//|   level: {"type":"select","label":"Significance level α","options":["0.05","0.01"],"value":"0.05"}
//|   replications: {"type":"range","label":"Monte Carlo replicates R [count]","min":500,"max":4000,"step":500,"value":2000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"no-effect","label":"f = 0","description":"Under the null hypothesis, power equals α.","inputs":{"effect":0}}
//|   - {"id":"large-design","label":"k = 3, n = 40, f = 0.4","description":"Large effects and samples bring power near 1.","inputs":{"groups":3,"per_group":40,"effect":0.4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"power","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":2.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"power","path":"/rows/0/7","range":{"min":-1e-9,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"simulation","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: no-effect
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"power","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.05,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"simulation","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: large-design
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"power","path":"/rows/0/7","range":{"min":-1e-9,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"simulation","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    Distribution, FisherF, InverseSurvival, NoncentralF, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64,
    Survival,
};

let sig5 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 5 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let (k, n) = (groups.round() as usize, per_group.round() as usize);
let alpha: f64 = level
    .parse()
    .map_err(|e: std::num::ParseFloatError| e.to_string())?;
// Degrees of freedom and noncentrality λ = k n f².
let (d1, d2) = ((k - 1) as f64, (k * (n - 1)) as f64);
let lambda = (k * n) as f64 * effect * effect;
let critical = FisherF::new(d1, d2)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .isf(alpha)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let alternative = NoncentralF::new(d1, d2, lambda).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tail = alternative
    .evaluate_tail(critical, true)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let power = RealScalar::exp(tail.log_probability);
// Independent check 1: directly sum central F tails with Poisson(λ/2) weights.
let half = 0.5 * lambda;
let terms = (half + 10.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(half) + 20.0) as usize;
let (mut weight, mut mixture) = (RealScalar::exp(-half), 0.0);
for j in 0..=terms {
    let a = d1 + 2.0 * j as f64;
    let central = FisherF::new(a, d2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    mixture += weight * central.sf(critical * d1 / a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    weight *= half / (j as f64 + 1.0);
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "d₁", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "d₂", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Noncentrality λ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Critical value c [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Power P(F′ > c) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Estimated absolute error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Evaluations", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Poisson mixture − power [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let row = (
    d1,
    d2,
    lambda,
    critical,
    power,
    tail.absolute_error,
    tail.evaluations,
    mixture - power,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "power" })
);
// Independent check 2: space group means equally and generate observations with √(Σ(μᵢ − μ̄)²/k) = f.
let centre = 0.5 * (k as f64 + 1.0);
let spread = (1..=k)
    .map(|i| RealScalar::powi(i as f64 - centre, 2))
    .sum::<f64>();
let scale = effect / RealScalar::sqrt(spread / k as f64);
let normal = Normal::new(0.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let (mut rejected, mut values, mut means) = (0_usize, vec![0.0; n], vec![0.0; k]);
for _ in 0..replications as usize {
    let mut within = 0.0;
    for (i, mean) in means.iter_mut().enumerate() {
        let shift = scale * ((i + 1) as f64 - centre);
        for value in values.iter_mut() {
            *value = shift + normal.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        }
        *mean = values.iter().sum::<f64>() / n as f64;
        within += values
            .iter()
            .map(|y| RealScalar::powi(y - *mean, 2))
            .sum::<f64>();
    }
    let grand = means.iter().sum::<f64>() / k as f64;
    let between = n as f64
        * means
            .iter()
            .map(|m| RealScalar::powi(m - grand, 2))
            .sum::<f64>();
    if (between / d1) / (within / d2) > critical {
        rejected += 1;
    }
}
let r = replications.round();
let rate = rejected as f64 / r;
let se = RealScalar::sqrt(power * (1.0 - power) / r);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Rejection count", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Replicate count R", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Rejection fraction [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Standard error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "z [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(rejected, r as usize, rate, se, (rate - power) / se)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "simulation" })
);
```

With the defaults ($k = 4$, $n = 10$, $f = 0.25$, $\alpha = 0.05$), $d_1 = 3$, $d_2 = 36$, and $\lambda = 2.5$, with critical value $c = 2.8663$ and power $0.21224$.
Even with a medium effect size, 10 observations per group detect the difference only about once in five trials.
The estimated absolute error of `evaluate_tail` is $3.2 \times 10^{-9}$ with 44 evaluations; the difference $8.1 \times 10^{-11}$ from the Poisson mixture summing central `FisherF` values is within the estimate.
Monte Carlo rejects 384 of 2,000 replicates, a fraction of 0.192, giving $z = -2.21$ with standard error 0.0091.
With the same settings, seed 1 gives $z = -1.17$ and seed 1000 gives $z = 0.90$, so $\lvert z \rvert$ slightly exceeding 2 can be read as seed-dependent variation.
At $f = 0$, power is $0.05$ (the same as the tail of `FisherF`), with difference $4.2 \times 10^{-17}$ from the Poisson mixture.
For $k = 3$, $n = 40$, and $f = 0.4$, $\lambda = 19.2$ gives power $0.97893$, while Monte Carlo gives $0.9845$ ($z = 1.74$).
Increasing each group's sample size from 10 to 40 raises power for $f = 0.25$ from $0.21224$ to $0.74940$.

## Division of responsibilities with scioxide-stats

`scioxide-random` provides only laws that take statistic values and return probabilities; it neither computes statistics from data nor returns test conclusions.
`scioxide-stats` performs tests on data; the following functions use the laws above.

- Tukey–Kramer and Games–Howell in `PreparedGroups::pairwise` use `StudentizedRange::evaluate_tail` of the [studentized range](oxref:random-tests-studentized-range) to construct simultaneous $p$-values and confidence intervals ([Regression, ANOVA, and GLM](oxref:stats-regression-anova-and-group-comparison)).
- F-tests in regression and ANOVA use `sf` of `FisherF`.
- Mean power `power` uses `NoncentralStudentT` when variance is estimated ([Power and sample-size design](oxref:stats-power-estimated-variance-t-test)).
- The relationship between KS tests and KS laws is in [KS tests in scioxide-stats](oxref:random-tests-ks-calibration).

`scioxide-random` does not provide the null distributions of Anderson–Darling or Cramér–von Mises statistics.
`scioxide-stats` computes their $p$-values using methods specific to each test.

- One-sample `cramer_von_mises_test` against a fully specified continuous distribution uses the Csörgő–Faraway limiting distribution.
- Two-sample `cramer_von_mises_two_sample_test` enumerates every label arrangement within the work limit; beyond it, Anderson's limiting approximation is used for data without ties.
- $k$-sample `anderson_k_sample_test` (Scholz–Stephens) interpolates published critical values, clipping the $p$-value to $[0.001, 0.25]$.
- One-sample Anderson–Darling returns only statistic `anderson_darling_statistic`.
  As with fitted parameters or discrete null distributions, calibrate it with Monte Carlo using borrowed-form `goodness_of_fit_test_into`, refitting parameters in every replicate.

## Summary

F tails evaluate the incomplete beta function on the smaller side; noncentral tails sum positive Poisson-mixture terms using Benton–Krishnamoorthy recurrences.
Noncentral quantiles start from Pearson or Patnaik approximations or a moment-matched normal and solve the smaller log-tail with safeguarded Newton iteration.
ANOVA power follows by evaluating the upper tail of `NoncentralF` at the critical value of `FisherF::isf`; noncentrality $\lambda = knf^2$ allows estimation of required sample sizes.
When checking power by Monte Carlo, interpret $z$ using binomial standard errors and multiple seeds, rather than one seed's $\lvert z \rvert > 2$ alone.
The null distributions of Anderson–Darling and Cramér–von Mises are absent from `scioxide-random`, so compute $p$-values with test functions in `scioxide-stats`.
