Counts with trial-dependent success probabilities and sampling without replacement with color-dependent selection probabilities cannot be expressed by the closed forms of binomial or hypergeometric distributions.
With finite support, precomputing all probability masses into a table makes subsequent queries simple lookups.
This page explains the construction of table-based Poisson binomial and noncentral hypergeometric laws in `scioxide-random`, the closed-form Boltzmann law, and the difference between Fisher and Wallenius noncentral hypergeometric distributions.
The shared sampling convention (`CountDistribution`) for count laws is in [Discrete distributions and count laws](oxref:random-discrete-count-distribution).

## Tables of probability masses and cumulative probabilities

At construction, `PoissonBinomial`, `FisherNoncentralHypergeometric`, and `WalleniusNoncentralHypergeometric` compute log-masses over the support and normalize by their sum to form tables.
Lower cumulative $\ln P(K \le k)$ is summed from the left support endpoint and upper cumulative $\ln P(K > k)$ from the right, separately using logarithmic addition (`logadd`).
Both begin with small masses, retaining relative accuracy even in far tails.
At each $k$, retain the logarithm $x$ of the smaller cumulative probability and reconstruct the logarithm of the larger using its complement $\ln(1 - e^{x})$, making the two cumulative probabilities sum to 1 apart from rounding.

For support size $L$, tables contain $3L$ values of type `f64`; construction work counts against `work_budget` and storage against `max_bytes`.
Mass and tail queries take one table lookup, $O(1)$, with no allocation.
Quantiles use bisection between the support endpoints, requiring $O(\log L)$ lookups; sampling inverts the CDF for an open-interval uniform with the same bisection.
Only `PoissonBinomial` generates samples directly from $m$ Bernoulli trials.

## Dynamic programming for Poisson binomial

The success count $K$ of independent Bernoulli trials with success probabilities $p_1, \dots, p_m$ follows a Poisson binomial distribution.
Its mean is $\sum_i p_i$ and variance $\sum_i p_i(1 - p_i)$; when all $p_i$ are equal, it reduces to a binomial distribution.
The probability mass $P_j(k) = P(k \text{ successes in the first } j \text{ trials})$ after the first $j$ trials satisfies the following recurrence by conditioning on failure or success of trial $j$:

$$
P_j(k) = (1 - p_j)\,P_{j-1}(k) + p_j\,P_{j-1}(k - 1),\qquad P_0(0) = 1
$$


The right side sums nonnegative terms, avoiding subtractive cancellation.
`PoissonBinomial::new(&probabilities)` evaluates this recurrence logarithmically, updating $k$ from largest to smallest in a single array.
Trials with probability exactly 0 are removed; trials with probability 1 only shift the support by one.
For the remaining $m$ trials, preparation uses $m(m+3)/2$ updates (error if the work limit is exceeded) and storage for at most $4m + 3$ values of type `f64`.
Mass and tail queries take $O(1)$, quantiles $O(\log m)$, and sampling $O(m)$ using $m$ Bernoulli trials.
No discrete Fourier transform is used.

## Boltzmann distribution

`Boltzmann::new(lambda, n)` is the truncated discrete exponential distribution on $\{0, 1, \dots, n - 1\}$ defined by

$$
P(X = k) = \frac{(1 - e^{-\lambda})\,e^{-\lambda k}}{1 - e^{-\lambda n}}
$$


The probability of occupying energy level $E_k = k\varepsilon$ with spacing $\varepsilon$ at temperature $T$ is proportional to Boltzmann factor $e^{-E_k/(k_BT)}$, so $\lambda = \varepsilon/(k_BT)$ (where $k_B$ is Boltzmann's constant).
Both CDF $F(k) = (1 - e^{-\lambda(k+1)})/(1 - e^{-\lambda n})$ and upper-tail probability $S(k) = e^{-\lambda(k+1)}(1 - e^{-\lambda(n-k-1)})/(1 - e^{-\lambda n})$ have closed forms, evaluated logarithmically without taking complements.
Mean and variance use Bernoulli-number expansions for $\lambda n < 0.1$ and closed forms otherwise.
Sampling has no specialized construction and uses the default method shared by count laws.
For open-interval uniform $U$, directly search for the smallest $k$ with $F(k) \ge U$ by bisection on $[0, n - 1]$, since the support $\{0, \dots, n - 1\}$ is known; only about $\log_2 n$ closed-form tail evaluations are needed.
$n$ is restricted to values from 1 through $2^{53}$.

## Fisher and Wallenius noncentral hypergeometric distributions

When drawing $n$ balls from an urn of $N$ balls containing $M$ successes (red) and $N - M$ failures (white), the number of red balls $K$ follows a hypergeometric distribution.
Its support is $\max(0, n - (N - M)) \le k \le \min(n, M)$.
If red balls are $\omega$ times as likely to be selected as white, two different laws arise depending on how bias is introduced.

Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric distribution selects red and white independently as binomials $X \sim \mathrm{Bin}(M, p_1)$ and $Y \sim \mathrm{Bin}(N - M, p_2)$, then gives the law of $X$ conditional on total $X + Y = n$.
Its probability mass is

$$
P(K = k) \propto \binom{M}{k}\binom{N - M}{n - k}\,\omega^k,\qquad \omega = \frac{p_1/(1 - p_1)}{p_2/(1 - p_2)}
$$

where $\omega$ is the odds ratio.
The upper-left cell of a $2 \times 2$ contingency table with fixed margins follows this law, used under the alternative hypothesis in Fisher's exact test and for conditional odds-ratio estimation.

Wallenius' noncentral hypergeometric distribution is the law of sequential draws, selecting each ball according to weighted proportions of the remaining red and white balls.
After $t$ draws containing $k$ red balls, the next-draw red probability is

$$
\frac{\omega\,(M - k)}{\omega\,(M - k) + \bigl(N - M - (t - k)\bigr)}
$$

with weights changing according to the remaining composition after each draw.
Examples include drawing balls with different weights one by one and sequential catches of fish with different catchabilities.
Both laws equal the hypergeometric distribution at $\omega = 1$ but differ for $\omega \ne 1$.
For $N = 10$, $M = 4$, $n = 3$, and $\omega = 2$, Fisher masses are proportional to $\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{3-k}2^k = 20, 120, 144, 32$, giving $P(K = 0) = 20/316 \approx 0.0633$.
For Wallenius this is the probability of drawing white three times in succession, giving $P(K = 0) = \frac{6}{14} \cdot \frac{5}{13} \cdot \frac{4}{12} = 5/91 \approx 0.0549$.

The arguments of both constructors correspond as follows: `population` is $N$, `successes` is $M$, `draws` is $n$, and `odds` is $\omega$.
`FisherNoncentralHypergeometric::new(population, successes, draws, odds)` forms log-masses from the adjacent-mass ratio

$$
\ln\frac{P(k+1)}{P(k)} = \ln\frac{(M - k)(n - k)}{(k + 1)(N - M - n + k + 1)} + \ln\omega
$$

without forming binomial coefficients themselves.
This ratio decreases monotonically with $k$, so bisection on its sign finds the mode, then ratios are accumulated in both directions.
Each table step moves toward smaller masses, preventing overflow even in far tails.

Since Wallenius' distribution has no cheaply evaluated closed form, `WalleniusNoncentralHypergeometric::new(population, successes, draws, odds)` constructs its table by forward dynamic programming with the transition probabilities above, updating row $\ln P(k \text{ red balls in } t \text{ draws})$ at each draw count $t$.
Updating $k$ from largest to smallest requires only a single array for the row.
The cost is $O(n \cdot \min(n, M))$ updates; preparation returns an error if their number exceeds `work_budget`.
If red balls outnumber white, colors are exchanged and $\omega$ becomes $1/\omega$.
The complement counting undrawn balls is not used because it defines a different law for the Wallenius process.
At $\omega = 1$ it builds the same table as Fisher.
The constructor returns `NotImplemented` if the support has at least two points and `population` exceeds $2^{53}$.

## Comparing the two noncentral hypergeometric distributions

The following cell compares Fisher and Wallenius masses for $n$ draws from an urn with $N = 10$ and $M = 4$ against two independent checks.
The first directly sums and normalizes Fisher's closed form $\binom{M}{k}\binom{N-M}{n-k}\omega^k$, reporting its maximum difference from the `FisherNoncentralHypergeometric` table.
The second uses relative frequencies from $m$ trials directly simulating the Wallenius process with uniforms, reporting $z = (\text{relative frequency} - P(k))/\sqrt{P(k)(1 - P(k))/m}$ at each $k$ against both Wallenius and Fisher masses.
The inputs are the base-2 logarithm $\log_2 \omega$ of the odds ratio, draw count $n$, trial count $m$, and SplitMix64 seed.
The plot band is Wallenius mass $\pm 2$ SE; dashed and dotted steps show Wallenius and Fisher masses, and markers show process relative frequencies.

```rust
//| id: random-noncentral-hypergeometric-urn
//| ref: random-noncentral-hypergeometric-urn
//| title: "Compare biased urn draws with Fisher and Wallenius probability masses"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   log2_odds: {"type":"range","label":"Log₂ odds ratio ω [-]","description":"At 0 both laws equal hypergeometric; larger absolute values increase their difference.","min":-3,"max":3,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//|   draws: {"type":"range","label":"Draw count n [count]","description":"Number drawn from an urn with N = 10 and M = 4; support runs from max(0, n − 6) to min(n, 4).","min":1,"max":9,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   trials: {"type":"range","label":"Process trials m [count]","description":"Changes the standard error of relative frequencies as 1/√m.","min":4000,"max":40000,"step":4000,"value":20000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"SplitMix64 seed [-]","description":"Observe sample-to-sample fluctuations in z.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":11}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"central","label":"ω = 1","description":"Both laws become hypergeometric, with total variation distance 0.","inputs":{"log2_odds":0}}
//|   - {"id":"strong","label":"ω = 8, n = 6","description":"Strong bias and many draws make process frequencies differ substantially from Fisher masses.","inputs":{"log2_odds":3,"draws":6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"masses","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.06329113924050633,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"masses","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.054945054945054944,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"masses","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"masses","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: central
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: strong
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.05,"max":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"masses","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
use scioxide_core::{NumericScalar, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{
    FisherNoncentralHypergeometric, Mass, Rng, SeedableRng, SplitMix64,
    WalleniusNoncentralHypergeometric,
};

let (population, successes) = (10_usize, 4_usize);
let n = draws.round() as usize;
let m = trials.round() as usize;
let omega = RealScalar::exp2(log2_odds);
let fisher = FisherNoncentralHypergeometric::new(population, successes, n, omega)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let wallenius = WalleniusNoncentralHypergeometric::new(population, successes, n, omega)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (low, high) = (n.saturating_sub(population - successes), n.min(successes));
// Independent check 1: directly sum and normalize Fisher's closed form C(M, k) C(N − M, n − k) ω^k.
let choose = |a: usize, b: usize| (0..b).fold(1.0, |c, i| c * (a - i) as f64 / (i + 1) as f64);
let weights: Vec<f64> = (low..=high)
    .map(|k| {
        choose(successes, k)
            * choose(population - successes, n - k)
            * RealScalar::powi(omega, k as i32)
    })
    .collect();
let total: f64 = weights.iter().sum();
// Independent check 2: simulate sequential draws according to remaining red weight ω·r and white weight w.
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let mut counts = vec![0.0_f64; high + 1];
for _ in 0..m {
    let (mut red, mut white, mut k) = (successes as f64, (population - successes) as f64, 0);
    for _ in 0..n {
        let u: f64 = rng.next_float().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if u * (omega * red + white) < omega * red {
            (red, k) = (red - 1.0, k + 1);
        } else {
            white -= 1.0;
        }
    }
    counts[k] += 1.0;
}
let mf = m as f64;
let (mut rows, mut band, mut fisher_line, mut wallenius_line, mut points) =
    (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut closed_form_error, mut total_variation) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
for (k, weight) in (low..=high).zip(&weights) {
    let f = fisher.pmf(k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let w = wallenius.pmf(k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let frequency = counts[k] / mf;
    let (se_w, se_f) = (
        RealScalar::sqrt(w * (1.0 - w) / mf),
        RealScalar::sqrt(f * (1.0 - f) / mf),
    );
    closed_form_error =
        RealScalar::max(closed_form_error, NumericScalar::abs(f - weight / total));
    total_variation += 0.5 * NumericScalar::abs(f - w);
    rows.push((
        k,
        f,
        weight / total,
        w,
        frequency,
        (frequency - w) / se_w,
        (frequency - f) / se_f,
    ));
    let x = k as f64;
    band.push([x, w, w - 2.0 * se_w, w + 2.0 * se_w]);
    wallenius_line.push([x, w]);
    fisher_line.push([x, f]);
    points.push([x, frequency]);
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Red count k [count]",
        "yLabel": "Probability / relative frequency [-]",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "layers": [
            {"id": "band", "kind": "band", "name": "Wallenius ± 2 SE", "points": band},
            {
                "id": "wallenius",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Wallenius probability mass",
                "style": {"lineType": "dashed", "step": "middle"},
                "points": wallenius_line
            },
            {
                "id": "fisher",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Fisher probability mass",
                "style": {"lineType": "dotted", "step": "middle"},
                "points": fisher_line
            },
            {"id": "process", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Process relative frequency", "points": points}
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "urn" })
);
let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Red count k [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Fisher pmf [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Fisher closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Wallenius pmf [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Process relative frequency [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "z against Wallenius [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "z against Fisher [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "masses" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum difference from closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Fisher–Wallenius total variation distance [-]", "type": "number", "format": digits4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![(closed_form_error, total_variation)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
```

With the defaults ($\omega = 2$, $n = 3$, $m = 20\,000$, seed 11), Fisher's table differs from the closed form by at most $2.8 \times 10^{-17}$, and $P(K = 0)$ agrees with the preceding section's $20/316$ and $5/91$.
Process relative frequencies have $z$ against Wallenius masses in the range $-2.13$ to $2.16$, while $z$ against Fisher masses has magnitudes from 5.9 to 8.0.
The four $z$ values are correlated through the constraint that relative frequencies sum to 1; each $z$ exceeds 2 with probability about 4.6%, so compare the whole column's magnitudes rather than a single value.
Changing seeds to 1 and 1000 keeps $|z|$ against Wallenius at most 1.52, while $|z|$ against Fisher stays at least 3.86.
The total variation distance $\frac{1}{2}\sum_k \lvert P_{\mathrm{F}}(k) - P_{\mathrm{W}}(k) \rvert$ between the laws is 0.02845, small but distinguishable with 20,000 trials.

At $\omega = 1$, both tables agree, total variation distance is 0, and both $z$ columns are identical.
For $\omega = 8$ and $n = 6$, total variation distance grows to 0.2738; $z$ against Fisher reaches magnitude 79.6, while $|z|$ against Wallenius stays at most 2.49.
In this urn, Wallenius mass shifts toward larger $k$ than Fisher for $\omega > 1$, and toward smaller values for $\omega < 1$ ($\log_2 \omega = -3$).
For every input, table values agree with the closed form within rounding, and process frequencies follow the Wallenius law.

## Summary

Finite-support count laws tabulate log-masses and both cumulative tails once, summing tails from the smaller side to retain relative accuracy even far into the tails.
`PoissonBinomial` uses dynamic programming with nonnegative terms, requiring $O(m^2)$ preparation and $O(m)$ storage and answering queries in $O(1)$.
`Boltzmann` builds no table, evaluating and sampling through closed-form tails and support bisection.
Fisher's noncentral hypergeometric law describes independent selections conditional on their total; Wallenius' describes biased sequential draws, with different values for $\omega \ne 1$.
Choose between the laws after establishing whether the data arose from sequential draws or independent selections with a fixed total.
