Computer experiments and multidimensional integration construct designs of $n$ points varying $d$ inputs simultaneously.
Independent uniform points create clusters and gaps, while a grid dividing each axis into $m$ parts requires $m^d$ points.
This page explains Latin hypercube designs whose point count can be chosen independently of dimension, discrepancy measures of point-set uniformity, and local exchange searches that reduce discrepancy.
Variance of one-dimensional stratified sampling is covered in [Uniform indices, permutations, and stratified sampling](oxref:random-sampling-stratified-variance).

## Latin hypercube designs

A Latin hypercube (LHS, McKay–Beckman–Conover 1979) divides every axis into $n$ equal strata $[s/n, (s+1)/n)$ and places $n$ points so every stratum on every axis contains exactly one point.
Axis $j$ is constructed using independently chosen uniform permutations $\pi_j$ for each axis and within-stratum positions $U_{ij} \in [0, 1)$ as

$$
x_{ij} = \frac{\pi_j(i) + U_{ij}}{n}, \qquad i = 0, \dots, n-1,\quad j = 1, \dots, d
$$


Each individual point is uniform on $[0, 1)^d$, so the mean $\hat{I} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i f(\mathbf{x}_i)$ is an unbiased estimator of $I = \int_{[0,1)^d} f$.

Write the ANOVA decomposition of the function as $f(\mathbf{x}) = I + \sum_j f_j(x_j) + r(\mathbf{x})$.
$f_j$ is the main effect of variable $x_j$ alone; $r$ is the interaction remainder.
Stein (1987) showed that, for square-integrable $f$,

$$
\mathrm{Var}\,\hat{I}_{\mathrm{LHS}} = \frac{1}{n}\Bigl(\sigma_f^2 - \sum_{j=1}^d \sigma_j^2\Bigr) + o\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right), \qquad \sigma_j^2 = \mathrm{Var}\,f_j(U)
$$


The main-effect variances are removed from the plain Monte Carlo variance $\sigma_f^2/n$, giving greater gains for functions close to sums of individual variables.
For functions with interactions alone, the gain is confined to the $o(1/n)$ term.

`latin_hypercube(n, d, rng)` places one value in each stratum on each axis and shuffles each axis with Fisher–Yates, returning the design in a row-major `Vec` with $n$ rows and $d$ columns.
Its cost is $O(nd)$, using $nd$ uniform words for within-stratum positions and about $nd$ for shuffling.
`latin_hypercube_with_options(n, d, rng, options)` accepts settings `LatinHypercubeOptions` and returns a pair of the design and report `LatinHypercubeReport`.
Passing `LatinHypercubeOptions::with_centered(true)` gives $U_{ij} = 1/2$ (stratum midpoints), consuming no words for within-stratum positions.
Zero $n = 0$, $d = 0$, or `stagnation_limit` gives `InvalidInput`.
For `f32` output, $n > 2^{24}$ also gives `InvalidInput` because adjacent stratum boundaries cannot be distinguished; if $nd$ exceeds `usize`, it gives `SizeOverflow`.
Both owned forms return nothing on failure, leaving the generator advanced by the words already consumed.

`latin_hypercube_into(d, rng, state, options, out, workspace, cx)` builds the entire design in the workspace and copies it to `out` only on success.
Rows are coupled through permutations, so partially written rows are not independently usable.
The point count is `out.len() / d`; if the length of `out` is not a multiple of $d$, it returns `DimensionMismatch` before consuming randomness.
A workspace smaller than `out` is grown; reuse avoids allocation from the second call onward.
`state` is `AdvanceOnError` or `Rollback`, determining whether to restore the generator on failure.

## Discrepancy measures

Point-set uniformity is measured by discrepancy.
Star discrepancy is the supremum of the difference between the fraction of points in an origin-anchored box and its volume, bounding integration error through the [Koksma–Hlawka inequality](oxref:random-qmc-koksma-hlawka).
Since exact maximization is difficult in high dimensions, `discrepancy(points, d, method)` evaluates $L^2$ discrepancies, representing mean squared box differences, using Hickernell's closed form

$$
D^2 = C^d - \frac{2}{n} \sum_{i} \prod_{k} g(x_{ik}) + \frac{1}{n^2} \sum_{i, j} \prod_{k} h(x_{ik}, x_{jk})
$$


Arrange points $\mathbf{x}_i = (x_{i1}, \dots, x_{id})$ row-major with $n$ rows and $d$ columns, and set $a = |x - 1/2|$, $b = |y - 1/2|$, and $\delta = |x - y|$.
Evaluation takes $O(n^2 d)$ time without allocating working storage.

| `DiscrepancyMethod` | $C$ | $g(x)$ | $h(x, y)$ | Returned value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Centered` (centered $L^2$) | $13/12$ | $1 + a/2 - a^2/2$ | $1 + a/2 + b/2 - \delta/2$ | $D^2$ |
| `WrapAround` (periodic) | $4/3$, constant term $-(4/3)^d$ | $0$ | $3/2 - \delta + \delta^2$ | $D^2$ |
| `Mixture`（Zhou–Fang–Ning） | $19/12$ | $5/3 - a/4 - a^2/4$ | $15/8 - a/4 - b/4 - 3\delta/4 + \delta^2/2$ | $D^2$ |
| `L2Star` (Warnock) | $1/3$ | $(1 - x^2)/2$ | $1 - \max(x, y)$ | $D$ (square root) |

`Centered` is invariant under reflection of each coordinate about $1/2$; `WrapAround` is invariant under shifting points modulo 1.
`L2Star` is the root mean square over origin-anchored boxes $[0, \mathbf{t})$, rather than star discrepancy defined by a supremum.
For independent uniform points, $g$ has mean $C$ and $h(x, \cdot)$ has mean $g(x)$, so expected centered discrepancy is $\mathrm{E}[D^2] = \bigl((5/4)^d - (13/12)^d\bigr)/n$.

Coordinates must lie in the closed interval $[0, 1]$; out-of-range coordinates, empty sets, and arrays ending mid-row give `InvalidInput`, while NaN and infinity give `NonFinite`.
Since $D^2$ subtracts terms of order $C^d$, cancellation is strong for uniform designs and the computed value may round to a small negative number.
Negative values within $64\varepsilon(d + 1)$ times the sum of absolute terms are replaced by 0; more negative values give `InvalidInput`.
Sums are accumulated exactly and rounded once at the end, bounding errors to a few ulps of $C^d$ and the term magnitudes.

`CenteredDiscrepancy` retains points and sums; `evaluate_append` returns the value after adding a row in $O(nd)$ without changing state, and `append` commits it.
Storage for `capacity` points is allocated at construction; `append` when full gives `InvalidInput`.
`geometric_discrepancy(points, d, method)` returns either the minimum interpoint distance (`MinimumDistance`) or the mean edge length of a minimum spanning tree computed with Prim's method (`MeanSpanningTreeEdge`, internally allocating one distance per point).
Larger values indicate greater separation.
`geometric_discrepancy` requires at least 2 points and uses the same coordinate-range rules as `discrepancy`.

## Improvement by exchanges

LHS stratifies only one-dimensional projections, so all points can still lie near a diagonal.
A positive $M$ in `with_max_proposals(M)` improves the generated design through greedy local search.
Each proposal uniformly chooses an axis and two rows and exchanges their values on that axis.
An exchange preserves the set of values on every axis and thus the Latin property.
Only proposals strictly reducing squared centered $L^2$ discrepancy $D^2$ are accepted; all others are reverted.

$D^2$ is determined by the point sum $\sum_i \prod_k g(x_{ik})$ and pair sum $\sum_{i,j} \prod_k h(x_{ik}, x_{jk})$.
An exchange changes only terms involving its two rows, so each proposal subtracts those terms and adds their post-exchange values.
Each proposal costs $O(nd)$, avoiding the $O(n^2 d)$ cost of full recomputation.
Because sums accumulate exactly, incremental values agree bit-for-bit with full recomputation using `discrepancy`.

It terminates with `LatinHypercubeTermination::Stagnation` after `stagnation_limit` consecutive nonimproving proposals (default 100), `BudgetExhausted` after exhausting $M$ proposals, or `Complete` when no proposals were requested ($M = 0$, the default).
A proposal choosing the same row twice also counts as nonimproving.
`latin_hypercube_into` checks cancellation and the deadline in `ExecutionContext` before initial sampling and before each proposal.
Detection before initial sampling returns an error `ExecutionStopped`; detection before a proposal stops with `Stopped`.
Every termination retains all $n$ design points; `Stopped`, `BudgetExhausted`, and `Stagnation` return the design including exchanges accepted so far.
`LatinHypercubeReport` returns the termination reason, proposal count (including rejected proposals), and $D^2$ before and after exchanges; with no requested proposals, $D^2$ is `None`.
This is a local search and does not establish an optimal design.

The following cell constructs designs of $n$ points in $d$ dimensions from the same seed, varying the proposal limit $M$ from 0 to 3,000.
Inputs are point count $n$, dimension $d$, stagnation limit, and seed.
With the same seed, initial designs and proposal sequences are shared, so increasing $M$ cannot increase $D^2$.
The table shows termination reason, proposal count, reported $D^2$, difference from $D^2$ recomputed by `discrepancy`, and the maximum number of points in any stratum on each axis.
Recomputation and stratum counts independently check the report; the plot's dashed line gives expectation $\bigl((5/4)^d - (13/12)^d\bigr)/n$ for independent uniform points.
The cell's conclusion is the termination reason at the largest limit $M = 3000$; local search stops at a limit or stagnation, so its outcome is partial.

```rust
//| id: random-lhs-swap-optimization
//| ref: random-lhs-swap-optimization
//| title: "Compare Latin hypercube improvements by exchanges across proposal limits"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   points: {"type":"range","label":"Point count n [points]","min":8,"max":64,"step":8,"value":32}
//|   dimension: {"type":"range","label":"Dimension d [-]","min":2,"max":6,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   stagnation: {"type":"range","label":"Stagnation limit [proposals]","description":"Stops after this many consecutive nonimproving proposals.","min":10,"max":400,"step":10,"value":100}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"short-patience","label":"Stagnation limit 10","description":"A short stagnation limit reaches Stagnation after few proposals.","inputs":{"stagnation":10}}
//|   - {"id":"six-dimensions","label":"n = 64, d = 6","description":"Increasing dimension preserves the Latin property and agreement of incremental updates.","inputs":{"points":64,"dimension":6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Complete"}
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"BudgetExhausted"}
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":10,"max":10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/6/5","range":{"min":0,"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/6/6","range":{"min":1,"max":1}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: short-patience
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/6/1","equals":"Stagnation"}
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/6/5","range":{"min":0,"max":0}}
//|   - case: six-dimensions
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/6/5","range":{"min":0,"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"budgets","path":"/rows/6/6","range":{"min":1,"max":1}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    DiscrepancyMethod, LatinHypercubeOptions, LatinHypercubeTermination, SeedableRng, SplitMix64,
    discrepancy, latin_hypercube_with_options,
};

let sci4 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 4 });
let (n, d) = (points.round() as usize, dimension.round() as usize);
let limit = stagnation.round() as usize;
// Expected centered D² for independent uniform points: ((5/4)^d − (13/12)^d)/n (analytical).
let iid =
    (RealScalar::powi(1.25, d as i32) - RealScalar::powi(13.0 / 12.0, d as i32)) / n as f64;
let (mut rows, mut curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut before = None;
let mut last = (LatinHypercubeTermination::Complete, 0, None);
for budget in [0_usize, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000, 3000] {
    let options = LatinHypercubeOptions::default()
        .with_max_proposals(budget)
        .with_stagnation_limit(limit);
    let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
    let (design, report) = latin_hypercube_with_options::<f64, _>(n, d, &mut rng, options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Independent check 1: recompute D² from the entire returned design.
    let direct =
        discrepancy(&design, d, DiscrepancyMethod::Centered).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let gap = report
        .final_discrepancy()
        .map(|value| (value - direct).abs());
    // Independent check 2: maximum points in a stratum [s/n, (s+1)/n) on each axis (1 for a Latin design).
    let mut crowded = 0;
    for axis in 0..d {
        let mut counts = vec![0_usize; n];
        for row in design.chunks_exact(d) {
            counts[(row[axis] * n as f64) as usize] += 1;
        }
        crowded = counts.into_iter().fold(crowded, usize::max);
    }
    if let (Some(initial), Some(after)) =
        (report.initial_discrepancy(), report.final_discrepancy())
    {
        before = Some(initial);
        curve.push([budget as f64, after]);
    }
    last = (
        report.termination(),
        report.proposals(),
        report.final_discrepancy(),
    );
    rows.push((
        budget,
        format!("{:?}", report.termination()),
        report.proposals(),
        report.initial_discrepancy(),
        report.final_discrepancy(),
        gap,
        crowded,
    ));
}
let mut annotations = vec![serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "line",
    "axis": "y",
    "value": iid,
    "label": "Expectation for independent uniform points",
    "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
})];
if let Some(value) = before {
    annotations.push(serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "line",
        "axis": "y",
        "value": value,
        "label": "D² before exchanges"
    }));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({
        "name": "D² after exchanges",
        "points": curve,
        "style": { "showSymbol": true, "symbol": "circle" }
    })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Proposal limit M [proposals]",
        "yLabel": "Squared centered L² discrepancy D² [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": annotations
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Proposal limit M [proposals]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Proposal count [proposals]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "D² before exchanges [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "D² after exchanges [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "|Report − recomputation| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Maximum points per stratum [points]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "budgets" })
);
// Conclusion: local search with largest limit M = 3000 stops at the limit or stagnation (partial).
let (termination, proposals, after) = last;
let mut outcome = Outcome::partial("Local exchange search stopped at the proposal limit or stagnation")
    .explanation("D² is a local-search result and does not establish an optimal design.")
    .status(format!("{termination:?}"))
    .diagnostic("Proposal count", proposals as f64, None);
if let (Some(initial), Some(value)) = (before, after) {
    outcome = outcome
        .diagnostic("D² before exchanges", initial, None)
        .diagnostic("D² after exchanges", value, None);
}
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

With the defaults ($n = 32$, $d = 3$, stagnation limit 100, seed 5), initial $D^2 = 2.471 \times 10^{-3}$ falls to $1.790 \times 10^{-3}$ at $M = 100$ and $1.347 \times 10^{-3}$ at $M = 1000$.
For $M = 3000$, it reaches `Stagnation` after 1,112 proposals, with $D^2 = 1.342 \times 10^{-3}$ at 54% of its initial value.
Even the initial value is about one ninth of expectation $2.130 \times 10^{-2}$ for independent uniform points, so stratification alone removes most discrepancy.
In every row, reported $D^2$ differs from recomputation by `discrepancy` by 0, and every stratum on every axis contains exactly one point.
Setting the stagnation limit to 10 stops after 40 proposals, leaving $D^2$ at $2.204 \times 10^{-3}$.
For $n = 64$ and $d = 6$, it falls from $1.001 \times 10^{-2}$ to $4.338 \times 10^{-3}$ after 2,676 proposals.
The decrease in $D^2$ slows with the proposal limit, while the stagnation limit determines when search ends.

## Summary

Latin hypercubes stratify with point counts independent of dimension, removing main-effect variances and improving on plain Monte Carlo for nearly additive functions.
`discrepancy` evaluates four $L^2$ discrepancies using closed forms and exact accumulation; all except `L2Star` return squared values, so compare identical measures and point counts.
Exchanges using `with_max_proposals` preserve the Latin property and reduce $D^2$ through incremental updates, with reported values agreeing bit-for-bit with full recomputation.
Termination is usually `Stagnation` or `BudgetExhausted`; both return usable designs but do not establish optimality.
