Laws taking values in vectors, matrices, or groups are used for correlated random generation, Bayesian priors, random matrices, and random rotations.
`scioxide-random` provides multivariate normal and t distributions with positive semidefinite covariance, simplex and count-vector laws, positive-definite and matrix-valued laws, Haar measure on orthogonal and unitary groups, and correlation matrices with specified eigenvalues.
This page explains multivariate-normal preparation (eigendecomposition and rank decisions), singular support, generation algorithms for each law, and why fixing signs yields Haar measure on groups.
Joint rectangle probabilities for multivariate normal and t distributions are covered in [Rectangle probabilities for multivariate normal and t distributions](../multivariate-rectangle-probability/).

## Multivariate-normal eigendecomposition and singular support

A normal law $\mathcal{N}_d(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)$ with mean $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and covariance $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ can be generated as $\mathbf{X} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + B\mathbf{Z}$ from a $d \times r$ matrix $B$ satisfying $\Sigma = BB^{\mathsf T}$ and a standard normal vector $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^r$.
If $\Sigma$ is positive definite, Cholesky factorization yields $B$.
Quantities with linear constraints (such as compositions with a fixed component sum) have positive semidefinite covariance; with rank $r < d$, Cholesky stops at a zero diagonal.
Therefore `MultivariateNormal` uses symmetric eigendecomposition $\Sigma = Q\Lambda Q^{\mathsf T}$ and constructs $B = Q_r\Lambda_r^{1/2}$ from positive eigenpairs alone.

`MultivariateNormal::new(&mean, &covariance)` takes its mean as `&[S]` and covariance as `&Matrix<S>` or a possibly strided `MatrixView<S>`, copies both, and prepares them in the following order.
`MultivariateNormal::with_options(&mean, &covariance, rank_options, options)` takes rank policy `MultivariateNormalOptions<S>` and tolerances and work limits `LawOptions<S>`; `new` uses defaults for both.

1. Check finite values, square shape, exactly equal floating-point symmetric entries, and nonnegative diagonals.
   Symmetry allows no rounding tolerance, so callers must symmetrize computed covariance matrices.
2. Divide by the largest absolute entry $s$ to form $\Sigma/s$, preventing overflow and underflow in eigenvalue computation.
   A nonzero entry becoming 0 after division returns `NumericalInstability` (`Underflow`).
3. Compute a symmetric eigendecomposition of the normalized matrix.
   A backward-stable eigenvalue calculation has errors of order $d\varepsilon\lambda_{\max}$, so the covariance is considered non-positive-semidefinite and `DomainError` is returned only if an eigenvalue is below $-8d\varepsilon\lambda_{\max}$; smaller negative magnitudes are treated as rounding error.
4. Retain only eigenvalues exceeding the threshold determined by `RankPolicy`; their count is rank $r$.
   Restore $s$ to compute retained-direction standard deviations $\sqrt{s\lambda_j}$ and log-pseudodeterminant $\sum_j \ln(s\lambda_j)$.

`MultivariateNormalOptions` contains only `rank_policy` (set by `with_rank_policy`), with no covariance regularization facility.
The three values of `RankPolicy` (`scioxide-linalg`) give the following normalized-eigenvalue thresholds.

| `RankPolicy` | Threshold (normalized units) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `DimensionScaledEpsilon` (default) | $d\varepsilon\lambda_{\max}$ | Discard eigenvalues at rounding scale |
| `Exact` | 0 | Retain every positive eigenvalue |
| `Threshold(t)` | $t_{\mathrm{abs}}/s + t_{\mathrm{rel}}\lambda_{\max}$ | Sum of absolute and relative terms specified in covariance units |

Every eigenvalue at or below the threshold is discarded, preventing rounding-scale eigenvalues from spuriously increasing dimension under the default policy.
Preparation takes $O(d^3)$ time and $O(d^2)$ storage; one sample uses $r$ standard normals in $O(dr)$.
`rank()` returns the retained rank, `support_basis()` the $d \times r$ orthonormal basis describing support directions, and `covariance()` the covariance reconstructed from retained components.
`dimension()` returns dimension $d$, `location()` mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, and `rank_options()` and `options()` the two construction settings.

Samples from a singular law ($r < d$) lie on the $r$-dimensional affine subspace through $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ spanned by the columns of $Q_r$.
`contains_support(x, tolerance)` measures the support-projection residual in the maximum norm and checks whether

$$
\bigl\|(I - Q_rQ_r^{\mathsf T})(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\bigr\|_\infty \le \tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\,\|\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}\|_\infty
$$


$\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}$ are the absolute and relative tolerances of `Tolerance`; a full-rank law includes every finite point in its support.
Since no density exists with respect to $d$-dimensional Lebesgue measure, `log_pseudo_density(x, tolerance)` returns the following log-density relative to $r$-dimensional measure on the support:

$$
\ln f_r(\mathbf{x}) = -\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[r\ln(2\pi) + \sum_{j=1}^{r}\ln(s\lambda_j) + \sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\bigl(\mathbf{q}_j^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\bigr)^2}{s\lambda_j}\Bigr]
$$

Outside support it returns $-\infty$.
Here $\lambda_j$ is an eigenvalue of normalized $\Sigma/s$, $\mathbf{q}_j$ its eigenvector, and thus $s\lambda_j$ an eigenvalue of $\Sigma$.
Calling `LogDensity::log_pdf` on a singular law gives `InvalidInput` directing the caller to use pseudodensity.
Support checks and pseudodensity take $O(dr)$; singular laws allocate $O(d + r)$ workspace per call.
`evaluate_support` and `evaluate_log_pseudo_density` accept the same workspace as sampling (`dimension + rank` elements) and evaluate without allocation.
[Normal distributions with positive semidefinite covariance and their support](../../examples/singular-normal-support/) shows how rank policies change samples and pseudodensities.

## Multivariate laws

Every vector-valued law samples in the following four forms.

- `sample` returns one sample in an owned `Vec`.
- `sample_n` returns $n$ samples arranged by row in an owned `Vec`.
- `sample_n_into` writes rows to the caller's row buffer according to `SamplingPolicy`, leaving failed rows unpublished.
- `sample_indexed_into` generates row $i$ from subsequence `root.split(i)`, making results independent of execution policy.

`sample_width` returns elements per sample, while `workspace_requirements(policy, n)` and `workspace(policy, n)` return batch workspaces; matrix-valued laws return row-major flattened samples.
Constructors in the table use default `LawOptions`; `with_options` takes settings after the same arguments to change accuracy and limits (`MultivariateNormal` and `MultivariateStudentT` also take `MultivariateNormalOptions` before them).

| Law | Constructor | Sample space | Generation algorithm |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MultivariateNormal` | `new(&mean, &covariance)` | $\boldsymbol{\mu} + \operatorname{span}Q_r \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ | Eigenfactor $B = Q_r\Lambda_r^{1/2}$ and $r$ standard normals |
| `MultivariateStudentT` | `new(&location, &shape, df)`, limit `gaussian_limit(&location, &shape)` | Same | $\boldsymbol{\mu} + B\mathbf{Z}\sqrt{\nu/W}$ ($W \sim \chi^2_\nu$ generated logarithmically) |
| `Dirichlet` | `new(&alpha)`, $\alpha_i > 0$ | Simplex $\{x_i \ge 0, \sum x_i = 1\}$ | Divide independent $\mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha_i, 1)$ values by their sum. Divide by their maximum before summation; normalize logarithmically if any $\alpha_i < 1$ |
| `Multinomial` | `new(trials, &probabilities)` | Counts summing to `trials` | Draw conditional binomials sequentially |
| `DirichletMultinomial` | `new(trials, &alpha)` | Counts summing to `trials` | Draw conditional beta-binomials sequentially without forming a Dirichlet vector |
| `MultivariateHypergeometric` | `new(&population, draws)` | Counts not exceeding each color's population | Draw a hypergeometric for each color against the remaining colors; the final color gets the remainder |
| `RandomTable` | `new(&rows, &cols)` | Contingency tables with fixed margins (row-major) | Draw each row as multivariate hypergeometric from remaining column sums; the final row gets the remainder |
| `NormalInverseGamma` | `new(mu, lambda, alpha, beta)` | $\mathbb{R} \times (0, \infty)$ | Return $[x, v]$ with $v \sim \mathrm{InvGamma}(\alpha, \beta)$ and $x \mid v \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, v/\lambda)$ |

`shape` of `MultivariateStudentT` is a scale matrix, not covariance; for $\nu > 2$, covariance is $\nu/(\nu - 2)$ times that matrix.
Mean exists only for $\nu > 1$ and variance only for $\nu > 2$; otherwise an undefined-moment error is returned.
Rank decisions, support checks, and pseudodensity follow the same rules as `MultivariateNormal`.
The only elliptical law provided is multivariate t (and its normal limit); elliptical laws with an arbitrary specified radial distribution are not provided.

Count-vector laws form log-masses as sums of conditional univariate log-masses, avoiding differences of large log-factorials.
`Multinomial` normalizes probabilities after checking their sum equals 1 within $8k\varepsilon$ (where $k$ is the category count); zero-probability categories never occur in samples.
`NormalInverseGamma` has a one-dimensional location; no normal-inverse-gamma law with a vector location is provided.
For vectors, draw variance $v$ from an inverse gamma, scale a zero-mean `MultivariateNormal` sample with covariance $\Sigma$ by $\sqrt{v/\lambda}$, and add $\boldsymbol{\mu}$.
Draw $v$ either by drawing $1/v$ with `Gamma::new(alpha, 1/beta)` ([Transformations, rejection, and tail control for continuous distributions](oxref:random-continuous-variate-combinations)) and taking its reciprocal, or directly from `Affine::new(GeneralizedGamma::new(alpha, -1.0)?, 0.0, beta)?` ([Classical continuous-distribution families](oxref:random-families-other-standard)).
The latter scales the generalized gamma with $X^{-1} \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha, 1)$ by $\beta$ and equals $\mathrm{InvGamma}(\alpha, \beta)$.

## Matrix and group laws

| Law | Constructor | Sample space | Generation algorithm |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Wishart` | `new(df, &scale)`, $\nu > p - 1$ | $p \times p$ positive-definite matrix | Bartlett decomposition $W = LBB^{\mathsf T}L^{\mathsf T}$ ($LL^{\mathsf T}$ is the scale; $B$ is lower triangular with $B_{ii}^2 \sim \chi^2_{\nu - i + 1}$ ($i = 1, \dots, p$) and standard normals strictly below the diagonal) |
| `InverseWishart` | `new(df, &scale)`, $\nu > p - 1$ | $p \times p$ positive-definite matrix | $Y^{\mathsf T}Y$ with $Y = B^{-1}L^{\mathsf T}$ |
| `MatrixNormal` | `new(&mean, &row, &col)` | $m \times n$ matrix | $M + L_UZL_V^{\mathsf T}$ ($U = L_UL_U^{\mathsf T}$ is the $m \times m$ row covariance; $V = L_VL_V^{\mathsf T}$ is the $n \times n$ column covariance) |
| `MatrixT` | `new(&mean, &row, &col, df)` | $m \times n$ matrix | $M + S^{1/2}ZL_V^{\mathsf T}$ ($S \sim W_m^{-1}(\nu + m - 1, \text{row})$) |
| `OrthogonalGroup` | `new(d)` | $O(d)$ | Factor a standard normal $d \times d$ matrix and fix signs of the orthogonal factor so the triangular factor has positive diagonal (Mezzadri 2007) |
| `SpecialOrthogonalGroup` | `new(d)` | $SO(d)$ | As above; reverse the sign of the final row if the determinant is $-1$ |
| `UnitaryGroup` | `new(d)` | $U(d)$ | Factor a complex normal matrix and fix diagonal phases. Entries are `Complex` |
| `RandomCorrelation` | `new(&eigenvalues)` | Correlation matrices with diagonal 1 | Form $Q\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)Q^{\mathsf T}$ with Haar $Q$, then set the diagonal to 1 with Givens rotations (Bendel–Mickey in the stable Davies–Higham formulation) |

`row` and `col` of `MatrixT` are positive-definite matrices describing row and column spread rather than covariance; in one dimension this is a t distribution with scale $\sqrt{\text{row}\cdot\text{col}/\nu}$.
Diagonal $\chi$ variates in Bartlett decomposition are generated logarithmically, avoiding zero diagonals even for small noninteger degrees of freedom.
If a diagonal still rounds to 0 in the output scalar type, it returns `NumericalInstability` (`Underflow`).
Positive-definite matrix laws check support through equal symmetric entries and successful Cholesky factorization.
Density quadratic forms $\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}X)$ and $\operatorname{tr}(V^{-1}A^{\mathsf T}U^{-1}A)$ are evaluated through Cholesky solves without forming inverses.
A sample from each group or correlation-matrix law costs $O(d^3)$.

Haar measure on the orthogonal group is the unique probability measure invariant under multiplication by any group element.
A matrix $G$ of independent standard normal entries has the same law after right multiplication by orthogonal matrix $H$.
Factoring $G = RQ$ with positive diagonal in triangular factor $R$ makes the factorization unique; from $GH = R(QH)$, the orthogonal factor of $GH$ is $QH$.
Since $GH$ and $G$ have the same law, so do $QH$ and $Q$, making the law of $Q$ Haar measure.
Without sign correction, the orthogonal factor lacks this invariance because the factorization implementation's sign choices depend on $G$.
`SpecialOrthogonalGroup` maps Haar measure from $O(d)$ to $SO(d)$ using the measure-preserving bijection from $O(d) \setminus SO(d)$ to $SO(d)$ given by reversing the final row.

`RandomCorrelation` takes nonnegative eigenvalues summing to $d$ and constructs a correlation matrix with those eigenvalues.
Rotations preserve eigenvalues, so output eigenvalues agree with the specified sequence within rounding.
This law is not uniform over correlation matrices with fixed eigenvalues.

## Checking Haar measure through rotation angles

An element of $SO(3)$ is a rotation through angle $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ about some axis, satisfying $\operatorname{tr} Q = 1 + 2\cos\theta$.
Under Haar measure its angle density is $(1 - \cos\theta)/\pi$, making small rotations uncommon (Miles 1965).
Integrating this density gives $\mathbb{E}[\cos\theta] = -1/2$, $\mathbb{E}[\cos^2\theta] = 1/2$, $\mathbb{E}[\cos^3\theta] = -3/8$, and $\mathbb{E}[\cos^4\theta] = 3/8$, yielding trace moments

$$
\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{tr} Q] = 0,\qquad \mathbb{E}[(\operatorname{tr} Q)^2] = 1,\qquad \mathbb{E}[(\operatorname{tr} Q)^4] = 3
$$


Thus $\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{tr} Q) = 1$ and $\operatorname{Var}((\operatorname{tr} Q)^2) = 2$; means of $n$ samples have standard errors $\sqrt{1/n}$ and $\sqrt{2/n}$.

The following cell generates $n$ rotations from `SpecialOrthogonalGroup::<f64>::new(3)`, counts angles in 18 bins of width 10°, and compares them with density $(1 - \cos\theta)/\pi$.
Interval $[a, b]$ has probability $\bigl[(\theta - \sin\theta)/\pi\bigr]_a^b$; the plot band gives $\pm 2$ SE of its mean density.
The first table gives sample means of two trace moments, their theoretical values, and $z = (\text{estimate} - \text{theory})/\mathrm{SE}$.
The second gives the largest absolute entry of $Q^{\mathsf T}Q - I$ and maximum $\lvert\det Q - 1\rvert$, computed directly for each sample.
Inputs are sample size $n$ and SplitMix64 seed.

```rust
//| id: random-haar-rotation-angle
//| ref: random-haar-rotation-angle
//| title: "Check SO(3) Haar rotation angles and trace moments"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   sample_count: {"type":"range","label":"Rotation sample size n [count]","description":"Changes frequency and moment standard errors as 1/√n.","min":1000,"max":20000,"step":1000,"value":5000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"SplitMix64 seed [-]","description":"Observe sample-to-sample fluctuations in z.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"large","label":"n = 20000","description":"Standard errors halve and frequencies approach the density curve.","inputs":{"sample_count":20000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"structure","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"structure","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: large
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
use scioxide_core::{NumericScalar, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{SeedableRng, SpecialOrthogonalGroup, SplitMix64};

let n = sample_count.round() as usize;
let nf = n as f64;
let bins = 18_usize;
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let width = pi / bins as f64;
let law = SpecialOrthogonalGroup::<f64>::new(3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let mut counts = vec![0.0_f64; bins];
let (mut first, mut second) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
let (mut orthogonality, mut determinant) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
for _ in 0..n {
    // q is a row-major 3 × 3 matrix; q[3i + j] is entry (i, j).
    let q = law.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    for i in 0..3 {
        for j in 0..3 {
            let dot: f64 = (0..3).map(|k| q[3 * k + i] * q[3 * k + j]).sum();
            let identity = if i == j { 1.0 } else { 0.0 };
            orthogonality = RealScalar::max(orthogonality, NumericScalar::abs(dot - identity));
        }
    }
    let det = q[0] * (q[4] * q[8] - q[5] * q[7]) - q[1] * (q[3] * q[8] - q[5] * q[6])
        + q[2] * (q[3] * q[7] - q[4] * q[6]);
    determinant = RealScalar::max(determinant, NumericScalar::abs(det - 1.0));
    let trace = q[0] + q[4] + q[8];
    (first, second) = (first + trace, second + trace * trace);
    // Rotation angle θ = arccos((tr Q − 1)/2) ∈ [0, π]. Clamp values rounded beyond ±1 to the endpoints.
    let cosine = RealScalar::clamp((trace - 1.0) / 2.0, -1.0, 1.0);
    let bin = ((RealScalar::acos(cosine) / width) as usize).min(bins - 1);
    counts[bin] += 1.0;
}
// Independent check: interval probability [(θ − sin θ)/π]ₐᵇ and ±2 SE of its mean density.
let (mut band, mut observed) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for (b, &count) in counts.iter().enumerate() {
    let (a, c) = (b as f64 * width, (b + 1) as f64 * width);
    let p = ((c - RealScalar::sin(c)) - (a - RealScalar::sin(a))) / pi;
    let se = RealScalar::sqrt(p * (1.0 - p) / nf) / width;
    let (center, mean_density) = ((a + 0.5 * width) * 180.0 / pi, p / width);
    band.push([
        center,
        mean_density,
        mean_density - 2.0 * se,
        mean_density + 2.0 * se,
    ]);
    observed.push([center, count / (nf * width)]);
}
let density: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..=90)
    .map(|i| {
        let theta = pi * i as f64 / 90.0;
        [theta * 180.0 / pi, (1.0 - RealScalar::cos(theta)) / pi]
    })
    .collect();
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Rotation angle θ [°]",
        "yLabel": "Probability density [1/rad]",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "layers": [
            {"id": "band", "kind": "band", "name": "Mean bin density ± 2 SE", "points": band},
            {
                "id": "density",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "(1 − cos θ)/π",
                "style": {"lineType": "dashed"},
                "points": density
            },
            {"id": "observed", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Sample frequency density", "points": observed}
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "angles" })
);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let moment_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Haar theoretical value [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "z [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
// Standard errors use Var(tr Q) = 1 and Var((tr Q)²) = 2.
let (mean, square) = (first / nf, second / nf);
let (se_mean, se_square) = (RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 / nf), RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 / nf));
emit_table_with_columns!(
    moment_columns,
    vec![
        ("tr Q", mean, 0.0, mean / se_mean),
        ("(tr Q)²", square, 1.0, (square - 1.0) / se_square),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "moments" })
);
let structure_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "max |QᵀQ − I| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "max |det Q − 1| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    structure_columns,
    vec![(orthogonality, determinant)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "structure" })
);
```

With the defaults ($n = 5\,000$, seed 3), the sample mean of $\operatorname{tr} Q$ is $-0.0066$ ($z = -0.47$), and that of $(\operatorname{tr} Q)^2$ is $1.0107$ ($z = 0.54$), both agreeing with theory within 1 SE.
Changing seeds to 1 and 1000 gives $z$ in the range $-0.77$ to $1.81$; with $n = 20\,000$, the values are $0.24$ and $0.22$.
Angle frequency densities follow the curve $(1 - \cos\theta)/\pi$, which is smaller as $\theta$ approaches 0 and increases toward $2/\pi \approx 0.64$ rad⁻¹ as $\pi$ is approached.
With defaults, 16 of 18 bins lie inside the $\pm 2$ SE band; each bin falls outside with probability about 4.6%, so two outside bins are not evidence of bias.
Entries of $Q^{\mathsf T}Q - I$ have maximum absolute value $1.1 \times 10^{-15}$, and $\lvert\det Q - 1\rvert$ has maximum $8.9 \times 10^{-16}$, establishing orthogonality and determinant at rounding scale.
Uncorrected orthogonal factors are also orthogonal matrices, so orthogonality cannot determine whether they follow Haar measure; check angle frequencies and trace moments.

## Summary

`MultivariateNormal` normalizes covariance by its largest entry before symmetric eigendecomposition and retains only eigenvalues exceeding the threshold of `RankPolicy` (default $d\varepsilon\lambda_{\max}$), supporting positive semidefinite covariance.
Handle singular laws through support checks and $r$-dimensional pseudodensity; `log_pdf` is unavailable.
Vector, matrix, and group laws sample through structure-appropriate decompositions (conditional univariate sampling, Bartlett decomposition, and orthogonal factorization with sign correction).
Group elements produced by a custom factorization do not follow Haar measure unless triangular diagonal signs are fixed.
Use `OrthogonalGroup`, `SpecialOrthogonalGroup`, and `UnitaryGroup` for group sampling; check Haar behavior through angles and trace moments rather than orthogonality.
