Joint failure probabilities in reliability analysis and coverage probabilities of simultaneous confidence intervals in multiple comparisons can be expressed as the probability $P(\mathbf{a} < \mathbf{X} \le \mathbf{b})$ that a correlated normal vector enters a rectangle.
General rectangles and correlations have no elementary closed form, so `scioxide-random` uses Genz variable separation to obtain a smooth integrand, integrates it with randomized quasi-random points, and reports estimated error and termination status.
This page explains the separation transformation, plan preparation, error estimation, termination statuses and closed-form cases, and workspace reuse.
Preparation of the law itself (eigendecomposition and rank) is in [Multivariate-normal eigendecomposition and singular support](oxref:random-multivariate-normal-eigen).

## Numerical calculation of multivariate joint probabilities

Consider rectangle probability $P(\mathbf{a} < \mathbf{X} \le \mathbf{b})$ for $\mathbf{X} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + L\mathbf{Z}$ (where $\mathbf{Z}$ contains $r$ independent standard normal variables).
If $L$ is a $d \times r$ lower trapezoidal matrix with positive diagonal, constraint $i$, conditional on fixed $Z_1, \dots, Z_{i-1}$, becomes the following interval for $Z_i$:

$$
\alpha_i = \frac{a_i - \mu_i - \sum_{k<i}L_{ik}z_k}{L_{ii}},\qquad
\beta_i = \frac{b_i - \mu_i - \sum_{k<i}L_{ik}z_k}{L_{ii}}
$$


At each stage, Genz variable separation multiplies the weight by normal interval probability $e_i = \Phi(\beta_i) - \Phi(\alpha_i)$ and constructs the next truncated normal coordinate as $z_i = \Phi^{-1}\bigl(\Phi(\alpha_i) + u_i e_i\bigr)$ from uniform coordinate $u_i$.
$\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF.
The probability becomes integral $\int e_1(\mathbf{u})\cdots e_r(\mathbf{u})\,d\mathbf{u}$ over unit hypercube $[0, 1]^{r-1}$, with a smooth integrand taking values in $[0, 1]$.
For multivariate t $\mathbf{X} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + L\mathbf{Z}/\sqrt{W/\nu}$, an additional coordinate generates $W$ through a $\chi^2_\nu$ quantile; multiply centered bounds by $\sqrt{W/\nu}$ before performing the same integration.

`prepare_probability(options, &root)` forms a lower trapezoidal factor from retained factor $B$ through column-pivoted Householder LQ factorization, fixes the diagonal signs to positive, and returns `MultivariateProbabilityPlan`.
Reversing column signs together with latent-variable signs preserves the law, so this normalization leaves probabilities unchanged.
Preparation costs $O(d^2 r)$; evaluating several rectangles with one plan reuses the factorization and sequence preparation.
The Sobol dimension is retained rank $r$, rather than $d$ ($r + 1$ for multivariate t, including its scale coordinate); the normal integrand uses $r - 1$ of these coordinates.

Each evaluation reorders the first $r$ constraints and rebuilds the factor to integrate constraints with smaller marginal interval probabilities first (Genz–Bretz ordering).
Integrating strongly restrictive constraints first concentrates integrand variation in leading Sobol coordinates and reduces estimator variance.

## Randomized Sobol sequences and error estimation

The plan's `rectangle(&lower, &upper)` uses independently randomized Sobol replicates (Matoušek linear-matrix scrambling and digital shifts) generated from the Philox root `root` supplied to `prepare_probability`, allocates a fresh workspace, and evaluates on the calling thread.
`rectangle_scoped(&lower, &upper, &mut workspace, scope)` reuses a workspace and runs replicates through the executor of the execution scope (`ExecutionContext` or an outer solve's `SolveContext`), checking cancellation and deadlines before each integrand evaluation.
Results are independent of execution order; a sequential scope produces the same report as `rectangle`.

With $R$ replicates (default 8) and $N$ points per replicate (a power of 2, default 65,536), quadrature always uses $RN$ evaluations, without adapting the point count to accuracy.
Each evaluation costs $O(dr)$.
Each replicate mean $\hat{p}_1, \dots, \hat{p}_R$ is unbiased; the reported probability is their mean $\bar{p}$, and `estimated_absolute_error` is six times the standard error computed from replicate means:

$$
6\,\mathrm{SE} = 6\sqrt{\frac{1}{R(R-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{R}(\hat{p}_j - \bar{p})^2}
$$


Replicate means are stored logarithmically and divided by their maximum before averaging, avoiding underflow even for very small probabilities.
6 SE is an empirical estimate, not a proven upper bound.
Assuming normally distributed replicate means, the probability that the error of $\bar{p}$ exceeds 6 SE is $P(|t_7| > 6) \approx 5.4 \times 10^{-4}$ from a t distribution with $R - 1 = 7$ degrees of freedom.

## Reports and termination statuses

`MultivariateProbabilityReport` contains `probability`, `log_probability` retained even under underflow, `estimated_absolute_error` and its logarithm, `evaluations`, and `termination`.
`termination` (`MultivariateProbabilityTermination`) is the authoritative status: `Converged` if 6 SE is at most $\tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}}\,\bar{p}$, or `AccuracyNotMet` if accuracy remains unmet after evaluating all specified points.
$\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}$ are the absolute and relative tolerances of the settings' `Tolerance`.
`AccuracyNotMet` is not an error; the estimate and estimated error remain usable.
If the integrand is 0 in every replicate, the result is `AccuracyNotMet` with probability 0 and estimated error 1.

The following cases return closed-form answers without quadrature, with `Converged` and 0 evaluations.

- Equal lower and upper bounds in any component give probability 0; rank 0 gives 0 or 1 according to whether the point mass lies in the rectangle.
- Rank 1 reduces to an interval probability of a single latent variable (univariate t interval probability for multivariate t).
- A full-rank normal with diagonal factor has independent components, giving a product of univariate interval probabilities.

Bounds of length other than $d$ give `DimensionMismatch`; NaN bounds or $a_i > b_i$ give `InvalidInput`.

## Settings, workspaces, and convenience law queries

`MultivariateProbabilityOptions` contains the following values.

| Setting | Default | Preparation check |
|---|---|---|
| `tolerance` | Absolute and relative both $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ | Accepts any `Tolerance` |
| `points_per_replicate` | 65,536 | `InvalidInput` unless a power of 2 |
| `replicates` | 8 | `InvalidInput` if below 2 |
| `work_budget` | 524,288 evaluations | `ConvergenceFailure` if $RN$ exceeds it |
| `max_bytes` | 64 MiB | `ResourceLimit` if plan and workspace exceed it |
| `execution_policy` | Sequential | Results are independent of execution policy |

`MultivariateProbabilityWorkspace` contains each replicate's conditional and sequence coordinates, the reordered factor, and bounds; allocating it once with `plan.workspace()` allows reuse for evaluations of the same plan.
`rectangle_scoped` checks only workspace shape (dimension, rank, replicate count, sequence dimension) and returns `InvalidInput` for a mismatch.
Workspaces from other plans are accepted when their shapes match; workspace contents after an error are unspecified.

The law's `rectangle(&lower, &upper, &root)` (`MultivariateNormal` and `MultivariateStudentT`) constructs a plan from its `LawOptions` tolerances, work, and storage limits, evaluates one rectangle, and returns only the probability.
Replicate count is the smaller of the work limit and 8; points per replicate are the largest power of 2 at most the work limit divided by replicate count, giving 8 replicates × 8,192 points for default `LawOptions` (65,536 work).
It returns `ConvergenceFailure` unless `Converged`, so use `prepare_probability` for reports or multiple rectangles sharing a plan.
Trait queries such as `Cdf` and `Survival` build the same plan with root `Philox4x64::from_seed(0)` seeded at 0; `cdf(x)` equals `rectangle` with lower bounds $-\infty$ and seed 0.
Correlated laws of rank at least 2 rarely meet default tolerance $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, so use a law constructed with looser tolerances through `with_options` for these forms.

## Checking estimated errors with quadrant probabilities

The following cell computes quadrant probability $P(X_1 \le 0, X_2 \le 0)$ of a bivariate standard normal with correlation $\rho$ using eight point counts per replicate from $N = 2^6$ through $2^{13}$.
Sheppard's closed form $1/4 + \arcsin(\rho)/(2\pi)$ provides the independent check.
Inputs are correlation (dimensionless, $-1$ through $1$), absolute-tolerance exponent, and Philox seed; replicate count stays at its default 8.
The log-log plot shows estimated error 6 SE (dashed) and actual closed-form error (markers) versus evaluations; the horizontal line gives absolute tolerance.
The table gives estimates, both errors, and termination statuses for each point count.
The final outcome uses the termination status at the largest count $N = 2^{13}$ as the cell conclusion, adding the closed-form value and difference as diagnostics.

```rust
//| id: random-mvn-orthant-probability
//| ref: random-mvn-orthant-probability
//| title: "Compare estimated and actual errors for bivariate-normal quadrant probabilities"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   correlation: {"type":"range","label":"Correlation ρ [-]","description":"At ±1 rank is 1; at 0 covariance is diagonal. Both are evaluated in closed form.","min":-1,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.5}
//|   tolerance_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Tolerance exponent t (absolute tolerance 10ᵗ)","description":"Changes the absolute tolerance compared with estimated error 6 SE.","min":-7,"max":-3,"step":1,"value":-5}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Philox seed [-]","description":"Determines the 8 Sobol randomizations.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":7}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"strong-negative","label":"ρ = −0.9","description":"Probability is small at 0.0718, changing relative estimated error for the same point count.","inputs":{"correlation":-0.9}}
//|   - {"id":"independent","label":"ρ = 0","description":"Diagonal covariance gives the closed-form 1/4 with 0 evaluations and no quadrature.","inputs":{"correlation":0}}
//|   - {"id":"degenerate","label":"ρ = 1","description":"Rank becomes 1, giving latent interval probability 1/2 in closed form.","inputs":{"correlation":1}}
//|   - {"id":"strong-positive","label":"ρ = 0.95","description":"Strong correlation makes the first coordinate determine most of the integral, reducing actual error.","inputs":{"correlation":0.95}}
//|   - {"id":"tight","label":"Tolerance 10⁻⁷","description":"Even the largest count leaves 6 SE above tolerance, giving AccuracyNotMet.","inputs":{"tolerance_exponent":-7}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"AccuracyNotMet"}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/7/5","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/7/2","approx":{"value":0.3333333,"atol":2e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/7/3","range":{"min":1e-8,"max":5e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/7/4","range":{"min":0,"max":5e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: strong-negative
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/7/2","approx":{"value":0.0717831,"atol":2e-6}}
//|   - case: independent
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.25,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: degenerate
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-15}}
//|   - case: tight
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/7/5","equals":"AccuracyNotMet"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"partial"}
use scioxide_core::{NumericScalar, RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_random::{
    MultivariateNormal, MultivariateProbabilityOptions, MultivariateProbabilityTermination,
    Philox4x64, SeedableRng,
};

let rho = correlation;
let covariance = Matrix::from_vec(2, 2, vec![1.0, rho, rho, 1.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let law = MultivariateNormal::new(&[0.0; 2], &covariance).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tolerance = RealScalar::powi(10.0, tolerance_exponent.round() as i32);
// Independent check: Sheppard's closed form P(X₁ ≤ 0, X₂ ≤ 0) = 1/4 + asin(ρ)/(2π).
let exact = 0.25 + RealScalar::asin(rho) / (2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI);
let root = Philox4x64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let (mut estimated_curve, mut actual_curve, mut rows) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut last = None;
for k in 6..=13_u32 {
    // Use 2ᵏ points in each of 8 randomized Sobol sequences.
    let options = MultivariateProbabilityOptions::default()
        .with_points_per_replicate(1 << k)
        .with_tolerance(Tolerance::new(tolerance, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    let plan = law
        .prepare_probability(options, &root)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let report = plan
        .rectangle(&[f64::NEG_INFINITY; 2], &[0.0; 2])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let actual = NumericScalar::abs(report.probability - exact);
    let total = report.evaluations as f64;
    // Omit zero errors (such as closed-form cases) from the logarithmic plot but retain them in the table.
    if report.estimated_absolute_error > 0.0 {
        estimated_curve.push([total, report.estimated_absolute_error]);
    }
    if actual > 0.0 {
        actual_curve.push([total, actual]);
    }
    let termination = match report.termination {
        MultivariateProbabilityTermination::Converged => "Converged",
        MultivariateProbabilityTermination::AccuracyNotMet => "AccuracyNotMet",
    };
    rows.push((
        1_usize << k,
        report.evaluations,
        report.probability,
        report.estimated_absolute_error,
        actual,
        termination,
    ));
    last = Some((report, actual, termination));
}
emit_line_chart!(

        serde_json::json!({

    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Integrand evaluations [count]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "annotations": [{"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": tolerance, "label": "Absolute tolerance"}]
    })
);
let fixed7 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 7 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Points per replicate N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Evaluations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Estimated probability [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed7 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Estimated error 6 SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Actual error against closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination" }

emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "points" })
);
// The cell conclusion uses termination at the largest point count N = 2¹³.
let (report, actual, status) = last.ok_or("Empty point-count sequence")?;
let outcome = if report.termination == MultivariateProbabilityTermination::Converged {
    Outcome::satisfied("Estimated error 6 SE met tolerance at N = 2¹³")
        .explanation("6 SE is estimated from replicate variation and is not an upper bound.")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Estimated error 6 SE remained above tolerance after N = 2¹³ evaluations")
        .explanation("The estimate remains usable. Rebuild the plan with more points or replicates.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(status)
        .diagnostic("Evaluations", report.evaluations as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Estimated error 6 SE", report.estimated_absolute_error, None)
        .diagnostic("Closed form 1/4 + asin(ρ)/(2π)", exact, None)
        .diagnostic("Difference from closed form", actual, None)
);
```

For default $\rho = 0.5$ (closed form $1/3$), tolerance $10^{-5}$, and seed 7, estimated error 6 SE is $3.5 \times 10^{-4}$ at 512 evaluations and $3.5 \times 10^{-7}$ at 65,536.
Multiplying evaluations by 128 reduces error by a factor of about 1000, giving a rate of roughly $N^{-1.4}$, faster than independent-random Monte Carlo’s $N^{-1/2}$.
Actual errors in every row are 0.06 to 0.23 times 6 SE, or 0.3 to 1.4 SE, so estimated error never underestimates actual error.
`termination` is `AccuracyNotMet` through 4,096 evaluations and `Converged` from 8,192 (6 SE $5.1 \times 10^{-6}$), giving a satisfied outcome.
Changing the seed to 1000 gives 6 SE $1.05 \times 10^{-5}$ at 8,192, slightly above tolerance, returning `AccuracyNotMet`.
Near tolerance, termination status depends on the chosen randomization.
At tolerance $10^{-7}$, even 6 SE $3.5 \times 10^{-7}$ at 65,536 fails to meet it, giving `AccuracyNotMet` in every row and a partial outcome.

For $\rho = -0.9$, probability is small at $0.07178$, reaching `Converged` at 1,024 evaluations, with actual error $9.2 \times 10^{-12}$ at 65,536.
Even for $\rho = 0.95$, actual error at 8,192 is $1.4 \times 10^{-9}$; with stronger correlation, most of the integral is determined by the first coordinate and variation from the remaining coordinates is small.
At $\rho = 0$ the factor is diagonal; at $\rho = \pm 1$ rank is 1, so every point count returns closed forms ($1/4$, $1/2$, 0) with 0 evaluations and both estimated and actual error 0.
In these cases no points can be plotted on logarithmic axes, leaving the plot empty.

## Summary

Joint rectangle probabilities are transformed into smooth integrals on $[0, 1]^{r-1}$ through Genz separation and evaluated with randomized Sobol replicates at fixed evaluation counts.
The report's `termination` is authoritative: `Converged` means 6 SE met tolerance; `AccuracyNotMet` means the estimate is usable but accuracy is unconfirmed.
For `AccuracyNotMet`, rebuild the plan with more points or replicates; since 6 SE is empirical, verify important values using closed forms or another method.
For multiple rectangles, prepare a plan once through `prepare_probability` and reuse the workspace through `rectangle_scoped`.
