Generators return two kinds of integers.
Native words are reference-implementation integers returned by `next_u32` and `next_u64`; their ranges need not cover all of $2^{32}$ or $2^{64}$.
Uniform words returned by `next_uniform_u32` and `next_uniform_u64` have every bit uniform.
This page explains how generators with narrow native ranges construct uniform words, why these are exactly uniform, and how many native words they consume.
Generator recurrences appear in [Generator families](oxref:random-prng-families).

## Native-word ranges

Each generator's native-word range is as follows.

| Generator | Native range $r$ | `next_u32` | `next_u64` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SplitMix64`, `Xoshiro256StarStar`, `Pcg64`, `Lcg64`, `Philox4x64`, `Threefry4x64` | $2^{64}$ | Part of a 64-bit native word (high 32 bits for `Lcg64`, `Philox4x64`, `Threefry4x64`; low 32 bits otherwise) | One native word |
| `Xoshiro128StarStar`, `Mt19937`, `Taus2`, `Taus113`, `Gfsr4` | $2^{32}$ | One native word | Two native words concatenated low first |
| `Mrg32k3a` | $2^{32} - 209$ (native words from $1$ through $2^{32} - 209$) | One native word | Two native words concatenated low first |
| `Mrg`, `Cmrg` | $2^{31} - 1$ (native words at least $0$ and below $2^{31} - 1$) | One native word | Two native words concatenated low first |
| `Ranlux`, `Ranlxs` | $2^{24}$ | One native word | Two native words concatenated low first |
| `Ranlxd` | $2^{48}$ | High 32 native bits | One native word (48 bits) |

For generators with ranges at most $2^{31}$ (`Mrg`, `Cmrg`, `Ranlux`, `Ranlxs`), the leading bit of `next_u32` is always zero, and `next_u64` likewise has a zero leading bit in each 32-bit half.
Native words of `Mrg32k3a` use the leading bit but exclude 209 values: zero and values at least $2^{32} - 208$.
Dividing narrow-range words by $2^{32}$ or $2^{64}$ to map into $[0, 1)$ reaches only part of the interval.
Sampling that uses integer bits directly, such as index sampling, integer laws, and fast Ziggurat paths, reads uniform words rather than native words.

## Constructing uniform words

`next_uniform_u32` combines native words whose range $r$ is narrower than $2^{32}$ as base-$r$ digits.
For `Mrg32k3a`, whose native lower limit is 1, subtract 1 to obtain digits at least $0$ and below $r$.
For the smallest $k$ satisfying $r^k \ge 2^{32}$, construct the following from $k$ native words $d_0, \dots, d_{k-1}$:

$$
v = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} d_j\, r^j \qquad (0 \le v < r^k)
$$

The first native word is the least significant digit.
Let $L = r^k - (r^k \bmod 2^{32})$ be the largest multiple of $2^{32}$ not exceeding $r^k$; if $v \ge L$, discard all $k$ words and draw again.
Return the low 32 bits $v \bmod 2^{32}$ of accepted $v$.

This word is exactly uniform for the following reason.
If digits $d_j$ are independent and uniform on $\{0, \dots, r-1\}$, then $v$ is uniform on $\{0, \dots, r^k - 1\}$.
Accepted $v$ is uniform on $\{0, \dots, L - 1\}$; since $L$ is a multiple of $2^{32}$, every value $w$ has exactly $L / 2^{32}$ values of $v$ satisfying $v \equiv w \pmod{2^{32}}$.
The returned word is therefore uniform on $\{0, \dots, 2^{32} - 1\}$.

Acceptance probability is $a = L / r^k$, and the redraw count follows a geometric law.
The expected native-word count (transitions) per uniform word is $k/a$.
For native range $2^{32}$, $k = 1$ and $a = 1$, returning the same value as `next_u32`.
`Ranlxd`, with range $2^b$ ($b > 32$), directly uses the high 32 bits of its 48-bit native word as a uniform word.

The 64-bit uniform word `next_uniform_u64` applies the same rule for $2^{64}$.
Native ranges $2^{24}$, $2^{31} - 1$, and $2^{32} - 209$ group $k = 3$ words; 48-bit native words of `Ranlxd` group $k = 2$.
For range $2^{32}$, simply concatenate two native words; return one 64-bit native word directly.

Stop after 1,024 redraws; exceeding this returns `ConvergenceFailure` with reason `IterationLimit`.
Used native words remain consumed and count toward position.
Intermediate digits are not retained for the next call, so generator state alone reproduces mixed sequences of native-word, uniform-word, and floating-point calls.

## Relationship to floating-point uniforms

Floating-point values in $[0, 1)$ returned by `Rng::next_float` map one native word rather than a uniform word.
For example, `f64` of `Mrg32k3a` is $z_n / (m_1 + 1)$ ($z_n$ is the native word, $m_1 = 2^{32} - 209$), while `Ranlux` maps a 24-bit native word packed into the high bits.
For narrow-range generators, uniform-word sampling and floating-point sampling such as inversion therefore read different native-word counts per sample even with identical seeds.
Floating-point conversion and open interval $(0, 1)$ are described in [Conversion to uniform words and floating-point values](oxref:random-algorithms-uniform-conversion).

The next cell generates $N$ native and uniform words each using classical generators and `Mrg32k3a` and `Ranlxd`.
Inputs are seed and word count $N$ (1,000–20,000).
The table shows native range $r$, maximum `next_u32`, fractions of native and uniform words with leading bit 1, standardized uniform-word fraction $z = (\hat{q} - 1/2)/\mathrm{SE}$, transitions consumed per uniform word, and theoretical value $k/a$.
For uniform words, the expected fraction with leading bit 1 is $1/2$, with standard error $\mathrm{SE} = 1/(2\sqrt{N})$.
The theoretical value $k/a$ is an independent check calculated by hand from $r$.

```rust
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    BitGenerator, Cmrg, Gfsr4, Mrg, Mrg32k3a, Mt19937, Ranlux, Ranlxd, Ranlxs, SeedableRng, Taus2,
};

// (generator, range r, maximum next_u32, native and uniform leading-bit fractions, uniform z, transitions/word, theory)
type Row = (String, u64, u32, f64, f64, f64, f64, f64);

// Independent check: expected transitions k / a to construct a 32-bit uniform word from native range r.
fn expected_transitions(radix: u64) -> f64 {
    let (target, r) = (1_u128 << 32, u128::from(radix));
    let (mut range, mut digits) = (r, 1);
    while range < target {
        range *= r;
        digits += 1;
    }
    let accepted = range - range % target;
    digits as f64 * (range as f64 / accepted as f64)
}

fn measure<R>(name: &str, radix: u64, seed: u64, n: usize) -> Result<Row, String>
where
    R: SeedableRng + BitGenerator,
{
    let mut native = R::from_seed(seed);
    let mut uniform = R::from_seed(seed);
    let (mut maximum, mut native_top, mut uniform_top) = (0_u32, 0_usize, 0_usize);
    for _ in 0..n {
        let word = native.next_u32().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        maximum = maximum.max(word);
        native_top += (word >> 31) as usize;
        let word = uniform.next_uniform_u32().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        uniform_top += (word >> 31) as usize;
    }
    let consumed = uniform.position().ok_or("Position is untracked")?;
    let rate = uniform_top as f64 / n as f64;
    let z = (rate - 0.5) / (0.5 / RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64));
    Ok((
        name.to_string(),
        radix,
        maximum,
        native_top as f64 / n as f64,
        rate,
        z,
        consumed as f64 / n as f64,
        expected_transitions(radix),
    ))
}

let seed = seed.round() as u64;
let n = words.round() as usize;
let full = 1_u64 << 32;
let rows = vec![
    measure::<Mt19937>("Mt19937", full, seed, n)?,
    measure::<Taus2>("Taus2", full, seed, n)?,
    measure::<Gfsr4>("Gfsr4", full, seed, n)?,
    measure::<Mrg32k3a>("Mrg32k3a", full - 209, seed, n)?,
    measure::<Mrg>("Mrg", (1 << 31) - 1, seed, n)?,
    measure::<Cmrg>("Cmrg", (1 << 31) - 1, seed, n)?,
    measure::<Ranlux>("Ranlux", 1 << 24, seed, n)?,
    measure::<Ranlxs>("Ranlxs", 1 << 24, seed, n)?,
    measure::<Ranlxd>("Ranlxd", 1 << 48, seed, n)?,
];
let rate4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let z2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let digits6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Generator" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Native range r", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum next_u32", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Native leading-bit fraction [-]", "type": "number", "format": rate4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Uniform leading-bit fraction [-]", "type": "number", "format": rate4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Uniform-word z [-]", "type": "number", "format": z2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Transitions per uniform word [transitions]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Theoretical k/a [transitions]", "type": "number", "format": digits6 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "words" })
);
```

With defaults (seed 7, $N = 10\,000$), native leading-bit fractions for `Mrg`, `Cmrg`, `Ranlux`, and `Ranlxs` are zero.
Uniform leading-bit fractions for the same generators scatter around $1/2$ with SE $= 0.005$; $z$ across nine rows ranges from $-0.94$ to $2.00$.
If nine rows were independent, the chance of at least one $|z|$ reaching 2 would be $1 - 0.9545^9 \approx 0.34$, so $z = 2.00$ for `Cmrg` is explained by statistical fluctuation.
`Mt19937`, `Taus2`, and `Gfsr4` have range $2^{32}$ and use one transition per uniform word; `Ranlxd` also needs one transition because it uses the high 32 bits.
`Mrg32k3a`, `Mrg`, `Cmrg`, `Ranlux`, and `Ranlxs` use two transitions, differing from theoretical $k/a$ by less than $10^{-14}$.
Acceptance probability $a$ is 1 for $r = 2^{24}$, $1 - r^{-2}$ for $r = 2^{31} - 1$, and $1 - 43\,681/r^2$ for $r = 2^{32} - 209$, so redraws are rare.
The maximum `next_u32` of `Ranlxd`, 4,294,800,509, is the high 32 bits of a 48-bit native word; its native leading-bit fraction is 0.5047.

## Summary

Native words follow reference implementations; for `Ranlux`, `Ranlxs`, `Mrg`, `Cmrg`, and `Mrg32k3a`, their range is smaller than $2^{32}$.
When all-bit-uniform integers are needed, use `next_uniform_u32` or `next_uniform_u64` for exactly uniform words from rejection combining native digits.
For narrow-range generators, 32-bit uniform words consume about two native words and 64-bit uniform words about three; include this factor when estimating consumption from position.
Uniform-word and floating-point sampling read different native-word counts per sample, so sequences differ even with identical seeds.
