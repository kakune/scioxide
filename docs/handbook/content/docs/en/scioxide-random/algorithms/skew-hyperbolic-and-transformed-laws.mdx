Families introducing skewness or tail weight into symmetric distributions divide into five constructions.

- Multiplying a normal density by a CDF to introduce skewness (skew normal).
- Changing scale or decay rate on either side of the origin (two-piece Cauchy, asymmetric Laplace).
- Transforming normal or beta variables, or adding another variable to a normal (Johnson SU, Jones–Faddy skew t, exponentially modified normal).
- Joining a normal center to a power tail on one side (Crystal Ball).
- Mixing the variance and mean of a normal variable (generalized hyperbolic, normal inverse Gaussian).
  The positive mixing-variable law (generalized inverse Gaussian) is included here too.

This page defines each family in constructor parameters and explains sampling, quantiles, and mean and variance existence.
The final cell inspects samples from five families using Kolmogorov–Smirnov distances and moment confidence intervals.

Interpret KS distances and $p$-values as in [Comparing CDFs and moments with samples](oxref:random-families-sample-check).

## Family definitions and parameter domains

These laws are standard forms without location or scale parameters; add location and scale through `Affine::new(law, μ, σ)`.
$\varphi$ and $\Phi$ are the standard normal density and CDF, $K_\nu$ the modified Bessel function of the second kind, and $B(a, b)$ the beta function.
For families marked `with_options`, `new` uses default `LawOptions` and `with_options` takes settings after the parameters.
Constructors return `DomainError` with reason `OutsideDomain` for finite parameters outside the domain, or `NonFinite` for NaN or infinite parameters.

| Constructor | Parameter domain | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| `SkewNormal::new(a)` (`with_options`) | $a$ finite | $f(x) = 2\varphi(x)\Phi(ax)$. $a = 0$ is standard normal |
| `SkewCauchy::new(a)` | $\lvert a \rvert < 1$ | $f(x) = 1/\bigl(\pi(1 + x^2/s^2)\bigr)$ with $s = 1 - a$ ($x < 0$) and $s = 1 + a$ ($x \ge 0$). $P(X < 0) = (1 - a)/2$ |
| `JonesFaddySkewT::new(a, b)` (`with_options`) | $a, b > 0$ | $f(x) = (1 + u)^{a + 1/2}(1 - u)^{b + 1/2}/\bigl(2^{r-1}B(a, b)\sqrt{r}\bigr)$ with $r = a + b$ and $u = x/\sqrt{r + x^2}$. $a = b$ is $t$ with $2a$ degrees of freedom |
| `AsymmetricLaplace::new(kappa)` | $\kappa > 0$ | $f(x) = \frac{\kappa}{1 + \kappa^2} e^{-\kappa x}$（$x \ge 0$）, $\frac{\kappa}{1 + \kappa^2} e^{x/\kappa}$（$x < 0$）. $P(X < 0) = \kappa^2/(1 + \kappa^2)$ |
| `ExponentiallyModifiedNormal::new(k)` (`with_options`) | $k > 0$ ($k$ causing overflow in $1/k$ gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`)) | $X = Z + kE$ ($Z$ standard normal, $E$ exponential with mean 1), $F(x) = \Phi(x) - e^{-x/k + 1/(2k^2)}\Phi(x - 1/k)$ |
| `JohnsonSU::new(a, b)` | $a$ finite, $b > 0$ | $X = \sinh\bigl((Z - a)/b\bigr)$, $F(x) = \Phi(a + b\operatorname{asinh} x)$ |
| `CrystalBall::new(beta, m)` | $\beta > 0$, $m > 1$ | $f(x) \propto e^{-x^2/2}$ ($x > -\beta$), $e^{-\beta^2/2}\bigl(1 + \beta(-x - \beta)/m\bigr)^{-m}$ ($x \le -\beta$). Only the left tail is a power law |
| `GeneralizedInverseGaussian::new(p, b)` (`with_options`) | $p$ finite, $b > 0$ | Support $x > 0$, $f(x) \propto x^{p-1}\exp\bigl(-b(x + 1/x)/2\bigr)$ |
| `GeneralizedHyperbolic::new(p, a, b)` (`with_options`) | $a > 0$ and $\lvert b \rvert < a$ (including $b = 0$), or $\lvert b \rvert = a$ and $p < 0$ | $f(x) = \dfrac{\gamma^p a^{1/2 - p}}{\sqrt{2\pi}K_p(\gamma)}(1 + x^2)^{(p - 1/2)/2}K_{p - 1/2}\bigl(a\sqrt{1 + x^2}\bigr)e^{bx}$ with $\gamma = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ ($\gamma = 0$ is its limit) |
| `NormalInverseGaussian::new(a, b)` (`with_options`) | $a > \lvert b \rvert$ (including $b = 0$) | $p = -1/2$ with `GeneralizedHyperbolic` |

`GeneralizedHyperbolic` is the standard form fixing $\delta = 1$ and $\mu = 0$ among the usual five parameters $(\lambda, \alpha, \beta, \delta, \mu)$; $(p, a, b)$ corresponds to $(\lambda, \alpha, \beta)$.
Construct the law with $\delta \ne 1$ by passing $\alpha\delta$ and $\beta\delta$ to $a$ and $b$, then adding scale $\delta$ and location $\mu$ through `Affine`.

## Sampling methods

Skew normal uses Azzalini's representation $X = \delta\lvert Z_0 \rvert + \sqrt{1 - \delta^2}\,Z_1$ ($\delta = a/\sqrt{1 + a^2}$, with independent standard normals $Z_0, Z_1$), without rejection.
Jones–Faddy skew t is generated from $U \sim \mathrm{Beta}(a, b)$ as $X = \sqrt{r}\,(2U - 1)/\bigl(2\sqrt{U(1 - U)}\bigr)$.
Exponentially modified normal sums a normal and an exponential variate; Johnson SU transforms a normal variate.
Two-piece Cauchy, asymmetric Laplace, and Crystal Ball use inverse-transform sampling, mapping open-interval uniforms through closed-form quantiles.

Generalized hyperbolic sampling uses the normal variance-mean mixture

$$
X = bW + \sqrt{W}\,Z, \qquad Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1),\ W \perp Z
$$


For $\gamma > 0$, mixing variable $W$ is $V/\gamma$ (where $V$ follows `GeneralizedInverseGaussian` with parameters $(p, \gamma)$), with density proportional to $w^{p-1}\exp\bigl(-(1/w + \gamma^2 w)/2\bigr)$.
At $\lvert b \rvert = a$ ($\gamma = 0$), $W$ degenerates to inverse gamma, so form $W = 1/(2G)$ directly from $G \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(-p, 1)$.
There is no location parameter; conditional on $W$, $X$ is normal with mean $bW$ and variance $W$.

Generalized inverse Gaussian samples use rejection in coordinates $y = \ln x$.
The density of $y$ is proportional to $e^{py - b\cosh y}$; for $p \ge 0$ its mode is $y^\ast = \operatorname{asinh}(p/b)$.
Setting $t = y - y^\ast$ makes the log-density $-\ell(t)$ ($\ell$ is convex with $\ell(0) = 0$).
The proposal distribution has three pieces.
Its center is flat at height $1$ ($e^{-\ell} \le 1$) over interval $[t_-, t_+]$ containing the region $\ell < 1$; determine endpoints by doubling the width until $\ell \ge 1$.
Both tails are exponentials with endpoint slopes $\ell'(t_\pm)$; convexity of $\ell$ gives $e^{-\ell(t)} \le e^{-\ell'(t_\pm)(t - t_\pm)}$, bounding the density from above.
Each proposal uses three open-interval uniforms; no acceptance after 1,024 proposals returns `ConvergenceFailure`.
Samples for $p < 0$ are reciprocals of samples with $\lvert p \rvert$, using that $1/V$ follows generalized inverse Gaussian with $(-p, b)$.

## Computing CDFs and quantiles

Closed-form CDFs and quantiles are available for two-piece Cauchy (cotangent on each piece), asymmetric Laplace, Johnson SU ($\Phi^{-1}$ and $\sinh$), and Crystal Ball (piecewise formulas splitting the power tail and normal center by probability).
Jones–Faddy skew t uses that $u = (1 + x/\sqrt{r + x^2})/2$ follows beta $\mathrm{Beta}(a, b)$, giving its CDF through regularized incomplete beta $I_u(a, b)$ and quantiles through its inverse.
Special-function iterations consume the work of `LawOptions`.
The CDF uses the smaller coordinate of $u$ and $1 - u$, and quantiles also form $x$ from the smaller coordinate, never forming $1 - (\text{a value near 1})$ even in far tails.

For $a > 0$ and $x < 8$, the skew-normal upper tail uses closed form $S(x) = \bar\Phi(x) + 2T(x, a)$ with Owen's $T$ function.
For $x \ge 8$ and on the $a < 0$ side where two terms nearly cancel, it integrates the density directly.

Exponentially modified normal combines its two CDF terms logarithmically and numerically inverts quantiles.
Generalized inverse Gaussian integrates tail densities in coordinates $y = \ln x$; generalized hyperbolic uses $t = \operatorname{asinh} x$.
Both integrate only the tail away from the mode, forming the opposite side as its complement.
The exponent $-a\sqrt{1 + x^2} + bx$ in the generalized hyperbolic density subtracts two large terms, so identity $a\sqrt{1 + x^2} - bx - \gamma = (\gamma x - b)^2/(a\sqrt{1 + x^2} + bx + \gamma)$ rewrites it in the right-hand form to avoid cancellation.

Skew-normal, generalized inverse Gaussian, and generalized hyperbolic (including normal inverse Gaussian) quantiles use bisection based only on whether the smaller log-tail exceeds the target, shrinking the interval to adjacent floating-point numbers.
Exponentially modified normal quantiles use numerical inversion stopping at the tolerance in `LawOptions`.
Every CDF evaluation for one quantile shares the same work limit.
Most skew-normal tails ($a > 0$) use Owen's $T$ function, allowing quantiles within default work 65,536.
Generalized hyperbolic CDFs perform quadrature per evaluation, so one quantile requires tens of quadratures.
For normal inverse Gaussian ($a = 2$, $b = 1$), one CDF evaluation uses thousands of work units; all tested quantiles from $p = 10^{-10}$ through $0.999$ reached the default limit and returned `ConvergenceFailure`.
For quantiles, pass a larger limit such as `LawOptions::default().with_work_budget(Budget::try_from(1 << 20)?)` to `with_options`.

## Mean and variance

| Family | Mean | Variance | Existence condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SkewNormal` | $\delta\sqrt{2/\pi}$ | $1 - 2\delta^2/\pi$ | Always |
| `SkewCauchy` | Not implemented | Not implemented | Nonexistent |
| `JonesFaddySkewT` | $\dfrac{(a - b)\sqrt{a + b}}{2}\dfrac{\Gamma(a - 1/2)\Gamma(b - 1/2)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}$ | $\dfrac{(a + b)\bigl((a - b)^2 + a + b - 2\bigr)}{4(a - 1)(b - 1)} - (\text{mean})^2$ | Mean: $a, b > 1/2$; variance: $a, b > 1$ |
| `AsymmetricLaplace` | $1/\kappa - \kappa$ | $1/\kappa^2 + \kappa^2$ | Always |
| `ExponentiallyModifiedNormal` | $k$ | $1 + k^2$ | Always |
| `JohnsonSU` | $-e^{1/(2b^2)}\sinh(a/b)$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}(e^{1/b^2} - 1)\bigl(e^{1/b^2}\cosh(2a/b) + 1\bigr)$ | Always |
| `CrystalBall` | Sum of power-tail and normal-center contributions | From second raw moment | Order $k$ requires $m > k + 1$ |
| `GeneralizedInverseGaussian` | $K_{p+1}(b)/K_p(b)$ | From second raw moment | Always |
| `GeneralizedHyperbolic` | $b\,\mathrm{E}[W]$ | $\mathrm{E}[W] + b^2\operatorname{Var}[W]$ | Always for $\gamma > 0$. For $\gamma = 0$, $\mathrm{E}[W^k]$ requires $-p > k$ |
| `NormalInverseGaussian` | $b/\gamma$ | $a^2/\gamma^3$ | Always |

Generalized hyperbolic $\mathrm{E}[W^k]$ is $K_{p+k}(\gamma)/\bigl(K_p(\gamma)\gamma^k\bigr)$ for $\gamma > 0$.
No family on this page implements `Entropy`.

## Comparing CDFs and moments with samples

The following cell generates $n$ samples with seed `seed` from five families using different sampling methods.
The first table reports KS distance $D_n$, $\sqrt{n}\,D_n$, and approximate $p$-value from the asymptotic Kolmogorov distribution, measuring disagreement between sampler and CDF.
The second gives 95% confidence intervals for sample mean and variance and $z$ against values computed in the cell from the table's formulas (independent check).
None of the five uses inverse-transform sampling, so KS distance compares independent implementations.
Variance standard errors require fourth moments, so Jones–Faddy parameters are chosen to satisfy $\min(a, b) > 2$.

use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    Cdf, Distribution, ExponentiallyModifiedNormal, JohnsonSU, JonesFaddySkewT, Kolmogorov,
    NormalInverseGaussian, SeedableRng, SkewNormal, SplitMix64, Survival,
};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

// Generate and sort samples, returning the CDF F(x) at each point.
fn draw<D: Distribution<f64> + Cdf<f64>>(
    law: &D,
    n: usize,
    seed: u64,
) -> Result<(Vec<f64>, Vec<f64>), String> {
    let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed);
    let mut x = (0..n)
        .map(|_| law.sample(&mut rng))
        .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    x.sort_by(f64::total_cmp);
    let f = x
        .iter()
        .map(|&v| law.cdf(v))
        .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok((x, f))
}

let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "fractionDigits": 3 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig5 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "significantDigits": 5 });
let n = sample_count as usize;
let seed = seed as u64;
let gamma = |x: f64| scioxide_special::gamma(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string());

let ((x, f), exact) = match family.as_str() {
    "SkewNormal(a=4)" => {
        let delta = 4.0 / RealScalar::sqrt(17.0);
        let law = SkewNormal::new(4.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let mean = delta * RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 / PI);
        (draw(&law, n, seed)?, [mean, 1.0 - mean * mean])
    }
    "JonesFaddySkewT(a=6, b=3)" => {
        let (a, b) = (6.0, 3.0);
        let ratio = gamma(a - 0.5)? * gamma(b - 0.5)? / (gamma(a)? * gamma(b)?);
        let mean = (a - b) * RealScalar::sqrt(a + b) / 2.0 * ratio;
        let second =
            (a + b) * ((a - b) * (a - b) + a + b - 2.0) / (4.0 * (a - 1.0) * (b - 1.0));
        let law = JonesFaddySkewT::new(a, b).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

For `JonesFaddySkewT` ($a = 6$, $b = 3$), sample mean 1.26722 ($z = -1.26$) compares with closed form 1.30465, and sample variance 1.77044 ($z = -1.48$) with 1.89789; both closed-form values lie within the 95% confidence intervals.
For `NormalInverseGaussian` ($a = 2$, $b = 1$), $D_n = 0.0294$ and the $p = 0.063$ value is relatively small.
Using $n = 5\,000$ with the same seed gives $p = 0.531$; for $n = 5\,000$ with seeds 1 and 1000, values are 0.844 and 0.350, so 0.063 is consistent with seed variation.
Differences from closed-form mean $b/\gamma = 0.57735$ and variance $a^2/\gamma^3 = 0.76980$ are $z = 1.22$ and $0.01$.
For `ExponentiallyModifiedNormal` ($k = 1.5$, $n = 5\,000$), $D_n = 0.0106$ ($p = 0.626$), mean $z = 0.32$, and variance $z = -0.24$.

Since normal inverse Gaussian CDFs integrate per point, the KS distance for $n = 5\,000$ repeats quadrature 5,000 times.
Work resets for each CDF point; all tested inputs (15 combinations from $n = 500$ through $5\,000$ and seeds 1 through 999) completed within default work 65,536.

## Summary

Skew normal, Jones–Faddy skew t, exponentially modified normal, and Johnson SU transform normal or beta variables; two-piece Cauchy, asymmetric Laplace, and Crystal Ball map open-interval uniforms through closed-form quantiles.
Generalized hyperbolic and normal inverse Gaussian are standard forms without location parameters, sampling through mixture $X = bW + \sqrt{W}Z$ with $W$ generated by generalized inverse Gaussian rejection (inverse gamma for $\gamma = 0$).
Their quantiles use bisection with tens of CDF quadratures, exceeding default work; increase the work of `LawOptions`.
Two-piece Cauchy implements neither mean nor variance; Jones–Faddy, Crystal Ball, and generalized hyperbolic with $\gamma = 0$ have parameter-dependent moment existence.
Transformed or mixed samples can be inspected independently of CDF implementations through both KS distance and closed-form moments.
