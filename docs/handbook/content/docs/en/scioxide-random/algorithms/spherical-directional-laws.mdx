Data describing three-dimensional directions, such as crystal orientations, cosmic-ray arrival directions, molecular orientations, and particle motion in turbulence, are points on the unit sphere.
Spherical probability laws generate such directions randomly or model directions concentrated around a mean direction.
`scioxide-random` provides `UniformDirection` and `VonMisesFisher` as laws on the unit sphere $S^{d-1} = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d : \lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert = 1\}$.
This page explains densities and generation algorithms for both laws, derives uniform-direction component moments, and describes the component along a von Mises–Fisher mean direction.
Circular angle laws and principal intervals are in [Probability laws for angles on the circle](../circular-and-directional-laws/).

## Uniform spherical directions

`UniformDirection::new(d)` is uniform on the unit sphere $S^{d-1}$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$.
Its density is reciprocal area $\Gamma(d/2)/(2\pi^{d/2})$, equal to $1/(4\pi)$ for $d = 3$.
For $d \ge 2$, a sample is $\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{Z}/\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert$, formed by generating standard normal vector $\mathbf{Z} \sim \mathcal{N}_d(\mathbf{0}, I)$ and dividing by its norm.
The density of $\mathbf{Z}$ depends only on $\lVert\mathbf{z}\rVert$ and is invariant under any rotation; the normalized $\mathbf{X}$ law is therefore rotationally invariant too.
The only rotationally invariant spherical probability measure is uniform, so $\mathbf{X}$ is uniform.
The norm accumulates component by component with `hypot` to avoid overflow; zero-norm vectors (a probability-zero event) are redrawn.
For $d = 1$ the sphere is $\{-1, +1\}$, returning either sign with probability 1/2.
`contains_support(x)` returns true when every component has magnitude at most $1 + 8\varepsilon$ and $\bigl|\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert^2 - 1\bigr| \le 8d\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon$ is the scalar's machine epsilon).
`new` returns `InvalidInput` for $d = 0$ and `ConvergenceFailure` if $d$ exceeds `work_budget`.

Component moments follow from symmetry and $\lVert\mathbf{X}\rVert = 1$.
$X_j$ and $-X_j$ have the same law, giving $\mathbb{E}[X_j] = 0$.
Permuting components preserves the law, and $\sum_j X_j^2 = 1$ gives $\mathbb{E}[X_j^2] = 1/d$.
Independence of $\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{Z}/\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert$ and $\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert$ gives, from $\mathbb{E}[Z_j^4] = \mathbb{E}[X_j^4]\,\mathbb{E}\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert^4$,

$$
\mathbb{E}[X_j^4] = \frac{3}{d(d+2)},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(X_j^2) = \frac{3}{d(d+2)} - \frac{1}{d^2} = \frac{2(d-1)}{d^2(d+2)}
$$

using $\mathbb{E}[Z_j^4] = 3$ and $\mathbb{E}\lVert\mathbf{Z}\rVert^4 = d(d+2)$.
`mean()` returns the zero vector; `variance()` returns $1/d$ in every component.

## Checking uniform-direction components

The following cell generates $n$ directions from `UniformDirection` and compares component sample means and second moments $\overline{X_j^2} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i X_{ij}^2$ with theoretical 0 and $1/d$.
Since the mean is known (0), variance estimation uses the uncentered second moment.
Inputs are dimension $d$ (2 through 8), sample size $n$, and SplitMix64 seed.
Independent checks are the previously derived $\mathbb{E}[X_j] = 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_j^2] = 1/d$, and $\operatorname{Var}(X_j^2) = 2(d-1)/(d^2(d+2))$, with standard errors $\mathrm{SE}(\bar{X}_j) = \sqrt{1/(dn)}$ and $\mathrm{SE}(\overline{X_j^2}) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X_j^2)/n}$.
The plot gives component $\overline{X_j^2}$ with $\pm 2$ SE error bars and a horizontal reference at $1/d$.
The table reports component means, second moments, and their $z = (\text{estimate} - \text{theory})/\mathrm{SE}$ values; the final table gives maximum norm error and $\sum_j \overline{X_j^2}$.

```rust
//| id: random-uniform-direction
//| ref: random-uniform-direction
//| title: "Compare uniform spherical component means and second moments with theory"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   dimension: {"type":"range","label":"Ambient dimension d [-]","description":"Determines the dimension of sphere S^{d−1} and changes theoretical second moment 1/d.","min":2,"max":8,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   sample_count: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size n [count]","description":"Changes standard error as 1/√n.","min":1000,"max":20000,"step":1000,"value":4000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"SplitMix64 seed [-]","description":"Observe sample-to-sample fluctuations in z.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":83}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"circle","label":"d = 2","description":"Uniform circular directions, with theoretical second moment 1/2 and Var(X²) = 1/8.","inputs":{"dimension":2}}
//|   - {"id":"high-dimension","label":"d = 8, n = 20000","description":"Smaller component fluctuations, with theoretical second moment 1/8.","inputs":{"dimension":8,"sample_count":20000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: circle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|   - case: high-dimension
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/7/2","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"components","path":"/rows/7/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
use scioxide_core::{NumericScalar, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{SeedableRng, SplitMix64, UniformDirection};

let d = dimension.round() as usize;
let n = sample_count.round() as usize;
let law = UniformDirection::<f64>::new(d).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
// Accumulate component sums Σ X_ij and Σ X_ij² and maximum norm error.
let (mut first, mut second) = (vec![0.0_f64; d], vec![0.0_f64; d]);
let mut max_norm_error = 0.0_f64;
for _ in 0..n {
    let x = law.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let norm = x.iter().fold(0.0, |s: f64, &v| RealScalar::hypot(s, v));
    max_norm_error = RealScalar::max(max_norm_error, NumericScalar::abs(norm - 1.0));
    for (j, &v) in x.iter().enumerate() {
        first[j] += v;
        second[j] += v * v;
    }
}
// Independent check: symmetry and ‖X‖ = 1 give E[X_j] = 0, E[X_j²] = 1/d, and E[X_j⁴] = 3/(d(d+2)).
let (nf, df) = (n as f64, d as f64);
let target = 1.0 / df;
let se_mean = RealScalar::sqrt(target / nf);
let se_second = RealScalar::sqrt((3.0 / (df * (df + 2.0)) - target * target) / nf);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut points = Vec::new();
for j in 0..d {
    let mean = first[j] / nf;
    let moment = second[j] / nf;
    rows.push((
        j + 1,
        mean,
        mean / se_mean,
        moment,
        (moment - target) / se_second,
    ));
    let label = (j + 1) as f64;
    points.push([
        label,
        moment,
        moment - 2.0 * se_second,
        moment + 2.0 * se_second,
    ]);
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "errorbar",
        "xLabel": "Component index j [-]",
        "yLabel": "Mean of second moment X_j² [-]",
        "xMin": 0.5,
        "xMax": 8.5,
        "series": [{"name": "Sample second moment (±2 SE)", "points": points}],
        "annotations": [{"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": target, "label": "Theoretical 1/d"}]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "second-moments" })
);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Component j", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Mean z [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Second moment [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Second-moment z [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "components" })
);
let norm_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum norm error max|‖X‖ − 1| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Σ_j second moments [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    norm_columns,
    vec![(max_norm_error, second.iter().sum::<f64>() / nf)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "norms" })
);
```

For default $d = 3$, $n = 4\,000$, and seed 83, mean $z$ values of the three components are $0.90$, $-0.45$, and $0.42$; second moments are $0.3335$, $0.3261$, and $0.3404$ ($z$ values $0.03$, $-1.54$, $1.51$), each within 2 SE of theoretical $1/3$.
Each quantity has probability about 4.6% of $|z| > 2$, so one of six $z$ values exceeding 2 is not alone evidence of bias.
Changing seeds to 1 and 1000 gives second-moment $z$ in $-1.73$ to $1.43$; for $n = 20\,000$ the range is $-1.70$ to $1.34$.
Maximum norm error is $3.3 \times 10^{-16}$, at normalization-rounding scale.

The second-moment sum $\sum_j \overline{X_j^2}$ equals 1 within rounding because every sample satisfies $\sum_j X_{ij}^2 = 1$, so it is not a statistical test.
For the same reason, component second moments are negatively correlated; at $d = 2$ the two $z$ values differ only in sign ($-0.18$ and $0.18$).
For $d = 8$ and $n = 20\,000$, theory falls to $1/8$; the eight second-moment $z$ values range from $-1.47$ to $1.15$ and mean $z$ from $-1.95$ to $1.28$.

## von Mises–Fisher distribution

The von Mises–Fisher density with mean direction $\boldsymbol{\mu} \in S^{d-1}$ and concentration $\kappa \ge 0$ is

$$
f(\mathbf{x}) = C_d(\kappa)\,e^{\kappa\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{x}},\qquad
C_d(\kappa) = \frac{\kappa^{d/2-1}}{(2\pi)^{d/2}I_{d/2-1}(\kappa)}
$$

with mean $A_d(\kappa)\boldsymbol{\mu}$ ($A_d = I_{d/2}/I_{d/2-1}$).
$I_\nu$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind; $\kappa = 0$ gives uniformity and $d = 2$ gives the von Mises distribution.
`VonMisesFisher::new(&mean, concentration)` requires $d \ge 2$ and unit vector `mean` within the same tolerance as `contains_support`; otherwise it returns `InvalidInput` or `DomainError`.
If $\kappa$ is below 1 or $d/2 - 1$, it uses a normalized Bessel series, otherwise scaled Bessel functions; for $\kappa > 10^8 d^2$ it uses an asymptotic expansion of $1 - A_d$.
`mean_resultant_length()` returns $A_d(\kappa)$ and `direction()` the supplied mean direction.

Samples are generated by separating component $W = \boldsymbol{\mu}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{X}$ along $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ from a uniform orthogonal direction.
The marginal density of $W$ is proportional to $(1 - w^2)^{(d-3)/2}e^{\kappa w}$, with methods varying by dimension.

- $\kappa = 0$ uses the same method as `UniformDirection`.
- $d = 2$ generates angles using the same sampler as `VonMises` (uniform-envelope rejection for $\kappa < 0.5$, Best–Fisher for $\kappa \ge 0.5$), then rotates by the mean-direction angle.
- $d = 3$ has marginal density proportional to $e^{\kappa w}$, allowing closed-form CDF inversion to produce $W = 1 + \kappa^{-1}\ln\bigl(U + (1 - U)e^{-2\kappa}\bigr)$.
- $d \ge 4$ uses Wood's (1994) rejection method with candidates from $\mathrm{Beta}\bigl(\frac{d-1}{2}, \frac{d-1}{2}\bigr)$ variates.

Every case stores $1 - W$ separately, preserving the small tangential component $\sqrt{(1 - W)(1 + W)}$ for large $\kappa$.
The tangential direction is uniform on $S^{d-2}$; a final Householder reflection sends the north pole to $\boldsymbol{\mu}$.
The reflection's reference pole is chosen opposite the sign of $\mu_1$ to avoid denominator cancellation.

## Checking von Mises–Fisher samples

For $d = 3$, $W = \boldsymbol{\mu}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{X}$ has density proportional to $e^{\kappa w}$ on $[-1, 1]$; integration gives

$$
\mathbb{E}[W] = \coth\kappa - \frac{1}{\kappa},\qquad
\mathbb{E}[W^2] = 1 - \frac{2\,\mathbb{E}[W]}{\kappa},\qquad
P(W > \cos\alpha) = \frac{1 - e^{-\kappa(1 - \cos\alpha)}}{1 - e^{-2\kappa}}
$$


$\mathbb{E}[W^2]$ follows by integration by parts, and $P(W > \cos\alpha)$ is the probability of falling within angle $\alpha$ of $\boldsymbol{\mu}$; both are closed forms without Bessel functions.
The $\kappa \to 0$ limits are uniform-distribution values $0$, $1/3$, and $(1 - \cos\alpha)/2$.

The following cell generates $n$ directions from `VonMisesFisher` with mean direction $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (0.6, 0, 0.8)$, comparing the sample mean of $W$ and fraction within $30^\circ$ of $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ with the closed forms above.
$\boldsymbol{\mu}$ is tilted from the north pole, so samples pass through the Householder reflection.
The standard error of $W$ is $\sqrt{(\mathbb{E}[W^2] - \mathbb{E}[W]^2)/n}$ and that of the fraction is $\sqrt{P(1 - P)/n}$.
The three-dimensional view plots the first 600 directions and $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ on the unit sphere, allowing rotation to inspect concentration.
Inputs are concentration $\kappa$, sample size $n$, and SplitMix64 seed.

```rust
//| id: random-von-mises-fisher-sphere
//| ref: random-von-mises-fisher-sphere
//| title: "Compare spherical von Mises–Fisher samples with closed forms"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   concentration: {"type":"range","label":"Concentration κ [-]","description":"At 0 it is uniform; larger values concentrate around μ.","min":0,"max":40,"step":0.5,"value":5}
//|   sample_count: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size n [count]","description":"Changes standard error as 1/√n.","min":1000,"max":20000,"step":1000,"value":4000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"SplitMix64 seed [-]","description":"Observe sample-to-sample fluctuations in z.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":29}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"uniform","label":"κ = 0","description":"Uniform distribution, with E[W] = 0 and fraction within 30° equal to (1 − cos 30°)/2 ≈ 0.067.","inputs":{"concentration":0}}
//|   - {"id":"concentrated","label":"κ = 40","description":"Almost all directions fall within 30° of μ, with E[W] = coth 40 − 1/40 = 0.975.","inputs":{"concentration":40}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.8000908039820194,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"norms","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: uniform
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|   - case: concentrated
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
use scioxide_core::{NumericScalar, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{SeedableRng, SplitMix64, VonMisesFisher};

let (kappa, n) = (concentration, sample_count.round() as usize);
let nf = n as f64;
let mu = [0.6, 0.0, 0.8];
let law = VonMisesFisher::<f64>::new(&mu, kappa).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
// sample_n returns n directions arranged by row in a sequence of length 3n.
let samples = law.sample_n(&mut rng, n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let cos_alpha = RealScalar::cos(30.0 * std::f64::consts::PI / 180.0);
let (mut w_sum, mut inside, mut norm_error) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
let mut points = Vec::new();
for (i, x) in samples.chunks(3).enumerate() {
    let w = mu[0] * x[0] + mu[1] * x[1] + mu[2] * x[2];
    w_sum += w;
    inside += if w > cos_alpha { 1.0 } else { 0.0 };
    let norm = x.iter().fold(0.0, |s: f64, &v| RealScalar::hypot(s, v));
    norm_error = RealScalar::max(norm_error, NumericScalar::abs(norm - 1.0));
    if i < 600 {
        points.push(serde_json::json!({
            "id": format!("x{i}"),
            "position": [x[0], x[1], x[2]],
            "group": "samples"
        }));
    }
}
points.push(serde_json::json!({ "id": "mu", "position": mu, "group": "mean", "label": "μ" }));
// Independent check: W = μᵀX has density proportional to e^{κw}, giving closed forms for E[W], E[W²], and P(W > cos α).
let (mean_w, second_w, cap) = if kappa == 0.0 {
    (0.0, 1.0 / 3.0, (1.0 - cos_alpha) / 2.0)
} else {
    let a = 1.0 / RealScalar::tanh(kappa) - 1.0 / kappa;
    let cap = RealScalar::exp_m1(-kappa * (1.0 - cos_alpha)) / RealScalar::exp_m1(-2.0 * kappa);
    (a, 1.0 - 2.0 * a / kappa, cap)
};
let library = law.mean_resultant_length().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (w_bar, fraction) = (w_sum / nf, inside / nf);
let se_w = RealScalar::sqrt((second_w - mean_w * mean_w) / nf);
let se_cap = RealScalar::sqrt(cap * (1.0 - cap) / nf);
let (z_w, z_cap) = ((w_bar - mean_w) / se_w, (fraction - cap) / se_cap);
emit_scene3d!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "points": points,
        "groups": [
            { "id": "samples", "label": "Sample directions" },
            { "id": "mean", "label": "Mean direction μ" }
        ],
        "axes": {
            "x": { "label": "x₁", "unit": "-" },
            "y": { "label": "x₂", "unit": "-" },
            "z": { "label": "x₃", "unit": "-" }
        }
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "directions", "title": "Directions on the unit sphere" })
);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample value [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "mean_resultant_length() [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": fixed4,
        "nullText": "Not applicable"
    },
    { "key": "4", "label": "z [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![
        ("Mean of W = μᵀX", w_bar, mean_w, Some(library), z_w),
        ("Fraction within 30° of μ", fraction, cap, None, z_cap),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum norm error max|‖X‖ − 1| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
    ]),
    vec![(norm_error,)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "norms" })
);
```

With the defaults ($\kappa = 5$, $n = 4\,000$, seed 29), the sample mean of $W$ is 0.7973, differing from closed form $\coth 5 - 1/5 = 0.8001$ by $z = -0.90$.
`mean_resultant_length()` returns the same 0.8001 as the closed form within rounding.
The fraction within $30^\circ$ of $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ is 0.4830, differing from closed form 0.4882 by $z = -0.66$.
With seeds 1 and 1000, both $z$ values remain between $-1.30$ and $0.76$.
At $\kappa = 0$, the fraction is 0.0655, close to uniform value $(1 - \cos 30^\circ)/2 = 0.0670$ ($z = -0.38$); at $\kappa = 40$ it is 0.9952, nearly equal to closed form 0.9953 ($z = -0.04$).
Maximum norm error is $4.4 \times 10^{-16}$, so unit vectors are preserved at rounding scale after reflection.
In the three-dimensional view, samples at $\kappa = 5$ cluster around $\boldsymbol{\mu} = (0.6, 0, 0.8)$; setting $\kappa$ to 0 spreads them over the whole sphere.

## Summary

`UniformDirection` normalizes normal vectors; component mean 0, second moment $1/d$, and $\operatorname{Var}(X_j^2) = 2(d-1)/(d^2(d+2))$ can be checked with standard errors.
`VonMisesFisher` switches generation by dimension (von Mises angles for $d = 2$, closed-form inversion for $d = 3$, Wood rejection for $d \ge 4$) and maps to the mean direction by Householder reflection.
For $d = 3$ samples, compare the mean of component $W$ along the mean direction and the fraction inside a cone around it against Bessel-free closed forms using standard errors.
Norm errors and sums of squared components check rounding only; use statistics and standard errors to check the law.
