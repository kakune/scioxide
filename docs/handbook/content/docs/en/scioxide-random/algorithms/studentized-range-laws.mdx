After ANOVA identifies differing group means, multiple comparisons examine every pair simultaneously to identify which groups differ.
Tukey's method constructs critical values controlling the overall type I error from the studentized range law, the largest minus smallest group mean divided by the error standard deviation.
This page explains the double integral for tails of `StudentizedRange` and its evaluation cases, comparing simultaneous error probabilities at critical values with independent methods.
Shared law contracts and tail reports `TestStatisticResult` are in [Test-statistic laws and mathematical contracts](oxref:random-tests-contracts).

## Studentized range

If $k$ group means independently follow $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2/r)$ and there is an independent variance estimate $s^2$ with $\nu$ degrees of freedom ($\nu s^2/\sigma^2 \sim \chi^2_\nu$), define

$$
Q = \frac{\max_i \bar{X}_i - \min_i \bar{X}_i}{s/\sqrt{r}}
$$

as the studentized range.
Tukey HSD simultaneous pairwise comparisons use an upper quantile of $Q$ as the critical value.
Writing $Q = R/S$ (where $R$ is the range of $k$ standard normals and $S = s/\sigma$), independence of $S$ and $R$ gives

$$
P(Q \le x) = \mathbb{E}_S\bigl[P(R \le xS)\bigr],\qquad
P(R \le w) = k\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \phi(z)\bigl[\Phi(z + w) - \Phi(z)\bigr]^{k-1}\,dz
$$

where $\phi$ and $\Phi$ are the standard normal density and CDF.
The inner integral sums probabilities that the minimum is at $z$ and the remaining $k - 1$ values fall in $(z, z + w]$.

`StudentizedRange::new(k, degrees)` accepts real $k > 1$ and positive $\nu$ or `f64::INFINITY`.
For $\nu = \infty$, $S = 1$ and there is no outer expectation.
Evaluation checks the following cases in order.

- $k = 2$ reduces to $Q = \sqrt{2}\,\lvert T \rvert$ ($T$ is Student t with $\nu$ degrees of freedom, standard normal for $\nu = \infty$), using its closed-form tail.
- Small $w$ ($kw^2$ below 1/100 of tolerance) uses $P(R \le w) \approx \sqrt{k}\,(w/\sqrt{2\pi})^{k-1}$; large $w$ uses the pairwise-difference tail $k(k-1)\,P(Z > w/\sqrt{2})$.
  The latter uses the fact that overlapping-pair exceedances are of order $e^{-w^2/3}$, smaller than the leading $e^{-w^2/4}$, and is restricted to omitted terms below 1/1000 of tolerance.
- The normal range for $k = 3$ is $P(R > w) = 12\,T(w/\sqrt{2}, 1/\sqrt{3})$ in terms of Owen's T function, so it uses that evaluation.
- Otherwise, evaluate the inner integral numerically.
  Compute upper tails directly with a positive integrand, without forming $1 - P(R \le w)$.

The outer expectation integrates over $r = \ln S$, evaluating $e^{2r} - 1 - 2r$ in the log-density of $S^2 \sim \chi^2_\nu/\nu$ without cancellation to retain accuracy near the density peak $r = 0$.
Every integral uses adaptive Gauss–Kronrod quadrature (a 7-point Gauss and 15-point Kronrod pair) on logarithmic integrands, deciding subdivision from the difference between the two interval estimates.
Inner tolerance is $1/32$ of outer tolerance to prevent inner error dominating the outer estimate.
This estimate is not a proven error bound.
For integer $k$, samples divide the range of $k$ normals by an independent chi scale; noninteger $k$ has no generative model and uses inverse-transform sampling.
Moments and quantiles repeat tail integrals and may require more than default work.

## Tukey critical values and simultaneous error probabilities

Tukey HSD compares all pairs among $k$ groups, using upper $\alpha$ quantile $q_{\alpha}(k, \nu)$ of the studentized range as a critical value keeping overall type I error at $\alpha$.
For critical value $q$, overall error probability is $P(Q > q)$.
The following cell computes $P(Q > q)$ at specified $q$ using `StudentizedRange::evaluate_tail` and reports estimated absolute error and evaluations.
For finite $\nu$, one tail is a double integral requiring over 100,000 evaluations, so the work limit is raised to $2^{24}$ and the cell runs by button.
Quantile `isf` repeats this tail evaluation tens of times and takes still longer.

There are two independent checks.
For $k = 2$, $Q = \sqrt{2}\,\lvert T \rvert$, so compare with $P(Q > q) = 2P(T > q/\sqrt{2})$ (replace $T$ by standard normal for $\nu = \infty$).
For any $k$, generate $R$ copies of $Q$ by dividing the range of $k$ `Normal` values by the chi scale $\sqrt{\chi^2_\nu/\nu}$ generated using `ChiSquared`; compare the fraction satisfying $Q > q$ with theoretical probability using binomial standard error.
These samples use neither the integrals nor sampler of `StudentizedRange`.

```rust
//| id: random-tukey-critical-value
//| ref: random-tukey-critical-value
//| title: "Compute simultaneous error probabilities at Tukey critical values from studentized-range tails and compare t and Monte Carlo"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-random, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   groups: {"type":"range","label":"Number of groups k [count]","min":2,"max":8,"step":1,"value":4}
//|   degrees: {"type":"select","label":"Error degrees of freedom ν","options":["5","10","20","60","∞"],"value":"20"}
//|   critical: {"type":"range","label":"Critical value q [-]","min":2,"max":6,"step":0.01,"value":3.96}
//|   replications: {"type":"range","label":"Monte Carlo replicates R [count]","min":10000,"max":50000,"step":10000,"value":20000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"two-groups","label":"k = 2, q = 2.95","description":"Check using identity Q = √2|T|. Near q_{0.05}(2, 20) = √2 t_{0.025, 20}.","inputs":{"groups":2,"critical":2.95}}
//|   - {"id":"normal-scale","label":"k = 3, ν = ∞, q = 3.31","description":"No outer integral; k = 3 normal range uses Owen's T function.","inputs":{"groups":3,"degrees":"∞","critical":3.31}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0.045,"max":0.055}}
//|       - {"artifact":"simulation","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: two-groups
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"simulation","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: normal-scale
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0.045,"max":0.055}}
//|       - {"artifact":"simulation","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{
    ChiSquared, Distribution, LawOptions, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64, StudentT,
    StudentizedRange, Survival,
};

let sig5 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 5 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let k = groups.round() as usize;
let nu: f64 = if degrees == "∞" {
    f64::INFINITY
} else {
    degrees
        .parse()
        .map_err(|e: std::num::ParseFloatError| e.to_string())?
};
let q = critical;
let work = Budget::try_from(1_usize << 24).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = LawOptions::default().with_work_budget(work);
let law = StudentizedRange::with_options(k as f64, nu, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tail = law.evaluate_tail(q, true).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let probability = RealScalar::exp(tail.log_probability);
// k = 2 identity P(Q > q) = 2 P(T > q/√2) (standard normal for ν = ∞).
let identity = if k == 2 {
    let x = q / std::f64::consts::SQRT_2;
    let upper = if nu.is_finite() {
        StudentT::new(nu).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.sf(x)
    } else {
        Normal::new(0.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.sf(x)
    };
    Some(probability - 2.0 * upper.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
} else {
    None
};
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Group count k", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Critical value q [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "P(Q > q) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Estimated absolute error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Difference from t distribution [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "k ≠ 2" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Evaluations", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        k,
        q,
        probability,
        tail.absolute_error,
        identity,
        tail.evaluations
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "tail" })
);
// Independent check: generate R copies of Q as the range of k Normal values divided by √(χ²_ν/ν).
let normal = Normal::new(0.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let chi = if nu.is_finite() {
    Some(ChiSquared::new(nu).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
} else {
    None
};
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let mut exceed = 0_usize;
for _ in 0..replications as usize {
    let (mut low, mut high) = (f64::INFINITY, f64::NEG_INFINITY);
    for _ in 0..k {
        let z = normal.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        (low, high) = (RealScalar::min(low, z), RealScalar::max(high, z));
    }
    let scale = match &chi {
        Some(chi) => RealScalar::sqrt(chi.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? / nu),
        None => 1.0,
    };
    if (high - low) / scale > q {
        exceed += 1;
    }
}
let r = replications.round();
let rate = exceed as f64 / r;
let se = RealScalar::sqrt(probability * (1.0 - probability) / r);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Count of Q > q", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Fraction [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Standard error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "z [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(exceed, rate, se, (rate - probability) / se)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "simulation" })
);
```

With the defaults ($k = 4$, $\nu = 20$, $q = 3.96$), $P(Q > q) = 0.049877$, with estimated absolute error $7.4 \times 10^{-10}$ and 169,996 evaluations.
At $q = 3.96$ the overall error probability is slightly below 0.05, so $q_{0.05}(4, 20)$ is just below 3.96.
Monte Carlo gives 979 of 20,000, or 0.04895, with $z = -0.60$ against standard error 0.0015.
For $k = 2$ and $q = 2.95$, $P(Q > q) = 0.0499998$ differs from the Student t value by $1.4 \times 10^{-17}$.
For $k = 3$, $\nu = \infty$, and $q = 3.31$, Owen’s T function reduces evaluations to 32; comparing Monte Carlo with $P(Q > q) = 0.050403$ gives $z = -0.33$.
At the same $q = 3.96$, setting $\nu$ to 5 raises $P(Q > q)$ to 0.12584, and setting $k$ to 8 raises it to 0.15030.
Smaller error degrees of freedom or more groups increase simultaneous errors at the same critical value, so recompute critical values for each $k$ and $\nu$.

## Summary

The studentized-range tail is a double integral combining the normal-range integral and expectation over a chi scale.
`StudentizedRange` reduces $k = 2$ to Student t and $k = 3$ to Owen's T function; small and large ranges use asymptotics and other cases adaptive Gauss–Kronrod quadrature.
For finite $\nu$, a tail requires over 100,000 evaluations; increase the work limit in `LawOptions` and allow time for quantile `isf`.
Simultaneous error probability at critical value $q$ is available with estimated absolute error through `evaluate_tail(q, true)` and changes substantially with $k$ and $\nu$ even at fixed $q$.
Tukey–Kramer and Games–Howell in `scioxide-stats` use these tails for simultaneous $p$-values and confidence intervals.
