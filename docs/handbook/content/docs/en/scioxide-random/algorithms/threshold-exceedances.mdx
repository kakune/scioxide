Flood levels and insurance losses often use every observation above a high threshold $u$, rather than annual maxima alone, to estimate tails (peaks over threshold).
The justification is the Pickands–Balkema–de Haan theorem in [Extreme-value limit theorems](oxref:random-extreme-limit-theorems).
Using standard generalized extreme-value distributions, this page derives in closed form how closely excess distributions match generalized Pareto and uses samples to examine the balance between approximation bias and statistical variation as thresholds change.
Check shape-parameter signs (`GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(c)` uses $c = -\xi$, `GeneralizedPareto::new(c)` uses $c = \xi$) in [Shape-parameter sign conventions](oxref:random-extreme-shape-sign).

## Threshold exceedances and generalized Pareto

The standard generalized extreme-value distribution (extreme-value index $\xi$) has CDF $F(x) = e^{-t(x)}$ with $t(x) = (1 + \xi x)^{-1/\xi}$.
Its upper tail is $S(x) = 1 - e^{-t(x)}$; for large $x$, $t(x)$ is small, giving $S(x) \approx t(x)$.
Thus the excess $Y = X - u$ above threshold $u$ satisfies

$$
\frac{S(u + y)}{S(u)} \approx \frac{t(u + y)}{t(u)} = \Bigl(1 + \frac{\xi y}{1 + \xi u}\Bigr)^{-1/\xi}
$$

so $Y/\sigma(u)$ ($\sigma(u) = 1 + \xi u$) approximately follows `GeneralizedPareto::new(ξ)`.
At $\xi = 0$, $\sigma(u) = 1$ and excesses approximately follow a unit exponential distribution.
The approximation improves as $S(u)$ decreases, meaning the threshold increases.

The generalized Pareto mean is $1/(1 - \xi)$ for $\xi < 1$, so mean excess is approximately $\sigma(u)/(1 - \xi) = (1 + \xi u)/(1 - \xi)$.
Mean excess linear in threshold $u$ with slope $\xi/(1 - \xi)$ characterizes generalized Pareto tails.

Raising the threshold reduces approximation bias but decreases exceedance count $k$ and increases statistical variation.
Threshold choice balances this bias and variation.

## Closed-form approximation bias

Standard generalized extreme-value laws allow closed-form approximation bias.
Since $1 + \xi y/\sigma(u) = (1 + \xi x)/(1 + \xi u)$ ($x = u + y$), substitution into the generalized Pareto CDF gives

$$
H_\xi\bigl(y/\sigma(u)\bigr) = 1 - \frac{t(x)}{t(u)}
$$


$U = F(X)$ is uniform, and $t(X) = -\ln U$.
Writing threshold upper probability $q = S(u)$ and $t_q = t(u) = -\ln(1 - q)$, transformed value $V = 1 + \ln U/t_q$ is independent of $\xi$.
Conditional on $X > u$, $U$ is uniform on $(1 - q, 1)$, giving the CDF of $V$ as

$$
P(V \le v \mid X > u) = \frac{(1 - q)^{1 - v} - (1 - q)}{q}, \qquad 0 \le v \le 1
$$


An exact approximation would make $V$ uniform, so the maximum difference between this CDF and $v$ is the population KS distance between excesses and generalized Pareto.
Differentiating the difference in $v$ and setting it to 0 gives $(1 - q)^{1 - v} = q/t_q$, where the difference magnitude is

$$
\delta(q) = \frac{1 - q/t_q}{q} - \frac{\ln(t_q/q)}{t_q}
$$


Substituting expansion $t_q = q + q^2/2 + O(q^3)$ gives $\delta(q) \approx q/8$ for small $q$.

By the triangle inequality, sample KS distance $D_k$ cannot exceed $\delta(q)$ plus the sample KS distance from the true conditional distribution.
The latter is at most approximately $1.358/\sqrt{k}$ with 95% probability, so approximation bias becomes detectable when $k$ is large enough for $\sqrt{k}\,\delta(q)$ to reach the Kolmogorov 95% quantile 1.358.
For $q = 0.05$, $\delta = 0.0064$, requiring about $4.5 \times 10^4$ exceedances (about $9 \times 10^5$ original samples).

## Sample comparison

The following cell generates $n$ generalized extreme-value samples and uses the point $u = $ `isf(q)` with upper probability $q$ as threshold.
It divides excesses by $\sigma(u)$, computes KS distance $D_k$ from `GeneralizedPareto::new(ξ)`, and reports $p$-values from both finite-sample `KolmogorovSmirnovTwoSided` (sample size $k$) and asymptotic `Kolmogorov`.
The first table's final column $\delta(q)$ is the population KS distance derived above in closed form (independent check).
The second compares the sample mean excess with generalized Pareto mean $\sigma(u)/(1 - \xi)$, reporting standard-error-scaled $z$.
Excess variance is finite over input range $\xi \le 0.4$, allowing standard error estimation by $s/\sqrt{k}$.

```rust
//| id: random-gev-threshold-excess
//| ref: random-gev-threshold-excess
//| title: "Compare generalized extreme-value exceedances with generalized Pareto"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-random, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   xi: {"type":"range","label":"Extreme-value index ξ","min":-0.3,"max":0.4,"step":0.05,"value":0.2}
//|   tail: {"type":"select","label":"Threshold upper probability q","description":"Smaller values raise the threshold and reduce exceedance count k = nq.","options":["0.5","0.2","0.05","0.01"],"value":"0.05"}
//|   sample_count: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size n [count]","min":10000,"max":50000,"step":10000,"value":40000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":7}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"median-threshold","label":"q = 0.5 (median threshold)","description":"Population distance δ(q) = 0.086 exceeds fluctuation scale 1.358/√k, giving an extremely small p-value.","inputs":{"tail":"0.5"}}
//|   - {"id":"high-threshold","label":"q = 0.01 (high threshold)","description":"δ(q) = 0.0013 is small, but exceedance count k falls to about 400, increasing variation.","inputs":{"tail":"0.01"}}
//|   - {"id":"weibull-type","label":"ξ = −0.3","description":"For fixed seed and q, D_k is independent of ξ; mean excess changes with ξ.","inputs":{"xi":-0.3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":1800,"max":2200}}
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/7","approx":{"value":0.0064114,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0.003,"max":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"mean-excess","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: median-threshold
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/7","approx":{"value":0.0860713,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0.07,"max":0.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|   - case: high-threshold
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":320,"max":480}}
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/7","approx":{"value":0.0012563,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"mean-excess","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4.5,"max":4.5}}
//|   - case: weibull-type
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"distance","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0.003,"max":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"mean-excess","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{
    Cdf, Distribution, GeneralizedExtremeValue, GeneralizedPareto, InverseSurvival, Kolmogorov,
    KolmogorovSmirnovTwoSided, LawOptions, SeedableRng, SplitMix64, Survival,
};

let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let q: f64 = tail
    .parse()
    .map_err(|e: std::num::ParseFloatError| e.to_string())?;
let gev = GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(-xi).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let gpd = GeneralizedPareto::new(xi).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Threshold u has upper probability q; GPD scale is σ(u) = 1 + ξu.
let u = gev.isf(q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sigma = 1.0 + xi * u;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let mut excess = Vec::new();
for _ in 0..sample_count as usize {
    let x = gev.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if x > u {
        excess.push(x - u);
    }
}
excess.sort_by(f64::total_cmp);
// KS distance between excesses divided by σ(u) and GPD(ξ).
let (k, kf) = (excess.len(), excess.len() as f64);
let mut d_k = 0.0;
for (i, &y) in excess.iter().enumerate() {
    let f = gpd.cdf(y / sigma).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    d_k = RealScalar::max(
        d_k,
        RealScalar::max((i + 1) as f64 / kf - f, f - i as f64 / kf),
    );
}
// p-values: finite-sample law (with sufficient matrix-method work) and asymptotic law.
let work = Budget::try_from(1_usize << 24).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = LawOptions::default().with_work_budget(work);
let finite = KolmogorovSmirnovTwoSided::with_options(k, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let p_finite = finite.sf(d_k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let limit = Kolmogorov::new().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let p_limit = limit
    .sf(RealScalar::sqrt(kf) * d_k)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: population KS distance δ(q) = (1 − q/t)/q − ln(t/q)/t, t = −ln(1 − q).
let t = -RealScalar::ln_1p(-q);
let delta = (1.0 - q / t) / q - RealScalar::ln(t / q) / t;
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Threshold u [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "σ(u) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Exceedance count k", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "D_k [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "√k·D_k [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "p-value (finite k)", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "p-value (asymptotic)", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "δ(q) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        u,
        sigma,
        k,
        d_k,
        RealScalar::sqrt(kf) * d_k,
        p_finite,
        p_limit,
        delta
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "distance" })
);
// Mean excess: GPD mean σ(u)/(1 − ξ) and sample-mean z.
let mean = excess.iter().sum::<f64>() / kf;
let var = excess
    .iter()
    .map(|&y| RealScalar::powi(y - mean, 2))
    .sum::<f64>()
    / (kf - 1.0);
let se = RealScalar::sqrt(var / kf);
let gpd_mean = sigma / (1.0 - xi);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "GPD mean excess [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample mean excess [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Standard error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "z [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(gpd_mean, mean, se, (mean - gpd_mean) / se)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "mean-excess" })
);
```

With the defaults ($\xi = 0.2$, $q = 0.05$, $n = 40\,000$, seed 7), threshold $u = 4.056$, $\sigma(u) = 1.811$, and exceedance count $k = 1\,984$.
With $D_k = 0.0197$ and $\sqrt{k}\,D_k = 0.878$, the $p$-value is 0.418 for the finite-sample law and 0.423 asymptotically; this $k$ cannot detect a difference from generalized Pareto.
Population bias $\delta = 0.0064$ is about one fifth of fluctuation scale $1.358/\sqrt{1\,984} = 0.030$.
Sample mean excess is 2.2209 ($z = -0.76$), compared with generalized Pareto 2.2641.
With the same seed and $q$, changing to $\xi = -0.3$ leaves $D_k = 0.0197$ unchanged and changes only the mean-excess comparison ($z = 0.33$).
Because samples use inverse-transform sampling, $D_k$ depends only on uniforms through transformation $V$ above.

Lowering the threshold to the median ($q = 0.5$) makes $\delta = 0.0861$ greatly exceed variation $1.358/\sqrt{19\,873} = 0.0096$, with $D_k = 0.0888$ and finite-sample $p$-value $6.8 \times 10^{-137}$.
Mean excess also gives $z = 19.5$, clearly showing approximation bias.
For $q = 0.01$, bias $\delta = 0.00126$ is small, but exceedance count falls to $k = 393$, increasing variation ($D_k = 0.0369$, $p = 0.646$).
Here mean-excess $z = -2.50$ is mainly fluctuation because this high threshold has little bias; the normal-approximation probability of $\lvert z \rvert > 2.5$ is about 1.2%.
Finite-sample and asymptotic $p$-values differ by 0.005 at $k = 1\,984$ and 0.014 at $k = 393$, increasing for smaller $k$.

## Summary

By Pickands–Balkema–de Haan, high-threshold excesses are approximated by generalized Pareto with scale $\sigma(u)$; standard generalized extreme-value gives $\sigma(u) = 1 + \xi u$.
Its approximation bias has closed form as population KS distance $\delta(q)$, of order $q/8$ for small upper probability $q$.
Lower thresholds make bias exceed $1.358/\sqrt{k}$ variation and become detectable; higher thresholds reduce exceedances and increase estimator variation, so compare both when selecting a threshold.
KS $p$-values use the finite-sample law with exceedance count $k$ through `KolmogorovSmirnovTwoSided`; differences from asymptotic Kolmogorov grow as $k$ decreases.
Comparing mean excess with generalized Pareto mean and standard error checks the approximation from a different perspective than KS distance.
