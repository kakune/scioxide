Under a microscope, particles about 1 μm in diameter suspended in water move irregularly through collisions with solvent molecules.
A. Einstein (1905) related the diffusion coefficient of this Brownian motion to temperature and viscosity, and J. Perrin determined the Avogadro constant from its measurement.
This page generates particle trajectories from `Normal` increments using the diffusion coefficient $D$ given by the Stokes–Einstein equation.
It estimates $D$ from the slope of the mean squared displacement (MSD) and the sum of squared increments, compares the estimates with the theoretical value through confidence intervals, and examines how much localization error biases each estimator.

## The Stokes–Einstein equation and mean squared displacement

By Stokes' law, the viscous drag coefficient of a sphere of radius $a$ [m] in a liquid at temperature $T$ [K] and viscosity $\eta$ [Pa·s] is $\gamma = 6\pi\eta a$.
The fluctuation–dissipation relation $D = k_B T/\gamma$ gives

$$
D = \frac{k_B T}{6\pi\eta a}
$$


$k_B = 1.380649 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K is the defining SI value.
For a particle with $a = 0.5$ μm in water ($\eta = 1.002$ mPa·s) at 20 ℃ ($T = 293.15$ K), the result is $D = 0.4286$ μm²/s.

The velocity relaxation time $m/\gamma$ associated with particle mass is about $10^{-7}$ s for a 1 μm particle, so each position component follows an independent Wiener process at longer time steps.
Each component of displacement over time $t$ follows a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance $2Dt$, and displacements over nonoverlapping time intervals are independent.
For the $d = 2$-dimensional displacement $\Delta\mathbf r$ tracked in the microscope image plane, the mean squared displacement is

$$
\mathrm{MSD}(t) = \mathrm E\,|\Delta\mathbf r(t)|^2 = 2dDt = 4Dt
$$


Tracking in three dimensions instead gives $6Dt$.

## Estimation from the MSD slope and increments

Track $N$ particles for $K$ frames at frame interval $\Delta t$, and let $\mathbf r_i(t_j)$ be the displacement from the origin at time $t_j = j\Delta t$ ($j = 1, \dots, K$).
A least-squares fit of the line $\mathrm{MSD} = 2dDt$ through the origin for each particle $i$ gives

$$
\hat D_i = \frac{\sum_j t_j\,|\mathbf r_i(t_j)|^2}{2d\sum_j t_j^2}
$$


Displacements of the same particle at different times are correlated, so treating MSD values at different times as independent regression points underestimates the standard error (SE).
Because particles are independent, the SE of $\hat D = \frac1N\sum_i \hat D_i$ is $s_D/\sqrt N$, using the standard deviation $s_D$ across particles, and its 95% interval is $\hat D \pm 1.96\,s_D/\sqrt N$.

An alternative estimate, based on the sum of squared increments, is

$$
\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}} = \frac{\sum |\Delta\mathbf r|^2}{2d\,N K\,\Delta t}
$$


Each component of an increment is an independent normal variable with variance $2D\Delta t$, so $\nu\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}}/D$ follows exactly a chi-squared distribution with $\nu = dNK$ degrees of freedom.
The quantiles of `ChiSquared` give the 95% interval $[\nu\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}}/\chi^2_{\nu,0.975},\ \nu\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}}/\chi^2_{\nu,0.025}]$, and the SE is $\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}}\sqrt{2/\nu}$.
For pure Brownian motion, $\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}}$ is the maximum likelihood estimator and has a smaller SE than the MSD slope.

## Bias from localization error

Measured positions include error from finding the centroid of the image.
Assume mutually independent normal errors $\varepsilon_j$ with mean 0 and standard deviation $\sigma$ are added to each position component in each frame.
Because the origin is also measured from an observed position, each component of observed displacement at time $t_j$ is $x(t_j) - x(0) + \varepsilon_j - \varepsilon_0$, with variance $2Dt_j + 2\sigma^2$.
The observed MSD is therefore $2d(Dt + \sigma^2)$, adding the constant $2d\sigma^2$ to the line.

This constant introduces different biases into the three estimators.
Each increment component has variance $2D\Delta t + 2\sigma^2$, so $\mathrm E\,\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}} = D + \sigma^2/\Delta t$.
The slope of the line through the origin gives $\mathrm E\,\hat D_i = D + \sigma^2\sum_j t_j/\sum_j t_j^2 = D + 3\sigma^2/((2K + 1)\Delta t)$; for $K = 50$, its bias shrinks to $3/101$ times that of $\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}}$.
The slope from fitting a line $\mathrm{MSD} = c + 2dDt$ with an intercept is

$$
\hat D_i^{\mathrm{free}} = \frac{\sum_j (t_j - \bar t)\,|\mathbf r_i(t_j)|^2}{2d\sum_j (t_j - \bar t)^2},
\qquad \bar t = \frac1K\sum_j t_j
$$

and is unbiased regardless of $\sigma$, because $\sum_j (t_j - \bar t) = 0$ cancels the constant term.
Estimating the intercept as well as the slope, however, gives a larger SE than fitting a line through the origin.
The bias $\sigma^2/\Delta t$ increases as the frame interval shortens, so the sum of squared increments is more sensitive to localization error in recordings from faster cameras.
With this error, adjacent increments share $\varepsilon_j$ and become negatively correlated, so the chi-squared interval for $\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}}$ no longer applies either.

The following cell takes particle radius $a$ [μm], temperature $T$ [K], viscosity $\eta$ [mPa·s], frame interval $\Delta t$ [s], localization error $\sigma$ [nm], and a random seed as inputs.
The particle count is fixed at $N = 200$ and the frame count at $K = 50$.
For each particle, draw $2K$ increments with variance $2D\Delta t$ in one call to `sample_n_into` of `Normal`, reusing the output and workspace.
Then draw $2(K + 1)$ standard normal variables, multiply them by $\sigma$, and add them to the positions at times $t_0, \dots, t_K$.
Changing $\sigma$ leaves the random sequence unchanged, allowing comparison of the same seeded trajectory with only the added error changed.
The plot shows the MSD at each time with symbols, its 95% interval as a band, and the theoretical value $2d(Dt + \sigma^2)$ as a dashed line.
The table compares the SEs and 95% intervals of the three estimates with their expectations including bias and the Stokes–Einstein theoretical value.

//| id: random-colloid-msd
//| ref: random-colloid-msd
//| title: "Estimate the diffusion coefficient from the mean squared displacement slope"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   radius_um: {"type":"range","label":"Particle radius a [μm]","description":"D is inversely proportional to a.","min":0.1,"max":2,"step":0.05,"value":0.5}
//|   temperature_k: {"type":"range","label":"Temperature T [K]","description":"D is proportional to T.","min":273.15,"max":363.15,"step":0.5,"value":293.15}
//|   viscosity_mpas: {"type":"range","label":"Viscosity η [mPa·s]","description":"Water at 20 ℃ has viscosity 1.002 mPa·s.","min":0.3,"max":5,"step":0.001,"value":1.002}
//|   frame_s: {"type":"range","label":"Frame interval Δt [s]","description":"Change the localization error bias σ²/Δt.","min":0.01,"max":0.5,"step":0.01,"value":0.1}
//|   localization_nm: {"type":"range","label":"Localization error σ [nm]","description":"Standard deviation of the normal error added to each position component in each frame.","min":0,"max":100,"step":5,"value":0}
//|   colloid_seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","description":"SplitMix64 seed.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":42}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"large-particle","label":"a = 1 μm","description":"D halves to 0.2143 μm²/s. With the same seed, the increments scale directly, so (D̂ − D)/SE stays unchanged.","inputs":{"radius_um":1}}
//|   - {"id":"hot-water","label":"Water at 80 ℃","description":"At T = 353.15 K and η = 0.355 mPa·s, D = 1.457 μm²/s, about 3.4 times its value at 20 ℃.","inputs":{"temperature_k":353.15,"viscosity_mpas":0.355}}
//|   - {"id":"localization","label":"σ = 30 nm","description":"The expected sum of squared increments increases by σ²/Δt = 0.009 μm²/s. The slope of a line with an intercept is unbiased.","inputs":{"localization_nm":30}}
//|   - {"id":"fast-camera","label":"σ = 30 nm, Δt = 0.02 s","description":"The bias σ²/Δt rises to 0.045 μm²/s, about 10% of D.","inputs":{"localization_nm":30,"frame_s":0.02}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":0.4285824,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.35,"max":0.51}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":0.3,"max":0.56}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":0.41,"max":0.45}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":0.00432,"atol":0.0002}}
//|   - case: large-particle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":0.2142912,"atol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: hot-water
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":1.45728,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":1.4,"max":1.52}}
//|   - case: localization
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/2/5","approx":{"value":0.4375824,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":0.4285824,"atol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: fast-camera
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/2/5","approx":{"value":0.4735824,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":0.46,"max":0.49}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    ChiSquared, Distribution, Normal, OutputPolicy, Quantile, SamplingPolicy, SamplingRequest,
    SeedableRng, SplitMix64,
};

const BOLTZMANN: f64 = 1.380649e-23; // [J/K]
let (particles, frames, dims) = (200_usize, 50_usize, 2_usize);
// Convert the Stokes–Einstein D from [m²/s] to [μm²/s] (reference for an independent check).
let drag = 6.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * viscosity_mpas * 1e-3 * radius_um * 1e-6;
let d_theory = BOLTZMANN * temperature_k / drag * 1e12;
let sigma = localization_nm * 1e-3; // [μm]
let step = Normal::new(0.0_f64, RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 * d_theory * frame_s))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let standard = Normal::<f64>::standard();
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(colloid_seed as u64);
let policy = SamplingPolicy::rollback(OutputPolicy::Atomic);
let mut step_ws = policy
    .workspace(SamplingRequest::scalars(dims * frames))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut noise_ws = policy
    .workspace(SamplingRequest::scalars(dims * (frames + 1)))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let times: Vec<f64> = (1..=frames).map(|j| j as f64 * frame_s).collect();
let t_bar = times.iter().sum::<f64>() / frames as f64;
let sum_t2: f64 = times.iter().map(|t| t * t).sum();
let sum_c2: f64 = times.iter().map(|t| (t - t_bar) * (t - t_bar)).sum();
let (mut msd, mut msd_sq) = (vec![0.0; frames], vec![0.0; frames]);
let (mut origin_fit, mut free_fit) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut squares = 0.0;
let mut increments = vec![0.0_f64; dims * frames]; // Row-major (Δx, Δy)
let mut noise = vec![0.0_f64; dims * (frames + 1)]; // Localization errors at times t₀, …, t_K
for _ in 0..particles {
    step.sample_n_into(&mut rng, policy, &mut increments, &mut step_ws)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    standard
        .sample_n_into(&mut rng, policy, &mut noise, &mut noise_ws)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Observed position = true position + σε. Measure displacement from the observed origin σε₀.
    let origin = [sigma * noise[0], sigma * noise[1]];
    let (mut position, mut previous) = ([0.0_f64; 2], origin);
    let (mut origin_moment, mut free_moment) = (0.0, 0.0);
    for (j, dr) in increments.chunks_exact(dims).enumerate() {
        let mut r2 = 0.0;
        for k in 0..dims {
            position[k] += dr[k];
            let seen = position[k] + sigma * noise[(j + 1) * dims + k];
            squares += (seen - previous[k]) * (seen - previous[k]);
            r2 += (seen - origin[k]) * (seen - origin[k]);
            previous[k] = seen;
        }
        msd[j] += r2;
        msd_sq[j] += r2 * r2;
        origin_moment += times[j] * r2;
        free_moment += (times[j] - t_bar) * r2;
    }
    // Fit MSD = 2dDt and MSD = c + 2dDt by least squares for each particle.
    origin_fit.push(origin_moment / (2.0 * dims as f64 * sum_t2));
    free_fit.push(free_moment / (2.0 * dims as f64 * sum_c2));
}
let nf = particles as f64;
// Mean across particles and SE from the standard deviation across particles.
let mean_se = |v: &[f64]| {
    let mean = v.iter().sum::<f64>() / nf;
    let var = v.iter().map(|s| (s - mean) * (s - mean)).sum::<f64>() / (nf - 1.0);
    (mean, RealScalar::sqrt(var / nf))
};
let (d_origin, se_origin) = mean_se(&origin_fit);
let (d_free, se_free) = mean_se(&free_fit);
// Sum of squared increments: for σ = 0, ν D̂/D follows a chi-squared distribution with ν = dNK degrees of freedom.
let nu = (dims * particles * frames) as f64;
let d_inc = squares / (nu * 2.0 * frame_s);
let chi = ChiSquared::new(nu).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let upper = chi.quantile(0.975).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lower = chi.quantile(0.025).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let se_inc = d_inc * RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 / nu);
// Expectation including bias: D + σ² Σt/Σt² for the line through the origin, D + σ²/Δt for increments.
let origin_bias = sigma * sigma * t_bar * frames as f64 / sum_t2;
let rows = vec![
    (
        "MSD slope (through the origin)",
        d_origin,
        se_origin,
        d_origin - 1.96 * se_origin,
        d_origin + 1.96 * se_origin,
        d_theory + origin_bias,
    ),
    (
        "MSD slope (with intercept)",
        d_free,
        se_free,
        d_free - 1.96 * se_free,
        d_free + 1.96 * se_free,
        d_theory,
    ),
    (
        "Sum of squared increments",
        d_inc,
        se_inc,
        nu * d_inc / upper,
        nu * d_inc / lower,
        d_theory + sigma * sigma / frame_s,
    ),
];
let rows: Vec<_> = rows
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, x, se, lo, hi, expected)| {
        (name, x, se, lo, hi, expected, d_theory, (x - d_theory) / se)
    })
    .collect();
let (mut band, mut marks, mut theory) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for (j, &t) in times.iter().enumerate() {
    let mean = msd[j] / nf;
    let se = RealScalar::sqrt((msd_sq[j] / nf - mean * mean) / (nf - 1.0));
    band.push([t, mean, mean - 1.96 * se, mean + 1.96 * se]);
    marks.push([t, mean]);
    theory.push([t, 2.0 * dims as f64 * (d_theory * t + sigma * sigma)]);
}
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "xLabel": "Elapsed time t [s]",
    "yLabel": "Mean squared displacement [μm²]",
    "xMin": 0.0,
    "yMin": 0.0,
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "layers": [
        {"id": "ci", "kind": "band", "name": "95% interval for MSD", "points": band},
        {"id": "msd", "kind": "scatter", "name": "MSD (200 particles)", "points": marks},
        {"id": "theory", "kind": "line", "name": "Theory 4(Dt + σ²)", "points": theory,
            "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}}
    ]
}));
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Estimation method" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "D̂ [μm²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "SE [μm²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "95% interval lower bound [μm²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "95% interval upper bound [μm²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Expectation including bias [μm²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Stokes–Einstein D [μm²/s]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "(D̂ − D)/SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "estimates" })
);

With the default inputs, the Stokes–Einstein diffusion coefficient is $D = 0.42858$ μm²/s and the localization error is $\sigma = 0$.
For seed 42, the MSD slope through the origin is $\hat D = 0.4221 \pm 0.0264$ μm²/s with 95% interval $[0.3703, 0.4739]$, differing from theory by $-0.25\,\mathrm{SE}$.
The slope of the line with an intercept is $0.4191 \pm 0.0325$ μm²/s, with difference $-0.29\,\mathrm{SE}$.
The sum of squared increments gives $\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}} = 0.42793 \pm 0.00428$ μm²/s with chi-squared interval $[0.41967, 0.43644]$, differing by $-0.15\,\mathrm{SE}$.
For the same data, the SE of the line through the origin is about 6.2 times that of the sum of squared increments, and the SE of the line with an intercept is another 1.23 times larger.
Least squares through the origin weights displacements at late times, which fluctuate widely, so its SE exceeds that of an estimate using each independent increment.
The plotted symbols follow the dashed line $4Dt$; the MSD standard deviation is proportional to $4Dt$, so the band widens with $t$.

Changing the seed to 1 gives differences of $+0.02$, $-0.71$, and $+0.54\,\mathrm{SE}$ for the three estimates.
In the “a = 1 μm” case, $D = 0.21429$ μm²/s is half the default value.
With the same seed, the standard normal coordinates are the same and the increments shrink in proportion to $\sqrt{D}$, so the difference divided by SE stays unchanged.
For the same reason, changing only the frame interval $\Delta t$ while keeping $\sigma = 0$ gives the same estimates at $\Delta t = 0.01$ s as at $0.1$ s.
In the “Water at 80 ℃” case (353.15 K, 0.355 mPa·s), $D = 1.4573$ μm²/s, giving $\hat D_{\mathrm{inc}} = 1.4551 \pm 0.0146$ μm²/s.

In the “σ = 30 nm” case, the sum of squared increments rises to $0.43745$ μm²/s, nearly matching the expectation including bias $D + \sigma^2/\Delta t = 0.43758$ μm²/s, and lies $+2.03\,\mathrm{SE}$ from $D$.
The expectation for the line through the origin is $0.42885$ μm²/s, with a bias of only $0.0003$ μm²/s.
In the “σ = 30 nm, Δt = 0.02 s” case, the bias $\sigma^2/\Delta t$ rises to $0.045$ μm²/s.
The sum of squared increments gives $0.47408$ μm²/s (expectation $0.47358$), lying $+9.60\,\mathrm{SE}$ from $D$, and the chi-squared interval $[0.4649, 0.4835]$ excludes $D$.
The slope of the line with an intercept is $0.4208 \pm 0.0327$ μm²/s, with a difference of just $-0.24\,\mathrm{SE}$.
The estimator with the smallest SE also has the largest bias when localization error is present.

## Summary

Using $D$ from the Stokes–Einstein equation $D = k_BT/(6\pi\eta a)$ and drawing each component's increments from `Normal` with variance $2D\Delta t$ gives a two-dimensional mean squared displacement of $4Dt$.
When estimating $D$ from the MSD slope, avoid correlation between points at different times by computing SE from the standard deviation of the slopes across particles.
Without localization error, estimation from the sum of squared increments has a smaller SE and gives an exact interval from the chi-squared distribution.
With localization error $\sigma$, the sum of squared increments is biased by $\sigma^2/\Delta t$, so fit a line with an intercept to the MSD for recordings with short frame intervals.
Regression in scioxide-stats can fit a common line with an intercept to the MSD of many particles.
