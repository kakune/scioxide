Point counting is a method of examining a thin section of sandstone under a microscope and counting grains at grid points as quartz (Q), feldspar (F), or lithic fragments (L).
The counted proportions $(x_Q, x_F, x_L)$ are compositional data: each component is nonnegative and their sum is 1; QFL ternary diagrams are used to classify sandstone.
Because the sum is fixed at 1, simulating components with independent normal distributions violates both nonnegativity and the sum constraint.
A Dirichlet distribution is defined on this constraint set, the simplex, and is a basic model for compositional data.
This page generates QFL compositions with `Dirichlet`, plots them on a ternary diagram, and compares their moments and marginal distributions with closed forms.
Variation in the counts for each thin section is examined in [Overdispersion in point counts and the Dirichlet–multinomial distribution](../dirichlet-point-counts/).

## The Dirichlet distribution on a simplex

A composition with $K$ components is a point on the simplex $\Delta^{K-1} = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^K : x_k \ge 0,\ \sum_k x_k = 1\}$.
For concentrations $\alpha = (\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_K)$ (each $\alpha_k > 0$) and $\alpha_0 = \sum_k \alpha_k$, the Dirichlet density with respect to the first $K - 1$ coordinates is

$$
f(x;\alpha) = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_0)}{\prod_k \Gamma(\alpha_k)} \prod_{k=1}^K x_k^{\alpha_k - 1}
$$


The means, variances, and covariances are

$$
E[x_k] = \frac{\alpha_k}{\alpha_0},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var} x_k = \frac{\alpha_k(\alpha_0 - \alpha_k)}{\alpha_0^2(\alpha_0 + 1)},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(x_j, x_k) = -\frac{\alpha_j \alpha_k}{\alpha_0^2(\alpha_0 + 1)} \quad (j \ne k)
$$

Because the sum is fixed at 1, distinct components have negative covariance.
The means depend only on the ratios of $\alpha$; increasing $\alpha_0$ while preserving these ratios shrinks the variances and covariances in proportion to $1/(\alpha_0 + 1)$.
For components with $\alpha_k < 1$, the density diverges at $x_k \to 0$, so points cluster at the triangle's edges and vertices.

The marginal distribution of component $k$ alone is $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_k, \alpha_0 - \alpha_k)$, and the grouped component $x_j + x_k$ follows $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_j + \alpha_k, \alpha_0 - \alpha_j - \alpha_k)$ as well.
For multinomial observations $n$ with probabilities $p$, a prior $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha)$ gives the posterior $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha + n)$.
This relationship, in which the posterior belongs to the same family as the prior, is called conjugacy; the Dirichlet distribution is a conjugate prior for multinomial probabilities $p$.

## Generation by normalizing Gamma variables

Draw $Y_k \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha_k, 1)$ independently and set $x_k = Y_k / \sum_j Y_j$; then $x \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha)$, and $x$ is independent of the sum $\sum_j Y_j$.
`Dirichlet::new(&alpha)` prepares the Gamma sampling constants for each component once.
An empty `alpha` gives `InvalidInput`, nonfinite components give `NonFinite`, and nonpositive components give `DomainError`; exceeding storage or work limits gives `ResourceLimit` or `ConvergenceFailure`.
`Dirichlet::with_options(&alpha, options)` accepts `LawOptions`, and settings outside the supported range give `InvalidInput`.

When all $\alpha_k \ge 1$, Gamma variables from the Marsaglia–Tsang method are divided by their maximum before normalization to avoid overflow in the sum.
When some $\alpha_k$ are below 1, the relation $\mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha) = \mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha + 1)\,U^{1/\alpha}$ (with independent uniform random variable $U$) is computed in logarithms, and the largest logarithm is subtracted before exponentiation.
Gamma variables with small $\alpha_k$ can easily underflow to 0, but comparing them in logarithms preserves component ratios.

`sample(&mut rng)` accepts any bit generator (`BitGenerator`) and returns a `Vec` of length $K$.
For many rows, use `sample_n` to return $n$ rows in a row-major `Vec` with one allocation, or `sample_n_into` to reuse workspace without allocating.
Both return the same rows as calling `sample` $n$ times.
`contains_support` checks whether components lie in $[0, 1]$ and the sum error is within $8K\epsilon$.
The law also provides `mean()`, `variance()`, and `covariance()`, but this page evaluates the closed forms by hand to keep the check independent.

## Ternary diagram and moments

The following cell takes concentrations $\alpha_Q, \alpha_F, \alpha_L$ (0.2–30), sample count $N$ (100–3000), and a seed, and generates $N$ compositions with `sample_n`.
The handbook's chart types do not include a ternary diagram, so barycentric coordinates are mapped to the plane and drawn as a scatter plot.
Place vertex Q at $(0, 0)$, F at $(1, 0)$, and L at $(1/2, \sqrt{3}/2)$, and map composition $(x_Q, x_F, x_L)$ to point $(x_F + x_L/2,\ \sqrt{3}\,x_L/2)$.
Overlay the triangle's edges as lines and the expectation $\alpha/\alpha_0$ as a diamond.
The table shows sample means, variances, and covariances, their theoretical values calculated by hand, SEs, and difference / SE.
Compute the mean's SE from the theoretical variance as $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var} x_k / N}$.
Compute the SEs of variances and covariances by dividing the sample standard deviation of centered products $(x_j - \bar{x}_j)(x_k - \bar{x}_k)$ by $\sqrt{N}$.
Pass the generated compositions and concentrations to the cell in the next section.

```rust
//| id: random-dirichlet-qfl-ternary
//| ref: random-dirichlet-qfl-ternary
//| title: "Generate QFL compositions with a Dirichlet distribution and examine the ternary diagram and moments"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| exports: [alpha, compositions]
//| inputs:
//|   alpha_q: {"type":"range","label":"Quartz concentration α_Q [-]","min":0.2,"max":30,"step":0.1,"value":6}
//|   alpha_f: {"type":"range","label":"Feldspar concentration α_F [-]","min":0.2,"max":30,"step":0.1,"value":2.5}
//|   alpha_l: {"type":"range","label":"Lithic fragment concentration α_L [-]","min":0.2,"max":30,"step":0.1,"value":1.5}
//|   count: {"type":"range","label":"Sample count N [samples]","min":100,"max":3000,"step":100,"value":1000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"corners","label":"α = (0.6, 0.5, 0.4)","description":"All α_k are below 1, so points cluster at the triangle's vertices and edges. Quartz variance is about 4.4 times the default.","inputs":{"alpha_q":0.6,"alpha_f":0.5,"alpha_l":0.4}}
//|   - {"id":"concentrated","label":"α = (18, 7.5, 4.5)","description":"The expectation stays at its default, α₀ triples, and variances and covariances shrink by the (α₀ + 1) ratio 11/31.","inputs":{"alpha_q":18,"alpha_f":7.5,"alpha_l":4.5}}
//|   - {"id":"few-samples","label":"N = 100","description":"SEs widen by √10, and the KS distances in the next section also increase.","inputs":{"count":100}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.6,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":0.02181818181818182,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/6/2","approx":{"value":-0.013636363636363636,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.58,"max":0.62}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/6/1","range":{"min":-0.0165,"max":-0.0108}}
//|   - case: corners
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.4,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":0.096,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: concentrated
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":0.007741935483870968,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":0.0063,"max":0.0092}}
//|   - case: few-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.014770978917519,"atol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{Dirichlet, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let alpha = [alpha_q, alpha_f, alpha_l];
let law = Dirichlet::<f64>::new(&alpha).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let n = count as usize;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
// Draw N rows at once in row-major order. Each row is one composition (x_Q, x_F, x_L).
let flat = law.sample_n(&mut rng, n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let xs: Vec<&[f64]> = flat.chunks_exact(3).collect();
// Ternary coordinates: Q at bottom left (0, 0), F at bottom right (1, 0), L at the apex (1/2, √3/2).
let h = RealScalar::sqrt(3.0) / 2.0;
let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = xs.iter().map(|x| [x[1] + 0.5 * x[2], h * x[2]]).collect();
// Independent check: E[x_k] = α_k/α₀, Var = α_k(α₀ − α_k)/(α₀²(α₀ + 1)), Cov = −α_jα_k/(α₀²(α₀ + 1)).
let a0: f64 = alpha.iter().sum();
let mean: Vec<f64> = alpha.iter().map(|a| a / a0).collect();
let cov = |j: usize, k: usize| {
    let diagonal = if j == k { alpha[j] * a0 } else { 0.0 };
    (diagonal - alpha[j] * alpha[k]) / (a0 * a0 * (a0 + 1.0))
};
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "F + L/2 [-]",
        "yLabel": "(√3/2) L [-]",
        "xMin": 0,
        "xMax": 1,
        "yMin": 0,
        "yMax": 0.9,
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "layers": [
            {
                "id": "edges",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Triangle edges (vertices Q, F, L)",
                "points": [[0.0, 0.0], [1.0, 0.0], [0.5, h], [0.0, 0.0]]
            },
            {"id": "samples", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Sample compositions", "points": points},
            {
                "id": "mean",
                "kind": "scatter",
                "name": "Expectation α/α₀",
                "style": {"symbol": "diamond"},
                "points": [[mean[1] + 0.5 * mean[2], h * mean[2]]]
            }
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ternary" })
);
let nf = n as f64;
let sample_mean: Vec<f64> = (0..3)
    .map(|k| xs.iter().map(|x| x[k]).sum::<f64>() / nf)
    .collect();
let names = ["Q", "F", "L"];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for k in 0..3 {
    let se = RealScalar::sqrt(cov(k, k) / nf);
    let label = format!("E[x_{}]", names[k]);
    rows.push((
        label,
        sample_mean[k],
        mean[k],
        se,
        (sample_mean[k] - mean[k]) / se,
    ));
}
for (j, k) in [(0, 0), (1, 1), (2, 2), (0, 1), (0, 2), (1, 2)] {
    // Mean of centered products (x_j − x̄_j)(x_k − x̄_k) and SE from their sample standard deviation.
    let products: Vec<f64> = xs
        .iter()
        .map(|x| (x[j] - sample_mean[j]) * (x[k] - sample_mean[k]))
        .collect();
    let estimate = products.iter().sum::<f64>() / (nf - 1.0);
    let spread = products
        .iter()
        .map(|p| (p - estimate) * (p - estimate))
        .sum::<f64>();
    let se = RealScalar::sqrt(spread / (nf - 1.0) / nf);
    let label = if j == k {
        format!("Var x_{}", names[j])
    } else {
        format!("Cov(x_{}, x_{})", names[j], names[k])
    };
    rows.push((label, estimate, cov(j, k), se, (estimate - cov(j, k)) / se));
}
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Theory (calculated by hand) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Difference / SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "moments" })
);
export!(alpha, &alpha);
export!(compositions, &flat);
```

With the default inputs ($\alpha = (6, 2.5, 1.5)$, $\alpha_0 = 10$, $N = 1000$ samples, seed 1), points spread around the expectation $(0.33, 0.13)$, which lies toward the quartz vertex.
The theoretical mean is $(0.6, 0.25, 0.15)$, quartz variance is 0.02182, and quartz–feldspar covariance is $-0.01364$.
The differences for the nine quantities lie between $-1.39$ and $+1.56$ SE.

In the “α = (0.6, 0.5, 0.4)” case, all $\alpha_k$ are below 1 and points cluster at the triangle's vertices and edges.
The theoretical quartz variance is 0.096, about 4.4 times the default; the sample variance 0.1032 differs by $+2.56$ SE.
Examining nine quantities together can produce a deviation of slightly more than 2.5 SE in one of them.
The “α = (18, 7.5, 4.5)” case has the same expectation as the default and $\alpha_0 = 30$; variances and covariances shrink to $11/31$ of the default.
In the “N = 100” case, SEs widen by $\sqrt{10}$, and the quartz mean differs by $+1.82$ SE.

## Marginal Beta distributions

Use the Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) distance to check whether the marginal distributions follow $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_k, \alpha_0 - \alpha_k)$.
The KS distance is the maximum difference $D_N = \sup_x |F_N(x) - F(x)|$ between the empirical distribution function $F_N$ and the theoretical distribution function $F$.
When $F$ is specified without estimating parameters, $\sqrt{N} D_N$ follows the Kolmogorov distribution as $N \to \infty$, with $P(\sqrt{N} D_N > 1.358) \approx 0.05$.
`Kolmogorov::new()` is this limiting distribution, and `sf` returns the asymptotic upper-tail probability.
`KolmogorovSmirnovTwoSided::new(n)` gives the distribution of the KS statistic for finite $N$.

The following cell receives the compositions and concentrations generated by the previous cell and tabulates $D_N$, $\sqrt{N} D_N$, and the upper-tail probability $P(K > \sqrt{N} D_N)$ for the three components.
Because it uses the same compositions, change inputs and the random seed in the previous cell.
Compute $D_N$ by taking the larger of $i/N - F(x_{(i)})$ and $F(x_{(i)}) - (i - 1)/N$ at each sorted sample point $x_{(i)}$.
The plot draws $F_N(x) - F(x)$ at $x = 0.01, 0.02, \dots, 0.99$ for each component, with reference lines at $\pm 1.358/\sqrt{N}$.
The plotting grid is coarse, so the tabulated $D_N$ is calculated at every sample point.

```rust
//| id: random-dirichlet-beta-marginal
//| ref: random-dirichlet-beta-marginal
//| title: "Compare each component's marginal with a Beta distribution using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| dependsOn:
//|   draw: random-dirichlet-qfl-ternary
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"ks","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":6,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ks","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":4,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ks","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":0.2,"max":2.0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ks","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"min":0.2,"max":2.0}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{Beta, Cdf, Kolmogorov, Survival};

// Receive concentrations α and row-major compositions (x_Q, x_F, x_L) from the previous cell.
let alpha: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(draw["alpha"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let flat: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(draw["compositions"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let a0: f64 = alpha.iter().sum();
let n = flat.len() / 3;
let kolmogorov = Kolmogorov::<f64>::new().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let names = ["Q", "F", "L"];
let (mut rows, mut layers) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for k in 0..3 {
    // Marginal distributions x_k ~ Beta(α_k, α₀ − α_k).
    let beta = Beta::new(alpha[k], a0 - alpha[k]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut values: Vec<f64> = flat.iter().skip(k).step_by(3).copied().collect();
    values.sort_by(f64::total_cmp);
    let mut distance = 0.0_f64;
    for (i, &x) in values.iter().enumerate() {
        let f = beta.cdf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        distance = distance
            .max((i + 1) as f64 / n as f64 - f)
            .max(f - i as f64 / n as f64);
    }
    let scaled = RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64) * distance;
    let tail = kolmogorov.sf(scaled).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((names[k], alpha[k], a0 - alpha[k], distance, scaled, tail));
    let mut gap = Vec::new();
    for j in 1..100 {
        let x = j as f64 / 100.0;
        let empirical = values.partition_point(|&v| v <= x) as f64 / n as f64;
        gap.push([x, empirical - beta.cdf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
    }
    layers.push(serde_json::json!({
        "id": names[k],
        "kind": "line",
        "name": format!("x_{}", names[k]),
        "points": gap
    }));
}
let limit = 1.358 / RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64);
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Composition value x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Empirical distribution function − Beta distribution function [-]",
        "xMin": 0,
        "xMax": 1,
        "yMin": -0.1,
        "yMax": 0.1,
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "annotations": [
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": limit, "label": "+1.358/√N"},
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": -limit, "label": "−1.358/√N"}
        ],
        "layers": layers
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ecdf-gap" })
);
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Component" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Beta a [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Beta b [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "KS distance D [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "√N D [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "P(K > √N D) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ks" }));
```

With the default inputs, $\sqrt{N} D_N$ ranges from 0.76 to 1.04 across the three components, with upper-tail probabilities from 0.23 to 0.61.
The largest $D_N$ is 0.033, and all three plotted differences remain within $\pm 1.358/\sqrt{N} = \pm 0.043$.
Selecting the “α = (0.6, 0.5, 0.4)” case in the previous cell gives marginals with $a < 1$, such as $\mathrm{Beta}(0.4, 1.1)$, and densities diverging at 0.
The distribution functions are continuous, so the KS distance scale still applies; $\sqrt{N} D_N$ ranges from 0.95 to 1.11, with upper-tail probabilities from 0.17 to 0.33.
In the “N = 100” case, $D_N$ increases to 0.075–0.149, while $\sqrt{N} D_N$ is 0.75–1.49.
Quartz has an upper-tail probability of 0.024; at $N = 100$, the asymptotic approximation introduces additional error.
The upper-tail probabilities scatter approximately uniformly as the seed changes, so a single seed's value cannot establish a difference between distributions.

## Summary

`Dirichlet::new(&alpha)` generates compositions on the simplex by normalizing Gamma variables, using logarithms to preserve ratios even for components with $\alpha_k < 1$.
The mean $\alpha_k/\alpha_0$, variance $\alpha_k(\alpha_0 - \alpha_k)/(\alpha_0^2(\alpha_0 + 1))$, and negative covariances can be checked against closed forms calculated by hand; changing $\alpha_0$ while preserving the ratios changes only the magnitude of compositional fluctuations.
Each component's marginal is $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_k, \alpha_0 - \alpha_k)$, and its KS distance $\sqrt{N} D_N$ from the distribution function of `Beta` can be interpreted through the upper-tail probability of `Kolmogorov`.
Upper-tail probabilities scatter across seeds, so avoid judging distributional differences from a single seed; use the finite-sample `KolmogorovSmirnovTwoSided` when $N$ is small.
