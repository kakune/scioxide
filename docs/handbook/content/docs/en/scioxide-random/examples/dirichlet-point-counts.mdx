[Generate compositional data with a Dirichlet distribution](../dirichlet-composition/) generated sandstone compositions $x$ of quartz (Q), feldspar (F), and lithic fragments (L) with a Dirichlet distribution.
Actual measurements count grains at grid points in a thin section rather than measuring the composition itself.
This page models the count distribution when composition varies across thin sections with a Dirichlet–multinomial distribution and examines how much larger the count variances are than those of a multinomial distribution with fixed composition.

## Point counts and the Dirichlet–multinomial distribution

Counting $m$ points in one thin section with fixed composition $p$ gives counts $n = (n_Q, n_F, n_L)$ following the multinomial distribution $\mathrm{Multinomial}(m, p)$.
When composition varies across thin sections as $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha)$, $n$ follows a Dirichlet–multinomial distribution.
Substituting $E[p_k^2] = \bar{p}_k^2 + \bar{p}_k(1 - \bar{p}_k)/(\alpha_0 + 1)$ into the law of total variance $\operatorname{Var} n_k = E[\operatorname{Var}(n_k \mid p)] + \operatorname{Var}(E[n_k \mid p])$ gives

$$
\operatorname{Var} n_k = m\,\bar{p}_k(1 - \bar{p}_k)\,\frac{m + \alpha_0}{1 + \alpha_0},
\qquad \bar{p}_k = \frac{\alpha_k}{\alpha_0}
$$


The factor $(m + \alpha_0)/(1 + \alpha_0)$ multiplying the multinomial variance $m\bar{p}_k(1 - \bar{p}_k)$ is called the overdispersion factor.
This factor increases approximately in proportion to $m$, so the variance of the proportion $n_k/m$ approaches $\bar{p}_k(1 - \bar{p}_k)/(\alpha_0 + 1)$ rather than 0 as $m \to \infty$.
Counting more points does not remove compositional variation between thin sections.

Considering only component $k$, its count $n_k$ follows a beta-binomial distribution.
Writing $a = \alpha_k$ and $b = \alpha_0 - \alpha_k$, its probability mass is

$$
P(n_k = j) = \binom{m}{j}\frac{B(j + a,\ m - j + b)}{B(a, b)}
$$

where $B$ is the beta function.
For a multinomial distribution with fixed composition, $n_k$ follows the binomial distribution $\mathrm{Binomial}(m, \bar{p}_k)$.

`Multinomial::new(trials, &probabilities)` checks that the probabilities sum to within $8K\epsilon$ of 1, normalizes by dividing by their sum, and generates counts by drawing one conditional binomial variable for each category.
An empty probability list gives `InvalidInput`, probabilities outside $[0, 1]$ or an invalid sum give `DomainError`, and a trial count exceeding $2^{53}$ gives `NotImplemented`.
The sum of a `Dirichlet` sample differs from 1 only by rounding error, so it can be passed directly to `Multinomial::new`.

The same law is directly available as `DirichletMultinomial::new(trials, &alpha)`, with `pmf`, `mean`, `variance`, `covariance`, and sampling.
Its sampler draws conditional beta-binomial variables for successive components without generating a Dirichlet composition.
One component's marginal is `BetaBinomial::new(trials, a, b)`.
To expose the mixture structure, this page draws `Dirichlet` and `Multinomial` in two stages and calculates beta-binomial probability masses by hand for verification.

## Point counts for each thin section

The following cell takes concentrations, points per thin section $m$ (50–1000 points), thin section count $R$ (200–4000), and a seed; for each section it draws a composition with `Dirichlet` and then counts $m$ points with `Multinomial`.
For comparison, it counts the same number of sections with `Multinomial` fixed at the expected composition $\bar{p}$.
Calling `Multinomial::new` for every thin section checks $K$ probabilities and makes a small allocation $R$ times.

The first table shows mean counts, sample variances and SEs for both models, and theoretical values calculated by hand.
The SE of the variance is $\sqrt{(\mu_4 - s^4)/R}$, computed from the fourth central moment $\mu_4$.
The plot groups quartz counts into bins $w$ points wide and shows relative frequencies divided by $w$ as symbols, with binomial and beta-binomial probability masses overlaid as dashed lines.
The beta-binomial probability mass is an independent check that uses `ln_beta` in scioxide-special to calculate $\binom{m}{j} = 1/((m + 1)\,B(j + 1,\ m - j + 1))$ and the numerator's beta function in logarithms.

```rust
//| id: random-dirichlet-point-count
//| ref: random-dirichlet-point-count
//| title: "Compare point counts under Dirichlet–multinomial and multinomial distributions"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   alpha_q: {"type":"range","label":"Quartz concentration α_Q [-]","min":0.2,"max":30,"step":0.1,"value":6}
//|   alpha_f: {"type":"range","label":"Feldspar concentration α_F [-]","min":0.2,"max":30,"step":0.1,"value":2.5}
//|   alpha_l: {"type":"range","label":"Lithic fragment concentration α_L [-]","min":0.2,"max":30,"step":0.1,"value":1.5}
//|   points: {"type":"range","label":"Points counted per thin section m [points]","min":50,"max":1000,"step":50,"value":300}
//|   sections: {"type":"range","label":"Thin section count R [sections]","min":200,"max":4000,"step":200,"value":2000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":7}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"few-points","label":"m = 50 points","description":"The overdispersion factor falls to (50 + 10)/(1 + 10) = 5.45.","inputs":{"points":50}}
//|   - {"id":"concentrated","label":"α = (18, 7.5, 4.5)","description":"At α₀ = 30, compositional variation between sections is smaller and the factor is (300 + 30)/31 = 10.6.","inputs":{"alpha_q":18,"alpha_f":7.5,"alpha_l":4.5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variances","path":"/rows/0/8","approx":{"value":2029.090909090909,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variances","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":1800,"max":2260}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variances","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":63,"max":82}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variances","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":72,"atol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: few-points
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variances","path":"/rows/0/8","approx":{"value":65.45454545454545,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variances","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":57,"max":74}}
//|   - case: concentrated
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variances","path":"/rows/0/8","approx":{"value":766.4516129032257,"atol":1e-6}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{Binomial, Dirichlet, Mass, Multinomial, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let alpha = [alpha_q, alpha_f, alpha_l];
let a0: f64 = alpha.iter().sum();
let mean: Vec<f64> = alpha.iter().map(|a| a / a0).collect();
let (m, r) = (points as usize, sections as usize);
let law = Dirichlet::<f64>::new(&alpha).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let fixed = Multinomial::new(m, &mean).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let (mut mixed, mut plain) = (Vec::with_capacity(r), Vec::with_capacity(r));
for _ in 0..r {
    // Draw composition p for each section and count m points with that p (Dirichlet–multinomial distribution).
    let p = law.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let counts = Multinomial::new(m, &p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    mixed.push(counts.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    plain.push(fixed.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}
// Sample variance and its SE from the fourth central moment.
let moments = |data: &[Vec<usize>], k: usize| {
    let rf = data.len() as f64;
    let mu = data.iter().map(|c| c[k] as f64).sum::<f64>() / rf;
    let d2 = data.iter().map(|c| (c[k] as f64 - mu) * (c[k] as f64 - mu));
    let var = d2.clone().sum::<f64>() / (rf - 1.0);
    let m4 = d2.map(|d| d * d).sum::<f64>() / rf;
    (mu, var, RealScalar::sqrt((m4 - var * var) / rf))
};
// Independent check: m p(1 − p) for the multinomial; multiply by (m + α₀)/(1 + α₀) for the Dirichlet–multinomial.
let factor = (m as f64 + a0) / (1.0 + a0);
let names = ["Quartz Q", "Feldspar F", "Lithic fragments L"];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for k in 0..3 {
    let (mu, var_dm, se_dm) = moments(&mixed, k);
    let (_, var_mn, se_mn) = moments(&plain, k);
    let binomial = m as f64 * mean[k] * (1.0 - mean[k]);
    rows.push((
        names[k],
        mu,
        m as f64 * mean[k],
        var_mn,
        se_mn,
        binomial,
        var_dm,
        se_dm,
        binomial * factor,
    ));
}
// Histogram of quartz counts (width w points) and binomial and beta-binomial probability masses.
let width = m.div_ceil(60);
let mut histogram = vec![[0_usize; 2]; m / width + 1];
for (c_mixed, c_plain) in mixed.iter().zip(&plain) {
    histogram[c_mixed[0] / width][0] += 1;
    histogram[c_plain[0] / width][1] += 1;
}
let scale = 1.0 / (r * width) as f64;
let center = |b: usize| (b * width) as f64 + 0.5 * (width - 1) as f64;
let observed_mixed: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..histogram.len())
    .filter(|&b| histogram[b][0] > 0)
    .map(|b| [center(b), histogram[b][0] as f64 * scale])
    .collect();
let observed_plain: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..histogram.len())
    .filter(|&b| histogram[b][1] > 0)
    .map(|b| [center(b), histogram[b][1] as f64 * scale])
    .collect();
let (a, b) = (alpha[0], a0 - alpha[0]);
let binomial = Binomial::new(m, mean[0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let ln_norm = scioxide_special::ln_beta(a, b).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut beta_binomial, mut plain_pmf) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for j in 0..=m {
    let (jf, mf) = (j as f64, m as f64);
    // ln C(m, j) = −ln(m + 1) − ln B(j + 1, m − j + 1).
    let ln_choose = -RealScalar::ln(mf + 1.0)
        - scioxide_special::ln_beta(jf + 1.0, mf - jf + 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let ln_ratio =
        scioxide_special::ln_beta(jf + a, mf - jf + b).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - ln_norm;
    beta_binomial.push([jf, RealScalar::exp(ln_choose + ln_ratio)]);
    plain_pmf.push([jf, binomial.pmf(j).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Quartz count per thin section n_Q [points]",
        "yLabel": "Probability mass / relative frequency per bin width [1/point]",
        "xMin": 0,
        "xMax": m,
        "yMin": 0,
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "layers": [
            {
                "id": "beta-binomial",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Beta-binomial distribution",
                "points": beta_binomial,
                "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
            },
            {
                "id": "binomial",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Binomial distribution Binomial(m, p̄_Q)",
                "points": plain_pmf,
                "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
            },
            {
                "id": "mixed",
                "kind": "scatter",
                "name": "Dirichlet–multinomial frequency",
                "points": observed_mixed
            },
            {"id": "plain", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Multinomial frequency", "points": observed_plain}
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "quartz-counts" })
);
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Component" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Mean count [points]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "m α_k/α₀ [points]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Multinomial variance [points²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Its SE [points²]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "m p(1 − p) [points²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Dirichlet–multinomial variance [points²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Its SE [points²]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "8", "label": "× (m + α₀)/(1 + α₀) [points²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "variances" })
);
```

With the default inputs ($\alpha = (6, 2.5, 1.5)$, $m = 300$ points, $R = 2000$ sections, seed 7), the overdispersion factor is $(300 + 10)/(1 + 10) = 28.2$.
Quartz count variance is 72.9 for the multinomial (theory 72.0, SE 2.3) and 2012 for the Dirichlet–multinomial (theory 2029, SE 56).
Dirichlet–multinomial variances differ from theory by $-0.31$ SE for quartz, $-0.25$ SE for feldspar, and $+0.07$ SE for lithic fragments.
Mean counts are 180.3, 74.7, and 45.0 points, close to theoretical values of 180, 75, and 45 points.
In the plot, multinomial frequencies form a narrow peak from 147 to 207 points, while Dirichlet–multinomial frequencies spread from 32 to 287 points.
Both sets of frequencies follow the dashed probability masses.

In the “m = 50 points” case, the factor falls to 5.45 and quartz variance is 11.8 for the multinomial (theory 12.0) and 62.7 for the Dirichlet–multinomial (theory 65.5, SE 1.8).
In the “α = (18, 7.5, 4.5)” case, $\alpha_0 = 30$ gives a factor of 10.6, and the quartz Dirichlet–multinomial variance is 759 (theory 766).
Counts within a thin section vary more than a multinomial predicts by the amount attributable to compositional variation.

## Conjugate updating and composition estimation

scioxide-stats does not provide Dirichlet–multinomial conjugate updating (a function constructing posterior $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha + n)$ from prior $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha)$ and counts $n$).
Obtain the posterior by constructing $\alpha + n$ by hand and passing it to `Dirichlet::new`.
Estimating $\alpha$ itself from several thin sections requires maximizing the beta-binomial or Dirichlet–multinomial likelihood.
The log likelihood of each section's counts can be evaluated with `DirichletMultinomial::log_pmf`.

## Summary

For point counts with composition varying across thin sections, the count variance is $(m + \alpha_0)/(1 + \alpha_0)$ times the multinomial variance.
Estimating the composition SE as $\sqrt{\bar{p}(1 - \bar{p})/m}$ using only a `Multinomial` model misses variation between thin sections and underestimates uncertainty.
One component's count follows a beta-binomial distribution, whose probability mass can be independently calculated with `ln_beta` and compared with observed frequencies.
Increasing the point count $m$ cannot reduce the proportion variance below $\bar{p}(1 - \bar{p})/(\alpha_0 + 1)$, so increase the number of thin sections to improve precision.
