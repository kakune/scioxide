Designing levees and detention basins requires estimating a discharge exceeded on average only once in $T$ years, the $T$-year discharge.
Records of each year's maximum discharge (annual maxima) are often fitted with a generalized extreme value (GEV) distribution.
If a sequence of maxima of independent identically distributed variables, normalized by location and scale, has a nondegenerate limiting distribution, that limit belongs to the GEV family (the Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko theorem).
This page models a river's annual maximum discharges with a GEV distribution and checks the closed-form return level against quantiles and tail probabilities of `GeneralizedExtremeValue`.
[Exceedance counts in finite discharge records](../gev-flood-records/) examines how many years in a finite record exceed a return level.

## Annual-maximum model and the shape sign convention

With location $\mu$ [m³/s], scale $\sigma > 0$ [m³/s], and shape $\xi$ [-], the GEV distribution function in the sign convention of Coles' textbook is

$$
F(x) = \exp\left\{-\left[1 + \xi\,\frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}\right]^{-1/\xi}\right\},
\qquad 1 + \xi\,\frac{x - \mu}{\sigma} > 0
$$


$\xi > 0$ gives an unbounded heavy tail (Fréchet type), $\xi = 0$ gives the Gumbel type with limit $F(x) = \exp(-e^{-(x-\mu)/\sigma})$, and $\xi < 0$ gives the Weibull type with upper endpoint $\mu - \sigma/\xi$.

`GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(c)` constructs the standard form with location 0 and scale 1, using shape sign $c = -\xi$.
The standard distribution function is $F(z) = \exp\{-(1 - cz)^{1/c}\}$ ($1 - cz > 0$), with upper support endpoint $1/c$ for $c > 0$ and lower endpoint $1/c$ for $c < 0$.
To represent Coles' $\xi = 0.15$, therefore, pass `new(-0.15)`.
`new` rejects nonfinite $c$ with `NonFinite` and $c$ that overflows the support endpoint $1/c$ with `NumericalInstability`; $c = 0$ gives a right-skewed Gumbel distribution.

Location and scale are not constructor arguments.
`Affine::new(law, mu, sigma)` constructs the law of $\mu + \sigma Z$ and inherits its underlying law's quantiles (`Quantile`), inverse survival function (`InverseSurvival`), tail probabilities (`Survival`), and sampling (`Distribution`).
`Affine::new` rejects nonfinite location and scale with `NonFinite` and zero scale with `DomainError`.
The comparison law `GumbelRight::new()` is the standard Gumbel distribution $F(z) = \exp(-e^{-z})$, also given location and scale through `Affine`.

## Closed-form return level

A discharge with annual exceedance probability $1/T$ is called the return level $z_T$ for return period $T$ years.
$z_T$ solves $F(z_T) = 1 - 1/T$.
Writing $y_T = -\ln(1 - 1/T)$ gives $[1 + \xi(z_T - \mu)/\sigma]^{-1/\xi} = y_T$, and hence

$$
z_T = \mu - \frac{\sigma}{\xi}\left[1 - y_T^{-\xi}\right],
\qquad
z_T \xrightarrow{\ \xi \to 0\ } \mu - \sigma \ln y_T
$$


The limit as $\xi \to 0$ is the Gumbel quantile $\mu - \sigma\ln(-\ln p)$ ($p = 1 - 1/T$).

For large $T$, $y_T \approx 1/T$, so this can be written as $z_T \approx \mu + (\sigma/\xi)(T^{\xi} - 1)$.
For $\xi > 0$, $z_T$ grows as a power of $T$, and the curve against $\ln T$ is convex.
For $\xi = 0$ it is approximately proportional to $\ln T$; for $\xi < 0$ it is concave and approaches the upper bound $\mu - \sigma/\xi$.
Even with the same $\mu, \sigma$, return levels beyond 100 years depend strongly on the sign and magnitude of $\xi$.

For small $|\xi|$, $1 - y_T^{-\xi}$ subtracts numbers close to 1 and suffers cancellation.
The cell's independent check uses $1 - y_T^{-\xi} = -\operatorname{expm1}(-\xi \ln y_T)$ to compute

$$
z_T = \mu + \sigma\,\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(-\xi \ln y_T)}{\xi}
= \mu - \sigma \ln y_T + \xi\,\sigma\,\frac{(\ln y_T)^2}{2} + O(\xi^2)
$$


Expanding the right-hand side shows that, for small $\xi$, the difference from Gumbel is $\xi\sigma(\ln y_T)^2/2$ and is proportional to $\xi$.

`quantile(p)` of `GeneralizedExtremeValue` is also a closed-form inverse involving $\ln(-\ln p)$, evaluated with $\operatorname{expm1}$.
Comparing the closed form with `quantile` therefore checks the sign convention, the `Affine` transformation, and rounding error.
`isf(q)` constructs $\ln(-\ln(1 - q))$ by computing $\ln(1 - q)$ with `ln_1p`, returning $z_T$ without forming $1 - 1/T$.
Even for extreme return periods where $1/T$ is near machine epsilon, `isf(1/T)` is less affected by rounding.
`sf` computes $1 - F$ from the distribution-function side using logarithmic tail formulas, so $T \cdot \mathrm{sf}(z_T) = 1$ checks a round trip against the inverse calculation.

## Check return levels

The following cell computes return levels for $T = 10, 50, 100, 200, 500$ years in closed form from inputs $\mu$, $\sigma$, and $\xi$, comparing them with `quantile(1 − 1/T)`, `isf(1/T)`, and `sf(z_T)`.
Input ranges are 800–1600 m³/s for $\mu$, 200–500 m³/s for $\sigma$, and −0.3–0.4 for $\xi$; at $\xi = 0$, the closed-form Gumbel expression is used.
The first table gives closed-form $z_T$, differences of `quantile` and `isf` from that closed form, $T \cdot \mathrm{sf}(z_T) - 1$, and the Gumbel $z_T$ with the same $\mu, \sigma$.
Independently of the inputs, the second table shows the standard form's ($\mu = 0$, $\sigma = 1$) 0.99 quantile approaching the values of $-\ln(-\ln 0.99)$ and `GumbelRight` as $\xi \to 0$.

```rust
//| id: random-gev-return-level
//| ref: random-gev-return-level
//| title: "Compare GEV return levels with closed forms, quantiles, and tail probabilities"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   location: {"type":"range","label":"Location μ [m³/s]","min":800,"max":1600,"step":50,"value":1200}
//|   scale: {"type":"range","label":"Scale σ [m³/s]","min":200,"max":500,"step":25,"value":350}
//|   xi: {"type":"range","label":"Shape ξ [-] (Coles' sign, c = −ξ)","description":"Positive gives an unbounded heavy tail, zero gives Gumbel, and negative gives upper endpoint μ − σ/ξ.","min":-0.3,"max":0.4,"step":0.05,"value":0.15}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"gumbel","label":"ξ = 0（Gumbel）","description":"The closed form becomes μ − σ ln y_T, and z₁₀₀ matches the Gumbel column at 2810.1 m³/s.","inputs":{"xi":0}}
//|   - {"id":"bounded","label":"ξ = −0.2 (bounded above)","description":"The upper support endpoint is μ − σ/ξ = 2950 m³/s, and z₅₀₀ = 2445.0 m³/s is below the Gumbel value of 3374.8 m³/s.","inputs":{"xi":-0.2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":3518.7737092424854,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"min":-1e-9,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"min":-1e-9,"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/4/4","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/2/5","approx":{"value":2810.0522293718027,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limit","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":1.0580702679341414e-05,"atol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limit","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: gumbel
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":2810.0522293718027,"atol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: bounded
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/4/1","approx":{"value":2444.953949564004,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"min":-1e-9,"max":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    Affine, GeneralizedExtremeValue, GumbelRight, InverseSurvival, Quantile, Survival,
};

// The scioxide-random shape uses the sign c = −ξ.
let gev = GeneralizedExtremeValue::<f64>::new(-xi).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let law = Affine::new(gev, location, scale).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let gumbel =
    Affine::new(GumbelRight::<f64>::new(), location, scale).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Independent check: z_T = μ − (σ/ξ)[1 − y^(−ξ)], y = −ln(1 − 1/T). Use expm1 to avoid cancellation for small ξ.
let closed_form = |t: f64| {
    let ln_y = RealScalar::ln(-RealScalar::ln_1p(-1.0 / t));
    if xi == 0.0 {
        location - scale * ln_y
    } else {
        location + scale * RealScalar::exp_m1(-xi * ln_y) / xi
    }
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for t in [10_u32, 50, 100, 200, 500] {
    let tf = f64::from(t);
    let z = closed_form(tf);
    let q = law.quantile(1.0 - 1.0 / tf).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let upper = law.isf(1.0 / tf).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let sf = law.sf(z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let g = gumbel.quantile(1.0 - 1.0 / tf).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((t, z, q - z, upper - z, tf * sf - 1.0, g));
}
let flow = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "T [years]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Closed form z_T [m³/s]", "type": "number", "format": flow },
    { "key": "2", "label": "quantile − closed form [m³/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "isf − closed form [m³/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "T·sf(z_T) − 1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Gumbel z_T [m³/s]", "type": "number", "format": flow }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "levels" })
);
// Limit ξ → 0: compare the standard form's 0.99 quantile with −ln(−ln p) and GumbelRight.
let p = 0.99_f64;
let by_hand = -RealScalar::ln(-RealScalar::ln(p));
let standard_gumbel = GumbelRight::<f64>::new()
    .quantile(p)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut limit = Vec::new();
for shape in [1e-1_f64, 1e-2, 1e-4, 1e-6, 0.0] {
    let z = GeneralizedExtremeValue::<f64>::new(-shape)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .quantile(p)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    limit.push((shape, z, z - by_hand, z - standard_gumbel));
}
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "ξ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Standard-form 0.99 quantile [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from −ln(−ln p) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference from GumbelRight [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    limit,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "limit" })
);
```

With the default inputs ($\mu = 1200$ m³/s, $\sigma = 350$ m³/s, $\xi = 0.15$), the 100-year discharge is $z_{100} = 3518.8$ m³/s.
The Gumbel distribution with the same $\mu, \sigma$ gives 2810.1 m³/s; the heavy tail at $\xi = 0.15$ raises the 100-year discharge by about 709 m³/s.
Return levels for 10, 50, 200, and 500 years are 2136.9, 3056.2, 4030.5, and 4792.6 m³/s.
The difference between `quantile` and the closed form is at most $9.1 \times 10^{-13}$ m³/s, the difference between `isf` and the closed form is 0 at all five $T$ values, and $T \cdot \mathrm{sf}(z_T) - 1$ is at most $1.3 \times 10^{-15}$.
All are at the scale of double-precision rounding error, showing that sign convention $c = -\xi$ and the `Affine` transformation correspond to the closed form.

In the second table, the difference between the standard-form 0.99 quantile and the Gumbel value 4.6001 is 0.107, $1.06 \times 10^{-3}$, and $1.06 \times 10^{-5}$ at $\xi = 10^{-2}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-6}$, respectively, shrinking in proportion to $\xi$.
The proportionality coefficient 10.58 equals the expansion coefficient $(\ln y)^2/2$ ($\ln y = \ln(-\ln 0.99) = -4.600$), and the difference vanishes at $\xi = 0$.
The difference 1.24 at $\xi = 0.1$ exceeds the first-order term 1.058; terms of order $\xi^2$ and higher contribute here.

In the “ξ = 0” case, the closed-form and Gumbel columns differ by at most $5 \times 10^{-13}$ m³/s.
In the “ξ = −0.2” case, the upper support endpoint is $\mu - \sigma/\xi = 2950$ m³/s; $z_{500} = 2445.0$ m³/s remains below it and is 930 m³/s below the Gumbel value of 3374.8 m³/s.

## Parameter estimation and uncertainty

This page examined properties of the law with known $\mu, \sigma, \xi$.
scioxide-stats has no GEV-specific function for estimating parameters from actual data.
For general fitting in scioxide-stats, construct `Affine::new(GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(-xi)?, mu, sigma)` from $(\mu, \sigma, \xi)$ in a `LawFitModel` constructor and pass `FitMethod::Likelihood` and an optimizer (such as `LbfgsBFitSolver` in scioxide-optimize) to `fit` to obtain maximum likelihood estimates.
Uncertainty in estimated $\xi$ strongly affects return levels beyond 100 years, so report $z_T$ with confidence intervals as well as point estimates.

## Summary

`GeneralizedExtremeValue::new(c)` is the standard form using the sign $c = -\xi$; add location and scale with `Affine`.
Return level $z_T = \mu - (\sigma/\xi)[1 - y_T^{-\xi}]$ is available from `quantile(1 − 1/T)` or `isf(1/T)`; for extreme return periods, use `isf`, which avoids forming $1 - 1/T$.
When calculating by hand for small $\xi$, avoid cancellation with `expm1` and use the difference from Gumbel shrinking as $\xi\sigma(\ln y_T)^2/2$ as a check.
For $\xi > 0$, return levels are convex against $\ln T$, and the default river's 100-year discharge of 3518.8 m³/s is about 709 m³/s above the Gumbel value of 2810.1 m³/s.
Reversing the shape sign interchanges heavy and upper-bounded tails, so pass values from sources using Coles' $\xi$ as `new(-xi)`.
