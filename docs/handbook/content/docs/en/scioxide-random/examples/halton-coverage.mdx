Independent uniform points produce clusters and gaps when averaging a function on the unit square or distributing initial points for optimization.
A Halton sequence is a deterministic sequence that progressively fills gaps between earlier points through radical inverses with different prime bases for each coordinate.
This example compares two-dimensional Halton configurations with independent uniform points through quadrant counts and discrepancy, examining the effects of starting partway through the sequence and randomizing it.

## Radical inverses and two-dimensional Halton sequences

For prime $b$, the radical inverse of a nonnegative integer $n = \sum_k a_k b^k$ ($0 \le a_k < b$) reflects its digits to the right of the decimal point:

$$
\phi_b(n) = \sum_k a_k\, b^{-(k+1)}
$$


For $b = 2$, $n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ maps to $1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/8, 5/8$, successively filling midpoints between earlier points.
The least significant digit becomes the most significant fractional digit, so the interval $[j b^{-k}, (j+1) b^{-k})$ of length $b^{-k}$ containing $\phi_b(n)$ depends only on $n \bmod b^k$.
Thus, any $b^k$ consecutive indices place one point in each interval of length $b^{-k}$ partitioning $[0, 1)$, regardless of the starting index.

The two-dimensional Halton sequence is $\mathbf{x}_n = (\phi_2(n), \phi_3(n))$, prepared by `QmcPlan::halton(2)`.
The rectangle of size $2^{-j} \times 3^{-k}$ containing a point is determined by $(n \bmod 2^j,\ n \bmod 3^k)$.
Because $2^j$ and $3^k$ are coprime, the Chinese remainder theorem implies that $2^j 3^k$ consecutive indices contain every such pair once, placing one point in each of $2^j \times 3^k$ rectangles.
The sequence starts at index 0 (the origin); the cursor's `skip_to(k)` starts reading at index $k$.
The cost per point of a Halton cursor is $O(d \log n)$; `skip_to` costs $O(1)$.

There are two randomization methods.
`with_scramble(QmcScramble::DigitPermutation(seed))` creates one seed-dependent permutation of digit values $1, \dots, b-1$ for each base $b$, applying the same permutation at every digit position.
Digit 0 remains fixed at 0, keeping each radical inverse a finite sum.
`with_randomization(QmcRandomization::RandomShift, rng)` draws one uniform 64-bit word per coordinate from the generator, converts it to the value $U_k$ in $[0, 1)$, and shifts points as $(x_{nk} + U_k) \bmod 1$ (a Cranley–Patterson shift).
`RandomShift` produces replicates for unbiased estimation; digit permutation changes points without making estimates unbiased.

## Two measures of discrepancy

The star discrepancy of a point set $\{\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_N\}$ is the supremum of the difference between the fraction of points in an origin-anchored rectangle $[0, a) \times [0, b)$ and its area $ab$.

$$
D_N^* = \sup_{a, b \in [0, 1]} \left| \frac{\#\{n : \mathbf{x}_n \in [0, a) \times [0, b)\}}{N} - ab \right|
$$

For two-dimensional Halton sequences, the known bound $D_N^* = O((\log N)^2 / N)$ decreases faster than the typical independent uniform-point scale $O(N^{-1/2})$.
This page uses two quantities instead of computing the supremum exactly.

The first is the maximum $\widehat{D}_N^*$ restricted to $a, b$ on the grid $\{1/G, 2/G, \dots, 1\}$ with spacing $1/G$; restricting the domain of the supremum makes it a lower bound on $D_N^*$.
Doubling the grid resolution never decreases the lower bound and brings it closer to $D_N^*$.

The other is the $L_2$ star discrepancy $T_N$, averaging squared differences over all $(a, b)$, returned by `discrepancy(points, 2, DiscrepancyMethod::L2Star)`.
Expansion and integration give the closed form (Warnock's formula)

$$
T_N^2 = \frac{1}{9} - \frac{2}{N}\sum_{n} \prod_{k=1}^{2} \frac{1 - x_{nk}^2}{2} + \frac{1}{N^2}\sum_{n}\sum_{m} \prod_{k=1}^{2} \bigl(1 - \max(x_{nk}, x_{mk})\bigr)
$$

with computational cost $O(N^2)$.
`discrepancy` adds the terms of each of the two sums in an accumulator without rounding error and rounds only once per sum.

For independent uniform points, the count inside the rectangle at each $(a, b)$ follows the binomial distribution $\mathrm{Binomial}(N, ab)$, so the expected squared difference is $ab(1 - ab)/N$.
Integrating this over $[0, 1]^2$ gives $\mathrm{E}[T_N^2] = (2^{-2} - 3^{-2})/N = 5/(36N)$; use $\sqrt{5/(36N)}$ as the reference scale for independent points.

## Quadrant counts

Also examine quadrant counts.
For independent points, each quadrant's count follows $\mathrm{Binomial}(N, 1/4)$ and has standard deviation $\sqrt{N \cdot \tfrac14 \cdot \tfrac34} = \sqrt{3N/16}$.
In the Halton sequence, the first coordinate's least significant digit alternates between 0 and 1, so consecutive pairs always split between left and right.
The second coordinate places one point in each of the three equal intervals of $[0, 1)$ in every three consecutive points.
The upper/lower boundary $x_2 = 1/2$ splits the middle interval $[1/3, 2/3)$ in two, so counts are not exactly equal, but quadrant counts stay close to $N/4$.

## Point configurations and quadrant counts

The following cell takes a point-generation method, point count $N$, initial point count to discard, and random seed, generating $N$ points and plotting them with lines at $x_1 = 0.5$ and $x_2 = 0.5$.
The table compares each quadrant's count with its difference standardized by the independent-point expectation $N/4$ and standard deviation $\sqrt{3N/16}$.
Pass the generated points to the next cell.

//| ref: random-halton-points
//| title: "Compare Halton sequences and independent uniform points through configurations and quadrant counts"

//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| exports: [flat_points, point_set_name]

//|   point_set: {"type":"select","label":"Point-generation method","options":["Halton","DigitPermutation: randomized Halton","RandomShift: randomized Halton","Independent uniform random points"],"value":"Halton"}

//|   - {"id":"random-shift","label":"RandomShift (seed 5)","description":"Quadrant counts remain nearly balanced after uniform-shift randomization.","inputs":{"point_set":"RandomShift: randomized Halton"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"quadrants","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":64}
//|       - {"artifact":"quadrants","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":62}
//|   - case: independent
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"quadrants","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"quadrants","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: skip
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"quadrants","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1,"max":1}}
//|   - case: random-shift
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"quadrants","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1,"max":1}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    QmcPlan, QmcRandomization, QmcScramble, QuasiRng, Rng, SeedableRng, SplitMix64,
};

let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let (n, skip, seed) = (
    point_count.round() as usize,
    starting_index.round() as u64,
    seed.round() as u64,
);
// Store points in a flat array of rows (x₁, x₂), the input layout for discrepancy.
let flat: Vec<f64> = match point_set.as_str() {
    "Independent uniform random points" => {
        let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed);
        (0..2 * n)
            .map(|_| rng.next_float())
            .collect::<Result<_, _>>()
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    }
    name => {
        let mut plan = QmcPlan::halton(2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if name.starts_with("DigitPermutation") {
            plan = plan
                .with_scramble(QmcScramble::DigitPermutation(seed))
                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        } else if name.starts_with("RandomShift") {
            plan = plan
                .with_randomization(
                    QmcRandomization::RandomShift,
                    &mut SplitMix64::from_seed(seed),
                )
                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        }
        let mut cursor = plan.sequence::<f64>().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        cursor.skip_to(skip).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        cursor.points(n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    }
};
let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = flat.chunks_exact(2).map(|p| [p[0], p[1]]).collect();
emit_scatter_chart!(

    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "First coordinate x₁ (base 2) [-]",
        "yLabel": "Second coordinate x₂ (base 3) [-]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 1.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": [
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": 0.5},
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.5}
        ]
    })
);
// For independent uniform points, each quadrant count is Binomial(N, 1/4), with standard deviation √(3N/16).
let mut quadrants = [0_usize; 4];
for p in &points {
    quadrants[usize::from(p[0] >= 0.5) + 2 * usize::from(p[1] >= 0.5)] += 1;
}

let sd = RealScalar::sqrt(3.0 * nf / 16.0);
let names = ["Bottom left", "Bottom right", "Top left", "Top right"];
let rows: Vec<_> = names
    .iter()
    .zip(quadrants)
    .map(|(&name, q)| (name, q, nf / 4.0, (q as f64 - nf / 4.0) / sd))
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quadrant" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Count [points]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "N/4 [points]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "(Count − N/4) / √(3N/16) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }

In the Halton scatter plot, points spread across the square without clustering, with nearly equal numbers in the four quadrants.
With the default inputs (Halton, $N = 256$, starting at the beginning), quadrant counts are 64, 66, 64, and 62, differing from $N/4 = 64$ by at most 2 points.
For independent uniform random points (seed 5), counts are 77, 67, 53, and 59, with a maximum difference of 13 points (about 1.9 times the standard deviation of $\sqrt{3N/16} \approx 6.9$ points).
For independent points, deviations of this size are consistent with binomial fluctuations; Halton deviations are much smaller.

## Measure discrepancy

The following cell receives the previous cell's point set and compares the behavior of $\widehat{D}_m^*$ and $T_m$ for the first $m$ points against the independent-point reference $\sqrt{5/(36m)}$ (dashed) on logarithmic axes.
The input is grid subdivision count $G$; change the generation method and point count in the previous cell.
Compute $\widehat{D}_m^*$ every 16 points from cumulative counts of $G \times G$ cells.
Compute $T_m$, whose cost is proportional to $m^2$, only at point counts $m = 16 \cdot 2^j$ and $24 \cdot 2^j$.
At $N$ points, the table compares $\widehat{D}_N^*$, $T_N$ from `discrepancy`, and $T_N$ recomputed in the cell by naive summation of Warnock's formula.

```rust
//| id: random-halton-discrepancy-curves
//| ref: random-halton-discrepancy-curves
//| title: "Measure point-set discrepancy by grid counts and Warnock's formula"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| dependsOn:
//|   design: random-halton-points
//| inputs:
//|   grid_cells: {"type":"select","label":"Grid subdivision count G (a, b ∈ {1/G, …, 1})","options":["8","16","32","64","128"],"value":"16"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fine-grid","label":"G = 128","description":"A finer grid raises the lower bound toward the star discrepancy.","inputs":{"grid_cells":"128"}}
//|   - {"id":"grid-64","label":"G = 64","description":"The grids are nested, so refining the grid never decreases the lower bound.","inputs":{"grid_cells":"64"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"discrepancy","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":0.0117,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrepancy","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0053,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrepancy","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrepancy","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.0233,"atol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: fine-grid
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"discrepancy","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":0.0117,"max":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"discrepancy","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-10}}
//|   - case: grid-64
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"discrepancy","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":0.013671875,"atol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{DiscrepancyMethod, discrepancy};

let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
// Receive the point set from the previous cell (flat array of rows x₁, x₂).
let flat: Vec<f64> = design["flat_points"]
    .as_array()
    .ok_or("No points")?
    .iter()
    .map(|v| v.as_f64().ok_or("Coordinate is not a number"))
    .collect::<Result<_, _>>()?;
let name = design["point_set_name"]
    .as_str()
    .ok_or("Missing point-generation method")?;
let n = flat.len() / 2;
let g: usize = grid_cells.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot read G as a number")?;
// Count points in boxes [0, k/G) × [0, l/G) using cumulative counts of G × G cells.
// Because G is a power of 2, floor(x G) < k and x < k/G are equivalent without rounding.
let mut cells = vec![0_usize; g * g];
let grid_bound = |cells: &[usize], m: usize| {
    let mut below = vec![0_usize; g];
    let mut worst = 0.0_f64;
    for k in 1..=g {
        let mut run = 0;
        for l in 0..g {
            run += cells[(k - 1) * g + l];
            below[l] += run;
            let volume = (k * (l + 1)) as f64 / (g * g) as f64;
            worst = worst.max((below[l] as f64 / m as f64 - volume).abs());
        }
    }
    worst
};
let (mut grid_curve, mut l2_curve, mut reference) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for m in 1..=n {
    let (x, y) = (flat[2 * m - 2], flat[2 * m - 1]);
    cells[(x * g as f64) as usize * g + (y * g as f64) as usize] += 1;
    if m % 16 != 0 {
        continue;
    }
    grid_curve.push([m as f64, grid_bound(&cells, m)]);
    // T_m costs O(m²), so evaluate only at m = 16·2ʲ and 24·2ʲ.
    if (m / 16).is_power_of_two() || (m % 24 == 0 && (m / 24).is_power_of_two()) {
        let l2 = discrepancy(&flat[..2 * m], 2, DiscrepancyMethod::L2Star)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        l2_curve.push([m as f64, l2]);
        reference.push([m as f64, RealScalar::sqrt(5.0 / (36.0 * m as f64))]);
    }
}
// Independent check: recompute T_N² from naive sums in Warnock's formula.
let mut cross = 0.0;
for p in flat.chunks_exact(2) {
    for q in flat.chunks_exact(2) {
        cross += (1.0 - p[0].max(q[0])) * (1.0 - p[1].max(q[1]));
    }
}
let single: f64 = flat
    .chunks_exact(2)
    .map(|p| 0.25 * (1.0 - p[0] * p[0]) * (1.0 - p[1] * p[1]))
    .sum();
let nf = n as f64;
let warnock = RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 / 9.0 - 2.0 * single / nf + cross / (nf * nf));
let l2_star = discrepancy(&flat, 2, DiscrepancyMethod::L2Star).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": format!("Grid-approximated D* ({name})"),
            "points": grid_curve,
            "style": {"symbol": "circle"}
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": format!("L2 star discrepancy T ({name})"),
            "points": l2_curve,
            "style": {"symbol": "triangle"}
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Independent-point reference √(5/(36m))",
            "points": reference,
            "style": {"lineType": "dashed"}
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Initial point count m [points]",
        "yLabel": "Discrepancy [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let expected = RealScalar::sqrt(5.0 / (36.0 * nf));
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Grid-approximated D* [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "T_N（discrepancy）[-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "T_N (Warnock's formula) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference between the two T_N values [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Reference √(5/(36N)) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Reference / T_N [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        grid_bound(&cells, n),
        l2_star,
        warnock,
        (l2_star - warnock).abs(),
        expected,
        expected / l2_star,
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "discrepancy" })
);
```

In the discrepancy plot, both Halton marker series lie below the dashed line and fall more steeply than it as $m$ increases.
Switching to independent uniform random points scatters the $T_m$ symbols around the dashed line.
If Halton symbols appear above the dashed line or have a slope close to $-1/2$, suspect sequence generation or usage, such as swapped coordinates or repeated points.
The $\widehat{D}_m^*$ curve is not monotone: it fluctuates as new points temporarily favor some rectangles before others fill the gaps.

With the default inputs (Halton, $N = 256$, $G = 16$), $\widehat{D}_N^* = 0.0117$ and $T_N = 0.0053$; $T_N$ is about 1/4.4 of the independent-point reference $0.0233$.
At $G = 64$ the lower bound is $0.0137$, rising to $0.0152$ at $G = 128$ (at $G = 8$, it is $0.0117$, the same as at $G = 16$).
The grid $1/G$ contains the previous grid whenever $G$ doubles, so the lower bound never decreases with $G$.
For independent uniform random points (seed 5), $\widehat{D}_N^* = 0.0703$ and $T_N = 0.0267$; $T_N$ is comparable to the reference $0.0233$.
For the Halton sequence with $N = 1024$, $T_N = 1.65 \times 10^{-3}$; the reference $0.0116$ is about 7 times as large.

The difference between $T_N$ from `discrepancy` and the recomputed Warnock value is $7.6 \times 10^{-13}$.
Warnock's formula obtains $T_N^2 \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-5}$ as a difference of three terms of order $1/9 \approx 0.11$.
An error $\delta$ in $T_N^2$ becomes error $\delta/(2T_N)$ in $T_N$ after taking the square root, an amplification of about 94 at $T_N \approx 0.0053$.
The observed difference $7.6 \times 10^{-13}$ corresponds to an error $\delta \approx 8 \times 10^{-15}$ in $T_N^2$.
This is comparable to rounding error from naively summing $N^2 = 65{,}536$ cross terms in the cell.

## Starting position and randomization

Even after discarding the first 2,048 points, $T_N$ at $N = 256$ is $0.0036$, preserving the Halton sequence's uniformity.
This follows because the property that $2^j 3^k$ consecutive points fill one rectangle each holds from any starting index.

Digit permutation preserves the use of each digit value once and thus preserves the one-point-per-rectangle property.
However, there are few permutation choices in two dimensions.
In base 2, the only permutation of $\{0, 1\}$ fixing 0 is the identity; in base 3, there are only two choices, swapping 1 and 2 or not, so only two randomized sequences are possible.
Seed 5 selects the identity permutation, giving the same sequence as unrandomized Halton ($T_N = 0.0053$).
Seed 7 swaps 1 and 2 in base 3, giving $T_N = 0.0058$.

With a uniform shift (`RandomShift`), seed 5 gives $T_N = 0.0036$ and seed 7 gives $0.0039$, both about one sixth of the reference.
The shift is constructed from the generator's uniform words and takes values on a grid with spacing $2^{-53}$.
Treating generator outputs as independent uniform random values makes each shifted point uniform apart from discretization at spacing $2^{-53}$, so use this method to construct independent replicates in low dimensions.
Randomization allows sequences with different seeds to serve as independent replicates for statistical error estimation ([Estimate standard errors with QmcReplicatePlan](../qmc-replicate-standard-error/)).

In higher dimensions, two coordinates with large prime bases exhibit strong correlations for small point counts, placing points along parallel lines.
Digit permutation weakens these correlations but does not make estimation unbiased.
Only one randomization applies to a `QmcPlan`: `with_scramble`, `with_randomization`, and `with_permutations` replace earlier randomization, so digit permutation and `RandomShift` cannot be combined.
Use Halton sequences in low dimensions and Sobol sequences in high dimensions.

## Summary

A `QmcPlan::halton(2)` sequence fills the square with lower discrepancy than independent uniform points and also balances quadrant counts.
Grid-approximated star discrepancy is a lower bound on the true value and never decreases when grid resolution doubles.
`DiscrepancyMethod::L2Star` is the root mean squared difference and can be recomputed independently with Warnock's formula; compare both measures against the independent-point reference $\sqrt{5/(36N)}$.
Starting partway through the sequence with `skip_to` preserves uniformity; two-dimensional `DigitPermutation` generates only two distinct sequences, so use `RandomShift` for independent replicates.
Discrepancy curves are not monotone, so judge the trend against point count instead of a single point-count value.
