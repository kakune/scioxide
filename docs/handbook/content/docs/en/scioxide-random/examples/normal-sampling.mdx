A generated normal sample mean differing from the population mean, or empirical quantiles deviating from the theoretical curve, does not by itself imply a sampler error.
Statistics calculated from finitely many samples fluctuate over widths determined by their standard errors.
Using diameters of lathe-turned shafts, this example checks statistics of `Normal` samples against closed-form theoretical values and standard errors, examining what size of deviation indicates a possible error.
Samples from `MultivariateNormal` with singular covariance are covered in [Support of multivariate normal samples with singular covariance](../singular-normal-support/).

## The process and sampling method

Consider a process whose shaft diameter $X$ [mm] follows a normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$, with specifications $[L, U] = [12, 24]$ mm.
The default process has $\mu = 18$ mm and $\sigma = 3$ mm, placing the specification endpoints exactly at $\mu \pm 2\sigma$.

`Normal::new(mean, std_dev)` takes the mean and standard deviation in that order, returning `DomainError` for a nonpositive standard deviation and `NonFinite` if either argument is NaN or infinite.
Sampling generates a standard normal variate $z$ with the 256-layer [Ziggurat method](oxref:random-continuous-ziggurat) and returns $\mu + \sigma z$ rounded by a single fused multiply-add.
`Distribution::sample_n(rng, n)` returns $n$ samples in an owned `Vec`.
The distribution function `Cdf::cdf` and survival probability `Survival::sf` directly evaluate the tail on the side of standardized $z = (x - \mu)/\sigma$ as $\tfrac12 \operatorname{erfcx}(|z|/\sqrt{2})\,e^{-z^2/2}$, using the scaled complementary error function $\operatorname{erfcx}(t) = e^{t^2}\operatorname{erfc}(t)$.
The survival probability therefore avoids evaluating $1 - F(x)$ by subtraction and retains relative accuracy in the tail.

## Distributions of normal sample statistics

When $X_1, \dots, X_N$ independently follow $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, the sample mean $\bar{X}$ and unbiased variance $s^2 = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_i (X_i - \bar{X})^2$ are independent and satisfy

$$
\bar{X} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{N}\right), \qquad \frac{(N-1)s^2}{\sigma^2} \sim \chi^2_{N-1}
$$


The sample mean's standard error is $\sigma/\sqrt{N}$; reducing it by one decimal order requires 100 times as many samples.
A chi-squared distribution with $k$ degrees of freedom has variance $2k$, so $\mathrm{Var}(s^2) = \sigma^4 \cdot 2(N-1)/(N-1)^2 = 2\sigma^4/(N-1)$, giving the unbiased variance standard error $\sigma^2\sqrt{2/(N-1)}$.

Let $q = F(L) + S(U)$ be the probability of falling outside the specifications.
Here $F$ is the distribution function and $S(x) = 1 - F(x)$ the survival probability.
The number of samples outside specifications follows the binomial distribution $\mathrm{Binomial}(N, q)$, so the proportion's standard error is $\sqrt{q(1-q)/N}$.

The theoretical quantile at probability $p$ is $x_p = \mu + \sigma\Phi^{-1}(p)$, computed by `Quantile::quantile`.
Here $\Phi$ is the standard normal distribution function.
For large $N$, the empirical quantile $\hat{x}_p$ is approximately normally distributed with mean $x_p$ and standard error

$$
\mathrm{SE}(\hat{x}_p) \approx \frac{1}{f(x_p)}\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{N}}
$$

where $f$ is the density.
The proportion of samples at or below $x_p$ has standard error $\sqrt{p(1-p)/N}$; this formula converts that fluctuation to a horizontal width using distribution-function slope $f(x_p)$.
At the low-density endpoints, the same probability fluctuation becomes a larger fluctuation in diameter.

In the following comparisons, read $z = (\text{measured value} - \text{theory})/\mathrm{SE}$, the difference between a statistic and theory divided by its standard error.
For a correct sampler, $z$ is approximately standard normal, with probability about 4.6% of $|z| > 2$.

## A reference independent of the Normal under test

The $\Phi$ used for out-of-specification probabilities and expected frequencies is computed from the complementary error function `erfc` in scioxide-special, independently of `cdf` of `Normal`, as

$$
\Phi(x) = \tfrac12 \operatorname{erfc}\!\left(-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)
$$


`Normal::cdf` uses the previous section's erfcx expression, so comparing these values checks consistency between separate evaluation paths.
The following definitions block shares this reference $\Phi$ and a helper constructing table rows between the two cells.

```rust
//| id: random-normal-reference-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Reference Φ from erfc and standardized differences"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};

/// Reference standard normal distribution function Φ(x) = erfc(−x/√2)/2. Does not use the `Normal` under test.
pub(crate) fn reference_phi(x: f64) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    Ok(0.5 * scioxide_special::erfc(-x / RealScalar::sqrt(2.0_f64))?)
}

/// One table row (quantity, measured value, theory, standard error, z = (measured value − theory) / standard error).
pub(crate) fn z_row(
    name: &'static str,
    value: f64,
    theory: f64,
    se: f64,
) -> (&'static str, f64, f64, f64, f64) {
    (name, value, theory, se, (value - theory) / se)
}
```

## Empirical quantiles of process diameters

The following cell takes process mean $\mu$ [mm], process standard deviation $\sigma$ [mm], sample count $N$, and a random seed, generating $N$ samples from `Normal::new(μ, σ)` with `sample_n`.
The plot overlays empirical quantiles (symbols) and theoretical quantiles (dashed) at $p = k/64$ ($k = 1, \dots, 63$), with a band of theoretical quantiles $\pm 2\,\mathrm{SE}(\hat{x}_p)$.
Use the $\operatorname{round}(p(N-1))$th sample (zero-based) in ascending order for the empirical quantile.
The dashed curve and band use `quantile` and `pdf` of the `Normal` under test, so this plot checks consistency between sampling, quantiles, and densities rather than providing an independent check.

The first table compares the mean, unbiased variance, and out-of-specification proportion with closed-form theory and standard errors.
The theoretical out-of-specification probability $q$ is computed as $q = \Phi((L - \mu)/\sigma) + \Phi((\mu - U)/\sigma)$ from the reference $\Phi$ above.
The second table gives the count of empirical quantiles outside the band and the difference between $q$ computed with `cdf(12) + sf(24)` and the reference $q$.

```rust
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });

let (lower, upper) = (12.0_f64, 24.0_f64);
let n = sample_count.round() as usize;

let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);

    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    + reference_phi((process_mean_mm - upper) / process_std_mm).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    let observed = samples[(p * (n - 1) as f64).round() as usize];
    outside_band += usize::from((observed - q).abs() > 2.0 * se);
    empirical.push([p, observed]);
    theoretical.push([p, q]);
    band.push([p, q, q - 2.0 * se, q + 2.0 * se]);
}

        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,

let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Empirical quantiles outside the ±2 SE band [quantiles]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Quantiles compared [quantiles]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference between cdf(12) + sf(24) and reference q [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    vec![(outside_band, 63_usize, (api_outside - theory_outside).abs())],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "quantile-checks" })
);
```

Empirical quantile symbols follow the dashed curve, while the band widens at the low-density endpoints.
Errors cause systematic deviations of symbols from the band.
For example, an incorrect standard deviation changes the slope, an incorrect mean shifts the whole curve vertically, and incorrect tail generation bends only the endpoints.

With the default inputs ($\mu = 18$ mm, $\sigma = 3$ mm, $N = 4096$, seed 901), the sample mean $18.0385$ mm has $z$ of $0.82$, and unbiased variance $8.7177$ mm² has $z$ of $-1.42$.
The out-of-specification proportion is $0.0449$, with $z = -0.18$ relative to theory $2(1 - \Phi(2)) = 0.04550$.
The difference between $q$ computed with `cdf(12) + sf(24)` and reference $q$ is $1.4 \times 10^{-17}$.
Of the 63 empirical quantiles, 0 fall outside the band.

The count outside the band cannot be interpreted as 63 independent trials.
Neighboring empirical quantiles are order statistics of the same sample and are strongly correlated.
Each point has about a 4.6% chance of falling outside, but neighboring quantiles tend to fall outside together.

At $\mu = 21$ mm, the theoretical out-of-specification proportion increases to $\Phi(-3) + (1 - \Phi(1)) = 0.1600$, and the sample proportion is $0.1626$ ($z = 0.45$).
At $N = 256$, the mean standard error widens to $3/\sqrt{256} = 0.1875$ mm, sample mean $17.8484$ mm has $z$ of $-0.81$, and 0 quantiles fall outside the band.

## Repeated sample means and coverage

A single sample set cannot rule out agreement by chance.
Generate $R$ independent groups of size $n$ and examine the distribution of standardized sample means $Z_r = (\bar{X}_r - \mu)/(\sigma/\sqrt{n})$.
Because $Z_r$ follows $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, the mean of $R$ values of $Z_r$ has standard error $1/\sqrt{R}$, and the unbiased variance has standard error $\sqrt{2/(R-1)}$ from the previous section's formula.
The proportion satisfying $|Z_r| \le 1.96$ is called coverage.
Its expectation is 0.95; coverage from $R$ repetitions follows a binomial distribution and has standard error $\sqrt{0.95 \times 0.05 / R}$.
The expected number of groups in interval $[a, b)$ is $R\,(\Phi(b) - \Phi(a))$, with binomial standard error $\sqrt{R\,p(1-p)}$ ($p = \Phi(b) - \Phi(a)$).

The following cell takes samples per group $n$, group count $R$, and a random seed, overlaying counts in 24 bins dividing $[-4, 4]$ with expected counts (dashed) and a band of expected counts $\pm 2$ SE.
Generate each group from `Normal::<f64>::standard()` with `sample_n`, dividing the sum by $\sqrt{n}$ to obtain $Z_r$.
The $\Phi$ in expected counts uses the previous section's reference $\Phi$.
The table compares the mean, unbiased variance, and coverage of $Z_r$, and the number of groups outside $[-4, 4]$, with theory and standard errors.

```rust
//| uses: [random-normal-reference-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   subgroup_size: {"type":"range","label":"Samples per group n [shafts]","min":4,"max":256,"step":4,"value":32}

//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":12}

//|   - {"id":"many-groups","label":"R = 2048","description":"Quadrupling groups halves the coverage standard error to 0.0048.","inputs":{"replication_count":2048}}

//|       - {"artifact":"replications","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"min":-3.5,"max":-2.5}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let (n, r) = (
    subgroup_size.round() as usize,
    replication_count.round() as usize,
);
let rf = r as f64;

let standard = Normal::<f64>::standard();
let mut z = Vec::with_capacity(r);
for _ in 0..r {
    // Dividing the sum of n standard normal samples in a group by √n gives a standardized sample mean.
    let group = standard.sample_n(&mut rng, n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    z.push(group.iter().sum::<f64>() / RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64));

    let index = ((value - lower) / width).floor();
    if (0.0..bins as f64).contains(&index) {
        counts[index as usize] += 1;
    }
}

    let (a, b) = (lower + i as f64 * width, lower + (i + 1) as f64 * width);
    let p = reference_phi(b).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        - reference_phi(a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (centre, se) = (0.5 * (a + b), RealScalar::sqrt(rf * p * (1.0 - p)));
    observed.push([centre, count as f64]);
    expected.push([centre, rf * p]);
    band.push([centre, rf * p, rf * p - 2.0 * se, rf * p + 2.0 * se]);
}

        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Standardized sample mean z [-]",

        "xMin": -4.0,
        "xMax": 4.0,
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "annotations": [
            {"kind": "range", "axis": "x", "from": -1.96, "to": 1.96, "label": "±1.96"}
        ],
        "layers": [
            {"id": "band", "kind": "band", "name": "Expected count ± 2 SE", "points": band},
            {
                "id": "expected",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Expected count R (Φ(b) − Φ(a))",
                "style": {"lineType": "dashed"},
                "points": expected
            },
            {"id": "observed", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Observed count", "points": observed}
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "histogram" })
);

let variance = z.iter().map(|v| (v - mean) * (v - mean)).sum::<f64>() / (rf - 1.0);
let coverage = z
    .iter()
    .filter(|v| v.abs() <= 1.959_963_984_540_054)
    .count() as f64
    / rf;

    z_row("Mean of z [-]", mean, 0.0, 1.0 / RealScalar::sqrt(rf)),

        variance,
        1.0,
        RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 / (rf - 1.0)),

    z_row(

    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Measured value", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
```

Regardless of $n$, observed count symbols follow the dashed normal curve and mostly lie inside the band.
The band is $\pm 2$ SE for each bin separately, so roughly one of the 24 bins falling outside is ordinary.
Incorrect sampler tails cause same-direction deviations from the band in the end bins; incorrect variance causes them in the central and shoulder bins.

With the default inputs ($n = 32$, $R = 512$, seed 12), $z$ has mean $0.0752$ (standard error $0.0442$, $z = 1.70$), variance $1.0090$ (standard error $0.0626$, $z = 0.14$), and coverage $0.9395$ (standard error $0.0096$, $z = -1.09$).
There are 0 groups outside $[-4, 4]$, against an expectation of $512 \times 6.3 \times 10^{-5} = 0.032$ groups.
At $R = 2048$, the coverage standard error halves to $0.0048$, with coverage $0.9521$ ($z = 0.45$).

For seed 364, the variance is $0.8072$, giving $z = -3.08$.
Under a normal approximation, the two-sided probability of $|z| \ge 3.08$ is about 0.21%, a size expected about once per 500 seeds when many are tried.
A value like this at one seed does not indicate a sampler error unless it recurs with other seeds or larger $R$.

These comparisons are consistent both with the sample mean standard error $\sigma/\sqrt{n}$ and with group means behaving as independent normal variables.
They do not, however, rule out forms of dependence that these statistics cannot detect, such as higher-order correlations.

## Summary

Interpret normal sample means, unbiased variances, and out-of-specification proportions as $z$ values, dividing their differences from closed-form theory by standard errors $\sigma/\sqrt{N}$, $\sigma^2\sqrt{2/(N-1)}$, and $\sqrt{q(1-q)/N}$.
Compare empirical quantiles with a $\pm 2\,\mathrm{SE}(\hat{x}_p)$ band, distinguishing error types by systematic deviations: slope changes, translations, or bending only at the endpoints.
Because neighboring quantiles are correlated, the count outside the band is not a count of independent trials.
Repeated sample means reproduce the square-root law and 95% coverage within standard errors determined by group count $R$.
Values of $|z| \approx 3$ occur about once per 500 seeds, so check whether they recur with other seeds or larger $R$ before drawing conclusions.
