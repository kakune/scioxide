Randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC) error cannot be measured directly without knowing the analytical solution.
Estimating the same integral $R$ times with independently randomized sequences allows a standard error and confidence interval to be obtained from variation between estimates.
This example uses the same mean absorbing-layer transmittance $I = (1 - e^{-1})^4 = 0.15966$ as [Compare Monte Carlo and Sobol quasi-Monte Carlo integration](../monte-carlo-vs-sobol/), checking standard errors returned by `QmcReplicatePlan` against the analytical solution.

## Replicate estimates and standard errors

For each of $R$ independent randomizations $r = 1, \dots, R$, compute the mean $I_r = \frac1N \sum_{n=1}^{N} f(\mathbf{x}_n^{(r)})$ of the sequence's first $N$ points.
The mean $\bar{I}$ of the replicate means and its standard error are

$$
\bar{I} = \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R} I_r, \qquad
\mathrm{SE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{R(R-1)}\sum_{r=1}^{R} (I_r - \bar{I})^2}
$$


$\mathrm{SE}$ is obtained by dividing the unbiased variance of replicate means $I_r$ by $R$ and taking the square root; it estimates the standard deviation of $\bar{I}$.

If randomization makes each point uniform on $[0, 1)^d$, then $I_r$ is an unbiased estimator of $I$, and $\mathrm{E}[\mathrm{SE}^2] = \mathrm{Var}(\bar{I})$ holds.
If, in addition, $I_r$ are independent normal variables, $t = (\bar{I} - I)/\mathrm{SE}$ follows a $t$ distribution with $R - 1$ degrees of freedom, and the interval

$$
\bar{I} \pm t_{R-1,\,0.975}\,\mathrm{SE}
$$

contains $I$ with probability 95%.
$I_r$ are not exactly normal, so this interval's coverage is approximate.
For small $R$, $\mathrm{SE}$ itself fluctuates substantially and $t_{R-1,\,0.975}$ is also larger ($3.18$ at $R = 4$, $2.13$ at $R = 16$).

## Prepare QmcReplicatePlan and estimate

`QmcReplicatePlan::new(&base, &root, options)` constructs and retains $R$ independent randomizations of the base sequence `base`.
`QmcReplicateOptions` specifies replicate count $R$ (default 8, at least 2) and points per replicate $N$ (default 1,024); Sobol and Niederreiter sequences require $N$ to be a power of 2.
$R < 2$, $N = 0$, a non-power-of-two $N$, and `base` for an inverse Halton sequence give `InvalidInput`.

Randomization uses `LinearMatrixShift` for Sobol and Niederreiter sequences (Matoušek's linear scramble and a digital shift) and `RandomShift` for Halton sequences (a Cranley–Patterson uniform shift), replacing any randomization already attached to `base`.
Randomization for replicate $r$ is drawn from a stream split from the root `Philox4x64` using the byte sequence of a domain tag and replicate index.
Thus the randomization of replicate $r$ is the same regardless of total replicate count or construction order, and `root` does not advance.
Preparation retains $R$ copies of the sequence and $O(Rd)$ words of randomization, reusable for estimating several functions.

`estimate(f)` evaluates `f` $R \cdot N$ times on the calling thread, returning each replicate mean `replicate_means`, their mean `mean`, standard error `standard_error`, and point count `points_per_replicate`.
Errors returned by `f` propagate unchanged; returning NaN or infinity gives `NonFinite`.
On error, no estimate is returned, even if some replicates have finished.
To distribute replicates through an executor or count their evaluations toward an outer solve, use `estimate_scoped(f, scope)`, which accepts an execution scope.
With either method, estimates are independent of execution order.

The following definitions block shares transmittance and its analytical solution between the two cells on this page.

```rust
//| id: random-replicate-transmittance-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Absorbing-layer transmittance and analytical solution (replicate example)"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};

/// Transmittance f(x) = exp(−Σⱼ xⱼ), in the form accepted by QmcReplicatePlan::estimate.
pub(crate) fn transmittance(x: &[f64]) -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    Ok(RealScalar::exp(-x.iter().sum::<f64>()))
}

/// Analytical solution I = (1 − e⁻¹)ᵈ.
pub(crate) fn exact_mean(d: usize) -> f64 {
    RealScalar::powi(1.0 - RealScalar::exp(-1.0_f64), d as i32)
}
```

## Check estimates and standard errors

The following cell takes a base sequence, points-per-replicate exponent $m$ ($N = 2^m$), replicate count $R$, and a Philox key seed, estimating mean transmittance in $d = 4$.
Recompute the standard error from the returned replicate means using the definition above, compare it with the reported value, and express the estimate's difference from the analytical solution in standard-error units.
Construct a 95% confidence interval from the 97.5% quantile of $t_{R-1}$ computed with `StudentT::quantile` and show whether it contains the analytical solution.

```rust
//| id: random-prepared-replicate-error
//| ref: random-prepared-replicate-error
//| title: "Check QmcReplicatePlan means and standard errors against the analytical solution"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [random-replicate-transmittance-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-random, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   base_family: {"type":"select","label":"Base sequence","options":["Sobol","Halton"],"value":"Sobol"}
//|   points_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Points-per-replicate exponent m (N = 2ᵐ)","min":5,"max":12,"step":1,"value":9}
//|   replicates: {"type":"range","label":"Replicate count R","min":4,"max":32,"step":4,"value":16}
//|   key_seed: {"type":"range","label":"Philox key seed","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":42}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"few-replicates","label":"R = 4","description":"The t₃ 97.5% quantile is 3.18, widening the interval.","inputs":{"replicates":4}}
//|   - {"id":"more-points","label":"N = 2¹²","description":"An eightfold point-count increase reduces the standard error to less than one tenth.","inputs":{"points_exponent":12}}
//|   - {"id":"halton","label":"Halton","description":"Uniform-shift randomized Halton has a larger standard error at the same N.","inputs":{"base_family":"Halton"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":16}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.1596613,"atol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":5e-6,"max":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.1314,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: few-replicates
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":-8,"max":8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":3.1824,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: more-points
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":2e-7,"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: halton
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":1e-5,"max":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"interval","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    Philox4x64, QmcPlan, QmcReplicateOptions, QmcReplicatePlan, Quantile, SeedableRng, StudentT,
};

let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let (points, r, d) = (
    1_usize << points_exponent.round() as u32,
    replicates.round() as usize,
    4_usize,
);
let exact = exact_mean(d);
let root = Philox4x64::from_seed(key_seed.round() as u64);
let base = if base_family == "Halton" {
    QmcPlan::halton(d)
} else {
    QmcPlan::sobol(d)
}
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = QmcReplicateOptions::default()
    .with_replicates(r)
    .with_points_per_replicate(points);
let plan = QmcReplicatePlan::new(&base, &root, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let estimate = plan
    .estimate(|x: &[f64]| transmittance(x))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Recompute the standard-error definition from replicate means (independent check).
let means = &estimate.replicate_means;
let rf = means.len() as f64;
let average = means.iter().sum::<f64>() / rf;
let spread: f64 = means.iter().map(|m| (m - average) * (m - average)).sum();
let recomputed = RealScalar::sqrt(spread / (rf * (rf - 1.0)));
let se = estimate.standard_error;
let t_law = StudentT::new(rf - 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let t_quantile = t_law.quantile(0.975).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (lower, upper) = (
    estimate.mean - t_quantile * se,
    estimate.mean + t_quantile * se,
);
let estimate_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Replicate count R", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Point count N [points]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Analytical solution I [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "standard_error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Difference from recomputed SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    estimate_columns,
    vec![(
        means.len(),
        estimate.points_per_replicate,
        estimate.mean,
        exact,
        se,
        (recomputed - se).abs(),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "estimate" })
);
let interval_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "t = (mean − I) / SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "97.5% quantile of t_{R−1} [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "95% interval lower bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "95% interval upper bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Contains I" }
]);
let contains = if (lower..=upper).contains(&exact) {
    "Contains"
} else {
    "Does not contain"
};
emit_table_with_columns!(
    interval_columns,
    vec![(
        (estimate.mean - exact) / se,
        t_quantile,
        lower,
        upper,
        contains
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "interval" })
);
```

The reported standard error matches the value recomputed from replicate means within rounding error.
With the default inputs (Sobol, $N = 512$, $R = 16$, key seed 42), the standard error is $2.02 \times 10^{-5}$, differing from the recomputed value by 0.
The estimate differs from the analytical solution by $-1.38$ standard errors, less than the 97.5% quantile $2.13$ of $t_{15}$, and the 95% interval $[0.159590, 0.159676]$ contains the analytical solution.
At $N = 4096$, the standard error becomes $1.16 \times 10^{-6}$, one seventeenth as large, with $t = 0.83$.
An eightfold point-count increase reduces standard error by far more than a factor of $\sqrt{8} \approx 2.8$ because randomized Sobol error decreases faster than MC's $N^{-1/2}$.

At $R = 4$, the 97.5% quantile of $t_3$ increases to $3.18$; the standard error itself fluctuates widely as an estimate with 3 degrees of freedom ($t = 0.73$ for this key).
Using Halton as the base sequence gives standard error $1.33 \times 10^{-4}$ for the same $N = 512$ uniformly shifted points, about 7 times the Sobol value.
Halton also accepts $N$ values that are not powers of 2.

## Coverage over many keys

A small $|t|$ in one estimate does not establish that the standard error is correctly calibrated.
The following cell varies Philox key seeds as $k_0 + 1, \dots, k_0 + K$ to obtain $K$ independent estimates, counting the fraction of 95% intervals containing the analytical solution (coverage).
If coverage persistently deviates from 0.95 by more than a few times its standard error $\sqrt{0.95 \times 0.05 / K}$, the standard error or interval construction has a problem.
Also compare the root mean square standard error $\sqrt{\overline{\mathrm{SE}^2}}$ with the RMSE of $K$ estimates relative to the analytical solution.
Because $\mathrm{E}[\mathrm{SE}^2] = \mathrm{Var}(\bar{I})$, the two are approximately equal without bias.
This requires $K \cdot R \cdot N$ evaluations, so run it with a button.

```rust
//| id: random-replicate-coverage
//| ref: random-replicate-coverage
//| title: "Measure QmcReplicatePlan interval coverage over many keys"
//| run: button
//| uses: [random-replicate-transmittance-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-random, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   key_count: {"type":"range","label":"Estimate count K","min":50,"max":400,"step":50,"value":200}
//|   coverage_replicates: {"type":"range","label":"Replicate count R","min":4,"max":32,"step":4,"value":16}
//|   coverage_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Points-per-replicate exponent m (N = 2ᵐ)","min":5,"max":10,"step":1,"value":9}
//|   first_key: {"type":"range","label":"Key-seed offset k₀","min":0,"max":10000,"step":1000,"value":0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"few-replicates","label":"R = 4","description":"Even at small R, t-distribution quantiles keep coverage close to 0.95.","inputs":{"coverage_replicates":4}}
//|   - {"id":"other-keys","label":"k₀ = 5000","description":"Coverage for another key set also stays close to 0.95 within standard errors.","inputs":{"first_key":5000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coverage","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":200}
//|       - {"artifact":"coverage","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coverage","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":0.7,"max":1.3}}
//|   - case: few-replicates
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coverage","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: other-keys
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coverage","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    Philox4x64, QmcPlan, QmcReplicateOptions, QmcReplicatePlan, Quantile, SeedableRng, StudentT,
};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let (k, r, d) = (
    key_count.round() as usize,
    coverage_replicates.round() as usize,
    4_usize,
);
let points = 1_usize << coverage_exponent.round() as u32;
let exact = exact_mean(d);
let base = QmcPlan::sobol(d).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = QmcReplicateOptions::default()
    .with_replicates(r)
    .with_points_per_replicate(points);
let t_quantile = StudentT::new((r - 1) as f64)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .quantile(0.975)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut covered, mut se_square, mut error_square) = (0_usize, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
for key in 1..=k as u64 {
    let root = Philox4x64::from_seed(first_key.round() as u64 + key);
    let plan = QmcReplicatePlan::new(&base, &root, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let estimate = plan
        .estimate(|x: &[f64]| transmittance(x))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let error = estimate.mean - exact;
    covered += usize::from(error.abs() <= t_quantile * estimate.standard_error);
    se_square += estimate.standard_error * estimate.standard_error;
    error_square += error * error;
}
let kf = k as f64;
let coverage = covered as f64 / kf;
// Coverage standard error (when intervals correctly have 95% coverage).
let coverage_se = RealScalar::sqrt(0.95 * 0.05 / kf);
let rms_se = RealScalar::sqrt(se_square / kf);
let rmse = RealScalar::sqrt(error_square / kf);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Estimate count K", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Coverage [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Coverage SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "z = (coverage − 0.95) / SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Root mean square SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Estimate RMSE [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "RMSE / SE ratio [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        k,
        coverage,
        coverage_se,
        (coverage - 0.95) / coverage_se,
        rms_se,
        rmse,
        rmse / rms_se,
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "coverage" })
);
```

With the default inputs ($K = 200$, $R = 16$, $N = 512$, $k_0 = 0$), coverage is $0.960$ (standard error $0.015$, $z = 0.65$).
The root mean square standard error is $1.73 \times 10^{-5}$ and the estimate RMSE is $1.61 \times 10^{-5}$, giving ratio $0.93$.
RMSE from $K$ estimates has relative uncertainty about $1/\sqrt{2K} = 0.05$, so this ratio agrees with 1 within sampling fluctuations.
Even at $R = 4$, coverage is $0.960$; the $t$-distribution quantile compensates for standard-error fluctuations at small $R$.
For the other key set $k_0 = 5000$, coverage is $0.930$ ($z = -1.30$) and the ratio is $1.04$.
For all three settings tested here, coverage is within twice its standard error of 0.95, and standard errors are consistent with the actual variability of estimates.

## Summary

`QmcReplicatePlan` constructs independent randomizations for each replicate from a Philox root and returns the mean of replicate means and an empirical standard error.
The standard error is a statistical estimate rather than a certified bound; interpret differences between estimates and true values using quantiles of a $t$ distribution with $R - 1$ degrees of freedom.
Recomputing the standard error from the returned `replicate_means` independently checks the reported value.
One estimate cannot establish interval calibration; check coverage over many keys and the ratio between standard error and RMSE.
Increasing $R$ reduces fluctuations in the standard error itself and the $t$ quantile, while increasing $N$ rapidly reduces standard error.
