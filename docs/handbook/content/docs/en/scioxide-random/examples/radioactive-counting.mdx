Nuclei in a radioactive sample decay independently, with an approximately constant decay rate $n$ per unit time.
Decay times then form a Poisson process, and the count in a fixed counting time $t_{\mathrm g}$ follows a Poisson distribution with mean $n t_{\mathrm g}$.
This page generates decay times from `Exponential` waiting times, comparing the count distribution with `Poisson` probability masses and the Fano factor with 1.
[Detector dead time and count-rate correction](../radioactive-dead-time/) covers how detector dead time changes the observed rate and count fluctuations.

## Poisson processes and the Fano factor

In a Poisson process with decay rate $n$ [s⁻¹], successive interarrival times independently follow the exponential distribution $\mathrm{Exponential}(n)$ (mean $1/n$).
The count $N$ in an interval of length $t_{\mathrm g}$ follows the Poisson distribution

$$
\Pr(N = k) = e^{-\mu}\frac{\mu^k}{k!}, \qquad \mu = n t_{\mathrm g}
$$

with both mean and variance equal to $\mu$.
The ratio of variance to mean,

$$
F = \frac{\mathrm{Var}\,N}{\mathrm E\,N}
$$

is called the Fano factor; for a Poisson process, $F = 1$.
Counts with $F < 1$ are called sub-Poissonian (variance below the mean), and those with $F > 1$ are super-Poissonian.

From counts in $R$ intervals, compute the sample mean $\bar N$ and unbiased variance $s^2$ and set $\hat F = s^2/\bar N$.
For a Poisson distribution, applying the delta method to the variance of $\hat F$ cancels the fourth-moment term against the covariance term of $s^2$ and $\bar N$, leaving

$$
\mathrm{SE}(\hat F) \approx F\sqrt{\frac{2}{R-1}}
$$


At $R = 2000$, $\mathrm{SE} \approx 0.032$, so the 95% interval $\hat F \pm 1.96\,\mathrm{SE}$ for $\hat F$ has width about $\pm 0.06$.

## Count distribution and Fano factor

The following cell sums `Exponential::new(n)` waiting times to generate decay times and counts arrivals in $R$ consecutive intervals $[g\,t_{\mathrm g}, (g+1)\,t_{\mathrm g})$ of length $t_{\mathrm g}$.
`Exponential::new` takes rate $n$ and returns waiting times with mean $1/n$.
Comparing summed waiting times against interval boundaries uses the definition of a Poisson process itself, so comparing with the `Poisson` probability mass provides an independent check.
Inputs are decay rate $n$ [s⁻¹], counting time $t_{\mathrm g}$ [ms], interval count $R$, and a random seed.
The plot shows relative frequencies of counts $k$ as symbols and `pmf(k)` of `Poisson::new(μ)` as a dashed step curve.
The table gives estimated means, variances, and Fano factors, standard errors, 95% intervals, theoretical values, and differences divided by standard errors.
The variance standard error is $\sqrt{(m_4 - s^4)/R}$ from the sample fourth central moment $m_4$, without assuming a distribution.

```rust
//| id: random-radioactive-poisson
//| ref: random-radioactive-poisson
//| title: "Generate counts from exponential waiting times and compare with a Poisson distribution"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   decay_rate: {"type":"range","label":"Decay rate n [s⁻¹]","description":"Change the mean count μ = n t_g.","min":100,"max":5000,"step":100,"value":800}
//|   gate_ms: {"type":"range","label":"Counting time t_g [ms]","description":"Length of one interval.","min":2,"max":50,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   gates: {"type":"range","label":"Interval count R [intervals]","description":"Change the Fano factor standard error √(2/(R−1)).","min":200,"max":5000,"step":200,"value":2000}
//|   poisson_seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","description":"SplitMix64 seed.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"seed-42","label":"Seed 42","description":"The Fano factor is 1.085, and the 95% interval [1.018, 1.153] excludes 1. Such a deviation occurs for about 5% of seeds.","inputs":{"poisson_seed":42}}
//|   - {"id":"short-gate","label":"t_g = 2 ms","description":"At mean count μ = 1.6, the distribution has a right tail, but the Fano factor is 1.009 ± 0.032.","inputs":{"gate_ms":2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":7.8,"max":8.2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":0.88,"max":1.12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":0.031,"atol":0.002}}
//|   - case: seed-42
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":1.07,"max":1.1}}
//|   - case: short-gate
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":1.6,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":0.88,"max":1.12}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Exponential, Mass, Poisson, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let gate = gate_ms * 1e-3; // [s]
let r = gates.round() as usize;
let mu = decay_rate * gate;
let waiting = Exponential::new(decay_rate).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(poisson_seed.round() as u64);
// Count decays in consecutive intervals [g t_g, (g + 1) t_g).
let mut counts = vec![0_usize; r];
let mut next = waiting.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
for (g, count) in counts.iter_mut().enumerate() {
    let close = (g + 1) as f64 * gate;
    while next < close {
        *count += 1;
        next += waiting.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
}
let rf = r as f64;
let mean = counts.iter().sum::<usize>() as f64 / rf;
let central = |power: i32| {
    counts
        .iter()
        .map(|&c| RealScalar::powi(c as f64 - mean, power))
        .sum::<f64>()
};
let var = central(2) / (rf - 1.0);
let m4 = central(4) / rf;
let fano = var / mean;
let rows = vec![
    ("Mean count [counts]", mean, RealScalar::sqrt(var / rf), mu),
    (
        "Variance [counts²]",
        var,
        RealScalar::sqrt((m4 - var * var) / rf),
        mu,
    ),
    (
        "Fano factor [-]",
        fano,
        fano * RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 / (rf - 1.0)),
        1.0,
    ),
];
let rows: Vec<_> = rows
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, x, se, theory)| {
        (
            name,
            x,
            se,
            x - 1.96 * se,
            x + 1.96 * se,
            theory,
            (x - theory) / se,
        )
    })
    .collect();
// Relative frequencies and Poisson probability masses (independent reference).
let poisson = Poisson::new(mu).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let k_max = counts.iter().copied().fold(0, usize::max);
let mut histogram = vec![0_usize; k_max + 1];
for &c in &counts {
    histogram[c] += 1;
}
let observed: Vec<[f64; 2]> = histogram
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, &h)| [k as f64, h as f64 / rf])
    .collect();
let mut pmf = Vec::new();
for k in 0..=k_max {
    pmf.push([k as f64, poisson.pmf(k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
}
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "xLabel": "Count in an interval k [counts]",
    "yLabel": "Probability / relative frequency [-]",
    "yMin": 0.0,
    "layers": [
        { "id": "observed", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Relative frequency", "points": observed },
        {
            "id": "poisson",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "Poisson(μ) probability mass",
            "points": pmf,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "step": "middle" }
        }
    ]
}));
let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Estimate", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "SE", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "95% interval lower bound", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "95% interval upper bound", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Theoretical value", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "z = (estimate − theory)/SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "moments" })
);
```

With default $n = 800$ s⁻¹ and $t_{\mathrm g} = 10$ ms, the mean count is $\mu = 8$.
For seed 1 and $R = 2000$, the sample mean is $8.017$ (standard error $0.063$) and variance is $7.874$ (standard error $0.258$), both within 0.5 standard errors of $\mu$.
The Fano factor is $\hat F = 0.982$ with standard error $0.031$ and 95% interval $[0.921, 1.043]$, which contains 1.
Relative-frequency symbols follow the Poisson probability-mass step curve.

As the seed changes, a 95% interval excludes 1 about 5% of the time.
Varying seeds from 1 to 40 gives two seeds, 6 and 28, where $|\hat F - 1|$ exceeds 1.96 standard errors.
For seed 42, $\hat F = 1.085$ and interval $[1.018, 1.153]$ excludes 1, with difference $2.49\,\mathrm{SE}$.
At $t_{\mathrm g} = 2$ ms, $\mu = 1.6$; the distribution concentrates near 0 with a right tail, but $\hat F = 1.009 \pm 0.032$.

## Summary

Generate decay times by summing `Exponential` waiting times and compare relative frequencies of fixed-time counts with `pmf` of `Poisson`.
For Poisson counts, the sample Fano factor's SE is approximately $F\sqrt{2/(R-1)}$, giving a width of about $\pm 0.06$ for interpreting differences from 1 with $R = 2000$ intervals.
About 5% of seeds give 95% intervals excluding 1; interpret results across seeds rather than rejecting Poisson behavior from one seed's deviation.
Even for small mean count $\mu$ and a skewed distribution, the Fano factor fluctuates around 1 on the SE scale.
