A radiation detector cannot count decays arriving while it processes a pulse.
This dead time $\tau$ lowers the observed rate below the true decay rate and reduces recorded-count fluctuations below those of a Poisson distribution.
Geiger–Müller counters have dead times of around 100 μs, making correction essential when counting thousands of decays per second.
This page generates decay sequences from the same `Exponential` waiting times as [Poisson behavior and the Fano factor in radioactive counting](../radioactive-counting/), applies nonparalyzable dead time, and checks the observed-rate equation, its correction, and the reduced Fano factor through simulation.

## Nonparalyzable dead time

After recording one decay, a nonparalyzable detector records nothing for the next $\tau$, and decays arriving during that interval do not extend the dead time.
Intervals between recorded decays are the sum $X = \tau + E$, $E \sim \mathrm{Exponential}(n)$ of $\tau$ and an exponential waiting time, with mean $\tau + 1/n$.
Observed rate $m$ is its reciprocal, giving

$$
m = \frac{1}{\tau + 1/n} = \frac{n}{1 + n\tau}, \qquad n = \frac{m}{1 - m\tau}
$$

The right-hand expression recovers the true rate from the observed rate.
If $\hat m$ has standard error $\mathrm{SE}(\hat m)$, the delta method gives $\mathrm{SE}(\hat n) \approx \mathrm{SE}(\hat m)/(1 - \hat m\tau)^2$.
As $m\tau$ approaches 1, the denominator shrinks, greatly increasing uncertainty in the corrected rate.

Because recorded intervals cannot be shorter than $\tau$, the recorded sequence is more regular than a Poisson process.
For a renewal process with interval mean $\mu_X$ and variance $\sigma_X^2$, the Fano factor approaches $\sigma_X^2/\mu_X^2$ at long counting times.
Because $\sigma_X^2 = 1/n^2$,

$$
F_{\mathrm{obs}} \to \frac{1/n^2}{(\tau + 1/n)^2} = \frac{1}{(1 + n\tau)^2} = (1 - m\tau)^2
$$

so observed counts are sub-Poissonian.
For finite $t_{\mathrm g}$, expanding the count variance for a stationary renewal process (Cox, 1962) gives $F_{\mathrm{obs}} \approx \rho^2 + c/(m t_{\mathrm g})$.
where $\rho = 1/(1+n\tau)$ and $c = 1/6 + \rho^4/2 - 2\rho^3/3$.
Since $c$ satisfies $0 \le c \le 1/6$, the correction does not exceed $1/(6 m t_{\mathrm g})$.
Use the same $F\sqrt{2/(R-1)}$ as an approximation for the standard error of $\hat F_{\mathrm{obs}}$.

The following cell applies nonparalyzable dead time to one continuous decay sequence and counts true and recorded arrivals in $R = 1000$ consecutive intervals.
Dead time continues across interval boundaries.
Inputs are decay rate $n$ [s⁻¹], dead time $\tau$ [μs], counting time $t_{\mathrm g}$ [ms], and a random seed.
The table compares the true-count Fano factor, observed rate $\hat m$, corrected rate $\hat n$, and recorded-count Fano factor with closed-form theory and standard errors.
The plot varies the true rate as $n/8, 2n/8, \dots, n$, showing $\hat m$ and $\hat n$ from 300 intervals each as symbols, $m = n/(1 + n\tau)$ as a dashed line, and the no-dead-time value $m = n$ as a dotted line.

```rust
//| ref: random-radioactive-dead-time
//| title: "Measure observed rates and Fano factors with nonparalyzable dead time"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   dead_rate: {"type":"range","label":"Decay rate n [s⁻¹]","description":"True rate in the table and the maximum horizontal-axis value in the plot.","min":200,"max":5000,"step":200,"value":2000}
//|   dead_time_us: {"type":"range","label":"Dead time τ [μs]","description":"At 0, the detector has no dead time.","min":0,"max":500,"step":25,"value":200}
//|   dead_gate_ms: {"type":"range","label":"Counting time t_g [ms]","description":"Shorter counting times increase the finite-time Fano factor correction.","min":2,"max":200,"step":2,"value":50}
//|   dead_seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","description":"SplitMix64 seed.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":42}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"no-dead-time","label":"τ = 0","description":"Recorded counts equal true counts, and both observed and corrected rates are 2000.1 s⁻¹.","inputs":{"dead_time_us":0}}
//|   - {"id":"short-gate","label":"t_g = 2 ms","description":"The finite-time correction c/(m t_g) rises to 0.019, changing the theoretical Fano factor from 0.510 to 0.529.","inputs":{"dead_gate_ms":2}}
//|   - {"id":"high-rate","label":"n = 5000 s⁻¹, τ = 500 μs","description":"At nτ = 2.5, the observed rate falls to 1429 s⁻¹, and the corrected-rate standard error is about 12 times the observed-rate standard error.","inputs":{"dead_rate":5000,"dead_time_us":500}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.86,"max":1.14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":1428.5714,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":1410,"max":1447}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":1960,"max":2040}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":0.44,"max":0.58}}
//|   - case: no-dead-time
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":1975,"max":2025}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: short-gate
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/4/5","approx":{"value":0.52906,"atol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: high-rate
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":0.0816327,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rates","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"min":15,"max":25}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Exponential, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let (tau, gate) = (dead_time_us * 1e-6, dead_gate_ms * 1e-3); // [s]
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(dead_seed.round() as u64);
// Return true and recorded counts per interval from one continuous decay sequence.
let mut simulate = |rate: f64, r: usize| -> Result<Vec<(f64, f64)>, String> {
    let waiting = Exponential::new(rate).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut counts = vec![(0.0, 0.0); r];
    let mut next = waiting.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut last = f64::NEG_INFINITY; // Time of the last recorded event
    for (g, (n_true, n_obs)) in counts.iter_mut().enumerate() {
        let close = (g + 1) as f64 * gate;
        while next < close {
            *n_true += 1.0;
            if next - last >= tau {
                *n_obs += 1.0;
                last = next;
            }
            next += waiting.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        }
    }
    Ok(counts)
};
let moments = |x: &[f64]| {
    let mean = x.iter().sum::<f64>() / x.len() as f64;
    let var = x.iter().map(|v| (v - mean) * (v - mean)).sum::<f64>() / (x.len() - 1) as f64;
    (mean, var)
};
let r = 1000_usize;
let counts = simulate(dead_rate, r)?;
let (true_counts, obs_counts): (Vec<f64>, Vec<f64>) = counts.into_iter().unzip();
let (mean_t, var_t) = moments(&true_counts);
let (mean_o, var_o) = moments(&obs_counts);
let fano_se = RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 / (r as f64 - 1.0));
let m_hat = mean_o / gate;
let m_se = RealScalar::sqrt(var_o / r as f64) / gate;
let n_hat = m_hat / (1.0 - m_hat * tau);
let n_se = m_se / RealScalar::powi(1.0 - m_hat * tau, 2);
// Closed-form theoretical values (independent reference).
let m = dead_rate / (1.0 + dead_rate * tau);
let rho = 1.0 / (1.0 + dead_rate * tau);
let c = 1.0 / 6.0 + RealScalar::powi(rho, 4) / 2.0 - 2.0 * RealScalar::powi(rho, 3) / 3.0;
let (f_t, f_o) = (var_t / mean_t, var_o / mean_o);
let rows: Vec<_> = [
    ("True-count Fano factor [-]", f_t, f_t * fano_se, 1.0),
    ("Observed rate m̂ [s⁻¹]", m_hat, m_se, m),
    ("Corrected rate n̂ [s⁻¹]", n_hat, n_se, dead_rate),
    (
        "Recorded-count Fano factor (asymptote ρ²) [-]",
        f_o,
        f_o * fano_se,
        rho * rho,
    ),
    (
        "Recorded-count Fano factor (ρ² + c/(m t_g)) [-]",
        f_o,
        f_o * fano_se,
        rho * rho + c / (m * gate),
    ),
]
.into_iter()
.map(|(name, x, se, theory)| {
    (
        name,
        x,
        se,
        x - 1.96 * se,
        x + 1.96 * se,
        theory,
        (x - theory) / se,
    )
})
.collect();
let digits4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Estimate", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "SE", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "95% interval lower bound", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "95% interval upper bound", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Theoretical value", "type": "number", "format": digits4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "z = (estimate − theory)/SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rates" })
);
let (mut observed, mut restored, mut curve) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for j in 1..=8 {
    let rate = dead_rate * j as f64 / 8.0;
    let sweep: Vec<f64> = simulate(rate, 300)?.into_iter().map(|c| c.1).collect();
    let rate_hat = moments(&sweep).0 / gate;
    observed.push([rate, rate_hat]);
    restored.push([rate, rate_hat / (1.0 - rate_hat * tau)]);
}
for j in 0..=40 {
    let rate = dead_rate * j as f64 / 40.0;
    curve.push([rate, rate / (1.0 + rate * tau)]);
}
let identity = vec![[0.0, 0.0], [dead_rate, dead_rate]];
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "xLabel": "True decay rate n [s⁻¹]",
    "yLabel": "Rate [s⁻¹]",
    "xMin": 0.0,
    "yMin": 0.0,
    "layers": [
        {
            "id": "model",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "m = n/(1 + nτ)",
            "points": curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        },
        {
            "id": "identity",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "No dead time m = n",
            "points": identity,
            "style": { "lineType": "dotted" }
        },
        { "id": "observed", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Observed rate m̂", "points": observed },
        {
            "id": "restored",
            "kind": "scatter",
            "name": "Corrected rate n̂",
            "points": restored,
            "style": { "symbol": "diamond" }
        }
    ]
}));
```

With default $n = 2000$ s⁻¹ and $\tau = 200$ μs, $n\tau = 0.4$ and the theoretical observed rate is $m = 1428.57$ s⁻¹.
For seed 42, the observed rate is $\hat m = 1425.04 \pm 3.73$ s⁻¹ (difference $-0.95\,\mathrm{SE}$), and the corrected rate is $\hat n = 1993.1 \pm 7.3$ s⁻¹, with the specified 2000 s⁻¹ inside its 95% interval $[1978.8, 2007.4]$.
The true-count Fano factor for the same sequence is $0.995 \pm 0.045$.
The recorded-count Fano factor is $0.489 \pm 0.022$, differing from the asymptote $(1 - m\tau)^2 = 0.510$ by $-0.95\,\mathrm{SE}$.
At $t_{\mathrm g} = 50$ ms, the finite-time correction is $0.0008$, much smaller than the standard error.

At $\tau = 0$, recorded and true counts coincide, giving $\hat m = \hat n = 2000.1$ s⁻¹.
At $t_{\mathrm g} = 2$ ms, the correction rises to $0.019$ and theory becomes $0.529$; the observed $\hat F_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0.523 \pm 0.023$ differs by $0.56\,\mathrm{SE}$ from the asymptote and by $-0.24\,\mathrm{SE}$ from the corrected theory.
At $n = 5000$ s⁻¹ and $\tau = 500$ μs, $n\tau = 2.5$ and the observed rate falls to $1430.4 \pm 1.6$ s⁻¹.
The corrected rate is $5022.7 \pm 19.5$ s⁻¹, with its standard error amplified by $(1 - \hat m\tau)^{-2} \approx 12$.
The recorded-count Fano factor is then $0.088 \pm 0.004$, differing from corrected theory $0.084$ by $0.97\,\mathrm{SE}$.
In the plot, observed-rate symbols follow the dashed curve, while corrected-rate symbols follow the dotted line $m = n$.

## Summary

For a detector with nonparalyzable dead time $\tau$, the observed rate falls to $n/(1 + n\tau)$, and $n = m/(1 - m\tau)$ restores the true rate.
The corrected-rate SE is $(1 - m\tau)^{-2}$ times the observed-rate SE, so estimate correction uncertainty first at high count rates where $m\tau$ approaches 1.
Recorded counts are sub-Poissonian with Fano factor $(1 - m\tau)^2$, with correction $c/(m t_{\mathrm g})$ at short counting times.
When estimating radioactivity uncertainty from count variance, therefore, use the measured variance rather than the Poisson value $\sqrt N$.
