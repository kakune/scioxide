With a singular covariance matrix, normal samples lie in a lower-dimensional subspace rather than spreading throughout the space.
Density is undefined outside this subspace, and the usual probability density formula cannot be used because the determinant is 0.
Using two dimensions perfectly linked by one factor, this example compares how `MultivariateNormal` generates samples from singular covariance, tests support membership, and evaluates on-support pseudodensity with closed forms.
[Sampling variation and standard errors of normal samples](../normal-sampling/) covers interpreting normal sample statistics through standard errors.

## Two linked dimensions

Suppose a machining fixture's positional error $u$ [mm] follows $\mathcal{N}(0, s)$ and determines deviations $(X_1, X_2)$ [mm] from two nominal dimensions as

$$
X_1 = 1 + u, \qquad X_2 = -2 + 2u
$$


The two dimensions are perfectly linked on the line $X_2 + 2 = 2(X_1 - 1)$, with mean and covariance

$$
\boldsymbol{\mu} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = s \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}
$$


$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is a positive semidefinite matrix of rank 1, with eigenvalues $5s$ and $0$; a unit eigenvector for eigenvalue $5s$ is $\mathbf{q} = (1, 2)/\sqrt{5}$.

Adding fluctuation $\varepsilon$ of variance $\delta$ [mm²] to the second dimension, independently of the positional error, gives $X_2 = -2 + 2u + \varepsilon$ and covariance

$$
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\delta = \begin{bmatrix} s & 2s \\ 2s & 4s + \delta \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\delta = s\delta
$$


It is nonsingular for $\delta > 0$, and the line residual $e = X_2 + 2 - 2(X_1 - 1) = \varepsilon$ follows $\mathcal{N}(0, \delta)$.
For small $\delta$, the smallest eigenvalue is approximately $\delta/5$.

## Support and pseudodensity

The support of the distribution with $\delta = 0$ is the line through $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ in direction $\mathbf{q}$.
Points on the support can be written as $\mathbf{x} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + t\mathbf{q}$, with coordinate $t = \mathbf{q}^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})$ following $\mathcal{N}(0, 5s)$.
Density with respect to one-dimensional length on the support is called pseudodensity; its logarithm is

$$
\log p(\mathbf{x}) = -\tfrac12 \left( \log(2\pi \cdot 5s) + \frac{t^2}{5s} \right)
$$


$p$ has units 1/mm and is undefined outside the support.

In the nonsingular case $\delta > 0$, writing $a = x_1 - 1$ and $e = x_2 + 2 - 2a$ separates the quadratic form as $(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})^{\mathsf{T}} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_\delta^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}) = e^2/\delta + a^2/s$, giving log density

$$
\log p(\mathbf{x}) = -\log(2\pi) - \tfrac12 \log(s\delta) - \tfrac12 \left( \frac{e^2}{\delta} + \frac{a^2}{s} \right)
$$

with units 1/mm².
Computing in this form without forming an inverse avoids cancellation even for small $\delta$.

## Rank and samples in MultivariateNormal

`MultivariateNormal::new(&mean, &covariance)` copies the mean and covariance, divides the covariance by its component of largest absolute value, and performs a symmetric eigendecomposition.
Covariance must be exactly symmetric; asymmetry, negative diagonal entries, or an eigenvalue $\lambda$ satisfying $\lambda < -8 d \varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \lambda_{\max}$ gives `DomainError`.
Here $d$ is dimension and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$ is machine epsilon; negative eigenvalues with smaller absolute values are treated as rounding error and set to 0.
The retained rank $r$ is the number of eigenvalues above $d \varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \lambda_{\max}$ under the default `RankPolicy::DimensionScaledEpsilon`.
Construction uses $O(d^3)$ time and $O(d^2)$ storage.

Samples are constructed from $r$ retained eigenpairs $(\lambda_i, \mathbf{v}_i)$ and $r$ independent standard normal variates $z_i$ as

$$
\mathbf{x} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sqrt{\lambda_i}\, z_i\, \mathbf{v}_i
$$

at a cost of $O(dr)$ per sample.
For this example's $\delta = 0$, $r = 1$, and one variate determines one point.
Eigenvector rounding error can move samples off the line by a rounding-scale amount.

`contains_support(x, tolerance)` returns true when the residual of projecting $\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}$ onto the span of the retained eigenvectors satisfies

$$
\bigl\|(I - VV^{\mathsf{T}})(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu})\bigr\|_\infty \le \tau_{\mathrm{abs}} + \tau_{\mathrm{rel}} \|\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}\|_\infty
$$

where $V$ is the $d \times r$ matrix of retained eigenvectors and $\tau$ are absolute and relative tolerances from `Tolerance`.
A nonsingular distribution ($r = d$) includes every finite point in its support.
`log_pseudo_density(x, tolerance)` projects an on-support point onto the support and returns the logarithm of its $r$-dimensional pseudodensity, returning $-\infty$ for off-support points without guessing a value.
Calling the ordinary `LogDensity::log_pdf` on a singular distribution gives `InvalidInput`.

## Inspect samples on a line and their pseudodensity

The following cell generates 64 samples from inputs of covariance scale $s$ [mm²], independent fluctuation variance $\delta$ [mm²] for the second dimension, and a random seed.
Compute line residual $e$ directly in the cell without the library's support test, comparing its maximum and root mean square with $\sqrt{\delta}$.
The second table compares log pseudodensities of the first sample and a point with only $X_2$ shifted by 0.1 mm (distance $0.1/\sqrt{5} \approx 0.045$ mm from the line) with the closed forms above for the retained rank.
Set both absolute and relative tolerances to $10^{-12}$.

```rust
//| id: random-psd-line-support
//| ref: random-psd-line-support
//| title: "Inspect samples on a line from positive semidefinite covariance"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-random, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   covariance_scale: {"type":"range","label":"Covariance scale s [mm²]","min":0.25,"max":4,"step":0.25,"value":1}
//|   noise_variance: {"type":"select","label":"Independent fluctuation variance of the second dimension δ [mm²]","options":["0","1e-15","1e-6","0.01"],"value":"0"}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":42}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"wide-scale","label":"s = 4 mm²","description":"Samples spread twice as far along the line, while residuals remain at rounding scale.","inputs":{"covariance_scale":4}}
//|   - {"id":"other-seed","label":"Seed 7","description":"All points in another set of 64 samples also belong to the support.","inputs":{"seed":7}}
//|   - {"id":"below-threshold","label":"δ = 10⁻¹⁵ mm²","description":"The smallest eigenvalue is below the rank threshold, so rank stays 1. The fluctuation is omitted from samples.","inputs":{"noise_variance":"1e-15"}}
//|   - {"id":"small-noise","label":"δ = 10⁻⁶ mm²","description":"Rank becomes 2, and the root mean square line residual is close to √δ = 10⁻³ mm.","inputs":{"noise_variance":"1e-6"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":64}
//|       - {"artifact":"density","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"density","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":null}
//|   - case: wide-scale
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":64}
//|       - {"artifact":"density","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: other-seed
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":64}
//|       - {"artifact":"density","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: below-threshold
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"density","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":null}
//|   - case: small-noise
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"support","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":6e-4,"max":1.4e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"density","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"density","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":-1000}}
use scioxide_core::{RealScalar, Tolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_random::{MultivariateNormal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let s = covariance_scale;
let delta: f64 = noise_variance.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot read δ as a number")?;
let covariance = Matrix::from_vec(2, 2, vec![s, 2.0 * s, 2.0 * s, 4.0 * s + delta])
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let law = MultivariateNormal::new(&[1.0, -2.0], &covariance).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rank = law.rank();
let tolerance = Tolerance::new(1e-12, 1e-12).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let (mut worst, mut square_sum, mut on_support) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0_usize);
let mut points = Vec::with_capacity(64);
for _ in 0..64 {
    let x = law.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let x = x.as_slice();
    // Line residual e = X₂ + 2 − 2 (X₁ − 1) (independent check without the support test).
    let residual = x[1] + 2.0 - 2.0 * (x[0] - 1.0);
    worst = worst.max(residual.abs());
    square_sum += residual * residual;
    on_support += usize::from(
        law.contains_support(x, tolerance)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    );
    points.push([x[0], x[1]]);
}
// Independent check: closed-form log density for the retained rank (one-dimensional on the line or two-dimensional in the plane).
let two_pi = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI;
let line_density = |x: [f64; 2]| {
    let t = ((x[0] - 1.0) + 2.0 * (x[1] + 2.0)) / RealScalar::sqrt(5.0_f64);
    -0.5 * (RealScalar::ln(two_pi * 5.0 * s) + t * t / (5.0 * s))
};
let plane_density = |x: [f64; 2]| {
    let a = x[0] - 1.0;
    let e = x[1] + 2.0 - 2.0 * a;
    -RealScalar::ln(two_pi)
        - 0.5 * RealScalar::ln(s * delta)
        - 0.5 * (e * e / delta + a * a / s)
};
let first = points[0];
// Shifting only X₂ by 0.1 mm gives distance 0.1/√5 ≈ 0.045 mm from the line.
let shifted = [first[0], first[1] + 0.1];
let (on_expected, off_expected) = if rank == 1 {
    (Some(line_density(first)), None)
} else {
    (Some(plane_density(first)), Some(plane_density(shifted)))
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, x, expected) in [
    ("First sample", first, on_expected),
    ("Point with X₂ shifted by 0.1 mm", shifted, off_expected),
] {
    let value = law
        .log_pseudo_density(&x, tolerance)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Use None (displayed as blank) instead of placing off-support −∞ in the table.
    let value = value.is_finite().then_some(value);
    let difference = value.zip(expected).map(|(v, w)| (v - w).abs());
    rows.push((name, value, expected, difference));
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Dimensional deviation X₁ [mm]",
        "yLabel": "Dimensional deviation X₂ [mm]",
        "xMin": -6.0,
        "xMax": 8.0,
        "yMin": -16.0,
        "yMax": 12.0,
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "layers": [
            {
                "id": "line",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Support line X₂ + 2 = 2(X₁ − 1)",
                "style": {"lineType": "dashed"},
                "points": [[-6.0, -16.0], [8.0, 12.0]]
            },
            {"id": "samples", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Sample", "points": points}
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "samples" })
);
let support_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Retained rank r", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum residual |e| [mm]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Root mean square residual [mm]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "√δ [mm]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "contains_support is true [points] (out of 64)", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    support_columns,
    vec![(
        rank,
        worst,
        RealScalar::sqrt(square_sum / 64.0),
        RealScalar::sqrt(delta),
        on_support
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "support" })
);
let density_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Point" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Library [ln mm⁻ʳ]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "−∞" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form [ln mm⁻ʳ]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "−∞" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Absolute difference", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "−" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    density_columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "density" })
);
```

With the default inputs ($s = 1$ mm², $\delta = 0$, seed 42), retained rank is 1, the maximum line residual is $4.44 \times 10^{-16}$ mm, and all 64 samples satisfy `contains_support`.
The first sample's log pseudodensity $-2.9524$ agrees with the closed form to $4.4 \times 10^{-16}$; shifting $X_2$ by 0.1 mm (about 0.045 mm from the line) gives log pseudodensity $-\infty$.
Even at $s = 4$ mm², residuals remain at $8.9 \times 10^{-16}$ mm and the support test is true for all 64 points.

At $\delta = 10^{-15}$ mm², the smallest eigenvalue is about $2 \times 10^{-16}$ mm², below the rank threshold $d \varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \lambda_{\max} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-15}$ mm², so it is discarded and rank stays 1.
The root mean square line residual remains at rounding scale, and the original fluctuation $\sqrt{\delta} \approx 3.2 \times 10^{-8}$ mm does not appear in samples.
At $\delta = 10^{-6}$ mm², rank becomes 2 and the root mean square residual is $9.47 \times 10^{-4}$ mm, differing from $\sqrt{\delta} = 10^{-3}$ mm by a relative 5%.
The root mean square estimated from 64 residuals has relative standard error about $1/\sqrt{2 \cdot 64} \approx 0.09$, so this difference lies within sampling fluctuations.
The point shifted by 0.1 mm then also lies in the support, with a large negative finite log density $-4839.40$.
The first sample's log density agrees with the closed form to $9.5 \times 10^{-10}$.
This exceeds rounding scale because the eigendecomposition obtains the smallest eigenvalue $\approx \delta/5 = 2 \times 10^{-7}$ mm² with absolute error proportional to the largest eigenvalue $5s$ (about $10^{-15}$ mm²).
This relative error of order $5 \times 10^{-9}$ enters the log density through the log determinant and quadratic form.

Choose support-test tolerances larger than rounding error.
A tolerance below rounding scale can classify samples carrying rounding error as off-support.
Conversely, to deliberately treat a distribution with slightly positive $\delta$ as rank 1, pass `MultivariateNormalOptions::default().with_rank_policy(RankPolicy::Threshold(tolerance))` to `MultivariateNormal::with_options` to increase the threshold.
Fluctuations in directions whose eigenvalues fall below the threshold are omitted from samples.

## Summary

`MultivariateNormal` performs a symmetric eigendecomposition of the covariance, by default retaining only eigenvalues above $d \varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}} \lambda_{\max}$ and generating samples from that number $r$ of standard normal variates.
For rank-one covariance, samples lie on the support line within rounding error; direct residual calculation checks this independently of the library's test.
On-support pseudodensity is a density with respect to $r$-dimensional measure and returns $-\infty$ outside the support, so use `log_pseudo_density` rather than `log_pdf`.
Eigenvalues below the threshold are omitted from samples, so choose `RankPolicy` according to whether small independent fluctuations should be retained or discarded.
