Angular data such as wind directions, ocean-current directions, and animal movement directions cannot be summarized by arithmetic averaging on the real line.
Winds at 350° and 10° are both within 10° of north (0°), but their numerical arithmetic mean is 180° (south).
Directional statistics treats each angle as a unit vector on the unit circle and defines mean direction and concentration through vector averages.
The von Mises distribution is bell-shaped on the circle, approaching a normal distribution for large concentration $\kappa$ and becoming uniform at $\kappa = 0$.
This page models wind direction with `VonMises` and compares its mean resultant length, a measure of concentration, with values calculated using modified Bessel functions in scioxide-special.
[Wind-direction samples and bias in the mean resultant length](../von-mises-wind-samples/) examines bias and standard errors of statistics from finitely many observations.

## Azimuth conventions

Express wind direction as azimuth $\theta$, with north at 0° and clockwise east at 90°, south at 180°, and west at 270°.
Meteorological wind direction names the direction the wind comes from, so 315° is a northwesterly wind.
The 16 compass names are spaced every 22.5°: north at 0°, north-northwest at 337.5°, and northwest at 315°.
350° is closer to north (360° = 0°) than north-northwest (337.5°) and is classified as north in the 16-direction system.

The unit vector for azimuth $\theta$ is (east component, north component) $= (\sin\theta, \cos\theta)$.
It relates to the mathematical angle $\vartheta$, measured counterclockwise from an east-pointing x-axis, by $\vartheta = \pi/2 - \theta$.
The von Mises family is closed under rotation and reflection, and the mean resultant length is invariant under both, so this page passes azimuths directly as angles to the von Mises distribution.
Mean direction is $\operatorname{atan2}(\text{mean east component}, \text{mean north component})$, obtained directly as an azimuth.

## The von Mises distribution and mean resultant length

The von Mises density with mean direction $\mu$ and concentration $\kappa \ge 0$ is

$$
f(\theta;\mu,\kappa) = \frac{\exp\{\kappa\cos(\theta - \mu)\}}{2\pi I_0(\kappa)}
$$


$I_\nu$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order $\nu$; for $\nu = 0, 1$, its series is

$$
I_\nu(\kappa) = \sum_{j \ge 0} \frac{(\kappa/2)^{2j+\nu}}{j!\,(j+\nu)!}
$$

(DLMF 10.25.2).
At $\kappa = 0$ it has uniform density $f = 1/(2\pi)$; for large $\kappa$ it approaches a normal distribution with mean $\mu$ and variance $1/\kappa$.

The unit vector's expectation, in components relative to $\mu$, is $E[\cos(\theta - \mu)] = I_1(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa)$ and $E[\sin(\theta - \mu)] = 0$.
The first identity follows by differentiating the density normalization $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{\kappa\cos\phi}\,d\phi = 2\pi I_0(\kappa)$ with respect to $\kappa$ and using $I_0' = I_1$.
The length of the expectation vector,

$$
A(\kappa) = \frac{I_1(\kappa)}{I_0(\kappa)}
$$

is called the population mean resultant length, and $1 - A(\kappa)$ is the circular variance.
$A$ ranges from 0 to 1; values closer to 1 indicate wind directions concentrated around the mean direction.
At $\kappa = 3$, $A = 0.8100$ and circular variance is 0.1900.

The leading series terms give the small-$\kappa$ behavior $A(\kappa) = \kappa/2 - \kappa^3/16 + O(\kappa^5)$.
For large $\kappa$, the asymptotic expansion of $I_\nu$ (DLMF 10.40.1) gives

$$
A(\kappa) = 1 - \frac{1}{2\kappa} - \frac{1}{8\kappa^2} + O(\kappa^{-3})
$$


At large $\kappa$, both $I_0(\kappa)$ and $I_1(\kappa)$ grow on the scale of $e^\kappa/\sqrt{2\pi\kappa}$ and exceed the double-precision limit at $\kappa \approx 714$.
Use exponentially scaled functions, each multiplied by $e^{-\kappa}$, to obtain the same ratio without overflow.

## The VonMises API

`VonMises::new(location, concentration)` accepts mean direction `location` [rad] and concentration `concentration`, using the default `LawOptions`.
Use `VonMises::with_options(location, concentration, options)` to change tolerance and the work limit.
Nonfinite `location` and `concentration` give `NonFinite`; negative `concentration` or `location` so large that rounding $\mu \pm \pi$ returns $\mu$ gives `DomainError`.
Reaching the Bessel evaluation work limit gives `ConvergenceFailure`; passing an out-of-range tolerance to `with_options` gives `InvalidInput`.

`pdf` is a density with period $2\pi$, evaluable at any real angle.
Distribution functions, quantiles, moments, and samples use angles in the principal interval $[\mu - \pi, \mu + \pi]$; `winding_cdf` extends the distribution function to the real line.
`sample` uses Best–Fisher (1979) rejection for $\kappa \ge 0.5$, uniform-envelope rejection for $0 < \kappa < 0.5$, and uniform random values for $\kappa = 0$.
Both rejection methods return an error if no proposal is accepted within 1,024 attempts.

`mean_resultant_length()` returns $A(\kappa)$ computed at construction, and `circular_variance()` returns $1 - A$.
Construction computes the ratio of exponentially scaled modified Bessel functions in scioxide-special; at $\kappa > 10^8$ it uses the two asymptotic terms $1 - 1/(2\kappa) - 1/(8\kappa^2)$ above.
`mean_direction()` returns $\mu$ for $\kappa > 0$ and `DomainError` for $\kappa = 0$ because the mean direction is undefined.

Values from `mean_resultant_length()` come from scioxide-special Bessel functions, so agreement with the `bessel_i(order, x)` ratio compares two functions of the same crate rather than providing an independent check.
This page includes a ratio from a series calculated by hand as an independent check.

## Four calculations of A(κ)

The following cell takes concentration $\kappa$ (0–10) and computes $A(\kappa)$ using the ratio of `bessel_i`, `VonMises::mean_resultant_length()`, a series calculated by hand, and asymptotic form $1 - 1/(2\kappa) - 1/(8\kappa^2)$.
Truncate the series after 80 terms.
For $\kappa \le 10$, the sum from term 81 onward is smaller than double-precision rounding error.
The plot shows the ratio of `bessel_i` for $\kappa \in [0, 10]$ as a solid line, series values every 0.5 as symbols, and the asymptotic form for $\kappa \ge 2$ as a dashed line, with a vertical line at the input $\kappa$.
The table gives all four values at input $\kappa$, circular variance $1 - A$, and differences from the Bessel ratio.
At $\kappa = 0$ the asymptotic form is undefined, so its row is blank.

// Independent check: compute A = I₁/I₀ with the series I_ν(κ) = Σ (κ/2)^(2j+ν) / (j! (j+ν)!).
let series = |k: f64| {
    let (mut i0, mut i1, mut term) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 1.0_f64);
    for j in 0..80 {
        i0 += term;
        i1 += term * k / (2.0 * (j + 1) as f64);
        term *= (k / 2.0) * (k / 2.0) / (((j + 1) * (j + 1)) as f64);
    }
    i1 / i0
};
let bessel = |k: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let i1 = scioxide_special::bessel_i(1.0, k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let i0 = scioxide_special::bessel_i(0.0, k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(i1 / i0)
};
// Large-κ asymptotic form 1 − 1/(2κ) − 1/(8κ²). Undefined at κ = 0.
let asymptotic = |k: f64| (k > 0.0).then(|| 1.0 - 0.5 / k - 0.125 / (k * k));

for j in 0..=80 {

    curve.push([k, bessel(k)?]);

    }
    if k >= 2.0 {
        tail.push([k, 1.0 - 0.5 / k - 0.125 / (k * k)]);
    }
}

At default $\kappa = 3$, the ratio of `bessel_i`, `mean_resultant_length()`, and the series all give $A = 0.8100$, with circular variance 0.1900 and differences from the Bessel ratio at most $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$.
The asymptotic form is 0.8194, still differing by $9.5 \times 10^{-3}$ at $\kappa = 3$.
The curve starts linearly as $A \approx \kappa/2$ for small $\kappa$ and reaches 0.9486 at $\kappa = 10$.
The dashed asymptotic curve approaches the solid curve as $\kappa$ grows.
The next expansion term is $-1/(8\kappa^3)$, so the difference from the asymptotic form shrinks proportionally to $\kappa^{-3}$ for large $\kappa$.
In the “κ = 10” case, the difference is $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$, close to the next term's magnitude $1.25 \times 10^{-4}$.
In the “κ = 0” case, $I_1(0) = 0$ gives $A = 0$ and circular variance 1, with a blank asymptotic row.

## Summary

Express wind directions as clockwise azimuths from north at 0°, deriving mean direction and mean resultant length from the average of unit vectors $(\sin\theta, \cos\theta)$.
`VonMises::new(location, concentration)` accepts angles in radians; `pdf` is the periodic density, and `sample` returns angles in $[\mu - \pi, \mu + \pi]$.
The population mean resultant length $A(\kappa) = I_1(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa)$ is available from `mean_resultant_length()` or the ratio of `bessel_i`, giving 0.8100 at $\kappa = 3$.
When computing $A(\kappa)$ yourself, use a series for small $\kappa$ and exponentially scaled Bessel ratios for large $\kappa$; around $\kappa = 3$, do not rely on only two asymptotic terms.
Agreement between `mean_resultant_length()` and the `bessel_i` ratio compares calculations from the same crate; use the series for an independent check.
