[Wishart distributions and sample covariance matrices](../wishart-sample-covariance/) checked componentwise means and variances of the scatter matrix $W$.
Even when individual components are estimated without bias, matrix eigenvalues are biased.
When dimension $p$ is not negligible relative to observation count $n$, sample covariance eigenvalues spread widely even if all true covariance eigenvalues are equal.
This page forms $S = W/n$ from `Wishart` with $\Sigma = I$ and compares its eigenvalue distribution, computed with scioxide-linalg symmetric eigenvalues, with the Marchenko–Pastur law.

## Eigenvalue spread and the Marchenko–Pastur law

For $\Sigma = I$, in the limit $p, n \to \infty$ and $p/n \to \gamma \in (0, 1]$, the eigenvalue distribution of $S = W/n$ approaches the density

$$
f(\lambda) = \frac{\sqrt{(\lambda_+ - \lambda)(\lambda - \lambda_-)}}{2\pi\gamma\lambda},
\qquad \lambda_\pm = (1 \pm \sqrt{\gamma})^2,
\qquad \lambda_- \le \lambda \le \lambda_+
$$

(the Marchenko–Pastur law).
Although population eigenvalues are all 1, sample eigenvalues spread over $[\lambda_-, \lambda_+]$, with the largest above 1 and the smallest below 1.
Interpreting the largest sample covariance eigenvalue directly as a population value in principal component analysis overestimates it by this bias.

The eigenvalue mean is $\operatorname{tr}(S)/p$; because $\operatorname{tr} W$ is the sum of squares of $np$ independent standard normal variables, $E[\operatorname{tr} W] = np$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\operatorname{tr} W) = 2np$.
Thus, the expected eigenvalue mean is 1, with per-matrix SE $\sqrt{2/(np)}$.
For finite $p$, the largest eigenvalue fluctuates on a scale of $n^{-2/3}$ (the Tracy–Widom distribution), with its mean slightly inside endpoint $\lambda_+$.
For the same reason, a few eigenvalues lie outside the support.

## Compute eigenvalues

`HermitianEigenvaluesResult::compute(&s, HermitianInput::Exact)` returns all eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix in ascending order.
After scaling the matrix by its largest component magnitude, it uses Householder transformations to reduce it to tridiagonal form and square-root-free QL/QR iteration to compute eigenvalues.
Computational cost is $O(p^3)$, with storage $O(p^2)$.
`Exact` requires exactly symmetric entries; matrices failing this condition give `InvalidInput`.
A `Wishart` sample has identical $(i, j)$ and $(j, i)$ entries, so it can be passed with `Exact` even after division by $n$.

The following cell takes dimension $p$ (10–100), degrees of freedom $n$ (100–1000), replicate count $R$ (2–20), and a seed, drawing $R$ matrices from `Wishart` with $\Sigma = I$.
At $p = 41$ and above, work exceeds the default limit, so raise the constructor's limit to $p^3$.
Sampling and eigendecomposition each require $O(p^3)$ operations; at $p = 100$ and $R = 20$, about $10^6$ operations per replicate are repeated 20 times, so run the cell with a button.
The plot groups all eigenvalues into bins of width 0.1, showing their density as symbols and the Marchenko–Pastur density as a dashed line, with reference lines at both support endpoints and population eigenvalue 1.
The first table gives the eigenvalue mean and the averages and SEs of minimum and maximum eigenvalues across replicates.
The second table gives the number of eigenvalues outside the support.

```rust
//| id: random-wishart-marchenko-pastur
//| ref: random-wishart-marchenko-pastur
//| title: "Compare the W/n eigenvalue distribution with the Marchenko–Pastur law"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   dimension: {"type":"range","label":"Dimension p [-]","min":10,"max":100,"step":10,"value":50}
//|   degrees: {"type":"range","label":"Degrees of freedom n [-]","min":100,"max":1000,"step":50,"value":200}
//|   replicates: {"type":"range","label":"Replicates R [replicates]","min":2,"max":20,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"square","label":"p = n = 100","description":"At γ = 1, support is [0, 4] and the density diverges at 0. The mean minimum eigenvalue falls to 5×10⁻⁵.","inputs":{"dimension":100,"degrees":100,"replicates":20}}
//|   - {"id":"tall","label":"p = 10, n = 1000","description":"At γ = 0.01, support shrinks to [0.81, 1.21]. At p = 10 the endpoint approximation is coarse, and the mean maximum eigenvalue is 1.158.","inputs":{"dimension":10,"degrees":1000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"edges","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.98,"max":1.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edges","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.25,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edges","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":2.25,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edges","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":2.05,"max":2.3}}
//|   - case: square
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"edges","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":4,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edges","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":0,"max":0.01}}
//|   - case: tall
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"edges","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.81,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edges","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":1.1,"max":1.25}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::{HermitianEigenvaluesResult, HermitianInput, Matrix};
use scioxide_random::{LawOptions, SeedableRng, SplitMix64, Wishart};

let (p, n, r) = (dimension as usize, degrees as usize, replicates as usize);
let identity = Matrix::<f64>::identity(p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Construction checks that p³ work fits the limit. The default 65 536 allows only p ≤ 40, so raise it to p³.
let work = Budget::try_from(p * p * p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = LawOptions::default().with_work_budget(work);
let law = Wishart::with_options(n as f64, &identity, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let gamma = p as f64 / n as f64;
let (low, high) = (
    RealScalar::powi(1.0 - RealScalar::sqrt(gamma), 2),
    RealScalar::powi(1.0 + RealScalar::sqrt(gamma), 2),
);
let mut values = Vec::with_capacity(p * r);
let (mut smallest, mut largest) = (Vec::with_capacity(r), Vec::with_capacity(r));
for _ in 0..r {
    let w = law.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Eigenvalues of S = W/n. Wishart samples have exactly symmetric entries and accept Exact.
    let s = Matrix::from_vec(p, p, w.iter().map(|v| v / n as f64).collect())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let eigen = HermitianEigenvaluesResult::compute(&s, HermitianInput::Exact)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let lambda = eigen.eigenvalues();
    smallest.push(lambda[0]);
    largest.push(lambda[p - 1]);
    values.extend_from_slice(lambda);
}
// Divide 0 to 4 into 40 bins of width 0.1 and convert frequencies to densities.
let bins = 40;
let width = 4.0 / bins as f64;
let mut counts = vec![0_usize; bins];
for &v in &values {
    if v < 4.0 {
        counts[(v / width) as usize] += 1;
    }
}
let histogram: Vec<[f64; 2]> = counts
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, &c)| {
        [
            (k as f64 + 0.5) * width,
            c as f64 / (values.len() as f64 * width),
        ]
    })
    .collect();
// Independent check: Marchenko–Pastur density √((λ₊ − λ)(λ − λ₋)) / (2π γ λ).
let mut density = Vec::new();
for k in 1..400 {
    let x = low + (high - low) * k as f64 / 400.0;
    let f =
        RealScalar::sqrt((high - x) * (x - low)) / (2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI * gamma * x);
    density.push([x, f]);
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "W/n eigenvalue λ [-]",
        "yLabel": "Probability density [-]",
        "xMin": 0,
        "xMax": 4,
        "yMin": 0,
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "annotations": [
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": low, "label": "(1 − √γ)²"},
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": high, "label": "(1 + √γ)²"},
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": 1.0, "label": "Eigenvalue 1 of Σ"}
        ],
        "layers": [
            {
                "id": "mp",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "Marchenko–Pastur density",
                "style": {"lineType": "dashed"},
                "points": density
            },
            {"id": "sample", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Eigenvalue histogram", "points": histogram}
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "spectrum" })
);
// Means and SEs across replicates. The eigenvalue mean has SE √(2/(npR)) from Var tr(W) = 2np.
let summary = |v: &[f64]| {
    let rf = v.len() as f64;
    let mu = v.iter().sum::<f64>() / rf;
    let var = v.iter().map(|x| (x - mu) * (x - mu)).sum::<f64>() / (rf - 1.0);
    (mu, RealScalar::sqrt(var / rf))
};
let (min_mean, min_se) = summary(&smallest);
let (max_mean, max_se) = summary(&largest);
let mean = values.iter().sum::<f64>() / values.len() as f64;
let mean_se = RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 / (n * p * r) as f64);
let outside = values.iter().filter(|&&v| v < low || v > high).count();
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig2 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Comparison value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![
        ("Mean eigenvalue tr(W)/(np)", mean, mean_se, 1.0),
        ("Mean minimum eigenvalue (compare λ₋)", min_mean, min_se, low),
        ("Mean maximum eigenvalue (compare λ₊)", max_mean, max_se, high),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "edges" })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "γ = p/n [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Eigenvalues outside support [eigenvalues]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Total eigenvalues [eigenvalues]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(gamma, outside, values.len())],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "outside" })
);
```

With the default inputs ($p = 50$, $n = 200$, $\gamma = 0.25$, $R = 10$ replicates, seed 3), support is $[0.25, 2.25]$ and the histogram follows the dashed density.
The eigenvalue mean is 1.0014 (SE 0.0045).
The mean minimum eigenvalue is 0.272 (SE 0.006) and the mean maximum is 2.191 (SE 0.022), both slightly inside the support endpoints.
Relative to population eigenvalue 1, the sample maximum is 2.19 times as large and minimum 0.27 times as large; this spread remains even with $n = 4p$ observations.
Four of 500 eigenvalues lie outside the support; finite $p$ allows eigenvalues beyond the endpoints.

In the “p = n = 100” case, $\gamma = 1$ gives support $[0, 4]$, and the mean minimum eigenvalue falls to $5.0 \times 10^{-5}$.
The mean maximum 3.867 lies 6 times its SE 0.022 inside endpoint 4, with 1 of 2000 eigenvalues outside support.
A sample covariance matrix with $p = n$ is nearly singular, making direct inversion numerically unstable.
In the “p = 10, n = 1000” case, $\gamma = 0.01$ shrinks support to $[0.81, 1.21]$, but the mean maximum 1.158 is separated from endpoint 1.21.
At $p = 10$, the Marchenko–Pastur law indicates the support scale, while endpoint locations are only coarsely approximated.

## Summary

Even for $\Sigma = I$, eigenvalues of $W/n$ spread over $[(1 - \sqrt{\gamma})^2, (1 + \sqrt{\gamma})^2]$, so the largest sample covariance eigenvalue overestimates its population counterpart and the smallest underestimates it.
The eigenvalue mean $\operatorname{tr}(W)/(np)$ remains 1; the bias concerns the eigenvalue spread.
Sample covariance matrices near $p = n$ are nearly singular, so check $p/n$ before computations involving inverses.
`Wishart` samples are exactly symmetric and can be passed to `HermitianEigenvaluesResult::compute` with `HermitianInput::Exact`.
