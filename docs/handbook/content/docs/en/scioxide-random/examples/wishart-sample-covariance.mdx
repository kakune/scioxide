Covariance matrices express relationships among simultaneously measured quantities such as temperature anomalies at several sites, returns on several assets, and multichannel signals.
A sample covariance matrix estimates a covariance matrix and is itself a randomly varying matrix; for normal samples, its distribution is Wishart.
This page generates scatter matrices with `Wishart` and checks their means and component variances against closed forms calculated by hand and scatter matrices from `MultivariateNormal`.
[Sample covariance eigenvalues and the Marchenko–Pastur law](../wishart-marchenko-pastur/) examines eigenvalue spread when dimension is not negligible relative to observation count.

## Wishart distributions and scatter matrices

For independent vectors $x_1, \dots, x_n$ following $N_p(0, \Sigma)$, the scatter matrix $W = \sum_{t=1}^n x_t x_t^\mathsf{T}$ follows a Wishart distribution $W_p(n, \Sigma)$ with degrees of freedom $n$ and scale matrix $\Sigma$.
For $p = 1$, $W/\sigma^2$ follows a chi-squared distribution with $n$ degrees of freedom; the Wishart distribution extends this to matrices.
The Wishart distribution is also defined for real degrees of freedom $n > p - 1$, where it has no interpretation as a sum of $n$ vectors.

Its mean is $E[W] = n\Sigma$.
The fourth moment $E[x_i x_j x_k x_l] = \Sigma_{ij}\Sigma_{kl} + \Sigma_{ik}\Sigma_{jl} + \Sigma_{il}\Sigma_{jk}$ of a normal vector (Isserlis' formula) gives $E[x_i^2 x_j^2] = \Sigma_{ii}\Sigma_{jj} + 2\Sigma_{ij}^2$ and hence $\operatorname{Var}(x_i x_j) = \Sigma_{ij}^2 + \Sigma_{ii}\Sigma_{jj}$; for a sum of $n$ independent terms,

$$
\operatorname{Var}(W_{ij}) = n\left(\Sigma_{ij}^2 + \Sigma_{ii}\Sigma_{jj}\right)
$$


When the mean is known to be 0, $S = W/n$ is an unbiased estimator of $\Sigma$.
When the mean is estimated by sample mean $\bar{x}$, $\sum_t (x_t - \bar{x})(x_t - \bar{x})^\mathsf{T}$ follows $W_p(n - 1, \Sigma)$, and dividing it by $n - 1$ gives an unbiased estimator.

This example models daily mean temperature anomalies (units K) measured at $p$ sites, with scale matrix $\Sigma_{ij} = \sigma^2\rho^{|i-j|}$.
This is the covariance of a stationary first-order autoregressive process.
Its leading $k$-by-$k$ submatrix has the same form and determinant $\sigma^{2k}(1 - \rho^2)^{k-1}$, positive for every $k$ when $|\rho| < 1$.
Sylvester's criterion therefore makes $\Sigma$ with $|\rho| < 1$ positive definite for every $p$.

## Generation by Bartlett decomposition

Cholesky-factor $\Sigma = LL^\mathsf{T}$ and form lower-triangular matrix $A$ with diagonal $A_{ii} = \sqrt{\chi^2_{n-i+1}}$ ($i = 1, \dots, p$) and independent standard normal variables below it; then $W = L A A^\mathsf{T} L^\mathsf{T}$ follows $W_p(n, \Sigma)$ (Bartlett decomposition).
`Wishart::new(df, &scale)` prepares the scale matrix's Cholesky factorization and sampling constants for $p$ chi-squared laws.
Empty or nonsquare matrices give `InvalidInput`, nonfinite values give `NonFinite`, and $\mathrm{df} \le p - 1$ gives `DomainError`; a scale matrix that is not positive definite returns the Cholesky factorization error.
Chi-squared variables are constructed from logarithms of Gamma variables, reducing the chance of diagonal entries rounding to 0 even for small noninteger degrees of freedom.
A sample whose diagonal still rounds to 0 gives a `NumericalInstability` error.
`sample(&mut rng)` returns a `Vec` of length $p^2$ containing a $p \times p$ matrix in row-major order; each sample costs $O(p^3)$.

Construction checks that $p^3$ units of work fit within the work limit of `LawOptions`.
The default limit is 65 536, restricting defaults to $p \le 40$; larger $p$ returns `ConvergenceFailure`.
For large $p$, raise the limit by passing `LawOptions::default().with_work_budget(Budget::try_from(p * p * p)?)` to `Wishart::with_options(df, &scale, options)`.
`InverseWishart::new(df, &scale)` is the law of $X$ with scale matrix $\Psi$ satisfying $X^{-1} \sim W_p(\mathrm{df}, \Psi^{-1})$; its constructor form and errors match `Wishart`.

The comparison law `MultivariateNormal::new(&mean, &covariance)` prepares a symmetric eigendecomposition of covariance in $O(p^3)$; each sample costs $O(p^2)$ for nonsingular covariance.
Its scatter matrix does not use Bartlett decomposition, providing a check independent of `Wishart` sampling.

## Check means and variances

The following cell takes site count $p$ (2–6), day count $n$ (7–60 days), anomaly variance $\sigma^2$ [K²], adjacent-site correlation $\rho$ (−0.9–0.9), replicate count $R$ (500–5000), and a seed.
Each replicate generates one `Wishart` sample and two scatter matrices from $n$ vectors from `MultivariateNormal`.
One is $\sum_t x_t x_t^\mathsf{T}$ about the origin, and the other is $\sum_t (x_t - \bar{x})(x_t - \bar{x})^\mathsf{T}$ about the sample mean.
The first table compares means of components $(1,1)$, $(1,2)$, and $(2,2)$ with $n\Sigma$ and $(n - 1)\Sigma$, computing mean SE as $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(W_{ij})/R}$ from variances calculated by hand.
The second compares component sample variances and SEs $\sqrt{(\mu_4 - s^4)/R}$ computed from fourth central moment $\mu_4$ with $n(\Sigma_{ij}^2 + \Sigma_{ii}\Sigma_{jj})$.

```rust
//| id: random-wishart-moments
//| ref: random-wishart-moments
//| title: "Compare means and variances of Wishart matrices and normal-vector scatter matrices"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   dimension: {"type":"range","label":"Site count p [sites]","min":2,"max":6,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   degrees: {"type":"range","label":"Days (degrees of freedom) n [days]","min":7,"max":60,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   variance: {"type":"range","label":"Anomaly variance σ² [K²]","min":0.5,"max":4,"step":0.5,"value":1}
//|   rho: {"type":"range","label":"Adjacent-site correlation ρ [-]","min":-0.9,"max":0.9,"step":0.1,"value":0.5}
//|   replicates: {"type":"range","label":"Replicates R [replicates]","min":500,"max":5000,"step":500,"value":2000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":11}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"negative","label":"ρ = −0.9","description":"Σ₁₂ = −0.9 K² gives E[W₁₂] = −9 K² and Var(W₁₂) = 18.1 K⁴.","inputs":{"rho":-0.9}}
//|   - {"id":"near-singular","label":"p = 6, n = 7","description":"Degrees of freedom are close to dimension, making W nearly singular, but mean nΣ and the variance formula still hold.","inputs":{"dimension":6,"degrees":7}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":10,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":9.6,"max":10.4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":4.6,"max":5.4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/0/7","range":{"min":8.6,"max":9.4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"spreads","path":"/rows/1/7","approx":{"value":12.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"spreads","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":10.5,"max":14.5}}
//|   - case: negative
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":-9,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"spreads","path":"/rows/1/7","approx":{"value":18.1,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: near-singular
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"means","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":7,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"spreads","path":"/rows/0/7","approx":{"value":14,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"spreads","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":11,"max":17}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_random::{MultivariateNormal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64, Wishart};

let (p, n, r) = (dimension as usize, degrees as usize, replicates as usize);
// Σ_ij = σ² ρ^|i−j| (AR(1)-type correlation). Positive definite for every p when |ρ| < 1.
let sigma: Vec<f64> = (0..p * p)
    .map(|k| variance * RealScalar::powi(rho, (k / p).abs_diff(k % p) as i32))
    .collect();
let scale = Matrix::from_vec(p, p, sigma.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let wishart = Wishart::new(n as f64, &scale).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let normal = MultivariateNormal::new(&vec![0.0; p], &scale).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let entries = [(0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1)];
// draws[s][e] holds values of entry e for matrix type s (Wishart, scatter about origin, scatter about sample mean).
let mut draws = vec![vec![Vec::with_capacity(r); entries.len()]; 3];
for _ in 0..r {
    let w = wishart.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut xs = Vec::with_capacity(n);
    for _ in 0..n {
        xs.push(normal.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    }
    let mean: Vec<f64> = (0..p)
        .map(|i| xs.iter().map(|x| x[i]).sum::<f64>() / n as f64)
        .collect();
    for (e, &(i, j)) in entries.iter().enumerate() {
        draws[0][e].push(w[i * p + j]);
        draws[1][e].push(xs.iter().map(|x| x[i] * x[j]).sum::<f64>());
        let centered = xs.iter().map(|x| (x[i] - mean[i]) * (x[j] - mean[j]));
        draws[2][e].push(centered.sum::<f64>());
    }
}
// Sample mean, sample variance, and variance SE from the fourth central moment.
let moments = |v: &[f64]| {
    let rf = v.len() as f64;
    let mu = v.iter().sum::<f64>() / rf;
    let var = v.iter().map(|x| (x - mu) * (x - mu)).sum::<f64>() / (rf - 1.0);
    let m4 = v.iter().map(|x| RealScalar::powi(x - mu, 4)).sum::<f64>() / rf;
    (mu, var, RealScalar::sqrt((m4 - var * var) / rf))
};
let (mut mean_rows, mut variance_rows) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for (e, &(i, j)) in entries.iter().enumerate() {
    let label = format!("W[{},{}]", i + 1, j + 1);
    // Independent check: E[W] = nΣ, Var(W_ij) = n(Σ_ij² + Σ_ii Σ_jj).
    let s_ij = sigma[i * p + j];
    let expected = n as f64 * s_ij;
    let spread = n as f64 * (s_ij * s_ij + sigma[i * p + i] * sigma[j * p + j]);
    let (mw, vw, sw) = moments(&draws[0][e]);
    let (ms, vs, ss) = moments(&draws[1][e]);
    let (mc, _, _) = moments(&draws[2][e]);
    let se = RealScalar::sqrt(spread / r as f64);
    mean_rows.push((
        label.clone(),
        mw,
        (mw - expected) / se,
        ms,
        (ms - expected) / se,
        expected,
        se,
        mc,
        (n - 1) as f64 * s_ij,
    ));
    variance_rows.push((
        label,
        vw,
        sw,
        (vw - spread) / sw,
        vs,
        ss,
        (vs - spread) / ss,
        spread,
    ));
}
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig3 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Component" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Wishart mean [K²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "(Wishart − nΣ)/SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Mean scatter Σxxᵀ [K²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "(scatter − nΣ)/SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "nΣ [K²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Mean SE [K²]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Mean Σ(x − x̄)(x − x̄)ᵀ [K²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "8", "label": "(n − 1)Σ [K²]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    mean_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "means" })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Component" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Wishart variance [K⁴]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Its SE [K⁴]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference / SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Variance of scatter Σxxᵀ [K⁴]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Its SE [K⁴]", "type": "number", "format": sig3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Difference / SE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "n(Σ_ij² + Σ_ii Σ_jj) [K⁴]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    variance_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "spreads" })
);
```

With the default inputs ($p = 3$, $n = 10$ days, $\sigma^2 = 1$ K², $\rho = 0.5$, $R = 2000$ replicates, seed 11), $n\Sigma_{11} = 10$ K² and $n\Sigma_{12} = 5$ K².
Means of `Wishart` are 10.14, 5.03, and 10.07 K², differing by $+1.41$, $+0.44$, and $+0.66$ SE for SEs 0.10, 0.079, and 0.10 K².
Scatter matrix means from `MultivariateNormal` are 9.94, 4.93, and 9.85 K², differing by $-0.56$, $-0.84$, and $-1.55$ SE.
Scatter matrices after subtracting the sample mean have means 8.95, 4.46, and 8.90 K², closer to $(n - 1)\Sigma = 9, 4.5, 9$ K² than to $n\Sigma$.
Component variances are 20.5, 13.2, and 20.5 K⁴ for `Wishart` and 20.3, 12.7, and 19.5 K⁴ for scatter matrices, all within 1.3 SE of theoretical values 20, 12.5, and 20 K⁴.

In the “ρ = −0.9” case, $E[W_{12}] = -9$ K² and $\operatorname{Var}(W_{12}) = 18.1$ K⁴; even negative correlation contributes positively to the variance formula through $\Sigma_{12}^2$.
For this seed, all three scatter matrix components have slightly small absolute values: $(1,1)$ differs by $-2.32$ SE and $(1,2)$ by $+2.22$ SE (closer to 0 than $-9$).
The means of `Wishart` for the same seed differ by $+1.41$, $-1.53$, and $+1.64$ SE, all opposite to the scatter-matrix deviations, showing no shared bias between the two generation methods.
The mean of the $(1,1)$ component of `Wishart` is 10.14 K², the same as the default.
In Bartlett decomposition, $W_{11} = L_{11}^2 A_{11}^2 = \sigma^2\chi^2_n$, and the same seed constructs it from the same random values regardless of $\rho$.
In the “p = 6, n = 7” case, degrees of freedom are close to dimension and $W$ is nearly singular, but differences for all six means and six variances remain within 1.7 SE; mean $7\Sigma$ and variance 14 K⁴ still hold.

## Summary

Normal-vector scatter matrices follow the Wishart distribution $W_p(n, \Sigma)$, and `Wishart::new(df, &scale)` returns samples through Bartlett decomposition with $O(p^3)$ computational cost.
Mean $n\Sigma$ and variance $n(\Sigma_{ij}^2 + \Sigma_{ii}\Sigma_{jj})$ can be checked by hand; scatter matrices from `MultivariateNormal` give the same values within SE.
Use $W/n$ for a known mean or divide by $n - 1$ when using the sample mean to obtain an unbiased estimator of $\Sigma$.
When constructing `Wishart` with $p$ above 40, raise the `LawOptions` work limit to at least $p^3$.
