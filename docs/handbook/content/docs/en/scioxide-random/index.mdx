`scioxide-random` creates inputs for probabilistic computation.
It provides five groups of tools: pseudorandom generators reproducible from seeds; probability laws with separate sampling, density, distribution-function, and quantile capabilities; low-discrepancy (quasi-random) sequences; sampling designs such as Latin hypercubes and Poisson-disk sampling; and path construction through Brownian bridges.
The generators support statistical simulation and do not provide cryptographic unpredictability.
Seeds are always explicit; the crate never reads process or OS entropy.

## First calculation: radioactive-decay waiting times

For a radionuclide with decay rate $\lambda$ [1/s], the waiting time $X$ between decays follows exponential distribution $\Pr(X > x) = e^{-\lambda x}$.
Both mean and standard deviation are $1/\lambda$ s, so the standard error of sample mean $\bar{X}$ from $n$ samples is $1/(\lambda\sqrt{n})$ s.

The next cell generates $n$ samples of `Exponential::new(λ)` from a seeded `Pcg64`.
Inputs are decay rate $\lambda$ [1/s], sample count $n$, and random seed.
It tabulates the first three waiting times, sample mean, closed-form mean $1/\lambda$, mean returned by `Mean::mean`, standard error, and standardized difference $z = (\bar{X} - 1/\lambda)/\mathrm{SE}$.
With generators recreated from the same seed, it calls the owned form `sample_n(rng, n)` returning a new `Vec` and `sample_n_into(rng, policy, output, workspace)` writing to caller-provided output, and reports the maximum difference between the two sequences.

```rust
//| id: random-first-exponential
//| ref: random-first-exponential
//| title: "Generate exponential waiting times from a seed and compare both forms with theory"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random]
//| inputs:
//|   decay_rate: {"type":"range","label":"Decay rate λ [1/s]","description":"Change waiting-time scale 1/λ.","min":0.5,"max":5,"step":0.25,"value":2}
//|   sample_count: {"type":"range","label":"Sample count n","description":"Change standard error 1/(λ√n).","min":100,"max":10000,"step":100,"value":1000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":24}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"fast-decay","label":"λ = 5 /s","description":"With the same seed, all waiting times shrink by 2/5 and z is unchanged.","inputs":{"decay_rate":5}}
//|   - {"id":"many-samples","label":"n = 10000","description":"Standard error decreases by √10 to 0.0050 s.","inputs":{"sample_count":10000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.6656,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"min":0.45,"max":0.55}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/4/1","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":0.0158,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/7/1","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/8/1","range":{"min":0,"max":0}}
//|   - case: fast-decay
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.2662,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/4/1","approx":{"value":0.2,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/7/1","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|   - case: many-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":0.005,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/7/1","range":{"min":-4,"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"waiting-times","path":"/rows/8/1","range":{"min":0,"max":0}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{
    Distribution, Exponential, Mean, OutputPolicy, Pcg64, SamplingPolicy, SamplingRequest,
    SeedableRng,
};

let n = sample_count.round() as usize;
let seed = seed.round() as u64;
let law = Exponential::new(decay_rate).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Owned form: return a Vec only on success. On failure, the generator advances by the words consumed.
let owned = law
    .sample_n(&mut Pcg64::from_seed(seed), n)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Caller-provided output form: roll back the generator on failure.
// Allocate workspace once from requirements.
let policy = SamplingPolicy::rollback(OutputPolicy::Atomic);
let mut workspace = policy
    .workspace(SamplingRequest::scalars(n))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut borrowed = vec![0.0_f64; n];
law.sample_n_into(
    &mut Pcg64::from_seed(seed),
    policy,
    &mut borrowed,
    &mut workspace,
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let max_difference = owned
    .iter()
    .zip(&borrowed)
    .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
let mean = owned.iter().sum::<f64>() / n as f64;
// Independent check: both the exponential mean and standard deviation are 1/λ in closed form.
let theory = 1.0 / decay_rate;
let standard_error = theory / RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64);
let api_mean = law.mean().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": sig4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![
        ("First waiting time X₁ [s]", owned[0]),
        ("Second waiting time X₂ [s]", owned[1]),
        ("Third waiting time X₃ [s]", owned[2]),
        ("Sample mean [s]", mean),
        ("Closed-form mean 1/λ [s]", theory),
        ("Mean::mean value [s]", api_mean),
        ("Standard error 1/(λ√n) [s]", standard_error),
        (
            "z = (sample mean − 1/λ) / standard error [-]",
            (mean - theory) / standard_error
        ),
        ("Maximum difference between sample_n and sample_n_into [s]", max_difference),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "waiting-times" })
);
```

## Reading results

With default inputs ($\lambda = 2$ /s, $n = 1000$, seed 24), the first three waiting times were $0.6656$, $0.3429$, and $0.5742$ s.
The sample mean is $0.5034$ s; its difference from the closed-form mean $0.5$ s is $0.22$ times the standard error $0.0158$ s.
For large $n$, the central limit theorem makes $z$ approximately standard normal, so the probability of $|z| > 2$ is about 4.6%, and of $|z| > 3$ about 0.27%.
`Mean::mean` returns $0.5$ s, matching the closed form, and the maximum difference between the two sequences is zero.

At $\lambda = 5$ /s, waiting times from the same seed are all scaled by $2/5$ (first value $0.2662$ s), while $z$ remains unchanged.
Exponential samples are generated by dividing standard-exponential samples by $\lambda$.
Setting $n = 10000$ reduces standard error by a factor of $\sqrt{10}$ to $0.0050$ s; sample mean $0.4944$ s has $z$ of $-1.12$.

Sample values depend on both the generator seed and the distribution's sampling algorithm.
The generator's word sequence is reproduced when its algorithm and the version of conventions converting words to uniform words or floating-point values are the same.
Distribution sampling algorithms and their rounding lie outside this guarantee; samples can change across releases even with the same seed ([Scope of reproducibility](oxref:random-algorithms-reproducibility)).
Interpret results through differences from theoretical values divided by standard errors rather than through sample values themselves.

Functions return failures as errors of `Result`, classified by `kind()`.
$\lambda \le 0$ gives `DomainError`; NaN or infinity gives `NonFinite`.
Quantities absent for every parameter value are expressed by lacking the capability; for example, `Cauchy` does not implement `Mean`.
Quantities absent for some parameters give `DomainError`; for example, `mean` for `StudentT` with degrees of freedom $\nu \le 1$ returns `DomainError` with reason `Undefined`.

## Summary

`scioxide-random` provides generators reproducible from explicit seeds, probability laws with capability-specific traits, quasi-random sequences, and sampling designs.
Generate samples through the three forms `sample`, `sample_n`, and `sample_n_into`; sequences from `sample_n` and `sample_n_into` initialized with the same seed agree.
Compare sample statistics with closed-form theoretical values and standard errors; an $|z|$ slightly above 2 is within seed-dependent fluctuation.
