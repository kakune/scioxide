Although $z^2 + 1$ has no real roots, its complex roots are $z = \pm i$.
Complex root finding extends both the approximations and the function's domain to the complex plane.
scioxide-rootfind does not automatically reinterpret real functions as complex functions.
For functions containing square roots or logarithms, choose a branch and check where it is differentiable.

## Holomorphicity and local convergence

A function is holomorphic near $z$ if it is complex differentiable in a neighborhood of that point.
Holomorphic functions admit Taylor expansions, allowing Newton errors to be analyzed by the same calculation as in the real case.
For a simple root $z_*$ ($f'(z_*) \ne 0$) of holomorphic $f$, Newton's method updates by:

$$
z_{k+1} = z_k - \frac{f(z_k)}{f'(z_k)}
$$

Writing $e_k = z_k - z_*$ gives $e_{k+1} = \bigl(f''(z_*) / (2 f'(z_*))\bigr) e_k^2 + O(e_k^3)$, so convergence is quadratic for sufficiently close initial guesses.
Halley's method uses

$$
z_{k+1} = z_k - \frac{2 f(z_k) f'(z_k)}{2 f'(z_k)^2 - f(z_k) f''(z_k)}
$$

and converges cubically near a simple root.
Like real `Halley`, `ComplexHalley` falls back to a Newton update when $\lvert f f'' / (2 f'^2) \rvert \ge 1$ ([Local cubic convergence of Halley's method](oxref:rootfind-halley-method)).
This argument does not apply at zero derivatives or to steps crossing nondifferentiable branch cuts.
Treat nonholomorphic equations containing $\overline z$ or $\lvert z \rvert$ as systems in two real variables, the real and imaginary parts ([Nonlinear systems and globalization](../nonlinear-systems/)).

## Interpolation-based updates

`ComplexSecant` approximates the slope from two complex samples.
Its convergence order near a simple root is $\varphi = (1 + \sqrt 5)/2 \approx 1.618$, as in the real case.
`ComplexMuller` uses the quadratic through three samples and selects whichever complex root is closer to the last point as the next point.
When computing the two quadratic roots, it selects the denominator with larger modulus to reduce cancellation.
The local convergence order is approximately 1.839; this does not guarantee convergence far from the initial guesses.

Real `Muller` stops with `ComplexStepRequired` when a complex update is needed.
In contrast, `ComplexMuller` accepts complex initial guesses with zero imaginary parts and can move from the real axis into the complex plane.
The function must also be supplied as `ComplexScalarFunction`; do not discard the imaginary part of a returned root merely because it is small.

## Function types and results

| Item | Information supplied or inspected |
|---|---|
| `ComplexScalarFunction` | Complex function values and available holomorphic first and second derivatives |
| `ComplexScalarFn` | A closure returning function values |
| `ComplexScalarFnWithDerivative` | A type with the first derivative added through `with_derivative` |
| `ComplexScalarFnWithDerivatives` | A type with the second derivative also added through `with_second_derivative` |
| `ComplexRootSolver` | `solve` and `solve_scoped`, which accepts an execution scope |
| `ComplexRootResult` | Complex approximate root `point()` and function value `residual()`, its modulus `residual_norm()`, termination reason `termination()`, and evaluation counts `counts()` |

`ComplexNewton`, `ComplexHalley`, `ComplexSecant`, and `ComplexMuller` initialize with one, one, two, and three points respectively.
`ComplexNewton` requires an analytic first derivative, and `ComplexHalley` requires first and second derivatives; absence produces a `InvalidInput` error.
Complex methods have no finite-difference derivative option.
As scalar methods, they accept no external workspace.

Residual magnitude is measured by $\lvert f(z) \rvert$ (complex modulus) and coordinate-step magnitude by $\lvert z_{k+1} - z_k \rvert$; tolerance meanings and default criteria match real open methods ([Default stopping criteria for methods in one variable](oxref:rootfind-scalar-default-stopping)).
Nonfinite components in function values or derivatives produce structured errors, while iteration limits and stagnation return unconverged results in `Ok`.
Use `termination().is_success()` to determine success; a small residual alone does not establish root-position accuracy.

## Basins of attraction of three roots

The set of initial points converging to the same root is called that root's basin of attraction.
The three roots of $z^3 - 1 = 0$ are $1$ and $(-1 \pm i\sqrt3)/2$, and its Newton map is:

$$
N(z) = z - \frac{z^3 - 1}{3 z^2} = \frac{2 z^3 + 1}{3 z^2}
$$

Because $N$ commutes with rotation by $z \mapsto e^{2\pi i/3} z$, the three basins are rotations of each other through 120°.
Their boundaries coincide with the Julia set of $N$; every neighborhood of every boundary point contains points from all three basins.
Near a boundary, a slight change in the initial guess can therefore change the root reached.
At the origin, $N$ is undefined, and points mapped to the origin also fail to converge to a root.

The following cell runs `ComplexNewton` from grid points in the square $[-2, 2]^2$.
Inputs are the subdivisions per side and iteration limit; the residual tolerance is explicitly set to absolute $10^{-12}$.
Successful points are classified by the nearest of the three known roots, and the maximum distance provides an independent check.
Outputs are a basin plot and counts in each class.

```rust
//| id: rootfind-complex-newton-basins
//| ref: rootfind-complex-newton-basins
//| title: "Basins of attraction of z³ − 1 and distances from known roots"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind]
//| inputs:
//|   grid: {"type":"range","label":"Subdivisions per side [-]","min":20,"max":80,"step":20,"value":40}
//|   iterations: {"type":"range","label":"Iteration limit [count]","min":5,"max":80,"step":5,"value":40}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"five-iterations","label":"Iteration limit 5","description":"Unconverged points increase to 1497; only 64 points converge to root 1 and 60 to each complex conjugate root.","inputs":{"iterations":5}}
//|   - {"id":"ten-iterations","label":"Iteration limit 10","description":"Unconverged points decrease to 238; many grid points meet the residual criterion in 6–10 iterations.","inputs":{"iterations":10}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":602}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":539}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":539}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"accuracy","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":3.3e-13,"atol":5e-15}}
//|   - case: five-iterations
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":64}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":60}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":60}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":1497}
//|   - case: ten-iterations
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":238}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, Complex, RealScalar, ResidualTolerance};
use scioxide_rootfind::{ComplexNewton, ComplexRootSolver, ComplexScalarFn, ScalarRootOptions};

let f = ComplexScalarFn::new(|z: Complex<f64>| Ok(z * z * z - Complex::new(1.0, 0.0)))
    .with_derivative(|z| Ok(Complex::new(3.0, 0.0) * z * z));
let options = ScalarRootOptions::default()
    .with_max_iterations(Budget::try_from(iterations as usize).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
    .with_residual_tolerance(ResidualTolerance::new(1e-12, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
// Independent check: three analytic roots on the unit circle.
let half_sqrt3 = RealScalar::sqrt(3.0_f64) / 2.0;
let roots = [
    Complex::new(1.0, 0.0),
    Complex::new(-0.5, half_sqrt3),
    Complex::new(-0.5, -half_sqrt3),
];
let mut counts = [0_usize; 4];
let mut data = Vec::new();
let mut maximum_error = 0.0_f64;
for j in 0..=grid as usize {
    for i in 0..=grid as usize {
        let x = -2.0 + 4.0 * i as f64 / grid;
        let y = -2.0 + 4.0 * j as f64 / grid;
        let result = ComplexNewton
            .solve(&f, Complex::new(x, y), &options)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        // Successful points use the nearest known root's index 0–2; unconverged points use 3.
        let category = if result.is_success() {
            let distances = roots.map(|root| (result.point() - root).norm());
            let nearest = (1..3).fold(0, |best, k| {
                if distances[k] < distances[best] {
                    k
                } else {
                    best
                }
            });
            maximum_error = maximum_error.max(distances[nearest]);
            nearest
        } else {
            3
        };
        counts[category] += 1;
        data.push([x, y, category as f64]);
    }
}
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "heatmap",
        "data": data,
        "xLabel": "Initial real part [-]",
        "yLabel": "Initial imaginary part [-]",
        "colorMin": 0,
        "colorMax": 3,
        "tooltip": true,
        "style": {
            "animation": false,
            "palette": {
                "light": ["#3987e5", "#d95926", "#199e70", "#6b7280"],
                "dark": ["#3987e5", "#d95926", "#199e70", "#9ca3af"]
            }
        }
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "basins", "title": "Colors correspond to classification numbers in the table" })
);
let rows: Vec<_> = ["Root 1", "Upper-half-plane root", "Lower-half-plane root", "Unconverged"]
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(k, label)| (k, *label, counts[k]))
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Class number [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Meaning" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Grid points [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "counts" })
);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let accuracy = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum position error of successful points [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    accuracy,
    vec![(maximum_error,)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "accuracy" })
);
```

Colors correspond to classes 0–3: 0 is root 1, 1 the upper-half-plane root, 2 the lower-half-plane root, and 3 (gray) unconverged.
With default inputs (40 subdivisions and 40 iterations), of 1681 points, 602 converge to root 1, 539 to each complex conjugate root, and one remains unconverged.
The unconverged point is the origin, where the zero derivative prevents an update and produces `Stagnation`.
Root 1 has more points because a square grid does not map to itself under a 120° rotation; this does not indicate different basin areas.
The maximum distance between successful points and their nearest roots is $3.3 \times 10^{-13}$, agreeing with the estimate obtained by dividing residual criterion $10^{-12}$ by slope $\lvert f'(z_*) \rvert = 3$.

Reducing the iteration limit to 5 increases unconverged points to 1497, with only 64 points converging to root 1 and 60 to each complex conjugate root.
Increasing the limit to 10 reduces unconverged points to 238, so many grid points require 6–10 iterations to reach residual $10^{-12}$.
For large $\lvert z \rvert$, $N(z) \approx 2z/3$, so the first few iterations merely multiply $\lvert z \rvert$ by approximately 2/3 and reach the limit before entering quadratic convergence.
This does not imply that those points diverge.
The plot observes a finite grid and does not mathematically determine the basin boundaries.

## Summary

For complex root finding, supply the function as `ComplexScalarFunction` and check holomorphicity and branches before use.
Local convergence orders match the real case: `ComplexNewton` is quadratic, `ComplexHalley` cubic, `ComplexSecant` approximately 1.618, and `ComplexMuller` approximately 1.839.
The initial guess determines which root is reached; near basin boundaries, slight changes in the initial guess change the result.
If a particular root is required, choose an initial guess nearby and check the result against known properties and the original equation's residual.
