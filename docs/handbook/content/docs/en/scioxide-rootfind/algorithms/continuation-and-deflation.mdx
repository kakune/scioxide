Solutions of parameterized nonlinear equation $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) = \mathbf{0}$ ($\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbf{F}: \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \to \mathbb{R}^n$) with parameter $\lambda$ trace a curve as $\lambda$ varies.
This curve is called a solution branch.
A single $\lambda$ may have multiple solutions, or solutions may disappear beyond some $\lambda$.
Continuation predicts the next solution from accepted ones and corrects it with Newton, tracing branches incrementally.
Deflation for additional solutions after finding one is covered in [Finding additional solutions with deflation](../deflation/).

## Solution branches and folds

Set $\mathbf{y} = (\mathbf{x}, \lambda) \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ and denote the Jacobian of $\mathbf{F}$ by $F_{\mathbf{y}} = [\,F_{\mathbf{x}}\ \ F_\lambda\,]$ ($n \times (n+1)$).
The implicit function theorem states that if $F_{\mathbf{x}}$ is nonsingular at solution $\mathbf{y}_0$, nearby solutions have a unique representation as a smooth function $\mathbf{x}(\lambda)$ of $\lambda$.
This representation breaks down where $F_{\mathbf{x}}$ becomes singular.

- At a fold (turning point), $F_{\mathbf{x}}$ is singular but $F_{\mathbf{y}}$ retains rank $n$, and the branch turns back in $\lambda$.
  Near a fold, fixing $\lambda$ gives two solutions on one side and none on the other.
- At a branch point, $F_{\mathbf{y}}$ also drops below rank $n$ and multiple branches intersect.

Where $F_{\mathbf{y}}$ has rank $n$, the solution set is a smooth curve in $\mathbf{y}$ space, with unit tangent $\mathbf{t}$ satisfying $F_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{0}$.
At folds, tangent parameter component $t_\lambda$ in $\lambda$ becomes zero and changes sign across the fold.

## Natural and pseudo-arclength continuation

### Natural continuation

Natural continuation moves $\lambda$ through target values, solving $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) = \mathbf{0}$ for $\mathbf{x}$ at each $\lambda$.
`ContinuationPath::Parameters(targets)` follows targets in order, including direction reversals.
Predictors use accepted-point tangents; moving $\lambda$ by $\Delta\lambda$ gives initial guess $\mathbf{x}_k + (\Delta\lambda / t_\lambda)\,\mathbf{t}_{\mathbf{x}}$.
Correctors solve with fixed $\lambda$, so accepted-point $\lambda$ matches the requested value exactly.

Approaching a fold makes $F_{\mathbf{x}}$ nearly singular; beyond it, no solution exists at the fixed $\lambda$.
Repeated corrector failure shrinking steps to the minimum stops with `StepLimit`.
If the tangent's $\lambda$ component at an accepted point reaches $|t_\lambda| \le \varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon$ is machine epsilon), no predictor can be formed, giving `TangentFailure`.
In either case, natural continuation cannot pass the fold.

### Pseudo-arclength continuation

Pseudo-arclength continuation (Keller 1977) includes $\lambda$ among unknowns, tracing curves in $\mathbf{y}$ space with one parameter $s$.
From accepted point $\mathbf{y}_k$ and unit tangent $\mathbf{t}_k$, construct predictor $\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{y}_k + \Delta s\,\mathbf{t}_k$; the corrector solves these $n + 1$ equations with Newton.

$$
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{y}) = \mathbf{0}, \qquad \mathbf{t}_k^{\mathsf T} M\,(\mathbf{y} - \hat{\mathbf{y}}) = 0
$$

$M = \operatorname{diag}(1/s_x^2, \dots, 1/s_x^2, 1/s_\lambda^2)$ is the arclength metric; `ContinuationOptions::with_scales(s_x, s_λ)` (defaults 1 and 1) aligns state and parameter units.
Because $\mathbf{t}_k$ is normalized by $M$, the second equation equals $\mathbf{t}_k^{\mathsf T}M(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{y}_k) = \Delta s$, making the corrected point's tangent projection $\Delta s$.
This is a first-order approximation to true arclength, hence “pseudo.”

The corrector's augmented Jacobian

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix} F_{\mathbf{y}} \\ \mathbf{t}_k^{\mathsf T}M \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{(n+1) \times (n+1)}
$$

is nonsingular if $F_{\mathbf{y}}$ has rank $n$ and $\mathbf{t}_k$ is sufficiently close to its nullspace (the nullspace of $F_{\mathbf{y}}$ is spanned by $\mathbf{t}$ and $\mathbf{t}^{\mathsf T}M\mathbf{t} \ne 0$).
This remains true at folds, so correction does not stop there.
At branch points, $F_{\mathbf{y}}$ loses rank and $A$ becomes singular, leaving the next branch undetermined.

Compute tangents by solving the similarly bordered system $\begin{bmatrix} F_{\mathbf{y}} \\ \mathbf{t}_k^{\mathsf T}M \end{bmatrix}\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{e}_{n+1}$ and normalizing with $M$.
The final right-hand-side component is 1, giving $\mathbf{t}_k^{\mathsf T}M\mathbf{t} > 0$ and inheriting orientation from the previous tangent.
Initially the border is $\mathbf{e}_{n+1}$ (increasing $\lambda$); `ContinuationState::with_initial_tangent` supplies another orientation.
A change in the tangent's $\lambda$-component sign from the preceding accepted point sets `ContinuationObservation::turning_point_candidate` true and identifies a candidate fold.

### Correctors and acceptance checks

The corrector solves the augmented system with `NewtonKrylovSolver` ([Matrix-free Newton–Krylov methods](../matrix-free-newton-krylov/)); change its settings through `with_corrector`.
The default corrector uses Newton–Krylov defaults except absolute and relative step tolerances are both $\varepsilon$.
Points from successful correctors are accepted only after every check below passes in order.

1. Reevaluate the original equation, requiring residual $\lVert \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{y}) \rVert_2$ to meet tolerance.
   Defaults are absolute 0 and relative $\sqrt\varepsilon$, referenced to the original residual norm at the predictor.
   `with_residual_tolerance` specifies tolerance and `with_residual_scale` a fixed reference; both also affect corrector settings.
2. Arclength constraint $|\mathbf{t}_k^{\mathsf T}M(\mathbf{y} - \hat{\mathbf{y}})|$ is at most the `with_constraint_tolerance` tolerance (default absolute/relative $\sqrt\varepsilon$, scale 1).
   Original residuals and constraints use separate tolerances because their units differ.
3. For correction direction $\mathbf{d}$ (predictor to corrected point), the Jacobian difference between old and new accepted points satisfies $\lVert (J_{\text{new}} - J_{\text{old}})\mathbf{d} \rVert \le 0.5\,\lVert J_{\text{old}}\mathbf{d} \rVert$ (`with_correction_contraction`).
   This checks against jumps to nearby branches with nearly parallel tangents, using two Jacobian-vector products.
4. Solve for the new tangent and verify its residual.
5. Tangents differ by at most 45° (minimum cosine $1/\sqrt2$ in `with_step_control`), and correction distance is at most 0.5 times predictor distance from the previous accepted point.
   Angles and distances use the `with_scales` metric.

Checks 3 and 5 are local and do not prove that the path remains on one branch.

### Step control

Reject proposals with failed correctors or any failed check, halve the step, and retry without going below minimum `minimum`.
Defaults permit 12 retries per accepted point (`with_rejection_limit`); exhaustion or reaching the minimum stops continuation.
At accepted points, set the next step to 1.5 times the accepted step if both $1 - \cos\theta$ ($\theta$ is the tangent angle) and the correction-distance ratio are within half their limits, Jacobian variation is within half its limit, and the corrector took at most four iterations.
Otherwise retain the accepted step size for the next step.
Steps always remain at most `maximum`.
`ContinuationOptions::new(initial, minimum, maximum, max_points)` requires $0 < \text{minimum} \le \text{initial} \le \text{maximum}$ and history capacity for at least two points including the seed.

Each trial uses $\mathbf{F}$ evaluations at the predictor and corrected point and one corrector solve.
Trials passing residual checks additionally use two Jacobian-vector products and one tangent solve.

## Termination status

Each `Continuation::advance` call accepts at most one point and returns the current status through `ContinuationReport::termination()`.
The first call corrects the seed at fixed $\lambda$ and computes its tangent.
`Continuation::follow` repeatedly calls `advance` with new workspace and a sequential context until status differs from `Advanced`, returning final `ContinuationState`.
Call `advance` yourself to observe intermediate points, stop early, or account work in the caller's context.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Advanced` | Accepted a point; part of the path remains |
| `Complete` (sole success) | Completed the requested path (target sequence or pseudo-arclength $s$) |
| `HistoryLimit` | Exhausted history capacity (`max_points`) |
| `StepLimit` | Exhausted retries or reached minimum step |
| `TangentFailure` | Failed to compute a tangent in the final trial; natural continuation reached a point with $\lvert t_\lambda\rvert \le \varepsilon$ |
| `WorkLimit`, `Cancelled`, `TimeLimit` | `SolveBudgets` or the execution context stopped work |

`StepLimit`, `HistoryLimit`, and `TangentFailure` are not errors; inspect them through `ContinuationState::termination()` and `is_success()`.
Seed-correction or initial-tangent failure returns a `ConvergenceFailure` error without changing state.
Accepted points and history survive later failures and errors.
If `follow` ends in error, it returns state containing prior accepted points as `PartialFailure` progress.

## Bratu solution branches

Approximate Bratu problem $u'' + \lambda e^{u} = 0$, $u(0) = u(1) = 0$ with second-order centered differences at $N$ interior points ($h = 1/(N+1)$):

$$
F_i(\mathbf{u}, \lambda) = \frac{u_{i-1} - 2u_i + u_{i+1}}{h^2} + \lambda e^{u_i} = 0, \qquad i = 1, \dots, N
$$

The cells multiply each equation by $h^2$ and use $u_{i-1} - 2u_i + u_{i+1} + h^2 \lambda e^{u_i} = 0$ to keep row magnitudes near 1 (see [Tangent linear systems and row scales](#tangent-linear-systems-and-row-scales)).
The continuous analytic solution parameterizes $u(1/2) = 2 \ln\cosh(\theta/4)$ and $\lambda = \theta^2 / \bigl(2\cosh^2(\theta/4)\bigr)$ by $\theta > 0$, with fold $\lambda_c = 3.513831$ ($\theta_c = 4.798715$, $u(1/2) = 1.186842$).
At $0 < \lambda < \lambda_c$, there are two solutions; the two $\lambda = 1$ values are $u(1/2) = 0.140539$ and $4.091467$.
The scioxide-ode chapter solves the same problem with [Collocation for two-point boundary problems](oxref:ode-bratu-two-solutions), obtaining both solutions at the same $\lambda = 1$.

The following definitions block is shared by two cells.
`Bratu(N)` maps augmented unknowns $\mathbf{y} = (u_1, \dots, u_N, \lambda)$; `residual_into` returns $h^2 F_i$, while `jvp_into` returns directional derivative $h^2 (F_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{v})_i = v_{i-1} - 2v_i + v_{i+1} + h^2 e^{u_i}(\lambda v_i + v_\lambda)$.
`trace` starts from exact solution $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}$, $\lambda = 0$, continuing through the complete path with `Continuation::follow`.
Minimum step is $10^{-6}$, state scale $\sqrt N$ (root-mean-square units), $\lambda$ scale 1, and original residual tolerance absolute $10^{-9}$; cells select initial/maximum steps and history capacity.

```rust
//| id: rootfind-bratu-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Bratu differences scaled by h² and continuation procedure"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind]
use scioxide_core::{
    Budget, SciOxideError, JacobianOperator, RealScalar, ResidualOperator, ResidualTolerance,
};
use scioxide_rootfind::{
    Continuation, ContinuationOptions, ContinuationPath, ContinuationProblem, ContinuationState,
};

/// Augmented unknowns y = (u_1, …, u_N, λ). Multiply each equation by h² and incorporate u_0 = u_{N+1} = 0.
pub(crate) struct Bratu(pub(crate) usize);

impl Bratu {
    /// Row i value: residual h² F_i for v = None, directional derivative h² (F_y v)_i for Some(v).
    fn row(&self, y: &[f64], v: Option<&[f64]>, i: usize) -> f64 {
        let (n, h2) = (self.0, 1.0 / ((self.0 + 1) * (self.0 + 1)) as f64);
        let w = v.unwrap_or(y);
        let left = if i > 0 { w[i - 1] } else { 0.0 };
        let right = if i + 1 < n { w[i + 1] } else { 0.0 };
        let growth = RealScalar::exp(y[i]);
        let source = match v {
            None => y[n] * growth,
            Some(v) => growth * (y[n] * v[i] + v[n]),
        };
        left - 2.0 * w[i] + right + h2 * source
    }
}

impl ResidualOperator<f64> for Bratu {
    fn input_dimension(&self) -> usize {
        self.0 + 1
    }
    fn output_dimension(&self) -> usize {
        self.0
    }
    fn residual_workspace_len(&self) -> Result<usize, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(0)
    }
    fn residual_into(&self, y: &[f64], out: &mut [f64], _: &mut [f64]) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        (0..self.0).for_each(|i| out[i] = self.row(y, None, i));
        Ok(())
    }
}

impl JacobianOperator<f64> for Bratu {
    fn jvp_workspace_len(&self) -> Result<usize, SciOxideError> {
        Ok(0)
    }
    fn jvp_into(
        &self,
        y: &[f64],
        v: &[f64],
        out: &mut [f64],
        _: &mut [f64],
    ) -> Result<(), SciOxideError> {
        (0..self.0).for_each(|i| out[i] = self.row(y, Some(v), i));
        Ok(())
    }
}

/// Continue the N = n problem from u = 0, λ = 0 to the end of route and return final state.
pub(crate) fn trace(
    n: usize,
    route: ContinuationPath<f64>,
    initial_step: f64,
    largest_step: f64,
    max_points: usize,
) -> Result<ContinuationState<f64>, String> {
    let map = Bratu(n);
    let problem = ContinuationProblem::new(&map).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let capacity = Budget::try_from(max_points).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let tolerance = ResidualTolerance::new(1e-9, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let options = ContinuationOptions::new(initial_step, 1e-6, largest_step, capacity)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .with_scales(RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64), 1.0)
        .with_residual_tolerance(tolerance);
    let continuation = Continuation::new(route, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // u = 0, λ = 0 is exact. Default orientation increases λ.
    let seed = continuation
        .state(&problem, &vec![0.0; n + 1])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    continuation
        .follow(&problem, seed)
        .map_err(|e| SciOxideError::from(e).to_string())
}
```

## Grid resolution and fold error

Difference truncation error is $O(h^2)$, so the discrete fold $\lambda_c(h)$ should approach $\lambda_c$ at rate $O(h^2)$ as $h \to 0$.
The maximum accepted $\lambda$ is sampled with step $\Delta s$, adding sampling error to discretization error.
The following cell continues for pseudo-arclength 8 at $N = 7, 15, 31$ ($h = 1/8, 1/16, 1/32$), estimating the fold from vertex $\lambda^*$ of parabola $\lambda(s)$ through the maximum-$\lambda$ point and its two neighbors.
Input is the maximum step (also the initial step), varying sampling-error magnitude.
Outputs are $\lambda^* - \lambda_c$ and error ratios against the preceding row.
Halving $h$ gives ratios approaching 4 for $O(h^2)$.
If all three continuations end with `Complete`, the outcome is satisfied; any early stop makes it partial, using finest-grid $N = 31$ termination as status.

// From accepted (pseudo-arclength s, λ), fit λ(s) through the maximum-λ point and adjacent points and find its vertex.
let fold = |points: &[[f64; 2]]| {
    let k = (1..points.len() - 1)
        .max_by(|&a, &b| points[a][1].total_cmp(&points[b][1]))
        .ok_or("Fewer than three accepted points")?;
    let ([s0, l0], [s1, l1], [s2, l2]) = (points[k - 1], points[k], points[k + 1]);
    // Vertex of divided-difference Newton form λ(s) = l0 + d1 (s − s0) + c (s − s0)(s − s1).
    let (d1, d2) = ((l1 - l0) / (s1 - s0), (l2 - l1) / (s2 - s1));
    let curvature = (d2 - d1) / (s2 - s0);
    let top = 0.5 * (s0 + s1) - d1 / (2.0 * curvature);
    let vertex = l0 + d1 * (top - s0) + curvature * (top - s0) * (top - s1);
    Ok::<_, String>((l1, vertex))
};
let exact = 3.513830719;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut previous: Option<f64> = None;
let mut completed = 0;
for n in [7, 15, 31] {
    // Advance for pseudo-arclength 8, using equal initial and maximum steps.
    let state = trace(n, ContinuationPath::PseudoArclength(8.0), step, step, 1000)?;
    let h = 1.0 / (n + 1) as f64;
    let status = state.termination().to_string();
    if !state.is_success() {
        rows.push((n, h, status, None, None, None, None));
        previous = None;
        continue;
    }
    completed += 1;
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = state
        .history()
        .iter()
        .map(|o| [o.arclength(), o.parameter()])
        .collect();
    let (largest, vertex) = fold(&points)?;
    let error = vertex - exact;
    let ratio = previous.map(|p| p / error);
    rows.push((
        n,
        h,
        status,
        Some(largest),
        Some(vertex),
        Some(error),
        ratio,
    ));
    previous = Some(error);
}
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "fractionDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "fractionDigits": 2 });
let fixed5 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "fractionDigits": 5 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "N [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "h [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Termination status" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum accepted λ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Parabolic vertex λ* [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "λ* − λc [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Error ratio against preceding row [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2, "nullText": "-" }
]);
let finest = rows.last().map(|r| (r.2.clone(), r.6));
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })

let mut outcome = if completed == 3 {
    Outcome::satisfied("All three grids completed pseudo-arclength 8 with Complete")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Some continuations stopped early; their grid vertices were not computed")
};
if let Some((status, _)) = &finest {
    outcome = outcome.status(status.clone());
}
outcome = outcome.diagnostic("Complete count", completed as f64, None);
if let Some((_, Some(ratio))) = finest {
    outcome = outcome.diagnostic("Finest-grid error ratio", ratio, None);
}
emit_outcome!(outcome);

The $\lambda^* = 3.512045$ for $N = 31$ also agrees closely with natural continuation's stop at $\lambda = 3.512044$ in the previous cell.
Manually applying Richardson extrapolation $\lambda^*(h) + \bigl(\lambda^*(h) - \lambda^*(2h)\bigr)/3$ to two rows gives 3.513833, reducing the discrepancy from $\lambda_c$ to approximately $2.5 \times 10^{-6}$.
Maximum step 0.1 shifts ratios to 4.06 and 4.18; at $N = 31$, maximum accepted $\lambda$ is $6.7 \times 10^{-4}$ below $\lambda^*$.
When sampling errors approach discretization errors, convergence order becomes unreadable.

## Tangent linear systems and row scales

The cells multiply equations by $h^2$ because of the tangent linear solver.
By default, `Continuation` solves bordered systems with unpreconditioned GMRES (restart 30, at most 200 iterations) to relative residual $100\varepsilon$ and independently verifies using the original action (change with `with_tangent_solver`).
With rows of magnitude $1/h^2$, rounding error alone prevents this check from passing; at $N = 31$ the seed tangent fails verification with error `ConvergenceFailure` (`initial tangent could not be verified`).
Even with $h^2$ scaling, at $N = 63$ unpreconditioned GMRES fails to reach this accuracy within 200 iterations and returns the same error.
For large problems, implement preconditioned or direct solvers as `NonlinearLinearSolver` and supply them through `ContinuationProblem::with_linear_solver` for the order-$n+1$ augmented system.
This solver handles both corrector Newton equations and tangent equations, using `with_tangent_solver` tolerances for tangents.

## Summary

Natural continuation solves at fixed $\lambda$ and reaches `StepLimit` or `TangentFailure` at folds; pseudo-arclength includes $\lambda$ as an unknown, passing folds through augmented-system nonsingularity.
Each accepted point independently checks original residuals and arclength constraints; rejected steps halve and favorable acceptances increase steps by 1.5, so `StepLimit` means progress failed even at the minimum step.
Use `with_scales` to align state and $\lambda$ units, scale rows to pass tangent verification, and supply preconditioned solvers through `with_linear_solver` for large problems.
Candidate folds are numerical observations; check them against analytic solutions or grid convergence (ratio-4 $O(h^2)$), as for Bratu.
