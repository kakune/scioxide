Even with one solution of nonlinear equation $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{0}$ ($\mathbf{F}: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$) known, Newton from the same starting point often returns to it.
A root's basin (the set of initial points converging to it) can be too broad to escape by small changes in the initial point.
Deflation multiplies $\mathbf{F}$ by factors growing near known roots, removing those roots from the equation.
Solving the resulting equation from the same initial point can converge to an undiscovered root.
Parameter-dependent tracking is covered in [Tracing solution branches with continuation](../continuation-and-deflation/).

## Deflated equations

For known roots $\mathbf{r}_1, \dots, \mathbf{r}_m$, `scioxide-rootfind` uses Farrell–Birkisson–Funke (2015) shifted deflation:

$$
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}) = M(\mathbf{x})\,\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}), \qquad
M(\mathbf{x}) = \prod_{j=1}^{m} \left( \frac{1}{\lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{r}_j \rVert_2^{\,p}} + \sigma \right)
$$

where $p$ is a positive integer exponent and $\sigma > 0$ a shift, with defaults $p = 2$ and $\sigma = 1$.
For multiple roots, multiply their individual factors.

Away from known roots, $M$ is positive and finite, giving these properties.

- Away from known roots, $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{0}$ are equivalent.
  Deflation neither creates new roots nor removes other roots.
- If known root $\mathbf{r}_j$ is simple ($J(\mathbf{r}_j)$ nonsingular), nearby $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) \approx J(\mathbf{r}_j)(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{r}_j)$, so $\lVert \mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}) \rVert \ge c\,\lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{r}_j \rVert^{1-p}$ ($c > 0$ is a constant determined by $\lVert J(\mathbf{r}_j)^{-1} \rVert$ and other root factors).
  For $p \ge 1$, $\lVert \mathbf{G} \rVert$ does not tend to zero as $\mathbf{x} \to \mathbf{r}_j$; for $p > 1$, it diverges.
  Thus $\mathbf{r}_j$ is no longer a root of $\mathbf{G}$.
  At repeated roots, $\mathbf{F}$ tends to zero faster, requiring larger $p$.
- Far from known roots, $M \to \sigma^m$ and $\mathbf{G}$ approaches a constant multiple of $\mathbf{F}$.
  Without a shift ($\sigma = 0$), directions where $\mathbf{F}$ grows slower than $\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert^p$ give $\mathbf{G} \to \mathbf{0}$ at infinity, producing apparent solutions whose iterates escape to infinity.
  `DeflationSet::new` accepts only positive finite $\sigma$.

### Relationship to Newton corrections

The Jacobian of $\mathbf{G}$ is $J_G = M\,\bigl(J + \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{g}^{\mathsf T}\bigr)$, $\mathbf{g} = \nabla \ln M$.
Each root contributes

$$
\nabla \ln\bigl(\lVert \boldsymbol{\delta} \rVert^{-p} + \sigma\bigr) = -\frac{p\,\lVert \boldsymbol{\delta} \rVert^{-p}}{\lVert \boldsymbol{\delta} \rVert^{-p} + \sigma}\,\frac{\boldsymbol{\delta}}{\lVert \boldsymbol{\delta} \rVert^2}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\delta} = \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{r}_j
$$

and $\mathbf{g}$ is their sum.
`DeflatedProblem` analytically computes $J_G\mathbf{v} = M\bigl(J\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{F}\,(\mathbf{g}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{v})\bigr)$ from the caller's JVP.
Writing the original Newton correction as $\mathbf{d}_F = -J^{-1}\mathbf{F}$, Sherman–Morrison gives the deflated equation's correction as

$$
\mathbf{d}_G = -J_G^{-1}\mathbf{G} = \frac{\mathbf{d}_F}{1 - \mathbf{g}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{d}_F}
$$

when $1 - \mathbf{g}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{d}_F \ne 0$.
With exact linear solves, deflation changes Newton correction length without changing its direction.
For a correction $\mathbf{d}_F \approx -\boldsymbol{\delta}$ pointing straight toward known root $\mathbf{r}_j$, $\mathbf{g}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{d}_F \approx p\,w$ ($w = \lVert \boldsymbol{\delta} \rVert^{-p} / (\lVert \boldsymbol{\delta} \rVert^{-p} + \sigma) \in (0, 1)$).
Approaching the root with $p\,w > 1$ reverses the correction, pushing iterations away from the known root.
When $p\,w$ approaches 1, corrections stretch greatly; for $p = 1$, $p\,w < 1$, so directions do not reverse.
$p$ and $\sigma$ alter this stretching, affecting iteration counts and which root is reached.

## Root sets and acceptance checks

`DeflationSet::new(n, capacity, options)` accepts dimension $n$, capacity for known roots, and `DeflationOptions`, preparing the set without residual callbacks.
A set belongs to one problem and parameter value; changing parameters requires rebuilding it and rechecking roots.
`DeflationOptions::with_shifted_power(p, σ)` sets exponent and shift; `with_separation_tolerance` sets distance tolerances for duplicate known roots (default absolute/relative $\sqrt\varepsilon$, with relative reference $\max(\lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert, 1, \lVert \mathbf{r}_j \rVert)$).

`DeflationSet::insert(problem, root)` evaluates the original residual before inserting roots.
Default residual tolerances are absolute 0 and relative $\sqrt\varepsilon$, but insertion has no initial residual as a relative reference.
To insert approximate roots, provide absolute tolerance with `DeflationOptions::with_residual_tolerance` or a reference with `with_residual_scale`.
Defaults accept only exact zeros.
Roots exceeding residual tolerance or duplicating known roots yield `InvalidInput`; insertions beyond capacity yield `ResourceLimit`; all leave the set unchanged.
`insert_in_place` performs the same operation with `DeflationWorkspace` prepared through `DeflationSet::workspace_requirements` and an execution context.

`DeflatedProblem::new(provider, &set, options)` borrows the original problem's `JacobianOperator` and root set, with `NewtonKrylovOptions` selecting how to solve the deflated equation.
Although `DeflatedProblem` is itself a `JacobianOperator`, use `solve` or `solve_into`.
Passing it directly to general solvers checks only deflated residuals.
`solve` proceeds as follows.

1. Evaluate the original residual at the initial point and fix the acceptance reference (that residual norm if `with_residual_scale` is absent).
2. Solve the deflated equation with `NewtonKrylovSolver`.
3. Independently verify the point lies outside known-root exclusion radii and its original residual meets tolerance.

Step 2 uses deflated residual tolerances in `NewtonKrylovOptions`, configured separately from original-equation tolerances `DeflationOptions::with_residual_tolerance` in step 3.
If the inner solve succeeds but step 3 fails, termination is `Unverified`.
If the inner solve is unsuccessful, its termination reason (such as `IterationLimit`) is retained.
If an iterate enters a known root's exclusion radius (distance `with_separation_tolerance`), the deflated residual returns `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), and `solve` ends with `Err`.
Line search handles trials returning `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) by shortening steps, but does not handle this error that way.

`DeflatedRootResult::termination()` is the authoritative termination reason including original-equation checks; `inner_termination()` and `inner_residual_report()` diagnose the deflated equation.
`residual_report()` and `residual_norm()` return original residuals; `point()` and `residual()` return the last evaluated point and its original residual.
`counts()` counts only verification evaluations; inner Newton–Krylov work belongs to the nested side of `accounting()`.
`DeflationWorkspaceRequirements` represents capacity needed for insertion or deflated solving, obtained from `DeflatedProblem::workspace_requirements(())` or `DeflationSet::workspace_requirements`.

Deflation does not establish that no roots remain undiscovered.
For all polynomial roots use [Computing all roots of real polynomials](../polynomial-root-pipeline/); for proofs of counts or existence use [scioxide-validated: interval arithmetic with guaranteed containment](../../../scioxide-validated/).

## A second solution of the Bratu problem

Discretize Bratu problem $u'' + e^u = 0$, $u(0) = u(1) = 0$ at $\lambda = 1$ using centered differences on $N$ interior points, solving $u_{i-1} - 2u_i + u_{i+1} + h^2 e^{u_i} = 0$ after multiplying each equation by $h^2$.
The continuous problem at $\lambda = 1$ has two solutions, with midpoint values $u(1/2) = 0.140539$ (lower) and $4.091467$ (upper); [Tracing solution branches with continuation](oxref:rootfind-bratu-branch) plots the complete branch.

The following cell obtains the lower solution from $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}$ with undeflated Newton–Krylov, inserts it into `DeflationSet`, then solves the deflated equation from the same $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}$.
If it finds a second solution, it inserts that too and searches for a third.
Inputs are grid-point count $N$, exponent $p$, and shift $\sigma$; original-equation tolerance is absolute $10^{-9}$ for both insertion and solving.
The table shows termination, inner termination, iterations, $u(1/2)$, differences between successful solutions and continuous analytic values (independent checks), and original residual norms.
The final outcome uses `termination()` of the solve deflating one known root.
Success gives satisfied, limits such as `IterationLimit` partial, and `Unverified` or `Err` failed.

```rust
//| id: rootfind-bratu-deflation
//| ref: rootfind-bratu-deflation
//| title: "Find a second Bratu solution at λ = 1 by deflation"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind]
//| inputs:
//|   interior_points: {"type":"range","label":"Interior grid points N [-]","description":"Difference spacing h = 1/(N+1). Odd counts place the midpoint on the grid.","min":7,"max":31,"step":8,"value":31}
//|   power: {"type":"range","label":"Deflation exponent p [-]","description":"Growth of the factor near known roots.","min":1,"max":3,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   shift: {"type":"select","label":"Deflation shift σ [-]","description":"Factor value far from known roots.","options":["0.01","0.1","0.3","1"],"value":"0.1"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"cubic-power","label":"p = 3 (strong factor growth)","description":"Deflating one known root reaches the upper solution after 22 iterations with ResidualTolerance.","inputs":{"power":3}}
//|   - {"id":"unit-shift","label":"σ = 1 (default shift)","description":"Fails to reach the upper solution, stopping after 100 iterations with IterationLimit and original residual norm 9.3 × 10⁻³.","inputs":{"shift":"1"}}
//|   - {"id":"linear-power-coarse","label":"p = 1, N = 7, σ = 0.3 (enters exclusion radius)","description":"An iterate enters the known-root exclusion radius, ending the deflated solve with an InvalidInput Err.","inputs":{"interior_points":7,"power":1,"shift":"0.3"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":5}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":1.4e-5,"atol":5e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":"ResidualTolerance"}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":45}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/4","approx":{"value":4.091140,"atol":5e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":-3.3e-4,"atol":5e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/6","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":"IterationLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/2/6","approx":{"value":2.8e-2,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: cubic-power
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"ResidualTolerance"}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":22}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/4","approx":{"value":4.091140,"atol":5e-7}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: unit-shift
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"IterationLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":100}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/6","approx":{"value":9.3e-3,"atol":5e-5}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: linear-power-coarse
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"ResidualTolerance"}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":"Err（InvalidInput）"}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":null}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"failed"}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, RealScalar, ResidualTolerance};
use scioxide_rootfind::{
    DeflatedProblem, DeflationOptions, DeflationSet, NewtonKrylovOptions, NewtonKrylovProblem,
    NewtonKrylovSolver, ResidualFn, RootTermination,
};
// N-variable differences at fixed λ = 1, multiplied by h²: u_{i−1} − 2u_i + u_{i+1} + h² e^{u_i} = 0.
let n = interior_points as usize;
let h2 = 1.0 / ((n + 1) * (n + 1)) as f64;
// Three-point differences w_{i−1} − 2w_i + w_{i+1}. Incorporate boundary values w_0 = w_{N+1} = 0.
let stencil = move |w: &[f64], i: usize| {
    let left = if i > 0 { w[i - 1] } else { 0.0 };
    let right = if i + 1 < n { w[i + 1] } else { 0.0 };
    left - 2.0 * w[i] + right
};
let map = ResidualFn::new(n, move |u: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
    (0..n).for_each(|i| out[i] = stencil(u, i) + h2 * RealScalar::exp(u[i]));
    Ok(())
})
// Directional derivative (J v)_i = v_{i−1} − 2v_i + v_{i+1} + h² e^{u_i} v_i.
.with_jvp(move |u: &[f64], v: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {
    (0..n).for_each(|i| out[i] = stencil(v, i) + h2 * RealScalar::exp(u[i]) * v[i]);
    Ok(())
});
let tolerance = ResidualTolerance::new(1e-9, 0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let shift: f64 = shift.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot read σ")?;
let options = NewtonKrylovOptions::default().with_residual_tolerance(tolerance);
let start = vec![0.0; n];
// 1. Without deflation: solve from u = 0 with Newton–Krylov.
let first = NewtonKrylovSolver::new(options.clone())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .solve(&NewtonKrylovProblem::with_jacobian(&map), &start)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
if !first.is_success() {
    return Err(format!("Undeflated solve unconverged: {}", first.termination()));
}
// Continuous analytic references for independent checking: lower and upper u(1/2) at λ = 1.
let continuous = |u: f64| if u < 1.0 { 0.140539214 } else { 4.091467246 };
let middle = first.point()[n / 2];
// Columns: approach, termination, inner termination, iterations, u(1/2), continuous-solution discrepancy, original residual norm.
let mut rows = vec![(
    "Without deflation".to_string(),
    first.termination().to_string(),
    None,
    Some(first.iterations()),
    Some(middle),
    Some(middle - continuous(middle)),
    first.residual_norm(),
)];
// 2. Check the found root against the original residual and insert it, then solve the deflated equation from the same starting point.
// If a second root is found, insert it too and search for a third.
let deflation = DeflationOptions::default()
    .with_shifted_power(power as u32, shift)
    .with_residual_tolerance(tolerance);
let capacity = Budget::try_from(2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut set = DeflationSet::new(n, capacity, deflation).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
set.insert(&map, first.point()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Second-root search result; the outcome uses this result.
let mut second: Option<Result<(RootTermination, usize, f64), String>> = None;
for known in 1..=2 {
    let label = format!("Deflate {known} known roots");
    let deflated =
        DeflatedProblem::new(&map, &set, options.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // An iterate entering a known-root exclusion radius returns Err; retain that result in the table.
    let result = match deflated.solve(&start) {
        Ok(result) => result,
        Err(e) => {
            let kind = format!("{:?}", e.kind());
            rows.push((
                label,
                format!("Err（{kind}）"),
                None,
                None,
                None,
                None,
                None,
            ));
            if known == 1 {
                second = Some(Err(kind));
            }
            break;
        }
    };
    let middle = result.point()[n / 2];
    let residual = result.residual_norm().ok_or("Original residual unevaluated")?;
    rows.push((
        label,
        result.termination().to_string(),
        Some(result.inner_termination().to_string()),
        Some(result.iterations()),
        Some(middle),
        // Do not compare unconverged candidates against analytic values.
        result.is_success().then(|| middle - continuous(middle)),
        Some(residual),
    ));
    if known == 1 {
        second = Some(Ok((result.termination(), result.iterations(), residual)));
    }
    if !result.is_success() || known == 2 {
        break;
    }
    set.insert(&map, result.point())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "standard", "fractionDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Approach" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Inner termination reason", "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Newton iterations [count]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "u(1/2) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Continuous-solution discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "-" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Original residual norm [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "-" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "roots" })
);

// Outcome: whether deflating one known root returned a new root checked in the original equation.
let outcome = match second.ok_or("Second root not searched")? {
    Ok((termination, iterations, residual)) => {
        let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
            Outcome::satisfied("Deflation found a second root and checked the original equation")
        } else if matches!(
            termination,
            RootTermination::Unverified
                | RootTermination::GlobalizationFailure
                | RootTermination::NumericalFailure(_)
                | RootTermination::LinearFailure(_)
        ) {
            Outcome::failed("Deflated solve returned no new root")
        } else {
            Outcome::partial("Deflated solve reached a limit; its last point is an unconverged candidate")
        };
        outcome
            .status(termination.to_string())
            .diagnostic("Newton iterations", iterations as f64, None)
            .diagnostic("Original residual norm", residual, None)
    }
    Err(kind) => {
        Outcome::failed("Iteration entered a known-root exclusion radius and ended in error").status(kind)
    }
};
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

At defaults $N = 31$, $p = 2$, and $\sigma = 0.1$, undeflated Newton–Krylov obtains lower solution $u(1/2) = 0.140553$ in five iterations.
Deflating one root reaches upper solution $u(1/2) = 4.091140$ from the same initial point in 45 iterations; both inner and outer termination are `ResidualTolerance`, with original residual norm $1.0 \times 10^{-11}$.
Continuous-solution discrepancies are $1.4 \times 10^{-5}$ for the lower branch and $-3.3 \times 10^{-4}$ for the upper.
Since original residuals are at most $10^{-9}$, these differences mainly reflect finite-difference discretization error.
The lower difference takes values $2.2 \times 10^{-4}$, $5.6 \times 10^{-5}$, and $1.4 \times 10^{-5}$ at $N = 7, 15, 31$, decreasing approximately fourfold per halving of $h$, consistent with second-order $O(h^2)$.
Deflating two known roots and searching for a third stops after 100 iterations with `IterationLimit`, returning an unconverged candidate with original residual norm $2.8 \times 10^{-2}$.
This agrees with the continuous problem's two solutions but does not prove absence of a third.

Deflated iteration is sensitive to exponent, shift, and dimension.
Keeping $N = 31$, setting $p = 3$ reaches the upper solution in 22 iterations; $\sigma = 0.01$ takes 15.
At $\sigma = 1$ (default shift), it stops after 100 iterations with `IterationLimit` and original residual norm $9.3 \times 10^{-3}$.
$N = 23$, $p = 2$, and $\sigma = 0.1$ also yield `IterationLimit`, with original residual norm $5.6 \times 10^{-2}$.
At $p = 1$, $N = 31$ and $\sigma = 0.1$ keep iterations near the known root ($u(1/2) = 0.140635$, original residual norm $3.2 \times 10^{-6}$), giving `IterationLimit`; $N = 7$ and $\sigma = 0.3$ enter the exclusion radius and yield `InvalidInput`'s `Err`.
This matches the earlier argument that at $p = 1$, $\lVert \mathbf{G} \rVert$ remains bounded near known roots and corrections toward them do not reverse.
Do not use these unconverged points as solutions.
For every configuration, assess results through `termination()` and original residuals.

## Summary

Deflation multiplies by factor $\prod_j(\lVert \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{r}_j \rVert^{-p} + \sigma)$ growing near known roots $\mathbf{r}_j$, removing only those roots.
With exact linear solves, deflated Newton corrections are scalar multiples of original corrections (same direction, changed length); $p$ and $\sigma$ affect reached roots and iterations through correction lengths.
When inserting approximate roots into `DeflationSet`, supply absolute tolerances or residual references; assess `DeflatedProblem::solve` results through `termination()` checked against the original equation.
For `IterationLimit` or `Err`, try changing $p$, $\sigma$, or the starting point, without confusing failure to find a root with its absence.
