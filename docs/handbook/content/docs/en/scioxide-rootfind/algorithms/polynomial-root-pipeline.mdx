By the fundamental theorem of algebra, a degree-$n$ polynomial with complex coefficients,

$$
p(z) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^k = a_n z^n + a_{n-1} z^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 z + a_0 \qquad (a_n \ne 0)
$$

has exactly $n$ roots $\{z_1^*, z_2^*, \dots, z_n^*\}$ in complex plane $\mathbb{C}$, counting multiplicities.
Vibration frequencies and damping, closed-loop transfer-function poles, and digital-filter stability require entire root sets rather than one root.
This page explains how `scioxide-rootfind` computes every root and how to interpret their reliability.

## Real coefficients and conjugate-root symmetry

For real coefficients ($a_k \in \mathbb{R}$), writing complex conjugation as $\bar{z}$ gives:

$$
p(\bar{z}) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k (\bar{z})^k = \sum_{k=0}^n \overline{a_k z^k} = \overline{p(z)}
$$

Thus $p(z^*) = 0$ implies $p(\overline{z^*}) = 0$, and nonreal roots occur in conjugate pairs $(z^*, \overline{z^*})$.

Floating-point root calculations slightly disturb conjugate symmetry through rounding.
For real inputs, `polynomial_roots_with_options` aligns conjugate pairs and moves sufficiently small-imaginary-part roots to the real axis before return.
The imaginary threshold is $8\varepsilon\kappa$, proportional to root condition number $\kappa$ below, clamped between $8\varepsilon(1 + |z|)$ and $\sqrt\varepsilon(1 + |z|)$ (using the lower limit if conditioning is unavailable).
Here $\varepsilon \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ is double-precision machine epsilon.
Real-axis projection and pair alignment occur only when moved points satisfy backward-error tolerance.
`PolynomialRootReport::canonicalization_changed` indicates whether this adjustment changed roots.

## Stable quadratic solutions

Direct double-precision evaluation of the quadratic formula for degree-$n = 2$ polynomial $a z^2 + b z + c = 0$,

$$
z_\pm = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
$$

can suffer cancellation.
If $b > 0$ and $|4ac| \ll b^2$, subtracting nearly equal values in $-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$ destroys many significant digits.

`scioxide-rootfind` uses a stable formula based on Vieta's relationship $z_1 z_2 = c/a$ between roots and coefficients.

$$
q = -\frac{1}{2} \left( b + \sigma \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac} \right), \qquad z_1 = \frac{q}{a}, \quad z_2 = \frac{c}{q}
$$

$\sigma$ is $+1$ when $b\ge0$, and $-1$ when $b<0$.
For real roots, adding same-sign values gives $q$, then the product relation gives the other root, avoiding subtraction cancellation.
Monomial inputs first extract zero roots when $c=0$, preventing division at $q=0$.
Discriminants also face overflow/underflow, separately from cancellation.
For real coefficients, scioxide-core's `checked_quadratic_roots` checks discriminant signs and errors; uncertain cases accumulate it exactly before taking an exponent-adjusted square root.
Each root $z$ has residual $|a z^2 + b z + c|$ approximately bounded by $32\varepsilon T$, with $x = |\operatorname{Re} z| + |\operatorname{Im} z|$ and $T = |a|x^2 + |b|x + |c|$.
This bounds residuals, hence backward error, without guaranteeing forward accuracy for ill-conditioned roots.
Complex coefficients form $q$ from the larger-magnitude choice in $-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$, avoiding subtraction for the same reason.

## Companion-matrix eigenvalues

For monic polynomial $p(z) = z^n + c_{n-1} z^{n-1} + \dots + c_1 z + c_0$, define Frobenius companion matrix $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ as follows.

$$
\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & \dots & 0 & -c_0 \\
1 & 0 & \dots & 0 & -c_1 \\
0 & 1 & \dots & 0 & -c_2 \\
\vdots & & \ddots & & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & \dots & 1 & -c_{n-1}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Cofactor expansion in the last column gives $\det(z \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{C}) = p(z)$; all-root computation is therefore equivalent to all eigenvalues of $\mathbf{C}$.

`PolynomialRootMethod::Companion` first scales monomial coefficients and variables by powers of two, equalizing magnitudes without losing coefficient digits.
It constructs a companion matrix (a colleague matrix from three-term recurrences for orthogonal bases) and computes all eigenvalues with scioxide-linalg's general eigensolver (balancing and shifted Hessenberg QR).
No initial guesses are needed; real matrices give real-root and conjugate-pair candidates for real coefficients.
Work is $O(n^3)$ and storage $O(n^2)$.
QR is backward stable for the balanced matrix, but matrix perturbations differ from coefficient perturbations, so polynomial-root backward errors need not be small (Edelman and Murakami).
`Companion` therefore polishes eigenvalue guesses against original coefficients with Aberth–Ehrlich rather than returning them directly.

## Aberth–Ehrlich simultaneous iteration

At high degrees, companion storage $O(n^2)$ and work $O(n^3)$ become expensive.
`Auto` selects Aberth–Ehrlich at declared degree 65 or above, without companion fallback.
Nonconvergence returns unsuccessful termination with final candidates.

Aberth–Ehrlich updates $n$ distinct complex candidates $z_1, \dots, z_n$ simultaneously.
Initial candidates lie on circles whose root-magnitude radii come from a Newton polygon of coefficients.
Updating candidate $z_i$ applies Newton to rational function $r_i(z) = p(z) / \prod_{j \ne i} (z - z_j)$, treating other candidates as roots to divide out.
Logarithmic derivative $r_i'/r_i = p'/p - \sum_{j \ne i} 1/(z - z_j)$ gives, using Newton correction $N_i = p(z_i) / p'(z_i)$:

$$
z_i \leftarrow z_i - \frac{N_i}{1 - N_i \displaystyle\sum_{j \ne i} \frac{1}{z_i - z_j}}
$$

The denominator sum repels other candidates, preventing accumulation at one root.
Each sweep updates every candidate simultaneously from previous-sweep values.
If every root is simple, local convergence is cubic; each sweep costs $O(n^2)$ work and $O(n)$ storage.
Near repeated roots, convergence drops to linear.
`DurandKerner` instead divides by $\prod_{j \ne i}(z_i - z_j)$ (Weierstrass corrections), giving quadratic local convergence for simple roots.
`Laguerre` generates candidates using Laguerre with deflation, then polishes the original polynomial with Aberth–Ehrlich.

Simultaneous iteration stops when every normalized backward error below is at most `backward_error_tolerance` and step $\lvert \Delta z_i \rvert$ meets `step_tolerance` thresholds with relative reference $1 + \lvert z_i \rvert$.
Both default absolute/relative tolerances are $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$; backward errors use reference 1, giving threshold $2\sqrt\varepsilon \approx 3.0 \times 10^{-8}$.
Each start permits `max_iterations` sweeps (default 200); nonconvergence retries other initial configurations, up to `restart_budget` starts including the first (default four).
If all fail, return candidates with the smallest observed maximum normalized backward error and status `IterationLimit`.
With `with_polish(true)` (default), finally polish each root with at most ten original-polynomial Newton steps, rejecting corrections that increase backward error.

## Normalized residuals and backward-error verification

Raw values $|p(z)|$ vary by orders of magnitude with root magnitude $|z|$ and coefficient scales, making them unsuitable directly for root decisions.
Monomial bases use normalized backward error under relative coefficient perturbations,

$$
\eta(z) = \frac{|p(z)|}{\sum_{k=0}^n |a_k| |z|^k}
$$

an Oettli–Prager-type formula.
$\eta(z)$ is the smallest relative perturbation magnitude $|\delta a_k| \le \eta\,|a_k|$ to each coefficient $a_k$ making $z$ an exact root.
If $\eta(z)$ is of order unit roundoff $u = \varepsilon/2 \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-16}$, $z$ is a root of a polynomial perturbed at rounding scale in its coefficients.
Orthogonal bases use coefficient-vector normwise backward error (`PolynomialBackwardErrorModel::Normwise`), with each coefficient perturbation proportional to $\max_k |a_k|$.
Backward error measures coefficient errors; estimate root forward error by multiplying by conditioning.

### Root conditioning and repeated roots

Perturbing coefficients at simple root $z^*$ by $|\delta a_k| \le \eta |a_k|$ moves the root to first order by:

$$
|\delta z| \le \kappa(z^*)\,\eta, \qquad \kappa(z) = \frac{\sum_k |a_k|\,|z|^k}{|p'(z)|}
$$

This follows by expanding $p(z^* + \delta z) + \delta p(z^* + \delta z) = 0$ to first order as $p'(z^*)\,\delta z \approx -\delta p(z^*)$ and using $|\delta p(z^*)| \le \eta \sum_k |a_k| |z^*|^k$.
$\kappa$ is the absolute condition number; `PolynomialRootDiagnostic::condition()` gives each root's value and `PolynomialRootReport::max_root_condition()` the maximum over returned roots.
Root errors are therefore approximately the condition number times backward error; rounding coefficients to double precision alone can move roots by order $\kappa u$.
At multiplicity $m$, $p'(z^*) = 0$ invalidates first-order estimates; $p(z) \approx p^{(m)}(z^*)(z - z^*)^m / m!$ gives root movement of order the perturbation's $m$th root.

$$
|\delta z| = O\left( \eta^{1/m} \right)
$$

For double roots ($m = 2$), coefficient perturbations $10^{-16}$ move positions by order $10^{-8}$.
This is ill-conditioning inherent to repeated roots, not a method defect.
`condition()` is a first-order estimate, not a multiplicity proof.

## Method comparison: Wilkinson's polynomial

$W_n(x) = \prod_{k=1}^{n} (x - k)$ is ill-conditioned: despite separated roots $1, 2, \dots, n$, tiny coefficient changes move roots considerably (sensitivity analysis in [Ill-conditioned Wilkinson roots](../../../scioxide-poly/examples/wilkinson-root-sensitivity/)).
Coefficients are integers, but at $n \ge 19$ some exceed exact double representation, moving roots away from original integers upon rounding.

## Input bases and actual algorithms

`PolynomialRootInput` borrows coefficients and evaluation bases.
Slice, array, and `Vec` coefficients are interpreted as ascending monomial powers from the constant term.
Passing `scioxide_poly::ApproxPolynomial` computes and checks roots directly in its basis, such as Chebyshev series or other three-term-recurrence orthogonal bases.
Complex coefficients are supported too, but conjugate symmetry applies only to real coefficients.
High-degree orthogonal-to-monomial expansion can inflate coefficients, so evaluate residuals in original bases.

`PolynomialRootMethod` is the requested method; `PolynomialRootAlgorithm` the algorithm actually generating candidates.
`PolynomialRootOptions` configures method (`with_method`, default `Auto`), backward/step tolerances, per-start iteration limits, start counts, polishing, execution policies, and workspace memory limits.
`with_rank_threshold` only reports effective degree `PolynomialRootReport::effective_degree()` excluding below-threshold coefficients; it never changes the requested root count.
`polynomial_roots` uses defaults, returning root sequences only when all pass checks.
Other termination becomes errors `ConvergenceFailure` or `NumericalInstability`.
Use `polynomial_roots_with_options` to change settings or inspect per-root diagnostics.
It returns `Ok` even without convergence; inspect `PolynomialRootReport::termination()` before using roots.
Method selection follows this order.

1. Monomial inputs first extract exact constant-side zero coefficients as zero roots.
   Remaining degree at most two uses analytic formulas regardless of requested method (`PolynomialRootAlgorithm::Analytic`).
2. `Auto` selects `Companion` if declared degree before extracting zeros is at most 64, or `Aberth` at 65 or above.
   Workspace allocation also uses declared degree; reducing degree by extracting zeros does not change selection.
3. `Companion` switches to `Aberth` when digit-preserving coefficient scaling fails, eigenvalue computation fails, or eigenvalue deflation produces zero eigenvalues; `fallback_used()` becomes true.
   Failed `Laguerre` candidate generation follows the same rule.

`DurandKerner` and `Laguerre` are monomial-only; other bases give `InvalidInput` errors.
The former uses simultaneous updates with denominator $\prod_{j \ne i}(z_i - z_j)$; the latter generates candidates and deflates using values and first/second derivatives, then polishes the candidates with Aberth using the original polynomial.
Nearby roots amplify denominator and deflation errors, so final decisions use the original polynomial.

## Per-root decisions and workspace reuse

`PolynomialRoots` from `polynomial_roots_with_options` owns roots, per-root `PolynomialRootDiagnostic`, and whole-set `PolynomialRootReport`.
Roots sort by ascending real part, then imaginary part on ties.
Diagnostics' backward errors and condition numbers use original bases and coefficient-perturbation models.
`is_validated()` indicates requested numerical backward accuracy, not an interval proof.
`termination()` in `PolynomialRootReport` alone decides whole-set success.
All roots passing backward checks gives `Complete`; unconverged simultaneous iteration gives `IterationLimit`; stopped iterations with failed root checks give `Unverified`.
Numerical breakdown or inability to finish within floating-point range gives `NumericalFailure(issue)`; limits/cancellation give `WorkLimit`, `Cancelled`, or `TimeLimit`.
`effective_method()` identifies actual candidate generation, and `fallback_used()` fallback use.
Proofs of root counts/locations with outward rounding use scioxide-validated's `PolynomialRootPlan` ([scioxide-validated: interval arithmetic with guaranteed containment](../../../scioxide-validated/)).
Never replace unavailable, singular, or indeterminate condition numbers with finite zero.
[Root matrices and root sensitivity](../../../scioxide-poly/algorithms/root-conditioning/) derives conditioning and coefficient perturbations in detail.

Allocate workspace for one degree and method.
`PolynomialRootWorkspaceRequirements` from `PolynomialRootWorkspace::workspace_requirements(degree, &options)` describes their requirements; `PolynomialRootWorkspace::workspace(degree, &options)` constructs workspace reserving candidates, diagnostics, eigenvalue storage, and other needs.
`polynomial_roots_into` accepts coefficients, settings, root output slice, workspace, and execution context, returning `PolynomialRootReport`.
Calls with allocated workspace do not allocate.
Inspect per-root diagnostics with `PolynomialRootWorkspace::diagnostics()`.
Even limit/cancellation stops leave last finite candidates in the output slice; use only roots with true `is_validated()`.
Changing degree or method makes `check` return `NeedsGrowth`; rereserve through `try_reserve`.

## Reusing output slices

Solve cubic $z^3-a=0$ for two $a$ values, reusing output and workspace.
Input is positive $a$; the second solve uses $2a$.
Pass coefficients $[-a, 0, 0, 1]$ from the constant term and choose `Aberth`.
The table gives roots, distances to the known roots $a^{1/3} e^{2\pi i k/3}$ ($k = 0, 1, 2$), per-root backward errors, and termination.
The lower table checks workspace against degree-three and degree-four requirements through `check`.

```rust
//| id: rootfind-polynomial-reuse
//| ref: rootfind-polynomial-reuse
//| title: "Vary cubic coefficients using the same workspace"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind]
//| inputs:
//|   constant: {"type":"range","label":"Constant a [-]","min":0.5,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"perfect-cube","label":"a = 8 (integer real root 2)","description":"First real root is 2, second (a = 16) 2.519842, written into the same output and workspace.","inputs":{"constant":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1.259921,"atol":5e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":1.587401,"atol":5e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/5/4","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/2/5","equals":"Complete"}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/5/5","equals":"Complete"}
//|       - {"artifact":"capacity","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":"Sufficient"}
//|       - {"artifact":"capacity","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":"NeedsGrowth"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: perfect-cube
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":2.519842,"atol":5e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"roots","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::{Complex, ExecutionContext, RealScalar};
use scioxide_rootfind::{
    PolynomialRootMethod, PolynomialRootOptions, PolynomialRootWorkspace, RootTermination,
    polynomial_roots_into,
};

let options = PolynomialRootOptions::default().with_method(PolynomialRootMethod::Aberth);
let cubic =
    PolynomialRootWorkspace::workspace_requirements(3, &options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace =
    PolynomialRootWorkspace::workspace(3, &options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut output = [Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); 3];
let (mut rows, mut last, mut largest) = (Vec::new(), RootTermination::Complete, 0.0_f64);
for a in [constant, 2.0 * constant] {
    let coefficients = [-a, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0];
    let report = polynomial_roots_into(
        &coefficients,
        &options,
        &mut output,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential_native(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if last.is_success() {
        last = report.termination();
    }
    // Independent check: real cube root a^{1/3} times cube roots of unity (closed form without root solving).
    let (radius, half) = (RealScalar::cbrt(a), RealScalar::sqrt(3.0_f64) / 2.0);
    let exact = [
        Complex::new(radius, 0.0),
        Complex::new(-radius / 2.0, radius * half),
        Complex::new(-radius / 2.0, -radius * half),
    ];
    for (&z, diagnostic) in output.iter().zip(workspace.diagnostics()) {
        let distance = exact
            .iter()
            .map(|&r| (z - r).norm())
            .fold(f64::INFINITY, f64::min);
        largest = largest.max(distance);
        rows.push((
            a,
            z.re,
            z.im,
            distance,
            diagnostic.backward_error(),
            report.termination().to_string(),
        ));
    }
}
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "a [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 1 } },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Root real part [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Root imaginary part [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Distance to known root [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Backward error η [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "Unevaluated" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Termination status" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "roots" }));

// Check degree-three workspace against requirements for degrees three and four.
let quartic =
    PolynomialRootWorkspace::workspace_requirements(4, &options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut capacity = Vec::new();
for (degree, requirements) in [(3_usize, &cubic), (4, &quartic)] {
    capacity.push((
        degree,
        requirements.required_bytes().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
        format!("{:?}", workspace.check(requirements).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Degree [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Required storage [byte]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Degree-three workspace check" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    capacity,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "capacity" })
);

let outcome = if last.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("All roots of both cubics passed backward-error checks")
} else if matches!(
    last,
    RootTermination::Unverified | RootTermination::NumericalFailure(_)
) {
    Outcome::failed("Some roots failed backward-error checks")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Simultaneous iteration stopped before meeting stopping criteria")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(last.to_string())
        .diagnostic("Maximum known-root distance", largest, None)
);
```

At $a = 2$, real roots 1.259921 ($= 2^{1/3}$) and 1.587401 ($= 4^{1/3}$) are written successively into the same output slice.
Known-root distances and per-root backward errors are both at most $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$, with `Complete` for both solves.
The second result overwrites the same output slice rather than appending to the first roots.
Required storage is 1040 byte for degree three and 1380 for degree four (wasm values with 32-bit `usize`).
Degree-three workspace gives `Sufficient` against degree-three requirements and `NeedsGrowth` against degree-four requirements; rereserve with `try_reserve` before degree-four solves.

## Summary

`PolynomialRootMethod::Auto` uses analytic monomial formulas through remaining degree two after zero extraction, eigenvalue-initialized Aberth–Ehrlich through declared degree 64, and Newton-polygon-initialized Aberth–Ehrlich above it.
Without `PolynomialRootReport::termination()` equal to `Complete`, roots are unverified candidates; even with `Complete`, errors are approximately the condition number times backward error, or perturbation $m$th roots at repeated roots.
For ill-conditioned polynomials, loose step tolerances permit large root displacements with `Complete`; inspect `backward_error()`, `max_root_condition()`, and independent checks together.
For matching degrees and methods, `polynomial_roots_into` reuses output and workspace without allocation.
