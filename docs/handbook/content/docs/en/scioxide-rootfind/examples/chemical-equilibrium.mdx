Chemical equilibria, including ligand–receptor binding, metal-complex formation, and acid–base equilibria, are typical nonlinear-system applications.

Consider reversible 1:1 association of components $A$ and $B$ in an ideal dilute solution:

$$
A + B \rightleftharpoons C
$$

Let equilibrium free concentrations be $a = [A]$ and $b = [B]$ and complex concentration $c = [C]$, all in mM.
Guldberg–Waage mass action relates concentration-based association constant $K$ [mM⁻¹] and concentrations as follows.

$$
K = \frac{c}{a b} \iff c = K a b
$$

With total introduced concentrations $A_T$ and $B_T$ [mM], material balances (conservation of each component) give the following.

$$
A_T = a + c = a + K a b, \qquad B_T = b + c = b + K a b
$$

Eliminating complex concentration $c$ leaves two nonlinear equations in unknowns $(a, b)$.

$$
\mathbf{F}(a, b) = \begin{bmatrix} F_1(a, b) \\ F_2(a, b) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a + K a b - A_T \\ b + K a b - B_T \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{0}
$$

## Physical constraints and logarithmic variables

Concentrations must be positive, with physically meaningful solutions in the following range.

$$
0 < a \le A_T, \qquad 0 < b \le B_T, \qquad 0 \le c \le \min(A_T, B_T)
$$

Newton directly in $(a, b)$ may make large early steps with $a < 0$ or $b < 0$.
Negative concentrations are physically meaningless and prevent residual evaluation for systems with chemical-potential terms such as $\ln a$.

Instead, use logarithms of dimensionless concentrations divided by reference concentration 1 mM.

$$
a = e^u, \qquad b = e^v \qquad (u, v \in \mathbb{R})
$$

Here $a$ and $b$ are numerical concentrations in mM.
For any $(u, v)$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$, $a > 0$ and $b > 0$ remain true.
The transformed system is as follows.

$$
\mathbf{G}(u, v) = \begin{bmatrix} G_1(u, v) \\ G_2(u, v) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} e^u + K e^{u+v} - A_T \\ e^v + K e^{u+v} - B_T \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{0}
$$

Residuals $\mathbf{G}$ retain the original material-balance units mM.

## Jacobian and uniqueness

The chain rule gives Jacobian $\mathbf{J}_G \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ entries for $\mathbf{G}(u, v)$ as follows.

$$
\frac{\partial G_1}{\partial u} = e^u + K e^{u+v} = a + c, \qquad \frac{\partial G_1}{\partial v} = K e^{u+v} = c
$$

$$
\frac{\partial G_2}{\partial u} = K e^{u+v} = c, \qquad \frac{\partial G_2}{\partial v} = e^v + K e^{u+v} = b + c
$$

The Jacobian is therefore symmetric.

$$
\mathbf{J}_G = \begin{bmatrix} a + c & c \\ c & b + c \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det \mathbf{J}_G = (a + c)(b + c) - c^2 = a b + a c + b c
$$

Since $a > 0$, $b > 0$, and $c > 0$, its determinant is positive, as is upper-left entry $a + c$.
Sylvester's criterion makes $\mathbf{J}_G$ positive definite everywhere in $(u, v)$.
No singular Jacobian points exist, so Newton equations $\mathbf{J}_G \mathbf{s} = -\mathbf{G}$ always have unique solutions.

Moreover, $\mathbf{G}$ is gradient of function

$$
\Phi(u, v) = e^u + e^v + K e^{u+v} - A_T u - B_T v
$$

The gradient is $\nabla \Phi$, and $\mathbf{J}_G$ is the Hessian of this function.
The everywhere positive-definite Hessian makes $\Phi$ strictly convex, giving at most one point with $\nabla \Phi = \mathbf{0}$, hence at most one equilibrium.
The closed form below supplies a positive solution, so exactly one equilibrium exists.
This does not imply rapid Newton convergence from every start (the results examine distant starts).

## Closed-form check

Subtracting balances gives $a - b = A_T - B_T = \Delta$.
Substituting into $B_T = b + K a b$ gives quadratic $K b^2 + (1 + K \Delta) b - B_T = 0$ in $b$, whose positive root is:

$$
b=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{2B_T}{\beta+d},&\beta\ge0,\\[4pt]
\displaystyle\frac{d-\beta}{2K},&\beta<0,
\end{cases}
\qquad \beta=1+K\Delta,\quad d=\sqrt{\beta^2+4KB_T}
$$

These expressions are mathematically equal, but choosing by sign of $\beta$ avoids subtractive cancellation of nearby values.
Compute $a$ from $A_T/(1 + Kb)$ rather than $b + \Delta$ to avoid subtraction for the same reason.
The cell uses this closed form as a check independent of Newton.

The following block provides definitions shared by two cells.
`mass_balance` gives material-balance residuals $\mathbf{F}(a, b)$ [mM], and `closed_form` closed-form equilibria $(a, b, c)$ [mM].
Construct the residual in logarithmic concentration variables $\mathbf{G}(u, v)$ by passing $a = e^u$ and $b = e^v$ to `mass_balance`.

```rust
//| id: rootfind-chemical-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Association material balances and closed forms"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

/// Material residual F(a, b) = (a + Kab − Aₜ, b + Kab − Bₜ) [mM].
pub(crate) fn mass_balance(k: f64, total_a: f64, total_b: f64, a: f64, b: f64) -> [f64; 2] {
    let c = k * a * b;
    [a + c - total_a, b + c - total_b]
}

/// Closed-form equilibrium concentrations [a, b, c] [mM].
/// Compute the positive root of K b² + (1 + KΔ) b − Bₜ = 0 after substituting a − b = Δ, without cancellation.
pub(crate) fn closed_form(k: f64, total_a: f64, total_b: f64) -> [f64; 3] {
    let beta = 1.0 + k * (total_a - total_b);
    let d = RealScalar::sqrt(beta * beta + 4.0 * k * total_b);
    let b = if beta >= 0.0 {
        2.0 * total_b / (beta + d)
    } else {
        (d - beta) / (2.0 * k)
    };
    let a = total_a / (1.0 + k * b);
    [a, b, k * a * b]
}
```

## Computing equilibrium concentrations

The following cell varies association constant $K$, total concentrations $A_T$/$B_T$, Newton starts, and line searches, finding $(u, v)$ with analytic Jacobians in `NewtonSystem`.
Starting values use free-concentration fraction $f_0$ of totals, giving $(u_0, v_0) = (\ln f_0 A_T, \ln f_0 B_T)$.

Default residual criteria of `NewtonSystem` require relative reduction (factor $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$) from the initial residual 2-norm.
Here specify `ResidualTolerance::new(1e-12, 0.0)` to accept when residual 2-norm is at most absolute threshold $10^{-12}$ mM.
Steps $\Delta\mathbf{x}$ satisfying $\lVert \Delta\mathbf{x} \rVert_2 \le \tau_a + \tau_r \max(1, \lVert \mathbf{x} \rVert_2)$ without residual reduction stop with stagnation `Stagnation`.
Reduce $\tau_a$ and $\tau_r$ of `StepTolerance` from default $\sqrt{\varepsilon} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-8}$ to $10^{-15}$, continuing through rounding-sized updates while residuals decrease.
Default `NewtonSystem` applies full Newton updates without line searches.
Setting line search to `BacktrackingLineSearch` makes `with_line_search(BacktrackingLineSearch::default())` shrink steps until merit $\lVert \mathbf{G} \rVert_2^2 / 2$ decreases sufficiently under Armijo.

The first table gives termination, iterations, residual evaluation counts, and final residuals.
Only successful termination produces a second table of equilibria, closed-form discrepancies, and material-balance errors.
The final outcome uses termination success; unsuccessful runs show no concentrations.
This cell also passes $K$, $A_T$, and $B_T$ to the residual-landscape cell.

    SystemRootSolver,
};

let (k, at, bt) = (association_constant, total_a_mm, total_b_mm);
// Unknowns u = ln a, v = ln b. G(u, v) = F(e^u, e^v) [mM].
let equilibrium = ResidualFn::new(2, move |x: &[f64], out: &mut [f64]| {

    j.copy_from_slice(&[a + c, c, c, b + c]);

        RealScalar::exp(result.point()[0]),
        RealScalar::exp(result.point()[1]),
    );

At defaults ($K = 4$ mM⁻¹, $A_T = 1.2$ mM, $B_T = 0.9$ mM, $f_0 = 0.6$), five iterations (six residual evaluations) reach `ResidualTolerance`, with $a = 0.57329$, $b = 0.27329$, and $c = 0.62671$ mM.
Final residual is $2.1 \times 10^{-14}$ mM, closed-form discrepancies at most $1.2 \times 10^{-14}$ mM, and balance errors $1.2 \times 10^{-14}$ mM and $1.7 \times 10^{-14}$ mM.
All remain within approximately 100 times concentration magnitude (about 1 mM) times double-precision unit roundoff $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$.
At $K = 30$ mM⁻¹, limiting B's free concentration falls to $b = 0.0737$ mM and complexes approach maximum $\min(A_T, B_T) = 0.9$ mM at $c = 0.8263$ mM (seven iterations).

Initial fraction $f_0 = 0.05$ takes 28 iterations to reach the same solution.
Small initial $a$/$b$ and Jacobian entries make the first step overshoot, giving $(u, v) \approx (11.2, 9.3)$ and residual approximately $5 \times 10^9$ mM.
After that, term $K e^{u+v}$ dominates residuals.
As Newton steps for $e^w$-dominated equations are $-1$, $w = u + v$ decreases by nearly one each iteration, taking over 20 iterations to return near the solution.
Then quadratic convergence reaches acceptance in a few iterations.
Line searches and trust regions (`TrustRegionSystem`) in [Nonlinear systems and globalization](../../algorithms/nonlinear-systems/) control such overshooting.
With line search `BacktrackingLineSearch`, even $f_0 = 0.05$ reaches the same solution in eight iterations (12 residual evaluations).
Evaluations exceed iterations because rejected step trials also evaluate residuals.
At default $f_0 = 0.6$, full Newton steps are accepted from the start, so line-search selection leaves iterations unchanged at five.

## Summary

Association equilibrium gives mass-action/material-balance systems; logarithmic free-concentration unknowns preserve positivity throughout iteration.
Logarithmic Jacobians are symmetric positive definite and residuals gradients of strictly convex functions, yielding exactly one equilibrium and always-solvable Newton linear systems.
Analytic Jacobians in `NewtonSystem` converge from nearby starts within a few iterations; distant starts can greatly overshoot without line search and need more iterations.
For absolute mM residual acceptance supply `ResidualTolerance::new(atol, 0.0)` and use concentrations only after checking successful termination.
Check results with relationships independent of Newton, such as closed forms and material balances.
