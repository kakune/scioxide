Characteristic roots from vibration equations give each mode's decay rate and damped natural angular frequency, determining stability.
This page computes every root of a two-mode quartic with `polynomial_roots_with_options`, comparing against modal quadratic roots.

## Characteristic polynomial of a two-degree-of-freedom system

For two independent modes with damping ratios $\zeta_j$ [-] and undamped natural angular frequencies $\omega_j$ [rad/s],

$$
\ddot{q}_j + 2 \zeta_j \omega_j \dot{q}_j + \omega_j^2 q_j = 0 \qquad (j = 1, 2)
$$

substitution of $q_j(t) = e^{st}$ gives modal quadratic factors:

$$
p_j(s) = s^2 + 2 \zeta_j \omega_j s + \omega_j^2
$$

The full characteristic polynomial is their monic quartic product $p(s) = p_1(s) p_2(s) = s^4 + a_3 s^3 + a_2 s^2 + a_1 s + a_0$, with coefficients:

$$
a_0 = \omega_1^2 \omega_2^2, \qquad a_1 = 2 (\zeta_1 \omega_1 \omega_2^2 + \zeta_2 \omega_2 \omega_1^2)
$$

$$
a_2 = \omega_1^2 + \omega_2^2 + 4 \zeta_1 \zeta_2 \omega_1 \omega_2, \qquad a_3 = 2 (\zeta_1 \omega_1 + \zeta_2 \omega_2)
$$

Real structures often have coupled modes whose characteristic polynomials do not factor this way.
We choose separated modal coordinates to use quadratic roots as independent checks.

## Interpreting characteristic roots

A mode with root $s = \sigma + i \omega_d$ behaves as $e^{\sigma t} \cos(\omega_d t)$.

- Real part $\sigma$ [1/s]: amplitude decay rate.
  If every real part is negative, the system is asymptotically stable.
- Imaginary part $\omega_d$ [rad/s]: damped natural angular frequency.
  For $\zeta < 1$ (underdamping), roots are $-\zeta\omega \pm i \omega \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$, and the mode decays while oscillating at angular frequency $\omega_d = \omega \sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$.
  For $\zeta \ge 1$ (critical/overdamping), roots are real $-\zeta\omega \pm \omega\sqrt{\zeta^2 - 1}$, and the mode decays without oscillation.

## Verification methods

Check root set $\{s_1, \dots, s_4\}$ in three ways.

1. Quadratic-root distances: compare each mode's $p_j(s) = 0$ roots from the quadratic formula.
2. Vieta relationships: $\sum_k s_k = -a_3$ and $\prod_k s_k = a_0$.
   These detect missing/duplicate roots that individual residuals cannot reveal.
3. Conjugate symmetry: nonreal roots of real polynomials occur in conjugate pairs, so $\max_k \min_j \lvert s_k - \overline{s_j} \rvert$ should be zero.

Also tabulate normalized backward errors per root:

$$
\eta(s) = \frac{\lvert p(s) \rvert}{\sum_m \lvert a_m \rvert \lvert s \rvert^m}
$$

This is the componentwise relative coefficient change needed to make $s$ an exact root, returned by `PolynomialRootDiagnostic::backward_error`.
Even rounding-level backward errors can have forward errors amplified by conditioning ([Root conditioning and repeated roots](oxref:rootfind-polynomial-root-condition)).

The following block provides definitions shared by three cells.
`characteristic` gives coefficients $[a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3, 1]$, `factor_roots` quadratic-factor roots, and `magnitude` corresponds to $\lvert p(s) \rvert$.
`factor_roots` and `magnitude` provide checks through expressions independent of all-root computation.

```rust
//| id: rootfind-vibration-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Two-mode characteristic polynomial and quadratic roots"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::{Complex, RealScalar};

/// Coefficients [a₀, a₁, a₂, a₃, 1] of p(s) = (s² + 2ζ₁ω₁s + ω₁²)(s² + 2ζ₂ω₂s + ω₂²), ascending from constant term.
pub(crate) fn characteristic(w1: f64, w2: f64, z1: f64, z2: f64) -> [f64; 5] {
    [
        w1 * w1 * w2 * w2,
        2.0 * (z1 * w1 * w2 * w2 + z2 * w2 * w1 * w1),
        w1 * w1 + w2 * w2 + 4.0 * z1 * z2 * w1 * w2,
        2.0 * (z1 * w1 + z2 * w2),
        1.0,
    ]
}

/// Modal roots of s² + 2ζωs + ω²: −ζω ± ω√(ζ² − 1) from the quadratic formula.
pub(crate) fn factor_roots(w: f64, z: f64) -> [Complex<f64>; 2] {
    let d = z * z - 1.0;
    if d < 0.0 {
        let im = w * RealScalar::sqrt(-d);
        [Complex::new(-z * w, im), Complex::new(-z * w, -im)]
    } else {
        let re = w * RealScalar::sqrt(d);
        [Complex::new(-z * w + re, 0.0), Complex::new(-z * w - re, 0.0)]
    }
}

/// Evaluate |p(s)| with complex Horner.
pub(crate) fn magnitude(c: &[f64; 5], s: Complex<f64>) -> f64 {
    c.iter()
        .rev()
        .fold(Complex::new(0.0, 0.0), |v, &a| v * s + Complex::new(a, 0.0))
        .norm()
}
```

## Computing four characteristic roots

The following cell fixes $\omega_1 = 1$ rad/s, varies $\omega_2$ [rad/s] and damping ratios $\zeta_1$/$\zeta_2$ [-], and computes all roots with `polynomial_roots_with_options` (default `PolynomialRootOptions`, method `PolynomialRootMethod::Auto`).
Pass ascending coefficients $[a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3, 1]$ from the constant term.
Checks here use only root-set relationships available without knowing answers.
Per-root tables show $\lvert p(s) \rvert$ and `PolynomialRootDiagnostic::backward_error` backward errors.
The summary table gives Vieta and conjugacy errors, `PolynomialRootReport::max_root_condition` conditioning, and maximum real parts.
The outcome gives `PolynomialRootReport` termination; `Complete` means every root passed backward-error checks against original coefficients.
Roots and inputs pass to comparisons and charts in the next cell.

## Quadratic-root comparisons and the characteristic polynomial on the imaginary axis

Complex-plane roots show modal decay (real parts) and oscillation (imaginary parts) together.
The next cell receives previous roots and inputs and compares quadratic-formula roots of modal factors $p_j(s) = 0$.
The table gives distances to nearest quadratic roots; the upper chart plots both root sets, the lower $\lvert p(i\Omega) \rvert$ on the imaginary axis using a logarithmic vertical axis.
Since $\lvert p_j(i\Omega) \rvert = \sqrt{(\omega_j^2 - \Omega^2)^2 + (2 \zeta_j \omega_j \Omega)^2}$, weak damping gives deep $\lvert p(i\Omega) \rvert$ valleys at $\Omega \approx \omega_j$ with depths proportional to $\zeta_j$.
$1/\lvert p(i\Omega) \rvert$ is the frequency-response amplitude of two modes connected in series.
Keep complex axes fixed to $-7 \le \operatorname{Re} s \le 0.5$ and $-4 \le \operatorname{Im} s \le 4$ and angular frequencies to $0 < \Omega \le 5$ rad/s to avoid input-dependent chart jumps.
Change inputs in the preceding cell.

This is of order double-precision spacing $4.4 \times 10^{-16}$ at $\lvert s \rvert \approx 2$, within prior estimate $\kappa\eta \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-16}$ plus reference-formula rounding.
The upper chart overlays both root sets, symmetric in pairs about the real axis.

At $\Omega = \omega_j$, $\lvert p(i\Omega) \rvert$ equals $2 \zeta_j \omega_j^2 \lvert p_k(i\omega_j) \rvert$ ($k \ne j$), becoming smaller as the damping ratio decreases.

## Repeated roots near critical damping

Modal factor $s^2 + 2\zeta \omega s + \omega^2$ has two coincident roots at $s = -\omega$ when $\zeta = 1$, producing a repeated root.
At simple root $s_k$, changing the constant term to $a_0 \to a_0(1 + \delta)$ and expanding $p(s) + \delta a_0 = 0$ to first order gives movement

$$
\lvert \Delta s \rvert \approx \frac{\delta \lvert a_0 \rvert}{\lvert p'(s_k) \rvert}
$$

proportional to $\delta$.
At repeated roots, $p'(-\omega) = 0$ invalidates that formula; $p(s) \approx \tfrac12 p''(-\omega)(s + \omega)^2$ gives

$$
\lvert \Delta s \rvert \approx \sqrt{\frac{2 \delta \lvert a_0 \rvert}{\lvert p''(-\omega) \rvert}}
$$

so roots move proportionally to $\sqrt{\delta}$.
Even relative coefficient rounding $\delta \approx 10^{-16}$ determines repeated-root positions only to order $10^{-8}$.
This is inherent conditioning, not a method defect.

The next cell fixes $\omega_1 = 1$, $\omega_2 = 2$ rad/s, and $\zeta_2 = 0.18$, perturbs constant $a_0$ relatively from $\delta = 10^{-2}$ to $10^{-12}$, and shows first-mode movement in log–log plots and tables.
Input is first-mode damping $\zeta_1$, giving a repeated root when this ratio equals 1.
Movement $\lvert \Delta s \rvert$ is the farther distance of the two computed roots nearest the original first-mode roots.
Marker lines show computed movements; dashed lines first-order predictions.
For $\zeta_1 = 1$ use repeated-root formula $\sqrt{2\delta a_0/\lvert p''(-1) \rvert}$; otherwise simple-root $\delta a_0/\lvert p'(s_1) \rvert$, taking the larger estimate over the two roots.
Predictions use original quadratic-formula roots, independently checking all-root computations.
The outcome shows whether all 11 all-root solves were `Complete`.

At default $\zeta_1 = 1$ (repeated), $\lvert \Delta s \rvert / \sqrt{\delta}$ is constant at 0.967 for every $\delta$, with movement proportional to $\sqrt{\delta}$.
Repeated-root formulas give $p''(-1) = 2 p_2(-1) = 2 \times 4.28$ and $a_0 = 4$, yielding ratio $\sqrt{4/4.28} = 0.967$ matching computation.
Even at $\delta = 10^{-12}$, roots move by $9.7 \times 10^{-7}$.
The maximum condition number in `PolynomialRootReport` also grows proportionally to $1/\sqrt{\delta}$, from $2.8 \times 10^{1}$ to $2.8 \times 10^{6}$.
This first-order conditioning is at two nearby roots separated by perturbations and does not determine repeated-root multiplicity.

At $\zeta_1 = 0.5$ (simple), $\lvert \Delta s \rvert / \delta$ stays 0.733 with movement proportional to $\delta$ and a condition number near 5.3.
Simple-root formulas also give ratio $4/5.45 = 0.733$ from $\lvert p'(s_1) \rvert = \lvert p_1'(s_1) \rvert \lvert p_2(s_1) \rvert = 1.732 \times 3.149$.
At $\delta = 10^{-12}$ the ratio becomes 0.7335, exposing rounding in the fourth digit.
$\zeta_1 = 1.5$ likewise scales with $\delta$ (ratio 0.462, condition number 10).
Near repeated cases such as $\zeta_1 = 0.99$ give movement 0.030 at $\delta = 10^{-2}$, below first-order prediction 0.033.
Large perturbations relative to root spacing $2\sqrt{1 - \zeta_1^2} \approx 0.28$ invalidate first-order predictions; at $\delta \le 10^{-4}$ markers follow dashed predictions (ratio 3.33).

## Summary

Characteristic roots of the separated two-mode system are all quartic roots, computed together by `polynomial_roots_with_options` and checked against original coefficients when `PolynomialRootReport` termination is `Complete`.
Check independent quadratic roots, Vieta sums/products, and conjugate symmetry, looking for rounding-level agreement in all three.
Undamped real parts can round positive or negative; judge stability from magnitudes and conditioning as well as signs.
Near critical damping, roots become repeated and move by order $\sqrt{\delta}$ under coefficient changes $\delta$; inspect the condition number from `PolynomialRootReport::max_root_condition` when interpreting positions.
