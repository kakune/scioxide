An infinite impulse response (IIR) filter is represented by a difference equation that also uses past outputs:

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{N} a_j\, y[k-j] = \sum_{i=0}^{M} b_i\, x[k-i], \qquad a_0 = 1
$$

Its transfer function is $H(z) = B(z)/A(z)$ ($B$ and $A$ are polynomials in $z^{-1}$).
Poles (zeros of $A$) allow the same cutoff sharpness with far fewer coefficients than FIR.
In return, phase is nonlinear, and output diverges if poles leave the unit circle.

Classical IIR design uses approximation theory for continuous-time (analog) filters.
Choose a low-pass prototype with cutoff 1, transform its frequencies to the desired band, then map it to discrete time with the bilinear transform.
This page covers those three stages, selecting order from specifications, and resonators that reject or pass specific frequencies.
[IIR filtering and zero phase](../iir-filtering-and-zero-phase/) covers applying the designed filter to a record.

Frequency units follow the chapter-wide convention.
Analog frequency is angular frequency $\omega$ [rad/unit time], evaluated at $s = i\omega$; digital frequency is Nyquist ratio $f$, evaluated at $z = e^{i\pi f}$.
`IirDomain::Analog` is a function of $s$, stable when poles have negative real parts; `IirDomain::Digital` is a function of $z$, stable when pole magnitudes are below 1.

## Analog low-pass prototypes

`iir_prototype(order, family)` returns an order-$N$ low-pass prototype in zero-pole-gain form `PoleZeroGain` (domain `Analog`).
`IirFamily` has five families, all placing characteristic frequency at $\omega = 1$ and normalizing maximum passband magnitude to 1.
Ripple and attenuation are given in dB; let $\varepsilon_p^2 = 10^{R/10} - 1$ ($R$ is passband ripple) and $\varepsilon_s^2 = 10^{A/10} - 1$ ($A$ is minimum stopband attenuation).

| Family | Squared magnitude $\lvert H(i\omega) \rvert^2$ | Meaning of $\omega = 1$ | Poles and zeros |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Butterworth` | $\dfrac{1}{1 + \omega^{2N}}$ | Half-power point | Poles on the unit circle, no finite zeros |
| `ChebyshevI { ripple }` | $\dfrac{1}{1 + \varepsilon_p^2 T_N^2(\omega)}$ | Passband edge | Poles on an ellipse, no finite zeros |
| `ChebyshevII { attenuation }` | $\dfrac{1}{1 + \varepsilon_s^2 / T_N^2(1/\omega)}$ | Stopband edge | Imaginary-axis zeros $\omega = 1/\cos\theta_k$ |
| `Elliptic { ripple, attenuation }` | $\dfrac{1}{1 + \varepsilon_p^2 R_N^2(\omega)}$ | Passband edge | Imaginary-axis zeros; equiripple in passband and stopband |
| `Bessel(normalization)` | No closed form | Depends on normalization | No finite zeros |

$T_N$ is the degree-$N$ Chebyshev polynomial, $R_N$ an elliptic rational function, and $\theta_k = (2k+1)\pi/(2N)$ ($k = 0, \dots, N-1$).
Each family has the following properties.

- Butterworth has zero magnitude derivatives through order $2N - 1$ at $\omega = 0$ (maximally flat).
  Its poles $p_k = -\sin\theta_k + i\cos\theta_k$ are equally spaced on the left half of the unit circle.
- Chebyshev type I oscillates with equal ripple between $1$ and $1/\sqrt{1 + \varepsilon_p^2}$ in passband $0 \le \omega \le 1$ and decreases monotonically in the stopband.
  With $\mu = \operatorname{asinh}(1/\varepsilon_p)/N$, its poles are $-\sinh\mu \sin\theta_k + i\cosh\mu \cos\theta_k$.
  Even orders have DC gain $1/\sqrt{1 + \varepsilon_p^2}$.
- Chebyshev type II is monotone in the passband and equiripple in stopband $\omega \ge 1$, with squared magnitude exactly $10^{-A/10}$ at stopband edge $\omega = 1$.
  Its poles are reciprocals of the points in the type-I formula with $\varepsilon_p$ replaced by $1/\varepsilon_s$.
- Elliptic (Cauer) is equiripple in both bands and has the narrowest transition for the same order, ripple, and attenuation.
  Its stopband edge is $\omega_s = 1/\sqrt{m}$; parameter $m$ is found by solving the order equation $K(m)/K'(m) = N K(m_1)/K'(m_1)$ ($m_1 = \varepsilon_p^2/\varepsilon_s^2$) with Brent's method.
  $K$ is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, $K'(m) = K(1-m)$, and poles and zeros are constructed from Jacobi elliptic functions.
- Bessel (Thomson) has $H(s) = \theta_N(0)/\theta_N(s)$, where $\theta_N(s) = \sum_{k=0}^{N} \frac{(N+k)!}{2^k\, k!\, (N-k)!} s^{N-k}$ is the reverse Bessel polynomial.
  Its group delay is maximally flat at $\omega = 0$, helping preserve pulse shapes.
  Poles are reciprocals of Bessel polynomial zeros, computed in `f64` to within a few $\varepsilon$ using evaluation that avoids coefficient cancellation and the Aberth–Ehrlich method.

`BesselNormalization` selects the Bessel prototype's frequency scale.
`Delay` sets low-frequency group delay to 1; `Phase` sets the geometric mean of pole magnitudes to 1, matching the Butterworth high-frequency asymptote $|H| \approx \omega^{-N}$; `Magnitude` sets $|H(i)|^2 = 1/2$.
Only `Magnitude` solves a normalization equation with Brent's method.

Order 0, nonpositive ripple or attenuation, and elliptic attenuation no greater than ripple produce `InvalidInput`; nonfinite values produce `NonFinite`.
Failure to converge in the elliptic-parameter or Bessel-normalization equation produces `ConvergenceFailure`.
`iir_prototype_scoped` accepts an execution context or outer solver's `SolveContext` as its final argument, counting these iterations against that scope's limit.

## Band transformations

`IirBand` specifies the design band, with each edge an image of the prototype's $\omega = 1$.
`PoleZeroGain::transform(band)` substitutes for the analog prototype's $s$ as follows.

| `IirBand` | Substitution |
|---|---|
| `Lowpass(ω_c)` | $s \to s/\omega_c$ |
| `Highpass(ω_c)` | $s \to \omega_c/s$ |
| `Bandpass(ω₁, ω₂)` | $s \to (s^2 + \omega_0^2)/(B s)$ |
| `Bandstop(ω₁, ω₂)` | $s \to B s/(s^2 + \omega_0^2)$ |

Here $\omega_0 = \sqrt{\omega_1 \omega_2}$ is the center and $B = \omega_2 - \omega_1$ the bandwidth.
Band-pass and band-stop substitute quadratic expressions in $s$, doubling the prototype's pole count.
Edges must be positive, with band edges satisfying $\omega_1 < \omega_2$; otherwise `InvalidInput` results.
Gain is rescaled in an extended exponent range and rounded only once, preserving representable results despite intermediate overflow or underflow.

## Bilinear transform and prewarping

Applying the trapezoidal rule with sampling period $T$ to the integrator $y(t) = \int_0^t x(\tau)\, d\tau$ gives

$$
y[k] - y[k-1] = \frac{T}{2}\bigl(x[k] + x[k-1]\bigr)
$$


Taking the $z$-transform makes integrator transfer function $1/s$ correspond to $\frac{T}{2} \frac{1 + z^{-1}}{1 - z^{-1}}$, giving the substitution

$$
s = \frac{2}{T} \frac{1 - z^{-1}}{1 + z^{-1}} = \frac{2}{T} \frac{z - 1}{z + 1}
$$


This is the **bilinear transform**.
Solving inversely gives $z = (1 + sT/2)/(1 - sT/2)$, mapping the left half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s < 0$ to the unit disk and the imaginary axis one-to-one onto the unit circle.
Stable analog prototypes become stable digital filters, preserving magnitude ripple values.

Substituting $s = i\Omega$ and $z = e^{i\omega}$ ($\omega$ in rad/sample) gives

$$
i\Omega = \frac{2}{T} \frac{e^{i\omega} - 1}{e^{i\omega} + 1} = i \frac{2}{T} \tan\frac{\omega}{2}
$$

so analog and digital frequencies satisfy $\Omega = (2/T)\tan(\omega/2)$.
Digital frequencies $\omega \in [0, \pi)$ map onto the whole of $\Omega \in [0, \infty)$, compressing higher frequencies (frequency warping).
Thus, to design a digital band edge $\omega_c$, place the analog band edge at $\Omega_c = (2/T)\tan(\omega_c/2)$.
This is **prewarping**.

For `IirDomain::Digital`, `design_iir(order, family, band, domain)` converts band edges $f$ (Nyquist ratio, 0 < $f$ < 1) to $\Omega = 2\tan(\pi f/2)$, then applies the $T = 1$ bilinear transform $s = 2(z-1)/(z+1)$.
The digital band-edge response therefore matches the prototype response at $\omega = 1$, and for arbitrary $f$,

$$
H_d(f) = H_{\mathrm{proto}}\!\left(\frac{\tan(\pi f/2)}{\tan(\pi f_c/2)}\right) \qquad \text{(low-pass)}
$$


For `IirDomain::Analog`, band edges remain angular frequencies, with no bilinear transform.

`PoleZeroGain::bilinear(sample_rate)` performs only substitution $s = 2f_s(z-1)/(z+1)$, without prewarping.
An analog pole or zero $r$ maps to $(2f_s + r)/(2f_s - r)$, while zeros at infinity map to $z = -1$.
If `r` equals $2f_s$, `NumericalInstability` results.
The bilinear transform does not preserve Bessel group-delay flatness; it deteriorates as the cutoff approaches Nyquist ([group-delay example](../../examples/iir-group-delay-zero-phase/)).

## Zero-pole-gain representations and design results

`design_iir` computes the prototype, band transformation, and bilinear transform in sequence and returns `IirDesign`.
Elliptic and Bessel `Magnitude` normalization solves scalar equations with Brent's method; `design_iir_scoped` counts these iterations against the outer scope's limit.

- `zpk()` returns zero-pole-gain representation `PoleZeroGain`.
  `PoleZeroGain::new(domain, zeros, poles, gain)` preserves roots as given, without canceling common roots or reflecting unstable poles.
  `response(f)` evaluates products of root factors without polynomial expansion.
- `prototype_order()` is the prototype order; band-pass and band-stop have twice as many poles.
- `stability_margin()` is the minimum distance from a pole to the stability boundary: $-\operatorname{Re}p$ for analog and $1 - |p|$ for digital.
  It indicates stability margin, not an error bound.
- `to_sos()` groups roots into second-order sections and returns real `SecondOrderSections`, dropping imaginary parts (roundoff in a real design).
  Poles are taken nearest the stability boundary first, paired with the pole nearest their conjugate, and assigned nearby zeros; sections are then reordered to place those near the boundary last.
  Pairing $n$ poles takes $O(n^2)$ comparisons.
  Poles not strictly stable under the rounded section coefficients produce `InvalidInput`; imaginary parts exceeding $\sqrt{\varepsilon}(1 + |\operatorname{Re}x|)$ produce `DomainError`.

`TransferFunction::new(domain, b, a)` stores numerator $b$ and denominator $a$ coefficients divided by $a_0$.
Digital coefficients are ordered in ascending powers of $z^{-1}$; analog coefficients in descending powers of $s$.
High-order polynomial coefficients are ill-conditioned with respect to roots, so use second-order sections for storage and filtering.
[IIR filtering and zero phase](../iir-filtering-and-zero-phase/) explains why and covers section execution.

`design_iir` produces `InvalidInput` for order 0, nonpositive or nonincreasing band edges, digital band edges at least 1, or nonpositive ripple or attenuation.
High order with a low cutoff can make gain smaller than the normal range, producing `NumericalInstability` (`Underflow`).
Rounded poles that are not strictly stable produce `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).

### Band-edge responses and frequency warping

The next cell takes family, order $N$, and cutoff $f_c$ as inputs and designs a digital low-pass filter with `design_iir`.
It obtains a same-family prototype with `iir_prototype` and compares digital response $|H_d(f)|$ with prototype response $|H_{\mathrm{proto}}(\tan(\pi f/2)/\tan(\pi f_c/2))|$ at 397 points in $0 \le f \le 0.99$ to check the mapping formula above.
For Butterworth, it also compares with closed form $1/(1 + x^{2N})$ ($x = \tan(\pi f/2)/\tan(\pi f_c/2)$).
The table's $|H_d(f_c)|^2$ is the band-edge value, whose meaning differs by family.
The dashed curve is prototype response $|H_{\mathrm{proto}}(f/f_c)|$ mapped linearly in frequency; its difference from the solid curve is frequency warping.

    "Chebyshev II (40 dB)" => IirFamily::ChebyshevII { attenuation: 40.0 },

    sections.sections().len(),
    design.stability_margin(),
    edge,
    unit,
    map_gap,
    butterworth.then_some(closed_gap),
);

    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "warping" })
);
emit_line_chart!(

        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Digital |H_d(f)|", "points": digital }),

            "name": "Linearly mapped prototype |H(f/f_c)|",
            "points": linear,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Frequency f [Nyquist ratio]",
        "yLabel": "Magnitude |H| [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "yMin": 1e-6,
        "yMax": 2.0,

    })

```

With the default Butterworth, $N = 4$, and $f_c = 0.5$, band-edge $|H_d(f_c)|^2$ is $0.5$, agreeing with prototype $|H(i)|^2$ within roundoff.
Across 397 points, the maximum difference between the digital response and mapping formula is $8.9 \times 10^{-16}$, and the maximum difference from closed form $1/(1 + x^{2N})$ is $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$.
This roundoff-level agreement shows the prewarping formula holds throughout $f$.
Four poles form two second-order sections, with stability margin $1 - \max|p| = 0.332$.

Chebyshev type II uses the stopband edge, giving $|H_d(f_c)|^2 = 1.0 \times 10^{-4}$ (40 dB); elliptic and Chebyshev type I use the passband edge, giving $0.794$ (1 dB loss).
Bessel `Magnitude` normalization makes the band edge the half-power point.
In the plot, the digital response decreases faster than the linearly mapped prototype response as it approaches Nyquist.
For Butterworth, prototype zeros at infinity map to $z = -1$, giving zero magnitude at $f = 1$.
For elliptic with $f_c = 0.8$, the stability margin shrinks to $0.060$ as poles approach the unit circle.

## Order selection from specifications

Filter specifications often require loss no greater than $R$ dB at passband edge $f_p$ and attenuation at least $A$ dB beyond stopband edge $f_s$.
`select_iir_order(family, pass, stop, ripple, attenuation, domain)` selects the minimum order meeting these specifications.
`pass` and `stop` are the same `IirBand` variant; stopband edges lie above passband edges for low-pass, below for high-pass, outside for band-pass, and inside for band-stop.
Digital edges are prewarped as in `design_iir`.

The specifications are mapped to stopband edge $\Omega > 1$ of an equivalent low-pass prototype with passband edge 1.
For digital low-pass designs, $\Omega = \tan(\pi f_s/2)/\tan(\pi f_p/2)$; band-pass and band-stop use the smaller value obtained from their two stopband edges.
The order is the smallest positive integer at least the following real solution $n^*$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Butterworth:}\quad & n^* = \frac{\ln(\varepsilon_s^2/\varepsilon_p^2)}{2 \ln \Omega} \\
\text{Chebyshev I and II:}\quad & n^* = \frac{\operatorname{acosh}(\varepsilon_s/\varepsilon_p)}{\operatorname{acosh}\Omega} \\
\text{Elliptic:}\quad & n^* = \frac{K(1/\Omega^2)\, K'(\varepsilon_p^2/\varepsilon_s^2)}{K'(1/\Omega^2)\, K(\varepsilon_p^2/\varepsilon_s^2)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Formula evaluation costs $O(1)$, with complete elliptic integrals evaluated only for elliptic filters.
For band-stop, one passband edge moves inward to balance the two stopband edges and reduce order.

`IirOrderFamily` has four choices: `Butterworth`, `ChebyshevI`, `ChebyshevII`, and `Elliptic`.
Bessel specifications concern group delay rather than band edges, so there is no order equation; choose the order directly.

The result `IirOrderReport` holds three values that can be passed directly to `design_iir`.

- `order()`: selected prototype order (twice as many poles for band-pass and band-stop)
- `family()`: `IirFamily` with the specified ripple and attenuation
- `band()`: design band edges (in the input units)

Butterworth `band()` is the half-power point; Chebyshev type II uses the stopband start, and Chebyshev type I and elliptic use the passband edge.
The margin from rounding order up to an integer adjusts band-edge position for Butterworth and Chebyshev type II.
It also holds `termination()`, `is_success()`, elliptic-integral evaluation counts `counts()`, a `order_deficit()` for order deficit $\max(n^* - n, 0)$, and result applicability `trust()`.

`IirOrderTermination` has two values.
`Selected` means the selected order meets specifications; `OrderLimit` means the required order exceeds the limit.
Three call forms handle limits differently.

- `select_iir_order` has no limit; if the required order does not fit in `usize`, it returns `ConvergenceFailure` (`TruncationLimit`).
- `select_iir_order_with_options` does not treat orders beyond $k$ in `IirOrderOptions::new().with_maximum_order(k)` as errors; it returns an `OrderLimit` report with order $k$ and positive order deficit.
  The limit is only compared with the required order and does not change computational cost.
  The default limit is `usize::MAX` (unlimited); a limit of 0 produces `InvalidInput`.
- `select_iir_order_scoped` makes the same selection as the options-based form within an outer scope, counting elliptic-integral evaluations against its limit.

Attenuation no greater than ripple, nonpositive edges or ripple, digital edges at least 1, mismatched band types, or incorrectly positioned stopband edges produce `InvalidInput`.

### Comparing orders for four families

The next cell takes passband edge $f_p$, transition width $\Delta$ (stopband edge $f_s = f_p + \Delta$), ripple $R$, attenuation $A$, and order limit, and selects orders for four families with `select_iir_order_with_options`.
As an independent check, it computes $n^*$ from the formulas above using `f64` reference calculations (complete elliptic integrals use arithmetic–geometric mean $K(m) = \pi/(2\,\mathrm{AGM}(1, \sqrt{1-m}))$).
It calls `design_iir` with report values `order()`, `family()`, and `band()`, then measures loss at the passband edge and minimum attenuation on a grid over $[f_s, 0.999]$ with spacing $10^{-3}$.
Rows with `OrderLimit` also use the limited order for design, showing the unmet specifications.
The outcome is satisfied if every family is `Selected` and partial if any family stops at the limit.

//|   ripple: {"type":"range","label":"Passband ripple R [dB]","description":"Allowed loss at the passband edge.","min":0.1,"max":3,"step":0.1,"value":1}
//|   attenuation: {"type":"range","label":"Stopband attenuation A [dB]","description":"Minimum required stopband attenuation.","min":20,"max":100,"step":5,"value":80}

//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"order-limit","label":"Limit 10","description":"Butterworth and Chebyshev stop at the limit, making the outcome partial.","inputs":{"maximum_order":10}}
//|   - {"id":"loose","label":"R = 3 dB, A = 40 dB, Δ = 0.2","description":"Looser specifications reduce order differences.","inputs":{"ripple":3,"attenuation":40,"transition":0.2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":22}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":11}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":11}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":7}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":21.9736,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/3","approx":{"value":6.5840,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":0.20582,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/2/5","approx":{"value":0.28858,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/1/7","approx":{"value":85.67,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/7","range":{"min":79.999,"max":80.01}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: order-limit
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"OrderLimit"}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":11.9736,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/7","approx":{"value":33.21,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":"Selected"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
//|   - case: loose
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":6}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":3}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_signal::{
    IirBand, IirDomain, IirOrderFamily, IirOrderOptions, design_iir,
    select_iir_order_with_options,
};

let stop_edge = pass_edge + transition;
let options = IirOrderOptions::new().with_maximum_order(maximum_order.round() as usize);
// Quantities for the independent check (f64 reference calculations)
let half_pi = 0.5 * std::f64::consts::PI;
let omega = (half_pi * stop_edge).tan() / (half_pi * pass_edge).tan();
let ep2 = 10f64.powf(ripple / 10.0) - 1.0;
let es2 = 10f64.powf(attenuation / 10.0) - 1.0;
let k = |m: f64| {
    let (mut a, mut b) = (1.0_f64, (1.0 - m).sqrt());
    while (a - b).abs() > 1e-15 * a {
        (a, b) = (0.5 * (a + b), (a * b).sqrt());
    }
    half_pi / a
};
let families = [
    ("Butterworth", IirOrderFamily::Butterworth),
    ("Chebyshev I", IirOrderFamily::ChebyshevI),
    ("Chebyshev II", IirOrderFamily::ChebyshevII),
    ("Elliptic", IirOrderFamily::Elliptic),
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut limited = 0_usize;
for (name, family) in families {
    let report = select_iir_order_with_options(
        family,
        IirBand::Lowpass(pass_edge),
        IirBand::Lowpass(stop_edge),
        ripple,
        attenuation,
        IirDomain::Digital,
        options,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    limited += usize::from(!report.is_success());
    let continuous = match family {
        IirOrderFamily::Butterworth => (es2 / ep2).ln() / (2.0 * omega.ln()),
        IirOrderFamily::ChebyshevI | IirOrderFamily::ChebyshevII => {
            (es2 / ep2).sqrt().acosh() / omega.acosh()
        }
        IirOrderFamily::Elliptic => {
            let (m, m1) = (1.0 / (omega * omega), ep2 / es2);
            k(m) * k(1.0 - m1) / (k(1.0 - m) * k(m1))
        }
    };
    let design = design_iir(
        report.order(),
        report.family(),
        report.band(),
        IirDomain::Digital,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let zpk = design.zpk();
    let pass_loss = -20.0
        * zpk
            .response(pass_edge)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            .norm()
            .log10();
    let mut stop_peak = 0.0_f64;
    let mut f = stop_edge;
    while f <= 0.999 {
        stop_peak = stop_peak.max(zpk.response(f).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.norm());
        f += 1e-3;
    }
    // Low-pass specifications return low-pass band edges
    let IirBand::Lowpass(edge) = report.band() else {
        return Err("No low-pass band edge returned".to_string());
    };
    rows.push((
        name,
        format!("{:?}", report.termination()),
        report.order(),
        continuous,
        report.order_deficit().raw_norm(),
        edge,
        pass_loss,
        -20.0 * stop_peak.log10(),
    ));
}

let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Family" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Termination status" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Order", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "n* (closed form) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Order deficit [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Design band edge [Nyquist ratio]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Loss at f_p [dB]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Minimum stopband attenuation [dB]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })
);
let outcome = if limited == 0 {
    Outcome::satisfied("Selected orders meeting specifications for all four families")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Some families stopped at the order limit")
        .explanation("OrderLimit rows show designs at the limited order and do not meet specifications.")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("Selected {}/4", 4 - limited))
        .diagnostic("Number of OrderLimit families", limited as f64, None)
);
```

With the default specifications ($f_p = 0.2$, $f_s = 0.3$, $R = 1$ dB, $A = 80$ dB), selected orders are 22 for Butterworth, 11 for Chebyshev types I and II, and 7 for elliptic.
Each matches the rounded-up closed-form $n^*$ (21.97, 10.36, 10.36, 6.58).
Designed passband-edge losses are 1 dB in every family (differences at most $10^{-12}$), and minimum stopband attenuation is at least 80 dB.
Chebyshev type I gives 85.67 dB of stopband attenuation, beyond the specification, because rounding up the order assigns its margin to the stopband.
Butterworth uses the margin for band-edge position; the half-power point of `band()` is 0.2058, above the passband edge.
Chebyshev type II `band()` is 0.2886, so its stopband starts before the specified $f_s = 0.3$.

Limiting order to 10 makes Butterworth and both Chebyshev families `OrderLimit`, changing the outcome to partial.
Butterworth has order deficit $21.97 - 10 = 11.97$, and its limited-order design achieves only 33.21 dB minimum stopband attenuation.
Using the reported order directly then fails specifications, so check `is_success()` before design.

## Notch, peak, comb, and gammatone filters

Filters that reject or pass specific frequencies are simpler to express in closed form than to construct from prototypes.

`design_notch_peak(frequency, quality, kind)` returns a second-order notch or peak as `SecondOrderSections`.
From center frequency $f_0$ (Nyquist ratio, $0 < f_0 < 1$) and quality factor $Q = f_0/\Delta f$ ($\Delta f$ is the −3 dB bandwidth), set $\beta = \tan(\pi\Delta f/2)$ and $g = 1/(1+\beta)$ to obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Notch:}\quad & H(z) = g\,\frac{1 - 2\cos(\pi f_0) z^{-1} + z^{-2}}{D(z)} \\
\text{Peak:}\quad & H(z) = (1-g)\,\frac{1 - z^{-2}}{D(z)} \\
& D(z) = 1 - 2g\cos(\pi f_0) z^{-1} + (2g-1) z^{-2}
\end{aligned}
$$


The notch has zeros at $f_0$ and unit gain at DC and Nyquist.
The peak has unit gain at $f_0$ and zeros at DC and Nyquist.
Using prewarped bandwidth in $\beta$ makes the half-power point spacing equal $\Delta f$.
$f_0/Q$ at least 1, nonpositive $Q$, or unstable rounded poles produce `InvalidInput`.

`ResonanceKind` selects `Notch` (reject the center frequency) or `Peak` (pass the center frequency), and is also used by `design_comb`.

`design_comb(delay, feedback, kind)` returns a comb filter with integer delay $D$ and feedback $\alpha$ ($|\alpha| < 1$) as `TransferFunction`.

$$
\text{Notch:}\ H(z) = \frac{1+\alpha}{2}\,\frac{1 - z^{-D}}{1 - \alpha z^{-D}}, \qquad
\text{Peak:}\ H(z) = \frac{1-\alpha}{1 - \alpha z^{-D}}
$$

At harmonics $f = 2k/D$ ($k = 0, 1, \dots$), the notch has zeros and the peak has unit gain.
For $0 < \alpha < 1$, bringing $\alpha$ closer to 1 narrows the notch or peak bandwidth.
Delay 0 or $|\alpha| \ge 1$ produces `InvalidInput`.
Use it to remove a fundamental and its harmonics together, as with mains noise.

`design_gammatone(order, frequency, bandwidth)` approximates the auditory filter model gammatone $t^{n-1} e^{-\pi b t} e^{i\pi f_0 t}$ ($t$ is a sample index; $f_0$ and $b$ are Nyquist ratios) by cascading $n$ identical first-order sections $H_1(z) = (1-r)/(1 - p z^{-1})$ ($p = r e^{i\pi f_0}$, $r = e^{-\pi b}$).
Coefficients are complex, with unit gain at $f_0$.
`design_real_gammatone` returns a cascade of real second-order sections with total order $2n$ for its real part $(H + \bar H)/2$, normalized to unit gain at $f_0$.
Zeros are found and checked by polynomial rootfinding; `design_real_gammatone_scoped` counts that rootfinding against the outer scope's limit.
At $f_0 = 0$, the complex design is already real, so $n$ first-order sections are returned.

### Checking notch bandwidth with half-power points

The next cell takes center $f_0$, quality factor, and kind (notch or peak), designs with `design_notch_peak`, and bisects to find the two frequencies $f_1 < f_0 < f_2$ where $|H(f)|^2 = 1/2$.
Bisection is written as an independent check; response evaluation uses `SecondOrderSections::response`.
It shows the relative difference between $f_2 - f_1$ and design $\Delta f = f_0/Q$, plus gains at $f_0$, DC, and Nyquist.

```rust
//| id: signal-iir-notch-bandwidth
//| ref: signal-iir-notch-bandwidth
//| title: "Measure spacing between notch and peak half-power points"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   center: {"type":"range","label":"Center f₀ [Nyquist ratio]","description":"Frequency to reject or pass.","min":0.02,"max":0.9,"step":0.02,"value":0.2}
//|   quality: {"type":"range","label":"Quality factor Q [-]","description":"Ratio of f₀ to the −3 dB bandwidth.","min":1,"max":50,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   kind: {"type":"select","label":"Kind","description":"Notch rejects f₀; Peak passes f₀.","options":["Notch","Peak"],"value":"Notch"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"peak","label":"Peak","description":"Unit gain at f₀, zero at DC and Nyquist.","inputs":{"kind":"Peak"}}
//|   - {"id":"wide","label":"f₀ = 0.8, Q = 1","description":"The wide bandwidth makes the two half-power points asymmetric about f₀.","inputs":{"center":0.8,"quality":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":0.190216,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.02,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-13}}
//|   - case: peak
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.02,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":0,"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: wide
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":0.180431,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.980431,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"bandwidth","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}

let resonance = if kind == "Peak" {
    ResonanceKind::Peak
} else {
    ResonanceKind::Notch
};
let sections = design_notch_peak(center, quality, resonance).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let power = |f: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    Ok(sections.response(f).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.norm_sqr())
};
// Independent check: bisect the interval where |H|² − 1/2 changes sign
let half_power = |mut lower: f64, mut upper: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let sign = (power(lower)? - 0.5).signum();
    for _ in 0..60 {
        let middle = 0.5 * (lower + upper);
        if (power(middle)? - 0.5).signum() == sign {
            lower = middle;
        } else {
            upper = middle;
        }
    }
    Ok(0.5 * (lower + upper))
};
let lower_edge = half_power(0.0, center)?;
let upper_edge = half_power(center, 1.0)?;
let measured = upper_edge - lower_edge;
let designed = center / quality;

let fixed5 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 5 });

    { "key": "0", "label": "f₁ [Nyquist ratio]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "f₂ [Nyquist ratio]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "f₂ − f₁ [Nyquist ratio]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "f₀/Q [Nyquist ratio]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "|H(f₀)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "|H(0)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "|H(1)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
let row = (
    lower_edge,
    upper_edge,
    measured,
    designed,
    (measured - designed) / designed,
    power(center)?.sqrt(),
    power(0.0)?.sqrt(),
    power(1.0)?.sqrt(),
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "bandwidth" })
);
// Frequency grid refined near the center
let mut grid: Vec<f64> = (0..=400).map(|i| i as f64 / 400.0).collect();
grid.extend((0..=200).map(|i| lower_edge - measured + 3.0 * measured * i as f64 / 200.0));
grid.retain(|f| (0.0..=1.0).contains(f));
grid.sort_by(f64::total_cmp);
let mut curve = Vec::new();
for f in grid {
    curve.push([f, power(f)?.sqrt()]);
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "|H(f)|", "points": curve })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Frequency f [Nyquist ratio]",
        "yLabel": "Magnitude |H| [-]",
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.05,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.5_f64.sqrt(), "label": "1/√2" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": lower_edge, "label": "f₁" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": upper_edge, "label": "f₂" }
        ]
    })
);
```

With the default $f_0 = 0.2$ and $Q = 10$ notch, half-power points are $f_1 = 0.19022$ and $f_2 = 0.21022$, with spacing $0.02$ agreeing with $f_0/Q$ to relative difference $2 \times 10^{-15}$.
$|H(f_0)|$ is $3 \times 10^{-15}$, and DC and Nyquist gains are 1.
Switching to peak retains the same half-power points, with $|H(f_0)| = 1$ and zero DC and Nyquist gains.
With $f_0 = 0.8$ and $Q = 1$, $f_1 = 0.1804$ and $f_2 = 0.9804$ give spacing still equal to 0.8, but the two half-power points are 0.62 left and 0.18 right of $f_0$.
Bandwidth is determined on the frequency axis compressed by the bilinear transform, making the side nearer Nyquist more crowded.

## Summary

IIR design takes an analog low-pass prototype determined by family and order, transforms it to the target band, and maps it to discrete time with a prewarped bilinear transform.
Band-edge meanings differ by family: half-power point for Butterworth, passband edge for Chebyshev type I and elliptic, and stopband edge for Chebyshev type II.
When selecting order from specifications, set a limit with `select_iir_order_with_options`, check `is_success()`, then pass report values `order()`, `family()`, and `band()` to `design_iir`.
Check pole margins in the design result with `stability_margin()`, then convert to second-order sections with `to_sos()` for filtering.
See [IIR filtering and zero phase](../iir-filtering-and-zero-phase/) for section execution, chunked processing, and forward–backward zero phase, and the [group-delay example](../../examples/iir-group-delay-zero-phase/) for family differences in group delay.
