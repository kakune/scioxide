This page addresses applying the zeros, poles, and gain from [IIR filter design](../iir-design/) to a record.
Even with the same transfer function, coefficient representation and computation order change how roundoff propagates.
Since IIR output depends on past outputs, processing a record in pieces requires carrying delay state forward.
Finally, the page derives forward–backward filtering that cancels phase shifts and its endpoint treatment.

## Filtering with second-order sections (SOS)

Using an order-$N$ transfer function $H(z) = B(z)/A(z)$ directly as a difference equation lets roundoff in denominator coefficients $a_1, \dots, a_N$ move pole positions.
Polynomial roots can be ill-conditioned with respect to coefficients.
In particular, low-pass filters with low cutoffs cluster poles near $z = 1$; for $m$ nearby poles, relative coefficient error $\varepsilon$ moves roots by about $\varepsilon^{1/m}$.
If poles leave the unit circle, a filter designed to be stable diverges.

A cascade of second-order sections (SOS) separates $H$ by conjugate pole pairs as

$$
H(z) = \prod_{\ell=1}^{L} \frac{b_{0\ell} + b_{1\ell} z^{-1} + b_{2\ell} z^{-2}}{1 + a_{1\ell} z^{-1} + a_{2\ell} z^{-2}}
$$


Each section's coefficients depend on just two roots, so roundoff does not spread to other poles.
`IirDesign::to_sos()` performs this decomposition, placing sections with poles near the stability boundary last.

`SecondOrderSections::new(domain, rows)` divides each row $[b_0, b_1, b_2, a_0, a_1, a_2]$ by its leading nonzero denominator coefficient, finds section poles with the quadratic formula in a cancellation-avoiding form, and checks strict stability.
Digital rows use ascending powers of $z^{-1}$, and analog rows use descending powers of $s$.
Nonfinite coefficients produce `NonFinite`; a zero denominator or unstable poles produce `InvalidInput`.

- `response(f)` is the product of section responses, and `group_delay(f)` is the sum of section group delays, evaluated without expansion into high-order polynomials.
- `to_zpk()` finds roots section by section to recover zeros, poles, and gain; `to_zpk_scoped` counts that rootfinding against the outer scope's limit.
- `to_real(tolerance)` converts complex sections to real sections when imaginary parts are within tolerance.
- `to_transfer()` multiplies section polynomials into a single transfer function.
  High-order coefficients are ill-conditioned, so this conversion is for comparison and display.

Each section uses the **transposed direct-form II** recurrence

$$
y[k] = b_0 x[k] + s_1[k-1], \qquad
s_j[k] = s_{j+1}[k-1] + b_j x[k] - a_j y[k] \quad (j = 1, 2), \quad s_3 = 0
$$

where $s_j$ denotes section delays.
Operations per sample per channel are proportional to delay count $d$; for a second-order-section cascade, $d = 2L$.
`SecondOrderSections::filter(input)` is a one-shot form that causally processes the entire record from zero initial delays; `TransferFunction::filter` uses the same recurrence with the transfer function as one section of order $\max(\mathrm{len}(a), \mathrm{len}(b)) - 1$.

### Errors from transfer-function expansion

The next cell designs a Butterworth low-pass filter (order $N$, cutoff $f_c$) with `design_iir` and compares second-order sections `sos` with their expanded transfer function `sos.to_transfer()`.
It finds roots of the expanded denominator with `to_zpk()`, showing the maximum distance to designed poles and stability.
Both forms process a 400-sample unit step, and the maximum output difference is measured.
Since DC gain should be 1, it also shows $|H(0)| - 1$ for each form.
Fields are left empty if transfer-function root checks or filtering fail.

With the default $N = 12$ and $f_c = 0.05$, there are 6 second-order sections, and expanded transfer-function denominator coefficients reach magnitude 498.
Roots of the expanded denominator shift by up to $7.6 \times 10^{-3}$ from the designed poles but remain inside the unit circle.
Even so, DC gain error is $3.0 \times 10^{-4}$, ten orders of magnitude above the sections' $6.4 \times 10^{-15}$, and the maximum step-response difference reaches $7.1 \times 10^{-4}$.
With $N = 4$, root displacement is $3.3 \times 10^{-13}$ and step-response difference is $5.0 \times 10^{-13}$, so the forms nearly agree.

With $N = 20$ and $f_c = 0.02$, denominator coefficients reach magnitude $1.2 \times 10^{5}$; roots shift by 0.13 and leave the unit circle.
The expanded-form step response diverges, with maximum difference $1.6 \times 10^{30}$ (the plot's vertical axis is fixed from $-0.5$ to $2$).
Second-order-section DC gain error remains $1.6 \times 10^{-15}$.
Even coefficient roundoff of order $10^{-16}$ is strongly amplified by clustered roots.

## Method objects and state

`SecondOrderSections::filter` suffices for processing an entire record at once.
Use method object `IirFilter` to process many records or channels with the same filter or to stream a record in chunks.

- `IirFilter::from_sos(&sos, options)` constructs from a stable second-order-section cascade; `IirFilter::from_transfer(&transfer, options)` constructs from a transfer function.
  Both produce `InvalidInput` unless the domain is digital.
  `from_transfer` also accepts unstable finite transfer functions; check `transfer.to_zpk()?.is_stable()` when stability is required.
- Construction only validates and retains coefficients, without computation.
  The `SignalExecutionOptions` of `options` limits threads when processing lanes in parallel along an axis.
- `delays()` is the delay count per channel: two per section in an SOS cascade (including first-order sections).

The caller owns delay values in `IirState`.
`IirState::new(&filter, channels)` initializes all channel delays to zero; values are arranged by channel, `delays()` per channel, in section order.
Read with `values()`, modify with `values_mut()` (finiteness is checked on the next execution), reset to zero with `reset()`, and obtain the channel count with `channels()`.

`filter_into(input, output, state, workspace, cx)` processes part of a one-channel record, writes `input.len()` elements at the beginning of `output`, and replaces `state` with delays after the final sample.
Passing the same `state` consecutively gives the same result as processing the whole record once.
Create workspace with `filter.workspace(IirRequest::Filter(SignalLayout::Contiguous(n)))`; it serves any call of length at most $n$.
The required value count is $n + d$.
Failed execution leaves both `output` and `state` unchanged.
`filter(input)` is an owned-result API starting from zero initial delays.

`filter_axis_into(input, axis, output, state, workspace, cx)` processes each lane along one array axis as an independent channel.
`state` has one channel per lane; lanes follow row-major order of the shape excluding `axis`.
Query workspace with `IirRequest::Filter(SignalLayout::Axis { shape, axis })`.
Each lane uses the same recurrence even in parallel, so results are independent of thread count.

### Chunked and whole-record processing

The next cell processes a 600-sample record with band-pass $[0.1, 0.3]$ (8 poles, 4 second-order sections) built from a fourth-order Chebyshev type-I (0.5 dB) prototype.
The input sums two sine waves and a unit impulse at sample 300.
It compares whole-record `IirFilter::filter` with `filter_into` called on length-$n_c$ chunks.
Checking the box resets delays with `IirState::reset` for every chunk, demonstrating the error from discarding state.

```rust
//| id: signal-iir-chunked-state
//| ref: signal-iir-chunked-state
//| title: "Process chunks while carrying delay state forward"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   chunk_len: {"type":"range","label":"Chunk length n_c [samples]","description":"Samples passed to filter_into at once.","min":1,"max":200,"step":1,"value":37}
//|   restart: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Reset state to zero for each chunk","description":"Incorrect processing that does not carry IirState forward.","value":false}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"restart","label":"Discard state","description":"Transients appear at every chunk boundary, causing large differences from whole-record processing.","inputs":{"restart":true}}
//|   - {"id":"single-sample","label":"n_c = 1","description":"Even one-sample chunks give identical output when state is retained.","inputs":{"chunk_len":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"chunks","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":17}
//|       - {"artifact":"chunks","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":8}
//|       - {"artifact":"chunks","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":45}
//|       - {"artifact":"chunks","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":0}
//|   - case: restart
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"chunks","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.9675,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: single-sample
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"chunks","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":600}
//|       - {"artifact":"chunks","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":9}
//|       - {"artifact":"chunks","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":0}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_signal::{
    IirBand, IirDomain, IirFamily, IirFilter, IirRequest, IirState, SignalExecutionOptions,
    SignalLayout, design_iir,
};

let band = IirBand::Bandpass(0.1, 0.3);
let family = IirFamily::ChebyshevI { ripple: 0.5 };
let sos = design_iir(4, family, band, IirDomain::Digital)
    .and_then(|design| design.to_sos())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let filter =
    IirFilter::from_sos(&sos, SignalExecutionOptions::new()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Add a unit impulse at sample 300 to passband (0.2) and stopband (0.6) sine waves
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let input: Vec<f64> = (0..600)
    .map(|k| {
        let t = k as f64;
        (pi * 0.2 * t).sin() + 0.5 * (pi * 0.6 * t).sin() + if k == 300 { 1.0 } else { 0.0 }
    })
    .collect();
let whole = filter.filter(&input).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let chunk = chunk_len.round() as usize;

let mut chunked = vec![0.0; input.len()];
for (piece, out) in input.chunks(chunk).zip(chunked.chunks_mut(chunk)) {
    if restart {
        state.reset();
    }
    filter
        .filter_into(
            piece,
            out,
            &mut state,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
let gap = whole
    .iter()
    .zip(&chunked)
    .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Chunk count", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Delay count d", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Workspace value count", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum difference from whole-record processing [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let values = filter
    .workspace_requirements(request)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .values_len();
let row = (input.len().div_ceil(chunk), filter.delays(), values, gap);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "chunks" })
);
let points = |y: &[f64]| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    y.iter().enumerate().map(|(k, &v)| [k as f64, v]).collect()
};
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Whole-record processing", "points": points(&whole) }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Chunked processing",
            "points": points(&chunked),
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({ "xLabel": "Sample k [-]", "yLabel": "Output y[k] [-]" })
);
```

With the default $n_c = 37$, processing takes 17 calls and differs from whole-record processing by exactly zero.
Carrying delays forward makes each sample's recurrence identical to whole-record processing, so results agree down to roundoff.
With $n_c = 1$, the difference remains zero and workspace value count becomes $n_c + d = 9$.
Resetting state on every call starts a transient at each chunk's beginning and produces a maximum difference of 0.97.
For streaming, save `IirState` as part of the record and pass it to the next call.

## Forward–backward zero-phase filtering

A causal IIR filter has frequency-dependent delay (group delay), changing waveform shape.
When the whole record is available, process it forward, reverse the output in time, process again, and reverse once more to cancel phase.
For real coefficients, time reversal conjugates frequency response, so the overall frequency response is

$$
H_{\mathrm{fb}}(f) = H(f)\, \overline{H(f)} = |H(f)|^2
$$


Phase is zero, magnitude is squared, and effective order doubles.
It uses future samples, so it is noncausal and cannot be used for streaming.

The $|H|^2$ formula describes steady-state response for an infinite record; finite records retain endpoint transients.
The forward pass starts at the record's beginning from zero delays; the backward pass does the same at its end.
Extend both endpoints, initialize delays in each direction to steady state, process, then remove the extension.

`IirPadding` selects the number $L$ of samples added at each endpoint and how to construct them.
For record $x_0, \dots, x_{n-1}$, samples prepended for $k = 1, \dots, L$ are as follows; the end is constructed similarly around $x_{n-1}$.

| `IirPadding` | Prepended samples |
|---|---|
| `None` | None |
| `Odd(L)` | $x_{-k} = 2x_0 - x_k$ (odd reflection around the endpoint) |
| `Even(L)` | $x_{-k} = x_k$ (even reflection without repeating the endpoint) |
| `Constant(L)` | $x_{-k} = x_0$ |

Positive $L$ must be less than $n$; otherwise `InvalidInput` results.
Odd reflection preserves continuous value and slope at the endpoint, reducing transients for records with a slope.

`IirInitialization` selects initial delays for one direction.
`Zero` uses zero; `SteadyState` uses steady state for repetition of that direction's first sample $x_0$, making output $H(1)\,x_0$ from the start ($H(1)$ is DC gain).
A filter with a section whose denominator vanishes at DC has no steady state and produces `DomainError`.
`State(&values)` uses caller-provided delays (in the same order as `IirState`).

`ForwardBackwardOptions` combines extension and initialization for both directions.
`ForwardBackwardOptions::new()` (equivalent to `default()`) uses no extension and zero initial delays in both directions and accepts records of any length.
Change these with `with_padding`, `with_forward`, and `with_backward` and pass them to `IirFilter::forward_backward_with_options(input, options)`.
Query workspace for the output-destination API `forward_backward_into(input, options, output, workspace, cx)` with `IirRequest::ForwardBackward(SignalLayout::Contiguous(n), padding)`; it holds as many values as the extended record's length.
The computational cost is $O(d(n + 2p))$ for $d$ delays, $n$ samples, and extension $p$.
The axis-based form is `forward_backward_axis_into`.

One-shot `SecondOrderSections::forward_backward(input)` fixes endpoint treatment as follows.
For $L$ second-order sections ($d = 2L$ delays) and record length $n$, extend each end by $\min(3(d+1), n-1)$ samples using odd reflection and start both directions with `SteadyState`.
`TransferFunction::forward_backward` uses the same rule, extending by $\min(3\max(\mathrm{len}(a), \mathrm{len}(b)), n-1)$ samples for $d = \max(\mathrm{len}(a), \mathrm{len}(b)) - 1$ delays.
Extension length depends on the filter and record; use `IirFilter::forward_backward_with_options` for other treatments.

### Endpoint treatment and errors

The next cell uses a fourth-order Butterworth low-pass filter (cutoff $f_c$, two second-order sections, $d = 4$) to process a 400-sample record containing a slope and a sine wave:

$$
x[k] = 1 + \frac{k}{2n} + \sin(\pi f_x k)
$$


A zero-phase filter with symmetric impulse response and unit DC gain passes a linear function unchanged, so the ideal infinite-record output is $1 + k/(2n) + |H(f_x)|^2 \sin(\pi f_x k)$.
The ideal causal output delays the linear function by DC group delay $\tau(0)$, multiplies the sinusoid's amplitude by $|H(f_x)|$, and shifts its phase by $\arg H(f_x)$.
For four treatments (`forward_backward` with fixed handling, no extension with zero initialization, odd-reflection extension with zero initialization, and causal `filter`), compare maximum differences from ideal output in the first and last 40 samples and the middle ($100 \le k < 300$).
The plot shows absolute differences from ideal output on a logarithmic axis (omitting exact-zero differences).

```rust
)
.and_then(|design| design.to_sos())
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let filter =
    IirFilter::from_sos(&sos, SignalExecutionOptions::new()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let trend = |k: f64| 1.0 + k / (2.0 * n as f64);
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|k| trend(k as f64) + (pi * tone * k as f64).sin())
    .collect();

// Ideal output (steady-state response for an infinite record). Evaluate H and τ(0) with SciOxide
let h = sos.response(tone).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let delay = sos.group_delay(0.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let zero_phase = |k: f64| trend(k) + h.norm_sqr() * (pi * tone * k).sin();
let causal = |k: f64| trend(k - delay) + h.norm() * (pi * tone * k + h.arg()).sin();

let padding = (3 * (filter.delays() + 1)).min(n - 1);
let steady = IirInitialization::SteadyState;
let odd_only = ForwardBackwardOptions::new().with_padding(IirPadding::Odd(padding));
let fixed = sos.forward_backward(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rest = filter
    .forward_backward_with_options(&x, ForwardBackwardOptions::new())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let padded = filter
    .forward_backward_with_options(&x, odd_only)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let forward = sos.filter(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Also check that fixed handling equals odd reflection combined with steady state
let explicit = filter
    .forward_backward_with_options(&x, odd_only.with_forward(steady).with_backward(steady))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let same = fixed
    .iter()
    .zip(&explicit)
    .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);

let edge = 40;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut series = Vec::new();
let runs: [(&str, &Vec<f64>, bool, usize); 4] = [
    ("forward_backward (fixed)", &fixed, true, padding),
    ("No extension, zero initialization", &rest, true, 0),
    ("Odd reflection, zero initialization", &padded, true, padding),
    ("Causal filter", &forward, false, 0),
];
for (name, y, symmetric, extension) in runs {
    let errors: Vec<f64> = y
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(k, &v)| {
            let ideal = if symmetric {
                zero_phase(k as f64)
            } else {
                causal(k as f64)
            };
            (v - ideal).abs()
        })
        .collect();
    // Take maximum errors over the first and last 40 samples and the middle 100 ≤ k < 300
    let largest = |part: &[f64]| part.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |m, &e| m.max(e));
    let (head, tail) = (largest(&errors[..edge]), largest(&errors[n - edge..]));
    rows.push((name, extension, head, tail, largest(&errors[100..300])));
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = errors
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .filter(|(_, e)| **e > 0.0)
        .map(|(k, &e)| [k as f64, e])
        .collect();
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points }));
}

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Treatment" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Extension [samples]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum error in first 40 samples [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum error in last 40 samples [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum error in middle [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ends" })
);
let summary = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "|H(f_x)|² [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "τ(0) [samples]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference between fixed and explicit settings [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    summary,
    vec![(h.norm_sqr(), delay, same)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "response" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Sample k [-]",
        "yLabel": "Difference from ideal output [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "yMin": 1e-17,
        "yMax": 10.0
    })
);
```

With the default $f_c = 0.1$ and $f_x = 0.05$, $|H(f_x)|^2 = 0.9963$, DC group delay is $\tau(0) = 8.25$ samples, and fixed extension is $\min(3 \cdot 5, 399) = 15$ samples.
Fixed `forward_backward` has maximum errors $1.8 \times 10^{-2}$ in the first 40 samples, $4.4 \times 10^{-2}$ in the last 40, and $4.8 \times 10^{-7}$ in the middle, the smallest of the four treatments.
It differs by exactly zero from passing explicit odd-reflection and steady-state settings to `forward_backward_with_options`.

Starting from zero without extension gives an endpoint error of 1.34.
The backward pass starts from zero delays at the record's final value (about 1.3).
Adding only odd-reflection extension lowers the initial error to $2.0 \times 10^{-2}$ but leaves 0.27 at the end; both extension and steady state are needed.
Causal `filter` reaches steady state at the end, with error $7 \times 10^{-15}$, but has a transient of 0.34 in the first 40 samples.
Every treatment's middle error is at most $3 \times 10^{-6}$, showing that zero-phase filtering preserves phase inside the record and multiplies amplitude by $|H|^2$.

With $f_x = f_c = 0.1$, $|H(f_x)|^2 = 0.5$ and the sine-wave amplitude halves (the Butterworth cutoff is the half-power point).
With $f_c = 0.03$, $\tau(0)$ increases to 27.7 samples, and even fixed handling leaves middle error $1.2 \times 10^{-2}$.
Filter poles determine transient length, so low-cutoff filters require records sufficiently longer than their transients.

## Summary

Apply IIR filters after converting to second-order sections with `IirDesign::to_sos()`; avoid computation with coefficients expanded into a high-order transfer function.
Use `SecondOrderSections::filter` for whole-record processing, or `IirFilter::filter_into` and `IirState` for chunks, carrying state between calls.
For offline processing that preserves phase, use `SecondOrderSections::forward_backward` and account during design for magnitude $|H|^2$.
To change endpoint treatment, select extension and initial state with `ForwardBackwardOptions` and check that the record is long enough relative to the transients.
See the [group-delay example](../../examples/iir-group-delay-zero-phase/) for differences in group delay and pulse distortion between families, and the [ECG example](../../examples/ecg-baseline-and-mains/) for application to biological signals.
