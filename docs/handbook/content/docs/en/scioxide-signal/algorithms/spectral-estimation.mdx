To determine how the power of a noisy record is distributed across frequencies, directly using the squared magnitude of its DFT gives highly variable estimates.
This page formulates power spectral density estimation as a stochastic-process problem and derives Welch's method for reducing variation by averaging, cross-spectra and coherence for measuring the relationship between two records, and Lomb–Scargle analysis for samples taken at irregular times.
Window measures follow [Windows and DPSS](../windows-and-dpss/), and frame conventions follow [STFT and reconstruction](../stft/).

## Power spectral density and the estimation problem

Let $R[\ell] = \mathrm{E}[x[n+\ell]\,\overline{x[n]}]$ be the autocorrelation of a zero-mean weakly stationary process $x[n]$ sampled at rate $f_s$.
The (two-sided) power spectral density (PSD) is

$$
S(f) = \frac{1}{f_s} \sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty} R[\ell]\, e^{-2\pi i f \ell / f_s}, \qquad -\frac{f_s}{2} \le f < \frac{f_s}{2}
$$

and satisfies $\int S(f)\, df = R[0]$ (the process variance).
Its units are squared signal units divided by frequency units (V²/Hz for voltage).
White noise with variance $\sigma^2$ has $S(f) = \sigma^2/f_s$; the one-sided density that places all power at positive frequencies for a real signal is $2\sigma^2/f_s$.

The periodogram formed from the DFT $X_k$ of a finite record $x[0], \dots, x[N-1]$ multiplied by a window $w$ is

$$
\hat S_k = \frac{|X_k|^2}{f_s \sum_n w[n]^2}
$$

and approaches an unbiased estimate of $S$ blurred by $|W|^2$ as $N$ grows.
For a Gaussian process, however, $\hat S_k$ at each bin is proportional to a $\chi^2$ distribution with 2 degrees of freedom, so its standard deviation equals its mean.
Lengthening the record only makes the bins finer; it does not reduce the variation in each bin.
Reducing variation requires averaging estimates that are nearly independent.

## Discrete power spectra and cross-spectra

`power_spectrum(input)` returns the discrete power spectrum $|X[k]|^2/n$ from the unnormalized DFT $X$ of a record of length $n$.
Bins follow natural DFT order; the result is not a density because it does not include the sample rate.
Parseval's relation gives $\sum_k |X[k]|^2/n = \sum_j |x[j]|^2$.
`cross_spectrum(a, b)` returns $A[k]\,\overline{B[k]}/n$; the phase of bin $k$ is the phase by which $a$ leads $b$ at that frequency.
Both use one FFT of length $n$ (two for the cross-spectrum); empty input gives `InvalidInput` (`Empty`), unequal lengths give `DimensionMismatch`, NaN or infinity gives `NonFinite`, and overflow gives `NumericalInstability`.
For repeated computations of the same length, `SpectralEstimatePlan::discrete(n)` prepares the same calculation.

## Welch's method

Welch's method divides a record into short overlapping segments and averages their periodograms.
For window length (segment length) $m$ and overlap $o$, a record of length $N \ge m$ has the following number of segments:

$$
K = 1 + \left\lfloor \frac{N - m}{m - o} \right\rfloor
$$


Segments start at sample 0; trailing samples that do not fit in the final segment are unused.
A trend (the mean by default) is subtracted from each segment, which is then windowed and transformed by an FFT of length $n \ge m$.
The segment estimate is $P_{xx} = |X|^2/S$, with scale $S$ defined in the next section.
Averaging segment estimates reduces variance by a factor of $1/K$ for independent, nonoverlapping segments.
Overlapping segments are correlated, so variance decreases more slowly.
For a Hann window with 50 % overlap, correlation with the adjacent segment is 16.7 %, giving an approximate variance factor of $(1 + 2 \cdot 0.167^2)/K$ for large $K$ (Harris, Table 1).

Shortening segments increases $K$ and reduces variance, but widens the bins $f_s/m$ and reduces resolution.
Tuning Welch's method involves this tradeoff between variance and resolution.

`SpectralAverage` selects how segment estimates are combined.
`Mean` (the default) takes the arithmetic mean, using compensated summation for each bin.
`Median` takes the median and corrects its bias by dividing by the median $\sum_{i=1}^{2J+1} (-1)^{i+1}/i$ ($J = \lfloor (K-1)/2 \rfloor$) of a unit-mean exponential distribution.
For even $K$, it averages the middle two values; for cross-spectra, it takes medians of the real and imaginary parts separately.
The median resists transient outliers affecting only some segments but has greater variance than the mean for Gaussian noise.

The one-shot call `welch(input, sample_rate, segment_len)` fixes three choices based on window length $m$.
It uses the periodic Hann window $w[j] = \sin^2(\pi j/m)$, an overlap of $\lfloor m/2 \rfloor$ samples, and one-sided packing for real samples or full packing for complex samples.
Other settings use the defaults of `SpectralEstimateOptions` (FFT length $m$, density scaling, mean removal, and arithmetic averaging).
A zero `segment_len` gives `InvalidInput` (`Empty`); a value at least $2^{52}$ or a nonpositive `sample_rate` gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`); a record shorter than one segment gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).
The cost is one FFT of length $m$ per segment.

## Density and spectrum units

`SpectralScaling` selects the scale $S$.

- `Density` (the default): $S = f_s \sum w^2$, a power spectral density with units of squared signal units per frequency unit.
  Multiplying all bins by the bin width $f_s/n$ and summing gives the average of $\sum_j (w[j] x[j])^2 / \sum_j w[j]^2$ (the window-weighted mean square) over segments.
- `Spectrum`: $S = (\sum w)^2$, a power spectrum with units of squared signal units.
  The one-sided peak height of a sinusoid of amplitude $A$ centered on a bin is its mean power $A^2/2$.

One-sided packing (`StftPacking::OneSided`) doubles all bins except DC and Nyquist (when $n$ is even), gathering negative-frequency power on the positive side.
Use density for power spread over a band, such as noise, and spectrum for line-spectrum amplitudes.
When reading a noise floor, density keeps its value independent of segment length.
Spectrum scaling multiplies the noise floor by ENBW $\cdot f_s/n$, making it depend on segment length.

## Estimation plans and workspace

`SpectralEstimatePlan::new(window, options)` accepts a real window and options and uses the window length as the segment length.
The defaults for `SpectralEstimateOptions` are zero overlap, FFT length equal to window length, sample rate 1, `StftPacking::Full`, `SpectralScaling::Density`, detrending `Some(DetrendKind::Constant)`, `SpectralAverage::Mean`, and sequential execution.
Configuration methods do not validate values; `new` returns `InvalidInput` for overlap at least the window length, an FFT length shorter than the window, or a nonpositive sample rate.
One-sided packing with a complex type gives `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`); a zero-sum window under `Spectrum` or an all-zero window under `Density` gives `DomainError`.

The plan is immutable and provides `power`, `csd`, and `coherence` APIs returning owned values, and `power_into`, `csd_into`, and `coherence_into` APIs writing to caller-provided outputs.
`bins()` returns the number of bins, `frequency(k)` returns the frequency of bin $k$ (cycles per unit time), and `segments(N)` returns the number of segments $K$.
Allocate workspace with `workspace(SignalLayout::Contiguous(N))`; one size serves all statistics.
Workspace for `Mean` is independent of segment count, while workspace for `Median` is proportional to it.
Estimation along one array axis with `power_axis_into`, `csd_axis_into`, or `coherence_axis_into` replaces the sample axis with a bin axis.

Records are rescaled by a power of 2 before calculation when needed to avoid premature overflow or underflow of estimates.
Each segment spectrum has the usual FFT normwise error; averaging $K$ segments adds an error at most $\gamma_{8K+32} M/K + (8K + 32)\eta$.
Here, $M$ is the sum of absolute products forming segment estimates, and $\eta$ is the smallest positive subnormal.

The following cell forms a whole-record periodogram and a Welch estimate from a 100 Hz sinusoid of amplitude 1 V plus white noise, recorded for 8.192 s at 1000 Hz.
Both use periodic Hann windows, one-sided packing, and density scaling.
In the 200–450 Hz noise band, compare the mean estimate with the theoretical noise floor $2\sigma^2/f_s$ and the relative standard deviation with the variance approximation above.
Estimate sinusoid power by subtracting the noise floor from the density within 4 bins on either side of 100 Hz and integrating, then compare with $A^2/2 = 0.5$ V².
The Parseval column is the relative difference between integrated density and window-weighted mean square.

```rust
//| id: signal-welch-variance
//| ref: signal-welch-variance
//| title: "Compare noise floors and variation in the periodogram and Welch's method"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   segment_len: {"type":"select","label":"Welch segment length m [samples]","options":["64","256","1024"],"value":"256"}
//|   median_average: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Average with the median","description":"Use SpectralAverage::Median.","value":false}
//|   noise_sigma: {"type":"range","label":"Noise standard deviation σ [V]","description":"White noise magnitude.","min":0.1,"max":3,"step":0.1,"value":1}
//|   seed: {"type":"integer","label":"Random seed","description":"Change the noise sequence.","min":1,"max":9999,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"short-segments","label":"m = 64","description":"More segments reduce variance, but the bin width increases to 15.6 Hz.","inputs":{"segment_len":"64"}}
//|   - {"id":"median","label":"Median","description":"Median bias correction aligns the noise floor, but variance exceeds that of the mean.","inputs":{"median_average":true}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":63}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/6","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/7","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: median
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/7","equals":null}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_signal::{
    SpectralAverage, SpectralEstimateOptions, SpectralEstimatePlan, StftPacking, WindowKind,
    WindowNormalization, WindowSymmetry, window,
};

let (fs, len, f0, amplitude) = (1000.0_f64, 8192_usize, 100.0_f64, 1.0_f64);
// Noise: splitmix64 and Box–Muller (converted with inherent f64 methods)
let mut state = seed as u64;
let mut uniform = || -> f64 {
    state = state.wrapping_add(0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15);
    let mut z = state;
    z = (z ^ (z >> 30)).wrapping_mul(0xBF58_476D_1CE4_E5B9);
    z = (z ^ (z >> 27)).wrapping_mul(0x94D0_49BB_1331_11EB);
    ((z ^ (z >> 31)) >> 11) as f64 / (1u64 << 53) as f64 + 0.5 / (1u64 << 53) as f64
};
let tau = 2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI;
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..len)
    .map(|i| {
        let gauss = (-2.0 * uniform().ln()).sqrt() * (tau * uniform()).cos();
        amplitude * RealScalar::sin(tau * f0 * i as f64 / fs) + noise_sigma * gauss
    })
    .collect();
let floor = 2.0 * noise_sigma * noise_sigma / fs;
let m: usize = segment_len.parse().map_err(|_| "Segment length")?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut curves = Vec::new();
for (name, seg, overlap) in [("Periodogram", len, 0), ("Welch's method", m, m / 2)] {
    let w = window::<f64>(WindowKind::Hann, seg, WindowSymmetry::Periodic, WindowNormalization::Native)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let average = if median_average { SpectralAverage::Median } else { SpectralAverage::Mean };
    let options = SpectralEstimateOptions::new()
        .with_sample_rate(fs)
        .with_overlap(overlap)
        .with_packing(StftPacking::OneSided)
        .with_average(average);
    let plan = SpectralEstimatePlan::new(&w, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let density = plan.power(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let segments = plan.segments(len).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let df = fs / seg as f64;
    // Mean and relative standard deviation in the 200–450 Hz noise band
    let band: Vec<f64> = density
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .filter(|(k, _)| (200.0..=450.0).contains(&(*k as f64 * df)))
        .map(|(_, &p)| p)
        .collect();
    let mean = band.iter().sum::<f64>() / band.len() as f64;
    let spread = (band.iter().map(|p| (p - mean).powi(2)).sum::<f64>() / band.len() as f64).sqrt();
    let correlation = if overlap > 0 { 0.167_f64 } else { 0.0 };
    let theory = ((1.0 + 2.0 * (1.0 - 1.0 / segments as f64) * correlation.powi(2)) / segments as f64).sqrt();
    // Sinusoid power: subtract the noise floor and integrate within 4 bins on either side of 100 Hz
    let center = (f0 / df).round() as usize;
    let line: f64 = density[center - 4..=center + 4].iter().map(|p| (p - mean) * df).sum();
    // Independent check (mean only): integrated density and window-weighted mean square (averaged over segments)
    let parseval = (!median_average).then(|| {
        let energy: f64 = w.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum();
        let step = seg - overlap;
        let weighted: f64 = (0..segments)
            .map(|s| {
                let segment = &x[s * step..s * step + seg];
                let mean_x = segment.iter().sum::<f64>() / seg as f64;
                segment.iter().zip(&w).map(|(v, c)| ((v - mean_x) * c).powi(2)).sum::<f64>()
            })
            .sum::<f64>()
            / (segments as f64 * energy);
        let integral = density.iter().sum::<f64>() * df;
        (integral - weighted).abs() / weighted
    });
    rows.push((name, segments, df, mean / floor, spread / mean, theory, line, parseval));
    let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = density
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .filter(|(_, p)| **p > 0.0)
        .map(|(k, &p)| [k as f64 * df, p])
        .collect();
    curves.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points }));
}

let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Estimate" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Number of segments K", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Bin width [Hz]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Mean noise floor / 2σ²/f_s [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative standard deviation of noise floor [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Variance approximation [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Sinusoid power [V²]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Parseval relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "Not applicable" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "estimates" }));
emit_line_chart!(
    &curves,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Frequency f [Hz]",
        "yLabel": "One-sided power spectral density [V²/Hz]",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 500.0,
        "yMin": 1e-7,
        "yMax": 10.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": floor, "label": "Theoretical noise floor 2σ²/f_s" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": f0, "label": "100 Hz" }
        ]
    })
);
```

With defaults $\sigma = 1$ V, $m = 256$, and seed 3, the periodogram ($K = 1$, bin width 0.122 Hz) has a noise floor 1.021 times the theoretical value and a relative standard deviation of 0.965.
The variation is about as large as the mean, as expected from the $\chi^2_2$ distribution above.
Welch's method ($K = 63$, bin width 3.906 Hz) gives a noise floor ratio of 0.990 and reduces the relative standard deviation to 0.105.
The variance approximation gives 0.129; changing the seed from 1 to 8 yields relative standard deviations ranging from 0.101 to 0.142.
The standard deviation itself, estimated from 64 bins in the band, has statistical variation of about 10 %, so this difference is within the estimation uncertainty.

At $m = 64$, $K = 255$ and the relative standard deviation falls to 0.041 (approximation 0.064), but the bin width increases to 15.6 Hz.
Here, the band contains only 16 bins, so the standard deviation estimate itself varies substantially.
At $m = 1024$, $K = 15$ and the relative standard deviation is 0.240 (approximation 0.265).
The sinusoid power is 0.515 V² from the periodogram and 0.511 V² from Welch's method, close to $A^2/2 = 0.5$ V².
The relative difference between integrated density and window-weighted mean square is at most $4.0 \times 10^{-15}$, so Parseval's relation holds to within rounding error.

With the median, the noise floor remains close to theory at a ratio of 0.988, but the relative standard deviation increases to 0.150.
Sinusoid power is overestimated at 0.750 V².
The median correction divisor (about 0.70 for $K = 63$) assumes that segment estimates follow an exponential distribution, as noise does.
For a line spectrum with nearly the same value in every segment, the correction inflates the value by about 1/0.70.
Changing σ leaves the noise floor ratio and relative standard deviation unchanged; only the variation in sinusoid power grows with σ.

## Cross-spectra and coherence

For DFTs $X$ and $Y$ of matching segments of records $x$ and $y$, the cross-spectral estimate is the mean $P_{xy}$ of segment values $X\overline{Y}/S$.
`csd` returns this estimate; its phase is the phase by which $x$ leads $y$ at that frequency.
If $y$ is $x$ delayed by $D$ samples, the phase of $P_{xy}$ is $2\pi f D/f_s$, proportional to frequency.

The magnitude-squared coherence is

$$
\hat\gamma^2(f) = \frac{|P_{xy}(f)|^2}{P_{xx}(f)\, P_{yy}(f)}
$$

and measures, at each frequency, the fraction of power in $y$ linearly related to $x$; the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality places it in $[0, 1]$.
With only one segment, $|X\overline Y|^2 = |X|^2 |Y|^2$, so $\hat\gamma^2 = 1$ for any records.
This is a property of the estimate and does not indicate a relationship between the records.
Even for independent records ($\gamma^2 = 0$), averaging $K$ independent segments gives a positive bias of about $1/K$.

`coherence` is computed from arithmetic-mean estimates, so a plan using `SpectralAverage::Median` returns `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).
Values exceeding 1 by at most $64\varepsilon$ due to rounding are set to 1; a larger excess gives `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`), while zero $P_{xx}$ or $P_{yy}$ at any bin gives `DomainError` (`Undefined`).
Multiplying either record by a power of 2 leaves coherence unchanged.

The following cell creates $x = s + n_1$ by adding independent noise (standard deviation $\sigma$) to unit-variance white noise $s$, and $y = s[\cdot - D] + n_2$ by delaying $s$ by $D$ samples and adding separate noise (sample rate 1).
The true coherence is then $\gamma^2 = 1/(1 + \sigma^2)^2$ at all frequencies.
Vary segment length from 4096 (one segment) down to 64 and tabulate mean coherence and delay $\hat D$ estimated from the cross-spectrum phase slope.
The approximate expectation column assumes $K$ independent segments and uses $\mathrm{E}[\hat\gamma^2] \approx \gamma^2 + (1 - \gamma^2)^2/K$ (Carter et al.'s approximation) as an independent check.
Estimate $\hat D$ by dividing the adjacent-bin phase difference $\arg \sum_k P_{xy}[k+1]\overline{P_{xy}[k]}$ by $2\pi/m$, avoiding phase unwrapping.

```rust
//| id: signal-coherence-delay
//| ref: signal-coherence-delay
//| title: "Read coherence bias and delay from cross-spectrum phase"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   noise_sigma: {"type":"range","label":"Independent noise standard deviation σ [-]","description":"Determines the true coherence 1/(1 + σ²)².","min":0,"max":2,"step":0.1,"value":1}
//|   delay: {"type":"range","label":"Delay D [samples]","description":"Number of samples by which y lags x.","min":0,"max":24,"step":1,"value":5}
//|   seed: {"type":"integer","label":"Random seed","description":"Change the noise sequence.","min":1,"max":9999,"value":17}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"independent","label":"σ = 2","description":"True coherence is 0.04; estimates with few segments are biased upward.","inputs":{"noise_sigma":2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coherence","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"coherence","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coherence","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":0.25,"atol":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"coherence","path":"/rows/3/4","approx":{"value":5.0,"atol":0.3}}
use scioxide_signal::{
    SpectralEstimateOptions, SpectralEstimatePlan, StftPacking, WindowKind, WindowNormalization,
    WindowSymmetry, window,
};

let len = 4096_usize;
let d = delay.round() as usize;
let mut state = seed as u64;
let mut gauss = || -> f64 {
    let mut uniform = || {
        state = state.wrapping_add(0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15);
        let mut z = state;
        z = (z ^ (z >> 30)).wrapping_mul(0xBF58_476D_1CE4_E5B9);
        z = (z ^ (z >> 27)).wrapping_mul(0x94D0_49BB_1331_11EB);
        ((z ^ (z >> 31)) >> 11) as f64 / (1u64 << 53) as f64 + 0.5 / (1u64 << 53) as f64
    };
    // Box–Muller (inherent f64 methods)
    (-2.0 * uniform().ln()).sqrt() * (2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI * uniform()).cos()
};
let source: Vec<f64> = (0..len + d).map(|_| gauss()).collect();
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..len).map(|i| source[i + d] + noise_sigma * gauss()).collect();
let y: Vec<f64> = (0..len).map(|i| source[i] + noise_sigma * gauss()).collect();
let truth = 1.0 / (1.0 + noise_sigma * noise_sigma).powi(2);

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for m in [4096_usize, 1024, 256, 64] {
    let w = window::<f64>(WindowKind::Hann, m, WindowSymmetry::Periodic, WindowNormalization::Native)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let overlap = if m == len { 0 } else { m / 2 };
    let options = SpectralEstimateOptions::new()
        .with_overlap(overlap)
        .with_packing(StftPacking::OneSided);
    let plan = SpectralEstimatePlan::new(&w, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let coherence = plan.coherence(&x, &y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let cross = plan.csd(&x, &y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let segments = plan.segments(len).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Mean over bins excluding DC and Nyquist
    let inner = &coherence[1..coherence.len() - 1];
    let mean = inner.iter().sum::<f64>() / inner.len() as f64;
    // Read delay from the mean phase difference between adjacent bins (phase slope 2πD/m)
    let turn = cross.windows(2).fold(scioxide_core::Complex::new(0.0, 0.0), |acc, pair| {
        acc + pair[1] * pair[0].conj()
    });
    let estimate = turn.im.atan2(turn.re) * m as f64 / (2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI);
    // Approximate expectation γ² + (1 − γ²)²/K assuming independent segments (the estimate is identically 1 for K = 1)
    let expected = (segments > 1).then(|| truth + (1.0 - truth).powi(2) / segments as f64);
    rows.push((m, segments, mean, expected, estimate));
}
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Segment length m [samples]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Number of segments K", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Mean coherence [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Approximate expectation [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "Not applicable" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Delay D̂ from phase [samples]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "coherence" }));
```

For $K = 7$, 31, and 127, mean coherence approaches the true value at 0.344, 0.278, and 0.256, close to the approximate expectations 0.330, 0.268, and 0.254.
The delay estimates are 5.23, 4.68, and 4.85 samples.
Correlation between adjacent noise bins contributes toward zero phase, slightly biasing the delay estimate toward 0.

For $\sigma = 2$ (true value 0.04), the means are 0.185, 0.080, and 0.057, greater than the approximate expectations 0.161, 0.067, and 0.047.
Overlapping segments are not independent, so the effective segment count is less than $K$.
Even with $\sigma = 0$, the estimate for $m = 64$ reaches only 0.927.
When delay $D$ is not negligible relative to segment length, segments of $x$ and $y$ cover different portions, reducing coherence.
At $D = 24$, coherence for $m = 64$ falls to 0.045, but the delay estimate is 23.07 samples and the phase slope remains readable.

## Irregular samples and Lomb–Scargle analysis

When sample times $t_i$ are irregular, as in astronomical observations or clinical records, DFT bins lose their orthogonality and the periodogram cannot be defined.
Lomb–Scargle analysis fits a sinusoid by least squares at each angular frequency $\omega$ and takes the reduction in residual as power.

For weights $w_i \ge 0$ (1 by default) and angular frequency $\omega$, fit the following by weighted least squares:

$$
x_i \approx c + a \cos(\omega t_i) + b \sin(\omega t_i)
$$


With normalized weights $\hat w_i = w_i / \sum_j w_j$, let $R_0 = \sum_i \hat w_i r_i^2$ be the baseline-only residual and $R(\omega)$ the residual after adding the sinusoid.
`LombScargleMean::Floating` (the default) also fits the constant $c$ at each frequency (the Zechmeister–Kürster generalized periodogram), while `LombScargleMean::Zero` sets $c = 0$.
`LombScargleNormalization` selects between two quantities.

- `Normalized` (the default): $(R_0 - R(\omega))/R_0 \in [0, 1]$, the fraction of the residual explained by the sinusoid, independent of record scale.
- `Power`: $(N/2)(R_0 - R(\omega))$, half the sample count times the reduction in mean squared residual, with units of squared signal units.

Neither is a density per unit frequency.
Unit weights, `Zero`, and `Power` give the classical Lomb and Scargle periodogram.
At equally spaced times $t_j = j/f_s$ and Fourier frequencies $\omega_k = 2\pi k f_s/N$ ($0 < k < N/2$), the cosine and sine columns are orthogonal and both have squared norm $N/2$, so the classical periodogram equals the discrete power spectrum $|X_k|^2/N$.

Pass frequencies as angular frequencies (radians per unit time).
These differ by a factor of $2\pi$ from Welch and STFT frequencies (cycles per unit time), so use $\omega = 2\pi/P$ when searching for a period $P$.
Times need not be sorted, and multiplying all weights by a positive constant leaves the result unchanged.

At each frequency, the cosine and sine columns are orthogonalized against the baseline and each other with reorthogonalization; a column whose weighted norm is at most the tolerance from `LombScargleOptions::with_tolerance` (default $\sqrt\varepsilon$) does not contribute to power.
For $N$ samples and $M$ frequencies, the cost is $O(NM)$, evaluating sine and cosine once each per sample–frequency pair.
The inner-product error for $k$ terms is at most $\gamma_{2k+8} \sum |a_i b_i| + (2k+8)\eta$, but no relative error bound holds where power is zero or the fit changes rank.

The one-shot call `lomb_scargle(times, input, frequencies)` calls `LombScargle::new` and then `power` with the defaults of unit weights, `Floating`, and `Normalized`.
For repeated calculations at the same times and frequencies, create the method object `LombScargle::new(times, frequencies, weights, options)`.
`LombScargle` only borrows and checks times, frequencies, and weights; it performs no computation during construction.
`power_into` uses workspace from `workspace(1)` ($4N + M$ values); `power_axis_into` along an array axis uses `workspace(lanes)` with the lane count.

The failure modes are as follows.
Empty times or frequencies give `InvalidInput` (`Empty`); fewer than 3 positively weighted samples under `Floating` (fewer than 2 under `Zero`), nonpositive frequencies, or negative weights give `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
Unequal input lengths give `DimensionMismatch`, and NaN or infinity gives `NonFinite`.
Under `Normalized`, $R_0 = 0$ (zero input, or constant input under `Floating`) gives `DomainError` (`Undefined`).
A fit explaining more than $(1 + 128\varepsilon) R_0$ gives `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`); overflow in the phase $\omega(t_i - t_0)$ or power gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).

The following cell compares Lomb–Scargle power using the classical combination (`Zero`, `Power`) with `power_spectrum` at Fourier frequencies for an equally spaced random record.
Compare the default combination (`Floating`, `Normalized`) with the `Power` value divided by $(N/2) R_0$.
Both comparisons check identities derived above.

```rust
//| id: signal-lomb-scargle-uniform
//| ref: signal-lomb-scargle-uniform
//| title: "Compare Lomb–Scargle analysis with the discrete power spectrum for equally spaced samples"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   record_len: {"type":"range","label":"Sample count N","description":"Length of the equally spaced record.","min":16,"max":512,"step":16,"value":128}
//|   seed: {"type":"integer","label":"Random seed","description":"Change the record.","min":1,"max":9999,"value":23}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"uniform","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"uniform","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_signal::{
    LombScargle, LombScargleMean, LombScargleNormalization, LombScargleOptions, lomb_scargle,
    power_spectrum,
};

let (n, fs) = (record_len.round() as usize, 10.0_f64);
let mut state = seed as u64;
let mut uniform = || -> f64 {
    state = state.wrapping_add(0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15);
    let mut z = state;
    z = (z ^ (z >> 30)).wrapping_mul(0xBF58_476D_1CE4_E5B9);
    z = (z ^ (z >> 27)).wrapping_mul(0x94D0_49BB_1331_11EB);
    ((z ^ (z >> 31)) >> 11) as f64 / (1u64 << 53) as f64 - 0.5
};
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..n).map(|_| uniform()).collect();
let times: Vec<f64> = (0..n).map(|j| j as f64 / fs).collect();
// Pass Fourier frequencies ω_k = 2π k f_s / N (0 < k < N/2) as angular frequencies
let omegas: Vec<f64> = (1..n / 2)
    .map(|k| 2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI * k as f64 * fs / n as f64)
    .collect();
let classical = LombScargleOptions::new()
    .with_mean(LombScargleMean::Zero)
    .with_normalization(LombScargleNormalization::Power);
let power = LombScargle::new(&times, &omegas, None, classical)
    .and_then(|p| p.power(&x))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let discrete = power_spectrum(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let classical_gap = power
    .iter()
    .zip(&discrete[1..n / 2])
    .map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs() / b)
    .fold(0.0, f64::max);

// Default combination (floating mean, normalized) and floating-mean Power divided by (N/2)R₀
let normalized = lomb_scargle(&times, &x, &omegas).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let floating = LombScargleOptions::new().with_normalization(LombScargleNormalization::Power);
let floating_power = LombScargle::new(&times, &omegas, None, floating)
    .and_then(|p| p.power(&x))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mean = x.iter().sum::<f64>() / n as f64;
let baseline = x.iter().map(|v| (v - mean).powi(2)).sum::<f64>() / n as f64;
let normalized_gap = normalized
    .iter()
    .zip(&floating_power)
    .map(|(z, p)| (z - p / (0.5 * n as f64 * baseline)).abs())
    .fold(0.0, f64::max);

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Comparison" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let rows = vec![
    ("Relative difference between classical combination and |X_k|²/N", classical_gap),
    ("Difference between Normalized and Power/((N/2)R₀)", normalized_gap),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "uniform" }));
```

With the default $N = 128$, the maximum relative difference between the classical combination and $|X_k|^2/N$ is $1.2 \times 10^{-13}$, and the difference between `Normalized` and $\mathrm{Power}/((N/2)R_0)$ is $4.2 \times 10^{-17}$.
At $N = 512$, the relative difference grows to $6.6 \times 10^{-13}$, consistent with rounding error from the larger number of inner-product terms and larger phase $\omega t_j$.
This agreement connects the `Power` scale and angular-frequency units to the equally spaced conventions.

## Summary

In PSD estimation, a single periodogram has a standard deviation as large as its mean, so averaging segments reduces variation at the cost of resolution.
Choose `SpectralScaling::Density` for noise floors and `Spectrum` for line-spectrum amplitudes; remember that one-sided packing adds negative-frequency power to the positive side.
`welch` fixes the periodic Hann window, half overlap, and mean removal; use `SpectralEstimatePlan` for other settings.
Coherence is always 1 with one segment and has bias of about $1/K$ even with $K$ segments, so interpret it together with the segment count.
For irregular samples, use `lomb_scargle` with angular frequencies; agreement with the discrete power spectrum for equally spaced samples checks the conventions.
See [STFT and reconstruction](../stft/) for spectra changing over time and [Windows and DPSS](../windows-and-dpss/) for choosing windows.
