For a signal whose frequencies change over time, the DFT of the whole record shows which frequencies were present but loses when they occurred.
The short-time Fourier transform (STFT) retains both time and frequency information by shifting a window in small steps and arranging the DFTs of successive intervals.
This page states the definition and conventions of the STFT and derives the conditions for synthesis that recovers the original signal from its coefficients.
The properties of windows themselves are covered in [Windows and DPSS](../windows-and-dpss/), and power estimation in [Spectral estimation](../spectral-estimation/).

## STFT definition and frames

Choose a window $w$ of length $m$, a hop $h$ (the difference between the starting positions of adjacent frames), and an FFT length $n \ge m$.
The coefficients of frame $p$ are

$$
X[p, k] = c_k \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} x[ph - \lfloor m/2 \rfloor + j]\, \overline{w[j]}\, e^{-2\pi i jk/n}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, n - 1
$$


Here, $c_k$ scales the coefficients, and $\overline{w}$ denotes the complex conjugate, which equals $w$ itself for a real window.
Frame $p$ covers $m$ samples starting at sample $ph - \lfloor m/2 \rfloor$, and its center is at sample $ph$.
Phase is referenced to the start of the frame.
When $n > m$, each frame is extended with zeros to $n$ points before the FFT, sampling the spectrum on a finer grid.
Zero padding does not change the frequency resolution.

Values outside the record are determined by the `SignalBoundary` passed to `StftOptions::with_boundary`.
`Zero` (the default) uses 0, `Periodic` repeats with period $N$, and `Reflect` reflects without repeating the endpoints.
Boundary extension is used only for analysis; synthesis returns only the original record of length $N$.

`StftPlan::frames(N)` returns a `StftFrames` describing the range of frames whose windows overlap any part of the record of length $N$.
The first frame number, `first()`, is $\lfloor(\lfloor m/2 \rfloor - m)/h\rfloor + 1 \le 0$, so the range includes frames that start before the beginning of the record.
`count()` is the number of frames, and `signal_len()` is the record length.
`frame_time(p)` returns the time $ph/f_s$ at the frame center, and `frame_sample_range(p)` returns the half-open interval of covered samples.
Coefficients are arranged in groups of `bins()` per frame, with `output_len(N)` coefficients in total.
The computation requires one FFT of length $n$ per frame.

## Bin ordering and coefficient scaling

For sample rate $f_s$, the frequency of bin $k$ is $k f_s/n$ when $k < \lceil n/2 \rceil$, and $(k - n) f_s/n$ otherwise, in cycles per unit time.
`StftPacking` selects the bin ordering within each frame.

- `Full`: the natural DFT order $k = 0, \dots, n-1$, with negative frequencies in the second half.
- `Centered`: ascending frequency order, with frequency 0 at bin $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$.
- `OneSided`: the $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor + 1$ nonnegative-frequency bins, available only for real sample types.

`StftScaling` selects the scale $c_k$, which synthesis reverses.

- `Raw` (the default): $c_k = 1$, the unnormalized DFT of the windowed frame.
- `Magnitude`: $c_k = 1/|\sum_j w[j]|$, giving a coefficient magnitude of 1 for a unit-amplitude complex exponential at a bin frequency.
  A real sinusoid $A\cos(2\pi f t)$ is split between positive and negative frequencies, so the magnitude at each bin is $A/2$.
- `Psd`: $c_k = 1/\sqrt{f_s \sum_j |w[j]|^2}$, making $|X|^2$ a power spectral density.
  For one-sided packing, bins other than DC and the Nyquist frequency (when $n$ is even) receive an additional factor of $\sqrt 2$.

`spectrogram` and `spectrogram_into` return the scaled $|X[p, k]|^2$, while `cross_spectrogram_into` returns $X[p,k]\,\overline{Y[p,k]}$.
In general, the original signal cannot be recovered from a spectrogram that discards phase.

## Synthesis with a dual window

Synthesis takes the inverse FFT of each frame, reverses the scale $c_k$, multiplies by a synthesis window $d$, and sums the results (overlap-add).
The first $m$ samples of the inverse FFT of frame $p$ are $x[ph - \lfloor m/2 \rfloor + j]\,\overline{w[j]}$, so all frames covering sample $t$ contribute to sample $t$ as follows:

$$
\hat x[t] = \sum_p x[t]\, \overline{w[t - ph + \lfloor m/2 \rfloor]}\, d[t - ph + \lfloor m/2 \rfloor]
$$


For fixed $t$, the remainder $r = j \bmod h$ of the position $j = t - ph + \lfloor m/2 \rfloor$ within the window modulo $h$ is independent of $p$.
Therefore, $\hat x = x$ holds if the following condition holds for each remainder $r = 0, \dots, h-1$:

$$
\sum_{q} \overline{w[r + qh]}\, d[r + qh] = 1
$$


A $d$ satisfying this condition is called a dual window of the window $w$ with hop $h$.
Because the condition is one independent linear equation per remainder, synthesis avoids solving a system over the entire record (a compactly supported “painless” dual, Daubechies–Grossmann–Meyer).

## NOLA and COLA

Let $E_r = \sum_q |w[r + qh]|^2$ be the window energy for remainder $r$.
A dual window exists if and only if $E_r > 0$ for every $r$.
This condition is called NOLA (nonzero overlap-add).
For a remainder with $E_r = 0$, samples at those positions have no weight in any frame and cannot be recovered from the coefficients.

COLA (constant overlap-add), in turn, requires the sum $S_r = \sum_q w[r + qh]$ for each remainder to be constant, independent of $r$.
When COLA holds, simply adding the windowed frames without a synthesis window recovers the record up to a constant factor.
COLA is not required for synthesis with a dual window.
For example, a symmetric Hann window does not satisfy COLA but satisfies NOLA, so its canonical dual can reconstruct the signal exactly.

`check_overlap(window, hop, tolerance)` divides the window by its maximum absolute value, tests the two conditions using $S_r$ and $E_r$, and returns `OverlapDiagnostics`.
COLA is tested using $\max_r |S_r - S_0| \le \mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol} \cdot \max_r |S_r|$, and NOLA using $\min_r E_r / \max_r E_r > \mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}$.
`energy_ratio()` returns this ratio $\min_r E_r / \max_r E_r$; smaller ratios mean synthesis amplifies coefficient errors more strongly.
Neither condition holds when the hop exceeds the window length or when the window is all zeros.
The cost is $O(m)$; the tests are independent of window scale and provide no error bound.

The cosine sum $\sum_{j=0}^{J} (-1)^j a_j \cos(2\pi j i/m)$ for a periodic window satisfies COLA when $m$ is divisible by $h$ and $m/h > J$.
Summing over $q$ makes the terms with $j \ge 1$ sums of the $m/h$th roots of unity, which vanish.
The Hann window ($J = 1$) satisfies COLA at $h = m/2$, and the four-term Blackman–Harris window ($J = 3$) at $h = m/4$.

## Choosing a dual window

Because the dual condition is one equation per remainder, there are generally infinitely many dual windows under NOLA.
scioxide-signal computes the following three in closed form, each in $O(m)$ for window length $m$.

- The canonical dual `canonical_stft_dual(window, hop, tolerance)` is the dual $d[j] = w[j]/E_r$ ($r = j \bmod h$) with minimum Euclidean norm.
  It follows by minimizing $\sum_q |d|^2$ for each remainder using Lagrange multipliers.
  This synthesis gives the signal whose STFT is closest to the modified coefficients $Y$ (the $x$ minimizing $\sum_{p,k} |X[p,k] - Y[p,k]|^2$), the Griffin–Lim least-squares estimate.
  `StftPlan::new` prepares this dual when NOLA holds; otherwise, it creates an analysis-only plan whose `dual_window()` returns `None`.
- The closest dual `closest_stft_dual(window, hop, desired, scale, tolerance)` is the dual closest in Euclidean norm to the desired synthesis window `desired`.
  `DualScale::Fixed` orthogonally projects onto the set of all duals; `DualScale::Optimized` also chooses a constant scale for the analysis window, returned by `ClosestStftDual::window_scale()`.
  For synthesis, pair the analysis window multiplied by `window_scale()` with `dual()`.
- The tight window `tight_stft_window(window, hop, tolerance)` is $g[j] = w[j]/\sqrt{E_r}$ and is its own dual.
  The same window can be used for analysis and synthesis, and coefficient energy is proportional to signal energy.

Pass a pair of your own choosing to `StftPlan::from_windows(window, dual, hop, options)`; use `StftPlan::from_dual` to specify only the synthesis window and make the analysis window its canonical dual.
Both check that the pair satisfies the dual condition within tolerance and return `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`) if it does not.
If a computed dual fails its own condition, the result is `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`), indicating loss of significance due to rounding.

The following cell selects a Hann window of length 64 and a hop, analyzes a random record of length 1024 with one-sided packing, and synthesizes it with three pairs.
These use the canonical dual, the dual closest to an all-ones synthesis window (`DualScale::Fixed`), and the tight window.
The first two use the same analysis window and therefore have the same coefficients.
Compare the error from synthesizing the original coefficients with the error after adding complex noise of standard deviation $\delta$ to the coefficients.
Dividing the error with noise by $\delta$ measures how strongly synthesis amplifies coefficient errors.
The residual $\max_r |\sum_q \overline{w} d - 1|$ of the dual condition is recomputed by direct summation before display.

```rust
//| id: signal-stft-dual-reconstruction
//| ref: signal-stft-dual-reconstruction
//| title: "Synthesize an STFT with canonical and closest duals and a tight window"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   hop: {"type":"range","label":"Hop h [samples]","description":"Frame spacing for a window of length 64.","min":4,"max":56,"step":4,"value":16}
//|   symmetric_window: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Use a symmetric Hann window","description":"Check that synthesis is possible even with a window that fails COLA.","value":true}
//|   noise_level: {"type":"range","label":"Coefficient noise δ [-]","description":"Standard deviation of the noise added to the real and imaginary parts of the coefficients.","min":0.001,"max":0.1,"step":0.001,"value":0.01}
//|   seed: {"type":"integer","label":"Random seed","description":"Change the record and noise sequences.","min":1,"max":9999,"value":11}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"periodic-half","label":"Periodic window, h = 32","description":"For the periodic Hann window satisfying COLA, the canonical dual is a constant multiple of the window.","inputs":{"symmetric_window":false,"hop":32}}
//|   - {"id":"sparse-hop","label":"h = 56","description":"Small overlap and a small E_r ratio strongly amplify noise.","inputs":{"hop":56}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: sparse-hop
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"duals","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-10}}
use scioxide_core::{Complex, Tolerance};
use scioxide_signal::{
    DualScale, StftOptions, StftPacking, StftPlan, WindowKind, WindowNormalization,
    WindowSymmetry, closest_stft_dual, tight_stft_window, window,
};

let (m, h, len) = (64_usize, hop.round() as usize, 1024_usize);
let symmetry = if symmetric_window {
    WindowSymmetry::Symmetric
} else {
    WindowSymmetry::Periodic
};
let w = window::<f64>(WindowKind::Hann, m, symmetry, WindowNormalization::Native)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Record and noise: uniform random values from splitmix64 (converted with inherent f64 methods)
let mut state = seed as u64;
let mut uniform = || -> f64 {
    state = state.wrapping_add(0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15);
    let mut z = state;
    z = (z ^ (z >> 30)).wrapping_mul(0xBF58_476D_1CE4_E5B9);
    z = (z ^ (z >> 27)).wrapping_mul(0x94D0_49BB_1331_11EB);
    ((z ^ (z >> 31)) >> 11) as f64 / (1u64 << 53) as f64 - 0.5
};
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..len).map(|_| uniform()).collect();
let tolerance = Tolerance::from_sqrt_epsilon();
let options = StftOptions::new().with_packing(StftPacking::OneSided);
let ones = vec![1.0; m];
// Keep the analysis window unchanged (Fixed) to compare synthesis from the same coefficients as the canonical dual
let closest =
    closest_stft_dual(&w, h, &ones, DualScale::Fixed, tolerance).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let tight = tight_stft_window(&w, h, tolerance).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plans = [
    ("Canonical dual", StftPlan::new(&w, h, options)),
    ("Dual closest to rectangular", StftPlan::from_windows(&w, closest.dual(), h, options)),
    ("Tight window", StftPlan::from_windows(&tight, &tight, h, options)),
];
// Add the same noise to all three syntheses, with standard deviation δ (uniform values in [−1/2, 1/2) have variance 1/12)
// One-sided DC and Nyquist bins must be real, so add noise only to their real parts
let amplitude = noise_level * 12.0_f64.sqrt();
let bins = m / 2 + 1;
let count = StftPlan::new(&w, h, options)
    .and_then(|p| p.output_len(len))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let perturbation: Vec<Complex<f64>> = (0..count)
    .map(|i| {
        let real_bin = i % bins == 0 || i % bins == bins - 1;
        let imaginary = if real_bin { 0.0 } else { amplitude * uniform() };
        Complex::new(amplitude * uniform(), imaginary)
    })
    .collect();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, plan) in plans {
    let plan = plan.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let dual = plan.dual_window().ok_or("NOLA does not hold")?;
    let analysis = plan.window();
    let coefficients = plan.analyze(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let restored = plan.synthesize(&coefficients, len).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let error = x.iter().zip(&restored).map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs()).fold(0.0, f64::max);
    let noisy: Vec<Complex<f64>> = coefficients
        .iter()
        .zip(&perturbation)
        .map(|(c, e)| *c + *e)
        .collect();
    let perturbed = plan.synthesize(&noisy, len).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let rms = (x.iter().zip(&perturbed).map(|(a, b)| (a - b).powi(2)).sum::<f64>()
        / len as f64)
        .sqrt();
    // Independent check: compute Σ_q w d − 1 for each remainder by direct summation
    let residual = (0..h)
        .map(|r| {
            let s: f64 = analysis.iter().zip(dual).skip(r).step_by(h).map(|(a, d)| a * d).sum();
            (s - 1.0).abs()
        })
        .fold(0.0, f64::max);
    let norm = dual.iter().map(|d| d * d).sum::<f64>().sqrt();
    rows.push((name, norm, error, residual, rms / noise_level));
}
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Synthesis window" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Synthesis window norm ‖d‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum reconstruction error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Dual condition residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Noise amplification factor [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "duals" }));
```

With the default symmetric Hann window and $h = 16$, reconstruction errors for all three pairs are at most $3.4 \times 10^{-16}$, and dual condition residuals are at most $2.3 \times 10^{-16}$.
Even for the symmetric window that fails COLA, the canonical dual recovers the signal to within rounding error.
The canonical dual has the smallest synthesis window norm, 3.29, while the dual closest to all ones has norm 5.73.
For noise with $\delta = 0.01$, the amplification factor is 0.143 for the canonical dual and 0.248 for the closest dual; the canonical dual has the smaller factor among the two syntheses using the same coefficients.
This reflects the least-squares property of synthesis with the canonical dual.
The tight window has energy 1 for each remainder, so its norm is $\sqrt h = 4$.
Because the tight window changes the analysis window itself, its amplification factor of 0.174 cannot be directly compared with the first two.

At $h = 56$, some remainders have small $E_r$, and the canonical dual norm rises to 47.3 and the amplification factor to 1.10.
With the periodic window and $h = 32$, the window satisfies COLA and the all-ones sequence is already a dual, so the closest dual has norm $\sqrt{64} = 8$ and residual 0.
Changing the noise magnitude $\delta$ barely changes the amplification factor, consistent with synthesis being linear in the coefficients.

## Plans and workspace

`StftPlan::new(window, hop, options)` checks the window and hop and prepares forward and inverse FFT plans and, under NOLA, the canonical dual.
The defaults for `StftOptions` are an FFT length equal to the window length, sample rate 1, `StftPacking::Full`, `SignalBoundary::Zero`, `StftScaling::Raw`, tolerance `Tolerance::from_sqrt_epsilon()`, and sequential execution.
Configuration methods do not validate values; `StftPlan::new` validates them together.
The plan is immutable, implements `Send + Sync + Clone`, and owns copies of the window and dual window.
`StftPlan::build` accepts an execution context to create the FFT plans but does not retain it.

`StftPlan::new` has the following failure modes.

- An empty window gives `InvalidInput` (`Empty`); a zero hop or nonpositive sample rate gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
- An FFT length shorter than the window gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`); one-sided packing with a complex type gives `InvalidInput` (`UnsupportedConfiguration`).
- A window or sample rate containing NaN or infinity gives `NonFinite`.
- A window with zero sum under `Magnitude`, or an all-zero window under `Psd`, gives `DomainError`.

The APIs `analyze`, `synthesize`, and `spectrogram` return owned results in a `Vec` and allocate workspace internally.
The APIs `analyze_into`, `synthesize_into`, `spectrogram_into`, `cross_spectrogram_into`, and `analyze_detrended_into` write into caller-provided outputs and workspace.
Query workspace size with `workspace_requirements(request)` and allocate it with `workspace(request)`.
`StftRequest` pairs an operation with a record length: `Analysis(N)`, `Synthesis(N)`, `Spectrogram(N)`, `CrossSpectrogram(N)`, and `DetrendedAnalysis(N)`, plus `Axis { shape, axis }` along one array axis.
Its size is proportional to the record length.
The transforms `analyze_axis_into` and `synthesize_axis_into` along an array axis replace the sample axis with two axes for frames and bins.

Synthesis first produces complex output and then converts it to the real type; for real types, an imaginary part exceeding tolerance gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
The tolerance $t = \mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}$ compares the imaginary part with the sum of the magnitudes of contributions from frames overlapping that sample.
Keep this check in mind when synthesizing modified coefficients, such as one-sided coefficients with an imaginary part in the DC bin.
`analyze_detrended_into` removes a trend from each frame before windowing, so synthesizing those coefficients does not recover the record.

Coefficient errors follow the usual FFT normwise error bound: error proportional to the operand norm, machine epsilon, and the logarithm of the transform length.
Adding contributions from $r$ frames during synthesis adds an error of $\gamma_r$ times the sum of the absolute contributions, plus $r\eta$ ($\gamma_r = ru/(1 - ru)$, where $u$ is unit roundoff and $\eta$ is the smallest positive subnormal).

## Analysis and synthesis in chunks

The `StftAnalysisStream` returned by `StftPlan::analysis_stream()` receives the record in chunks and writes frame coefficients in order as soon as all samples covered by a frame are available.
The coefficients are independent of chunk boundaries and match those from `analyze_into` with zero extension.
The stream retains the last window-length samples; on receiving `StreamChunk::Final`, it writes frames extending beyond the record end and closes.
A closed stream returns `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`) until `reset` is called.

The `StftSynthesisStream` returned by `StftPlan::synthesis_stream(N)` receives coefficients of a record of length $N$ in chunks of whole frames and writes samples once all overlapping frames are available.
A chunk ending within a frame or exceeding the record's frame count gives `InvalidInput` (`Inconsistent`).
Both streams can be created only from plans with boundary `SignalBoundary::Zero`.

`output_len(input_len, chunk)` returns the number of values the next call will write; workspace allocated with `workspace_requirements(max)` is usable for all subsequent calls with at most `max` inputs.
The `StreamProgress` returned by `process_into` has `consumed` (number read), `produced` (number written), and `first_output_index` (position of the first written value within the whole record).
In analysis, `consumed` counts samples and `produced` counts coefficients; synthesis reverses these roles.
Streams are values: save their state with `clone` and restore it without allocation with `clone_from`.

The following cell analyzes a record sampled at 1000 Hz (a 125 Hz cosine plus random values) with a periodic Hann window ($m = 64$, $h = 16$), one-sided packing, and `Magnitude` scaling.
Compare coefficients obtained by feeding chunks of length $L$ to the analysis stream with those from the caller-provided-output API `analyze_into`.
Because 125 Hz is centered on bin 8, $|X[p, 8]|$ is close to half the amplitude, 0.5, in frames inside the record.
Then feed coefficients to the synthesis stream in groups of $F$ frames and measure the difference from the original record.

```rust
//| id: signal-stft-streaming
//| ref: signal-stft-streaming
//| title: "Compare STFT analysis and synthesis in chunks with batch processing"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   chunk_len: {"type":"range","label":"Analysis chunk length L [samples]","description":"Number of samples passed in one call.","min":1,"max":400,"step":1,"value":100}
//|   frames_per_chunk: {"type":"range","label":"Synthesis chunk F [frames]","description":"Number of frames passed in one call.","min":1,"max":20,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   seed: {"type":"integer","label":"Random seed","description":"Change the random values added to the cosine.","min":1,"max":9999,"value":5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"single-samples","label":"L = 1, F = 1","description":"Even when samples are passed one at a time, the coefficients match batch processing.","inputs":{"chunk_len":1,"frames_per_chunk":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"stream","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"stream","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"stream","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":0.02}}
//|   - case: single-samples
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"stream","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"stream","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{Complex, ExecutionContext, RealScalar};
use scioxide_signal::{
    StftOptions, StftPacking, StftPlan, StftRequest, StftScaling, StreamChunk, WindowKind,
    WindowNormalization, WindowSymmetry, window,
};

let (fs, len, m, hop) = (1000.0_f64, 1000_usize, 64_usize, 16_usize);
let w = window::<f64>(WindowKind::Hann, m, WindowSymmetry::Periodic, WindowNormalization::Native)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = StftOptions::new()
    .with_sample_rate(fs)
    .with_packing(StftPacking::OneSided)
    .with_scaling(StftScaling::Magnitude);
let plan = StftPlan::new(&w, hop, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut state = seed as u64;
let mut uniform = || -> f64 {
    state = state.wrapping_add(0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15);
    let mut z = state;
    z = (z ^ (z >> 30)).wrapping_mul(0xBF58_476D_1CE4_E5B9);
    z = (z ^ (z >> 27)).wrapping_mul(0x94D0_49BB_1331_11EB);
    ((z ^ (z >> 31)) >> 11) as f64 / (1u64 << 53) as f64 - 0.5
};
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..len)
    .map(|i| RealScalar::cos(2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI * 125.0 * i as f64 / fs) + 0.1 * uniform())
    .collect();

// Batch processing (caller-provided-output API): allocate workspace with StftRequest
let cx = ExecutionContext::sequential();
let mut ws = plan.workspace(StftRequest::Analysis(len)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut offline = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); plan.output_len(len).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?];
plan.analyze_into(&x, &mut offline, &mut ws, cx).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Analysis stream: pass chunks of length L and mark the last chunk Final
let l = chunk_len.round() as usize;
let mut stream = plan.analysis_stream().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut stream_ws = stream.workspace(l).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut streamed = Vec::with_capacity(offline.len());
let mut calls = 0;
for (index, chunk) in x.chunks(l).enumerate() {
    let kind = if (index + 1) * l >= len { StreamChunk::Final } else { StreamChunk::Partial };
    let count = stream.output_len(chunk.len(), kind).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut out = vec![Complex::new(0.0, 0.0); count];
    stream.process_into(chunk, kind, &mut out, &mut stream_ws, cx).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    streamed.extend_from_slice(&out);
    calls += 1;
}
let analysis_gap = offline
    .iter()
    .zip(&streamed)
    .map(|(a, b)| (*a - *b).norm())
    .fold(0.0, f64::max);

// Synthesis stream: pass F frames at a time
let bins = plan.bins();
let block = frames_per_chunk.round() as usize * bins;
let mut synthesis = plan.synthesis_stream(len).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut synth_ws = synthesis.workspace(block).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut restored = Vec::with_capacity(len);
for (index, chunk) in streamed.chunks(block).enumerate() {
    let kind = if (index + 1) * block >= streamed.len() { StreamChunk::Final } else { StreamChunk::Partial };
    let count = synthesis.output_len(chunk.len(), kind).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut out = vec![0.0; count];
    synthesis.process_into(chunk, kind, &mut out, &mut synth_ws, cx).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    restored.extend_from_slice(&out);
}
let synthesis_error = x.iter().zip(&restored).map(|(a, b)| (a - b).abs()).fold(0.0, f64::max);

// |X[p, 8]| in interior frames (frames whose windows fit within the record)
let frames = plan.frames(len).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let inner: Vec<f64> = (0..frames.count())
    .filter(|&i| {
        let start = (frames.first() + i as i64) * hop as i64 - (m / 2) as i64;
        start >= 0 && start + m as i64 <= len as i64
    })
    .map(|i| offline[i * bins + 8].norm())
    .collect();
let tone = inner.iter().sum::<f64>() / inner.len() as f64;

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let rows = vec![
    ("Maximum coefficient difference between streaming and batch processing [-]", analysis_gap),
    ("Maximum synthesis stream reconstruction error [-]", synthesis_error),
    ("Mean |X[p, 8]| in interior frames [-]", tone),
    ("Number of frames [-]", frames.count() as f64),
    ("First frame number [-]", frames.first() as f64),
    ("Number of analysis calls [-]", calls as f64),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "stream" }));
```

With the default $L = 100$, analysis takes 10 calls, and the coefficients match batch processing bit for bit (zero difference).
The same holds for 1000 calls with $L = 1$ and for 3 calls with $L = 400$.
The synthesis stream reconstruction error is $4.4 \times 10^{-16}$, independent of $F$.
The record of length 1000 has 66 frames starting at number −1; frame −1 starts at sample −48.
The mean $|X[p, 8]|$ in interior frames is 0.4993, differing from half the amplitude, 0.5, by the random contribution.

## Summary

The STFT is a sequence of DFTs with a window shifted by one hop at a time; frame $p$ is centered at sample $ph$, and phase is referenced to the frame start.
Synthesis is possible when NOLA holds (positive window energy for every remainder); COLA is only a condition for recovery by simply adding windowed frames.
The default canonical dual has minimum norm and makes synthesis from modified coefficients optimal in the least-squares sense.
Avoid window and hop pairs with a small `energy_ratio()` from `check_overlap`, because they strongly amplify coefficient errors.
Records arriving in chunks can be processed with `StftAnalysisStream` and `StftSynthesisStream`, giving the same result as batch processing.
The tradeoff between STFT time and frequency resolution is covered in [Chirp spectrograms and time–frequency uncertainty](../../examples/chirp-spectrogram-uncertainty/); estimating power by averaging frames is covered in [Spectral estimation](../spectral-estimation/).
