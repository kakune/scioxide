Sound propagation in a room and transmission characteristics of a communication channel can be represented as linear time-invariant (LTI) systems.
An LTI system is determined by its unit-impulse response $h[n]$, and its output for any input $x[n]$ is the linear convolution $y = x * h$.
This example convolves a short pulse with a sound-pressure impulse response comprising direct sound and two wall reflections, using `ConvolutionNdPlan` from `scioxide-signal`.
Compare the result with a weighted sum of delayed inputs and use input autocorrelation to check the cross terms in output energy when reflections overlap.

## Linear time-invariant systems and impulse responses

Suppose a discrete-time system $T$ satisfies linearity $T\{a x_1 + b x_2\} = aT\{x_1\} + bT\{x_2\}$ and time invariance (shifting the input by $k$ samples shifts the output by $k$ samples).
Decomposing the input into a sum of unit impulses $x[n] = \sum_k x[k]\,\delta[n-k]$ gives

$$
y[n] = \sum_{k} x[k]\, T\{\delta[n-k]\} = \sum_{k} x[k]\, h[n-k]
$$


Here $h = T\{\delta\}$ is the impulse response, and the output is the [linear convolution](oxref:signal-linear-convolution) of the input and impulse response.

## Image sources and sound-pressure attenuation

Sound reflected from a flat wall can be treated as arriving directly from an image source at the mirror position of the source across the wall.
Sound pressure at distance $r$ from a point source decreases in proportion to $1/r$ through spherical spreading (sound intensity is proportional to $1/r^2$).
Let $r_0$ be the direct path length, $r_i$ the path length of reflection $i$, and $R_i$ the wall's pressure reflection coefficient; then the reflected-to-direct amplitude ratio and delay are

$$
a_i = R_i\, \frac{r_0}{r_i}, \qquad \tau_i = \frac{r_i - r_0}{c}
$$


Here $c = 343$ m/s is the speed of sound in air.
An acoustically rigid wall has $R_i > 0$ (in-phase reflection), while a pressure-release boundary such as the sea surface in underwater acoustics has $R_i < 0$ (opposite-phase reflection).
Rounding the delay to a sample count $d_i = \mathrm{round}(\tau_i f_s)$ at sampling rate $f_s$ approximates the impulse response as a sum of delayed impulses:

$$
h[n] = \delta[n] + \sum_{i=1}^{P} a_i\,\delta[n - d_i]
$$


This example sets $r_0 = 3$ m and takes the first-reflection delay $\tau_1$ and two reflection coefficients $R_1$ and $R_2$ as inputs.
The second reflection arrives 18 ms after the first; calculate amplitude ratios $a_i$ from the path lengths $r_i = r_0 + c\,\tau_i$.

## Convolution method and output length

The linear convolution of an input of length $N$ and a response of length $M$ has length $N + M - 1$.
`ConvolutionNdPlan::new(&[N], h, &[0], ConvolutionOptions::default())` owns the response as its kernel and creates a plan reusable for inputs of the same shape.
The default `ConvolutionMethod::Automatic` selects a method by comparing the direct-sum product count $NM$ with the FFT work estimate $P \log_2 P$ for a power of two $P$ at least $N + M - 1$ ([Choosing direct summation or FFT](oxref:signal-convolution-method)).
When the FFT is selected, the plan handles zero-padding internally, so the caller need not adjust the length.
See [Zero-padding length](oxref:fft-zero-padding) in the `scioxide-fft` chapter for constructing the same convolution from `scioxide-fft` transforms and the condition on zero-padding length.

This estimate does not examine whether response values are zero.
This example's response has only three nonzero coefficients, so summing delayed inputs requires just three products per output sample.
For a sparse response, using delay–coefficient pairs directly can require fewer operations than the method selected by `Automatic`.

The next cell uses 128 samples of a Gaussian pulse centered at 14 ms with standard deviation $\sigma = 2$ ms (4 samples), at a sampling rate of 2 kHz (sample interval 0.5 ms).
Create a `ConvolutionNdPlan` owning the response as its kernel, and call `convolve_into` with workspace allocated by `workspace(ConvolutionKernel::Prepared)`.
As an independent check, calculate $y[n] = x[n] + a_1 x[n - d_1] + a_2 x[n - d_2]$ directly and compare.
The second table shows the energy ratio $\lVert y \rVert^2 / \lVert x \rVert^2$, comparing its measured value, the cross-term formula in the next section, and the value for nonoverlapping reflections.

```rust
//| id: signal-echo-convolution
//| ref: signal-echo-convolution
//| title: "Convolve an image-source response with ConvolutionNdPlan and check energy cross terms"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   first_delay_ms: {"type":"range","label":"First-reflection delay τ₁ [ms]","description":"Delay from the direct sound to the first wall reflection.","min":2,"max":20,"step":0.5,"value":7}
//|   first_reflection: {"type":"range","label":"First pressure reflection coefficient R₁ [-]","description":"A positive value near 1 for a rigid wall.","min":0,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.9}
//|   second_reflection: {"type":"range","label":"Second pressure reflection coefficient R₂ [-]","description":"Negative values give opposite-phase reflection (a pressure-release boundary).","min":-1,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":-0.7}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"short-delay","label":"τ₁ = 2 ms","description":"The first reflection strongly overlaps the direct sound, increasing energy through the cross term.","inputs":{"first_delay_ms":2}}
//|   - {"id":"separated","label":"τ₁ = 20 ms","description":"Reflections do not overlap, and the energy ratio matches 1 + a₁² + a₂².","inputs":{"first_delay_ms":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"convolution","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":178}
//|       - {"artifact":"convolution","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Fft"}
//|       - {"artifact":"convolution","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: separated
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|   - case: short-delay
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"energy","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":1.0}}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, NdView, NdViewMut, RealScalar, StridedLayout};
use scioxide_signal::{ConvolutionKernel, ConvolutionNdPlan, ConvolutionOptions};

// 128 samples of a Gaussian pulse at 2 kHz, centered at 14 ms with standard deviation 2 ms (4 samples)
let (rate, sigma) = (2000.0_f64, 4.0_f64);
let input: Vec<f64> = (0..128)
    .map(|i| RealScalar::exp(-0.5 * ((i as f64 - 28.0) / sigma).powi(2)))
    .collect();
// Image sources: path length r_i = r₀ + c τ_i, pressure ratio a_i = R_i r₀ / r_i
let (direct_path, speed) = (3.0_f64, 343.0_f64);
let delays = [first_delay_ms / 1000.0, first_delay_ms / 1000.0 + 0.018];
let gains = [first_reflection, second_reflection]
    .iter()
    .zip(&delays)
    .map(|(r, tau)| r * direct_path / (direct_path + speed * tau))
    .collect::<Vec<_>>();
let lags: Vec<usize> = delays
    .iter()
    .map(|tau| (tau * rate).round() as usize)
    .collect();
let mut response = vec![0.0_f64; lags[1] + 1];
response[0] = 1.0;
response[lags[0]] = gains[0];
response[lags[1]] = gains[1];

// Prepare the plan owning the response kernel and its workspace only once
let (n, m) = (input.len(), response.len());
let (signal_shape, kernel_shape, output_shape, unit) = ([n], [m], [n + m - 1], [1_usize]);
let kernel_layout = StridedLayout::new(&kernel_shape, &unit).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let signal_layout = StridedLayout::new(&signal_shape, &unit).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let output_layout = StridedLayout::new(&output_shape, &unit).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let kernel = NdView::new(&response, kernel_layout).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let plan = ConvolutionNdPlan::new(&signal_shape, kernel, &[0], ConvolutionOptions::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = plan
    .workspace(ConvolutionKernel::Prepared)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut output = vec![0.0_f64; n + m - 1];
plan.convolve_into(
    NdView::new(&input, signal_layout).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    NdViewMut::new(&mut output, output_layout).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    &mut workspace,
    ExecutionContext::sequential(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check: calculate y[n] = x[n] + a₁ x[n − d₁] + a₂ x[n − d₂] directly
let shifted = |k: usize, d: usize| {
    if k >= d && k - d < n {
        input[k - d]
    } else {
        0.0
    }
};
let direct: Vec<f64> = (0..n + m - 1)
    .map(|k| shifted(k, 0) + gains[0] * shifted(k, lags[0]) + gains[1] * shifted(k, lags[1]))
    .collect();
let difference = direct
    .iter()
    .zip(&output)
    .fold(0.0_f64, |acc, (a, b)| acc.max((a - b).abs()));
let selection = plan.selection();
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Output length N + M − 1 [samples]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Selected method" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Direct-method products [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "FFT work estimate [-]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum difference from weighted sum [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let row = (
    output.len(),
    format!("{:?}", selection.method()),
    selection.direct_products(),
    selection.fft_work(),
    difference,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "convolution" })
);

// Cross-term formula: calculate input autocorrelation R_xx[d] = Σ x[k] x[k − d] by direct summation (independent check)
let autocorrelation = |d: usize| (d..n).map(|k| input[k] * input[k - d]).sum::<f64>();
let base = autocorrelation(0);
let measured = output.iter().map(|y| y * y).sum::<f64>() / base;
let separated = 1.0 + gains[0] * gains[0] + gains[1] * gains[1];
let cross = 2.0 * gains[0] * autocorrelation(lags[0])
    + 2.0 * gains[1] * autocorrelation(lags[1])
    + 2.0 * gains[0] * gains[1] * autocorrelation(lags[1] - lags[0]);
let predicted = separated + cross / base;
let fixed5 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 5 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "a₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "a₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Measured ‖y‖²/‖x‖² [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Cross-term formula [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed5 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Difference between measurement and formula [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Difference from nonoverlapping case [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let row = (
    gains[0],
    gains[1],
    measured,
    predicted,
    (measured - predicted).abs(),
    (measured - separated).abs(),
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "energy" })
);

let ms = |k: usize| 1000.0 * k as f64 / rate;
let curve = |v: &[f64]| {
    v.iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(k, &y)| [ms(k), y])
        .collect::<Vec<_>>()
};
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Input pulse x[n]", "points": curve(&input) }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Output including reflections y[n]", "points": curve(&output) }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Time [ms]",
        "yLabel": "Sound-pressure ratio [-]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 110.0,
        "annotations": [
            {
                "kind": "line",
                "axis": "x",
                "value": ms(lags[0]) + 14.0,
                "label": "First-reflection center"
            },
            {
                "kind": "line",
                "axis": "x",
                "value": ms(lags[1]) + 14.0,
                "label": "Second-reflection center"
            }
        ]
    })
);
```

At the default inputs ($\tau_1 = 7$ ms, $R_1 = 0.9$, $R_2 = -0.7$), path lengths are 5.40 m and 11.58 m, giving amplitude ratios $a_1 = 0.49991$ and $a_2 = -0.18143$.
The output length is $128 + 51 - 1 = 178$, and `Automatic` selects `Fft` because the direct product count of 6528 exceeds the FFT work estimate $256 \times 8 = 2048$.
The maximum difference from the weighted sum is $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$.
The energy ratio is 1.3296, agreeing with the cross-term formula to a difference of $8.9 \times 10^{-16}$ and exceeding the nonoverlapping value $1 + a_1^2 + a_2^2$ by 0.0468.
The first-reflection delay of 14 samples is 3.5 times the pulse standard deviation of 4 samples, so the in-phase reflection overlaps the direct pulse's tail.
At $\tau_1 = 2$ ms (4 samples), the amplitude ratio increases to $a_1 = 0.7325$ and the energy ratio becomes 2.7229, exceeding the nonoverlapping value by 1.14.
At $\tau_1 = 20$ ms, the difference from the nonoverlapping case falls to $1.1 \times 10^{-10}$, with the remaining difference of the order of the Gaussian-tail overlap $e^{-40^2/(4 \cdot 4^2)} = e^{-25}$ at a separation of 40 samples.
Setting $R_2$ to either $-1$ or $+1$ gives the same ratio, 1.3638: the second reflection does not overlap the other components, so its sign has no effect.

## Waveform overlap and energy cross terms

Expanding the energy of the output $y[n] = x[n] + a_1 x[n - d_1] + a_2 x[n - d_2]$ gives

$$
\lVert y \rVert^2 = \left(1 + a_1^2 + a_2^2\right)\lVert x \rVert^2 + 2a_1 R_{xx}[d_1] + 2a_2 R_{xx}[d_2] + 2a_1a_2 R_{xx}[d_2 - d_1]
$$


Here $R_{xx}[d] = \sum_n x[n]\,x[n-d]$ is the input autocorrelation, equal to the value at index $N - 1 + d$ of `correlate(x, x)` ([Correlation lag ordering](oxref:signal-correlation-lags)).
When the pulses of two delayed components do not overlap in time, their autocorrelation is 0 and the energy is the sum of the individual component energies.
When they overlap, cross terms appear: in-phase reflections ($a_i > 0$) increase energy, while opposite-phase reflections ($a_i < 0$) interfere destructively and reduce it.

For a Gaussian pulse with standard deviation $\sigma$ samples, the continuous approximation gives $R_{xx}[d] \approx \lVert x \rVert^2 e^{-d^2/(4\sigma^2)}$, and cross terms matter for delays shorter than about $2\sigma$.
The cell's cross-term formula uses direct summation of the discrete autocorrelation rather than this approximation, so it agrees with measured values to rounding error.
Thus reflection coefficients cannot be estimated from peak amplitude or total energy alone; autocorrelation at each delay must be considered.

## Complexity and processing long signals

Direct convolution costs $O(NM)$ operations, while FFT convolution costs $O(P \log P)$.
For a long, dense response such as a highly reverberant room ($M \sim 10^5$ samples), the FFT requires fewer operations; for a response with few nonzero coefficients as in this example, summing delayed inputs requires fewer.
When processing many inputs with the same response, prepare the plan and workspace once as in this cell and repeat `convolve_into`.
For streaming long audio in chunks, use overlap-add `OverlapPlan` and `OverlapStream`, passing `StreamChunk::Final` on the last chunk ([Streaming with overlap-add](oxref:signal-overlap-add)).
To remove reflections with a known response, `deconvolve` can perform polynomial division, but error grows exponentially if the response is not minimum phase ([Deconvolution and polynomial division](oxref:signal-deconvolution)).

## Summary

An LTI output is the linear convolution of input and impulse response; wall reflections can be modeled as image-source paths with pressure ratios $R_i r_0 / r_i$ and delays $(r_i - r_0)/c$.
`ConvolutionNdPlan` owns the response kernel and handles zero-padding and method selection internally, allowing repeated convolution of inputs with the same response.
Because `Automatic` does not account for response sparsity, summing delayed inputs takes fewer operations for responses with few nonzero coefficients.
When reflections overlap the direct sound, autocorrelation cross terms contribute to energy, with opposite effects for in-phase and opposite-phase reflections.
