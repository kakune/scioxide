AM radio receivers, vibration monitoring, and ultrasonic reflection detection extract slow amplitude variations (the envelope) carried by a rapidly oscillating carrier.
Taking the magnitude $\lvert z \rvert$ of the analytic signal $z = x + i\,\mathcal{H}[x]$ is a standard way to obtain an envelope.
However, $\lvert z \rvert$ matches the envelope only when the envelope bandwidth is below the carrier frequency (Bedrosian's condition).
This example obtains the analytic signal of an amplitude-modulated wave with `hilbert` and `envelope` from `scioxide-signal`, comparing it with the closed-form analytic signal.
Then examine what $\lvert z \rvert$ represents under overmodulation and when the carrier frequency is below the modulation frequency.

[Analytic signals, Riesz transforms, and vector strength](oxref:signal-analytic-signal) derives the discrete analytic-signal weights (1 for DC and an even-length Nyquist bin, 2 for positive frequencies, and 0 for negative frequencies) and explains the conditions for interpreting the envelope.
See [Analytic signals and the discrete Hilbert transform](../../../scioxide-fft/algorithms/analytic-signal/) in the FFT chapter for constructing the same weights from `rfft` and `ifft`.

## Analytic signal of an amplitude-modulated wave

An amplitude-modulated wave with carrier frequency $f_c$, modulation frequency $f_m$, and modulation depth $m$ is

$$
x(t) = \left[1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t)\right]\cos(2\pi f_c t)
$$


Expanding with the product-to-sum identity gives

$$
x(t) = \cos(2\pi f_c t) + \frac m2\cos\left(2\pi (f_c + f_m) t\right) + \frac m2\cos\left(2\pi (f_c - f_m) t\right)
$$

This is a sum of three cosines: the carrier and the upper and lower sidebands.
The analytic signal of a cosine $\cos(2\pi g t)$ at frequency $g \ge 0$ is $e^{2\pi i g t}$ (at $g = 0$ it remains the constant 1).
Because cosine is even, we can rewrite it as $\cos(2\pi (f_c - f_m) t) = \cos(2\pi \lvert f_c - f_m \rvert t)$.
Thus, for any $f_c$ and $f_m$, the analytic signal has the closed form

$$
z(t) = e^{2\pi i f_c t} + \frac m2 e^{2\pi i (f_c + f_m) t} + \frac m2 e^{2\pi i \lvert f_c - f_m \rvert t}
$$


This page uses this $z$ as an independent check of `hilbert`.

If $f_c \ge f_m$, then $\lvert f_c - f_m \rvert = f_c - f_m$ and $e^{2\pi i f_c t}$ can be factored out of all three terms.

$$
z(t) = \left[1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t)\right] e^{2\pi i f_c t},
\qquad
\lvert z(t) \rvert = \left\lvert 1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t) \right\rvert
$$

This is the conclusion of Bedrosian's theorem: when $m \le 1$, $\lvert z \rvert$ is exactly the envelope $1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t)$.
At $f_c = f_m$, the lower sideband becomes DC, but the same factorization holds.

For $m > 1$ (overmodulation), the envelope $1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t)$ is negative over some intervals.
Because $\lvert z \rvert$ is nonnegative by definition, the recovered quantity is $\lvert 1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t) \rvert$, with the negative parts folded upward.
The trajectory of $z$ then passes through the origin, and the phase $\arg z$ jumps by $\pi$ as the envelope crosses 0.
Instantaneous frequency computed from phase differences has large spikes at these crossings.

## Discretization and check design

Use sampling rate $f_s = 256$ Hz and $N = 512$ samples (observation time 2 s).
When $f_c$ and $f_m$ are integer Hz, all three cosines complete integer numbers of cycles in 2 s, so the record joins periodically at its ends.
The DFT's periodicity assumption then holds, and the discrete analytic signal should agree with samples of the closed-form $z$ above to rounding error.
The input range gives $f_c + f_m \le 124$ Hz, below the Nyquist frequency of 128 Hz.

The following block defines functions shared by the two cells.
`am_signal` returns $x(t)$ and `closed_form` returns the closed-form analytic signal $z(t)$, both as reference calculations using f64 inherent methods.

```rust
//| id: signal-hilbert-envelope-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Amplitude-modulated wave and closed-form analytic signal"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::Complex;

/// Sample count N and sampling rate f_s [Hz] (observation time 2 s).
pub(crate) const SAMPLES: usize = 512;
pub(crate) const SAMPLE_RATE: f64 = 256.0;

/// x(t) = [1 + m cos(2π f_m t)] cos(2π f_c t). Use f64 inherent methods for this reference calculation.
pub(crate) fn am_signal(depth: f64, carrier: f64, modulation: f64, t: f64) -> f64 {
    let tau = std::f64::consts::TAU;
    (1.0 + depth * (tau * modulation * t).cos()) * (tau * carrier * t).cos()
}

/// z(t) = e^{2πi f_c t} + (m/2) e^{2πi (f_c + f_m) t} + (m/2) e^{2πi |f_c − f_m| t}.
pub(crate) fn closed_form(depth: f64, carrier: f64, modulation: f64, t: f64) -> Complex<f64> {
    let tau = std::f64::consts::TAU;
    let tone = |frequency: f64, amplitude: f64| {
        let phase = tau * frequency * t;
        Complex::new(amplitude * phase.cos(), amplitude * phase.sin())
    };
    tone(carrier, 1.0)
        + tone(carrier + modulation, 0.5 * depth)
        + tone((carrier - modulation).abs(), 0.5 * depth)
}
```

## Envelope recovery

The next cell creates an amplitude-modulated wave from inputs $m$, $f_c$, and $f_m$, obtains its analytic signal with `hilbert`, and obtains its envelope with `envelope`.
There are four checks.
The maximum difference from the closed-form $z$ checks the `hilbert` calculation itself.
The maximum difference between $\lvert z \rvert$ and $\lvert 1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t) \rvert$ checks whether $\lvert z \rvert$ can be interpreted as the envelope.
The maximum difference between the real part and input checks the weighting (it is 0 because `hilbert` copies the input directly to the real part).
The difference between the `envelope` result and the magnitude of the `hilbert` result checks that the two functions perform the same calculation.
The chart's solid line is $\lvert z \rvert$ and its dashed line is the signed theoretical envelope $1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t)$.

```rust
//| id: signal-hilbert-envelope
//| ref: signal-hilbert-envelope
//| title: "Compare the analytic signal and envelope of an amplitude-modulated wave with closed forms"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [signal-hilbert-envelope-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   modulation_depth: {"type":"range","label":"Modulation depth m [-]","description":"Values above 1 give overmodulation.","min":0,"max":1.2,"step":0.05,"value":0.65}
//|   carrier_hz: {"type":"range","label":"Carrier frequency f_c [Hz]","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":32}
//|   modulation_hz: {"type":"range","label":"Modulation frequency f_m [Hz]","min":1,"max":24,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"overmodulation","label":"m = 1.2 (overmodulation)","description":"Negative envelope portions fold upward, and |z| matches |1 + m cos|.","inputs":{"modulation_depth":1.2}}
//|   - {"id":"low-carrier","label":"f_c = 8 Hz, f_m = 20 Hz","description":"The lower sideband moves to negative frequency; |z| deviates from the envelope by up to 0.62 but still matches the closed-form z.","inputs":{"carrier_hz":8,"modulation_hz":20}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":0}
//|   - case: overmodulation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: low-carrier
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.62,"atol":0.01}}
use scioxide_signal::{envelope, hilbert};

let (m, fc, fm) = (modulation_depth, carrier_hz, modulation_hz);
let time = |i: usize| i as f64 / SAMPLE_RATE;
let signal: Vec<f64> = (0..SAMPLES)
    .map(|i| am_signal(m, fc, fm, time(i)))
    .collect();
let analytic = hilbert(&signal).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let magnitude = envelope(&signal).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Theoretical envelope 1 + m cos(2π f_m t) (signed).
let theory = |t: f64| 1.0 + m * (std::f64::consts::TAU * fm * t).cos();
let (mut closed_gap, mut envelope_gap) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
let (mut real_gap, mut same_gap) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
let (mut estimated, mut expected) = (Vec::with_capacity(SAMPLES), Vec::with_capacity(SAMPLES));
for (i, z) in analytic.iter().enumerate() {
    let t = time(i);
    closed_gap = closed_gap.max((*z - closed_form(m, fc, fm, t)).norm());
    envelope_gap = envelope_gap.max((magnitude[i] - theory(t).abs()).abs());
    real_gap = real_gap.max((z.re - signal[i]).abs());
    same_gap = same_gap.max((magnitude[i] - z.norm()).abs());
    estimated.push([t, magnitude[i]]);
    expected.push([t, theory(t)]);
}

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Check" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let rows = vec![
    ("hilbert and closed-form z", closed_gap),
    ("envelope and |1 + m cos(2π fₘ t)|", envelope_gap),
    ("Re z and x", real_gap),
    ("envelope and |hilbert|", same_gap),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "|z(t)| (envelope)", "points": estimated }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "1 + m cos(2π fₘ t) (theory)",
            "points": expected,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Time t [s]",
        "yLabel": "Envelope [-]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 2.0,
        "yMin": -0.3,
        "yMax": 2.3,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.0 }]
    })
);
```

At the defaults $m = 0.65$, $f_c = 32$ Hz, and $f_m = 3$ Hz, the maximum difference between `hilbert` and the closed-form $z$ is $4.8\times10^{-14}$, and the maximum difference between $\lvert z \rvert$ and the envelope is $2.9\times10^{-14}$.
The difference between real part and input, and that between `envelope` and the magnitude of `hilbert`, are both 0.
These differences are of the same order as the rounding error $u \cdot 440 \approx 5\times10^{-14}$ ($u = 2^{-53}$) in evaluating the reference phase $2\pi f t$ (up to about 440 rad) in double precision.
At $f_c = 100$ Hz, the phase grows to about 1300 rad and the difference from the closed form increases to $2.0\times10^{-13}$.

At $m = 1.2$, the dashed line (signed envelope) drops below 0, while the solid line $\lvert z \rvert$ folds upward at 0.
The difference between $\lvert z \rvert$ and $\lvert 1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t) \rvert$ is $3.8\times10^{-14}$, showing agreement with the folded shape.

At $f_c = 8$ Hz and $f_m = 20$ Hz, the difference between `hilbert` and the closed-form $z$ remains $1.8\times10^{-14}$, but the difference between $\lvert z \rvert$ and the envelope grows to 0.62.
The solid line separates from the dashed line and oscillates rapidly.
The next section examines this discrepancy as a function of carrier frequency.

## When the carrier is below the modulation frequency

For $f_c < f_m$, the lower-sideband frequency $f_c - f_m$ is negative.
For a real signal, $\cos(2\pi(f_c - f_m)t) = \cos(2\pi(f_m - f_c)t)$, so this component is indistinguishable from a cosine at positive frequency $f_m - f_c$ and becomes $e^{2\pi i (f_m - f_c) t}$ in the analytic signal.
Factoring $e^{2\pi i f_c t}$ out of the closed-form $z$ gives

$$
z(t) = e^{2\pi i f_c t}\left[1 + \frac m2 e^{2\pi i f_m t} + \frac m2 e^{2\pi i (f_m - 2 f_c) t}\right]
$$

The expression in brackets does not reduce to $1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t)$.
This occurs because the spectrum of the envelope factor $1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t)$ ($\lvert f \rvert \le f_m$) overlaps the carrier spectrum ($\lvert f \rvert = f_c$), violating Bedrosian's condition.
$\lvert z \rvert$ acquires oscillations at frequency $2 f_c$ and becomes a different quantity from the envelope.
The `hilbert` calculation is correct; the assumption that permits interpreting $\lvert z \rvert$ as the envelope no longer holds.

The next cell holds $m$ and $f_m$ fixed, sweeps $f_c = 1, 2, \dots, 60$ Hz, and evaluates `envelope` at each $f_c$.
For each $f_c$, record the maximum difference between $\lvert z \rvert$ and $\lvert 1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t) \rvert$ (envelope error), and between `hilbert` and the closed-form $z$.
The chart plots both differences on a logarithmic axis and marks $f_c = f_m$ with a vertical line.
Omit exactly zero differences, which cannot be plotted on a logarithmic axis, and report their count in the table.

```rust
//| id: signal-hilbert-bedrosian-sweep
//| ref: signal-hilbert-bedrosian-sweep
//| title: "Sweep carrier frequency and measure envelope error"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [signal-hilbert-envelope-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-signal]
//| inputs:
//|   modulation_depth: {"type":"range","label":"Modulation depth m [-]","min":0.05,"max":1,"step":0.05,"value":0.65}
//|   modulation_hz: {"type":"range","label":"Modulation frequency f_m [Hz]","min":1,"max":24,"step":1,"value":20}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"slow-modulation","label":"f_m = 3 Hz","description":"Only f_c = 1, 2 Hz have large errors.","inputs":{"modulation_hz":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":5e-13}}
//|   - case: slow-modulation
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_signal::{envelope, hilbert};

let (m, fm) = (modulation_depth, modulation_hz);
let time = |i: usize| i as f64 / SAMPLE_RATE;
let theory = |t: f64| (1.0 + m * (std::f64::consts::TAU * fm * t).cos()).abs();
let (mut envelope_points, mut closed_points) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut above, mut below, mut closed_worst) = (0.0_f64, f64::INFINITY, 0.0_f64);
let mut zero_points = 0_usize;
for carrier in 1..=60 {
    let fc = carrier as f64;
    let signal: Vec<f64> = (0..SAMPLES)
        .map(|i| am_signal(m, fc, fm, time(i)))
        .collect();
    let magnitude = envelope(&signal).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let analytic = hilbert(&signal).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let envelope_gap = (0..SAMPLES).fold(0.0_f64, |acc, i| {
        acc.max((magnitude[i] - theory(time(i))).abs())
    });
    let closed_gap = (0..SAMPLES).fold(0.0_f64, |acc, i| {
        acc.max((analytic[i] - closed_form(m, fc, fm, time(i))).norm())
    });
    // Aggregate the maximum for f_c ≥ f_m and the minimum for f_c < f_m.
    if fc >= fm {
        above = above.max(envelope_gap);
    } else {
        below = below.min(envelope_gap);
    }
    closed_worst = closed_worst.max(closed_gap);
    // Omit and count zero differences, which cannot be plotted on a logarithmic axis.
    for (points, gap) in [
        (&mut envelope_points, envelope_gap),
        (&mut closed_points, closed_gap),
    ] {
        if gap > 0.0 {
            points.push([fc, gap]);
        } else {
            zero_points += 1;
        }
    }
}

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum envelope error for f_c ≥ f_m [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Minimum envelope error for f_c < f_m [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "Not applicable" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum difference from closed-form z [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Zero points omitted from chart [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let below = below.is_finite().then_some(below);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(above, below, closed_worst, zero_points)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sweep" })
);
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "scatter",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "series": [
        { "name": "Maximum | |z| − |1 + m cos| |", "points": envelope_points },
        { "name": "Maximum |z − closed form|", "points": closed_points }
    ],
    "xLabel": "Carrier frequency f_c [Hz]",
    "yLabel": "Maximum difference [-]",
    "yType": "log",
    "xMin": 0.0,
    "xMax": 61.0,
    "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": fm, "label": "f_c = f_m" }]
}));
```

At the defaults $m = 0.65$ and $f_m = 20$ Hz, the maximum envelope error across 41 cases with $f_c \ge 20$ Hz is $8.1\times10^{-14}$; across 19 cases with $f_c < 20$ Hz, even the minimum is $0.60$.
At the vertical line $f_c = f_m$, the chart shows envelope error jumping from the rounding-error level to order $10^{-1}$.
Meanwhile, the difference between `hilbert` and the closed-form $z$ remains at or below $1.3\times10^{-13}$ in all 60 cases.
At $f_c = f_m$, the lower sideband becomes DC, but the previous section's factorization holds, so error remains at the rounding-error level.
Error is approximately proportional to modulation depth: the minimum for $f_c < f_m$ is $0.045$ at $m = 0.05$ and $0.96$ at $m = 1$.
At $f_m = 3$ Hz, only $f_c = 1, 2$ Hz have large errors, with a minimum of $0.54$.
Before interpreting an envelope, check that its bandwidth (here $f_m$) does not exceed the carrier frequency.

## Summary

`hilbert` returns an analytic signal treating the record as one period; for amplitude-modulated waves with integer numbers of periods, it agrees with the closed-form analytic signal to rounding error.
$\lvert z \rvert$ can be interpreted as the envelope only when the envelope bandwidth does not exceed the carrier frequency (Bedrosian's condition), here only for $f_c \ge f_m$.
Even when the condition fails, `hilbert` remains correct, but $\lvert z \rvert$ acquires oscillations at $2 f_c$ and differs from the envelope.
Under overmodulation, $\lvert z \rvert$ returns $\lvert 1 + m\cos(2\pi f_m t) \rvert$ with negative portions folded upward, and the phase jumps by $\pi$.
If the record does not join periodically at its ends, the envelope is disturbed near the ends; apply a window or leave margins and use only the center.

See the chirp example in [Analytic signals, Riesz transforms, and vector strength](oxref:signal-instantaneous-frequency) for instantaneous-frequency calculation and the effect of endpoint jumps on error.
See [Analytic signals and the discrete Hilbert transform](../../../scioxide-fft/algorithms/analytic-signal/) for constructing the same analytic signal with `rfft` and `ifft`.
