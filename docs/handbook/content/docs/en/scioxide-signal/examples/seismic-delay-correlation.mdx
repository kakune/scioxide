The arrival-time difference of the same seismic wave at two seismometers is a basic observation for estimating source direction and subsurface velocity structure.
For similar waveforms, find the shift that best aligns one record with the other; this amounts to maximizing cross-correlation.
This example estimates arrival-time differences from noisy synthetic records at two stations.
Use repeated trials with different seeds to examine the sign of `correlate` lags, parabolic interpolation for delays finer than the sample interval, and noise-induced errors and large outliers.

## Synthetic-record model

Use sampling rate $f_s = 100$ Hz and record length $N = 512$ samples (5.12 s).
Represent the arriving wave by a Gaussian-modulated cosine with carrier frequency $f_c = 5$ Hz and envelope standard deviation $\sigma_w = 0.08$ s:

$$
s(t) = e^{-t^2/(2\sigma_w^2)} \cos(2\pi f_c t)
$$

Its maximum amplitude is 1.
This is a defined model of a short wave packet such as the onset of a P wave, rather than a reproduction of a real seismic waveform.
The same packet reaches station A at $t_A = 1.5$ s and station B at $t_B = t_A + \tau$, with independent white Gaussian noise of standard deviation $\sigma$ added to each.

$$
a[n] = s(n/f_s - t_A) + e_a[n], \qquad b[n] = s(n/f_s - t_B) + e_b[n]
$$

Define SNR as $20 \log_{10}(1/\sigma)$, the ratio $1/\sigma$ of the packet's maximum amplitude to the noise standard deviation expressed in decibels.
Generate the packet with `Waveform::GaussianPulse` from `scioxide_signal::waveform` and draw noise from `Normal` in `scioxide-random` with a specified seed.

## Cross-correlation and parabolic interpolation

`correlate(b, a)` arranges $c[\ell] = \sum_k b[k]\, a[k - \ell]$ in ascending lag order $\ell = -(N-1), \dots, N-1$, with lag 0 at index $N - 1$ ([Correlation lag ordering](oxref:signal-correlation-lags)).
When $b$ contains a waveform delayed by $d$ samples relative to $a$, $c[\ell]$ peaks near $\ell = d$.
Thus, compute lag $\ell^\ast = j^\ast - (N-1)$ from the maximizing index $j^\ast$ and set $\tau = \ell^\ast / f_s$; a positive value indicates later arrival at B. Swapping arguments to `correlate(a, b)` reverses the sign of the maximizing lag.

Integer-lag resolution is the sample interval $1/f_s = 10$ ms.
Taking the vertex of the parabola through the maximum and its neighboring points $c_- = c[j^\ast - 1]$, $c_0 = c[j^\ast]$, and $c_+ = c[j^\ast + 1]$ gives the sub-sample shift

$$
\delta = \frac{c_- - c_+}{2\,(c_- - 2c_0 + c_+)}, \qquad \hat\tau = \frac{\ell^\ast + \delta}{f_s}
$$

with $\lvert \delta \rvert \le 1/2$.
The correlation peak is not parabolic and is close to a carrier cosine in this example, so $\delta$ retains a small bias even without noise.

## Linearizing estimation error

For small noise, a first-order expansion of the correlation peak's displacement gives the delay-estimate standard deviation

$$
\sigma_\tau \approx \sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^2}{\sum_n s'(t_n)^2}}
$$


Here $s'$ is the packet's time derivative and $t_n$ denotes times in station A's record.
The derivation expands the maximum condition $c'(\ell) = 0$ around the true delay, retaining only products of noise and the wave packet.
Each record's noise contributes one term with variance $\sigma^2 \sum s'^2$, producing the numerator $2\sigma^2$.
A steeper wave packet (higher carrier frequency) gives a more precise delay for the same noise.

The noise–noise product $\sum_k e_b[k]\, e_a[k-\ell]$ neglected by this approximation sums over all $N$ samples, so its variance is proportional to $N\sigma^4$.
The wave-packet–noise term is proportional to $\sigma^2$, so as SNR decreases the noise–noise contribution grows and error exceeds the linearized prediction.
At still lower SNR, a neighboring peak one carrier period $1/f_c = 0.2$ s away or a noise–noise correlation peak becomes the maximum, producing a large estimation outlier.
Once outliers begin, root-mean-square error departs sharply from the linearized prediction.
If approximate arrival times are known, cropping records around them before correlation reduces both noise–noise terms and outliers.

The following block defines the model shared by the two cells.
`record` creates a record from arrival time and noise standard deviation, while `estimate` returns the maximizing lag of `correlate(b, a)` and the parabolically interpolated delay [s].
`predicted_sd` computes $\sigma_\tau$ above from the analytic derivative $s'(t)$ (an independent check).

```rust
//| id: signal-seismic-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Synthetic seismic records and cross-correlation delay estimation"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-signal]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SplitMix64};
use scioxide_signal::{Waveform, correlate, waveform};

/// Sampling rate [Hz], record sample count, and arrival time at station A [s]
pub(crate) const RATE: f64 = 100.0;
pub(crate) const LENGTH: usize = 512;
pub(crate) const ARRIVAL: f64 = 1.5;
/// Packet carrier frequency [Hz] and envelope standard deviation [s]
pub(crate) const CARRIER: f64 = 5.0;
pub(crate) const WIDTH: f64 = 0.08;

/// Compute noise standard deviation σ from SNR [dB] = 20 log₁₀(1/σ)
pub(crate) fn noise_sd(snr_db: f64) -> f64 {
    RealScalar::exp(-snr_db / 20.0 * core::f64::consts::LN_10)
}

/// Record of a packet arriving at arrival [s], plus white Gaussian noise with standard deviation noise
pub(crate) fn record(
    arrival: f64,
    noise: f64,
    rng: &mut SplitMix64,
) -> Result<Vec<f64>, String> {
    let pulse = Waveform::GaussianPulse {
        frequency: CARRIER,
        phase: 0.0,
        sigma: WIDTH,
    };
    let times: Vec<f64> = (0..LENGTH).map(|n| n as f64 / RATE - arrival).collect();
    let clean = waveform(pulse, &times).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let normal = Normal::new(0.0, noise).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    clean
        .iter()
        .map(|s| Ok(s + normal.sample(rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?))
        .collect()
}

/// Maximizing lag ℓ* [samples] of correlate(b, a) and parabolically interpolated delay [s]
pub(crate) fn estimate(a: &[f64], b: &[f64]) -> Result<(i64, f64), String> {
    let c = correlate(b, a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Exclude endpoints when finding the maximum because neighboring points are needed
    let j = (1..c.len() - 1).fold(1, |best, j| if c[j] > c[best] { j } else { best });
    let lag = j as i64 - (a.len() as i64 - 1);
    let (left, mid, right) = (c[j - 1], c[j], c[j + 1]);
    let shift = 0.5 * (left - right) / (left - 2.0 * mid + right);
    Ok((lag, (lag as f64 + shift) / RATE))
}

/// Linearized delay standard deviation [s]: √(2σ² / Σ s'(tₙ)²). s' is the analytic derivative
pub(crate) fn predicted_sd(noise: f64) -> f64 {
    let omega = core::f64::consts::TAU * CARRIER;
    let slope: f64 = (0..LENGTH)
        .map(|n| {
            let t = n as f64 / RATE - ARRIVAL;
            let envelope = RealScalar::exp(-0.5 * (t / WIDTH) * (t / WIDTH));
            let carrier = RealScalar::cos(omega * t);
            let derivative = -envelope
                * (t / (WIDTH * WIDTH) * carrier + omega * RealScalar::sin(omega * t));
            derivative * derivative
        })
        .sum();
    RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 * noise * noise / slope)
}
```

## Estimation from one pair of records

The next cell creates two records from true delay $\tau$, SNR, and random seed, and estimates delay from `correlate(b, a)`.
The first two charts show station A and B records with linked time axes.
The third plots correlation against lag [s], with a vertical line at the true delay.
The table lists the integer-lag delay, parabolically interpolated delay, error from truth, linearized standard deviation, and maximizing lag of `correlate(a, b)` with arguments swapped.

```rust
//| id: signal-seismic-single-record
//| ref: signal-seismic-single-record
//| title: "Estimate arrival-time difference from records at two stations"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [signal-seismic-model]
//| inputs:
//|   delay: {"type":"range","label":"True delay τ [s]","description":"Time by which arrival at station B is later than at A.","min":0.5,"max":2.5,"step":0.001,"value":1.234}
//|   snr_db: {"type":"range","label":"SNR [dB]","description":"Ratio of packet maximum amplitude to noise standard deviation.","min":-5,"max":40,"step":1,"value":20}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","description":"Determine the noise in the two records.","min":1,"max":200,"step":1,"value":100}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"noisy","label":"SNR = 0 dB","description":"Noise becomes as large as the packet, and error can exceed the linearized prediction.","inputs":{"snr_db":0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":123}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.234,"atol":0.003}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimate","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":-123}
use scioxide_random::{SeedableRng, SplitMix64};
use scioxide_signal::correlate;

let noise = noise_sd(snr_db);
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let a = record(ARRIVAL, noise, &mut rng)?;
let b = record(ARRIVAL + delay, noise, &mut rng)?;
let (lag, estimated) = estimate(&a, &b)?;
// Swapping arguments reverses the sign of the maximizing lag
let swapped = correlate(&a, &b).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let swapped_peak =
    (0..swapped.len()).fold(
        0,
        |best, j| if swapped[j] > swapped[best] { j } else { best },
    );
let swapped_lag = swapped_peak as i64 - (b.len() as i64 - 1);

let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "True delay τ [s]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximizing lag ℓ* [samples]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "ℓ*/f_s [s]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Parabolic interpolation τ̂ [s]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Error τ̂ − τ [ms]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Linearized σ_τ [ms]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "correlate(a, b) lag [samples]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let row = (
    delay,
    lag,
    lag as f64 / RATE,
    estimated,
    1000.0 * (estimated - delay),
    1000.0 * predicted_sd(noise),
    swapped_lag,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "estimate" })
);

let trace = |x: &[f64]| {
    x.iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(n, &v)| [n as f64 / RATE, v])
        .collect::<Vec<_>>()
};
for (name, values) in [("Station A record a[n]", &a), ("Station B record b[n]", &b)] {
    emit_line_chart!(
        &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": trace(values) })],
        options = &serde_json::json!({
            "link": { "group": "seismic-time" },
            "xLabel": "Time t [s]",
            "yLabel": "Amplitude [-]",
            "xMin": 0.0,
            "xMax": LENGTH as f64 / RATE
        })
    );
}
let c = correlate(&b, &a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lags: Vec<[f64; 2]> = c
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, &v)| [(j as f64 - (LENGTH as f64 - 1.0)) / RATE, v])
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "c[ℓ] = correlate(b, a)", "points": lags })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Lag ℓ/f_s [s]",
        "yLabel": "Correlation [-]",
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": delay, "label": "True delay τ" }]
    })
);
```

At the defaults $\tau = 1.234$ s, SNR 20 dB, and seed 100, correlation peaks at lag 123 samples (1.23 s), and parabolic interpolation gives $\hat\tau = 1.2329$ s.
The error is $-1.06$ ms, within the linearized standard deviation $\sigma_\tau = 1.63$ ms.
With arguments swapped, `correlate(a, b)` peaks at lag $-123$, reversing only the sign.
At SNR 40 dB, $\sigma_\tau$ drops to 0.16 ms and error becomes $-0.12$ ms.
At SNR 0 dB, the packet is buried in noise, the maximizing lag moves to $-11$ samples, and error becomes $-1343$ ms.
This outlier is orders of magnitude larger than the linearized $\sigma_\tau = 16$ ms, showing that peak heights alone cannot identify the correct correlation peak.

## SNR and estimation error

Because the error for one pair depends on randomness, assess its magnitude through repeated trials with different seeds.
The next cell varies SNR over eight levels from 0 to 40 dB, independently generating $R$ record pairs and estimating their delays at each level.
Fix the true delay at $\tau = 1.234$ s (123.4 samples, fractional part 0.4 sample).
From each level's errors $e_i = \hat\tau_i - \tau$, calculate root mean square $\mathrm{RMS} = \sqrt{\overline{e^2}}$, its standard error $\mathrm{SE}(\overline{e^2}) / (2\,\mathrm{RMS})$, mean bias, and the count of outliers with $\lvert e_i \rvert > 0.1$ s (half a carrier period).
The chart plots the ratio of RMS to the linearized prediction $\sigma_\tau$ with ±2 SE error bars, and a reference line at ratio 1 on the vertical axis.
Because generation and correlation of $R$ pairs repeat for each SNR, run the cell with a button.

```rust
//| id: signal-seismic-snr-sweep
//| ref: signal-seismic-snr-sweep
//| title: "Compare delay-estimation error across SNR values with the linearized prediction"
//| run: button
//| uses: [signal-seismic-model]
//| inputs:
//|   trials: {"type":"range","label":"Trial count R [-]","description":"Number of record pairs generated at each SNR.","min":50,"max":200,"step":50,"value":100}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed [-]","description":"Determine the noise sequence across all trials.","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":11}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":50}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/7/1","range":{"min":0.12,"max":0.21}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sweep","path":"/rows/7/5","equals":0}
use scioxide_random::{SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

let delay = 1.234;
let runs = trials.round() as usize;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed.round() as u64);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut bars = Vec::new();
for snr_db in [0.0_f64, 5.0, 10.0, 15.0, 20.0, 25.0, 30.0, 40.0] {
    let noise = noise_sd(snr_db);
    let mut errors = Vec::with_capacity(runs);
    for _ in 0..runs {
        let a = record(ARRIVAL, noise, &mut rng)?;
        let b = record(ARRIVAL + delay, noise, &mut rng)?;
        errors.push(estimate(&a, &b)?.1 - delay);
    }
    let count = runs as f64;
    let mean_square = errors.iter().map(|e| e * e).sum::<f64>() / count;
    let spread = errors
        .iter()
        .map(|e| (e * e - mean_square).powi(2))
        .sum::<f64>()
        / (count - 1.0);
    let rms = mean_square.sqrt();
    // RMS standard error: SE(mean square) / (2 RMS) (delta method)
    let rms_se = (spread / count).sqrt() / (2.0 * rms);
    let bias = errors.iter().sum::<f64>() / count;
    let skips = errors.iter().filter(|e| e.abs() > 0.1).count();
    let predicted = predicted_sd(noise);
    rows.push((
        snr_db,
        1000.0 * rms,
        1000.0 * rms_se,
        1000.0 * predicted,
        1000.0 * bias,
        skips,
    ));
    let ratio = rms / predicted;
    let half = 2.0 * rms_se / predicted;
    bars.push([snr_db, ratio, ratio - half, ratio + half]);
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "SNR [dB]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "RMS error [ms]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "RMS SE [ms]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Linearized σ_τ [ms]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Mean bias [ms]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Outliers |e| > 0.1 s [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sweep" })
);
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "errorbar",
    "xLabel": "SNR [dB]",
    "yLabel": "RMS error / linearized σ_τ [-]",
    "yType": "log",
    "series": [{ "name": "Ratio ±2 SE", "points": bars }],
    "annotations": [{
        "kind": "line",
        "axis": "y",
        "value": 1.0,
        "label": "Linearized prediction",
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }]
}));
```

At the default $R = 100$ and seed 11, RMS error at 40 dB is $0.173 \pm 0.011$ ms (±1 SE), agreeing within 1 SE with the linearized $\sigma_\tau = 0.163$ ms.
As SNR decreases, the RMS-error-to-prediction ratio departs from 1: 1.13 at 30 dB, 1.36 at 25 dB, 1.80 at 20 dB, and 2.17 at 15 dB, showing the growing contribution of noise–noise products.
No outliers occur at 15 dB or above; mean bias at 30 and 40 dB has absolute value below 0.05 ms, smaller than the RMS.
At 10 dB, 19 of 100 pairs are outliers and RMS error jumps to 473 ms; at 5 dB, 85 pairs are outliers, and at 0 dB, 95 pairs.
The plotted ratio jumps from about 2 to about 90 between 15 and 10 dB, placing the lower limit of the linearization's range of validity between them.
Setting $R = 200$ reduces SE by about a factor of $1/\sqrt 2$; ratios at levels of 15 dB or above remain unchanged within twice the SE.

## Summary

If subtracting $N - 1$ from the maximizing index of `correlate(b, a)` gives a positive lag, arrival at B is later than at A. Parabolic interpolation gives delays finer than the 10 ms sample interval, reaching an RMS error of 0.17 ms at 40 dB in this example.
At high SNR, error can be estimated by the linearized $\sigma_\tau = \sqrt{2\sigma^2/\sum s'^2}$, but as SNR drops, noise–noise products make it larger than predicted.
At still lower SNR, outliers that select neighboring or noise peaks increase error by orders of magnitude.
Assess estimate reliability from the error distribution over repeated trials, and crop records around arrival times before correlation if needed.
