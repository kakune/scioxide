Domain decomposition preconditions a large sparse system by combining solutions of smaller systems on subsets of unknowns.
Each smaller system can use sparse direct or incomplete factorization; additive combinations solve subdomains independently and can run in parallel.
Overlapping neighboring subdomains allows local solves to correct errors crossing their boundaries.
This page explains the 3 overlapping-Schwarz combinations, checks and omissions in `SchwarzPlan`, and iteration accounting, then examines overlap and subdomain-count effects on a two-dimensional Poisson equation.
[Incomplete factorizations and iterative-method preconditioning](oxref:sparse-preconditioning-role) explains what preconditioned iterations solve.

## Subdomains and local solves

Overlapping domain decomposition first partitions unknowns into $s$ disjoint **owned sets**, then expands each by $\delta$ graph-adjacency layers to form subdomains $\Omega_1, \dots, \Omega_s$.
Neighboring subdomains overlap by $2\delta$ layers.
Let $\mathbf{R}_i$ restrict to subdomain-$i$ unknowns, $\mathbf{A}_i = \mathbf{R}_i\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}_i^{\mathsf{T}}$ be the local matrix, and $\mathbf{M}_i \approx \mathbf{A}_i^{-1}$ its inverse action (local solve).
$\mathbf{M}_i$ may be a sparse direct solve or an approximation such as incomplete factorization.

## Three combinations

`SchwarzKind` combines preconditioner action $\mathbf{z}$ on residual $\mathbf{r}$ in 3 ways.

- **Additive Schwarz** (`Additive`) computes local corrections simultaneously, weights them by each subdomain's diagonal $\mathbf{W}_i$, and sums them.

  $$
  \mathbf{z} = \sum_i \mathbf{R}_i^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{W}_i\mathbf{M}_i\mathbf{R}_i\mathbf{r}
  $$

  Choose weights summing to 1 at every unknown (a partition of unity).
  The weighted form is generally non-Hermitian; combine it with GMRES or similar rather than CG.
  Classical unweighted additive Schwarz ($\mathbf{W}_i = \mathbf{I}$) is Hermitian, but overlap makes weight sums differ from 1, so `SchwarzPlan` cannot represent it.
  Only when every unknown belongs to exactly $c$ subdomains can $\mathbf{W}_i = \mathbf{I}/c$ represent it up to a constant factor.
- **Restricted additive Schwarz** (`RestrictedAdditive`) uses weight 1 on owned sets and 0 elsewhere, writing each correction only on its owned set.
  It requires less communication and often converges faster than classical additive Schwarz (Cai and Sarkis, 1999).
- **Multiplicative Schwarz** (`Multiplicative`) solves subdomains sequentially in supplied order, updating the residual each time.

  $$
  \mathbf{z} \leftarrow \mathbf{0}, \qquad \mathbf{z} \leftarrow \mathbf{z} + \mathbf{R}_i^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}_i\mathbf{R}_i(\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}) \quad (i = 1, \dots, s)
  $$

  Writing the full preconditioner action $\mathbf{r} \mapsto \mathbf{z}$ as $\mathbf{M}^{-1}$ gives $\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{A} = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{\Pi}_s)\cdots(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{\Pi}_1)$ ($\mathbf{\Pi}_i = \mathbf{R}_i^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}_i^{-1}\mathbf{R}_i\mathbf{A}$) for $\mathbf{M}_i = \mathbf{A}_i^{-1}$, corresponding to block Gauss–Seidel.
  It performs only one forward sweep, so this preconditioner is also non-Hermitian.
  It requires the full operator and runs sequentially even under a parallel policy.

## One-level methods and unavailable capabilities

Increasing overlap $\delta$ generally reduces iterations but enlarges local systems.
One-level Schwarz without coarse-space correction propagates information only to adjacent subdomains per application, so more subdomains require more iterations.
Reducing global errors requires a coarse correction such as multigrid.

`SchwarzPlan` provides only the 3 one-level methods above, excluding classical overlapping additive Schwarz ($\mathbf{W}_i = \mathbf{I}$) and two-level methods with coarse-space correction.
If either is needed, implement scioxide-core `Preconditioner` and combine local solves and corrections yourself.
For grid-independent convergence, use `AmgPreconditioner` from [Algebraic multigrid preconditioning](../algebraic-multigrid/).

## Constructing and validating SchwarzPlan

`SchwarzPlan::new(dimension, kind, subdomains, operator)` validates and copies subdomain indices and weights while borrowing local solves and the full operator.
`SchwarzPlan::with_options(dimension, kind, subdomains, operator, options)` constructs the same plan using tolerances and limits in `SchwarzOptions`.
`SchwarzSubdomainView` contains strictly increasing global indices `indices`, weights `weights` for the 2 additive methods, and local solve `solve` (`SchwarzLocalSolveView::InverseOperator(&op)` or `Preconditioner(&p)`).
The plan checks the following, returning `InvalidInput` or `DimensionMismatch` on failure (`NonFinite` for NaN/infinite weights).

- Nonempty subdomains with in-range, strictly increasing indices and local-solve dimension equal to index count.
- For both additive methods, weights in [0, 1] whose sum at each unknown is positive and equal to 1.
  Additive Schwarz permits tolerance `SchwarzOptions::with_partition_tolerance` (default `Tolerance::from_machine_epsilon()`); restricted additive requires exact sum 1 and weights only 0 or 1.
  Positive sums also establish that every unknown belongs to some subdomain.
- Multiplicative Schwarz takes no weights and requires full operator `SchwarzOperatorView::Sparse(view)` or `MatrixFree(&op)`; neither additive method takes a full operator.
  Multiplicative Schwarz accepts uncovered unknowns, leaving their corrections 0.

## Plan storage and accounting

`with_max_plan_bytes` and `with_max_workspace_bytes` in `SchwarzOptions::new()` bound logical plan and workspace bytes (unlimited by default); read usage through `plan_bytes()` and `preparation_peak_bytes()`.
Multiplicative sparse operators recompute residuals only for rows changed by local corrections; matrix-free operators apply the full operator after every correction.
The additive methods distribute subdomains among workers according to `SparseSolveOptions`'s execution policy at application time and sum corrections in subdomain order, producing worker-count-independent results.
One right-side application counts one iteration, each local solve one evaluation, and multiplicative residual updates each count one operator evaluation too.

## Overlap and subdomain count

The following cell partitions a five-point Laplacian on a $32 \times 32$ grid ($N = 1024$, row-major numbering) into $s$ owned grid-row strips, expanding each upward and downward by $\delta$ rows for overlap.
Strip unknowns are consecutive, so build each local matrix as principal submatrix $\mathbf{A}_i = \mathbf{R}_i\mathbf{A}\mathbf{R}_i^{\mathsf{T}}$ of $\mathbf{A}$ and factor it with single-call constructor `SparseCholesky::new`.
Additive weights are reciprocal to strip-coverage counts per point; restricted additive weights are 1 on owned sets.
Use all 3 Schwarz preconditioners with scioxide-linalg GMRES (restart 60, iteration limit 3000) to solve uniform-source system $\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{1}$ to $\lVert \mathbf{r} \rVert_2 \le 10^{-8}\lVert \mathbf{b} \rVert_2$, comparing iterations with no preconditioning.
Inputs are subdomain count $s$ (2–8) and overlap $\delta$ (0–4 rows).
Besides iteration counts and termination reasons, the table reports true relative residual $\lVert \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} \rVert_2/\lVert \mathbf{b} \rVert_2$ recomputed from the returned solution and plan size `plan_bytes()`.

```rust
//| id: sparse-schwarz-overlap
//| ref: sparse-schwarz-overlap
//| title: "Schwarz-preconditioned iteration counts versus overlap and subdomain count"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse, scioxide-linalg]
//| inputs:
//|   parts: {"type":"range","label":"Subdomains (strips) s","description":"Divide grid rows into s strips.","min":2,"max":8,"step":1,"value":4}
//|   overlap: {"type":"range","label":"Overlap δ [grid rows]","description":"Rows added above and below each strip.","min":0,"max":4,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"no-overlap","label":"δ = 0","description":"Without overlap, additive and restricted additive become the same block-Jacobi preconditioner and require more iterations.","inputs":{"overlap":0}}
//|   - {"id":"many-parts","label":"s = 8","description":"Without coarse correction, increasing subdomains increases additive iterations.","inputs":{"parts":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":59}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":14}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":13}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":10}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: no-overlap
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":21}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":21}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":15}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: many-parts
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/1/1","equals":17}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":13}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"schwarz","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
use scioxide_core::{Budget, LinearOperator, RealScalar, ResidualTolerance};
use scioxide_linalg::{GmresSolver, Preconditioning};
use scioxide_sparse::{
    CsrMatrix, ExplicitZeroPolicy, SchwarzKind, SchwarzLocalSolveView, SchwarzOperatorView,
    SchwarzOptions, SchwarzPlan, SchwarzSubdomainView, SparseCholesky, sparse_diagonals,
};

let (k, s, delta) = (32, parts as usize, overlap as usize);
let n = k * k;
// Five-point Laplacian on an m-row × k-column grid. Grid-row-strip principal submatrices have the same form.
let laplacian = |m: usize| -> Result<CsrMatrix<f64>, String> {
    let size = m * k;
    // Entry p connects indices p and p + 1; it is 0 at grid-row boundaries (dropped later).
    let x: Vec<f64> = (1..size)
        .map(|p| if p % k == 0 { 0.0 } else { -1.0 })
        .collect();
    let (y, diagonal) = (vec![-1.0_f64; size - k], vec![4.0_f64; size]);
    let offsets = [-(k as isize), -1, 0, 1, k as isize];
    sparse_diagonals(size, size, &offsets, &[&y, &x, &diagonal, &x, &y])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .view()
        .to_csr(ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let a = laplacian(k)?;
// Owned grid rows [i k/s, (i + 1) k/s); subdomains extend them δ rows upward and downward (index ranges).
let owned: Vec<(usize, usize)> = (0..s)
    .map(|i| (i * k / s * k, (i + 1) * k / s * k))
    .collect();
let spans: Vec<(usize, usize)> = owned
    .iter()
    .map(|&(lo, hi)| (lo.saturating_sub(delta * k), (hi + delta * k).min(n)))
    .collect();
// Cholesky-factor local matrix A_i = R_i A R_iᵀ with a single-call constructor.
let mut factors = Vec::new();
for &(start, end) in &spans {
    let local = laplacian((end - start) / k)?;
    factors.push(SparseCholesky::new(&local).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}
let indices: Vec<Vec<usize>> = spans
    .iter()
    .map(|&(start, end)| (start..end).collect())
    .collect();
// Additive weights are reciprocal strip-coverage counts; restricted additive weights are 1 on owned sets.
let cover: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|j| spans.iter().filter(|r| r.0 <= j && j < r.1).count() as f64)
    .collect();
let additive: Vec<Vec<f64>> = indices
    .iter()
    .map(|ix| ix.iter().map(|&j| 1.0 / cover[j]).collect())
    .collect();
let restricted: Vec<Vec<f64>> = owned
    .iter()
    .zip(&indices)
    .map(|(&(lo, hi), ix)| {
        ix.iter()
            .map(|&j| if (lo..hi).contains(&j) { 1.0 } else { 0.0 })
            .collect()
    })
    .collect();
let b = vec![1.0_f64; n];
let tolerance = ResidualTolerance::new(0.0, 1e-8).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let limit = Budget::try_from(3000).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let restart = Budget::try_from(60).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solver = GmresSolver::new(tolerance, limit).with_restart(restart);
// Independent check: recompute true relative residual ‖b − Ax‖₂ / ‖b‖₂ from returned solutions (b = 1, ‖b‖₂ = √N).
let mut scratch = vec![0.0_f64; a.apply_workspace_len().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?];
let mut relative = |x: &[f64]| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let mut ax = vec![0.0_f64; n];
    a.apply_into(x, &mut ax, &mut scratch)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let squares = ax
        .iter()
        .fold(0.0_f64, |sum, y| sum + (1.0 - y) * (1.0 - y));
    Ok(RealScalar::sqrt(squares / n as f64))
};
let none = solver
    .solve_with_options(&a, &b[..], Preconditioning::None, None)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rows = vec![(
    "None",
    none.report().iterations(),
    format!("{:?}", none.report().termination()),
    relative(none.solution().as_slice())?,
    None,
)];
for (name, kind) in [
    ("Additive", SchwarzKind::Additive),
    ("Restricted additive", SchwarzKind::RestrictedAdditive),
    ("Multiplicative", SchwarzKind::Multiplicative),
] {
    let weights = |i: usize| match kind {
        SchwarzKind::Additive => Some(&additive[i][..]),
        SchwarzKind::RestrictedAdditive => Some(&restricted[i][..]),
        SchwarzKind::Multiplicative => None,
    };
    let domains: Vec<SchwarzSubdomainView<'_, f64>> = (0..s)
        .map(|i| SchwarzSubdomainView {
            indices: &indices[i],
            weights: weights(i),
            solve: SchwarzLocalSolveView::InverseOperator(&factors[i]),
        })
        .collect();
    // Only multiplicative Schwarz uses the full matrix for residual updates.
    let global =
        (kind == SchwarzKind::Multiplicative).then(|| SchwarzOperatorView::Sparse(a.view()));
    let options = SchwarzOptions::new()
        .with_max_plan_bytes(1 << 24)
        .with_max_workspace_bytes(1 << 24);
    let plan = SchwarzPlan::with_options(n, kind, &domains, global, options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let result = solver
        .solve_with_options(&a, &b[..], Preconditioning::Inverse(&plan), None)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        name,
        result.report().iterations(),
        format!("{:?}", result.report().termination()),
        relative(result.solution().as_slice())?,
        Some(plan.plan_bytes()),
    ));
}
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Preconditioner" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "GMRES iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "True relative residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "plan_bytes() [B]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "schwarz" })
);
```

At the defaults $s = 4$, $\delta = 1$, unpreconditioned GMRES requires 59 iterations, while additive converges in 14, restricted additive in 13, and multiplicative in 10.
All true relative residuals recomputed from solutions lie between $7.7 \times 10^{-10}$ and $7.6 \times 10^{-9}$, meeting tolerance $10^{-8}$ as termination `Converged` indicates.
Increasing overlap as $\delta = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ decreases restricted-additive iterations to 21, 13, 10, 9, 8 and multiplicative iterations to 15, 10, 8, 6, 6.
At $\delta = 0$ all weights are 1; additive and restricted additive both become block Jacobi with strip diagonal-block inverses and both require 21 iterations.
Weighted additive iterations are 21, 14, 11, 9, 12, increasing again at $\delta = 4$ rather than decreasing monotonically with overlap.
Keeping $\delta = 1$ and increasing subdomains as $s = 2, 4, 8$ (equal-row strips) increases additive iterations to 8, 14, 17.
Unequal-row partitions $s = 5, 6, 7$ require 18, 20, 20 iterations, more than $s = 8$.
From $s = 2$ through 8, multiplicative iterations increase without decrease as 6, 8, 10, 10, 11, 12, 13.
Without coarse correction, more strips require more iterations to propagate information between distant strips.
Here sequential residual updates let multiplicative Schwarz converge in fewer iterations than additive for the same partition, but its subdomain work cannot run in parallel.
`plan_bytes()` sums copied indices and weights, per-subdomain records, and, for multiplicative Schwarz, lists of rows affected by each correction.
At the default partition, additive uses 14816 B and multiplicative 9440 B (wasm values with 32-bit `usize`).

## Summary

Overlapping Schwarz combines subdomain local solves as a preconditioner; `SchwarzPlan` provides weighted additive, restricted additive, and multiplicative forms.
With overlap all 3 are generally non-Hermitian, so combine them with Krylov methods such as GMRES rather than CG.
Increasing overlap from 0 to only 1 row cuts iterations to about 2/3 but enlarges local systems; weighted additive iterations need not decrease monotonically with overlap.
These are one-level methods without coarse correction, so iterations tend to grow with subdomains; use multigrid when grid-independent convergence is required.
Multiplicative Schwarz uses the fewest iterations for the same partition here, but solves subdomains sequentially and cannot run them in parallel.
