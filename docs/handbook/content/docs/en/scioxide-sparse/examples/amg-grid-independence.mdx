For PDEs on fine grids, iterative work is the work per iteration multiplied by the iteration count.
One iteration costs proportionally to the number of unknowns $N$; if iteration count is independent of refinement, total work is $O(N)$.
This example solves the two-dimensional Poisson equation by conjugate gradients (CG), comparing iteration counts under refinement for Jacobi, incomplete Cholesky IC(0), and smoothed-aggregation algebraic multigrid (AMG).
Independent checks use the closed-form condition number's iteration bound and growth (roughly proportional to points per grid side $n$ for Jacobi) and operator complexity recounted from level nonzeros.

## Condition numbers and iteration counts

For symmetric positive definite $\mathbf{A}$ with symmetric positive definite preconditioner $\mathbf{M}$, CG error $\mathbf{e}_k = \mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}^\ast$ at iteration $k$ is bounded in energy norm $\|\mathbf{e}\|_{\mathbf{A}} = \sqrt{\mathbf{e}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{e}}$ by

$$
\|\mathbf{e}_k\|_{\mathbf{A}} \le 2\rho^k\,\|\mathbf{e}_0\|_{\mathbf{A}}, \qquad \rho = \frac{\sqrt{\kappa} - 1}{\sqrt{\kappa} + 1}, \qquad \kappa = \kappa(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{A})
$$

where $\kappa$ is the ratio of largest to smallest eigenvalues of $\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{A}$.
`CgSolver` tests stopping by residual, not error.
When the recurrence residual satisfies tolerance, and also every 50 iterations and at the iteration limit, it recomputes the true residual $\mathbf{r}_k = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_k$ and stops with `Converged` only if $\|\mathbf{r}_k\|_2 \le \mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}\,\|\mathbf{b}\|_2$ holds.
Use $\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{A})\|\mathbf{e}\|_{\mathbf{A}}^2 \le \|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{e}\|_2^2 \le \lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{A})\|\mathbf{e}\|_{\mathbf{A}}^2$ to transfer the bound to residuals.
Starting from $\mathbf{x}_0 = \mathbf{0}$ gives

$$
\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_k\|_2}{\|\mathbf{b}\|_2} \le 2\sqrt{\kappa(\mathbf{A})}\,\rho^k
$$

so the smallest count making the right side no greater than tolerance $\varepsilon$,

$$
k_\kappa = \left\lceil \frac{\ln\bigl(2\sqrt{\kappa(\mathbf{A})}/\varepsilon\bigr)}{\ln(1/\rho)} \right\rceil \approx \frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}\,\ln\frac{2\sqrt{\kappa(\mathbf{A})}}{\varepsilon}
$$

bounds the iteration count in exact arithmetic.

The five-point Laplacian on a $n \times n$ grid (diagonal 4, four neighbors $-1$, mesh spacing $h = 1/(n + 1)$) has eigenvalues $4 - 2\cos(i\pi h) - 2\cos(j\pi h)$ ($1 \le i, j \le n$).
The maximum and minimum occur at $i = j = n$ and $i = j = 1$; using $\cos(n\pi h) = -\cos(\pi h)$ gives

$$
\kappa(\mathbf{A}) = \frac{1 + \cos(\pi h)}{1 - \cos(\pi h)} = \cot^2\frac{\pi h}{2} \approx \frac{4}{\pi^2 h^2}
$$

Since $\sqrt{\kappa(\mathbf{A})} \approx 2(n + 1)/\pi$, the unpreconditioned CG bound $k_\kappa$ grows proportionally to $n = \sqrt{N}$ apart from logarithmic factors.
All diagonal entries are 4, so Jacobi $\mathbf{M} = 4\mathbf{I}$ merely divides the whole matrix by a constant; exact arithmetic follows the same iterates as unpreconditioned CG, preserving condition number and iteration count.
IC(0) reduces the condition-number constant but not the growth of $O(h^{-2})$, so iterations still grow roughly proportionally to $n$.
scioxide-sparse does not provide modified incomplete Cholesky (MIC, Gustafsson, 1978), which lowers conditioning to $O(h^{-1})$; use multigrid to change the growth rate.
Multigrid combines smoothing and coarse-grid correction to reduce all error frequencies by a fixed fraction, making iteration counts nearly independent of $n$ for elliptic problems such as Poisson ([algebraic multigrid preconditioning](../../algorithms/algebraic-multigrid/)).

### Operator complexity

An AMG hierarchy builds coarse matrices $\mathbf{A}_1, \mathbf{A}_2, \dots$ from fine-grid matrix $\mathbf{A}_0 = \mathbf{A}$.
Work per application can be estimated from smoothing counts and operator complexity

$$
C_{\mathrm{op}} = \frac{\sum_{\ell} \mathrm{nnz}(\mathbf{A}_\ell)}{\mathrm{nnz}(\mathbf{A}_0)}
$$

as an approximate work measure.
If $C_{\mathrm{op}}$ remains a constant near 1, each V-cycle costs a constant multiple of fine-grid matrix–vector work; constant iterations then give nearly $O(N)$ total work.
In `AmgDiagnostics` returned by `AmgHierarchy::diagnostics()`, `operator_complexity()` is this ratio, `levels()` the level count, and `termination()` the coarsening stop reason (`CoarseSize`, `LevelLimit`, or `Stagnation` of `AmgSetupTermination`).
These estimate cycle work and do not predict convergence rate.

## Grid size and iterations

The following cell solves five-point Laplacians for $n = 16, 32, 48, 64$ ($N = n^2$ from 256 to 4096) with right-hand side $\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{1}$ using scioxide-linalg's `CgSolver`.
Stopping uses $\|\mathbf{r}\|_2 \le 10^{-8}\|\mathbf{b}\|_2$ (`ResidualTolerance::new(0.0, 1e-8)`), with iteration limit 2000.
The preconditioners are Jacobi and IC(0) from all-in-one setup constructors `JacobiPreconditioner::new` and `IncompleteFactor::ic0`, and AMG built with `AmgHierarchy::build` and `AmgPreconditioner::build`.
AMG uses a constant near-nullspace vector, default coarsening settings (`AmgSetupOptions::new()`: coarse size 16, strength threshold 1/16), and limits `AmgLimits::default()` (up to 64 levels, no entry or byte limit); the coarsest matrix is factorized by `SparseCholesky` with limits from `SparseSymmetricLimits`.
All-in-one setup constructor `AmgPreconditioner::new` builds the same hierarchy but fixes a V-cycle with one pre- and post-smoothing step; because this cell varies cycles, it uses `build`.
All three implement scioxide-core's `Preconditioner` and are passed through `Preconditioning::Inverse` to options form `solve_with_options`.
CG requires symmetric positive definite preconditioning.
For an AMG cycle, this requires mutually adjoint pre/post smoothers with equal counts, $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$ at every level, and a symmetric positive definite coarsest solver ([use as a preconditioner](oxref:sparse-amg-as-preconditioner)).
`AmgPreconditioner` uses damped Jacobi smoothing, $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$, and coarsest-level Cholesky, leaving only the pre/post counts to match.
Since `AmgCycleOptions` allows separate pre/post counts, the cell sets both to $\nu$.

Inputs are AMG pre/post smoothing count $\nu$ (1–3) and cycle kind (V or W).
The table shows iteration count, termination reason, and true relative residual $\|\mathbf{r}\|_2/\|\mathbf{b}\|_2$ for each grid and preconditioner.
Compare counts with the closed-form bound $k_\kappa$ computed in the next cell.
This cell passes tolerance, grid-wise AMG hierarchy diagnostics (level count, `operator_complexity()`, operator complexity recounted from each level matrix's `nnz()`, and coarsening stop reason), each solve's iterations, reason and success, and whether finest-grid AMG-preconditioned CG broke down to the next cell.
The twelve solves take time, so execution uses a button and reuses results for identical inputs.

```rust
//| id: sparse-amg-convergence
//| ref: sparse-amg-convergence
//| title: "CG iterations and grid size for the two-dimensional Poisson equation"
//| run: button
//| cache: inputs
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse, scioxide-linalg]
//| exports: [epsilon, hierarchies, solves, converged, breakdown]
//| inputs:
//|   sweeps: {"type":"range","label":"AMG pre/post smoothing count ν","description":"Damped Jacobi steps before and after coarse-grid correction at each cycle level.","min":1,"max":3,"step":1,"value":1}
//|   cycle: {"type":"select","label":"AMG cycle","description":"Perform one (V) or two (W) coarse-grid corrections per level.","options":["V","W"],"value":"V"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"two-sweeps","label":"ν = 2","description":"More smoothing reduces AMG iterations but increases work per application.","inputs":{"sweeps":2}}
//|   - {"id":"w-cycle","label":"W-cycle","description":"Two coarse corrections further reduce grid dependence of iteration counts.","inputs":{"cycle":"W"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":20,"max":116}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/9/2","range":{"min":100,"max":473}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/10/2","range":{"min":40,"max":119}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"max":20}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/11/2","range":{"max":20}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/11/3","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/11/4","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: two-sweeps
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/11/2","range":{"max":15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/11/3","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/11/4","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|   - case: w-cycle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"max":15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/11/2","range":{"max":15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"solves","path":"/rows/11/3","equals":"Converged"}
use scioxide_core::{
    BlockVectorView, Budget, ExecutionContext, Preconditioner, ResidualTolerance,
};
use scioxide_linalg::{CgSolver, LinearSolveTermination, Preconditioning};
use scioxide_sparse::{
    AmgCycleKind, AmgCycleOptions, AmgHierarchy, AmgLimits, AmgPreconditioner, AmgSetupOptions,
    CsrMatrix, DuplicatePolicy, ExplicitZeroPolicy, IncompleteFactor, JacobiPreconditioner,
    SparseBuilder, SparseSolveOptions, SparseSymmetricLimits,
};

let kind = if cycle == "W" {
    AmgCycleKind::W
} else {
    AmgCycleKind::V
};
let cycle_options = AmgCycleOptions::new()
    .with_kind(kind)
    .with_pre_sweeps(sweeps as usize)
    .with_post_sweeps(sweeps as usize);
let (options, scope) = (SparseSolveOptions::new(), ExecutionContext::sequential());
let epsilon = 1e-8;
let tolerance = ResidualTolerance::new(0.0, epsilon).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let budget = Budget::try_from(2000).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solver = CgSolver::new(tolerance, budget);
// Five-point differences: grid point (i, j) has index p = i n + j, diagonal 4, and four neighbors −1.
let poisson = |n: usize| -> Result<CsrMatrix<f64>, String> {
    let mut builder = SparseBuilder::new(n * n, n * n);
    for (i, j) in (0..n).flat_map(|i| (0..n).map(move |j| (i, j))) {
        let p = i * n + j;
        let neighbors = [
            (j > 0, p.wrapping_sub(1)),
            (j + 1 < n, p + 1),
            (i > 0, p.wrapping_sub(n)),
            (i + 1 < n, p + n),
        ];
        builder.push(p, p, 4.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        for (_, q) in neighbors.into_iter().filter(|e| e.0) {
            builder.push(p, q, -1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        }
    }
    builder
        .finish_csr(DuplicatePolicy::Reject, ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let names = ["Jacobi", "IC(0)", "AMG"];
let (mut hierarchies, mut solves, mut converged) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut finest = None;
for n in [16usize, 32, 48, 64] {
    let (a, size) = (poisson(n)?, n * n);
    let b = vec![1.0_f64; size];
    let jacobi = JacobiPreconditioner::new(&a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let ic = IncompleteFactor::ic0(&a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let modes = BlockVectorView::new(&b, size, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let hierarchy = AmgHierarchy::build(
        a.view(),
        modes,
        AmgSetupOptions::new(),
        AmgLimits::default(),
        options,
        scope,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let coarse_limits = SparseSymmetricLimits::new(1 << 12, 1 << 12, 1 << 22);
    let amg = AmgPreconditioner::build(hierarchy, cycle_options, coarse_limits, options, scope)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let d = amg.hierarchy().diagnostics();
    // Recount operator complexity from each level matrix's nonzeros (independent check).
    let entries: usize = (0..d.levels())
        .filter_map(|level| amg.hierarchy().matrix(level))
        .map(|m| m.nnz())
        .sum();
    let recount = entries as f64 / a.nnz() as f64;
    let setup = format!("{:?}", d.termination());
    hierarchies.push((n, d.levels(), d.operator_complexity(), recount, setup));
    let preconditioners: [&dyn Preconditioner<f64>; 3] = [&jacobi, &ic, &amg];
    for (name, preconditioner) in names.into_iter().zip(preconditioners) {
        let result = solver
            .solve_with_options(&a, &b[..], Preconditioning::Inverse(preconditioner), None)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let report = result.report();
        let termination = report.termination();
        let residual = report.true_residual().map(|r| r.scaled_norm());
        let status = format!("{termination:?}");
        solves.push((n, name, report.iterations(), status, residual));
        converged.push(termination.is_success());
        // The final retained status is AMG-preconditioned CG on the finest grid (n = 64).
        finest = Some(termination);
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Preconditioner" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Termination reason" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative residual [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    solves,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "solves" })
);
// Store success of the last solve used for the outcome in converged and breakdown in breakdown.
let finest = finest.ok_or("No AMG row")?;
let breakdown = matches!(finest, LinearSolveTermination::Breakdown(_));
export!(epsilon, epsilon);
export!(hierarchies, hierarchies);
export!(solves, solves);
export!(converged, converged);
export!(breakdown, breakdown);
```

The next cell receives these results and computes $\kappa(\mathbf{A}) = \cot^2(\pi h/2)$ and bound $k_\kappa$ for each grid from closed forms (Jacobi values where $\kappa(\mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{A}) = \kappa(\mathbf{A})$).
The table shows $\kappa(\mathbf{A})$, $k_\kappa$, and AMG hierarchy diagnostics, checking agreement between `operator_complexity()` and the recount.
The chart plots tolerance-satisfying iteration counts against $n$ on logarithmic axes, with bound $k_\kappa$ dashed.
The final outcome uses finest-grid AMG-preconditioned CG status `LinearSolveTermination`.
It reports satisfied if `is_success()` is true (`Converged`), failed for `Breakdown`, and partial otherwise (iteration limit, stagnation, execution stop, and similar reasons).
This cell follows the preceding cell's inputs and has no controls of its own.

```rust
//| id: sparse-amg-convergence-report
//| ref: sparse-amg-convergence-report
//| title: "CG iterations, condition-number bounds, and AMG hierarchy diagnostics"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
//| dependsOn:
//|   solved: sparse-amg-convergence
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"grids","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":1711.661375826,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"grids","path":"/rows/3/2","equals":473}
//|       - {"artifact":"grids","path":"/rows/3/4","approx":{"value":1.3640723892,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"grids","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":1.3640723892,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"grids","path":"/rows/3/6","equals":"CoarseSize"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

let epsilon = solved["epsilon"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing tolerance")?;
let hierarchies: Vec<(usize, usize, Option<f64>, f64, String)> =
    serde_json::from_value(solved["hierarchies"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let solves: Vec<(usize, String, usize, String, Option<f64>)> =
    serde_json::from_value(solved["solves"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let converged: Vec<bool> =
    serde_json::from_value(solved["converged"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut grids, mut bounds) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for (n, levels, complexity, recount, setup) in hierarchies {
    // Closed forms: κ(A) = cot²(πh/2), and bound k_κ from ‖r_k‖/‖b‖ ≤ 2√κ ρ^k.
    let h = 1.0 / (n + 1) as f64;
    let kappa = RealScalar::powi(1.0 / RealScalar::tan(0.5 * core::f64::consts::PI * h), 2);
    let rho = (RealScalar::sqrt(kappa) - 1.0) / (RealScalar::sqrt(kappa) + 1.0);
    let bound = RealScalar::ceil(
        RealScalar::ln(2.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(kappa) / epsilon) / RealScalar::ln(1.0 / rho),
    );
    bounds.push([n as f64, bound]);
    grids.push((n, kappa, bound as usize, levels, complexity, recount, setup));
}
let (sci3, fixed3) = (
    serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 }),
    serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 }),
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "κ(A) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Bound k_κ [iterations]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "AMG levels [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "C_op [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Recounted C_op [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Coarsening stop reason" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    grids,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "grids" })
);
// Plot only solves satisfying tolerance.
let mut series: Vec<serde_json::Value> = ["Jacobi", "IC(0)", "AMG"]
    .into_iter()
    .map(|name| {
        let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = solves
            .iter()
            .zip(&converged)
            .filter(|(row, ok)| row.1 == name && **ok)
            .map(|(row, _)| [row.0 as f64, row.2 as f64])
            .collect();
        serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points, "style": { "showSymbol": true } })
    })
    .collect();
series.push(serde_json::json!({
    "name": "Bound k_κ",
    "points": bounds,
    "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
}));
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Points per grid side n [-]",
        "yLabel": "CG iterations [count]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
// Outcome: termination status of the final solve (AMG-preconditioned CG at n = 64).
let (_, _, iterations, status, residual) = solves.last().ok_or("No solve row")?;
let success = converged.last() == Some(&true);
let outcome = if success {
    Outcome::satisfied("AMG-preconditioned CG at n = 64 satisfied tolerance")
} else if solved["breakdown"].as_bool() == Some(true) {
    Outcome::failed("AMG-preconditioned CG at n = 64 broke down")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("AMG-preconditioned CG at n = 64 stopped before satisfying tolerance").explanation(
        "The returned solution has the smallest residual among iterates whose true residual was checked at checkpoints.",
    )
};
let mut outcome =
    outcome
        .status(status.clone())
        .diagnostic("Iteration count", *iterations as f64, None);
if let Some(value) = residual {
    outcome = outcome.diagnostic("Relative residual", *value, None);
}
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```

With default $\nu = 1$ and a V-cycle, Jacobi iterations are 28, 59, 89, and 119 at $n = 16, 32, 48, 64$, growing roughly proportionally to $n$.
Bounds $k_\kappa$ are 116, 233, 352, and 473; actual counts stay near one quarter of the bound on every grid (ratios 0.24–0.25), matching its predicted growth.
Growth from $n = 16$ to 64 is a factor of 4.25, near the bound ratio 4.1 (the $\sqrt{\kappa(\mathbf{A})}$ ratio 3.8 multiplied by the ratio of the logarithmically growing factors).
IC(0) takes 17, 29, 40, and 52 iterations, 0.44–0.61 times Jacobi, but still grows roughly proportionally to $n$.
AMG takes 12, 15, 16, and 17 iterations: a sixteenfold increase in unknowns increases iterations only 1.4-fold, appearing nearly horizontal on the chart.
AMG hierarchies have three to four levels and stop at coarse size 16 on every grid (`CoarseSize`), with operator complexity remaining between 1.357 and 1.374.
Recounting each level's `nnz()` agrees with `operator_complexity()`.
Every row terminates with `Converged` and true relative residual below tolerance $10^{-8}$, giving a satisfied outcome.

At $\nu = 2$, AMG iterations fall to 9, 11, 12, and 13, but doubled smoothing per application means work decreases less than the iteration count.
W-cycles take 12, 12, 13, and 13 iterations, further reducing grid-size dependence.
W-cycles perform two coarse corrections per level and cost more per application than V-cycles.

## Summary

The five-point Poisson condition number is $\cot^2(\pi h/2) = O(n^2)$; unpreconditioned and Jacobi-preconditioned CG iterations grow roughly proportionally to points per side $n$.
Condition-number bound $k_\kappa$ predicts growth; actual counts here are about one quarter of it.
IC(0) reduces the iteration constant but not its growth, widening the gap from multigrid under refinement.
Smoothed-aggregation AMG keeps iterations nearly constant and operator complexity near 1, making total work roughly proportional to unknown count.
For AMG with CG, use equal pre/post smoothing counts; these convergence comparisons are observations, not guarantees, so recheck `AmgDiagnostics` and iterations when the problem changes.
