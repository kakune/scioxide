This chapter's examples assemble sparse matrices from concrete models in physics, engineering, networks, and surveying, solving them with direct methods, preconditioned iteration, and graph algorithms.
Every example includes an independent check using an analytical solution, identity, closed form, or another method.
Changing controls from their defaults reveals how matrix size, coefficient variation, random seed, and algorithm selection affect results.

## Example index

## Summary

The spring, Poisson-matrix, and resistor-network examples cover symmetric positive definite assembly and direct methods.
PageRank compares iterative and direct methods for nonsymmetric systems; algebraic multigrid covers preconditioner selection, leveling networks rank-deficient least squares, and graph partitioning Laplacian eigenvectors.
Use quantities known independently of the computation, such as analytical values and identities, rather than accepting results solely for small residuals.
Ordering and preconditioning effects emerge with matrix size, so vary size controls in addition to the defaults.
