In leveling, height differences between pairs of benchmarks are measured, and observations over many sections form a network that determines each point's elevation.
The observations contain errors and there are more sections than points, so elevations are estimated by least squares.
However, height differences determine only differences in elevation, so the solution is not unique unless the elevation of some point is supplied as a reference.
This example solves a network without a fixed reference point using the minimum-norm solution of a sparse QR factorization and compares it with a solution with a fixed reference point.
Independent checks use the estimated rank, the sum of minimum-norm elevations, the difference between the two solutions, the relation between misclosures and residuals, the sum of redundancy numbers and degrees of freedom, and the symmetry of standard errors and effective resistances.

## Observation equations

Let $h_i$ [m] be the elevations of benchmarks $P_0, \dots, P_{n-1}$, and suppose the height difference $\Delta h_k$ [m] from point $u_k$ to point $v_k$ is observed along section $k$.
Writing the observation error as $\epsilon_k$, the observation equation is

$$
\Delta h_k = h_{v_k} - h_{u_k} + \epsilon_k \qquad (k = 0, \dots, m - 1)
$$

which can be written in matrix form as $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h} \approx \Delta\mathbf{h}$.
Row $k$ of $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ has $-1$ in column $u_k$, $+1$ in column $v_k$, and zeros elsewhere; this is the incidence matrix of the network graph.
With only two nonzeros per row, $\mathbf{A}$ is sparse.

### Missing datum and rank

If the network is connected, adding the same value to every elevation leaves height differences unchanged, giving $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{0}$.
The null space of a connected graph's incidence matrix is spanned by $\mathbf{1}$, so the rank of $\mathbf{A}$ is exactly $n - 1$.
The least-squares solutions form a line of the form $\mathbf{h} + t\mathbf{1}$ (with arbitrary $t$), so they are not unique.

Fixing the elevation of one point $P_0$ at $h_0 = 0$ m makes the columns of $\mathbf{A}_0$, obtained by removing column 0 from $\mathbf{A}$, linearly independent and determines a unique least-squares solution $\mathbf{h}^{(0)}$.
Without fixing a point, choose the minimum-norm solution $\mathbf{h}^{+}$, the point on the solution line with the smallest norm.
Because $\mathbf{h}^{+}$ lies in the row space of $\mathbf{A}$ and is orthogonal to the null space $\mathbf{1}$, it satisfies

$$
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} h_i^{+} = 0
$$

The two solutions lie on the same line, so their difference $\mathbf{h}^{+} - \mathbf{h}^{(0)}$ is the same constant $-\frac{1}{n}\sum_i h_i^{(0)}$ at every point.
Both are least-squares solutions, so their residual norms $\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h} - \Delta\mathbf{h}\|_2$ are also equal.

### Misclosures and residuals

Along a cycle $C$ that follows sections back to its starting point, the sum of true height differences is 0.
The observation sum $w_C = \sum_{k \in C} \pm\Delta h_k$ (with signs chosen to match the cycle direction) is called the **misclosure** and represents the sum of observation errors.
Writing the residual as $r_k = (\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h})_k - \Delta h_k$, the sum of $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h}$ along a cycle is 0, so

$$
\sum_{k \in C} \pm r_k = -w_C
$$

holds for every $\mathbf{h}$.
Adjustment can be viewed as distributing the misclosure among section residuals.

### Estimating the error magnitude

Suppose the observation errors $\epsilon_k$ are independent random variables with mean 0 and standard deviation $\sigma$.
The anchored solution has residual $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{A}_0\mathbf{h}^{(0)} - \Delta\mathbf{h} = -(\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi})\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$, where $\boldsymbol{\Pi} = \mathbf{A}_0(\mathbf{A}_0^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}_0)^{-1}\mathbf{A}_0^{\mathsf{T}}$ is the orthogonal projection onto the column space of $\mathbf{A}$.
Since $\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi}$ is an orthogonal projection of rank $m - (n - 1)$, $\mathrm{E}\|\mathbf{r}\|_2^2 = \sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi}) = \sigma^2(m - n + 1)$, and the square of

$$
\hat{\sigma} = \sqrt{\frac{\|\mathbf{r}\|_2^2}{m - (n - 1)}}
$$

is an unbiased estimator of $\sigma^2$.
$\hat{\sigma}$ is called the a posteriori standard deviation of unit weight.
The denominator $m - (n - 1)$ is the number of redundant observations (degrees of freedom); few degrees of freedom give a highly variable estimate.

$\rho_k = 1 - \Pi_{kk}$ is called the **redundancy number** of observation $k$.
$\rho_k$ lies between 0 and 1 and represents the fraction of the error in observation $k$ that appears in residual $r_k$; the sum is $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{I} - \boldsymbol{\Pi}) = m - n + 1$.
A section that forms no cycle with other observations has $\rho_k = 0$; its error does not appear in the residual and cannot be detected.

### Standard errors and effective resistances

The covariance matrix of the anchored solution is $\sigma^2(\mathbf{A}_0^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}_0)^{-1}$, so the standard error at point $P_i$ can be estimated by $\hat{\sigma}\sqrt{G_{ii}}$, $\mathbf{G} = (\mathbf{A}_0^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}_0)^{-1}$.
$\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}$ is the graph Laplacian of the network when each section is treated as a 1 Ω resistor, and $\mathbf{A}_0^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}_0$ is the matrix obtained by removing the reference point's row and column (the Laplacian with the reference point grounded).
Thus $G_{ii}$ equals the effective resistance $R_{\mathrm{eff}}(P_0, P_i)$ [Ω] between the reference point and $P_i$ ([Nodal analysis of resistor networks and Foster's theorem](../resistor-network/)).
Changing the reference point to $P_j$ changes $\mathbf{G}$, but the symmetry of effective resistance, $R_{\mathrm{eff}}(P_i, P_j) = R_{\mathrm{eff}}(P_j, P_i)$, means the standard error at $P_i$ with $P_j$ fixed equals the standard error at $P_j$ with $P_i$ fixed.
The standard error measures the accuracy of a height difference from the reference point and grows for distant points with fewer alternative paths.

The diagonal of the projection is $\Pi_{kk} = G_{u_k u_k} + G_{v_k v_k} - 2G_{u_k v_k} = R_{\mathrm{eff}}(u_k, v_k)$ (taking $G$ with a reference-point index as 0), and the redundancy number is $\rho_k = 1 - R_{\mathrm{eff}}(u_k, v_k)$.
The column space of $\mathbf{A}_0$ is the column space of $\mathbf{A}$ regardless of the reference point, so redundancy numbers also do not depend on the reference point.
The fact that redundancy numbers sum to $m - n + 1$ is equivalent to Foster's theorem, $\sum_k R_{\mathrm{eff}}(u_k, v_k) = n - 1$.

## Network and observation model

The experimental network has 6 points and 9 sections.
The true elevations are $(0, 1.250, 2.875, 1.640, 0.430, 3.210)$ m, and the sections are $0 \to 1$, $1 \to 2$, $2 \to 3$, $3 \to 0$, $1 \to 4$, $4 \to 3$, $2 \to 5$, $5 \to 3$, and $0 \to 4$.
A normal random variate with standard deviation $\sigma$ [mm], generated by the Box–Muller method, is added to each observation.
The following block contains definitions shared by the two cells on this page.
`HEIGHTS` and `SECTIONS` correspond to the true elevations and sections, `observations` to observations $\Delta\mathbf{h}$, and `design` to the observation matrix $\mathbf{A}$ (or $\mathbf{A}_j$ with the reference-point column removed when a reference is fixed).

```rust
//| id: sparse-leveling-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Leveling network sections and observation equations"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_sparse::{CsrMatrix, DuplicatePolicy, ExplicitZeroPolicy, SparseBuilder};

/// True elevations of benchmarks P₀–P₅ [m].
pub(crate) const HEIGHTS: [f64; 6] = [0.0, 1.250, 2.875, 1.640, 0.430, 3.210];

/// Start point u_k and end point v_k of section k (observe the height difference from u_k to v_k).
pub(crate) const SECTIONS: [(usize, usize); 9] = [
    (0, 1),
    (1, 2),
    (2, 3),
    (3, 0),
    (1, 4),
    (4, 3),
    (2, 5),
    (5, 3),
    (0, 4),
];

/// Observation Δh_k = h_{v_k} − h_{u_k} + ε_k [m]. ε_k is a normal variate with standard deviation σ [mm] (Box–Muller).
pub(crate) fn observations(noise_mm: f64, seed: u64) -> Vec<f64> {
    let mut state = 0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15_u64 ^ seed;
    let mut uniform = move || {
        state ^= state << 13;
        state ^= state >> 7;
        state ^= state << 17;
        ((state >> 11) as f64 + 0.5) / (1u64 << 53) as f64
    };
    SECTIONS
        .iter()
        .map(|&(u, v)| {
            let z = RealScalar::sqrt(-2.0 * RealScalar::ln(uniform()))
                * RealScalar::cos(2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI * uniform());
            HEIGHTS[v] - HEIGHTS[u] + noise_mm * 1e-3 * z
        })
        .collect()
}

/// Observation matrix A (row k has −1 in column u_k and +1 in column v_k).
/// For fixed = Some(j), use point j as the reference and remove its column.
pub(crate) fn design(fixed: Option<usize>) -> Result<CsrMatrix<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let cols = HEIGHTS.len() - usize::from(fixed.is_some());
    let mut builder = SparseBuilder::new(SECTIONS.len(), cols);
    for (k, &(u, v)) in SECTIONS.iter().enumerate() {
        for (point, sign) in [(u, -1.0), (v, 1.0)] {
            // Shift columns to the right of the removed column one position to the left.
            match fixed {
                Some(j) if point == j => {}
                Some(j) if point > j => builder.push(k, point - 1, sign)?,
                _ => builder.push(k, point, sign)?,
            }
        }
    }
    builder.finish_csr(DuplicatePolicy::Reject, ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop)
}
```

## Adjusting a network without a fixed datum

The following cell solves the unanchored $9 \times 6$ system and the $9 \times 5$ system with reference point $P_0$ fixed at 0 m using the same sparse QR procedure.
The procedure is `SparseQrSymbolic::analyze` (approximate minimum-degree column ordering), `SparseQr::factor`, `SparseQrMinimumNormPlan::build` supplied with a `RankThreshold` of relative threshold $10^{-10}$, and `solve`.
`build` sets the rank cutoff $\tau = 10^{-10}s$ from the largest column norm $s$, and `solve` returns the solution and `SparseQrSolveReport`.
In the report, `rank()` is the estimated rank, `threshold()` is $\tau$, `residual()` is the residual $\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h} - \Delta\mathbf{h}\|_2$, and `normal_residual()` is the normal-equation residual $\|\mathbf{A}^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{h} - \Delta\mathbf{h})\|_2$; both residuals are recomputed using the original matrix $\mathbf{A}$.
The inputs are the error standard deviation $\sigma$ (0–5 mm) and the random seed.
The first table shows each point's true elevation, the two solutions, and their difference.
The second table shows the two solve reports and sums of elevations; the third shows the misclosure $w$ and signed residual sum for the two cycles $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 0$ and $1 \to 4 \to 3 \to 2 \to 1$.
This cell passes $\sigma$, the observations, the minimum-norm residuals, and the rank to the next cell.

```rust
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"heights","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":2.875,"rtol":1e-12}}

//|       - {"artifact":"loops","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":-1e-9,"max":1e-9}}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, RankThreshold};
use scioxide_sparse::{
    CsrMatrix, SparseOrdering, SparseQr, SparseQrLimits, SparseQrMinimumNormPlan,
    SparseQrSolveReport, SparseQrSymbolic, SparseSolveOptions,
};

let observed = observations(noise_mm, seed as u64);
let (options, scope) = (SparseSolveOptions::new(), ExecutionContext::sequential());
let threshold = RankThreshold::new(0.0, 1e-10).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Solve in order: symbolic analysis, numerical factorization, a Plan with a rank threshold, then the minimum-norm solution.
let adjust = |a: &CsrMatrix<f64>| -> Result<(Vec<f64>, SparseQrSolveReport<f64>), String> {
    let limits = SparseQrLimits::new(256, 256, 1 << 20);
    let symbolic =
        SparseQrSymbolic::analyze(a.structural_pattern(), SparseOrdering::FillReducing, limits)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    let plan = SparseQrMinimumNormPlan::build(factor, threshold, options, scope)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

};

let (mut anchored, anchored_report) = adjust(&anchored_design)?;
anchored.insert(0, 0.0);

    .map(|i| {
        (
            format!("P{i}"),
            HEIGHTS[i],
            free[i],

            free[i] - anchored[i],
        )
    })
    .collect();
let (fixed4, sci2) = (

);

]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    heights,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "heights" })
);
let reports: Vec<(&str, usize, f64, f64, f64, f64)> = [
    ("Unanchored", &free_report, &free),
    ("P₀ anchored", &anchored_report, &anchored),
]
.into_iter()
.map(|(name, report, h)| {
    (
        name,
        report.rank(),
        report.threshold(),
        report.residual().raw_norm() * 1e3,
        report.normal_residual().raw_norm() * 1e3,

let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "System" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Rank rank()", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Cutoff τ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },

    { "key": "4", "label": "Normal-equation residual [mm]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Elevation sum Σh [m]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }

// Independent check: cycle misclosure w and signed sum of residual r = A h⁺ − Δh (section indices and directions).
let residuals: Vec<f64> = SECTIONS

    .map(|(&(u, v), d)| free[v] - free[u] - d)
    .collect();
let loops: [(&str, &[(usize, f64)]); 2] = [
    ("0→1→2→3→0", &[(0, 1.0), (1, 1.0), (2, 1.0), (3, 1.0)]),
```

At the default $\sigma = 2$ mm and seed 1, both systems have estimated rank 5, matching $n - 1 = 5$ for the unanchored system.
Four sections meet at $P_3$, so the largest column norm is $s = \sqrt{4} = 2$ and the cutoff is $\tau = 2 \times 10^{-10}$.
The sum of minimum-norm elevations is $-2.0 \times 10^{-15}$ m, zero to rounding accuracy.
The minimum-norm solution is shifted downward overall, as in $h_0^{+} = -1.5669$ m, but differs from the anchored solution by $-1.5669$ m at every point, equal to $-1/6$ times the anchored elevation sum of 9.4012 m.
The residual norms of both systems are 3.7780 mm, and the normal-equation residuals are at most $2 \times 10^{-12}$ mm, so both solutions attain the same minimum.
The largest difference between anchored and true elevations is 3.7 mm ($P_5$).
The two cycle misclosures are 0.2329 mm and 3.2276 mm, and the signed residual sums are their negatives.
Setting $\sigma = 0$ makes misclosures and residuals 0 and the anchored solution equal the true elevations; the minimum-norm solution differs from it by $-1.5675$ m, $-1/6$ times the true elevation sum of 9.405 m.

## Residuals and accuracy

The following cell receives observations, the minimum-norm residuals, and the rank from the previous cell.
All least-squares solutions have the same residuals, so the minimum-norm residuals are used.
$\hat{\sigma}$ is obtained as $\|\mathbf{r}\|_2/\sqrt{m - \mathrm{rank}}$ from the report's residual norm and rank.
From $\mathbf{A}_j$, obtained by removing the chosen reference point $P_j$ column, form the normal-equation matrix $\mathbf{A}_j^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}_j$ ($5 \times 5$) with `transpose`, `to_csr`, and `multiply`, and factor it with `SparseCholesky::new`, the constructor that performs preparation in one step.
Solve 5 times with unit-vector right-hand sides to obtain $\mathbf{G} = (\mathbf{A}_j^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}_j)^{-1}$ one column at a time, then compute each point's effective resistance $G_{ii}$ and standard error and each section's redundancy number.
The input is the reference point $P_j$; distinguish quantities unchanged by the reference point (redundancy numbers) from those that change (standard errors).
The first table gives observations, residuals, and redundancy numbers by section; the second gives effective resistance and standard error by point; the third gives input $\sigma$, $\hat{\sigma}$, degrees of freedom, and the sum of redundancy numbers.

```rust
//| id: sparse-leveling-precision
//| ref: sparse-leveling-precision
//| title: "Leveling network residuals, redundancy numbers, a posteriori standard deviation, and standard errors"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [sparse-leveling-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse]
//| dependsOn:
//|   adjust: sparse-leveling-network
//| inputs:
//|   reference: {"type":"select","label":"Reference point P_j for standard errors","description":"Point whose elevation is fixed as known.","options":["P0","P1","P2","P3","P4","P5"],"value":"P0"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"reference-p5","label":"Reference point P₅","description":"Standard errors depend on the reference point, but redundancy numbers and degrees of freedom do not change.","inputs":{"reference":"P5"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.8890228343,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":4}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":4,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.53125,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":1.03125,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/5/2","approx":{"value":1.9183117566,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sections","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.46875,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"sections","path":"/rows/8/3","approx":{"value":0.5,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: reference-p5
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.03125,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.9183117566,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"points","path":"/rows/5/2","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"sections","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.46875,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":4,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_sparse::SparseCholesky;

let noise_mm = adjust["noise_mm"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing σ")?;
let observed: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(adjust["observed"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let residuals: Vec<f64> =
    serde_json::from_value(adjust["residuals"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rank = adjust["rank"].as_u64().ok_or("Missing rank")? as usize;
let residual_norm = adjust["residual_norm"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing residual")?;
let (n, m) = (HEIGHTS.len(), SECTIONS.len());
// A posteriori standard deviation of unit weight σ̂ = ‖r‖₂/√(m − rank) [m].
let dof = m - rank;
let sigma_hat = residual_norm / RealScalar::sqrt(dof as f64);
// A_j with the reference point P_j column removed, and grounded Laplacian N = A_jᵀA_j.
let j: usize = reference[1..].parse().map_err(|_| "Invalid reference point name")?;
let a = design(Some(j)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let normal = a
    .transpose()
    .and_then(|t| t.to_csr())
    .and_then(|t| t.multiply(&a))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let cholesky = SparseCholesky::new(&normal).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Obtain G = N⁻¹ one column at a time and expand to n × n with zero reference-point row and column.
let full = |i: usize| if i < j { i } else { i + 1 };
let mut g = vec![0.0_f64; n * n];
for c in 0..n - 1 {
    let mut unit = vec![0.0_f64; n - 1];
    unit[c] = 1.0;
    let column = cholesky.solve(&unit).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    for (r, value) in column.into_iter().enumerate() {
        g[full(r) * n + full(c)] = value;
    }
}
// Redundancy number ρ_k = 1 − R_eff(u_k, v_k) = 1 − (G_uu + G_vv − 2 G_uv).
let redundancy: Vec<f64> = SECTIONS
    .iter()
    .map(|&(u, v)| 1.0 - (g[u * n + u] + g[v * n + v] - 2.0 * g[u * n + v]))
    .collect();
let sections: Vec<(String, f64, f64, f64)> = (0..m)
    .map(|k| {
        let (u, v) = SECTIONS[k];
        (
            format!("{u}→{v}"),
            observed[k],
            residuals[k] * 1e3,
            redundancy[k],
        )
    })
    .collect();
let (fixed2, fixed3, fixed4) = (
    serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 }),
    serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 }),
    serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 }),
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Section" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Observation Δh [m]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Residual r [mm]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Redundancy number ρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    sections,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "sections" })
);
// Standard error σ̂√G_ii [mm]. Undefined at the reference point itself.
let points: Vec<(String, Option<f64>, Option<f64>)> = (0..n)
    .map(|i| {
        let resistance = (i != j).then(|| g[i * n + i]);
        let error = resistance.map(|r| sigma_hat * RealScalar::sqrt(r) * 1e3);
        (format!("P{i}"), resistance, error)
    })
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Point" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Effective resistance [Ω]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "Reference" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Standard error [mm]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2, "nullText": "Reference" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    points,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "points" })
);
let summary = vec![(
    noise_mm,
    sigma_hat * 1e3,
    dof,
    redundancy.iter().sum::<f64>(),
)];
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Input σ [mm]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "σ̂ [mm]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Degrees of freedom m − rank", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Redundancy sum Σρ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    summary,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
```

At the default reference point $P_0$, section residuals lie between $-2.23$ and $0.82$ mm, and $\hat{\sigma} = 1.89$ mm is close to the input 2 mm.
Redundancy numbers range from 0.375 to 0.5 and sum to the degrees of freedom $9 - 5 = 4$.
Because every section is 1 Ω, effective resistances and redundancy numbers are rational, and table values agree to rounding accuracy with exact values ($17/32$ Ω, $3/8$, and so on).
Effective resistances at $P_1$, $P_3$, and $P_4$, directly connected to $P_0$, are small: $17/32 = 0.53$, $17/32$, and $1/2$ Ω.
Among points 2 sections from $P_0$, $P_2$ with 3 incident sections has $25/32 = 0.78$ Ω, while $P_5$ with only 2 has $33/32 = 1.03$ Ω; $P_5$ with fewer alternative paths has the larger value.
Standard errors are proportional to the square root of effective resistance and range from 1.34 to 1.92 mm.
Changing the reference to $P_5$ makes the standard error at $P_5$ undefined; the effective resistance at $P_0$ is 1.03125 Ω and its standard error is 1.92 mm, matching the values at $P_5$ when $P_0$ is the reference.
Redundancy numbers and degrees of freedom remain unchanged when the reference point changes.

With only 4 degrees of freedom, $\hat{\sigma}$ varies considerably with the seed.
For normally distributed errors, $4\hat{\sigma}^2/\sigma^2$ follows a $\chi^2$ distribution with 4 degrees of freedom, and the relative variability of $\hat{\sigma}/\sigma$ is of order $1/\sqrt{2 \cdot 4} \approx 0.35$.
Setting $\sigma = 5$ mm in the previous cell gives $\hat{\sigma}$ ranging from 1.70 to 6.97 mm for seeds 1–10.

## Summary

The observation matrix of an unanchored leveling network is an incidence matrix with null space $\mathbf{1}$, so its rank is exactly $n - 1$.
`SparseQrMinimumNormPlan` estimates the rank as $n - 1$ and returns the minimum-norm solution whose elevations sum to 0, allowing adjustment of the network's shape without choosing a datum.
It differs from an anchored solution by the same constant at every point, so the elevation of any point can be supplied later and the whole solution shifted accordingly.
Along a cycle, the residual sum is the negative of the misclosure, confirming that adjustment distributes the misclosure among sections.
The observation error magnitude can be estimated from residuals by $\hat{\sigma}$; the denominator's degrees of freedom equal the redundancy sum, so networks with few degrees of freedom give highly variable estimates.
Standard errors are proportional to the square root of the effective resistance to the reference point and change with the reference point, while redundancy numbers do not.
