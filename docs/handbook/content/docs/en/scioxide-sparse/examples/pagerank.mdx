On directed graphs such as Web links or paper citations, PageRank measures each vertex's importance by the stationary distribution of a random walk that continually follows links.
The stationary distribution solves a linear system with a large sparse coefficient matrix and can be found by either an iterative or a direct method.
For a random graph containing dangling nodes (vertices with no outgoing links), this example computes PageRank by power iteration with repeated sparse transpose products and by a direct sparse LU method.
Independent checks use the probability sum, agreement between the methods, an a posteriori error bound computable during power iteration, and the convergence rate predicted from the transition matrix's eigenvalues.

## Formulation

In a directed graph with $n$ vertices, let $k_i$ be the number of outgoing links (out-degree) from vertex $i$.
Let $\mathbf{H}$ be the matrix with $h_{ij} = 1/k_i$ if a link from $i$ to $j$ exists, and $h_{ij} = 0$ otherwise.
A vertex with no outgoing links is called a dangling node.
Rows of dangling nodes $i$ ($k_i = 0$) are zero, so the row sums of $\mathbf{H}$ are either 1 or 0.
Let $\mathbf{d}$ be the vector equal to 1 at dangling nodes and 0 elsewhere, and stipulate equal-probability transitions from a dangling node to every vertex; then the transition matrix

$$
\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{H} + \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{d}\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}
$$

is nonnegative and every row sums to 1.
With probability $\alpha$ (the damping factor, commonly 0.85), the random walk follows a link according to $\mathbf{P}$; with probability $1 - \alpha$ it jumps to a vertex chosen uniformly from all vertices.
PageRank is the stationary distribution $\mathbf{x}$ ($x_i \ge 0$, $\sum_i x_i = 1$) of this walk and satisfies

$$
\mathbf{x} = \alpha\,\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x} + \frac{1 - \alpha}{n}\mathbf{1}
$$

The matrix 1-norm $\|\cdot\|_1$ is the maximum absolute column sum, and $\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$ is nonnegative with column sums 1, so $\|\alpha\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\|_1 = \alpha < 1$.
Thus $\mathbf{I} - \alpha\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$ is nonsingular and the solution is unique.
The Neumann series $\mathbf{x} = \frac{1 - \alpha}{n}\sum_{k \ge 0}(\alpha\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}})^k\mathbf{1}$ shows that the solution components are nonnegative.
Summing components on both sides gives $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x} = \alpha\,\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x} + 1 - \alpha$, so the sum is 1 and the solution is a probability distribution.

$\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$ contains the dense rank-one term $\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{d}^{\mathsf{T}}$.
However, its action $\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x} + \frac{1}{n}(\mathbf{d}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x})\mathbf{1}$ can be computed from the single scalar inner product $\mathbf{d}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x}$, so only sparse $\mathbf{H}$ needs to be stored.

## Power iteration

Starting from $\mathbf{x}_0 = \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}$, power iteration repeats

$$
\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \alpha\,\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x}_k + \frac{\alpha\,\mathbf{d}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x}_k + 1 - \alpha}{n}\,\mathbf{1}
$$

The right-hand side equals $\alpha\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x}_k + \frac{1 - \alpha}{n}\mathbf{1}$, and the column sums of $\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$ are 1, giving $\sum_i (x_{k+1})_i = \alpha\sum_i (x_k)_i + 1 - \alpha$.
The components of $\mathbf{x}_0$ sum to 1, so the sum remains 1 at every iteration.
Each iteration requires the transpose product $\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x}_k$ and inner product $\mathbf{d}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x}_k$, costing $O(n + \mathrm{nnz}(\mathbf{H}))$.

scioxide-sparse computes the transpose product by passing `SparseOperation::Transpose` to the output-buffer API `sparse_dense_into`, without forming $\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}$ from CSR $\mathbf{H}$.
`sparse_dense_into` only checks the size of workspace `SparseProductWorkspace` without growing it, and returns `DimensionMismatch` if it is insufficient.
Preallocating once before iteration according to `SparseProductRequirements::sparse_dense` therefore makes transpose products allocation-free inside the iteration.

### A priori and a posteriori error bounds

The error $\mathbf{e}_k = \mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}$ satisfies $\mathbf{e}_{k+1} = \alpha\,\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{e}_k$, and $\|\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}\|_1 = 1$, so

$$
\|\mathbf{e}_k\|_1 \le \alpha^k\,\|\mathbf{e}_0\|_1 \le 2\alpha^k
$$

the bound above holds.
Both $\mathbf{x}_0$ and $\mathbf{x}$ are probability distributions, so $\|\mathbf{e}_0\|_1 \le 2$.
This a priori bound gives the iteration count $\lceil \ln(\tau/2)/\ln\alpha \rceil$ needed to reduce the error to at most $\tau$ before computation, independently of the graph.

To estimate error during iteration, use an a posteriori bound that does not require the solution $\mathbf{x}$.
From $\mathbf{e}_k = \mathbf{e}_{k+1} - (\mathbf{x}_{k+1} - \mathbf{x}_k)$ and $\|\mathbf{e}_{k+1}\|_1 \le \alpha\|\mathbf{e}_k\|_1$, we have $\|\mathbf{e}_{k+1}\|_1 \le \alpha\|\mathbf{e}_{k+1}\|_1 + \alpha\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1} - \mathbf{x}_k\|_1$ and obtain

$$
\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1} - \mathbf{x}\|_1 \le \frac{\alpha}{1 - \alpha}\,\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1} - \mathbf{x}_k\|_1
$$

the bound above.
The right-hand side is computable at every iteration and can serve as a stopping criterion.

### Asymptotic convergence rate

For many graphs, the actual error decreases faster than the a priori bound $\alpha^k$.
The components of both $\mathbf{x}_0$ and $\mathbf{x}$ sum to 1, so $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{e}_0 = 0$; from $\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}} = \mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}$, $\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$ maps the subspace $\{\mathbf{e} : \mathbf{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{e} = 0\}$ into itself.
The eigenvalues of $\mathbf{P}^{\mathsf{T}}$ on this subspace are those of $\mathbf{P}$ with one occurrence of eigenvalue 1 removed.
Ordering the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{P}$ by decreasing magnitude as $1 = |\lambda_1| \ge |\lambda_2| \ge \cdots$, the error is multiplied approximately by $\alpha|\lambda_2|$ per iteration as iteration proceeds.
A set of vertices containing no dangling nodes and no links leaving the set is called a closed part.
Once a random walk enters a closed part, it cannot leave without a jump.
If $\mathbf{P}$ has two or more disjoint closed parts, eigenvalue 1 is repeated and $|\lambda_2| = 1$ (Haveliwala and Kamvar, 2003).
If a closed part can be partitioned into $d \ge 2$ groups visited cyclically by the walk (such as 2 vertices linking only to each other), the $d$th roots of unity are also eigenvalues, so $|\lambda_2| = 1$.
In either case, error decreases at $\alpha$, the same rate as the bound.

## Direct method

Setting $\gamma = (\alpha\,\mathbf{d}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x} + 1 - \alpha)/n$, the stationary equation can be written as $(\mathbf{I} - \alpha\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}})\mathbf{x} = \gamma\mathbf{1}$.
Solve the sparse system

$$
\mathbf{M}\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{1}, \qquad \mathbf{M} = \mathbf{I} - \alpha\,\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}
$$

and normalize as $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}/\sum_i y_i$ to obtain PageRank ($\mathbf{x} = \gamma\mathbf{y}$ and $\sum_i x_i = 1$ give $\gamma = 1/\sum_i y_i$).
$\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}$ is nonnegative and its column sums are the row sums of $\mathbf{H}$, so $\|\alpha\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}\|_1 \le \alpha < 1$.
Thus the Neumann series $\mathbf{M}^{-1} = \sum_{k \ge 0}\alpha^k(\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}})^k$ converges, $\mathbf{M}$ is nonsingular, and $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{M}^{-1}\mathbf{1} \ge \mathbf{1}$ makes the normalization denominator positive.
The same estimates give $\|\mathbf{M}\|_1 \le 1 + \alpha$ and $\|\mathbf{M}^{-1}\|_1 \le 1/(1 - \alpha)$, bounding the 1-norm condition number by

$$
\kappa_1(\mathbf{M}) \le \frac{1 + \alpha}{1 - \alpha}
$$

The bound is 12.3 at $\alpha = 0.85$ and 199 at $\alpha = 0.99$.
For a non-dangling vertex, the absolute column sum of $\mathbf{M}$ is $1 + \alpha$.
For vertex $j$ in a closed part, $(\mathbf{H}^k\mathbf{1})_j = 1$ for every $k$, so column $j$ of $\mathbf{M}^{-1}$ sums to $1/(1 - \alpha)$.
Thus equality holds in this bound for graphs with a closed part.
As $\alpha$ approaches 1, the solution becomes more sensitive to rounding errors.

Since $\mathbf{M}$ is nonsymmetric, solve it by sparse LU factorization.
In column $j$ of $\mathbf{M}$, the diagonal 1 exceeds the sum of absolute values of the other entries, $\alpha\sum_i h_{ji} \le \alpha$.
For a matrix strictly diagonally dominant by columns in this way, partial pivoting selects the original diagonal entries even after column reordering, and the growth factor is at most 2 (Wilkinson's theorem; Higham, 2002, §9.5).
Because pivot order is independent of $\alpha$, fill and factorization cost are also independent of $\alpha$.
The one-shot function `scioxide_sparse::solve(&m, &b)` constructs the same factorization as `SparseLu::new` (approximate minimum-degree column ordering followed by left-looking LU with partial pivoting) and returns the solution through two triangular substitutions.
When repeatedly solving systems with the same $\mathbf{M}$ and different right-hand sides, retain the factorization returned by `SparseLu::new(&m)` and call `solve`.
Factorization cost and memory use depend on fill determined by the structure of $\mathbf{M}$ and its ordering.
For very large graphs, fill can exceed available memory, so power iteration is used.

## Graph used in the experiment

The experiment generates a graph from the vertex count $n$ and random seed.
Each vertex becomes a dangling node with probability 0.15; otherwise it has 1–4 links (equally likely) to uniformly chosen vertices other than itself.
When $c$ links reach the same target, set $h_{ij} = c/k_i$ and combine the weights with `DuplicatePolicy::Sum`.
The following block contains definitions shared by the two cells on this page.
`random_web` constructs $\mathbf{H}$ and $\mathbf{d}$; in `Web`, `links` corresponds to $\mathbf{H}$ and `dangling` to $\mathbf{d}$.

```rust
//| id: sparse-pagerank-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Random directed graph with dangling nodes"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_sparse::{CsrMatrix, DuplicatePolicy, ExplicitZeroPolicy, SparseBuilder};

/// Link matrix H (h_ij = link count/k_i) and dangling-node indicator d.
pub(crate) struct Web {
    pub(crate) links: CsrMatrix<f64>,
    pub(crate) dangling: Vec<bool>,
}

/// Generate a graph of nodes vertices from seed (xorshift uniform variates).
pub(crate) fn random_web(nodes: usize, seed: u64) -> Result<Web, SciOxideError> {
    let mut state = 0x9E37_79B9_7F4A_7C15_u64 ^ seed;
    let mut uniform = move || {
        state ^= state << 13;
        state ^= state >> 7;
        state ^= state << 17;
        (state >> 11) as f64 / (1u64 << 53) as f64
    };
    let mut builder = SparseBuilder::new(nodes, nodes);
    let mut dangling = vec![false; nodes];
    for (i, mark) in dangling.iter_mut().enumerate() {
        if uniform() < 0.15 {
            *mark = true;
            continue;
        }
        // Create k links to vertices other than itself, assigning each weight 1/k.
        let k = 1 + (4.0 * uniform()) as usize;
        for _ in 0..k {
            let j = (i + 1 + (uniform() * (nodes - 1) as f64) as usize) % nodes;
            builder.push(i, j, 1.0 / k as f64)?;
        }
    }
    let links = builder.finish_csr(DuplicatePolicy::Sum, ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop)?;
    Ok(Web { links, dangling })
}
```

## Comparing the two methods

The following cell computes direct-method PageRank $\mathbf{x}^\ast$ with the one-shot function `solve`.
It then repeats power iteration until the a posteriori bound $\frac{\alpha}{1 - \alpha}\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1} - \mathbf{x}_k\|_1$ is at most $\tau = 10^{-12}$ (at most 5000 iterations), recording error $\|\mathbf{x}_k - \mathbf{x}^\ast\|_1$ at each iteration.
The direct solution is used only for checking, not to decide when power iteration stops.

//| title: "PageRank by sparse transpose-product power iteration and direct sparse LU"

//|     expect:

use scioxide_sparse::{

    SparseProductWorkspace, sparse_dense_into,
};

let (n, alpha) = (nodes as usize, damping_alpha);
let web = random_web(n, seed as u64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let h = &web.links;
// Direct method: M = I − αHᵀ (scaled converts the CSC transpose view of αHᵀ to CSR), M y = 1.
let m = h
    .transpose()
    .and_then(|t| t.view().scaled(alpha))
    .and_then(|t| CsrMatrix::identity(n)?.subtract(&t))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let my = m.apply(&y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lu_residual = my.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |r, v| r.max((v - 1.0).abs()));
let total: f64 = y.iter().sum();
let exact: Vec<f64> = y.iter().map(|v| v / total).collect();
// Power iteration: compute Hᵀx with a Transpose product while retaining CSR. Preallocate workspace only once.
let mut workspace = SparseProductWorkspace::new();
let requirements =
    SparseProductRequirements::sparse_dense(h.view(), SparseOperation::Transpose, 1)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
workspace
    .try_reserve(&requirements)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    |p: &[f64], q: &[f64]| p.iter().zip(q).fold(0.0_f64, |s, (a, b)| s + (a - b).abs());

let (tolerance, mut posterior) = (1e-12, f64::INFINITY);
while posterior > tolerance && errors.len() <= 5000 {

    let mut output = BlockVectorViewMut::new(&mut next, n, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    sparse_dense_into(
        h.view(),
        SparseOperation::Transpose,
        input,
        SparseProductOptions::new(),
        &mut output,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // x_{k+1} = αHᵀx_k + (α dᵀx_k + 1 − α)/n · 1.

    next.iter_mut().for_each(|v| *v = alpha * *v + jump);
    posterior = alpha / (1.0 - alpha) * distance(&next, &x);
    std::mem::swap(&mut x, &mut next);
    errors.push([errors.len() as f64, distance(&x, &exact)]);

    .iter()
    .map(|&i| (i, exact[i], x[i], (x[i] - exact[i]).abs()))
    .collect();

    serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 6 }),
    serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 }),
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Vertex", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Direct method x* [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Power iteration x_k [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference |x_k − x*| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(

    ranking,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ranking" })
);
// Smallest k satisfying the a priori bound 2α^k ≤ τ.
let a_priori =
    RealScalar::ceil(RealScalar::ln(tolerance / 2.0) / RealScalar::ln(alpha)) as usize;
let summary = vec![(
    web.dangling.iter().filter(|&&d| d).count(),
    iterations,
    a_priori,
    posterior,
    errors[iterations][1],
    (x.iter().sum::<f64>() - 1.0).abs(),
    lu_residual,
)];
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Dangling nodes [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Iterations from a priori bound [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "A posteriori bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Error ‖x_k − x*‖₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },

emit_table_with_columns!(

    summary,

export!(vertices, n);
export!(graph_seed, seed as u64);

Power iteration stops after 47 iterations, about one quarter of the 175 prescribed by the a priori bound.
At termination the a posteriori bound is $7.8 \times 10^{-13}$ and the actual difference from the direct solution is $7.1 \times 10^{-14}$, so the bound overestimates the error by about a factor of 11.
The top 5 vertex values agree between methods within $5 \times 10^{-15}$.
The power-iteration probability sum differs from 1 by $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$, and the direct-method residual is $8.9 \times 10^{-16}$; both are at the rounding-error level.
Setting $\alpha = 0.99$ increases the a priori bound to 2819 iterations, but power iteration stops after 69.
Even at $n = 200$, the iteration count is 50 and barely depends on the vertex count.

In the graph with $n = 40$ and seed 7, vertices 26 and 37 link only to each other, forming a 2-vertex cycle with no outgoing links (a closed part).
Once the walk enters this cycle it can leave only by a jump, so the two vertices rank first and second with PageRanks 0.134 and 0.127.
Power iteration takes 151 iterations, close to the a priori bound of 175.
At $\alpha = 0.99$, both vertices have PageRank 0.42 and the iteration count rises to 2715.

## Speed of power-iteration convergence

// Independent check: form P = H + d1ᵀ/n as a dense matrix (row-major) and compute all eigenvalues.
let web = random_web(n, seed).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut dense: Vec<f64> = web
    .dangling
    .iter()
    .flat_map(|&d| std::iter::repeat_n(if d { 1.0 / n as f64 } else { 0.0 }, n))
    .collect();

    dense[i * n + j] += v;
}
let matrix = Matrix::from_vec(n, n, dense).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let spectrum = GeneralEigenvaluesResult::compute(&matrix).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut moduli: Vec<f64> = spectrum.eigenvalues().iter().map(|z| z.norm()).collect();
moduli.sort_by(|a, b| b.total_cmp(a));
let second = moduli[1];
let unit = moduli.iter().filter(|&&r| r > 1.0 - 1e-10).count();
// Measure convergence by the geometric mean of the last 10 error ratios above rounding noise (errors greater than 10⁻¹²).
let positive: Vec<[f64; 2]> = errors.iter().copied().filter(|p| p[1] > 0.0).collect();
let resolved: Vec<[f64; 2]> = errors.iter().copied().filter(|p| p[1] > 1e-12).collect();
let tail = &resolved[resolved.len().saturating_sub(11)..];
let span = (tail.len() - 1) as f64;
let measured = RealScalar::powf(tail[tail.len() - 1][1] / tail[0][1], 1.0 / span);
let rows = vec![(alpha, second, alpha * second, measured, unit)];
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "α [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "|λ₂(P)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Predicted rate α|λ₂| [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Measured rate [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Eigenvalues with |λ| = 1 [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rate" })
);
// Bound α^k‖e₀‖₁ and error ratio per iteration.
let e0 = errors[0][1];
let bound: Vec<[f64; 2]> = errors
    .iter()
    .map(|p| [p[0], e0 * RealScalar::powi(alpha, p[0] as i32)])
    .collect();
let ratios: Vec<[f64; 2]> = resolved
    .windows(2)
    .map(|w| [w[1][0], w[1][1] / w[0][1]])
    .collect();
let ratio_series = vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Error ratio", "points": ratios })];
let error_series = vec![
    serde_json::json!({

    }),

        "points": bound,

    }),
];
emit_line_chart!(
    &error_series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "pagerank-iteration" },
        "xLabel": "Iteration k [count]",
        "yLabel": "Error ‖x_k − x*‖₁ [-]",

    })

emit_scatter_chart!(

        "yMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": alpha, "label": "Bound α" },

For default inputs, $|\lambda_2(\mathbf{P})| = 0.6456$; the predicted rate is $\alpha|\lambda_2| = 0.5487$ and the measured rate is 0.5547.
In the upper chart, error falls to $7.1 \times 10^{-14}$ at iteration 47, over 9 orders of magnitude below the a priori bound $\alpha^{47}\|\mathbf{e}_0\|_1 = 2.3 \times 10^{-4}$ at that iteration.
The ratios in the lower chart fluctuate between 0.45 and 0.62 around the predicted horizontal line and never exceed the bound $\alpha$.
For $\alpha = 0.5$ and $0.99$, predictions are 0.3228 and 0.6391 and measured values are 0.3170 and 0.6454.
Because $|\lambda_2|$ depends only on the graph and not on $\alpha$, the convergence rate is proportional to $\alpha$ and remains only $0.99 \times 0.6456$ even at $\alpha = 0.99$.

For $n = 40$ and seed 7, the transition matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ restricted to the 2-vertex cycle has eigenvalues $\pm 1$, so $\mathbf{P}$ also has eigenvalue $-1$ and there are 2 eigenvalues of magnitude 1.
Since $|\lambda_2| = 1$, the predicted rate is $\alpha$ itself, and the measured rate 0.847 is also close to $\alpha = 0.85$.
Here error decreases only as fast as the bound, so the iteration count approaches the a priori bound.

## Summary

PageRank is the stationary distribution of a transition matrix; the dense contribution from dangling nodes reduces to the inner product $\mathbf{d}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x}$, so only sparse $\mathbf{H}$ needs to be stored.
Each power iteration costs $O(n + \mathrm{nnz})$ for the transpose product, and termination can be decided from the a posteriori bound $\frac{\alpha}{1 - \alpha}\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1} - \mathbf{x}_k\|_1$ without the solution.
The iteration count is determined by $\alpha|\lambda_2(\mathbf{P})|$ rather than the a priori bound's $\alpha$; when $|\lambda_2|$ is well below 1, as in the default graph, bringing $\alpha$ close to 1 adds few iterations.
The direct method solves $\mathbf{I} - \alpha\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf{T}}$ by sparse LU and normalizes the result; its condition number is at most $(1 + \alpha)/(1 - \alpha)$, and its cost depends on fill rather than $\alpha$.
For graphs with closed parts and $\alpha$ close to 1, or repeated solves with the same matrix and different right-hand sides, the direct method is suitable because its cost is independent of $\alpha$ and its factorization can be reused.
