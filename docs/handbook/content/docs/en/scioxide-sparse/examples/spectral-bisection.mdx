Partitioning graph vertices into two sets with as few edges between them as possible arises in domain decomposition for parallel computing, image segmentation, and clustering.
Exactly minimizing the cut subject to balanced sizes is NP-hard, so a relaxation using Laplacian eigenvectors is often used in practice.
This example computes the Laplacian's second-smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ (algebraic connectivity) and its eigenvector (Fiedler vector) by shifted inverse iteration with `SparseCholesky` and partitions the graph by component signs.
Independent checks use analytical $\lambda_2$ values for paths and grids, eigenpair residuals, an upper bound on $\lambda_2$ from the resulting partition's cut, and the cut-edge count of a graph with two weakly connected clusters.

## Laplacian spectrum

For an undirected graph of $n$ vertices with nonnegative weights $w_{ij}$, let $\mathbf{W}$ be the weight matrix and $\mathbf{D}$ the diagonal matrix of degrees $d_i = \sum_j w_{ij}$.
The Laplacian $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{D} - \mathbf{W}$ satisfies

$$
\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x} = \sum_{\{i,j\}} w_{ij}(x_i - x_j)^2 \ge 0
$$

(summing over edges), so it is positive semidefinite; ordering eigenvalues as $0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \cdots \le \lambda_n$, the eigenvector for $\lambda_1 = 0$ is $\mathbf{1}$ ([Graph Laplacian](oxref:sparse-graph-laplacian)).
The Courant–Fischer theorem gives

$$
\lambda_2 = \min_{\mathbf{x} \perp \mathbf{1},\ \mathbf{x} \ne \mathbf{0}} \frac{\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{x}}
$$

and $\lambda_2 > 0$ is equivalent to graph connectivity (Fiedler, 1973).

For a partition into vertex set $S$ and complement $\bar{S}$, denote the sum of weights of edges crossing between them by the cut $\mathrm{cut}(S)$.
Setting $x_i = 1/|S|$ ($i \in S$) and $x_i = -1/|\bar{S}|$ ($i \in \bar{S}$) gives $\mathbf{x} \perp \mathbf{1}$ and Rayleigh quotient $\mathrm{cut}(S)\,(1/|S| + 1/|\bar{S}|)$.
Thus every partition satisfies

$$
\lambda_2 \le \mathrm{cut}(S)\left(\frac{1}{|S|} + \frac{1}{|\bar{S}|}\right)
$$

and minimizing the right-hand side (the ratio cut) over partitions corresponds to minimizing over $\mathbf{x}$ whose components take only two values.
Removing this constraint and minimizing over real $\mathbf{x}$ gives $\lambda_2$; partitioning by signs of its minimizing Fiedler vector approximates a small-cut partition.
This is a heuristic and does not guarantee a minimum cut.
The inequality above also assesses partition quality by how much the resulting ratio cut exceeds $\lambda_2$.

For the symmetrically normalized Laplacian $\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{sym}} = \mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}$, the eigenvector for $\lambda_1 = 0$ is $\mathbf{D}^{1/2}\mathbf{1}$.
Converting eigenvector $\mathbf{g}$ back to vertex values by $\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\mathbf{g}$ gives Rayleigh quotient $\mathbf{f}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{f}/\mathbf{f}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{f}$, replacing $|S|$ in the preceding argument by the degree sum $\mathrm{vol}(S) = \sum_{i \in S} d_i$.
The bound is $\lambda_2 \le \mathrm{cut}(S)\,(1/\mathrm{vol}(S) + 1/\mathrm{vol}(\bar{S}))$ (the right-hand side is the normalized cut), and partitioning uses the signs of $\mathbf{f}$.
Normalized $\lambda_2$ is related to the Cheeger constant $h(G) = \min_S \mathrm{cut}(S)/\min(\mathrm{vol}(S), \mathrm{vol}(\bar{S}))$ by Cheeger's inequality $\lambda_2/2 \le h(G) \le \sqrt{2\lambda_2}$ (Chung, 1997).

### Analytical values

The Laplacian of a path graph with $s$ vertices (unit weights) is tridiagonal with endpoint diagonal entries 1 and interior diagonal entries 2; its eigenvalues and eigenvectors are

$$
\lambda_k = 2\left(1 - \cos\frac{(k - 1)\pi}{s}\right), \qquad x_i = \cos\frac{(k - 1)\pi\,(i + 1/2)}{s} \qquad (k = 1, \dots, s;\ i = 0, \dots, s - 1)
$$

Thus $\lambda_2 = 2(1 - \cos(\pi/s))$, and the Fiedler vector is a half-period cosine changing sign in the middle.
An $s \times s$ grid is the Cartesian product of two paths, so its eigenvalues are sums of path eigenvalues, $\lambda_k + \lambda_l$.
The two smallest positive eigenvalues are $\lambda_2 + \lambda_1$ and $\lambda_1 + \lambda_2$, so the grid's $\lambda_2$ is also $2(1 - \cos(\pi/s))$, with multiplicity 2.
For the normalized Laplacian, path eigenvalues are $1 - \cos((k - 1)\pi/(s - 1))$ and eigenvectors converted back to vertex values are $f_i = \cos((k - 1)\pi i/(s - 1))$, giving $\lambda_2 = 1 - \cos(\pi/(s - 1))$.
A grid's normalized Laplacian is not a Cartesian product because degrees vary by vertex, and has no simple closed form.

## Shifted inverse iteration

Since $\lambda_2$ is a small eigenvalue, factor $\mathbf{L} + \sigma\mathbf{I}$ (a small shift $\sigma > 0$) once by Cholesky and repeat

$$
\mathbf{y}_{k+1} = (\mathbf{L} + \sigma\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{x}_k, \qquad \mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \frac{\mathbf{y}_{k+1} - (\mathbf{v}_1^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}_{k+1})\mathbf{v}_1}{\|\mathbf{y}_{k+1} - (\mathbf{v}_1^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{y}_{k+1})\mathbf{v}_1\|_2}
$$

$\mathbf{L}$ itself is singular, so make it positive definite with a shift before factorization.
$\mathbf{v}_1$ is the normalized eigenvector for $\lambda_1$ ($\mathbf{1}/\sqrt{n}$, or normalized $\mathbf{D}^{1/2}\mathbf{1}$ for the normalized Laplacian); removing its component at every step makes the iteration converge to an eigenvector of $\lambda_2$.
The error is multiplied approximately by $(\lambda_2 + \sigma)/(\lambda_3 + \sigma)$ per iteration.
When $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$, as on a grid, $\mathbf{x}_k$ approaches the space spanned by the two eigenvectors at rate $(\lambda_2 + \sigma)/(\lambda_4 + \sigma)$; the initial value determines which vector in that space results.

Compute the eigenvalue from the Rayleigh quotient $\theta = \mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x}$ ($\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 = 1$).
Because $\mathbf{L}$ is symmetric, at least one eigenvalue lies within distance $\|\mathbf{L}\mathbf{x} - \theta\mathbf{x}\|_2$ of $\theta$.
If the eigenvalue gap is sufficiently larger than the residual, the error in $\theta$ is of order the squared residual divided by the gap.

scioxide-sparse constructs `LaplacianPlan` from the weight matrix with `GraphPlan::new` and `GraphPlan::laplacian`; `matrix()` returns $\mathbf{L}$ and `degrees()` returns degrees.
To form the shifted matrix, scale `CsrMatrix::identity` by $\sigma$ with `scaled` and add with `add`; factor it with the preparation constructor `SparseCholesky::new` (approximate minimum-degree ordering, reading both triangles to check symmetry).
`SparseCholesky::new` rejects matrices that are not exactly symmetric with `InvalidInput`.
The symmetrically normalized Laplacian rounds entry $(u, v)$ by division in the order $(w_{uv}/\sqrt{d_u})/\sqrt{d_v}$ and entry $(v, u)$ in the order $(w_{uv}/\sqrt{d_v})/\sqrt{d_u}$, so their last digits can differ.
The first experimental cell therefore symmetrizes with $(\mathbf{L} + \mathbf{L}^{\mathsf{T}})/2$ before factorization (unnormalized $\mathbf{L}$ is unchanged).
Within iteration, the output-buffer API `solve_into` receives workspace created once by `workspace(1)`, avoiding allocation per iteration.
When many eigenvalues of a large graph are needed, use a partial eigensolver such as scioxide-linalg's [LOBPCG](../../../scioxide-linalg/algorithms/partial-spectra/#lobpcg).

## Partitioning three graph types

The experiment uses a path ($s$ vertices), a grid ($s \times s$ vertices), and two clusters (two complete graphs of $s/2$ vertices joined by 2 edges of weight $w$); all edge weights are 1 except those 2 intercluster edges.
The following block contains definitions shared by the two cells on this page.
`graph_edges` generates the vertex count and edge list from graph type and size; the second cell reconstructs the same edges from the first cell's inputs to count the cut.

```rust
//| id: sparse-spectral-graphs
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Edges of a path, grid, and two clusters"
//| crates: []
/// Return the vertex count and list of edges (u, v, weight), listing each edge once.
/// Grid vertices use p = row index · s + column index; the two clusters are vertices 0–s/2 − 1 and s/2–s − 1.
pub(crate) fn graph_edges(kind: &str, s: usize, bridge: f64) -> (usize, Vec<(usize, usize, f64)>) {
    let mut edges: Vec<(usize, usize, f64)> = Vec::new();
    let n = match kind {
        "Grid" => {
            for p in 0..s * s {
                if p % s + 1 < s {
                    edges.push((p, p + 1, 1.0));
                }
                if p + s < s * s {
                    edges.push((p, p + s, 1.0));
                }
            }
            s * s
        }
        "Two clusters" => {
            let h = s / 2;
            for c in [0, h] {
                for i in 0..h {
                    for j in i + 1..h {
                        edges.push((c + i, c + j, 1.0));
                    }
                }
            }
            edges.push((0, h, bridge));
            edges.push((h - 1, 2 * h - 1, bridge));
            2 * h
        }
        _ => {
            edges.extend((0..s - 1).map(|i| (i, i + 1, 1.0)));
            s
        }
    };
    (n, edges)
}
```

It forms the unnormalized or symmetrically normalized Laplacian and performs 200 shifted inverse iterations with $\sigma = 10^{-3}$ to obtain $\lambda_2$ and the Fiedler vector.
Inputs are graph type, size $s$ (6–30), intercluster weight $w$ (0.01–1), and whether to normalize.

```rust
//| id: sparse-spectral-bisection
//| ref: sparse-spectral-bisection
//| title: "Laplacian λ₂ and Fiedler vector by shifted inverse iteration"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [sparse-spectral-graphs]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse]
//| exports: [graph, size, bridge, normalized, degrees, lambda2, fiedler]
//| inputs:
//|   graph_kind: {"type":"select","label":"Graph","description":"Shape of the graph to partition.","options":["Path","Grid","Two clusters"],"value":"Path"}
//|   size: {"type":"range","label":"Size s","description":"Path vertex count, grid vertices per side, or total vertices in the two clusters.","min":6,"max":30,"step":2,"value":16}
//|   bridge: {"type":"range","label":"Intercluster weight w","description":"Weight of the 2 edges joining the clusters.","min":0.01,"max":1.0,"step":0.01,"value":0.1}
//|   normalized: {"type":"checkbox","label":"Use the symmetrically normalized Laplacian","description":"Seek a partition with a small degree-weighted normalized cut.","value":false}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"grid","label":"Grid","description":"λ₂ equals the path value with multiplicity 2; the sign partition cuts the grid along a straight line.","inputs":{"graph_kind":"Grid"}}
//|   - {"id":"clusters","label":"Two clusters, w = 0.1","description":"Only the 2 intercluster edges are cut, and λ₂ is close to the ratio-cut upper bound.","inputs":{"graph_kind":"Two clusters"}}
//|   - {"id":"strong-bridge","label":"Two clusters, w = 1","description":"Stronger intercluster connections increase λ₂.","inputs":{"graph_kind":"Two clusters","bridge":1.0}}
//|   - {"id":"normalized-path","label":"Path, normalized","description":"λ₂ matches 1 − cos(π/(s − 1)).","inputs":{"normalized":true}}
//|   - {"id":"normalized-clusters","label":"Two clusters, normalized","description":"λ₂ is slightly below the normalized-cut bound 2w(1/vol(S) + 1/vol(S̄)).","inputs":{"graph_kind":"Two clusters","normalized":true}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":16}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0384294391935391,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: grid
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":256}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0384294391935391,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: clusters
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.045,"max":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: strong-bridge
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.4,"max":0.5}}
//|   - case: normalized-path
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0218523992661943,"rtol":1e-10}}
//|   - case: normalized-clusters
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.0065,"max":0.0071174377}}
//|       - {"artifact":"eigenpair","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, RealScalar};
use scioxide_sparse::{
    CsrMatrix, DuplicatePolicy, ExplicitZeroPolicy, GraphDirection, GraphPlan, LaplacianDegree,
    LaplacianNormalization, SparseBuilder, SparseCholesky, SparseOperation, SparseSolveOptions,
};

let s = size as usize;
let (n, edges) = graph_edges(&graph_kind, s, bridge);
// Form L = D − W or D^(−1/2)(D − W)D^(−1/2) from weight matrix W (storing both directions).
let mut builder = SparseBuilder::new(n, n);
for &(u, v, w) in &edges {
    builder.push(u, v, w).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    builder.push(v, u, w).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
let weights = builder
    .finish_csr(DuplicatePolicy::Reject, ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let normalization = if normalized {
    LaplacianNormalization::Symmetric
} else {
    LaplacianNormalization::Unnormalized
};
let plan = GraphPlan::new(weights.view(), GraphDirection::Undirected)
    .and_then(|graph| graph.laplacian::<f64>(LaplacianDegree::Out, normalization))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Normalized values at (u, v) and (v, u) are rounded separately and can differ in the last digit.
// SparseCholesky::new requires exact symmetry, so symmetrize with (L + Lᵀ)/2.
let l = plan
    .matrix()
    .transpose()
    .and_then(|t| t.to_csr())
    .and_then(|t| t.add(plan.matrix()))
    .and_then(|t| t.view().scaled(0.5))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let degrees = plan.degrees();
// Normalize eigenvector v₁ for λ₁ (1 without normalization, D^(1/2) 1 with normalization).
let norm = |v: &[f64]| RealScalar::sqrt(v.iter().fold(0.0_f64, |t, x| t + x * x));
let mut v1: Vec<f64> = degrees
    .iter()
    .map(|&d| if normalized { RealScalar::sqrt(d) } else { 1.0 })
    .collect();
let scale = norm(&v1);
v1.iter_mut().for_each(|x| *x /= scale);
// Factor L + σI (σ = 10⁻³) by Cholesky with the preparation constructor.
let shifted = CsrMatrix::identity(n)
    .and_then(|identity| identity.view().scaled(1e-3))
    .and_then(|shift| l.add(&shift))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let factor = SparseCholesky::new(&shifted).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = factor.workspace(1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Inverse iteration. Start with a sloped vector not parallel to v₁; remove its v₁ component and normalize at each step.
let mut x: Vec<f64> = (0..n).map(|i| i as f64 - 0.37 * n as f64).collect();
let mut y = vec![0.0_f64; n];
for _ in 0..200 {
    factor
        .solve_into(
            &x,
            SparseOperation::Normal,
            SparseSolveOptions::new(),
            &mut y,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let c = y.iter().zip(&v1).fold(0.0_f64, |t, (a, b)| t + a * b);
    y.iter_mut().zip(&v1).for_each(|(a, b)| *a -= c * b);
    let length = norm(&y);
    x.iter_mut().zip(&y).for_each(|(a, b)| *a = b / length);
}
// Rayleigh quotient θ = xᵀLx and eigenpair residual ‖Lx − θx‖₂.
let lx = l.apply(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lambda2 = x.iter().zip(&lx).fold(0.0_f64, |t, (a, b)| t + a * b);
let pair: Vec<f64> = lx.iter().zip(&x).map(|(a, b)| a - lambda2 * b).collect();
let (fixed10, sci2) = (
    serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 }),
    serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 }),
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Vertex count n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "λ₂ (Rayleigh quotient) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Residual ‖Lx − λ₂x‖₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(n, lambda2, norm(&pair))],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "eigenpair" })
);
// Pass vertex values f = D^(−1/2) x (unchanged x if unnormalized) to the next cell.
let fiedler: Vec<f64> = x
    .iter()
    .zip(degrees)
    .map(|(&a, &d)| {
        if normalized {
            a / RealScalar::sqrt(d)
        } else {
            a
        }
    })
    .collect();
export!(graph, graph_kind);
export!(size, s);
export!(bridge, bridge);
export!(normalized, normalized);
export!(degrees, degrees);
export!(lambda2, lambda2);
export!(fiedler, fiedler);
```

Analytical values (for paths and unnormalized grids) and dashed eigenvectors are computed from closed forms, providing an independent check of inverse iteration.

}));

For the default path ($s = 16$), inverse-iteration $\lambda_2 = 0.0384294392$ differs from analytical $2(1 - \cos(\pi/16))$ by $1.4 \times 10^{-17}$, and the eigenpair residual is $2.3 \times 10^{-16}$.
The Fiedler vector overlaps the dashed half-period cosine; sign partitioning gives 8 vertices in each set, cutting only the middle edge.
This partition has ratio cut $1 \times (1/8 + 1/8) = 0.25$.
Any partition cutting 2 or more edges has ratio cut at least $2 \times 4/16 = 0.5$, so 0.25 is the path's minimum ratio cut; as the table ratio shows, it is still 6.5 times $\lambda_2$.
The bound $\lambda_2 \le \mathrm{cut}(S)(1/|S| + 1/|\bar{S}|)$ holds, but the relaxation gap is large for a path.

The grid ($16 \times 16$) has the same $\lambda_2$ as the path, with eigenpair residual $7.2 \times 10^{-16}$.
The sign partition cuts the grid along a straight central line into 128 vertices each, crossing 16 edges.
On the grid, $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$, so the Fiedler vector is not unique.
Vertex index $i$ is $s$ times the row index plus the column index, so initial value $x_i = i - 0.37n$ changes strongly with row index and contains a large component of the eigenvector varying in the row direction.
The two eigenvector components decay at the same rate, preserving their ratio after iteration and giving a horizontal straight-line sign partition.

For two clusters ($8 + 8$ vertices, $w = 0.1$), the Fiedler vector has a common sign within each cluster and cuts only the 2 intercluster edges.
$\lambda_2 = 0.0491$ is slightly below this partition's ratio cut $2w(1/8 + 1/8) = 0.05$, making the upper bound nearly an equality.
The minimum ratio cut is at least $\lambda_2$, so this partition's ratio cut is within a factor 1.02 of the minimum.
Increasing the weight to $w = 1$ raises $\lambda_2$ to 0.417, widening its gap from the ratio cut 0.5.
Stronger intercluster connections appear as increased algebraic connectivity.

For the normalized Laplacian, the path's $\lambda_2 = 0.0218523993$ differs from $1 - \cos(\pi/15)$ by $1.9 \times 10^{-16}$, and the Fiedler vector overlaps the dashed $\cos(\pi i/15)$.
For two clusters, each cluster has degree sum $\mathrm{vol}(S) = 8 \times 7 + 2w = 56.2$, so the normalized cut is $2w \times 2/56.2 = 0.00712$ and $\lambda_2 = 0.00699$ lies slightly below it.
The grid's normalized $\lambda_2$ has no closed form, but its sign partition cuts 16 edges along a straight line, as in the unnormalized case.
For these graphs, normalization does not change the partition.

## Summary

The Laplacian's second eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ measures graph connectivity strength; partitioning vertices by its eigenvector's signs approximates a small-cut partition.
Since $\lambda_2$ is a small eigenvalue, add a small Laplacian shift, factor once with `SparseCholesky`, and repeat inverse iteration while removing the eigenvector for $\lambda_1$.
Analytical values are known: $\lambda_2 = 2(1 - \cos(\pi/s))$ for paths and grids, and $1 - \cos(\pi/(s - 1))$ for normalized paths; these and eigenpair residuals check the iteration results.
For every partition, $\lambda_2$ is at most the ratio cut (normalized cut when normalized), so the resulting ratio cut divided by $\lambda_2$ bounds its ratio to the minimum.
For two weakly connected clusters, the Fiedler vector has a common sign in each cluster and its sign partition cuts only intercluster edges.
