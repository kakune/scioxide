Finite-element (FEM) and finite-volume (FVM) methods construct global matrices by adding element-local matrices at global degree-of-freedom indices.
This process is called **assembly**.
Adjacent elements share nodes, so multiple contributions enter the same position $(i, j)$.

This example assembles global stiffness from the elastic energy of one-dimensional point masses linked by grounding and coupling springs.
Closed-form entry counts and row sums check duplicate summation with `SparseBuilder` and finalization into `CsrMatrix`.
Matrix–vector products are then independently checked with a difference formula, a closed form for sinusoidal displacement, and elastic energy.

## Local elastic energy and stiffness matrices

Let $n$ point masses have displacements $x_i$ [m] ($i = 0, \dots, n - 1$).
Each mass is attached to a wall by a grounding spring of stiffness $a$ [N/m]; adjacent masses $i$ and $i + 1$ are joined by a coupling spring of stiffness $k$ [N/m].
The system's elastic energy [J] is

$$
E(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{a}{2} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} x_i^2 + \frac{k}{2} \sum_{i=0}^{n-2} (x_{i+1} - x_i)^2
$$

the expression above.
Its gradient $\nabla E(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}$ is the **nodal force** [N] applied to each mass to maintain displacement $\mathbf{x}$, with entries

$$
(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})_i = \frac{\partial E}{\partial x_i} = a x_i + k (x_i - x_{i-1}) + k (x_i - x_{i+1})
$$

where the left-neighbor term is absent at $i = 0$ and the right-neighbor term at $i = n - 1$.
The springs' restoring force on the masses is the negated nodal force $-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}$.

Element $e = (i, i + 1)$ has energy $\frac{k}{2}(x_{i+1} - x_i)^2 = \frac{1}{2} [x_i, x_{i+1}]\, \mathbf{K}_e\, [x_i, x_{i+1}]^{\mathsf{T}}$ and local stiffness

$$
\mathbf{K}_e = k \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

the expression above.
Global stiffness $\mathbf{A}$ sums $n$ grounding-spring diagonal contributions $(i, i, a)$ and four contributions $(i, i, k)$, $(i+1, i+1, k)$, $(i, i+1, -k)$, and $(i+1, i, -k)$ from each element.
There are $n + 4(n - 1) = 5n - 4$ inserted triplets and $3n - 2$ tridiagonal nonzeros after duplicate summation.
Diagonals are $a + k$ at endpoints and $a + 2k$ in the interior.
Every row sum of $\mathbf{K}_e$ is zero, so row sums of $\mathbf{A}$ equal grounding stiffness $a$, giving $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{1} = a\mathbf{1}$.
This expresses that uniform displacement does not stretch coupling springs and checks the assembled matrix.

When $a > 0$, $\mathbf{x} \ne \mathbf{0}$ implies $\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = 2E(\mathbf{x}) \ge a\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_2^2 > 0$, so $\mathbf{A}$ is symmetric positive definite.
Without grounding springs, $a = 0$ gives zero energy at $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{1}$, making $\mathbf{A}$ singular.

The following block defines helpers shared by two cells.
`GROUND_STIFFNESS` corresponds to grounding stiffness $a = 0.5$ N/m, and `spring_builder` to the function returning `SparseBuilder` containing the $5n - 4$ triplets above.

```rust
//| id: sparse-spring-assembly-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Inserting local spring contributions"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse]
use scioxide_core::SciOxideError;
use scioxide_sparse::SparseBuilder;

/// Grounding-spring stiffness a [N/m].
pub(crate) const GROUND_STIFFNESS: f64 = 0.5;

/// An n × n SparseBuilder containing grounding contributions (i, i, a) and, for each element (i, i + 1),
/// the four contributions of k [[1, −1], [−1, 1]] (5n − 4 triplets).
pub(crate) fn spring_builder(n: usize, k: f64) -> Result<SparseBuilder<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let mut builder = SparseBuilder::new(n, n);
    for i in 0..n {
        builder.push(i, i, GROUND_STIFFNESS)?;
    }
    for (i, j) in (0..n).zip(1..n) {
        for (row, col, value) in [(i, i, k), (j, j, k), (i, j, -k), (j, i, -k)] {
            builder.push(row, col, value)?;
        }
    }
    Ok(builder)
}
```

## Duplicate summation and finalization

`SparseBuilder::push` checks index bounds (`OutOfBounds`) and value finiteness (`NonFinite`) on every insertion.
`finish_csr` sorts triplets in row-major order ($O(t \log t)$ for $t$ triplets), handles duplicates with `DuplicatePolicy` and then exact zeros with `ExplicitZeroPolicy`, and builds CSR.
`DuplicatePolicy::Sum` accumulates equal-coordinate values exactly and rounds once, independent of insertion order.
`DuplicatePolicy::Reject` rejects the first sorted duplicate with `InvalidInput`, so it cannot assemble these overlapping diagonal contributions.
`ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop` removes exactly zero sums, but this model has no coordinate summing to zero, giving the same matrix as `Preserve`.
`CsrMatrix::from_triplets` performs the same process directly with `(Sum, Preserve)`.

## Duplicate summation and row sums

The following cell inserts triplets using the number of degrees of freedom $n$ and coupling stiffness $k$ as inputs, then finalizes with both `Sum` and `Reject`.
The first table compares triplet and stored-entry counts with closed forms $5n - 4$ and $3n - 2$ and shows the result under `Reject`.
The second compares summed diagonals with $a + k$ and $a + 2k$ and row sums with $a$ for endpoint rows 0 and $n - 1$ and interior row 1.
Row sums are nodal forces $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{1})_i$ [N] under uniform 1 m displacement, computed by product `apply`.

```rust
//| id: sparse-spring-assembly
//| ref: sparse-spring-assembly
//| title: "Sum duplicate local spring contributions and finalize CSR"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [sparse-spring-assembly-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse]
//| inputs:
//|   sparse_dimension: {"type":"range","label":"Degrees of freedom n","description":"Number of point masses.","min":16,"max":96,"step":8,"value":32}
//|   spring_stiffness: {"type":"range","label":"Coupling-spring stiffness k [N/m]","description":"Stiffness of springs joining adjacent masses.","min":0.2,"max":5,"step":0.2,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"many-masses","label":"n = 96","description":"There are 3n − 2 = 286 stored entries, about 3 % of dense n² = 9216 entries.","inputs":{"sparse_dimension":96}}
//|   - {"id":"inexact-stiffness","label":"k = 0.2 N/m","description":"k is not exactly representable in binary, so rounding shifts the row sum from a by one last-place unit.","inputs":{"spring_stiffness":0.2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":156}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":156}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":94}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":94}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":"InvalidInput"}
//|       - {"artifact":"rows","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.5,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rows","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":2.5,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rows","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rows","path":"/rows/1/4","equals":0}
//|   - case: many-masses
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":476}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":286}
//|       - {"artifact":"counts","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":9216}
//|       - {"artifact":"rows","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-15}}
//|   - case: inexact-stiffness
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rows","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.7,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"rows","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":-5.551115123125783e-17,"atol":1e-30}}
use scioxide_sparse::{DuplicatePolicy, ExplicitZeroPolicy};

let n = sparse_dimension as usize;
let k = spring_stiffness;
let a = GROUND_STIFFNESS;
let builder = spring_builder(n, k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let triplets = builder.triplet_count();
// Rejecting duplicates fails finalization on overlapping diagonal contributions.
let rejected = match builder
    .clone()
    .finish_csr(DuplicatePolicy::Reject, ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop)
{
    Ok(_) => "Success".to_string(),
    Err(e) => format!("{:?}", e.kind()),
};
// Sum duplicates exactly and round once, removing entries that become zero afterward.
let matrix = builder
    .finish_csr(DuplicatePolicy::Sum, ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Row sum (A1)ᵢ [N]: nodal force for uniform displacement of 1 m.
let row_sums = matrix.apply(&vec![1.0; n]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

let counts = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Inserted triplets t", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "5n − 4", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Stored entries nnz(A)", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "3n − 2", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Dense entries n²", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Finalization result with Reject" }
]);
let count_row = (
    triplets,
    5 * n - 4,
    matrix.nnz(),
    3 * n - 2,
    n * n,
    rejected,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    counts,
    vec![count_row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "counts" })
);

// Compare endpoint rows 0 and n − 1 and interior row 1 diagonals and row sums with closed forms.
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (i, expected) in [(0, a + k), (1, a + 2.0 * k), (n - 1, a + k)] {
    let diagonal = matrix.get(i, i).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((i, diagonal, expected, row_sums[i], row_sums[i] - a));
}
let sig17 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 17 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Row i", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Summed diagonal Aᵢᵢ [N/m]", "type": "number", "format": sig17 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "a + k or a + 2k [N/m]", "type": "number", "format": sig17 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Row sum (A1)ᵢ [N]", "type": "number", "format": sig17 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Row sum − a [N]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rows" })
);
```

At the default $n = 32$, 156 inserted triplets and 94 stored entries match $5n - 4$ and $3n - 2$, far fewer than 1024 dense entries.
Finalizing the same triplets with `Reject` returns `InvalidInput` for duplicated diagonals.
Endpoint diagonals sum the grounding and one coupling spring to $a + k = 1.5$ N/m; interior diagonals add two coupling springs for $a + 2k = 2.5$ N/m; all row sums equal $a = 0.5$ N.
All input values here are binary-exact, so neither summation nor products round, and row sums equal $a$ exactly.
$k = 0.2$ N/m is not binary-exact, so summed diagonal $0.7$ and intermediate product sums round, making row sums one last-place unit ($2^{-54} \approx 5.6 \times 10^{-17}$ N) smaller than $a$.
This difference is rounding-scale; an assembly error would instead appear on the scale of $k$.

At $n = 96$, 286 stored entries are about 3 % of the 9216 dense entries.
The dense-storage ratio $(3n - 2)/n^2$ decreases roughly inversely with $n$.

## Nodal forces for sinusoidal displacement

With $\theta = 2\pi/(n - 1)$ and amplitude $X$ [m], prescribe sinusoidal displacement $x_i = X\sin(i\theta)$, zero at both endpoints.
For interior masses ($1 \le i \le n - 2$), $x_{i-1} + x_{i+1} = 2\cos\theta\, x_i$, giving

$$
(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})_i = (a + 2k)\,x_i - k\,(x_{i-1} + x_{i+1}) = \lambda x_i, \qquad \lambda = a + 4k\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2}
$$

so nodal force is proportional to displacement.
Endpoint masses have coupling springs on only one side and do not follow this proportionality.
Using $x_0 = x_{n-1} = 0$ and $x_{n-2} = -X\sin\theta$ gives $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})_0 = -kX\sin\theta$ and $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})_{n-1} = kX\sin\theta$.
Interior force magnitude is at most $\lambda X$, so endpoint forces are largest when $k\sin\theta > \lambda$.
This condition is equivalent to $k(\sin\theta - 4\sin^2(\theta/2)) > a$; for $n = 32$ and $a = 0.5$ N/m it becomes $k > 3.12$ N/m.

## Checking CSR products

The following cell assembles the same matrix and computes nodal forces $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}$ for sinusoidal displacement of amplitude $X = 0.01$ m.
It uses standard product form `apply`.
`apply` is a `Accuracy::Standard` (ordinary floating-point) product visiting each stored entry once, costing $O(\mathrm{nnz}(\mathbf{A}) + n)$.

There are three independent checks.
The first directly evaluates the entrywise difference formula with double-precision arithmetic; the second uses interior proportionality $\lambda x_i$; the third compares energy $2E(\mathbf{x})$ with quadratic form $\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})$.
The chart shows CSR products as symbols and interior proportionality $\lambda x_i$ as a dashed line.
The table shows maximum nodal force and all three checks.

```rust
//| id: sparse-spring-product
//| ref: sparse-spring-product
//| title: "Check CSR products for sinusoidal displacement in three ways"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [sparse-spring-assembly-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-sparse]
//| inputs:
//|   sparse_dimension: {"type":"range","label":"Degrees of freedom n","description":"Number of point masses.","min":16,"max":96,"step":8,"value":32}
//|   spring_stiffness: {"type":"range","label":"Coupling-spring stiffness k [N/m]","description":"Stiffness of springs joining adjacent masses.","min":0.2,"max":5,"step":0.2,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"stiff-coupling","label":"k = 5 N/m","description":"Endpoint force kX sin θ exceeds the maximum interior force.","inputs":{"spring_stiffness":5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":5.402458247333163e-3,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":2e-17}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":2e-17}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: stiff-coupling
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1.0064926004433e-2,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_sparse::{DuplicatePolicy, ExplicitZeroPolicy};

let n = sparse_dimension as usize;
let k = spring_stiffness;
let a = GROUND_STIFFNESS;
let matrix = spring_builder(n, k)
    .and_then(|b| b.finish_csr(DuplicatePolicy::Sum, ExplicitZeroPolicy::Drop))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Nodal forces Ax [N] for sinusoidal displacement xᵢ = X sin(iθ) [m].
let amplitude = 0.01_f64;
let theta = 2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI / (n - 1) as f64;
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|i| amplitude * RealScalar::sin(theta * i as f64))
    .collect();
let force = matrix.apply(&x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Independent check 1: entrywise difference formula (f64 arithmetic).
let max_difference = (0..n)
    .map(|i| {
        let left = if i > 0 { x[i] - x[i - 1] } else { 0.0 };
        let right = if i + 1 < n { x[i] - x[i + 1] } else { 0.0 };
        (force[i] - (a * x[i] + k * (left + right))).abs()
    })
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
// Independent check 2: (Ax)ᵢ = λxᵢ for interior masses (λ is a reference from f64 standard functions).
let lambda = a + 4.0 * k * (theta / 2.0).sin().powi(2);
let interior_gap = (1..n - 1)
    .map(|i| (force[i] - lambda * x[i]).abs())
    .fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);
// Independent check 3: quadratic form xᵀ(Ax) and twice the elastic energy 2E(x) [J].
let quadratic: f64 = x.iter().zip(&force).map(|(p, q)| p * q).sum();
let stretch: f64 = x.windows(2).map(|w| (w[1] - w[0]) * (w[1] - w[0])).sum();
let energy2 = a * x.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum::<f64>() + k * stretch;
let max_force = force.iter().map(|f| f.abs()).fold(0.0_f64, f64::max);

let observed: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..n).map(|i| [i as f64, force[i]]).collect();
let proportional: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..n).map(|i| [i as f64, lambda * x[i]]).collect();
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "xLabel": "Mass index i [-]",
        "yLabel": "Nodal force (Ax)ᵢ [N]",
        "layers": [
            {
                "id": "proportional",
                "kind": "line",
                "name": "λxᵢ (interior closed form)",
                "points": proportional,
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed", "showSymbol": false }
            },
            { "id": "csr", "kind": "scatter", "name": "CSR product (Ax)ᵢ", "points": observed }
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "forces" })
);

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig16 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 16 });
let checks = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Maximum nodal force max|(Ax)ᵢ| [N]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum difference from difference formula [N]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum interior difference from λxᵢ [N]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "xᵀ(Ax) [J]", "type": "number", "format": sig16 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "2E(x) [J]", "type": "number", "format": sig16 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
let relative = (quadratic - energy2).abs() / energy2;
let check_row = (
    max_force,
    max_difference,
    interior_gap,
    quadratic,
    energy2,
    relative,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    checks,
    vec![check_row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
```

At defaults $n = 32$ and $k = 1$ N/m, the 30 interior symbols lie on dashed $\lambda x_i$, with only the two endpoints departing from it.
Endpoint forces are $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})_0 = -2.01 \times 10^{-3}$ N and $(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})_{n-1} = 2.01 \times 10^{-3}$ N, smaller than the interior maximum $\lambda X \max_i \lvert\sin(i\theta)\rvert = 5.40 \times 10^{-3}$ N.
At $k = 5$ N/m, endpoint force $1.0065 \times 10^{-2}$ N exceeds the interior maximum $7.04 \times 10^{-3}$ N, and the table maximum agrees with closed form $kX\sin\theta$ within rounding.

Maximum differences are $1.7 \times 10^{-18}$ N from the difference formula and $5.2 \times 10^{-18}$ N from interior proportionality.
Both are on the scale of $5.6 \times 10^{-18}$ N, entry scale $(a + 2k)X = 2.5 \times 10^{-2}$ N times machine epsilon $\varepsilon \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$, explained by rounding in both product and checks.
Differences well beyond rounding suggest endpoint or local-assembly errors.
To compare with a once-rounded product, use `matrix.view().with_accuracy(Accuracy::Exact)` as the operator.

$\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf{T}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})$ and $2E(\mathbf{x})$ are mathematically equal, with computed relative difference $2.6 \times 10^{-16}$.
The energy formula uses no matrix, so agreement independently confirms that assembly represents the model's quadratic form.
A positive value only shows consistency with positive definiteness for this $\mathbf{x}$; the proof of positive definiteness is the earlier inequality $2E(\mathbf{x}) \ge a\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert_2^2$.

## Summary

Insert local contributions into `SparseBuilder` in any order, sum duplicates exactly with `DuplicatePolicy::Sum`, and finalize CSR once.
Overlapping diagonal assembly fails with `InvalidInput` under `Reject`; use duplicate rejection to check inputs known to have no overlapping contributions.
Stored entries match tridiagonal $3n - 2$, giving memory use $O(n)$ rather than dense $n^2$.
Check assembly with row sums $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{1} = a\mathbf{1}$ and CSR products against the difference formula, interior proportionality, and elastic energy; assembly and boundary errors then appear as differences exceeding rounding.
