`HalfspaceIntersection` determines active halfspaces and vertex adjacency exactly, but returns rounded vertex coordinates.
Construction certifies each coordinate's error and fails if certification is unsuccessful.
This page derives that certification procedure and why its error bounds hold.
For vertices on or very near coordinate axes, some coordinates are zero or much smaller than the others, making ordinary preconditioned-residual certification difficult.
We also explain direct certification of such coordinates through bordered-determinant signs and independently check certificates for vertices of rotated regular polygons.
Problem formulation, polar duality, and boundedness tests are covered in [Halfspace intersections and dual convex hulls](../halfspace-intersection/).

## Vertex coordinates and error certification

A vertex of a bounded intersection is the solution $x^\ast$ of the system $A x = \beta$ (rows of $A$ are $a_i$, $\beta_i = -b_i$), obtained by making the $d$ inequalities on a dual facet equalities.
Let $\varepsilon$ be the scalar type's machine epsilon ($2^{-52} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ for `f64`), and write the radius coefficient as $\rho = 32\,\varepsilon\,(d+1)$.
For each vertex, `HalfspaceIntersection` computes coordinates $\tilde x$ as follows and certifies that each coordinate's error is within radius $t_i = \rho\,\lvert \tilde x_i \rvert$.

1. Equilibrate $A$ by dividing every row, including the constant term, by its largest absolute component; solve with LU factorization and partial pivoting, and round the approximate solution $\tilde x$ to the scalar type.
2. For coordinates with $0 < \lvert \tilde x_i \rvert \le \rho \max_j \lvert \tilde x_j \rvert$, determine the sign of the bordered determinant $D_i(0)$ (defined below) exactly.
   If the sign is 0, the true coordinate $x^\ast_i$ is zero, so replace $\tilde x_i$ by 0.
3. Compute residual $\beta - A\tilde x$ with exact summation; if every component is exactly zero, $\tilde x$ is the true vertex.
4. Otherwise, at the first candidate, undo the row scaling on the inverse of the equilibrated matrix to construct an approximate inverse $R \approx A^{-1}$ of the original $A$.
   Use upward and downward rounding to bound the absolute value of every component of $E = I - RA$.
   If $\lVert E \rVert_\infty \ge 1$, construction immediately returns `NumericalInstability`.
5. Set radius $t_i = \rho\,\lvert \tilde x_i \rvert$, compute $z = R(\beta - A\tilde x)$ from exact sums of products, and check the condition
   $$
   \lvert z_i \rvert + \sum_j \lvert E_{ij} \rvert\, t_j \le t_i
   $$
   for each coordinate using upward rounding.
6. For coordinates failing condition 5, determine the signs of $D_i$ exactly at the endpoints of interval $[\tilde x_i - t_i,\ \tilde x_i + t_i]$ (rounded inward); certify if the signs differ or either is zero.
   A coordinate with $t_i = 0$ is certified only if $D_i(\tilde x_i) = 0$.
7. If any coordinates remain uncertified, correct by $\tilde x \leftarrow \tilde x + z$, round to the scalar type, and repeat from step 2 (reuse the initial $R$ and $E$).
   At most two corrections are made; if certification fails for all three candidates including the initial solution, construction returns `NumericalInstability`.

Construction also returns `NumericalInstability` if a pivot becomes zero during LU factorization or a component of $R$ cannot be represented as a finite value.

### Componentwise certification using preconditioned residuals

Condition 5 provides an error bound for the following reason.
Writing $e = x^\ast - \tilde x$ gives $A e = \beta - A \tilde x$, so $e = R(\beta - A\tilde x) + (I - RA)e = z + E e$ holds.
If $\lVert E \rVert_\infty < 1$, the Neumann series $(I - \lvert E \rvert)^{-1} = \sum_k \lvert E \rvert^k$ converges and has nonnegative entries.
Multiplying $\lvert e \rvert \le \lvert z \rvert + \lvert E \rvert\,\lvert e \rvert$ and $\lvert z \rvert + \lvert E \rvert\,t \le t$ by this matrix gives $\lvert e \rvert \le (I - \lvert E \rvert)^{-1}\lvert z \rvert \le t$.
This is componentwise certification using preconditioned residuals from Rump's verification method (Acta Numerica 2010, Section 10.3).

The condition can fail when coordinates differ in magnitude.
Because $t_j / t_i = \lvert \tilde x_j \rvert / \lvert \tilde x_i \rvert$, the condition for component $i$ requires at least $\lvert \tilde x_i \rvert \ge \sum_j \lvert E_{ij} \rvert\, \lvert \tilde x_j \rvert$.
The entries of $E$ are of rounding magnitude (order $\varepsilon$), so for a coordinate truly zero or only order $\varepsilon$ times the other coordinates, condition 5 often fails even if $\tilde x_i$ is correct.
A typical case is a regular polygon with rounded coefficients rotated so a vertex lies on a coordinate axis.
Steps 6 and 2 certify such coordinates without using $E$.

### Coordinate certification using bordered determinants

The bordered determinant used in steps 6 and 2 is, with $u_i$ the $i$th unit vector,

$$
D_i(v) = \det \begin{pmatrix} A & \beta \\ u_i^{\mathsf T} & v \end{pmatrix} = \det(A)\,\big(v - x^\ast_i\big)
$$

as follows from the Schur complement $v - u_i^{\mathsf T} A^{-1}\beta$.
$D_i$ is linear in $v$ with its zero at the true coordinate $x^\ast_i$; opposite endpoint signs therefore place $x^\ast_i$ inside the interval, while a zero at an endpoint means that endpoint is $x^\ast_i$.
The signs use the same exact determinant predicates as the interior-point test and are unaffected by rounding in $R$ or $E$.
Thus certification succeeds regardless of coordinate magnitude if the error in $\tilde x_i$ itself is within $t_i$, apart from the rounding width at the endpoints.
Step 6 costs two exact determinants of order $(d+1)$ per coordinate; their term counts are charged to `max_predicate_terms`, which limits predicate work.
If the true coordinate is exactly zero, $t_i < \lvert \tilde x_i \rvert$ prevents construction of an interval containing 0.
Step 2 therefore checks $D_i(0) = 0$ and replaces it by $\tilde x_i = 0$, and step 6 treats it as a coordinate with radius zero.

### Combining the certificates and bounding violations

Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the index set of coordinates certified by step 6 and $\mathcal{U}$ those certified by condition 5.
We have $e_{\mathcal{U}} = z_{\mathcal{U}} + E_{\mathcal{U}\mathcal{U}}\, e_{\mathcal{U}} + E_{\mathcal{U}\mathcal{B}}\, e_{\mathcal{B}}$, and step 6 establishes $\lvert e_{\mathcal{B}} \rvert \le t_{\mathcal{B}}$.
Condition 5 checked for every component in $\mathcal{U}$ is equivalent to $\lvert z_{\mathcal{U}} \rvert + \lvert E_{\mathcal{U}\mathcal{B}} \rvert\, t_{\mathcal{B}} + \lvert E_{\mathcal{U}\mathcal{U}} \rvert\, t_{\mathcal{U}} \le t_{\mathcal{U}}$.
Because $\lVert E_{\mathcal{U}\mathcal{U}} \rVert_\infty \le \lVert E \rVert_\infty < 1$, the same Neumann-series argument gives $\lvert e_{\mathcal{U}} \rvert \le t_{\mathcal{U}}$.
Thus $\lvert x^\ast_i - \tilde x_i \rvert \le t_i$ holds for coordinates certified by either method.

The true vertex $x^\ast$ satisfies every inequality, so

$$
a_i \cdot \tilde x + b_i = a_i \cdot (\tilde x - x^\ast) + (a_i \cdot x^\ast + b_i) \le \sum_j \lvert a_{ij} \rvert\, t_j \le \rho \sum_j \lvert a_{ij} \tilde x_j \rvert
$$

bounds violations even for inactive inequalities.
The public contract uses the relaxed bound

$$
a_i \cdot \tilde x + b_i \le 128\,\varepsilon\,(d+1) \Big( \lvert b_i \rvert + \sum_j \lvert a_{ij} \tilde x_j \rvert \Big)
$$

which holds for every vertex and every inequality.
The derivation above bounds violations by $\rho / (128\,\varepsilon\,(d+1)) = 1/4$ of the public bound.
Certification fails if $A$ is too ill-conditioned to establish $\lVert E \rVert_\infty < 1$, or if each of the three candidates retains a coordinate uncertifiable by either step 5 or step 6; the entire construction returns `NumericalInstability`.

## Vertices on coordinate axes and exact checks

The following cell uses the same regular $m$-gon circumscribing the unit circle as [Halfspace intersections and dual convex hulls](oxref:spatial-halfspace-polygon),

$$
\cos\theta_k\, x + \sin\theta_k\, y - 1 \le 0, \qquad \theta_k = \varphi + \frac{2\pi k}{m}, \quad k = 0, \dots, m-1
$$

adds redundant inequality $x - 3 \le 0$, fixes the number of sides $m$, rotates it through 19 orientations $\varphi = 0^\circ, 5^\circ, \dots, 90^\circ$, and constructs each intersection.
At some orientations, vertices lie on or very near axes, making a coordinate zero or order $\varepsilon$ times the others, so condition 5 is difficult to satisfy.
Errors in returned vertices $\tilde x$ are checked independently of the certification procedure.
For the two active rows $r$ and $s$ at each vertex, compute the numerator and denominator in Cramer's rule $x^\ast_i = N_i / \det A$ without rounding using `RangeAccumulator` ([Exact accumulation and range-safe arithmetic](../../../scioxide-core/algorithms/range-safe-arithmetic/)).
Obtain $x^\ast_i - \tilde x_i = (N_i - \tilde x_i \det A) / \det A$ by dividing the exact numerator by rounded $\det A$ (relative error of order $2\varepsilon$).
The exact sign of $N_i$ also determines exactly whether $x^\ast_i = 0$.

For each orientation, the table shows construction results, vertex count, minimum relative magnitude of nonzero coordinates $\lvert \tilde x_i \rvert / \max_j \lvert \tilde x_j \rvert$ ($j$ are coordinates of the same vertex), and counts of coordinates satisfying $\tilde x_i = 0$ and $x^\ast_i = 0$.
It also shows the maximum error-to-radius ratio $\lvert x^\ast_i - \tilde x_i \rvert / t_i$ for nonzero coordinates and the maximum violation-to-bound ratio.
Violations are accumulated without rounding and rounded just once afterward.
For correct certificates, the error-to-radius ratio is at most 1, the violation-to-bound ratio at most $1/4$, and counts of $\tilde x_i = 0$ and $x^\ast_i = 0$ coordinates agree.
Orientations where construction fails display the failure classification in the result column.

```rust
//| id: spatial-halfspace-axis-scan
//| ref: spatial-halfspace-axis-scan
//| title: "Rotate regular polygons and check vertex errors near coordinate axes using exact Cramer's rule"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   sides: {"type":"range","label":"Number of sides m","min":3,"max":12,"step":1,"value":6}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"square","label":"m = 4","description":"At φ = 45°, all four vertices lie very near coordinate axes.","inputs":{"sides":4}}
//|   - {"id":"dodecagon","label":"m = 12","description":"At φ = 15°, 45°, and 75°, vertices lie on axes; at two of these orientations, coordinates are exactly zero.","inputs":{"sides":12}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Bounded"}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/6/1","equals":"Bounded"}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/14/6","range":{"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/14/7","range":{"max":0.25}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/18/1","equals":"Bounded"}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/18/6","range":{"max":1}}
//|   - case: square
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/9/1","equals":"Bounded"}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/9/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/9/6","range":{"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/8/6","range":{"max":1}}
//|   - case: dodecagon
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":"Bounded"}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/3/4","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/3/5","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/3/6","range":{"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orientations","path":"/rows/9/4","equals":0}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RangeAccumulator, RealScalar};
use scioxide_spatial::{HalfspaceIntersection, PointSet};

let m = sides as usize;
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let rho = 32.0 * f64::EPSILON * 3.0; // Radius coefficient ρ = 32 ε (d + 1), d = 2
let scale = 128.0 * f64::EPSILON * 3.0; // Violation-bound coefficient 128 ε (d + 1)
// Independent check: for coordinate axis of the solution of active rows r, s, return x* − x̃ and whether x* = 0.
// Accumulate x*₀ det A = −b_r s₁ + b_s r₁ and x*₁ det A = −r₀ b_s + s₀ b_r without rounding.
let offset = |r: &[f64], s: &[f64], axis: usize, value: f64| -> Result<_, SciOxideError> {
    let mut det = RangeAccumulator::<f64>::new();
    det.add_product(r[0], s[1])?;
    det.subtract_product(r[1], s[0])?;
    let mut numerator = RangeAccumulator::<f64>::new();
    if axis == 0 {
        numerator.subtract_product(r[2], s[1])?;
        numerator.add_product(s[2], r[1])?;
    } else {
        numerator.subtract_product(r[0], s[2])?;
        numerator.add_product(s[0], r[2])?;
    }
    let exact_zero = numerator.sign().is_eq();
    // (x* − x̃) det A = N − x̃ (r₀ s₁ − r₁ s₀)
    numerator.subtract_triple_product(value, r[0], s[1])?;
    numerator.add_triple_product(value, r[1], s[0])?;
    Ok((numerator.finish_quotient(det.finish()?)?, exact_zero))
};
// Accumulate violation a·x̃ + b without rounding, then round only once at the end.
let violation = |row: &[f64], x: &[f64]| -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> {
    let mut sum = RangeAccumulator::<f64>::new();
    sum.add(row[2])?;
    sum.add_product(row[0], x[0])?;
    sum.add_product(row[1], x[1])?;
    sum.finish()
};
let mut table = Vec::new();
for step in 0..=18 {
    let rotation = 5.0 * step as f64;
    let phase = rotation * pi / 180.0;
    // Row k < m is [cos θ_k, sin θ_k, −1]; row m is redundant x − 3 ≤ 0.
    let mut rows: Vec<f64> = Vec::with_capacity(3 * (m + 1));
    for k in 0..m {
        let theta = phase + 2.0 * pi * k as f64 / m as f64;
        rows.extend([RealScalar::cos(theta), RealScalar::sin(theta), -1.0]);
    }
    rows.extend([1.0, 0.0, -3.0]);
    let halfspaces = PointSet::from_rows(&rows, 3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let polygon = match HalfspaceIntersection::new(&halfspaces, &[0.0, 0.0]) {
        Ok(polygon) => polygon,
        Err(e) => {
            let kind = format!("{:?}", e.kind());
            table.push((rotation, kind, None, None, None, None, None, None));
            continue;
        }
    };
    let vertices = polygon.vertices();
    let (mut smallest, mut zeros, mut exact_zeros) = (f64::INFINITY, 0, 0);
    let (mut error_ratio, mut violation_ratio) = (0.0_f64, f64::NEG_INFINITY);
    for (v, x) in vertices.chunks(2).enumerate() {
        let active = polygon.active_halfspaces(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let (r, s) = (&rows[3 * active[0]..][..3], &rows[3 * active[1]..][..3]);
        let largest = x[0].abs().max(x[1].abs());
        for axis in 0..2 {
            let (error, exact_zero) = offset(r, s, axis, x[axis]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            exact_zeros += usize::from(exact_zero);
            if x[axis] == 0.0 {
                zeros += 1;
            } else {
                smallest = smallest.min(x[axis].abs() / largest);
                error_ratio = error_ratio.max(error.abs() / (rho * x[axis].abs()));
            }
        }
        for row in rows.chunks(3) {
            let excess = violation(row, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            let bound = scale * (row[2].abs() + (row[0] * x[0]).abs() + (row[1] * x[1]).abs());
            violation_ratio = violation_ratio.max(excess / bound);
        }
    }
    let kind = format!("{:?}", polygon.kind());
    table.push((
        rotation,
        kind,
        Some(vertices.len() / 2),
        Some(smallest),
        Some(zeros),
        Some(exact_zeros),
        Some(error_ratio),
        Some(violation_ratio),
    ));
}
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "φ [deg]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 0 } },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Result" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Vertices [count]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Minimum relative magnitude [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Coordinates with x̃ᵢ = 0 [count]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Coordinates with x*ᵢ = 0 [count]", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Maximum error / radius [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Maximum violation / bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    table,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orientations" })
);
```

With the default ($m = 6$), construction returns `Bounded` for all 19 orientations.
The minimum relative magnitude is $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$ at $\varphi = 0^\circ$, $30^\circ$, and $60^\circ$, and $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$ at $\varphi = 90^\circ$, with vertices very near the coordinate axes.
No orientation has a coordinate with $x^\ast_i = 0$; near-axis coordinates are genuinely small nonzero values for the rounded coefficients.
The maximum error-to-radius ratio is 0.067 ($\varphi = 70^\circ$); for every coordinate, including near-axis coordinates, the true vertex lies within the certified radius, with at least a tenfold margin.
The maximum violation-to-bound ratio is 0.0017, far below the derived $1/4$.

For “m = 4,” all four vertices lie on coordinate axes at $\varphi = 45^\circ$, with minimum relative magnitude $7.9 \times 10^{-17}$, yet construction succeeds and the error-to-radius ratio is 0.0027.
The maximum ratio over all orientations is 0.093 ($\varphi = 40^\circ$).
For “m = 12,” vertices lie on coordinate axes at $\varphi = 15^\circ$, $45^\circ$, and $75^\circ$.
At $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$, one coordinate each satisfies $x^\ast_i = 0$ exactly, and the returned coordinate is also exactly zero.
Unless step 2 replaces it with zero, this coordinate cannot be certified with a radius proportional to $\lvert \tilde x_i \rvert$.
At $45^\circ$, the coordinate of the on-axis vertex is not truly zero (relative magnitude $2.1 \times 10^{-16}$), so it is certified as is without replacement by zero.
The maximum error-to-radius ratio over all orientations is 0.18 ($\varphi = 10^\circ$).

Because radius $t_i = \rho\,\lvert \tilde x_i \rvert$ is smaller than $\lvert \tilde x_i \rvert$, the certified interval excludes zero.
Successful construction therefore ensures returned coordinate signs match true coordinate signs, and $\tilde x_i = 0$ is equivalent to $x^\ast_i = 0$.
This property explains the agreement of the two count columns.

## Summary

Each vertex coordinate returned by `HalfspaceIntersection` is certified to lie within $32\,\varepsilon\,(d+1)\,\lvert \tilde x_i \rvert$ of its true value.
Certification primarily uses Rump's componentwise preconditioned-residual condition; coordinates near axes that fail it have their intervals checked directly through bordered-determinant signs.
On successful construction, returned coordinate signs match the true signs, and coordinates whose true values are zero are returned as exact zeros.
Violations of all inequalities are at most the public bound $128\,\varepsilon\,(d+1)(\lvert b_i \rvert + \sum_j \lvert a_{ij} \tilde x_j \rvert)$, and the derivation gives at most $1/4$ of that bound.
If an ill-conditioned vertex system cannot be certified, the entire construction returns `NumericalInstability`, so a result cannot contain a subset of uncertain vertices.
