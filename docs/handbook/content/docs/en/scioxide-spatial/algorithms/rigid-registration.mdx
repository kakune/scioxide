Given measurements of the same object in two coordinate frames, rigid registration finds the rotation and translation superimposing them.
Applications include molecular superposition, registration of laser-ranging point clouds, and coordinate transformations from surveying control points.
With known correspondences, the least-squares solution has a closed form through singular value decomposition (SVD) of a 3×3 matrix (Kabsch 1976).
This page explains the mathematics and computation of `estimate_rigid_transform` and checks estimation accuracy on noisy points with outliers.
[Uniqueness and failures in rigid registration](../rigid-registration-degeneracy/) covers configurations without unique solutions and invalid input.
The estimate is a rigid transformation `RigidTransform`; [Rigid and batch transformations](../rigid-transforms/) covers composition, inversion, and batch application to many points.

## Weighted least-squares problem

For corresponding points $p_i, q_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ ($i = 1, \ldots, n$) and weights $w_i \ge 0$ ($W = \sum_i w_i > 0$), minimize

$$
E(R, t) = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \left\lvert R p_i + t - q_i \right\rvert^2
$$

over $R \in \mathrm{SO}(3)$ and $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$.
Call $p_i$ the source points and $q_i$ the target points.
Weights express pointwise reliability: if measurement-error variance is $s_i^2$, set $w_i = 1/s_i^2$; molecular applications often use atomic weights.
Zero-weight points do not affect the solution.

## Eliminating translation

Differentiating $E$ with respect to $t$ and setting it to zero gives, from $\sum_i w_i (R p_i + t - q_i) = 0$:

$$
t = \bar q - R \bar p, \qquad \bar p = \frac{1}{W}\sum_i w_i p_i, \qquad \bar q = \frac{1}{W}\sum_i w_i q_i
$$

The optimal translation aligns weighted centroids.
Using centered points $p_i' = p_i - \bar p$ and $q_i' = q_i - \bar q$ reduces the problem to rotation alone:

$$
\sum_i w_i \lvert R p_i' - q_i' \rvert^2 = \sum_i w_i \left(\lvert p_i'\rvert^2 + \lvert q_i'\rvert^2\right) - 2\,\mathrm{tr}(R H), \qquad H = \sum_i w_i\, p_i' {q_i'}^\top
$$

The first term is independent of $R$, so find the rotation maximizing $\mathrm{tr}(RH)$.
$H$ is a 3×3 cross-covariance matrix.

## Singular value decomposition and reflection correction

Let $H = U \Sigma V^\top$ ($U, V$ orthogonal, $\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3)$, $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \sigma_3 \ge 0$); then $Z = V^\top R U$ is orthogonal and

$$
\mathrm{tr}(R H) = \mathrm{tr}(Z \Sigma) = \sum_k z_{kk}\sigma_k \le \sigma_1 + \sigma_2 + \sigma_3
$$

holds ($\lvert z_{kk}\rvert \le 1$).
Equality holds for $Z = I$, equivalently $R = V U^\top$.
If $\det(VU^\top) = -1$, however, $VU^\top$ includes a reflection and is not a rotation.
In this case, a rotation $R$ implies $\det Z = \det(VU^\top)\det R = -1$, so maximize $\sum_k z_{kk}\sigma_k$ among orthogonal matrices $Z$ of determinant $-1$.
The maximizer is $Z = \mathrm{diag}(1, 1, -1)$, giving the optimal rotation

$$
R = V\,\mathrm{diag}(1, 1, -1)\,U^\top = V U^\top - 2\, v_3 u_3^\top, \qquad \mathrm{tr}(RH) = \sigma_1 + \sigma_2 - \sigma_3
$$

where $u_3, v_3$ are the third columns of $U, V$, respectively.
Correcting reflection along the smallest-singular-value direction loses only $2\sigma_3$.
For noiseless mirrored points, this correction prevents a zero residual.

The optimum is unique when points are not collinear ($\sigma_2 > 0$) and, when correcting reflection, $\sigma_2 > \sigma_3$ holds.
Even planar points ($\sigma_3 = 0$) uniquely determine the rotation.
[Uniqueness and rank tests](oxref:spatial-kabsch-uniqueness) explains numerical tests and configurations triggering them.

## Computational procedure and accuracy

Estimation has three calling forms.
`estimate_rigid_transform(source, target)` uses all weights 1 and default options; `estimate_rigid_transform_with_options(source, target, weights, options)` accepts a weight array (`None` means all weights 1) and options.
Both execute sequentially without cancellation; use `estimate_rigid_transform_scoped(source, target, weights, options, scope)` to supply an execution or solve context.
All three perform the same computation on two three-dimensional `PointSet` values, scanning points as follows.

1. If weights are supplied, check that they are finite and nonnegative and find their maximum.
2. Find the largest absolute coordinate among positive-weight points and sum weights divided by the maximum weight.
3. Divide coordinates by their maximum and compute weighted centroids $\bar p, \bar q$.
4. Accumulate $H$ from centered points.
5. Divide $H$ by its largest absolute component, compute its SVD, test numerical rank, and obtain rotation and translation.
6. Compute the weighted RMS residual.

Dividing coordinates and weights by their maxima makes sums and covariance sums of quantities of order 1.
Rotation is independent of input units; translation and residual are converted back to original units at the end.
Step 4 avoids the form $H = \sum_i w_i p_i q_i^\top - W\bar p\bar q^\top$ because its two large terms cancel for point clouds far from the origin, losing significant digits.
Dividing $H$ in step 5 makes rank tests independent of the point cloud's spatial scale.
If sums of squares in step 6 risk underflow or overflow, recompute using scaled norm accumulation.

The SVD uses `scioxide_linalg::SvdDecomposition::compute_into` on a 3×3 matrix.
It uses Householder bidiagonalization and implicitly shifted bidiagonal QR, with singular-value absolute errors of order $\varepsilon\sigma_1$ (Golub & Van Loan 2013, §5.4, §8.6).
The resulting matrix is tested for orthogonality by passing `Tolerance::new(128ε, 128ε)` to `Rotation::from_matrix`; failure returns `NumericalInstability`.
Since each tested component of $R R^\top - I$ and $\det R - 1$ has scale 1, the effective threshold is $128\varepsilon + 128\varepsilon \cdot 1 = 256\varepsilon$.
This checks orthogonality drift from SVD and matrix-product rounding; it does not guarantee fitting accuracy.
Complexity is $O(n)$; each call allocates fixed-size 3×3 matrices, SVD results, and workspace, independently of point count.
Cancellation and deadlines are checked every 256 rows.
Passing a solve context to `estimate_rigid_transform_scoped` makes estimation a nested solve; estimation and SVD iterations are recorded as nested iterations of the outer solve ([Nested solves and evaluation counts](oxref:core-nested-accounting)).
Estimation counts as one iteration within its own solve, without an iteration limit there.
Nested iterations do not count toward the outer solve's iteration limit.

The resulting `RigidEstimationResult` has the following fields.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `transform` | `RigidTransform` mapping source points to target points ([Definition of a rigid transformation](oxref:spatial-rigid-transform)) |
| `rms_residual` | $\left(\sum_i w_i \lvert R p_i + t - q_i\rvert^2 / W\right)^{1/2}$ [input units]. Normalized by the weight sum |
| `singular_values` | Singular values $[\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3]$ of the matrix obtained by dividing $H$ to make its largest absolute component 1 |
| `rank` | Numerical rank (3, or 2 for planar points) |

Since `singular_values` contains singular values of the matrix obtained by dividing $H$ by its largest component, read point-cloud shape through ratios $\sigma_2/\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_3/\sigma_1$.
The table in [Failure classifications](oxref:spatial-kabsch-failures) lists failure categories and conditions.

## Recovery from noisy points with outliers

The next cell transforms uniformly random points $p_i$ ($n$ points) in cube $[-1, 1]^3$ m by rotation angle $\theta$ about axis $(1, 2, 2)/3$ and translation $(10, 20, 30)$ m, then adds independent normal noise $N(0, \sigma^2)$ to each coordinate to produce $q_i$.
An additional outlier $o$ [m] is added to point 0's $x$ coordinate.
Random numbers use the seeded scioxide-random generator `SplitMix64`, with the seed exposed as an input.
Point arrays are borrowed without cloning through `PointSet::from_points`.

Estimate in two ways.
Use `estimate_rigid_transform` with every weight 1, and `estimate_rigid_transform_with_options` with point 0's weight set to 0.
Table `fits` shows rank, singular values, rotation-angle error $d(R_{\text{true}}, R)$ [deg] (rotation distance from [Composition order and inverse rotations](oxref:spatial-rotation-composition)), translation error $\lvert t - t_{\text{true}}\rvert$ [m], RMS residual, and the noise-only expectation.
For small noise and uniform weights, fitting six degrees of freedom leaves $3m - 6$ residual degrees of freedom, giving

$$
\mathrm{E}\left[\mathrm{RMS}^2\right] \approx \frac{\sigma^2 (3m - 6)}{m}
$$

where $m$ is the number of positive-weight points.
The expectation column contains the square root of this formula, $\sigma\sqrt{(3m - 6)/m}$.
The three-dimensional scene shows source points, target points, and points transformed by the estimate with point 0's weight 0 in the same units [m], connecting transformed and target points with lines.

```rust
//| id: spatial-kabsch-recovery
//| ref: spatial-kabsch-recovery
//| title: "Recover a rigid transformation from noisy corresponding points with outliers"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   angle_degrees: {"type":"range","label":"True rotation angle θ [deg]","description":"Angle about axis (1, 2, 2)/3.","min":0,"max":180,"step":15,"value":90}
//|   noise: {"type":"range","label":"Noise standard deviation σ [m]","description":"Normal noise added to each coordinate.","min":0.001,"max":0.2,"step":0.001,"value":0.02}
//|   point_count: {"type":"range","label":"Point count n","description":"Uniformly random points in a cube.","min":4,"max":64,"step":4,"value":16}
//|   outlier: {"type":"range","label":"Outlier o [m]","description":"Offset added to point 0's x coordinate.","min":0,"max":2,"step":0.1,"value":0.5}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","description":"Seed generating points and noise.","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":7}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"no-outlier","label":"o = 0","description":"Without an outlier, both estimated RMS residuals lie near their expectations.","inputs":{"outlier":0}}
//|   - {"id":"many-points","label":"n = 64, σ = 0.1 m","description":"As point count increases, expected RMS residual σ√((3m − 6)/m) approaches √3 σ.","inputs":{"point_count":64,"noise":0.1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":3}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/7","range":{"min":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/7","range":{"min":0.01,"max":0.04}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"max":3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/8","approx":{"value":0.032249,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: no-outlier
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/0/7","range":{"min":0.01,"max":0.04}}
//|   - case: many-points
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"fits","path":"/rows/1/7","range":{"min":0.15,"max":0.2}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64, Uniform};
use scioxide_spatial::{
    PointSet, RigidEstimationOptions, RigidTransform, Rotation, estimate_rigid_transform,
    estimate_rigid_transform_with_options,
};

let n = point_count as usize;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let cube = Uniform::new(-1.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let normal = Normal::new(0.0, noise).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let theta = RealScalar::to_radians(angle_degrees);
let rotation = Rotation::from_axis_angle([1.0, 2.0, 2.0], theta).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let truth = RigidTransform::new(rotation, [10.0, 20.0, 30.0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut source = Vec::with_capacity(n);
for _ in 0..n {
    let mut p = [0.0; 3];
    for x in &mut p {
        *x = cube.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
    source.push(p);
}
// Apply the true rigid transformation, then add normal noise and the point-0 outlier.
let mut target = Vec::with_capacity(n);
for p in &source {
    let mut q = truth.apply(*p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    for x in &mut q {
        *x += normal.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
    target.push(q);
}
target[0][0] += outlier;

// Uniform weights (default form) and point-0 weight 0 (form accepting weights and options).
let source_points = PointSet::from_points(&source).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let target_points = PointSet::from_points(&target).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut weights = vec![1.0; n];
weights[0] = 0.0;
let uniform =
    estimate_rigid_transform(&source_points, &target_points).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let weighted = estimate_rigid_transform_with_options(
    &source_points,
    &target_points,
    Some(&weights),
    RigidEstimationOptions::new(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// (weights, estimate, positive-weight point count)
let fits = [("Uniform", &uniform, n), ("Point 0 weight 0", &weighted, n - 1)];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (label, result, active) in fits {
    let estimated = result.transform;
    // Angular difference from the true rotation d(R_true, R) = ∠(R_true⁻¹ R) [rad].
    let relative = truth
        .rotation()
        .inverse()
        .compose(estimated.rotation())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let error = relative.as_axis_angle().1;
    let (shift, truth_shift) = (estimated.translation(), truth.translation());
    let gap = [0, 1, 2].map(|k| shift[k] - truth_shift[k]);
    let translation_error = RealScalar::hypot(RealScalar::hypot(gap[0], gap[1]), gap[2]);
    // Expected noise-only RMS residual σ √((3m − 6)/m).
    let m = active as f64;
    let expected = noise * RealScalar::sqrt((3.0 * m - 6.0) / m);
    let s = result.singular_values;
    rows.push((
        label,
        result.rank,
        s[0],
        s[1],
        s[2],
        RealScalar::to_degrees(error),
        translation_error,
        result.rms_residual,
        expected,
    ));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Weights" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Rank", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "σ₁ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "σ₂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "σ₃ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Rotation error [deg]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Translation error [m]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "RMS residual [m]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Expected noise-only RMS [m]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "fits" })
);

// Source points, target points, and points transformed using the estimate with point 0 weight 0 (same units m).
let mut points = Vec::new();
let mut edges = Vec::new();
for i in 0..n {
    let aligned = weighted
        .transform
        .apply(source[i])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let groups = [
        ("source", source[i]),
        ("target", target[i]),
        ("aligned", aligned),
    ];
    for (group, position) in groups {
        points.push(serde_json::json!({
            "id": format!("{group}-{i}"),
            "position": position,
            "group": group
        }));
    }
    edges.push(
        serde_json::json!({ "from": format!("aligned-{i}"), "to": format!("target-{i}") }),
    );
}
emit_scene3d!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "points": points,
        "edges": edges,
        "groups": [
            { "id": "source", "label": "Source points p" },
            { "id": "target", "label": "Target points q (noise and outlier)" },
            { "id": "aligned", "label": "Estimated transformed points (point 0 weight 0)" }
        ],
        "axes": {
            "x": { "label": "x", "unit": "m" },
            "y": { "label": "y", "unit": "m" },
            "z": { "label": "z", "unit": "m" }
        }
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "alignment", "title": "Before and after registration" })
);
```

With defaults ($\theta = 90°$, $\sigma = 0.02$ m, $n = 16$, $o = 0.5$ m, seed 7), the uniform-weight RMS residual is 0.119 m, about 3.7 times its noise-only expectation 0.0324 m.
One outlier pulls the fit, producing rotation error 1.04° and translation error 0.036 m.
Setting point 0's weight to 0 brings the RMS residual to 0.0331 m, near expectation 0.0322 m, and lowers rotation error to 0.70° and translation error to 0.0092 m.
With the outlier set to 0, even uniform weights give RMS residual 0.0328 m (expectation 0.0324 m) and rotation error 0.70°.
An RMS residual far above expectation suggests an outlier or incorrect correspondence, guiding weight revision.
At $n = 64$ and $\sigma = 0.1$ m, setting point 0's weight to 0 gives RMS residual 0.180 m, near expectation 0.170 m.
In the three-dimensional scene, only outlier point 0 remains far from its transformed point; the other points nearly overlap their targets.

## Summary

Weighted rigid registration aligns weighted centroids through translation and obtains rotation from the SVD of cross-covariance $H$ as $R = VU^\top$.
If $\det(VU^\top) = -1$, correct reflection along the smallest-singular-value direction.
`estimate_rigid_transform` divides coordinates and weights by their maxima and constructs $H$ from centered points, avoiding cancellation for point clouds far from the origin or coordinates expressed in large units.
The estimated matrix becomes a rotation only after passing an orthogonality check; failure returns `NumericalInstability`.
When comparing `rms_residual` with noise magnitude, use $\sigma\sqrt{(3m - 6)/m}$ as the uniform-weight baseline; larger residuals suggest outliers or correspondence errors and call for revisiting weights.
Even mirrored points are fitted by a rotation alone; a nonzero residual can reveal the reflection.
