Orientation trajectories connecting timed key orientations are used for spacecraft maneuvers, robot end-effector orientation, and camera orientation in computer animation.
[Spherical linear interpolation (Slerp)](oxref:spatial-slerp) rotates at constant angular velocity on each interval but has angular-velocity jumps at knots.
A rigid body following this trajectory requires torque impulses at knots.
This page derives `RotationSpline` with continuous angular velocity and acceleration, compares it with Slerp, and checks continuity tests and iterative convergence numerically.

## Problem and notation

Rotations $R_0, \ldots, R_{n-1}$ are given at times $t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_{n-1}$; let $\Delta t_i = t_{i+1} - t_i$.
Write $v_i$ for the rotation vector of relative rotation $R_i^{-1}R_{i+1}$ on interval $i$ (principal angle $\lvert v_i\rvert \le \pi$).
$\hat r$ is vector $r$'s cross-product matrix ($\hat r\,x = r \times x$); $\exp(\hat r)$ is rotation through angle $\lvert r\rvert$ about axis $r/\lvert r\rvert$.
Body-frame angular velocity $\omega$ of orientation $R(t)$ is defined by $\dot R = R\,\hat\omega$; write angular acceleration as $\alpha = \dot\omega$.
Rotation angles, angular velocities, and angular accelerations use rad, rad/(time unit), and rad/(time unit)², where the time unit is that of the knot times.

Slerp uses $R(t) = R_i\exp(u\,\hat v_i)$ ($u = (t - t_i)/\Delta t_i$), with constant interval angular velocity $v_i/\Delta t_i$.
We seek $R(t)$ passing through every given knot rotation, with $\omega$ and $\alpha$ continuous throughout $[t_0, t_{n-1}]$.
A cubic spline in Euclidean space obtains second-derivative continuity from a tridiagonal linear system with knot slopes as unknowns.
A rotation spline is its $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ counterpart; rotation-vector nonlinearity makes the equations nonlinear.

## Piecewise cubic rotation vectors

`RotationSpline` uses the following representation on interval $[t_i, t_{i+1}]$:

$$
R(t) = R_i \exp\left(\widehat{r_i(u)}\right), \qquad r_i(u) = a_i u^3 + b_i u^2 + c_i u
$$

Conditions $r_i(0) = 0$ and $r_i(1) = v_i$ ensure passage through the prescribed rotations at knots.
Using the right Jacobian of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$,

$$
J(r) = I - \frac{1 - \cos\lvert r\rvert}{\lvert r\rvert^2}\,\hat r + \frac{\lvert r\rvert - \sin\lvert r\rvert}{\lvert r\rvert^3}\,\hat r^2
$$

body-frame angular velocity is $\omega = J(r)\,\dot r$.
Here $\dot r = r'(u)/\Delta t_i$ is a time derivative and $r'$ a derivative with respect to $u$.
Angular acceleration is

$$
\alpha = \dot\omega = J(r)\,\ddot r + \dot J(r)\,\dot r
$$

where the second term is quadratic and nonlinear in $\dot r$.
Coefficients of $J(r)$ and $\dot J(r)$ use truncated Taylor series for $\lvert r\rvert \le 1$ and trigonometric functions for larger angles, avoiding cancellation near 0.
The inverse needed in the next section is, with $\theta = \lvert r\rvert$:

$$
J(r)^{-1} = I + \frac{1}{2}\hat r + \frac{1 - (\theta/2)\cot(\theta/2)}{\theta^2}\,\hat r^2
$$

Its final coefficient also suffers cancellation at $\theta \to 0$, so for $\theta < 0.1$ it is replaced by Taylor series $1/12 + \theta^2/720 + \theta^4/30240 + \theta^6/1209600$.

## Hermite conditions and angular-velocity continuity

Let $\omega_i$ be angular velocity at knot $t_i$; each interval polynomial is determined by the Hermite conditions:

$$
r_i(0) = 0, \quad r_i(1) = v_i, \quad r_i'(0) = \Delta t_i\,\omega_i, \quad r_i'(1) = \Delta t_i\,J(v_i)^{-1}\omega_{i+1}
$$

Since $J(0) = I$, the initial angular velocity is $\omega_i$ and the final angular velocity is $J(v_i)\,J(v_i)^{-1}\omega_{i+1} = \omega_{i+1}$, making angular velocity automatically continuous at knots.
Solving the cubic gives, with $c_i = \Delta t_i\,\omega_i$ and $e_i = \Delta t_i\,J(v_i)^{-1}\omega_{i+1}$:

$$
a_i = c_i + e_i - 2 v_i, \qquad b_i = 3 v_i - 2 c_i - e_i
$$

Endpoint angular velocities are fixed to adjacent-interval secants ($\omega_0 = v_0/\Delta t_0$, $\omega_{n-1} = v_{n-2}/\Delta t_{n-2}$).
The unknowns are internal-knot angular velocities $\omega_1, \ldots, \omega_{n-2}$.

## Angular-acceleration continuity and the block-tridiagonal system

At internal knot $t_k$, equate angular accelerations at the left interval's end and the right interval's start.
Substituting the coefficients above using $J(v)v = v$ and $\dot J(0)\,\dot r = 0$ gives, for every $k = 1, \ldots, n-2$:

$$
\frac{2}{\Delta t_{k-1}} J(v_{k-1})\,\omega_{k-1} + 4\left(\frac{1}{\Delta t_{k-1}} + \frac{1}{\Delta t_k}\right)\omega_k + \frac{2}{\Delta t_k} J(v_k)^{-1}\omega_{k+1}
= \frac{6 v_{k-1}}{\Delta t_{k-1}^2} + \frac{6 v_k}{\Delta t_k^2} - N_{k-1}(\omega_k)
$$

$N_{k-1}(\omega_k) = \dot J\,\dot r$ is the nonlinear term at the left interval's end, depending on $\omega_k$.
The left side is a tridiagonal linear system with 3×3 blocks, whose coefficients depend only on knot times and rotations.

Singular values of $J(v)$ are 1 along the rotation axis and $2\sin(\lvert v\rvert/2)/\lvert v\rvert \le 1$ in the perpendicular plane.
Thus $\lVert J(v)\rVert_2 \le 1$ and $\lVert J(v)^{-1}\rVert_2 = \lvert v\rvert/(2\sin(\lvert v\rvert/2)) \le \pi/2$ ($\lvert v\rvert \le \pi$), and the diagonal block's $4(1/\Delta t_{k-1} + 1/\Delta t_k)$ exceeds the sum of off-diagonal block norms $2/\Delta t_{k-1} + \pi/\Delta t_k$.
This block-diagonally dominant system is nonsingular and can be solved stably by the block Thomas algorithm (block LU factorization) without row swaps between blocks (Golub & Van Loan 2013, §4.5).
During elimination, each 3×3 diagonal block is row- and column-scaled by powers of two, then factored by LU with partial pivoting.
An exactly zero pivot returns `Singular`.

Spline preparation (`RotationSpline::new`, `with_options`, `build`) proceeds as follows.

1. Initialize each internal knot's angular velocity to the incoming interval's secant $v_{k-1}/\Delta t_{k-1}$.
   Form coefficients from these initial values and stop without iterating if angular-acceleration jumps already meet tolerance.
2. Factor the linear part once.
3. Evaluate nonlinear term $N_{k-1}(\omega_k)$ at current $\omega_k$ and form the right side.
4. Solve the factored system by forward elimination and back substitution, update internal angular velocities, and rebuild coefficients.
5. Measure angular-acceleration jumps at all internal knots and stop if every jump meets tolerance.
   Otherwise return to step 3 unless the iteration limit has been reached.

Each iteration costs work proportional to knot count $n$ and uses $O(n)$ memory.
The iteration has not been shown to be a contraction, so convergence is not guaranteed.
Nonlinear terms grow with rotations between keys; larger angles require more iterations.

## Termination status and continuity residual

Measure continuity by each internal knot's angular-acceleration jump $\lvert\alpha(t_k^+) - \alpha(t_k^-)\rvert$ (Euclidean norm).
Accept the knot when its jump is at most $\mathrm{atol} + \mathrm{rtol}\cdot s_k$.
Scale $s_k$ is the maximum of angular-acceleration magnitudes on both sides and pre-cancellation term magnitudes in both intervals ($6\lvert v\rvert + 4\lvert c\rvert + 4\lvert e\rvert$ divided by $\Delta t^2$).
The latter is included because nearly constant angular velocity makes angular acceleration cancel toward 0, so acceleration itself does not represent the scale of rounding noise.
Defaults for `RotationSplineOptions::new()` are iteration limit 32, $\mathrm{rtol} = \sqrt{\varepsilon}$, and $\mathrm{atol} = 0$.
$\mathrm{atol}$ has units rad/(time unit)² and defaults to 0 so acceptance is independent of time units.
Changing time units scales the jump and $s_k$ equally, leaving the relative test unchanged.
Change them with `with_max_iterations` and `with_continuity_tolerance`.
A zero limit succeeds only when secant-based initial values are already continuous.
Two knots have no internal knots and give `Converged` without iteration.

`RotationSpline::with_options(times, rotations, options)` and `RotationSpline::build(times, rotations, options, cx)`, which also accepts an execution or solve context, return results as `RotationSplineResult`.
`RotationSplineResult` has the following fields.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `termination` | `RotationSplineTermination::Converged` (all internal knots meet tolerance) or `IterationLimit` (the limit was reached first) |
| `spline` | `Some(RotationSpline)` only for `Converged`. No spline is returned for `IterationLimit` |
| `iterations` | Number of fixed-point iterations performed |
| `continuity_residual` | Largest internal-knot angular-acceleration jump [rad/(time unit)²]. Zero for two knots |

`with_options` and `build` return `IterationLimit` as termination status, not an error.
`RotationSpline::new(times, rotations)` prepares with default options and returns the spline itself, reporting `IterationLimit` as a `ConvergenceFailure` error.
Continuity holds within tolerance and only when a spline is returned.
Knot validation matches `Slerp::new`; singular linear systems return `Singular`, unrepresentable intermediate values `NumericalInstability`, and cancellation or deadlines in a context passed to `build` return `ExecutionStopped`.
`with_options` calls `build` with a sequential execution context without cancellation.
With a solve context passed to `build`, spline preparation becomes a nested solve and fixed-point iterations are recorded as nested iterations of the outer solve.
Nested iterations do not count toward outer limits, so only `max_iterations` determines the iteration limit ([Nested solves and evaluation counts](oxref:core-nested-accounting)).

`spline.evaluate(t)` returns `RotationSplineSample`, with rotation in `rotation`, body-frame angular velocity [rad/(time unit)] in `angular_velocity`, and angular acceleration [rad/(time unit)²] in `angular_acceleration`.
At knot times it returns the supplied rotation unchanged; angular velocity and acceleration use the right interval (the left interval at the final knot).
Times outside the interval give `InvalidInput`, NaN or infinite times `NonFinite`, and unrepresentable derivatives `NumericalInstability`.
Evaluation costs $O(\log n)$ without allocating memory.
For rotations alone at many times, use allocating `evaluate_rotations(&times)` or `evaluate_rotations_into` writing to preallocated workspace.
Reserve workspace as described in [Batch evaluation and workspaces](oxref:spatial-rotation-batches).

## Key orientations for comparison

The three cells on this page place five key orientations at times $t_i = i$ s ($i = 0, \ldots, 4$): identity, rotations by $\theta$ about $x$, $y$, and $z$, then identity.
Relative-rotation axes change between intervals, so Slerp angular velocity also changes direction at knots.
In the definitions block, `KEY_TIMES` supplies times, `key_rotations` these orientations, and `norm3` the Euclidean norm of a three-component vector.

```rust
//| id: spatial-rotation-keyframes
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Key orientations for rotation-spline comparisons"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-spatial]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_spatial::Rotation;

/// Key times t_i = i [s].
pub(crate) const KEY_TIMES: [f64; 5] = [0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0];

/// Identity, rotations by theta [rad] about x, y, and z, then identity.
pub(crate) fn key_rotations(theta: f64) -> Result<[Rotation<f64>; 5], SciOxideError> {
    Ok([
        Rotation::identity(),
        Rotation::from_axis_angle([1.0, 0.0, 0.0], theta)?,
        Rotation::from_axis_angle([0.0, 1.0, 0.0], theta)?,
        Rotation::from_axis_angle([0.0, 0.0, 1.0], theta)?,
        Rotation::identity(),
    ])
}

/// Euclidean norm of a three-component vector.
pub(crate) fn norm3(v: [f64; 3]) -> f64 {
    RealScalar::hypot(RealScalar::hypot(v[0], v[1]), v[2])
}
```

## Comparing angular velocity and acceleration

The next cell accepts key rotation angle $\theta$ and iteration limit and constructs a spline with `RotationSpline::build`.
Slerp angular velocities on intervals are computed from relative rotations as $v_i/\Delta t_i$.
Table `knots` shows Slerp angular-velocity jump $\lvert v_k/\Delta t_k - v_{k-1}/\Delta t_{k-1}\rvert$ and spline angular-velocity and acceleration jumps at internal knots.
Spline jumps are differences between left limits and `evaluate(t_k)` values (the right interval's start).
Left limits use quadratic extrapolation $3f(t_k - h) - 3f(t_k - 2h) + f(t_k - 3h)$ from three points $t_k - jh$ left of the knot ($j = 1, 2, 3$, $h = 10^{-3}$ s), with truncation error $O(h^3)$.
This difference checks continuity independently of the `build` residual.
Finally, an outcome shows the termination status.
The next cell plots angular velocity and acceleration over time from values exported by this cell.

```rust
//| id: spatial-rotation-spline-interpolation
//| ref: spatial-rotation-spline-interpolation
//| title: "Compare angular velocity and acceleration for Slerp and a rotation spline"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [spatial-rotation-keyframes]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-spatial]
//| exports: [slerp_curve, speed, acceleration]
//| inputs:
//|   key_angle: {"type":"range","label":"Key rotation angle θ [deg]","description":"Key-orientation angle about x, y, and z.","min":15,"max":150,"step":15,"value":60}
//|   max_iterations: {"type":"range","label":"Fixed-point iteration limit [iterations]","description":"Default: 32 iterations.","min":0,"max":32,"step":1,"value":32}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"large-angle","label":"θ = 150°","description":"Larger inter-key rotations increase angular-velocity jumps and iteration count.","inputs":{"key_angle":150}}
//|   - {"id":"one-iteration","label":"Iteration limit 1","description":"One iteration fails to meet continuity tolerance; IterationLimit returns no spline.","inputs":{"max_iterations":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"knots","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":0.5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"knots","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"knots","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-5}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: large-angle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"knots","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"min":4.0}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: one-iteration
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"knots","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":null}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"failed"}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, RealScalar};
use scioxide_spatial::{
    RotationSpline, RotationSplineOptions, RotationSplineSample, RotationSplineTermination,
};

let theta = RealScalar::to_radians(key_angle);
let rotations = key_rotations(theta).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = RotationSplineOptions::new().with_max_iterations(max_iterations as usize);
let result = RotationSpline::build(
    &KEY_TIMES,
    &rotations,
    options,
    ExecutionContext::sequential(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

// Slerp angular velocity v_i / Δt_i on interval i (v_i is the rotation vector of R_i⁻¹ R_{i+1}).
let mut slerp_rates = Vec::new();
for (pair, step) in rotations.windows(2).zip(KEY_TIMES.windows(2)) {
    let relative = pair[0]
        .inverse()
        .compose(pair[1])
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    slerp_rates.push(
        relative
            .as_rotation_vector()
            .map(|x| x / (step[1] - step[0])),
    );
}
let mut slerp_curve = Vec::new();
for (i, rate) in slerp_rates.iter().enumerate() {
    slerp_curve.push([KEY_TIMES[i], norm3(*rate)]);
    slerp_curve.push([KEY_TIMES[i + 1], norm3(*rate)]);
}

// Evaluate spline angular velocity and acceleration every 0.01 s.
let (mut speed, mut acceleration) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
if let Some(spline) = &result.spline {
    for j in 0..=400 {
        let t = 0.01 * f64::from(j);
        let sample = spline.evaluate(t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        speed.push([t, norm3(sample.angular_velocity)]);
        acceleration.push([t, norm3(sample.angular_acceleration)]);
    }
}

// Left limit at a knot: 3f(t − h) − 3f(t − 2h) + f(t − 3h) (quadratic extrapolation, error O(h³)).
let h = 1e-3;
let left_limit = |spline: &RotationSpline<f64>, t: f64| -> Result<[[f64; 3]; 2], String> {
    let at = |j: f64| -> Result<RotationSplineSample<f64>, String> {
        spline.evaluate(t - j * h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())
    };
    let (s1, s2, s3) = (at(1.0)?, at(2.0)?, at(3.0)?);
    let extrapolate = |a: [f64; 3], b: [f64; 3], c: [f64; 3]| {
        [0, 1, 2].map(|m| 3.0 * a[m] - 3.0 * b[m] + c[m])
    };
    Ok([
        extrapolate(
            s1.angular_velocity,
            s2.angular_velocity,
            s3.angular_velocity,
        ),
        extrapolate(
            s1.angular_acceleration,
            s2.angular_acceleration,
            s3.angular_acceleration,
        ),
    ])
};
let difference = |a: [f64; 3], b: [f64; 3]| norm3([a[0] - b[0], a[1] - b[1], a[2] - b[2]]);
let mut rows: Vec<(f64, f64, Option<f64>, Option<f64>)> = Vec::new();
for k in 1..KEY_TIMES.len() - 1 {
    let slerp_jump = difference(slerp_rates[k], slerp_rates[k - 1]);
    let (mut velocity_jump, mut acceleration_jump) = (None, None);
    if let Some(spline) = &result.spline {
        let [velocity, accel] = left_limit(spline, KEY_TIMES[k])?;
        let right = spline.evaluate(KEY_TIMES[k]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        velocity_jump = Some(difference(right.angular_velocity, velocity));
        acceleration_jump = Some(difference(right.angular_acceleration, accel));
    }
    rows.push((KEY_TIMES[k], slerp_jump, velocity_jump, acceleration_jump));
}

// The next cell draws the plots. Pass empty sequences if no spline is returned.
export!(slerp_curve, slerp_curve);
export!(speed, speed);
export!(acceleration, acceleration);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Knot t_k [s]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 0 } },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Slerp |Δω| [rad/s]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    {
        "key": "2",
        "label": "Spline |Δω| [rad/s]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": sci3,
        "nullText": "No spline"
    },
    {
        "key": "3",
        "label": "Spline |Δα| [rad/s²]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": sci3,
        "nullText": "No spline"
    }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "knots" })
);

let outcome = match result.termination {
    RotationSplineTermination::Converged => {
        Outcome::satisfied("Angular-acceleration jumps meet continuity tolerance at every internal knot")
    }
    RotationSplineTermination::IterationLimit => Outcome::failed(
        "Angular-acceleration jumps did not meet tolerance within the iteration limit; no spline is returned",
    )
    .explanation("Increase the iteration limit or decrease rotation angles between keys."),
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{:?}", result.termination))
        .diagnostic("Fixed-point iteration count", result.iterations as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Continuity residual", result.continuity_residual, Some("rad/s²"))
);
```

With default $\theta = 60°$, `build` reaches `Converged` in six fixed-point iterations with continuity residual $2.1 \times 10^{-7}$ rad/s².
At internal Slerp knots, angular-velocity vectors jump by 2.27–2.44 rad/s.
Extrapolated spline knot jumps are at most $2.2 \times 10^{-8}$ rad/s in angular velocity and $1.8 \times 10^{-7}$ rad/s² in angular acceleration, on the same order as the `build` residual.
Nonzero angular-velocity jumps come from extrapolation truncation error.

“θ = 150°” increases iterations to 16, with residual $5.5 \times 10^{-7}$ rad/s² and Slerp angular-velocity jumps 4.4–4.5 rad/s.
“Iteration limit 1” gives `IterationLimit` with residual 0.31 rad/s².
No spline is returned, so the outcome is `failed` and spline columns show “No spline.”

## Fixed-point convergence

The next cell calls `build` with the same key orientations and iteration limits $m$ increasing from 0 by 1, recording each $m$'s termination status and `continuity_residual`.
The iteration is deterministic, so the residual at limit $m$ is the residual after iteration $m$.
Stop at the $m$ that gives `Converged`.
Table `history` shows $m$, termination status, residual, and ratio to the preceding residual; the chart plots residuals on a logarithmic axis.
An approximately constant ratio indicates linear fixed-point convergence, with that ratio estimating the contraction factor.

Ratios to preceding residuals are 0.03–0.09, indicating linear convergence.
At $\theta = 15°$, ratios are 0.002–0.004 and convergence occurs at $m = 3$.
At $\theta = 150°$, ratios vary in an alternating pattern within the range 0.2–0.57, requiring $m = 16$.
For these three angles, ratios grew approximately in proportion to $\theta^2$.
Larger inter-key rotations increase nonlinear terms and may prevent convergence within 32 iterations; add more closely spaced key orientations in that case.

## Summary

`RotationSpline` makes interval rotation vectors cubic, imposes knot angular-velocity continuity through Hermite conditions, and solves angular-acceleration continuity equations for internal-knot angular velocities.
The equations' linear part is a block-diagonally dominant block-tridiagonal system, factored once; nonlinear terms enter the right side through fixed-point iteration.
Acceptance compares each internal angular-acceleration jump with scaled relative tolerance (default $\sqrt\varepsilon$); success returns a spline with `Converged`, while reaching the iteration limit first returns `IterationLimit` without a spline.
The iteration's contraction factor grows with inter-key rotation angle; for `IterationLimit`, increase the limit or add more closely spaced key orientations.
Endpoint angular velocities are fixed to adjacent-interval secants and endpoint accelerations are nonzero, so this cannot directly produce a trajectory starting and ending at rest.
