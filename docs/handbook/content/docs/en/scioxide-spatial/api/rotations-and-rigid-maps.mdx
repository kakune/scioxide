This table groups items for representing, interpolating, and estimating three-dimensional rotations and rigid transformations.
Rotations are active, right-handed rotations with angles in radians.
Quaternions store the scalar part last, in $[x, y, z, w]$ order, and canonicalize the sign because $q$ and $-q$ represent the same rotation.
Matrices use row-major storage and act on column vectors.
The `*_into` methods that process many points or times together write results to a preallocated `TransformWorkspace` before copying them to the output.
Obtain workspace requirements with the associated function `TransformWorkspace::workspace_requirements(request)`.

## Orientation interpolation

| [`Slerp`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-spatial/0.1.0/scioxide_spatial/struct.Slerp.html) | Struct | Spherical linear interpolation | Uses $R(t) = R_i \exp(u v_i)$ between knots. $u = (t - t_i)/(t_{i+1} - t_i)$, and $v_i$ is the rotation vector of $R_i^{-1} R_{i+1}$ with angle at most $\pi$. At exactly a half turn, the sign convention of `Rotation` selects the direction; at knots, the supplied rotation is returned unchanged. `new` clones and owns knot times and rotations; fewer than two knots or times that are not strictly increasing give `InvalidInput`, mismatched lengths give `DimensionMismatch`, and non-finite times give `NonFinite`. Evaluation costs $O(\log n)$ without allocation, and a time outside the knot interval gives `InvalidInput`. `evaluate_rotations(&times)` allocates and returns rotations at many times, while `evaluate_rotations_into` uses a `TransformWorkspace` to write to the output | [Spherical linear interpolation (Slerp)](oxref:spatial-slerp) | [Compare angular velocity and acceleration for Slerp and a rotation spline](oxref:spatial-rotation-spline-interpolation), [Compare a rotation spline's angular velocity and acceleration with Slerp](oxref:spatial-attitude-interpolation) |
| [`RotationSpline`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-spatial/0.1.0/scioxide_spatial/struct.RotationSpline.html) | Struct | Cubic rotation spline | Uses $R(t) = R_i \exp(r_i(t))$ on each interval, where $r_i$ is a rotation vector represented by a cubic polynomial. Knot errors are the same as for `Slerp::new`; a singular linear system in the iteration gives `Singular`, and an unrepresentable intermediate result gives `NumericalInstability`. Evaluation costs $O(\log n)$ without allocation, and `evaluate_rotations` allocates and returns rotations at many times. A time outside the interval gives `InvalidInput` | [Piecewise cubic rotation vectors](oxref:spatial-rotation-spline), [Termination status and continuity residual](oxref:spatial-rotation-spline-termination) | [Compare angular velocity and acceleration for Slerp and a rotation spline](oxref:spatial-rotation-spline-interpolation), [Measure how quickly the continuity residual decreases in rotation-spline fixed-point iteration](oxref:spatial-rotation-spline-convergence), [Compare a rotation spline's angular velocity and acceleration with Slerp](oxref:spatial-attitude-interpolation) |

## Summary

Rotations use unit quaternions and are renormalized after every composition, so repeated composition keeps deviations from unit norm at the scale of rounding error.
Euler angles support 12 sequences in both intrinsic and extrinsic form; `gimbal_lock` becomes true when the middle angle is near a singularity.
Use `Slerp` to connect knots at constant angular velocity, or `RotationSpline` to make angular velocity and acceleration continuous at knots.
The result contains a spline only when the termination status is `Converged`.
Estimate a rigid transformation from corresponding points with `estimate_rigid_transform` (or `estimate_rigid_transform_with_options` when supplying weights and settings); configurations that do not uniquely determine a rotation, such as collinear points, give `InvalidInput`.
Examine the mathematics in [Three-dimensional rotations and orientation interpolation](../../algorithms/rotations-and-transforms/) and [Rigid alignment](../../algorithms/rigid-registration/).
