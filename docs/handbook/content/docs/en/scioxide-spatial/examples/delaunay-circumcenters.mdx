A Voronoi diagram is dual to a Delaunay triangulation, with Voronoi vertices at Delaunay-triangle circumcenters.
Topology (which triangles correspond to which vertices) is exact, while vertex coordinates are rounded.
This example measures coordinate accuracy by comparing `Voronoi` vertices with an independent circumcenter formula, and checks triangle and vertex counts with Euler's formula.
Point configurations use the same randomness as [Delaunay maximization of the minimum angle](../delaunay-min-angle/), contrasting nearly and exactly cocircular points.

## Circumcenters and Voronoi vertices

For a planar Delaunay triangulation of $N$ points with $h$ hull-boundary points, Euler's formula gives $2N - h - 2$ triangles.
Hull-boundary points (including points along hull-edge interiors) are exactly those with unbounded Voronoi regions.
Use $h$ counted with `region_is_unbounded` to predict triangle count and compare with the `Delaunay` count.

Four or more cocircular points are triangulated through symbolic perturbation, producing neighboring triangles sharing a circumcircle.
`Voronoi` assigns one vertex to a group of adjacent exactly cocircular triangles; `simplex_vertices()` returns each triangle's vertex index.
Thus two triangles splitting a lattice square share one Voronoi vertex.
Input-row/point-index mappings come from `input_mapping` of `Delaunay` retained by `Voronoi`.

Vertex coordinates are computed with rounding during construction.
Translate triangle vertex $a$ to the origin and let $E$ be the $2\times 2$ matrix with edge vectors $e_1, e_2$ from $a$ as columns.
Circumcenter $c$ satisfies $E^{\mathsf T} c = \tfrac12(|e_1|^2, |e_2|^2)^{\mathsf T}$ and is obtained from the inverse of $E$.
Scale $E$ to largest component 1 and let $\|E^{-1}\|$ be the largest absolute inverse entry; triangles with $\|E^{-1}\|\,\varepsilon\,d^2 > \sqrt{\varepsilon}$ (machine epsilon $\varepsilon$, $d = 2$) are deemed ill-conditioned and have no usable circumcenter.
For a group sharing a circumcircle, use the lowest-index available triangle's circumcenter; if all triangles are ill-conditioned, the vertex's `vertex` returns `NumericalInstability`.
Other vertices and topology remain usable.
No vertex-coordinate error bound is supplied.

For reference circumcenters, set $b' = b - a$, $c' = c - a$, and $D = 2(b'_x c'_y - b'_y c'_x)$ and define

$$
u_x = \frac{c'_y |b'|^2 - b'_y |c'|^2}{D}, \qquad u_y = \frac{b'_x |c'|^2 - c'_x |b'|^2}{D}
$$

to obtain circumcenter $a + u$.
This solution uses Cramer's rule, independently of SciOxide's inverse-matrix calculation.
Since $|u|$ is the circumradius, relative difference $|v - (a + u)|/|u|$ measures vertex $v$'s displacement relative to radius.

## Comparing circumcenters

The next cell selects point count $N$, configuration, and random seed, constructs `Voronoi::new`, and compares vertices with reference circumcenters of each triangle.
There are three configurations.
“Uniform” uses uniform unit-square points; “On circle” uses uniform angles on the unit circle (coordinates round to `f64`, so cocircularity holds only within rounding); “Integer lattice” uses an integer grid with $\lceil\sqrt{N}\,\rceil$ columns.
The first table checks combinatorics: deduplicated count $N$, hull-boundary count $h$, triangle count and $2N - h - 2$, Voronoi-vertex count, triangles sharing vertices, input rows with coordinate mismatches at their canonical indices, and symbolic-perturbation use.
The second table shows largest relative circumcenter difference, largest relative spread $(\max - \min)/\max$ among distances from a Voronoi vertex to three triangle vertices, and triangles with uncomputable Voronoi vertices.
The reference circumcenter uses ordinary `f64` arithmetic and lengths from `RealScalar::hypot`.

```rust
//| id: spatial-delaunay-voronoi-circumcenters
//| ref: spatial-delaunay-voronoi-circumcenters
//| title: "Compare circumcenters with Voronoi vertices"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-spatial]
//| inputs:
//|   site_count: {"type":"range","label":"Point count N","min":20,"max":400,"step":10,"value":100}
//|   configuration: {"type":"select","label":"Point configuration","options":[{"label":"Uniform (unit square)","value":"uniform"},{"label":"On circle (nearly cocircular)","value":"circle"},{"label":"Integer lattice (exactly cocircular)","value":"lattice"}],"value":"uniform"}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"circle","label":"100 points on a circle","description":"Thin triangles with three nearby points on the circle have larger relative circumcenter differences.","inputs":{"configuration":"circle"}}
//|   - {"id":"lattice","label":"Integer lattice","description":"Two triangles splitting a square share one vertex, and symbolic perturbation is used.","inputs":{"configuration":"lattice"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":14}
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":184}
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":184}
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"circumcenters","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"circumcenters","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":0}
//|   - case: circle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":100}
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":98}
//|       - {"artifact":"circumcenters","path":"/rows/0/0","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|   - case: lattice
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":36}
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":162}
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":81}
//|       - {"artifact":"combinatorics","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":162}
//|       - {"artifact":"circumcenters","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":0}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{Rng, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};
use scioxide_spatial::{PointSet, Voronoi};

// Return n points of configuration in (x, y) order, one per row.
fn sites(configuration: &str, n: usize, seed: u64) -> Result<Vec<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed);
    let mut coordinates: Vec<f64> = Vec::with_capacity(2 * n);
    if configuration == "lattice" {
        // Integer grid with ⌈√n⌉ columns. Four vertices of each square are exactly cocircular.
        let mut columns = 1;
        while columns * columns < n {
            columns += 1;
        }
        for i in 0..n {
            coordinates.push((i % columns) as f64);
            coordinates.push((i / columns) as f64);
        }
    } else if configuration == "circle" {
        for _ in 0..n {
            let uniform: f64 = rng.next_float()?;
            let angle = 2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI * uniform;
            coordinates.push(RealScalar::cos(angle));
            coordinates.push(RealScalar::sin(angle));
        }
    } else {
        for _ in 0..2 * n {
            coordinates.push(rng.next_float()?);
        }
    }
    Ok(coordinates)
}

let n = site_count as usize;
let coordinates = sites(&configuration, n, seed as u64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let points = PointSet::from_rows(&coordinates, 2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let voronoi = Voronoi::new(&points).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let delaunay = voronoi.delaunay();
let canonical = delaunay.points();
let unique = canonical.len() / 2;

// Input row i maps to canonical index input_mapping()[i]. Count rows with coordinate mismatches.
let mapping = delaunay.input_mapping();
let moved = (0..n)
    .filter(|&i| canonical[2 * mapping[i]..2 * mapping[i] + 2] != coordinates[2 * i..2 * i + 2])
    .count();
let mut hull = 0;
for site in 0..unique {
    let unbounded = voronoi
        .region_is_unbounded(site)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    hull += usize::from(unbounded);
}
let mut sharing = vec![0_usize; voronoi.vertex_count()];
for &vertex in voronoi.simplex_vertices() {
    sharing[vertex] += 1;
}
let shared = sharing.iter().filter(|&&c| c > 1).sum::<usize>();

// Relative differences between reference circumcenter a + u (Cramer's rule) and Voronoi vertex, and spread of distances to three triangle vertices.
let (mut center_gap, mut spread, mut failed) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0);
for simplex in 0..delaunay.simplex_count() {
    let corner = |i: usize| -> Result<[f64; 2], String> {
        let site = delaunay.simplex(simplex).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?[i];
        Ok([canonical[2 * site], canonical[2 * site + 1]])
    };
    let [a, b, c] = [corner(0)?, corner(1)?, corner(2)?];
    let (bx, by, cx, cy) = (b[0] - a[0], b[1] - a[1], c[0] - a[0], c[1] - a[1]);
    let (b2, c2, det) = (
        bx * bx + by * by,
        cx * cx + cy * cy,
        2.0 * (bx * cy - by * cx),
    );
    let u = [(cy * b2 - by * c2) / det, (bx * c2 - cx * b2) / det];
    let radius = RealScalar::hypot(u[0], u[1]);
    let Ok(vertex) = voronoi.vertex(voronoi.simplex_vertices()[simplex]) else {
        failed += 1;
        continue;
    };
    let gap = RealScalar::hypot(vertex[0] - a[0] - u[0], vertex[1] - a[1] - u[1]);
    center_gap = center_gap.max(gap / radius);
    let reach = [a, b, c].map(|p| RealScalar::hypot(vertex[0] - p[0], vertex[1] - p[1]));
    let (low, high) = (
        reach.iter().copied().fold(f64::MAX, f64::min),
        reach.iter().copied().fold(0.0, f64::max),
    );
    spread = spread.max((high - low) / high);
}

let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Deduplicated points N [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Hull-boundary points h [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Triangles [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "2N − h − 2 [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Voronoi vertices [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Triangles sharing vertices [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Input rows with coordinate mismatches [rows]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Symbolic perturbation" }
]);
let ties = if delaunay.provenance().resolved_ties {
    "Used"
} else {
    "Unused"
};
let row = (
    unique,
    hull,
    delaunay.simplex_count(),
    2 * unique - hull - 2,
    voronoi.vertex_count(),
    shared,
    moved,
    ties,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "combinatorics" })
);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let accuracy = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Largest relative circumcenter difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Largest relative difference in three vertex distances [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Triangles with uncomputable Voronoi vertices [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    accuracy,
    vec![(center_gap, spread, failed)],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "circumcenters" })
);
```

With defaults (100 uniform points, seed 1), 14 points have unbounded regions, matching hull-boundary count; 184 triangles equal $2N - h - 2$.
There are 184 Voronoi vertices, with no sharing between triangles.
The largest relative circumcenter difference is $4.4 \times 10^{-15}$ and relative spread of three distances $5.7 \times 10^{-15}$, both on the rounding scale.
For circular configurations, nearby triples make circumcenter computation ill-conditioned, raising relative differences to $1.4 \times 10^{-13}$.
The relative spread of three distances remains small at $3.3 \times 10^{-16}$.
For thin triangles of nearby points, moving the circumcenter slightly toward the three vertices changes all distances almost equally, so this spread does not measure vertex-position error.
On the integer lattice, 162 triangles share 81 vertices in pairs and symbolic perturbation is used.
Small integer coordinates give circumcenters without rounding, so relative differences are 0.

## Summary

Voronoi vertices are rounded Delaunay-triangle circumcenters; topology is exact, but no coordinate error bound is supplied.
For well-conditioned triangles, independent Cramer's-rule circumcenters agree at around $10^{-14}$.
Thin triangles of nearby circular points can give relative differences of order $10^{-13}$; inspect triangle shape before using Voronoi coordinates in subsequent calculations.
Exactly cocircular neighboring triangles share one vertex, so there are fewer Voronoi vertices than triangles.
Compute $2N - h - 2$ from hull-boundary count $h$ found by `region_is_unbounded` to check triangle count independently.
