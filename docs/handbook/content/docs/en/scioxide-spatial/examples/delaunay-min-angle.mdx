For finite-element meshes and scattered interpolation, triangulation quality affects errors and conditioning.
Thin triangles (small minimum angles) increase stiffness-matrix condition numbers and constants in interpolation error bounds.
One point set admits many triangulations; Delaunay triangulation maximizes their minimum angle.
This example randomly flips edges of a triangulation constructed with `Delaunay` and compares minimum angles and local Delaunay conditions.

## Triangulations and edge flips

A triangulation of $N$ planar points uses all points as vertices and covers their convex hull with nonoverlapping triangles.
With $h$ hull-boundary points, Euler's formula gives $T = 2N - h - 2$ triangles and $3N - h - 3$ edges, the same for every triangulation.
Of these, $h$ are hull edges and $3N - 2h - 3$ are internal.

Internal edge $pq$ lies between triangles $pqr$ and $qps$.
If quadrilateral $p\,r\,q\,s$ is convex, replacing edge $pq$ with the other diagonal $rs$ gives another triangulation.
This operation is an edge flip.
For a nonconvex quadrilateral, $rs$ passes outside and cannot be flipped.
Convexity holds exactly when $p$ and $q$ lie on opposite sides of the line through $r$ and $s$: `orient2d(r, s, p)` and `orient2d(r, s, q)` must be nonzero with opposite signs.

## Delaunay minimum-angle maximization

`incircle(a, b, c, d)` returns the sign of a 4×4 determinant with rows $(x, y, x^2 + y^2, 1)$.
For counterclockwise $a, b, c$, $d$ inside their circle gives positive, outside negative, and on the circle zero.
For internal edge $pq$, orient one adjoining triangle counterclockwise as $a, b, c$ and let $d$ be the other triangle's opposite vertex.
The edge $pq$ is locally Delaunay when $d$ is not inside the circle (`incircle` is nonpositive).

The inscribed-angle theorem explains how edge flips affect angles.
If a convex quadrilateral's diagonal $pq$ is not locally Delaunay, flipping it lexicographically increases the sorted sequence of six angles in the two triangles (de Berg et al. 2008, Chapter 9).
A triangulation with all internal edges locally Delaunay is Delaunay: no triangle's circumcircle contains another point inside (Delaunay's lemma).
Without four cocircular points, the Delaunay triangulation is unique and lexicographically maximizes the sorted sequence of all angles among all triangulations (Lawson 1977, Sibson 1978).
In particular, it maximizes the minimum angle, a property retained by every Delaunay triangulation even with cocircular points (de Berg et al. 2008, Theorem 9.9).

Four or more cocircular points make the Delaunay triangulation nonunique.
By the inscribed-angle theorem, triangle angles with vertices on a circle equal half their opposite arcs.
Thus, when all points are cocircular, every triangulation has the same minimum angle, but other angles differ.
Maximizing the minimum angle also does not imply reducing the maximum angle.
Linear-element interpolation gradient errors grow when the maximum angle approaches $180°$ (Babuška and Aziz 1976), but Delaunay triangulation need not minimize that angle.

## Delaunay construction and indices

`Delaunay::new` uses randomized incremental construction of the lower hull of three-dimensional points lifted to paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$.
Insertion order uses fixed pseudorandom values from canonical indices (defined below), making results identical across runs and independent of input order.
Projecting lower hull faces to the plane gives the Delaunay triangulation (Brown 1979).
Exact predicates determine faces, so the combinatorial structure is exact for supplied coordinates.
Exactly zero determinants, as for four cocircular points, are resolved by canonical-index symbolic perturbation; `provenance().resolved_ties` indicates that this occurred at least once.

Construction first sorts points lexicographically by coordinates and merges equal points.
Sorted point indices are called canonical indices.
Vertex indices from `simplex(i)` are sorted-site canonical indices; `points()` returns coordinates in canonical order.
Input row $i$ corresponds to canonical index `input_mapping()[i]`; the first input row for canonical index $j$ is `representatives()[j]`.
The three indices in `simplex(i)` are increasing and encode no orientation.
`triangle_mesh()` returns a `TriangleMesh` using canonical-index points and counterclockwise triangles; flips are performed on this mesh.

## Flip procedure and point configurations

Construct the flipped triangulation as follows.

1. Convert the Delaunay triangulation to `TriangleMesh` with `Delaunay::triangle_mesh`.
2. Select an edge with `uniform_index`.
   If it is an internal edge with two triangles returned by `face_cells` and their quadrilateral is convex, flip; otherwise select again.
3. Reorder the two flipped triangles counterclockwise with `orient2d` and construct a new mesh with `with_cells`.
   `with_cells` checks orientations, degeneracy, duplication, unused vertices, shared-edge directions, and vertex manifoldness, so faulty flips fail here.
4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 for the requested count.

There are four point configurations.
“Uniform” uses unit-square uniform points; “Clusters” scatters points around five centers with normal standard deviation 0.04.
“On circle” uses uniform angles on the unit circle; coordinates round to `f64`, so cocircularity holds only within rounding.
“Integer lattice” uses an integer grid with $\lceil\sqrt{N}\,\rceil$ columns, whose square vertices are exactly cocircular.

The following block defines quantities shared by two cells.
`sites` returns each configuration's coordinates in $(x, y)$ order with one point per row.
Each cell borrows this sequence with `PointSet::from_rows` and passes it to `Delaunay`.

```rust
//| id: spatial-delaunay-sites
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Four point configurations"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-spatial]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, Rng, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};

/// Return n points of configuration in (x, y) order, one per row.
pub(crate) fn sites(configuration: &str, n: usize, seed: u64) -> Result<Vec<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed);
    let mut coordinates: Vec<f64> = Vec::with_capacity(2 * n);
    match configuration {
        "clustered" => {
            let centers = [
                [0.2, 0.2],
                [0.8, 0.25],
                [0.5, 0.55],
                [0.25, 0.8],
                [0.8, 0.8],
            ];
            let spread = Normal::new(0.0, 0.04)?;
            for i in 0..n {
                let [x, y] = centers[i % centers.len()];
                coordinates.push(x + spread.sample(&mut rng)?);
                coordinates.push(y + spread.sample(&mut rng)?);
            }
        }
        "circle" => {
            for _ in 0..n {
                let uniform: f64 = rng.next_float()?;
                let angle = 2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI * uniform;
                coordinates.push(RealScalar::cos(angle));
                coordinates.push(RealScalar::sin(angle));
            }
        }
        "lattice" => {
            // Integer grid with ⌈√n⌉ columns. Four vertices of each square are exactly cocircular.
            let mut columns = 1;
            while columns * columns < n {
                columns += 1;
            }
            for i in 0..n {
                coordinates.push((i % columns) as f64);
                coordinates.push((i / columns) as f64);
            }
        }
        _ => {
            for _ in 0..2 * n {
                coordinates.push(rng.next_float()?);
            }
        }
    }
    Ok(coordinates)
}
```

## Random edge flips and mesh generations

The next cell selects point count $N$, flip count, configuration, and random seed, then repeatedly flips edges of a Delaunay triangulation.
Edge selection uses `SplitMix64` initialized with a separate seed (point seed + 1000).
The table shows input-point count, canonical count (after deduplication), triangle count, flipped and selected edges, and final mesh generation.
A mesh from `with_cells` belongs to the original family and advances generation by one per flip.
This cell passes flipped triangles (canonical vertices), $N$, configuration, and seed to the next cell.

```rust
//| id: spatial-delaunay-flip-walk
//| ref: spatial-delaunay-flip-walk
//| title: "Randomly flip edges of a Delaunay triangulation"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [spatial-delaunay-sites]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-spatial]
//| exports: [site_count, configuration, seed, flipped_cells]
//| inputs:
//|   site_count: {"type":"range","label":"Point count N","min":20,"max":400,"step":10,"value":100}
//|   flips: {"type":"range","label":"Flip count","min":0,"max":500,"step":10,"value":100}
//|   configuration: {"type":"select","label":"Point configuration","options":[{"label":"Uniform (unit square)","value":"uniform"},{"label":"Clusters (five normal distributions)","value":"clustered"},{"label":"On circle (nearly cocircular)","value":"circle"},{"label":"Integer lattice (exactly cocircular)","value":"lattice"}],"value":"uniform"}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"circle","label":"100 points on a circle","description":"All points lie on the hull, giving N − 2 triangles.","inputs":{"configuration":"circle"}}
//|   - {"id":"lattice","label":"Integer lattice","description":"On a 10 × 10 lattice, symbolic perturbation chooses each square's diagonal.","inputs":{"configuration":"lattice"}}
//|   - {"id":"many-flips","label":"500 flips","description":"More flips move the triangulation farther from Delaunay.","inputs":{"flips":500}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"flips","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":184}
//|       - {"artifact":"flips","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":100}
//|       - {"artifact":"flips","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":100}
//|   - case: circle
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"flips","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":98}
//|       - {"artifact":"flips","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":100}
//|   - case: lattice
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"flips","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":162}
//|   - case: many-flips
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"flips","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":500}
//|       - {"artifact":"flips","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":500}
use scioxide_random::{SeedableRng, SplitMix64, uniform_index};
use scioxide_spatial::{Delaunay, PointSet, PredicateSign, orient2d};

let n = site_count as usize;
let coordinates = sites(&configuration, n, seed as u64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let points = PointSet::from_rows(&coordinates, 2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let delaunay = Delaunay::new(&points).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Mesh vertices use canonical indices (sorted-point indices), with every triangle counterclockwise.
let original = delaunay.triangle_mesh().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let at = |v: usize| *original.points()[v].as_array();
let side =
    |a: usize, b: usize, c: usize| orient2d(&at(a), &at(b), &at(c)).map_err(|e| e.to_string());

// Randomly select and flip internal edges of convex quadrilaterals.
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64 + 1000);
let mut mesh = original.clone();
let target = flips as usize;
let (mut performed, mut attempts) = (0, 0);
while performed < target && attempts < 100 * target {
    attempts += 1;
    let face = uniform_index(mesh.face_count(), &mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let pair = mesh.face_cells(face).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.to_vec();
    if pair.len() != 2 {
        continue; // Hull edges cannot be flipped.
    }
    let edge = mesh
        .face_vertices(face)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .to_vec();
    let mut apex = [0; 2];
    for (slot, &cell) in apex.iter_mut().zip(&pair) {
        let vertices = mesh.cell_vertices(cell).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        *slot = vertices
            .iter()
            .copied()
            .find(|v| !edge.contains(v))
            .ok_or("Missing opposite vertex")?;
    }
    let [r, s] = apex;
    // Quadrilateral p r q s is convex exactly when p and q lie strictly on opposite sides of line rs.
    let (side_p, side_q) = (side(r, s, edge[0])?, side(r, s, edge[1])?);
    if side_p == PredicateSign::Zero || side_q == PredicateSign::Zero || side_p == side_q {
        continue;
    }
    let mut cells: Vec<[usize; 3]> = mesh.cells().map(|c| [c[0], c[1], c[2]]).collect();
    for (cell, end, sign) in [(pair[0], edge[0], side_p), (pair[1], edge[1], side_q)] {
        // Positive orient2d(r, s, end) means (r, s, end) is counterclockwise.
        cells[cell] = if sign == PredicateSign::Positive {
            [r, s, end]
        } else {
            [s, r, end]
        };
    }
    mesh = mesh.with_cells(cells).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    performed += 1;
}

let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Input points [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Deduplicated points [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Triangles [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Flipped edges [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Selected edges [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Mesh generation after flips", "type": "integer" }
]);
let generation = mesh.mesh_token().generation().get();
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        n,
        mesh.vertex_count(),
        mesh.cell_count(),
        performed,
        attempts,
        generation
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "flips" })
);
let cell_list = |m: &scioxide_spatial::TriangleMesh<f64>| -> Vec<[usize; 3]> {
    m.cells().map(|c| [c[0], c[1], c[2]]).collect()
};
export!(site_count, &n);
export!(configuration, &configuration);
export!(seed, &(seed as u64));
export!(flipped_cells, &cell_list(&mesh));
```

With defaults (100 uniform points, 100 flips, seed 1), 14 points lie on the hull and there are $2 \cdot 100 - 14 - 2 = 184$ triangles.
Of 160 selected edges, 60 were hull edges or diagonals of nonconvex quadrilaterals; 100 were flipped.
The final mesh shares the original family and has generation 100.
In the circular configuration, all points lie on the hull, giving 98 triangles.

## Minimum angles and local Delaunay conditions after flips

The next cell regenerates points from $N$, configuration, and seed, reconstructs the Delaunay triangulation, and rebuilds received flipped triangles on the same canonical-index points with `with_cells`.
Coordinates themselves are not transferred.
Values pass between cells as JSON; rereading decimal representations can change final bits, which alone can alter `incircle` signs for nearly cocircular points.
Then inspect both triangulations' angles and internal-edge `incircle` signs.
Compute angles at each triangle vertex from `RealScalar::atan2(|cross product|, dot product)` and express them in degrees.
For each triangulation, the table shows triangles, internal edges, positive `incircle` edges (not locally Delaunay), zero edges (cocircular), minimum angle, mean triangle minimum angle, and maximum angle.
The plot overlays histograms of triangle minimum angles in 2.5° bins.

```rust
//| id: spatial-delaunay-min-angle-check
//| ref: spatial-delaunay-min-angle-check
//| title: "Compare angles and local conditions of Delaunay and flipped triangulations"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [spatial-delaunay-sites]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-spatial]
//| dependsOn:
//|   walk: spatial-delaunay-flip-walk
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"angles","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"angles","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":53}
//|       - {"artifact":"angles","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":1.4134061048729,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"angles","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":0.27616828917769,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"histogram","path":"/spec/layers/1/points/0/1","equals":10}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_spatial::{Delaunay, PointSet, PredicateSign, incircle};

let n = walk["site_count"].as_u64().ok_or("Missing point count")? as usize;
let configuration = walk["configuration"].as_str().ok_or("Missing configuration")?;
let seed = walk["seed"].as_u64().ok_or("Missing seed")?;
let flipped: Vec<[usize; 3]> =
    serde_json::from_value(walk["flipped_cells"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Regenerate Delaunay from identical points without transferring coordinates.
let coordinates = sites(configuration, n, seed).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let points = PointSet::from_rows(&coordinates, 2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let delaunay = Delaunay::new(&points).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let original = delaunay.triangle_mesh().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let reflipped = original.with_cells(flipped).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut layers = Vec::new();
for (name, key, mesh) in [
    ("Delaunay triangulation", "delaunay", &original),
    ("Flipped triangulation", "flipped", &reflipped),
] {
    let at = |v: usize| *mesh.points()[v].as_array();
    // From each triangle's three angles [deg], find triangle minima and the overall maximum.
    let mut minima = Vec::with_capacity(mesh.cell_count());
    let mut largest = 0.0_f64;
    for cell in mesh.cells() {
        let corners = [at(cell[0]), at(cell[1]), at(cell[2])];
        let angles = (0..3).map(|i| {
            let [o, u, w] = [corners[i], corners[(i + 1) % 3], corners[(i + 2) % 3]];
            let (ux, uy, wx, wy) = (u[0] - o[0], u[1] - o[1], w[0] - o[0], w[1] - o[1]);
            let (cross, dot) = (ux * wy - uy * wx, ux * wx + uy * wy);
            RealScalar::to_degrees(RealScalar::atan2(cross.abs(), dot))
        });
        let (low, high) = angles.fold((180.0_f64, 0.0_f64), |(l, h), a| (l.min(a), h.max(a)));
        minima.push(low);
        largest = largest.max(high);
    }
    // For each internal edge, compare one counterclockwise triangle abc's circumcircle with the other opposite vertex d.
    let (mut interior, mut inside, mut cocircular) = (0, 0, 0);
    for face in 0..mesh.face_count() {
        let pair = mesh.face_cells(face).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if pair.len() != 2 {
            continue;
        }
        interior += 1;
        let abc = mesh.cell_vertices(pair[0]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let other = mesh.cell_vertices(pair[1]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let d = *other
            .iter()
            .find(|v| !abc.contains(v))
            .ok_or("Missing opposite vertex")?;
        let sign = incircle(&at(abc[0]), &at(abc[1]), &at(abc[2]), &at(d))
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        match sign {
            PredicateSign::Positive => inside += 1,
            PredicateSign::Zero => cocircular += 1,
            PredicateSign::Negative => {}
        }
    }
    let smallest = minima.iter().copied().fold(180.0, f64::min);
    let mean = minima.iter().sum::<f64>() / minima.len() as f64;
    rows.push((
        name,
        mesh.cell_count(),
        interior,
        inside,
        cocircular,
        smallest,
        mean,
        largest,
    ));
    // Draw 2.5°-bin frequencies as steps centered on bins.
    let mut counts = [0_usize; 24];
    for &angle in &minima {
        counts[((angle / 2.5) as usize).min(23)] += 1;
    }
    let steps: Vec<[f64; 2]> = counts
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(i, &c)| [2.5 * i as f64 + 1.25, c as f64])
        .collect();
    layers.push(serde_json::json!({
        "id": key,
        "kind": "line",
        "name": name,
        "points": steps,
        "style": { "step": "middle", "showSymbol": false }
    }));
}

let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Triangulation" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Triangles [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Internal edges [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Positive incircle edges [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Zero incircle edges [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Minimum angle [deg]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Mean triangle minimum angle [deg]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Maximum angle [deg]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "angles" })
);
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "xy",
        "xLabel": "Triangle minimum angle [deg]",
        "yLabel": "Triangles [count]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 60.0,
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "layers": layers
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "histogram" })
);
```

With defaults, none of Delaunay's 269 internal edges has positive `incircle`, while the flipped triangulation has 53 non-locally-Delaunay edges.
Minimum angle falls from Delaunay's $1.413°$ to $0.276°$ after flipping, and mean triangle minimum angle falls from $28.16°$ to $21.18°$.
Uniform points still leave thin triangles around $1.4°$ even in Delaunay; maximizing the minimum angle does not make it large.
Maximum angles are $175.52°$ for Delaunay and $179.10°$ after flipping.
The flipped histogram's $0$–$2.5°$ bin increases from three to ten triangles, shifting toward smaller angles.

Across configurations, Delaunay's minimum angle never falls below the flipped triangulation's.
Clusters give $0.782°$ versus $0.223°$.
For circular points, both minimum angles equal $0.01701°$, but mean triangle minimum angles differ at $3.53°$ and $3.16°$.
Coordinate rounding breaks exact cocircularity, so the flipped triangulation has 31 positive `incircle` edges.
Final coordinate bits determine Delaunay diagonals; x86_64 evaluation with different rounding of `cos` and `sin` changes the Delaunay triangulation itself, giving mean $3.85°$.
The integer lattice has 81 cocircular Delaunay diagonals (`incircle` zero) and minimum angle $45°$.
After flips, the minimum angle falls to $7.13°$ and 28 edges cease to be locally Delaunay.

## Summary

Edge flips exchange convex-quadrilateral diagonals; use `orient2d` for exact convexity and `incircle` signs on counterclockwise triangles for local Delaunay conditions.
A triangulation with nonpositive `incircle` on every internal edge is Delaunay, and its minimum angle is no smaller than any other triangulation's.
For cocircular points, only the minimum angle is triangulation-independent; other angles vary, so Delaunay alone does not fix the angle distribution.
Delaunay need not reduce maximum angles; check them separately when they control behavior, such as gradient errors in linear elements.
Vertices of `Delaunay` use sorted-site canonical indices, mapped to input rows through `input_mapping` and `representatives`.
