In quantum-mechanical WKB approximations and optical caustics, approximations fail at the boundary between oscillatory and exponentially decaying regions (a turning point).
Expanding the equation to first order near this boundary gives Airy's equation, whose solutions are not elementary functions.
This page explains definitions and properties of the Airy functions $\operatorname{Ai}$ and $\operatorname{Bi}$, the real- and complex-argument evaluation methods used by `scioxide-special`, scaled forms and derivatives, accuracy guarantees, and the API.
Bessel functions arising from wave equations in cylindrical coordinates are covered in [Cylindrical Bessel and Hankel functions](../airy-bessel-and-hankel/), where Airy functions approximate large-order turning points.

## Airy's equation and turning points

Consider the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi'' + V(x)\psi = E\psi$ near a classical turning point $x_0$ where $V(x_0) = E$.
Expanding to first order as $V(x) - E \approx F\,(x - x_0)$ and nondimensionalizing length by $(\hbar^2/(2mF))^{1/3}$ gives Airy's equation (DLMF 9.2.1).

$$
w''(z) = z\,w(z).
$$

$z < 0$ is the classically allowed region with oscillatory solutions; $z > 0$ is forbidden, with exponentially decaying or growing solutions.
A concrete application appears in [Neutron quantization in a gravitational field and Airy functions](../../examples/airy-turning-point/).

The two independent solutions $\operatorname{Ai}$ and $\operatorname{Bi}$ are determined by their values and slopes at the origin (DLMF §9.2).

$$
\operatorname{Ai}(0) = \frac{3^{-2/3}}{\Gamma(2/3)}, \quad
\operatorname{Ai}'(0) = -\frac{3^{-1/3}}{\Gamma(1/3)}, \quad
\operatorname{Bi}(0) = \frac{3^{-1/6}}{\Gamma(2/3)}, \quad
\operatorname{Bi}'(0) = \frac{3^{1/6}}{\Gamma(1/3)}.
$$

Both are entire functions, accepting all finite real and complex arguments with no branch cuts.
The Wronskian ($f g' - f' g$ for two solutions $f$ and $g$) is constant (DLMF 9.2.7).

$$
\mathscr{W}\{\operatorname{Ai}, \operatorname{Bi}\}(z) = \operatorname{Ai}(z)\operatorname{Bi}'(z) - \operatorname{Ai}'(z)\operatorname{Bi}(z) = \frac{1}{\pi}.
$$

Writing $\zeta = \frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}$ ($x > 0$), the leading asymptotic terms are as follows (DLMF 9.7.5, 9.7.7, 9.7.9, 9.7.11).

$$
\operatorname{Ai}(x) \sim \frac{e^{-\zeta}}{2\sqrt{\pi}\,x^{1/4}}, \qquad
\operatorname{Bi}(x) \sim \frac{e^{\zeta}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,x^{1/4}}, \qquad
\operatorname{Ai}(-x) \sim \frac{\cos(\zeta - \pi/4)}{\sqrt{\pi}\,x^{1/4}}, \qquad
\operatorname{Bi}(-x) \sim -\frac{\sin(\zeta - \pi/4)}{\sqrt{\pi}\,x^{1/4}}.
$$

Exterior solutions for bound states or tunneling must tend to 0 as $x \to +\infty$, so choose $\operatorname{Ai}$.

### Relations to Bessel functions

Airy functions can be expressed with Bessel functions of order $\pm 1/3$ (DLMF §9.6).
With $\zeta = \frac{2}{3}z^{3/2}$, the following relations hold, for example.

$$
\operatorname{Ai}(z) = \frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{z}{3}}\,K_{1/3}(\zeta), \qquad
\operatorname{Bi}(z) = \sqrt{\frac{z}{3}}\left[I_{-1/3}(\zeta) + I_{1/3}(\zeta)\right].
$$

On the negative side, $J_{\pm 1/3}$ and $Y_{\pm 1/3}$ replace $K$ and $I$.
`scioxide-special` uses these relations for complex arguments with moderate $\lvert z\rvert$.
Conversely, large-order Bessel functions near the turning point $x = \nu$ can be approximated by Airy functions (DLMF §10.20).

## Evaluation methods

### Evaluating real Airy functions

For real $\lvert x\rvert \le 16$, `airy_ai` and `airy_bi` use degree-35 Taylor polynomials centered at the nearest integer $m$.
The distance from the center is $\lvert x - m\rvert \le 1/2$, so terms decrease rapidly with degree.
Coefficients are precomputed by recurrence $(k+2)(k+1)c_{k+2} = m\,c_k + c_{k-1}$ from the differential equation $w'' = (m + h)\,w$ ($h = x - m$) and tabulated with the center values $c_0 = w(m)$ and $c_1 = w'(m)$.

The eight lowest-degree terms use compensated Horner evaluation with double-word arithmetic (about 106 bits represented by two `f64` values); the remaining higher-degree terms are summed in `f64`.
When the estimated roundoff error exceeds $\varepsilon/16$ times the value (where $\varepsilon$ is `f64` machine epsilon), namely near zeros of the function or derivative, evaluation is repeated with 16 compensated terms and then all terms.
The omitted term magnitude is estimated from above using coefficient ratios decreasing by about $(\lvert m\rvert + \lvert h\rvert)h^2/(36 \cdot 35)$ and added to the estimated error.

For $\lvert x\rvert > 16$, evaluation uses the asymptotic expansions in DLMF §9.7.
On the negative real axis, the oscillatory phase $\zeta + \pi/4$ is held in two words; for $\zeta > 1024$, $\zeta/(2\pi) = x^{3/2}/(3\pi)$ is computed with fixed-point $1/(3\pi)$ and reduced modulo $2\pi$, preventing phase roundoff from growing even for large $\lvert x\rvert$.

As $x \to +\infty$, $\operatorname{Ai}(x)$ becomes subnormal in `f64` from $x \approx 103.9$ and rounds to 0 at $x \approx 107.5$ (without an error).
$\operatorname{Bi}(x)$ overflows from $x \approx 104.5$, returning `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
In either case, scaled forms return finite values.

### Evaluating complex arguments

For complex $z$, the method depends on the region.
Use the first applicable row from the top of the table.

| Region | Method |
|---|---|
| On the real axis (zero imaginary part) | Same method as for real arguments |
| $\lvert\operatorname{Re}z\rvert \ge 4$ and $\lvert\operatorname{Im}z\rvert\sqrt{\lvert\operatorname{Re}z\rvert} \le 1/4$ | Taylor expansion in the imaginary direction from real-axis values and derivatives |
| $\lvert z\rvert > 32$ | Asymptotic expansions (DLMF §9.7), retaining only the exponential components that contribute to the value |
| $\lvert z\rvert \le 4$ | Maclaurin series at the origin (DLMF §9.4) |
| Otherwise | Relations through Bessel functions of orders $1/3$ and $2/3$ (DLMF §9.6) |

Near $\arg z = \pm\pi/3$, where zeros of $\operatorname{Bi}$ lie, the Bessel relations lose digits through cancellation.
Thus, for $\operatorname{Re}z > 0$, $\lvert z\rvert > 4$, and $\lvert\operatorname{Im}z/\operatorname{Re}z - \sqrt{3}\rvert < 0.6$, values are transported from the origin to $z$ in short Taylor steps along the differential equation.
$\lvert\operatorname{Ai}(z)\rvert$ grows exponentially for large $\lvert z\rvert$ in $\pi/3 < \lvert\arg z\rvert < \pi$ and returns `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`) when it exceeds the `f64` range.

## Scaled forms and derivatives

Scaled forms return values with exponentially varying factors removed.
For real $x$, `airy_ai_scaled` returns $e^{\zeta}\operatorname{Ai}(x)$ and `airy_bi_scaled` returns $e^{-\zeta}\operatorname{Bi}(x)$; no factor is applied for $x \le 0$.
For complex $z$, the factor for `airy_ai_scaled` is $e^{\zeta}$ ($\zeta = \frac{2}{3}z\sqrt{z}$) using the principal square root and is applied for every $z$.
On the negative real axis, a complex input therefore gives a result differing from the real result by the unit-modulus phase $e^{\zeta}$; the sign of the zero imaginary part selects the upper or lower side.
The complex factor for `airy_bi_scaled` is $e^{-\lvert\operatorname{Re}\zeta\rvert}$.

There is no standalone derivative function.
`derivative()` on results from the forms with options `airy_ai_with_options` and `airy_bi_with_options` returns $\operatorname{Ai}'$ and $\operatorname{Bi}'$.
Scaled forms with options return derivatives multiplied by the same factor, without differentiating the factor itself.
Values and derivatives are computed together from the same Taylor polynomial or expansion, so adding the derivative hardly increases work.

## Accuracy guarantees

A unit in the last place (ULP) is the spacing of floating-point numbers at the exact result.
Real `airy_ai`, `airy_bi`, `airy_ai_scaled`, and `airy_bi_scaled` are compared with independently generated high-precision references, targeting at most 64 ULP for `f64` and `f32`.
Reference data for `f64` span $x = -10^{300}$ through $x = 110$.
Near zeros, relative errors are not meaningful, so assessment uses absolute-error limits derived from condition numbers.
This target is verified over tested ranges, not established for every accepted argument.

Complex arguments have no uniform ULP contract.
Read estimated errors in forms with options (the error in `ValueWithError`, not an enclosure), and check identities such as the Wronskian $1/\pi$ if needed.

## API

Every function has a standard form returning only the value, a form with options returning `SpecialFunctionResult`, and a scoped API accepting a solve scope.
All four functions use `NumericScalar` and accept real (`f32`, `f64`) and complex inputs.
Work limits (`Budget`, not accuracy requirements) use `AiryOptions`, defaulting to 65536 units.

| Standard form | Form with options | Scoped API | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| `airy_ai` | `airy_ai_with_options` | `airy_ai_scoped` | $\operatorname{Ai}(z)$; the form with options also returns $\operatorname{Ai}'(z)$ |
| `airy_bi` | `airy_bi_with_options` | `airy_bi_scoped` | $\operatorname{Bi}(z)$; the form with options also returns $\operatorname{Bi}'(z)$ |
| `airy_ai_scaled` | `airy_ai_scaled_with_options` | `airy_ai_scaled_scoped` | $e^{\zeta}\operatorname{Ai}(z)$ (no factor for real $x \le 0$) |
| `airy_bi_scaled` | `airy_bi_scaled_with_options` | `airy_bi_scaled_scoped` | $e^{-\lvert\operatorname{Re}\zeta\rvert}\operatorname{Bi}(z)$ (no factor for real $x \le 0$) |

Non-finite inputs return `NonFinite`.
Unrepresentable unscaled values return `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
Evaluations stopped at the work limit return partial results with termination `IterationLimit` in forms with options, or `ConvergenceFailure` if no estimate exists.

## Airy functions across the turning point

The following cell moves the center $x_c$ of a width-12 observation window and plots the scaled Airy functions $e^{\zeta}\operatorname{Ai}(x)$ and $e^{-\zeta}\operatorname{Bi}(x)$ ($\zeta = \frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}$ for $x > 0$, with no scaling for $x \le 0$).
Unscaled $\operatorname{Bi}$ reaches $4.6 \times 10^8$ at $x = 10$, obscuring the oscillatory side, while scaled forms stay of order 1 throughout the window.
Dashed reference curves are the leading asymptotic terms $1/(2\sqrt{\pi}x^{1/4})$ and $1/(\sqrt{\pi}x^{1/4})$ for $x > 0$, computed with `f64` standard functions.
The table shows unscaled values and derivatives at the window center, error estimates from forms with options, and the difference between the Wronskian and $1/\pi$.
Derivatives come from forms with options, not finite differences.

```rust
//| id: special-airy-turning-window
//| ref: special-airy-turning-window
//| title: "Move the Airy observation window across the turning point"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   window_center: {"type":"range","label":"Observation-window center x_c [-]","description":"Move a length-12 window from the negative to the positive side.","min":-6,"max":4,"step":0.5,"value":-1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"forbidden","label":"x_c = 4","description":"At the forbidden-region center, Ai is small and Bi large, but the Wronskian remains 1/π.","inputs":{"window_center":4}}
//|   - {"id":"oscillatory","label":"x_c = −6","description":"The entire window lies on the oscillatory side, and the two curves oscillate with a π/2 phase difference.","inputs":{"window_center":-6}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"center","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.5355608832923521,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"center","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.10399738949694461,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"center","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-16}}
//|   - case: forbidden
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"center","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":9.515638512048008e-4,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"center","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":83.84707140846553,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"center","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: oscillatory
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"center","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-15}}
use scioxide_special::{
    AiryOptions, SpecialFunctionResult, airy_ai_scaled, airy_ai_with_options, airy_bi_scaled,
    airy_bi_with_options,
};

let left = window_center - 6.0;
let mut ai = Vec::new();
let mut bi = Vec::new();
let mut ai_theory = Vec::new();
let mut bi_theory = Vec::new();
for index in 0..=120 {
    let x = left + 12.0 * f64::from(index) / 120.0;
    ai.push([x, airy_ai_scaled(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
    bi.push([x, airy_bi_scaled(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?]);
    if x >= 0.5 {
        // Leading asymptotic terms for x > 0 (reference curves using f64 standard functions).
        let envelope = 1.0 / (std::f64::consts::PI.sqrt() * x.powf(0.25));
        ai_theory.push([x, 0.5 * envelope]);
        bi_theory.push([x, envelope]);
    }
}
let dashed = serde_json::json!({ "lineType": "dashed" });
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "e^ζ Ai(x)", "points": ai }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "e^−ζ Bi(x)", "points": bi }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "1/(2√π x^¼)", "points": ai_theory, "style": dashed }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "1/(√π x^¼)", "points": bi_theory, "style": dashed }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Dimensionless coordinate x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Scaled Airy functions [-]",
        "xMin": -12.0,
        "xMax": 10.0,
        "yMin": -0.8,
        "yMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": 0.0, "label": "Turning point x = 0" }]
    })
);

// Window center: unscaled values, derivatives and error estimates from forms with options, and Wronskian.
let options = AiryOptions::default();
let a = airy_ai_with_options(window_center, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let b = airy_bi_with_options(window_center, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let value = |r: &SpecialFunctionResult<f64>| r.value().map(|v| *v.value()).ok_or("Missing value");
let slope =
    |r: &SpecialFunctionResult<f64>| r.derivative().map(|v| *v.value()).ok_or("Missing derivative");
let error =
    |r: &SpecialFunctionResult<f64>| r.value().map(|v| v.absolute_error()).ok_or("Missing value");
let wronskian = value(&a)? * slope(&b)? - slope(&a)? * value(&b)?;
let sci6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Window center x_c [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Ai(x_c) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Bi(x_c) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Ai′(x_c) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Bi′(x_c) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Estimated Ai error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "|W − 1/π| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        window_center,
        value(&a)?,
        value(&b)?,
        slope(&a)?,
        slope(&b)?,
        error(&a)?,
        (wronskian - std::f64::consts::FRAC_1_PI).abs(),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "center" })
);
```

For $x < -2$, both curves oscillate under the same envelope $\lvert x\rvert^{-1/4}/\sqrt{\pi}$ with phase difference $\pi/2$.
For $x > 0$, scaled $\operatorname{Ai}$ and $\operatorname{Bi}$ approach their respective dashed curves; both differ by about 2 % at $x = 3$.
The difference from the leading term has the size of the next term $\mp 5/(72\zeta)$ and decreases as $x^{-3/2}$ with $x$.
If the Wronskian difference from $1/\pi$ substantially exceeds `f64` roundoff (about $10^{-16}$), either the values or derivatives have lost accuracy.
Values and derivatives come from the same expansions, however, so a small residual does not replace comparisons with reference values.

## Summary

Airy functions solve a linear turning-point problem; decaying $\operatorname{Ai}$ and growing $\operatorname{Bi}$ are linked by the Wronskian $1/\pi$.
Real $\lvert x\rvert \le 16$ uses degree-35 Taylor polynomials centered at integers, with accurately phase-reduced asymptotics outside that range, targeting 64 ULP over tested ranges.
On the positive side with $x$ above 104, $\operatorname{Bi}$ overflows and $\operatorname{Ai}$ becomes subnormal, so compare with `airy_ai_scaled` and `airy_bi_scaled`.
Derivatives are obtained with values through `derivative()` of forms with options; complex arguments have no uniform ULP contract, so read estimated errors.
Check energy levels obtained from zeros in [Neutron quantization in a gravitational field and Airy functions](../../examples/airy-turning-point/), and large-order Bessel approximations with Airy functions in [Cylindrical Bessel and Hankel functions](../airy-bessel-and-hankel/).
