Incomplete elliptic integrals depend on two variables, amplitude $\phi$ and parameter $m$, and require many case distinctions, such as $\phi$ exceeding $\pi/2$ or $m$ approaching 1.
Carlson's symmetric forms express all elliptic integrals through a small set of integrals symmetric under interchange of their arguments, allowing evaluation by a single iteration, the duplication transformation.
This page explains the definitions of Carlson symmetric forms, reduction of Legendre integrals to these forms, the duplication algorithm and its truncation, domains and Cauchy principal values, accuracy, and calling forms.
[Definitions of elliptic integrals and complete elliptic integrals K and E](../elliptic-and-legendre/) covers the definitions, the parameter convention $m = k^2$, and evaluation of complete elliptic integrals $K$ and $E$.

## Carlson symmetric forms

Carlson introduced the following integrals, symmetric under interchange of their arguments (DLMF §19.16).

$$
R_F(x, y, z) = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{\sqrt{(t + x)(t + y)(t + z)}}, \qquad
R_J(x, y, z, p) = \frac{3}{2}\int_0^\infty \frac{dt}{(t + p)\sqrt{(t + x)(t + y)(t + z)}}
$$


$R_D(x, y, z) = R_J(x, y, z, z)$ and $R_C(x, y) = R_F(x, y, y)$ are degenerate forms; $R_C$ is elementary ($\arccos(\sqrt{x/y})/\sqrt{y - x}$ for $0 \le x < y$).
$R_G(x, y, z)$ is the completely symmetric integral of the second kind; choosing $z$ as the median of the three arguments gives the following identity (DLMF §19.21).

$$
2 R_G(x, y, z) = z R_F(x, y, z) - \frac{(x - z)(y - z)}{3} R_D(x, y, z) + \sqrt{\frac{xy}{z}}
$$


Putting the median in $z$ gives $(x - z)(y - z) \le 0$, making all three terms nonnegative and avoiding cancellation.
$R_F$ and $R_C$ are homogeneous of degree $-1/2$, $R_D$ and $R_J$ of degree $-3/2$, and $R_G$ of degree $1/2$.
`carlson_rf`, `carlson_rd`, `carlson_rj`, `carlson_rc`, and `carlson_rg` return these integrals.

## Carlson forms of Legendre integrals

With $s = \sin\phi$, $c = \cos\phi$, and $\Delta^2 = 1 - m s^2$, Legendre integrals have the following forms (DLMF §19.25).

$$
F(\phi \mid m) = s R_F(c^2, \Delta^2, 1), \qquad
E(\phi \mid m) = s R_F(c^2, \Delta^2, 1) - \frac{m}{3} s^3 R_D(c^2, \Delta^2, 1),
$$

$$
\Pi(n \mid m) = R_F(0, 1 - m, 1) + \frac{n}{3} R_J(0, 1 - m, 1, 1 - n), \qquad
K(m) = R_F(0, 1 - m, 1), \qquad E(m) = 2 R_G(0, 1 - m, 1).
$$

No function for the incomplete elliptic integral of the third kind is provided, but for $|\phi| \le \pi/2$ it can be assembled with the same notation as

$$
\Pi(n; \phi \mid m) = s R_F(c^2, \Delta^2, 1) + \frac{n}{3} s^3 R_J(c^2, \Delta^2, 1, 1 - n s^2)
$$

using `carlson_rf` and `carlson_rj`.

`elliptic_f` and `elliptic_e_inc` first reduce the amplitude to $[0, \pi/2]$ using its sign and the relation $F(\phi + j\pi \mid m) = F(\phi \mid m) + 2jK(m)$.
The complete elliptic integral terms added during reduction use the fixed-work approximations in [Evaluation of complete elliptic integrals K and E](oxref:special-elliptic-k-e-evaluation).
Evaluate the formulas above at the reduced amplitude; $E$ shares the duplication state of $R_F$ with $R_D$.
`elliptic_pi` first uses the closed forms $\Pi(0 \mid m) = K(m)$, $\Pi(m \mid m) = E(m)/(1 - m)$, and $\Pi(n \mid 0) = \pi/(2\sqrt{1 - n})$; otherwise, it evaluates $R_F$ and $R_J$ with a shared duplication state.

## Duplication transformation

The duplication theorem states the following identity with $\lambda = \sqrt{x}\sqrt{y} + \sqrt{x}\sqrt{z} + \sqrt{y}\sqrt{z}$ (DLMF §19.26).

$$
R_F(x, y, z) = R_F\!\left(\frac{x + \lambda}{4}, \frac{y + \lambda}{4}, \frac{z + \lambda}{4}\right)
$$


Each transformation reduces the relative spread about the argument mean by about a factor of 4, and the arguments converge linearly to a common value.
When the maximum spread $\delta$ (each argument's difference from the mean, divided by the mean) is small enough, terminate with the following symmetric-polynomial expansion through degree 5 (Carlson 1995, DLMF §19.36(i)).

$$
R_F \approx A^{-1/2}\left(1 - \frac{E_2}{10} + \frac{E_3}{14} + \frac{E_2^2}{24} - \frac{3 E_2 E_3}{44}\right)
$$


$A$ is the argument mean, and $E_2$ and $E_3$ are the elementary symmetric polynomials of degrees 2 and 3 in the normalized deviations.
$R_D$ and $R_J$ accumulate each step's contribution ($4^{-k}/(\sqrt{z_k}(z_k + \lambda_k))$ for $R_D$) with compensated summation; $R_J$ performs one nested evaluation of $R_C$ per step.

For real arguments whose nonzero values all lie within $2^{\pm 300}$, repeat the transformation in binary64 arithmetic until $\delta < 0.001$.
The omitted sixth-degree terms are of order $\delta^6 < 10^{-18}$, below binary64 rounding.
Starting with $\delta$ of order 1 reaches the threshold in $\log_4 1000 \approx 5$ steps; even $R_F(0, 0.5, 1)$, which includes a zero argument, stops in 7 steps ([Complete elliptic integral endpoint experiment](oxref:special-elliptic-endpoint-experiment)).
binary32 arguments are also widened to binary64 for the same transformation.
Other real and complex arguments use the same transformation in complex arithmetic, switching to arithmetic with an extended exponent range when a component is outside $2^{\pm 100}$ or outside the right half-plane.
The relative truncation target in that case is $10^{-12}$.
One transformation step (including nested $R_C$ steps for $R_J$) consumes one unit of the `EllipticOptions` work budget.

### Domains, poles, and principal values

For real types, negative arguments of $R_F$, $R_D$, and $R_G$, negative $x$, $y$, or $z$ in $R_J$, and negative $x$ in $R_C$ give `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
Poles occur when at least two arguments of $R_F$ are 0; when $z = 0$ or $x = y = 0$ for $R_D$; when $p = 0$ or at least two of $x$, $y$, and $z$ are 0 for $R_J$; and when $y = 0$ for $R_C$; they give `DomainError` (`Pole`).
$y < 0$ in $R_C(x, y)$ and $p < 0$ in $R_J(x, y, z, p)$ place a pole on the integration path and return the Cauchy principal value (DLMF §19.20).
The principal value of $R_C(0, y)$ ($y < 0$) is 0.
All arguments of $R_G$ may be 0, with $R_G(0, 0, 0) = 0$ and $R_G(0, 0, z) = \sqrt{z}/2$.
Complex arguments use the principal square root of each factor; on the negative real axis, signed zero in the imaginary part selects the side of the cut.
For complex $R_J$, signed zeros selecting opposite sides for arguments on the negative real axis give `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
For complex $R_J$ outside the right half-plane, shift the integration path into the right half-plane and add the contribution of the shifted segment by quadrature.

## Error, stability, and computational cost

The duplication transformation's estimated error uses a rounding model proportional to the number of steps (confidence `ModelBased`).
For binary64 $R_F$, with $N$ steps and final spread $\delta$, the relative error estimate is $8(N + 1)\varepsilon + 4\delta^6$ ($\varepsilon$ is binary64 machine epsilon).
The binary64 relative error estimate for `elliptic_f`, `elliptic_e_inc`, and `elliptic_pi` is $12\varepsilon + 8(N + 1)\varepsilon$.
The estimated error is not an enclosure.
The number of steps depends logarithmically on the argument spread; for real arguments it ranges from a few to a few dozen, far below the default `EllipticOptions` budget of 65536 units.
Use the budget to count work in a parent solve through a scoped API or to stop a calculation early.

When $\phi$ exceeds $\pi/2$, amplitude reduction subtracts only a multiple of $\pi$; the complete elliptic integral terms have fixed work, so the number of steps does not increase.
Amplification of rounding in $m$ when $m$ is close to 1 is the conditioning issue explained in [Error, stability, and computational cost](oxref:special-elliptic-error-cost); when $1 - m$ can be formed accurately, passing it directly to a Carlson form avoids this issue.

## Accuracy guarantees

Accuracy over the range tested against independently generated high-precision reference data is as follows.

| Function (real arguments) | binary64 | binary32 | Test findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| `carlson_rf`, `carlson_rd`, `carlson_rj` | 64 ULP | 64 ULP | Maximum measured binary64 error: 4.8 ULP |
| `elliptic_f`, `elliptic_e_inc` | 64 ULP | 64 ULP | Maximum measured binary64 error: 5.7 ULP |
| `elliptic_pi`, `carlson_rc`, `carlson_rg` | 64 ULP | 64 ULP | Target: 64 ULP |

Complex arguments and paths with extended exponent range have no uniform ULP specification; use the estimated error in the options form.
The reference data also check whether estimated errors cover actual errors for complex arguments, including neighborhoods of corners where cancellation occurs in the complete third-kind integral.

## Functions and calling forms

All functions accept `NumericScalar` (`f32`, `f64`, `Complex<f32>`, `Complex<f64>`), using the same names for real and complex arguments.
Standard forms return only the value; options forms `*_with_options(.., options)` return `SpecialFunctionResult`, and scoped APIs `*_scoped(.., options, scope)` return the same result within a solve scope.

| Family | Standard form | Options form | Scoped API | Domain (real types) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incomplete elliptic integrals | `elliptic_f(phi, m)`, `elliptic_e_inc(phi, m)` | `elliptic_f_with_options`, `elliptic_e_inc_with_options` | `elliptic_f_scoped`, `elliptic_e_inc_scoped` | $m \le 1$; $\phi$ is finite and real |
| Complete elliptic integral of the third kind | `elliptic_pi(n, m)` | `elliptic_pi_with_options` | `elliptic_pi_scoped` | $n \le 1$, $m \le 1$ |
| Carlson symmetric forms | `carlson_rf(x, y, z)`, `carlson_rd(x, y, z)`, `carlson_rj(x, y, z, p)`, `carlson_rc(x, y)`, `carlson_rg(x, y, z)` | `carlson_rf_with_options`, `carlson_rd_with_options`, `carlson_rj_with_options`, `carlson_rc_with_options`, `carlson_rg_with_options` | `carlson_rf_scoped`, `carlson_rd_scoped`, `carlson_rj_scoped`, `carlson_rc_scoped`, `carlson_rg_scoped` | See above |

Options forms and scoped APIs accept `EllipticOptions`.
`EllipticOptions` contains only a work budget (`Budget`), defaulting to 65536 units and changed with `EllipticOptions::new(Budget::try_from(n)?)`.
It accepts no accuracy request; increasing the budget does not change the result's accuracy.
On reaching the budget, both standard and options forms return `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`) without an intermediate value.
Options results contain an estimated error, termination status (`SpecialFunctionTermination`), work counts, and algorithm identifier.
The algorithm identifier records the path used (`real_carlson_rf` is binary64 duplication; `period_reduction` is an incomplete elliptic integral with amplitude reduction).
The parameters of `elliptic_f` and `elliptic_e_inc` may remain real when the amplitude is complex (`m: impl Parameter<T>`).
Use the same type for $n$ and $m$ in `elliptic_pi` and for the arguments of Carlson forms.

//| id: special-carlson-detail-rows
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Collect options results and errors into table rows"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SciOxideErrorView};
use scioxide_special::SpecialFunctionResult;

/// One table row: call, value, estimated error, confidence, termination status or error, work, algorithm.
pub(crate) type DetailRow = (
    String,
    Option<f64>,
    Option<f64>,
    String,
    String,
    Option<usize>,
    String,
);

/// Describe an error with the classification name from kind() and details from view().
pub(crate) fn error_label(error: &SciOxideError) -> String {
    match error.view() {
        SciOxideErrorView::DomainError { reason, .. } => format!("DomainError（{reason:?}）"),
        SciOxideErrorView::InvalidInput { reason, .. } => format!("InvalidInput（{reason:?}）"),
        SciOxideErrorView::ConvergenceFailure { reason, .. } => {
            format!("ConvergenceFailure（{reason:?}）")
        }
        SciOxideErrorView::NumericalInstability { issue, .. } => {
            format!("NumericalInstability（{issue:?}）")
        }
        _ => format!("{:?}", error.kind()),
    }
}

/// Turn an options result or error into one row. Leave the value empty for infinite limits and describe them in the termination-status column.
pub(crate) fn detail_row(
    call: &str,
    result: Result<SpecialFunctionResult<f64>, SciOxideError>,
) -> DetailRow {
    let result = match result {
        Ok(result) => result,
        Err(error) => {
            let label = error_label(&error);
            return (
                call.into(),
                None,
                None,
                String::new(),
                label,
                None,
                String::new(),
            );
        }
    };
    let estimate = result.value();
    let value = estimate.map(|v| *v.value());
    let status = match value {
        Some(v) if v.is_infinite() => format!("{:?}（+∞）", result.termination()),
        _ => format!("{:?}", result.termination()),
    };
    (
        call.into(),
        value.filter(|v| v.is_finite()),
        estimate
            .map(|v| v.absolute_error())
            .filter(|e| e.is_finite()),
        estimate
            .map(|v| format!("{:?}", v.reliability()))
            .unwrap_or_default(),
        status,
        Some(result.counts().iterations()),
        result.algorithm().to_string(),
    )
}

/// Columns for the options-form table.
pub(crate) fn detail_columns() -> serde_json::Value {
    let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
    let digits16 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 16 });
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Call" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": digits16, "nullText": "—" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Estimated error", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Confidence" },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Termination or error" },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Work", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },
        { "key": "6", "label": "Algorithm" }
    ])
}

## Summary and further reading

Incomplete elliptic integrals $F$ and $E$ accept every finite real amplitude, reduce it to $[0, \pi/2]$, and evaluate it using Carlson forms such as $s R_F(c^2, \Delta^2, 1)$.
The complete third-kind integral combines $R_F$ and $R_J$; no incomplete third-kind function is provided, so assemble it from the same Carlson forms.
Duplication reduces argument spread by about a factor of 4 per step and stops within a few to a few dozen steps for real arguments; the estimated error uses a rounding model proportional to the step count (`ModelBased`).
$y < 0$ in $R_C$ and $p < 0$ in $R_J$ return Cauchy principal values; poles give `DomainError` (`Pole`), and reaching the work budget gives `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).
See [Jacobi elliptic functions](../jacobi-elliptic-functions/) for elliptic-integral inverses, [Definitions of elliptic integrals and complete elliptic integrals K and E](../elliptic-and-legendre/) for fixed-work complete-integral approximations, and [Elliptic integral APIs](../../api/elliptic/) for individual specifications.
