Factorials and binomial coefficients occur in counting, probability-distribution normalization constants, statistical likelihoods, and series coefficients.
Both are integers, but they grow faster than exponentially with their arguments and soon exceed both the range of exactly representable binary64 integers (up to $2^{53} \approx 9.0 \times 10^{15}$) and the binary64 maximum $1.80 \times 10^{308}$.
`scioxide-special` returns floating-point values and provides logarithmic functions (`ln_*`) and logarithmic representations in options results for values that overflow.
For exact integers, use `checked_factorial` and `checked_binomial` in `scioxide-core`.
This page explains each function's definition, the boundary between exact and approximate values, method selection, accuracy guarantees, and API choices.
[Stirling, Bernoulli, and Euler numbers](../stirling-bernoulli-euler-numbers/) covers those three number sequences.

## Factorials and multifactorials

The factorial of a nonnegative integer $n$ is $n! = 1 \cdot 2 \cdots n = \Gamma(n+1)$, with $0! = 1$ (DLMF 5.4.1).
The multifactorial with step $k \ge 1$ is the product of positive factors obtained by decrementing $n$ by $k$.

$$
n!^{(k)} = n\,(n-k)\,(n-2k)\cdots r, \qquad m = \left\lceil \frac{n}{k} \right\rceil, \quad r = n - (m-1)k
$$

$m$ is the number of factors, and $r \in [1, k]$ is the smallest factor.
$k = 1$ gives the factorial, $k = 2$ gives the double factorial $n!! = n(n-2)(n-4)\cdots$, and the empty product at $n = 0$ returns 1.
The endpoint values satisfy $0!! = 1!! = 1$.
`multifactorial(n, 0)` gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).

For many factors, the multifactorial can be expressed using gamma functions.
Factoring out $k$ gives $n!^{(k)} = k^m \cdot (r/k)(r/k + 1)\cdots(r/k + m - 1)$; the remaining product is the Pochhammer symbol $(r/k)_m$, giving the following gamma-function form.

$$
n!^{(k)} = k^m \frac{\Gamma(m + r/k)}{\Gamma(r/k)}
$$


For $n \le 20$, `factorial` returns the factorial from an exact table.
Although $20! \approx 2.43 \times 10^{18}$ exceeds $2^{53}$, its many prime factors of 2 make it exactly representable in binary64.
For $21 \le n \le 128$, form the product in double-word arithmetic (two floating-point words with about 106 significand bits) and round once; for $n > 128$, evaluate $\ln\Gamma(n+1)$ in double-word arithmetic and exponentiate.
$n!$ gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`) for binary64 $n > 170$ and binary32 $n > 34$.
`double_factorial` and `multifactorial` use a double-word product for at most 128 factors and logarithmic evaluation of the gamma-function form above for more factors.
Values exceeding the range give `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).

The logarithmic forms `ln_factorial`, `ln_double_factorial`, and `ln_multifactorial` do not overflow for any $n$.
`ln_factorial(n)` evaluates $\ln\Gamma(n+1)$ in double-word arithmetic and returns 0 for $n \le 1$.
For the integer factorial itself, `checked_factorial` in `scioxide-core` returns $n!$ as an exact `usize` integer and gives `SizeOverflow` when it exceeds `usize`.

## Binomial coefficients

The integer binomial coefficient is $\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}$ ($0 \le k \le n$, DLMF 26.3.1); `binomial(n, k)` rejects $k > n$ with `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
It does not return 0.
The endpoint values are $\binom{n}{0} = \binom{n}{n} = 1$; symmetry $\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n}{n-k}$ reduces evaluation to $k' = \min(k, n-k)$.

For $k' < 128$, form the following product in double-word arithmetic and round once.

$$
\binom{n}{k} = \prod_{i=1}^{k'} \frac{n - k' + i}{i}
$$


Intermediate values are always integer binomial coefficients, avoiding overflow from forming large intermediate factorials.
For $k' \ge 128$ or if the product overflows, use the logarithm of the beta function.

$$
\binom{n}{k} = \frac{1}{(n+1)\,B(n-k+1,\ k+1)}
$$

The result is rounded to floating point, so coefficients above $2^{53}$ are generally not exact integers.
Values beyond the range give `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`); `ln_binomial` returns $\ln\binom{n}{k}$ without forming the coefficient.
For an exact integer, use `checked_binomial` in `scioxide-core`.
`checked_binomial` gives `SizeOverflow` on exceeding `usize`; unlike `binomial`, it returns 0 rather than an error for $k > n$.
`generalized_binomial` computes $\binom{z}{k} = z(z-1)\cdots(z-k+1)/k!$ for a real or complex upper argument $z$; [APIs, branches, and value representations](oxref:special-entry-exact-integers) explains branches and representations.

The next cell tabulates the boundaries between factorial tables, double-word products, and $\ln\Gamma$, and the method switches for binomial coefficients and multifactorials.
For an independent check, it computes factorials for $n \le 34$ exactly with `u128` integers and reports differences from `factorial` in ULP of the result.
Logarithmic forms are compared with manual sums of factor logarithms $\sum \ln(\text{factor})$.
Rounding error in the manual sum accumulates with the number of terms, so differences of about $10^{-13}$ are treated as agreement.

```rust
//| id: special-combinatorial-factorial-range
//| ref: special-combinatorial-factorial-range
//| title: "Compare factorial, binomial, and multifactorial method switches with exact integers"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"factorials","path":"/rows/3/4","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"factorials","path":"/rows/7/4","range":{"max":4.5e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"factorials","path":"/rows/12/1","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"factorials","path":"/rows/12/7","equals":"NumericalInstability"}
//|       - {"artifact":"factorials","path":"/rows/13/5","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ratios","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ratios","path":"/rows/5/1","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"ratios","path":"/rows/9/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_special::{
    CombinatorialOptions, binomial, binomial_with_options, factorial, factorial_with_options,
    ln_binomial, ln_factorial, ln_multifactorial, multifactorial, multifactorial_with_options,
};

let sci4 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 4 });
let sig10 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let options = CombinatorialOptions::default();

let mut factorial_rows = Vec::new();
for n in [
    0_usize, 5, 18, 20, 21, 22, 25, 34, 100, 128, 129, 170, 171, 1000,
] {
    let value = factorial::<f64>(n);
    let method = factorial_with_options::<f64>(n, options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .algorithm()
        .to_string();
    // Independent check 1: multiply n ≤ 34 factorials exactly in u128 (34! < 2^128).
    let exact = (n <= 34).then(|| (1..=n as u128).product::<u128>() as f64);
    let exact_error = match (&value, exact) {
        (Ok(v), Some(e)) => Some((v - e).abs() / e),
        _ => None,
    };
    // Independent check 2: form ln n! by summing factor logarithms (reference using f64 intrinsic methods).
    let log_sum: f64 = (2..=n).map(|k| (k as f64).ln()).sum();
    let log_value = ln_factorial::<f64>(n).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let kind = match &value {
        Ok(_) => "None".to_string(),
        Err(e) => format!("{:?}", e.kind()),
    };
    factorial_rows.push((
        n,
        value.ok(),
        exact,
        log_value,
        exact_error,
        (log_value - log_sum).abs() / log_sum.max(1.0),
        method,
        kind,
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "factorial(n)", "type": "number", "format": sig10, "nullText": "Error" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Exact n! (u128)", "type": "number", "format": sig10, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "ln_factorial(n)", "type": "number", "format": sig10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative difference from exact value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative difference from Σ ln k [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Error classification" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    factorial_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "factorials" })
);

// (name, value, log value, sum of factor logarithms, method). The factor-logarithm sum is a reference using f64 intrinsic methods.
let mut ratio_rows = Vec::new();
for (n, k) in [
    (10_usize, 3_usize),
    (60, 30),
    (1000, 127),
    (1000, 128),
    (1000, 500),
    (1100, 550),
] {
    let value = binomial::<f64>(n, k);
    let method = binomial_with_options::<f64>(n, k, options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .algorithm()
        .to_string();
    let log_value = ln_binomial::<f64>(n, k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let log_sum: f64 = (1..=k)
        .map(|i| (((n - k + i) as f64) / i as f64).ln())
        .sum();
    ratio_rows.push((
        format!("C({n}, {k})"),
        value.ok(),
        log_value,
        log_sum,
        method,
    ));
}
for (n, step) in [(10_usize, 3_usize), (256, 2), (257, 2), (3000, 7)] {
    let value = multifactorial::<f64>(n, step);
    let method = multifactorial_with_options::<f64>(n, step, options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .algorithm()
        .to_string();
    let log_value = ln_multifactorial::<f64>(n, step).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let log_sum: f64 = (0..n.div_ceil(step))
        .map(|i| ((n - i * step) as f64).ln())
        .sum();
    ratio_rows.push((
        format!("{n}!^({step})"),
        value.ok(),
        log_value,
        log_sum,
        method,
    ));
}
let rows: Vec<_> = ratio_rows
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, value, log_value, log_sum, method)| {
        let difference = (log_value - log_sum).abs() / log_sum.abs().max(1.0);
        (name, value, log_value, log_sum, difference, method)
    })
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": sig10, "nullText": "Overflow" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Logarithmic value", "type": "number", "format": sig10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Sum of factor logarithms", "type": "number", "format": sig10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Method" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ratios" })
);
```

In the first table, $n \le 20$ uses the table (`combinatorial/factorial_table@1`), $21 \le n \le 128$ uses a double-word product (`multifactorial_product`), and $n \ge 129$ uses $\ln\Gamma$ (`multifactorial_gamma`).
For the $n \le 34$ rows, all relative differences from the rounded exact integer are 0; values from $21!$ onward, exceeding $2^{53}$, are also correctly rounded.
For $n = 171$ and $1000$, the standard form returns `NumericalInstability`, whereas `ln_factorial` returns $\ln 1000! \approx 5912.13$.
The relative difference from the sum of factor logarithms is $1.4 \times 10^{-15}$, at the scale of rounding in the manual sum of 1000 terms.

In the second table, the binomial method switches from a double-word product (`binomial_twofold`) to the beta-function logarithm (`binomial_combined_gamma`) at the boundary from $\min(k, n-k) = 127$ to $128$.
$\binom{1100}{550} \approx 10^{329.5}$ exceeds the binary64 range, displaying Overflow in the value column; only the logarithmic form returns $758.73$.
The multifactorial also switches from a product to the gamma-function form at the boundary from $256!!$ with 128 factors to $257!!$ with 129 factors.
In every row, the relative difference between the logarithmic form and the sum of factor logarithms is at most $2 \times 10^{-15}$.

## Accuracy guarantees

The following ULP targets are checked against independently generated high-precision reference data.

| Function | binary64 | binary32 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `factorial`, `ln_factorial`, `binomial` | 4 ULP | 1 ULP | $n! \le 20!$ is exact in binary64 |
| `ln_binomial`, `double_factorial`, `ln_double_factorial`, `multifactorial`, `ln_multifactorial`, `generalized_binomial` | No uniform specification | No uniform specification | Tested with independent references and identities; options forms return estimated errors |

Even without a uniform specification, the options form's estimated error (an error for `ValueWithError`, not an enclosure) can be checked over the intended range using independent checks like the cell above.
The Pochhammer symbol also has no uniform specification ([Gamma functions, polygamma functions, and the Pochhammer symbol](oxref:special-gamma-pochhammer)).

## API

| Function | Standard-form arguments | Options form | Scoped API | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `factorial` | `n` | `factorial_with_options` | None | $n!$ |
| `ln_factorial` | `n` | `ln_factorial_with_options` | None | $\ln n!$ |
| `double_factorial` | `n` | `double_factorial_with_options` | None | $n!!$ |
| `ln_double_factorial` | `n` | `ln_double_factorial_with_options` | None | $\ln n!!$ |
| `multifactorial` | `n, step` | `multifactorial_with_options` | None | $n!^{(k)}$ |
| `ln_multifactorial` | `n, step` | `ln_multifactorial_with_options` | None | $\ln n!^{(k)}$ |
| `binomial` | `n, k` | `binomial_with_options` | None | $\binom{n}{k}$ |
| `ln_binomial` | `n, k` | `ln_binomial_with_options` | None | $\ln\binom{n}{k}$ |
| `generalized_binomial` | `z, k` | `generalized_binomial_with_options` | `generalized_binomial_scoped` | $\binom{z}{k}$ (real or complex $z$) |

Arguments `n`, `k`, and `step` are all `usize`; floating-point functions select result type `S: RealScalar` through a type argument, as in `factorial::<f64>(n)` (only `generalized_binomial` derives it from the type of argument `z`).
Options forms accept `CombinatorialOptions` and return `SpecialFunctionResult` containing the ordinary value and estimated error, scaled value, logarithmic form, termination status, algorithm identifier, and work counts.
Even on overflow, options forms do not return an error; projection to an ordinary value has status `ScalarRepresentation::Overflow`, and the logarithmic form remains available.
`CombinatorialOptions` contains only a work budget (`Budget`), defaulting to 65 536 units and changed with `CombinatorialOptions::new(budget)` or `with_iteration_budget`.
The same options are used by options forms of `pochhammer`, `pochhammer_relative`, Stirling numbers, Bernoulli numbers, and Euler numbers.
Of this page's functions, only `generalized_binomial`, whose work grows with $k$, has a scoped API; it returns the same result as the options form and reports work to the parent execution context.

## Summary and further reading

Factorials give exact binary64 values for $n \le 20$ and rounded values for $n \le 170$; for larger $n$ or ratio calculations, use `ln_factorial`, `ln_binomial`, `ln_multifactorial`, or the logarithmic representation in an options result.
Binomial coefficients use double-word products for $k' = \min(k, n-k) < 128$ and beta-function logarithms beyond that; coefficients above $2^{53}$ are rounded values.
Since `binomial` returns `InvalidInput` rather than 0 for $k > n$, callers must restrict summation ranges to $k \le n$.
For exact integers, use `checked_factorial` and `checked_binomial` in `scioxide-core`.
See [Stirling, Bernoulli, and Euler numbers](../stirling-bernoulli-euler-numbers/) for those sequences, [Gamma functions, polygamma functions, and the Pochhammer symbol](../gamma-family/) for real gamma functions and Pochhammer symbols, and [APIs, branches, and value representations](oxref:special-entry-exact-integers) for the real and complex extension `generalized_binomial` of binomial coefficients.
