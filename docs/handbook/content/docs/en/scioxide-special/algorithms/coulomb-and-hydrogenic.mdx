In scattering between charged particles, the potential $Z_1Z_2e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 r)$ decreases only as $1/r$, so the wave phase continues to shift even far away.
Coulomb wave functions, solutions of the Coulomb radial equation, therefore replace free-particle spherical Bessel functions.
Negative-energy solutions of the same potential are the bound states of hydrogen-like atoms.

This page covers the regular Coulomb wave function $F_l(\eta, \rho)$, the irregular function $G_l(\eta, \rho)$, their derivatives and normalization $C_l(\eta)$, and the hydrogenic radial function $R_{nl}(r)$.
After definitions and properties, it explains the methods `scioxide-special` uses in each radial region (Steed's method, outgoing asymptotic expansions, and continuation inside the turning point), order-recurrence sequences, accuracy guarantees, and APIs; the final two cells check the Wronskian and spherical Bessel limit.
Applications appear in [Hydrogenic radial wave functions and expectation values](../../examples/hydrogen-radial-states/) and [Coulomb barrier penetration and the Gamow factor](../../examples/coulomb-barrier-penetration/).

## Coulomb wave equation and solution conventions

Decompose relative motion with reduced mass $\mu$ and energy $E = \hbar^2k^2/(2\mu) > 0$ into partial waves of orbital angular momentum $l$.
With dimensionless radius $\rho = kr$, the radial function $w(\rho)$ satisfies the Coulomb wave equation (DLMF 33.2.1).

$$
w'' + \left(1 - \frac{2\eta}{\rho} - \frac{l(l+1)}{\rho^2}\right)w = 0, \qquad
\eta = \frac{Z_1Z_2e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar v} = \frac{Z_1Z_2\,\alpha c}{v}.
$$

$\eta$ is the Sommerfeld parameter, $v$ is the relative speed, and $\alpha$ is the fine-structure constant.
Repulsion ($Z_1Z_2 > 0$) gives $\eta > 0$ and attraction gives $\eta < 0$; as energy decreases, $\lvert\eta\rvert$ grows as $1/\sqrt{E}$.
In `scioxide-special`, pass $\rho$ as `z`, $l$ as `l` (real), and $\eta$ as `eta`.

The regular solution $F_l$ behaves as $F_l(\eta, \rho) \sim C_l(\eta)\,\rho^{l+1}$ at the origin, with the following normalization constant (DLMF 33.2.5).

$$
C_l(\eta) = \frac{2^l e^{-\pi\eta/2}\,\lvert\Gamma(l + 1 + i\eta)\rvert}{\Gamma(2l + 2)}, \qquad
C_0(\eta)^2 = \frac{2\pi\eta}{e^{2\pi\eta} - 1}.
$$

$C_0(\eta)^2$ is called the Gamow–Sommerfeld factor and becomes $2\pi\eta\,e^{-2\pi\eta}$ for $\eta \gg 1$.
It represents the probability of penetrating the repulsive Coulomb barrier to the origin and governs low-energy nuclear reaction rates.

Far away, the two solutions approach phase-shifted sinusoidal waves (DLMF 33.2.10, 33.10.1).

$$
F_l \sim \sin\theta_l, \quad G_l \sim \cos\theta_l, \qquad
\theta_l = \rho - \eta\ln(2\rho) - \frac{l\pi}{2} + \sigma_l(\eta), \quad
\sigma_l(\eta) = \arg\Gamma(l + 1 + i\eta).
$$

The phase term $-\eta\ln(2\rho)$ reflects the long-range potential, and $\sigma_l$ is the Coulomb phase shift.
The irregular solution $G_l$ is fixed by this asymptotic form and diverges as $\rho^{-l}$ at the origin.
Outgoing and incoming waves are $H_l^\pm = G_l \pm iF_l$ (DLMF 33.2.7); the Wronskian ($fg' - f'g$ for solutions $f$ and $g$) has the following constant value (DLMF 33.2.12).

$$
F_l'G_l - F_lG_l' = 1.
$$

At $\eta = 0$, the Coulomb potential vanishes and the solutions can be expressed using spherical Bessel functions.
They satisfy $F_l(0, \rho) = \rho\,j_l(\rho)$ and $G_l(0, \rho) = -\rho\,y_l(\rho)$; at $l = 0$, these become $F_0 = \sin\rho$ and $G_0 = \cos\rho$.

### Turning point and solution magnitudes

The classical turning point is the following zero of the equation's coefficient $1 - 2\eta/\rho - l(l+1)/\rho^2$ (DLMF 33.2.2).

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{tp}} = \eta + \sqrt{\eta^2 + l(l+1)}
$$


For $\rho > \rho_{\mathrm{tp}}$, the solutions oscillate, with $F_l$ and $G_l$ of comparable magnitude.
Inside the barrier, where $\rho < \rho_{\mathrm{tp}}$, $F_l$ decreases exponentially toward the origin while $G_l$ grows exponentially.
At $\eta = 20$, $l = 2$, and $\rho = 0.1$, the values are $F_2 \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-28}$ and $G_2 \approx 1.1 \times 10^{26}$, with their product kept of order 1 by the Wronskian.
This difference in magnitude determines which continuation direction avoids error growth.

## Evaluation at positive real radii

For real $\rho > 0$, choose methods in this order.
Internal calculations use double-word arithmetic (about 106 bits represented by two `f64` values), storing values as significand × $e^{\text{scale}}$.

1. At $l = \eta = 0$, return $F_0 = \sin\rho$ and $G_0 = \cos\rho$ directly.
2. For $\rho \ge 32$, try the outgoing-wave asymptotic expansion (DLMF 33.11.1) $H_l^+ \sim e^{i\theta_l}\sum_n (l + 1 + i\eta)_n(-l + i\eta)_n/(n!\,(2i\rho)^n)$

   Terms initially grow by about $(l^2 + \eta^2)/(2n\rho)$ per step; accept the expansion only when the remainders for the value and derivative converge to the rounding level and double-word rounding of the grown terms is below one `f64` unit.
   This corresponds to $(l^2 + \eta^2)/\rho$ below about 59; there is no fixed radial threshold.
3. If the expansion is rejected, use Steed's method.
   Evaluate two continued fractions at the point $\rho_a = \max(\rho, \eta + \sqrt{\eta^2 + (l + 1/2)^2} + 1)$ outside the turning point.
4. For $\rho < \rho_a$ (inside the turning point), continue $G_l$ inward from $\rho_a$ and $F_l$ outward from its origin series, then normalize using the Wronskian.

### Steed's method

Steed's method (Barnett 1981) determines $F_l$, $G_l$, $F_l'$, and $G_l'$ simultaneously from two continued fractions and the Wronskian.
The first continued fraction, CF1 (DLMF 33.8.1), gives the logarithmic derivative $f = F_l'/F_l$.
CF1 uses the fact that $F_l$ is the minimal solution in order; it is evaluated with the modified Lentz method, and denominator sign changes also determine the sign of $F_l$.
The second continued fraction, CF2 (DLMF 33.8.2), gives the outgoing wave's logarithmic derivative $(H_l^+)'/H_l^+ = p + iq$.
CF2 converges outside the turning point, so move the evaluation point outward to $\rho_a$.

The four equations for $f$, $p$, $q$, and the Wronskian determine the four unknowns in the following closed form.

$$
F_l = \pm\sqrt{\frac{q}{(f - p)^2 + q^2}}, \quad F_l' = fF_l, \quad G_l = \frac{f - p}{q}F_l, \quad G_l' = pG_l - qF_l.
$$

Choose the sign to match the sign of $F_l$ obtained from CF1.
Advance CF2 in double-word arithmetic until the increment is at most $2^{-64}$ of the sum, add the remaining tail in `f64`, and include that rounding in the estimated error.
The term counts in CF1 and CF2 grow roughly in proportion to $\rho - \eta$, so Steed's work is also proportional to $\rho - \eta$.
For large $\rho$ where the outgoing asymptotic expansion is rejected (large $l$ or $\lvert\eta\rvert$, with $(l^2 + \eta^2)/\rho > 59$), this work may exceed the default budget.

### Inside the turning point

Inside the turning point, CF2 converges more slowly and the magnitudes of $F_l$ and $G_l$ separate widely.
Use $G_l$ and $G_l'$ from Steed's method at $\rho_a$ as initial values, then continue inward to $\rho$ with Taylor-series steps of the differential equation.
Because $G_l$ grows inward, relative continuation error does not increase.
For $F_l$, sum the Frobenius series about the origin $F_l \propto \rho^{l+1}\sum_n a_n\rho^n$ (DLMF 33.6) to a radius where cancellation amplification stays within $2^{23}$, then continue outward with Taylor series.
This is also stable because $F_l$ grows outward.
Finally, normalize the resulting $F_l$ using the Wronskian $F_l'G_l - F_lG_l' = 1$.

When called separately, `coulomb_f`, `coulomb_f_prime`, and `coulomb_spherical_f` do not form $G_l$ for $\rho < \rho_a$; they normalize $F_l$ from the origin series and outward continuation directly with $C_l(\eta)$.
For $\rho \ge \rho_a$, they use the same computation as `coulomb_wave`.
Thus, inside the turning point, $F_l$ in `coulomb_f` and `coulomb_wave` follows different paths and can differ at the rounding level, as compared in the cell below.

### Complex radii

For complex $z$, combine origin series, Taylor-series continuation of the differential equation, asymptotic expansions of $H_l^\pm$ ($\lvert z\rvert > 8 + \lvert l\rvert + \lvert\eta\rvert$), and complex CF2.
The branch cut follows the negative real axis; when the imaginary part is $\pm 0$, its sign selects the side.
A negative $z$ passed with a real type is outside the domain.

## Order recurrences and sequences

Neighboring orders are connected by the following raising and lowering relations (forms of DLMF 33.4.1, 33.4.2), with $u$ equal to either $F$ or $G$.

$$
R_{l+1}\,u_{l+1} = S_{l+1}u_l - u_l', \qquad
R_l\,u_{l-1} = S_lu_l + u_l', \qquad
S_l = \frac{l}{\rho} + \frac{\eta}{l}, \quad R_l = \sqrt{1 + \frac{\eta^2}{l^2}}.
$$

Rearranging the same relations obtains derivatives from the next order's value, so each sequence element advances as a value–derivative pair.
$F_l$ is a minimal solution, decreasing as $l$ increases, so downward recurrence from high order avoids error growth.
$G_l$ is a dominant solution, growing with $l$, so use upward recurrence.

`coulomb_sequence(component, l, eta, z, count)` returns the component selected by `CoulombComponent` (`F`, `G`, `FPrime`, `GPrime`, `SphericalF`) at orders $l, l+1, \ldots, l + \text{count} - 1$.
$F$, $F'$, and $F/z$ are evaluated once at the highest order and recurred downward; $G$ and $G'$ are evaluated at the lowest order and recurred upward, for one scalar evaluation plus $O(\text{count})$ recurrence steps.
`coulomb_wave_sequence(l, eta, z, count)` returns `[F, G, F′, G′]` for each order.
`coulomb_normalization_sequence(l, eta, count)` accumulates logarithms of the ratios $C_{l+1}/C_l = \sqrt{(l+1)^2 + \eta^2}/((l+1)(2l+3))$ in double-word arithmetic to return $C_l(\eta), C_{l+1}(\eta), \ldots$.

These three APIs return owned values, allocating the result `Vec`.
`coulomb_sequence_into`, `coulomb_wave_sequence_into`, and `coulomb_normalization_sequence_into` accept an output array `output` and a work array `staging` at least as long; they first write detailed results to `staging` and copy to `output` only if every order succeeds.
On failure, they leave `output` unchanged and allocate nothing.
The `_into` forms accept `CoulombOptions` and a solve scope, sharing a single work budget across all orders.
Each element's work counts describe the entire sequence.

## Normalization constant

The function `coulomb_normalization(l, eta)` returns $C_l(\eta)$.
Its domain $l > -1$ is wider than the wave-function domain $l > -1/2$.
It computes $\ln\lvert\Gamma(l + 1 + i\eta)\rvert$ in double-word arithmetic and assembles $\ln C_l$; even when large positive $\eta$ makes $C_l$ smaller than the `f64` range, the options form `coulomb_normalization_with_options` returns scaled and logarithmic forms.
There is no scoped API because work is bounded independently of the arguments.

## Hydrogenic radial functions

The radial function for bound state $(n, l)$ of a hydrogen-like atom with nuclear charge $Z$ is defined below, using the Bohr radius $a_0$ as the length unit.

$$
R_{nl}(r) = \left(\frac{2Z}{n}\right)^{3/2}\sqrt{\frac{(n - l - 1)!}{2n\,(n + l)!}}\;e^{-\rho/2}\rho^l\,L_{n-l-1}^{(2l+1)}(\rho), \qquad \rho = \frac{2Zr}{n}.
$$

$n \ge 1$ is the principal quantum number, $0 \le l < n$ is the orbital quantum number, and $L_k^{(\alpha)}$ is a generalized Laguerre polynomial (DLMF §18.3).
The normalization is $\int_0^\infty R_{nl}(r)^2 r^2\,dr = 1$; the angular factor, a spherical harmonic, is excluded.
This convention gives $R_{nl} > 0$ near the origin.
Formulas using the older Laguerre notation $L_{n+l}^{2l+1}$ and $[(n+l)!]^3$ have different factors and signs.

`hydrogenic_radial(n, l, charge, r)` accepts $r$ in Bohr-radius units.
$Z$ (`charge`) may be any positive real number and need not be an integer.
$r$ may be real with $r \ge 0$ or complex; $R_{nl}$ is entire in $r$.
Evaluate the Laguerre polynomial as the terminating Kummer function $U(-(n - l - 1), 2l + 2, \rho) = (-1)^{n-l-1}(n - l - 1)!\,L_{n-l-1}^{(2l+1)}(\rho)$ and compute normalization factorials using log-gamma functions.
There are $n - l - 1$ radial nodes ($r > 0$ where $R_{nl} = 0$).
[Hydrogenic radial wave functions and expectation values](../../examples/hydrogen-radial-states/) checks expectation values and probability densities.

The similarly named `RadialKind` selects radial-function kinds for Mathieu and spheroidal functions and is unrelated to Coulomb wave or hydrogenic radial functions ([Mathieu functions and spectral methods](../mathieu-and-spheroidal/), [Spheroidal wave functions](../spheroidal-wave-functions/)).

## Accuracy guarantees

ULP is the spacing of floating-point numbers at the exact result.
For real $F_l$, $G_l$, and their derivatives, the target checked against independent references is 64 ULP in `f64` and `f32` over the tested range; near zeros, assessment uses an absolute-error bound derived from the condition number.
The reference grid covers $-1/2 < l \le 32$, $-100 \le \eta \le 250$, and $10^{-4} \le \rho \le 10^6$, with additional individual points such as $\rho = 10^{12}$ and $10^{30}$.
No reference comparisons cover larger $l$, $\lvert\eta\rvert$, or $\rho$.
A sweep with the default work budget checks combinations of $l, \lvert\eta\rvert \le 1300$ and $2 \times 10^4 \le \rho \le 10^8$ for successful evaluation, a Wronskian within relative $10^{-13}$ of 1, and estimated errors of about $64\varepsilon$ of the amplitude.
For larger $l$ or $\lvert\eta\rvert$ and $\rho$ between about $6 \times 10^4$ and $(l^2 + \eta^2)/59$, Steed's work exceeds the default budget, giving `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).
Evaluation succeeds with a budget above about $\rho - \eta$ units ($l = \eta = 3000$, $\rho = 10^5$ succeeds with $2^{17}$ units).

Complex radii, normalization constants, and hydrogenic radial functions have no uniform ULP specification.
Complex references include orders through 50, $\lvert\eta\rvert \le 100$, and $10^{-3} \le \lvert z\rvert \le 10^4$; normalization tests extend to $\lvert\eta\rvert \le 10^4$.
Assess these values using options-form estimated errors that include recurrence, continuation, and normalization errors (the error in `ValueWithError`, not an enclosure).

## API

Wave functions such as `coulomb_f` and `hydrogenic_radial` use `NumericScalar`, accepting real (`f32`, `f64`) and complex `z` and `r`, while `l`, `eta`, and `charge` are real (`T::Real`).
All have standard forms, options forms returning `SpecialFunctionResult` (`CoulombWaveResult` for `coulomb_wave`), and scoped APIs accepting a solve scope.
The options `CoulombOptions` contain only a work budget (`Budget`, not an accuracy request), defaulting to 65536 units.

| Standard form | Form with options | Scoped API | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| `coulomb_f` | `coulomb_f_with_options` | `coulomb_f_scoped` | $F_l(\eta, z)$ |
| `coulomb_g` | `coulomb_g_with_options` | `coulomb_g_scoped` | $G_l(\eta, z)$ |
| `coulomb_f_prime` | `coulomb_f_prime_with_options` | `coulomb_f_prime_scoped` | $dF_l/dz$ |
| `coulomb_g_prime` | `coulomb_g_prime_with_options` | `coulomb_g_prime_scoped` | $dG_l/dz$ |
| `coulomb_spherical_f` | `coulomb_spherical_f_with_options` | `coulomb_spherical_f_scoped` | $F_l(\eta, z)/z$ (finite at the origin) |
| `coulomb_wave` | `coulomb_wave_with_options` | `coulomb_wave_scoped` | `[F, G, F′, G′]` in one evaluation |
| `coulomb_normalization` | `coulomb_normalization_with_options` | None | $C_l(\eta)$ (real only) |
| `hydrogenic_radial` | `hydrogenic_radial_with_options` | `hydrogenic_radial_scoped` | $R_{nl}(r)$ (`n`, `l` are `u32`) |

| Owned-result API (default budget, returns `Vec`) | Writing form (accepts options and scope) | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `coulomb_sequence` | `coulomb_sequence_into` | Selected component at orders $l, \ldots, l + N - 1$ |
| `coulomb_wave_sequence` | `coulomb_wave_sequence_into` | `[F, G, F′, G′]` per order (`CoulombWaveResult` in the writing form) |
| `coulomb_normalization_sequence` | `coulomb_normalization_sequence_into` | $C_l(\eta), \ldots, C_{l+N-1}(\eta)$ |

The standard form of `coulomb_wave` returns the array `[F, G, F′, G′]` and errors if any component cannot be represented as an ordinary value.
The options-form `CoulombWaveResult` returns each component's `SpecialFunctionResult` through `f()`, `g()`, `f_prime()`, and `g_prime()`; `is_success()` is true only when all four termination statuses indicate success.
The four components share work counts from the same evaluation; do not add them.
`CoulombWaveResult::zero()` initializes arrays of `coulomb_wave_sequence_into` and is not an evaluation result.

Classify errors using `kind()` from `SciOxideError`.
$l \le -1/2$ ($l \le -1$ for normalization constants) and negative real $z$ give `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
At $z = 0$, $F$ returns a value for any $l$, and $F'$ and $F/z$ return values for $l \ge 0$.
$G$ returns $1/C_0(\eta)$ only for $l = 0$, while $G'$ and `coulomb_wave` return values only for $l = \eta = 0$; other $l \ge 0$ give `DomainError` (`Pole`).
$z = 0$ in $-1/2 < l < 0$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) except at $F$.
`hydrogenic_radial` returns `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) for $n = 0$, $l \ge n$, $Z \le 0$, and negative real $r$.
Sequence functions return `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) identifying the length argument (`count` or `output`) if an order $l + k$ is nonfinite or rounds to the previous order; `_into` forms return `DimensionMismatch` if `staging` is too short.
Values beyond the type's range give `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`); stopping at the work budget gives `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).

## Wronskian across the turning point

The next cell selects $\eta$ and $l$ and evaluates `coulomb_wave_with_options` at 10 points from $\rho = 0.1$ to $10^5$.
The table shows whether each point is inside or outside the turning point $\rho_{\mathrm{tp}}$, $F_l$ and $G_l$, the difference of the Wronskian $F_l'G_l - F_lG_l'$ from 1, the relative difference from $F_l$ returned by `coulomb_f`, and options-form work counts.
The Wronskian uses four components from one evaluation, checking consistency of values and derivatives.
Comparison with `coulomb_f` is an independent check inside the turning point because $F_l$ follows a different path, using the normalization constant.

//|   - {"id":"deep-barrier","label":"η = 20, l = 2","description":"Deep inside the barrier, F is 10⁻²⁸ and G is 10²⁶ at ρ = 0.1.","inputs":{"eta":20,"order":2}}
//|   - {"id":"attractive","label":"η = −3","description":"For attraction, the turning point is near the origin, and the solutions oscillate almost everywhere.","inputs":{"eta":-3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default

//|       - {"artifact":"wronskian","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskian","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"max":4}}

//|   - case: deep-barrier

//|       - {"artifact":"wronskian","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.4673178434732155e-28,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskian","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":4}}

    } else if rho < anchor {
        "Outside (below ρ_a)"
    } else {
        "Outside"
    };

        rho,
        region,
        f,
        g,
        (wronskian - 1.0).abs() / eps,
        (single - f).abs() / (eps * f.abs()),

    ));

For the defaults $\eta = 5$ and $l = 0$, the turning point is $\rho_{\mathrm{tp}} = 10$, with solutions ranging from $F_0 \approx 1.3 \times 10^{-7}$ and $G_0 \approx 3.3 \times 10^5$ at $\rho = 0.1$ to outer oscillations with amplitude of order 1.
The Wronskian difference is 0 or 0.5 $\varepsilon$ units at every point, showing rounding-level consistency among the four double-word components.
The relative difference from `coulomb_f` is also at most 1 $\varepsilon$ unit, showing agreement between the two paths inside the turning point.
Work ranges from 300–900 units at $\rho \le 10$, using inward continuation, and 78–199 units outside.
For $\eta = 20$, $l = 2$, points through $\rho = 40$ lie inside; even when the ratio of $F_2$ and $G_2$ reaches $10^{54}$, the Wronskian stays at 1 because values are stored as significand × $e^{\text{scale}}$.
If differences exceed a few dozen $\varepsilon$ units, check whether the arguments lie outside the tested range and inspect options-form estimated errors.

## Spherical Bessel limit and order sequences

At $\eta = 0$, the relations are $F_l(0, \rho) = \rho\,j_l(\rho)$, $G_l(0, \rho) = -\rho\,y_l(\rho)$, and $C_l(0) = 2^l\,l!/(2l+1)!$.
The next cell selects $\rho$, forms `[F, G, F′, G′]` for $l = 0, \ldots, 8$ together with `coulomb_wave_sequence`, and compares against `spherical_jn` and `spherical_yn`.
For the same orders, it forms $F_l/\rho$ using `coulomb_sequence` (`CoulombComponent::SphericalF`) and $C_l(0)$ using `coulomb_normalization_sequence`, comparing them with $j_l(\rho)$ and the factorial formula, respectively.
The sequences use downward recurrence for $F$ and upward recurrence for $G$, so this table also checks recurrence stability.

    .zip(spherical.iter().zip(&normalization))

        n,
        *f,
        *g,
        relative(*f, rho * j),
        relative(*g, -rho * y),

For the default $\rho = 3$, the evaluation point lies inside the turning point $\sqrt{l(l+1)}$ when $l \ge 3$; $F_l$ decreases with $l$ while $G_l$ increases.
Every column's relative differences stay within 5 $\varepsilon$ units, showing that downward and upward recurrences do not amplify errors.
At $\rho = 0.5$ and $l = 8$, the values $F_8 \approx 5.6 \times 10^{-11}$ and $G_8 \approx 5.2 \times 10^8$ have a ratio of about $10^{19}$, yet relative differences remain within 3 $\varepsilon$ units.
Reversing the recurrence direction (recurring $F$ upward) would amplify error by this ratio, which is why downward recurrence for the minimal solution matters here.

## Summary

Coulomb wave functions use the outgoing asymptotic expansion for $\rho \ge 32$ if accepted; otherwise they use Steed's method (CF1, CF2, and the Wronskian) outside the turning point, continuing $G_l$ inward and $F_l$ outward from the origin inside it.
Steed's work is proportional to $\rho - \eta$, so increase the `CoulombOptions` budget for combinations of large $l$ or $\lvert\eta\rvert$ with large $\rho$.
For many orders, use `coulomb_sequence` and `coulomb_wave_sequence`, which recur $F$ downward and $G$ upward; use `_into` forms to avoid allocation.
For real radii, $F_l$, $G_l$, and their derivatives target 64 ULP over the tested range; assess complex radii, normalization constants, and hydrogenic radial functions with options-form estimated errors.
See [Coulomb barrier penetration and the Gamow factor](../../examples/coulomb-barrier-penetration/) and [Hydrogenic radial wave functions and expectation values](../../examples/hydrogen-radial-states/) for calculations with physical units, and [Coulomb and combinatorial functions](../../api/coulomb-and-combinatorics/) for individual specifications.
