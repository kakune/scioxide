Gravitational potentials outside an ellipsoid with three unequal axes (a triaxial ellipsoid), and electrostatic potentials around ellipsoidal conductors, are obtained by separating Laplace's equation in ellipsoidal coordinates.
The resulting ordinary differential equation is Lamé's equation; its polynomial-type solutions are Lamé functions, and their products are ellipsoidal harmonics.
Spheroids and spheres are limits in which two or three axes become equal.
`scioxide-special` provides first-kind Lamé functions $E_n^p$, second-kind functions $F_n^p$ that decay at infinity, and surface normalization constants $\gamma_n^p$ through the coefficient-retaining plan `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan` and one-shot functions.
This page explains coordinates and the equation, the four classes, the coefficient eigenproblem, quadrature for the second kind and normalization, accuracy guarantees, and APIs in that order.
Notation and definitions follow Dassios (2012).

## Ellipsoidal coordinates and Lamé's equation

For an ellipsoid with semiaxes $a_1 > a_2 > a_3 > 0$, define the semifocal distances by $h^2 = a_1^2 - a_2^2$ and $k^2 = a_1^2 - a_3^2$ ($0 < h < k$).
Ellipsoidal coordinates $(\rho, \mu, \nu)$ satisfy $\rho \ge k \ge \mu \ge h \ge \nu \ge 0$ and relate to Cartesian coordinates as follows.

$$
x^2 = \frac{\rho^2\mu^2\nu^2}{h^2k^2},\qquad
y^2 = \frac{(\rho^2 - h^2)(\mu^2 - h^2)(h^2 - \nu^2)}{h^2(k^2 - h^2)},\qquad
z^2 = \frac{(\rho^2 - k^2)(k^2 - \mu^2)(k^2 - \nu^2)}{k^2(k^2 - h^2)}
$$


A surface of constant $\rho$ is an ellipsoid with semiaxes $\rho$, $\sqrt{\rho^2 - h^2}$, and $\sqrt{\rho^2 - k^2}$; $\rho = a_1$ gives the original ellipsoid.
Surfaces of constant $\mu$ and $\nu$ are one-sheet and two-sheet hyperboloids, respectively, serving as angular coordinates on the ellipsoid.

Substituting $V = E(\rho)E(\mu)E(\nu)$ into Laplace's equation $\nabla^2 V = 0$ makes all three factors satisfy the same Lamé equation below.

$$
(s^2 - h^2)(s^2 - k^2)\,E''(s) + s\,(2s^2 - h^2 - k^2)\,E'(s) + \left(\Lambda - n(n+1)s^2\right)E(s) = 0
$$


$n$ is the degree, and $\Lambda$ is the separation constant ($\Lambda = (h^2 + k^2)p$ in Dassios's notation).
Suitable choices of $\Lambda$ give solutions that are products of polynomials and focal factors $\sqrt{|s^2 - h^2|}$ and $\sqrt{|s^2 - k^2|}$; there are exactly $2n + 1$ for each $n$.
These are the first-kind Lamé functions $E_n^p(s)$ ($1 \le p \le 2n+1$).
The interior harmonic $E_n^p(\rho)E_n^p(\mu)E_n^p(\nu)$ is a polynomial of degree $n$ in $x, y, z$.

## Four classes of Lamé functions

$E_n^p$ falls into four classes according to the presence of focal factors (`LameClass`).

| Class | Form | Count ($r = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$) |
|---|---|---|
| `K` | Polynomial of degree $n$ | $r + 1$ |
| `L` | $\sqrt{\lvert s^2 - h^2\rvert}$ × polynomial of degree $n-1$ | $n - r$ |
| `M` | $\sqrt{\lvert s^2 - k^2\rvert}$ × polynomial of degree $n-1$ | $n - r$ |
| `N` | $\sqrt{\lvert s^2 - h^2\rvert}\sqrt{\lvert s^2 - k^2\rvert}$ × polynomial of degree $n-2$ | $r$ |

The polynomial is even or odd in $s$, as determined by the parity of degree $n$.
The index $p$ counts classes in the order `K`, `L`, `M`, `N`, and within each class in increasing order of separation constant.
Normalization sets the leading coefficient to 1 ($E_n^p(s) \sim s^n$ as $s \to \infty$).
For example, at $n = 1$ the functions are $E_1^1 = s$ (`K`, $\Lambda = h^2 + k^2$), $E_1^2 = \sqrt{|s^2 - h^2|}$ (`L`, $\Lambda = k^2$), and $E_1^3 = \sqrt{|s^2 - k^2|}$ (`M`, $\Lambda = h^2$).
For real $s$, focal factors are positive; `EllipsoidalSigns::new(negative_h, negative_k)` can reverse each sign.
This selects signs in each octant because ellipsoidal coordinates determine only the squares of $x, y, z$.
For complex $s$, focal factors are analytically continued; when the imaginary part is 0, its sign selects the side of the cut.

## Eigenproblem for the coefficients

`EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::new(h2, k2, n, p, options)` takes $h^2$ and $k^2$ as arguments.
Preparation sets $s = h\cos\phi$, removes the factor $(1 - s^2/k^2)^{1/2}$ for classes `M` and `N`, and expands the remainder in $\cos j\phi$ (`K`, `M`) or $\sin j\phi$ (`L`, `N`).
This is equivalent to expansion in Chebyshev polynomials $T_j$ and $U_{j-1}$ of $s/h$.
Lamé's equation becomes a three-term recurrence for the coefficients, terminating in a finite tridiagonal matrix for polynomial solutions.
Because the matrix is nonsymmetric, symmetrize by replacing each off-diagonal pair with the square root of its product; compute all eigenvalues and eigenvectors using the symmetric tridiagonal solver in `scioxide-linalg` (implicit QL for size at most 32, divide and conquer above that), and select the eigenvalue for $p$.
The eigenvalue is the separation constant $\Lambda/k^2$, available through `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::eigenvalue_over_k_squared`.

QL eigenvalues and the location of the largest component are accurate, but small components near the ends may have poor relative accuracy.
Insert the eigenvalue into the recurrence to reconstruct components from both ends toward the largest component, then undo the symmetrization weights to obtain Chebyshev coefficients.
A relative residual above $10^{-12}$ in the symmetric eigenproblem returns `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`); `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::spectral_residual` exposes the residual.
The coefficient error estimate is $16\varepsilon(n+1)$ plus the residual divided by the minimum gap to a neighboring eigenvalue.
Preparation requires $O(n^2)$ work and temporary memory; the plan retains $O(n)$ coefficients.

For $n > 0$, prepare a second expansion.
For real $s$ between $h$ and $k$ ($h < |s| < k$), $s/h > 1$, and the Chebyshev terms grow and cancel outside the interval.
In this range, use the complementary problem's expansion in $t = \sqrt{k^2 - s^2}$, replacing $h^2$ by $k^2 - h^2$.
The two separation constants divided by $k^2$ should sum to $n(n+1)$; a relative discrepancy above $10^{-12}$ returns `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).

`EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::first_kind(s, signs)` sums the Chebyshev series with Clenshaw's recurrence, multiplies by the focal factors, and returns the value and derivative with respect to $s$.
Work is one unit per coefficient.
The estimated error is the coefficient error estimate multiplied by the condition number of Clenshaw summation, the sum of absolute term values divided by the value's magnitude.
Values at $s = \pm h$ and $s = \pm k$ are finite, but the derivative is omitted for classes in which it diverges there.
First-kind accuracy does not depend on the tolerance in `EllipsoidalOptions`.

### Checking the first kind

The next cell selects $h^2$, $k^2$, degree $n$, and coordinate $s$, creating plans for $p = 1, \dots, 2n+1$.
The table shows the class, separation constant $\Lambda/k^2$, $E_n^p(s)$ and its derivative, the Lamé-equation residual, and a leading-coefficient check.
The relative equation residual uses the centered difference of API derivatives (step $10^{-5}$) for the second derivative and divides by the sum of absolute values of the three terms; the equation is satisfied if the residual is below the finite-difference error, about $10^{-9}$.
The leading-coefficient check tests that $E_n^p(10^3 k)/(10^3k)^n$ is close to 1.

//| id: special-ellipsoidal-lame-first-order
//| ref: special-ellipsoidal-lame-first-order
//| title: "Separation constants and equation residuals of first-kind Lamé functions"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   h2: {"type":"range","label":"h²","description":"Square of the shorter semifocal distance. Keep below k².","min":0.1,"max":0.9,"step":0.05,"value":0.5}
//|   degree: {"type":"integer","label":"Degree n","min":0,"max":5,"value":1}
//|   s: {"type":"range","label":"Coordinate s","description":"Between h and k, use the complementary expansion.","min":0.1,"max":3,"step":0.05,"value":1.5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"degree-three","label":"n = 3, s = 0.8","description":"The seven functions split into the four classes K, L, M, and N, using the complementary expansion for h < s < k.","inputs":{"degree":3,"s":0.8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"first-kind","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.5,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"first-kind","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":1.3228756555322954,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"first-kind","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":0.5,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"first-kind","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|   - case: degree-three
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"first-kind","path":"/rows/6/1","equals":"N"}
//|       - {"artifact":"first-kind","path":"/rows/3/5","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"first-kind","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":1,"rtol":1e-5}}
use scioxide_special::{EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan, EllipsoidalOptions, EllipsoidalSigns};

let k2 = 1.0;
let n = degree as usize;
let signs = EllipsoidalSigns::default();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for p in 1..=2 * n + 1 {
    let plan = EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::new(h2, k2, n, p, EllipsoidalOptions::default())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let evaluate = |x: f64| {
        plan.first_kind(x, signs)
            .and_then(|r| Ok((r.try_value()?, r.try_derivative()?)))
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
    };
    let (value, slope) = evaluate(s)?;
    // Second derivative from centered differences of API derivatives (independent check)
    let step = 1e-5;
    let curvature = (evaluate(s + step)?.1 - evaluate(s - step)?.1) / (2.0 * step);
    let lambda = plan.eigenvalue_over_k_squared() * k2;
    let terms = [
        (s * s - h2) * (s * s - k2) * curvature,
        s * (2.0 * s * s - h2 - k2) * slope,
        (lambda - (n * (n + 1)) as f64 * s * s) * value,
    ];
    let scale: f64 = terms.iter().map(|t| t.abs()).sum();
    let residual = terms.iter().sum::<f64>().abs() / scale;
    // Check that the leading coefficient is 1 (E ~ sⁿ)
    let far = 1e3 * k2.sqrt();
    let leading = evaluate(far)?.0 / far.powi(n as i32);
    let class = format!("{:?}", plan.class());
    rows.push((
        p as u32,
        class,
        plan.eigenvalue_over_k_squared(),
        value,
        slope,
        residual,
        leading,
    ));
}
let fixed10 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "p", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Class" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Λ/k²", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Eₙᵖ(s)", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "dEₙᵖ/ds", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative equation residual", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "E(10³k)/(10³k)ⁿ", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "first-kind" })
);
```

For the defaults $h^2 = 0.5$, $k^2 = 1$, $n = 1$, and $s = 1.5$, the three functions appear in the order `K`, `L`, `M`, with separation constants $\Lambda/k^2 = 1 + h^2/k^2 = 1.5$, $1$, and $h^2/k^2 = 0.5$.
The values are $E_1^1 = s = 1.5$, $E_1^2 = \sqrt{s^2 - h^2} = 1.3229$, and $E_1^3 = \sqrt{s^2 - k^2} = 1.1180$, agreeing with the closed forms above.
The relative equation residual is at most $5 \times 10^{-11}$, at the error level of the finite-difference second derivative.
The leading-coefficient check gives values such as $1 - 2.5 \times 10^{-7}$; the remainder is the contribution of lower-degree terms (the $s^{-2}$ term in $\sqrt{s^2 - h^2} = s(1 - h^2/(2s^2) + \cdots)$).
For the case $n = 3$, $s = 0.8$, the seven functions split into two `K`, two `L`, two `M`, and one `N`, ordered by increasing $\Lambda/k^2$ within each class.
Since $s = 0.8$ lies between $h \approx 0.707$ and $k = 1$, it uses the complementary expansion; equation residuals remain at most $2.4 \times 10^{-9}$.


## Second-kind Lamé functions and exterior harmonics

Exterior solutions decaying at infinity use the following second-kind Lamé function.

$$
F_n^p(s) = (2n+1)\,E_n^p(s)\int_s^\infty \frac{dt}{E_n^p(t)^2\sqrt{t^2 - h^2}\sqrt{t^2 - k^2}}
$$


As $s \to \infty$, $E_n^p(t)^2 \sim t^{2n}$ implies $F_n^p(s) \sim s^{-n-1}$; with the unit-leading-coefficient convention, $s^{n+1}F_n^p(s) \to 1$.
The exterior harmonic is $F_n^p(\rho)E_n^p(\mu)E_n^p(\nu)$, used to expand gravitational potentials outside ellipsoids.
The definition gives $\frac{d}{ds}(F/E) = -(2n+1)/(E^2\sqrt{(s^2-h^2)(s^2-k^2)})$, so the Wronskian is

$$
E_n^p F_n^{p\prime} - E_n^{p\prime} F_n^p = -\frac{2n+1}{\sqrt{(s^2 - h^2)(s^2 - k^2)}}
$$


`EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::second_kind(s)` uses adaptive quadrature for the following integral over $u \in (0, 1]$, obtained by the substitution $t = s/u$.

$$
\int_s^\infty \frac{dt}{E(t)^2\sqrt{t^2 - h^2}\sqrt{t^2 - k^2}} = \frac{1}{s}\int_0^1 \frac{du}{E(s/u)^2\sqrt{1 - h^2u^2/s^2}\sqrt{1 - k^2u^2/s^2}}
$$


The integrand behaves as $u^{2n}$ near $u = 0$ and is smooth.
Quadrature compares a 32-point Gauss–Legendre rule with the sum over two half-intervals, repeatedly bisecting intervals whose discrepancy exceeds the tolerance (0.05 times the relative tolerance in `EllipsoidalOptions`, but at least $32\varepsilon$).
$s$ must satisfy $s > k$ when real or $|s| > k$ when complex; otherwise it gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
As $s$ approaches $k$, the square-root denominator near $u = 1$ shrinks, requiring more bisections.

For low degrees, the integral can be expressed using Carlson symmetric forms.
Applying a DLMF 19.29-type substitution to the $u$ integral and the homogeneity of $R_F$ and $R_D$ gives the following expressions.

$$
F_0^1(s) = R_F(s^2,\ s^2 - h^2,\ s^2 - k^2),\qquad
F_1^1(s) = s\,R_D(s^2 - h^2,\ s^2 - k^2,\ s^2)
$$


`carlson_rf` and `carlson_rd` use Carlson duplication, providing a check independent of adaptive quadrature.

## Normalization constant

The normalization constant for the surface harmonic $E_n^p(\mu)E_n^p(\nu)$ is

$$
\gamma_n^p = 8\int_0^h\!\!\int_h^k \frac{E_n^p(\nu)^2E_n^p(\mu)^2\,(\mu^2 - \nu^2)}{\sqrt{(h^2 - \nu^2)(k^2 - \nu^2)(\mu^2 - h^2)(k^2 - \mu^2)}}\,d\mu\,d\nu
$$

where 8 accounts for the ellipsoid's eight octants.
The integrand has inverse-square-root singularities at four endpoints, but the integral is finite.
`EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::normalization` removes these singularities through Jacobi elliptic substitutions $\nu = h\,\operatorname{sn}(u \mid m)$ and $\mu = h/\operatorname{dn}(v \mid 1 - m)$ ($m = h^2/k^2$).
Splitting $\mu^2 - \nu^2 = (\mu^2 - h^2) + (h^2 - \nu^2)$ turns the double integral into a sum of products involving four one-dimensional integrals in total, each evaluated by the same adaptive quadrature as above.
The upper limits are complete elliptic integrals of the first kind, evaluated with Carlson's $R_F$.
For low degrees, $\gamma_0^1 = 4\pi$, $\gamma_1^1 = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}h^2k^2$, $\gamma_1^2 = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}h^2(k^2 - h^2)$, and $\gamma_1^3 = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}k^2(k^2 - h^2)$ (Dassios 2012).

### Checking the second kind

//|       - {"artifact":"exterior","path":"/rows/7/3","approx":{"value":1,"rtol":1e-4}}
use scioxide_special::{
    EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan, EllipsoidalOptions, EllipsoidalSigns, carlson_rd, carlson_rf,
};

let k2 = 1.0;
let n = degree as usize;
// Keep p between 1 and 2n + 1
let p = (order as usize).min(2 * n + 1);

let mut curve = Vec::new();
let mut asymptote = Vec::new();
for step in 0..8 {
    let s = s_start * 2.0_f64.powi(step);
    let pair = |r: scioxide_special::SpecialFunctionResult<f64>| -> Result<(f64, f64), String> {
        Ok((
            r.try_value().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
            r.try_derivative().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
        ))
    };
    let (f, df) = pair(plan.second_kind(s).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)?;
    let (e, de) = pair(
        plan.first_kind(s, EllipsoidalSigns::default())
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    )?;
    // Closed forms using Carlson symmetric forms (only n ≤ 1, p = 1; independent check)
    let (x, y, z) = (s * s, s * s - h2, s * s - k2);
    let closed = match (n, p) {
        (0, 1) => Some(carlson_rf(x, y, z).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?),
        (1, 1) => Some(s * carlson_rd(y, z, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?),
        _ => None,

    let relative = closed.map(|c| (f - c).abs() / c.abs());
    let wronskian = e * df - de * f;
    let expected = -((2 * n + 1) as f64) / (y * z).sqrt();
    let wronskian_error = (wronskian - expected).abs() / expected.abs();
    rows.push((s, f, relative, f * s.powi(n as i32 + 1), wronskian_error));
    curve.push([s, f]);
    asymptote.push([s, s.powi(-(n as i32) - 1)]);

        "yType": "log"

);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed10 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "s [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Fₙᵖ(s)", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative difference from closed form", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "No closed form" },

//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":12.566370614359172,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/1/4","range":{"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":1e-11}}
//|   - case: near-degenerate
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"normalization","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"max":1e-11}}
use scioxide_special::{EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan, EllipsoidalOptions};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let k2 = 1.0;
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed10 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 });
// Normalization constants and closed forms (Dassios 2012)
let closed = [
    (0, 1, 4.0 * PI),
    (1, 1, 4.0 * PI / 3.0 * h2 * k2),
    (1, 2, 4.0 * PI / 3.0 * h2 * (k2 - h2)),
    (1, 3, 4.0 * PI / 3.0 * k2 * (k2 - h2)),
];
let mut norms = Vec::new();
for (degree, index, expected) in closed {
    let options = EllipsoidalOptions::default();
    let plan = EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::new(h2, k2, degree, index, options)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let result = plan.normalization().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let gamma = result.try_value().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Relative estimated error and termination status from detailed results
    let estimate = result.value().and_then(|v| v.relative_error());
    let status = format!("{:?}", result.termination());
    let relative = (gamma - expected).abs() / expected;
    norms.push((
        degree as u32,
        index as u32,
        gamma,
        expected,
        relative,
        estimate,
        status,
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "p", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "γₙᵖ (quadrature)", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Closed form", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative difference", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative estimated error", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination status" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    norms,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "normalization" })
);
```

For the default $h^2 = 0.5$, all four normalization constants agree with the closed forms within relative $7 \times 10^{-16}$, with termination status `Converged`.
Relative estimated errors are about $10^{-13}$, two orders of magnitude above the actual differences and within the tolerance $10^{-12}$.
The estimate is not an upper bound, but it does not underestimate the actual error in this example.
At $h^2 = 0.95$, $a_2$ and $a_3$ are nearly equal, giving a nearly prolate ellipsoid; $\gamma_1^2$ and $\gamma_1^3$ shrink, but differences remain at the same level.

## Accuracy guarantees

Ellipsoidal harmonics have no uniform ULP specification across the function family.
`spectral_residual` is a diagnostic, not an error upper bound.
Each evaluation's error estimate uses coefficient estimates, the Chebyshev sum's condition number, and, for quadrature, the tolerance; it is not an enclosure.

Comparison tests use independently generated high-precision references with the following tolerances.
For `f64`, $E_n^p$ and its derivative, $F_n^p$ and its derivative, $\gamma_n^p$, and the separation constant all agree within relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ (tested with quadrature tolerance $10^{-8}$).
Plans for `f32` agree within relative $2 \times 10^{-4}$.
For every $p$ at $n \le 8$, tests check the Wronskian within relative $5 \times 10^{-10}$ and the Lamé-equation residual within finite-difference accuracy.
$F_0^1$ agrees with `carlson_rf` within relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$; $\gamma_1^p$ agrees with the closed form within relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$.

## API

| Item | Role |
|---|---|
| `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::new(h2, k2, n, p, options)` | Prepare coefficients of $E_n^p$ (also the complementary expansion for $n > 0$). `build(.., scope)` performs the same preparation within a scope |
| `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::first_kind(s, signs)`, `first_kind_scoped` | Detailed results for $E_n^p(s)$ and its derivative (real or complex `s`) |
| `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::second_kind(s)`, `second_kind_scoped` | Detailed results for $F_n^p(s)$ and its derivative |
| `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::normalization()`, `normalization_scoped` | Detailed result for normalization constant $\gamma_n^p$ |
| `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::eigenvalue_over_k_squared`, `spectral_residual`, `preparation_accounting` | Separation constant $\Lambda/k^2$, relative eigenproblem residual, and preparation work counts |
| `EllipsoidalHarmonicPlan::geometry`, `degree`, `order`, `class` | $(h^2, k^2)$, $n$, $p$, `LameClass` |
| `ellipsoidal_harmonic(h2, k2, n, p, s, signs)` | One-shot function for $E_n^p(s)$ |
| `ellipsoidal_harmonic_second_kind(h2, k2, n, p, s)` | One-shot function for $F_n^p(s)$ |
| `ellipsoidal_normalization(h2, k2, n, p)` | One-shot function for $\gamma_n^p$ |
| `LameClass` | Classes `K`, `L`, `M`, `N` |
| `EllipsoidalSigns::new(negative_h, negative_k)` | Signs of focal factors; both positive by default |
| `EllipsoidalOptions` | Relative quadrature tolerance (`with_relative_tolerance`; defaults $10^{-12}$ for `f64`, $10^{-5}$ for `f32`) and work budget (`with_iteration_budget`; default 262,144 units) |

Create a plan to evaluate many points for the same ellipsoid and $(n, p)$, or when estimated errors, derivatives, or termination status are needed.
A plan retains the options from its construction; preparation and each evaluation have separate work budgets.
One-shot functions create one plan with the default `EllipsoidalOptions`, evaluate once, share a single budget between preparation and evaluation, and return the ordinary value.
One-shot functions have no options forms `*_with_options` or scoped APIs `*_scoped`; use a plan and its `*_scoped` methods for those purposes.

Errors are as follows.
NaN or infinity in $h^2$, $k^2$, or $s$ gives `NonFinite`; failure of $0 < h^2 < k^2$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`), and failure of $1 \le p \le 2n+1$ gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
A degree $n$ so large that the eigenproblem size overflows gives `SizeOverflow`; failure to obtain eigenvectors or coefficients in `f64` gives `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`); exceeding the work budget gives `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).
One-shot functions return `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`) when the value exceeds the scalar type's range.
Detailed results retain out-of-range values in scaled form.

## Summary

Ellipsoidal harmonics are assembled from polynomial-type solutions $E_n^p$ of Lamé's equation and second-kind functions $F_n^p$ formed by integration.
$E_n^p$ comes from a finite tridiagonal eigenproblem; accuracy is independent of the tolerance, and `spectral_residual` and the condition number of Clenshaw summation diagnose it.
$F_n^p$ and $\gamma_n^p$ use adaptive quadrature, so the `EllipsoidalOptions` tolerance changes both accuracy and work; work increases as $s$ approaches $k$.
For low degrees, Carlson symmetric forms and closed forms provide checks; for every degree, use the Wronskian and $s^{n+1}F_n^p \to 1$.

For a spheroid with two equal axes, separated Laplace solutions are products of Legendre functions; proceeding to the Helmholtz equation gives spheroidal wave functions.
See [Spheroidal wave functions](../spheroidal-wave-functions/) for their characteristic values, angular functions, and radial functions.
[Carlson symmetric forms and incomplete elliptic integrals](../carlson-symmetric-integrals/) explains the algorithm for the Carlson forms used to check second-kind functions.
