Elliptic integrals arise in quantities that cannot be expressed by integrating elementary functions, such as the period of a large-amplitude pendulum, an ellipse's perimeter, and a spheroid's capacitance.
This page explains elliptic-integral definitions, the parameter convention, domains, and endpoint values, then summarizes evaluation methods, error, accuracy, and calling forms for the complete integrals $K$ and $E$.
[Carlson symmetric forms and incomplete elliptic integrals](../carlson-symmetric-integrals/) covers evaluation of incomplete integrals and the complete third-kind integral; [Jacobi elliptic functions](../jacobi-elliptic-functions/) covers inverses of elliptic integrals of the first kind.
[Legendre functions](../legendre-functions-and-spherical-harmonics/) covers Legendre functions.

## Parameter convention and Legendre normal forms

Elliptic integrals are integrals of $R(t, \sqrt{P(t)})$, where $P(t)$ is a cubic or quartic polynomial without repeated roots and $R$ is rational.
Legendre reduced them to normal forms of the first, second, and third kinds.

The literature uses both the modulus $k$ and the parameter $m = k^2$.
DLMF writes the modulus $k$ as the argument, but every `scioxide-special` function accepts the parameter $m$.
The amplitude $\phi$ is in radians, and $n$ is the characteristic of the third-kind integral.
For $\phi$ and $m$, the incomplete integrals of the first and second kinds are

$$
F(\phi \mid m) = \int_0^\phi \frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1 - m \sin^2\theta}}, \qquad
E(\phi \mid m) = \int_0^\phi \sqrt{1 - m \sin^2\theta} \, d\theta
$$

and their values at $\phi = \pi/2$ are the complete integrals $K(m) = F(\pi/2 \mid m)$ and $E(m) = E(\pi/2 \mid m)$.
The complete integral of the third kind is

$$
\Pi(n \mid m) = \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{d\theta}{(1 - n \sin^2\theta)\sqrt{1 - m \sin^2\theta}}
$$


`elliptic_k(m)`, `elliptic_e(m)`, `elliptic_f(phi, m)`, `elliptic_e_inc(phi, m)`, and `elliptic_pi(n, m)` return these integrals.
No function for the incomplete integral of the third kind is provided.
For $|\phi| \le \pi/2$, assemble it from `carlson_rf` and `carlson_rj`, which return Carlson symmetric forms $R_F$ and $R_J$ ([Carlson forms of Legendre integrals](oxref:special-carlson-legendre-forms)).

### Domains and endpoint values

Real types require $m \le 1$; $m > 1$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
$m < 0$ is also accepted and corresponds to a purely imaginary modulus.
The values at zero satisfy $K(0) = E(0) = \pi/2$; as $m \to 1^-$, $E(m) \to 1$ remains bounded while $K(m)$ diverges logarithmically.

$$
K(m) = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{16}{1 - m} + O\bigl((1 - m)\ln(1 - m)\bigr) \quad (m \to 1^-)
$$

`elliptic_k(1.0)` returns the limit $+\infty$ as a successful value; the options-form termination status is `Limit`.
`elliptic_e(1.0)` returns 1.

The complete integrals satisfy the following Legendre relation (DLMF §19.7).

$$
E(m) K(1 - m) + E(1 - m) K(m) - K(m) K(1 - m) = \frac{\pi}{2} \qquad (0 < m < 1)
$$

It checks values at the two parameters $m$ and $1 - m$ simultaneously.

Incomplete elliptic integrals are odd in $\phi$ and satisfy $F(\phi + j\pi \mid m) = F(\phi \mid m) + 2jK(m)$ and $E(\phi + j\pi \mid m) = E(\phi \mid m) + 2jE(m)$ for integer $j$.
The implementation uses these relations to reduce the amplitude to $[0, \pi/2]$ and accepts every finite real $\phi$.
At $m = 1$, $F(\phi \mid 1) = \operatorname{artanh}(\sin\phi)$ is finite for $|\phi| < \pi/2$ and gives $\pm\infty$ (`Limit`) at $\phi = \pm\pi/2$ ($\pi/2$ rounded to the scalar type); for $|\phi| > \pi/2$, the integration path crosses an integrand singularity, giving `DomainError` (`Undefined`).
At $m = 1$, the integrand of $E(\phi \mid 1)$ is $|\cos\theta|$, so the integral is finite for every finite $\phi$.

$\Pi(n \mid m)$ requires $n \le 1$.
At $n = 1$ or $m = 1$, it returns the limit $+\infty$.
For $n > 1$, the integrand has a pole inside $(0, \pi/2)$; it returns `DomainError` (`Undefined`) rather than a Cauchy principal value.

Complex types (`Complex<f32>`, `Complex<f64>`) are also accepted, using principal square roots.
The cuts of $K$ and $E$ are $m \in [1, \infty)$; for real-axis arguments, signed zero in the imaginary part selects the side.
For `elliptic_f` and `elliptic_e_inc`, the parameter can remain real when the amplitude is complex.

## Evaluation of complete elliptic integrals K and E

Because of the logarithmic singularity as $m \to 1^-$, direct quadrature for $K$ needs more points as $1 - m$ shrinks.
For real $0 \le m < 1$, `elliptic_k` and `elliptic_e` use the following approximations combining polynomials in $x = 1 - m$ with logarithmic terms.

$$
K(m) = P_K(x) - \ln x \, Q_K(x), \qquad E(m) = P_E(x) - x \ln x \, Q_E(x)
$$


$P_K$, $Q_K$, and $P_E$ have degree 10, and $Q_E$ degree 9; their coefficients come from Moshier's Cephes library (`ellpk`, `ellpe`).
Since $P_K(0) = \ln 4$ and $Q_K(0) = 1/2$, the first expression agrees with the asymptotic form $\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(16/x)$ as $x \to 0$.
Separating the singularity into the $\ln x$ factor leaves only a part smooth up to the endpoint for polynomial approximation.
Evaluation uses binary64 Horner's method with fused multiply-add, without iteration; work is independent of $m$.
binary32 inputs are widened to binary64 before forming $1 - m$ and rounded only once at the end.

Negative $m$ uses the following imaginary-modulus transformation (DLMF §19.7 written in terms of the parameter),

$$
K(m) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - m}} K\!\left(\frac{-m}{1 - m}\right), \qquad E(m) = \sqrt{1 - m}\, E\!\left(\frac{-m}{1 - m}\right)
$$

mapping to $0 < -m/(1 - m) < 1$.
The transformed complementary parameter is $x = 1/(1 - m)$; the approximation accepts $x$ and $-\ln x = \ln(1 - m)$ directly.
When $-m$ exceeds $2^{512}$, these quantities are formed using power-of-two scaling, so even the most negative representable $m$ returns finite values.

For complex $m$, evaluate $K(m) = R_F(0, 1 - m, 1)$ and $E(m) = R_F(0, 1 - m, 1) - \tfrac{m}{3} R_D(0, 1 - m, 1)$ using Carlson duplication ([Duplication transformation](oxref:special-carlson-duplication)).
For $|1 - m| \le 10^{-6}$, instead use the endpoint expansion through two terms in $1 - m$ (DLMF §19.12).
Complex $m = 1$ is a pole of $K$, giving `DomainError` (`Pole`); $E(1) = 1$ returns its exact value.

### Arithmetic–geometric mean

Gauss's arithmetic–geometric mean (AGM), a classical algorithm, starts from $a_0 = 1$, $b_0 = \sqrt{1 - m}$, and $c_0 = \sqrt{m}$ and repeats the following updates.

$$
a_{j+1} = \frac{a_j + b_j}{2}, \quad b_{j+1} = \sqrt{a_j b_j}, \quad c_{j+1} = \frac{a_j - b_j}{2}
$$


$a_j$ and $b_j$ converge quadratically to their common limit $M(1, \sqrt{1 - m})$ ($c_{j+1} = c_j^2 / (4 a_{j+1})$, so the number of correct digits roughly doubles per iteration), yielding the following identities (DLMF §19.8).

$$
K(m) = \frac{\pi}{2 M(1, \sqrt{1 - m})}, \qquad E(m) = K(m)\left(1 - \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} 2^{j-1} c_j^2\right)
$$


`scioxide-special` uses the AGM to evaluate Jacobi elliptic functions, rather than $K$ and $E$ ([Evaluation by AGM and descending Landen transformations](oxref:special-jacobi-agm-landen)).
When $b_0 = \sqrt{1 - m}$ can be formed without going through $m$ ($\cos(\theta_0/2)$ for a pendulum), the AGM provides an independent reference unaffected by rounding in $m$ ([Compute the period of a large-amplitude pendulum](../../examples/pendulum-period/)).

## Error, stability, and computational cost

Complete elliptic integrals $K$ and $E$ have fixed work with a reported count of 0; options-form estimated errors are envelopes measured against reference data (confidence `StatisticallyCalibrated`).
The estimated error is not an enclosure.
The work budget does not affect the values of $K$ and $E$.

When $m$ is near 1, the value of $K(m)$ itself is sensitive to rounding in $m$.
Since $dK/dm \approx 1/(2(1 - m))$, a relative error $\varepsilon$ in $m$ is amplified into relative error $\varepsilon/(2(1 - m)K)$ in $K$.
This is the function's conditioning, not an error in the evaluation method.
When $1 - m$ can be formed accurately from another quantity, such as the cosine of a swing angle, use a form that avoids $m$, for example passing $1 - m$ directly to `carlson_rf` in $K = R_F(0, 1 - m, 1)$.

## Accuracy guarantees

Accuracy over the range tested against independently generated high-precision reference data is as follows.

| Function (real arguments) | binary64 | binary32 | Test findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| `elliptic_k`, `elliptic_e` ($0 \le m < 1$) | 2 ULP | 1 ULP | Measured and boundary values both meet this range. Estimated errors use a relative envelope $2\varepsilon$ of the value ($\varepsilon$ for binary32) |

Tests confirm that $K$ and $E$ for negative real $m$ return finite values down to the most negative representable argument, but there is no ULP specification.
Complex $m$ has no uniform ULP specification; use the options-form estimated error.
See [Accuracy guarantees for Carlson symmetric forms and incomplete elliptic integrals](oxref:special-carlson-accuracy) for incomplete-integral and complete third-kind specifications.

## Functions and calling forms

All functions accept `NumericScalar` (`f32`, `f64`, `Complex<f32>`, `Complex<f64>`), using the same names for real and complex arguments.
Standard forms return only the value; options forms `*_with_options(.., options)` return `SpecialFunctionResult`, and scoped APIs `*_scoped(.., options, scope)` return the same result within a solve scope.

| Family | Standard form | Options form | Scoped API | Domain (real types) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Complete elliptic integrals | `elliptic_k(m)`, `elliptic_e(m)` | `elliptic_k_with_options`, `elliptic_e_with_options` | `elliptic_k_scoped`, `elliptic_e_scoped` | $m \le 1$ |

Incomplete integrals `elliptic_f` and `elliptic_e_inc` and the complete third-kind integral `elliptic_pi` have the same three forms ([Functions and calling forms](oxref:special-carlson-call-forms)).
Options forms and scoped APIs accept `EllipticOptions`.
`EllipticOptions` contains only a work budget (`Budget`), defaulting to 65536 units.
It accepts no accuracy request; increasing the budget does not change the result's accuracy.
Options results contain an estimated error, termination status (`SpecialFunctionTermination`), work counts, and algorithm identifier.
The algorithm identifiers `complete_minimax` and `exact_m_one` indicate the fixed-work complete-integral approximation and the closed value at $m = 1$, respectively.

```rust
//| id: special-elliptic-detail-rows
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Collect options results and errors into table rows"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SciOxideErrorView};
use scioxide_special::SpecialFunctionResult;

/// One table row: call, value, estimated error, confidence, termination status or error, work, algorithm.
pub(crate) type DetailRow = (
    String,
    Option<f64>,
    Option<f64>,
    String,
    String,
    Option<usize>,
    String,
);

/// Describe an error with the classification name from kind() and details from view().
pub(crate) fn error_label(error: &SciOxideError) -> String {
    match error.view() {
        SciOxideErrorView::DomainError { reason, .. } => format!("DomainError（{reason:?}）"),
        SciOxideErrorView::InvalidInput { reason, .. } => format!("InvalidInput（{reason:?}）"),
        SciOxideErrorView::ConvergenceFailure { reason, .. } => {
            format!("ConvergenceFailure（{reason:?}）")
        }
        SciOxideErrorView::NumericalInstability { issue, .. } => {
            format!("NumericalInstability（{issue:?}）")
        }
        _ => format!("{:?}", error.kind()),
    }
}

/// Turn an options result or error into one row. Leave the value empty for infinite limits and describe them in the termination-status column.
pub(crate) fn detail_row(
    call: &str,
    result: Result<SpecialFunctionResult<f64>, SciOxideError>,
) -> DetailRow {
    let result = match result {
        Ok(result) => result,
        Err(error) => {
            let label = error_label(&error);
            return (
                call.into(),
                None,
                None,
                String::new(),
                label,
                None,
                String::new(),
            );
        }
    };
    let estimate = result.value();
    let value = estimate.map(|v| *v.value());
    let status = match value {
        Some(v) if v.is_infinite() => format!("{:?}（+∞）", result.termination()),
        _ => format!("{:?}", result.termination()),
    };
    (
        call.into(),
        value.filter(|v| v.is_finite()),
        estimate
            .map(|v| v.absolute_error())
            .filter(|e| e.is_finite()),
        estimate
            .map(|v| format!("{:?}", v.reliability()))
            .unwrap_or_default(),
        status,
        Some(result.counts().iterations()),
        result.algorithm().to_string(),
    )
}

/// Columns for the options-form table.
pub(crate) fn detail_columns() -> serde_json::Value {
    let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
    let digits16 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 16 });
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Call" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": digits16, "nullText": "—" },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Estimated error", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Confidence" },
        { "key": "4", "label": "Termination or error" },
        { "key": "5", "label": "Work", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },
        { "key": "6", "label": "Algorithm" }
    ])
}
```

The Legendre-relation residual stays at most $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ for every $m$, and relative differences from Carlson forms are at most $2.4 \times 10^{-16}$; the imaginary-modulus transformation at $m = -100$ also agrees with $R_F$.
Even at $p = -15$, $K = 18.65608235729033$ agrees with the asymptotic form within $3.6 \times 10^{-15}$, showing that the approximation's logarithmic term remains effective up to the endpoint.
In the second table, `elliptic_k_with_options` reports work 0, confidence `StatisticallyCalibrated`, and estimated error $8.2 \times 10^{-16}$ (relative $2\varepsilon$ of the value).
Evaluating the same $K(0.5)$ with `carlson_rf_with_options` counts 7 duplication steps and gives the larger estimate $2.6 \times 10^{-14}$ from a rounding model proportional to the step count (`ModelBased`).
$K(1)$ returns $+\infty$ with termination status `Limit`; $E(1)$ returns the closed value 1, and $K(1.5)$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).

## Summary and further reading

Pass the parameter $m = k^2$, rather than the modulus $k$, to elliptic integrals; real types require $m \le 1$.
$K(1) = +\infty$ and $\Pi(1 \mid m) = +\infty$ are successful values with termination status `Limit`, while $m > 1$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
For real $m < 1$, $K$ and $E$ use fixed-work approximations reporting 0 work (2 ULP in binary64); negative $m$ uses an imaginary-modulus transformation to reach the same approximation.
Near $m$ = 1, $K$ is sensitive to rounding in $m$; when $1 - m$ can be formed accurately, pass it directly to `carlson_rf`.
