Exponential and logarithmic integrals are defined by integrals of elementary functions such as $e^{-t}/t$ and $1/\ln t$.
$E_n$ appears in radiative transfer and neutron shielding, $E_1$ in the Theis solution for well pumping tests, and $\operatorname{li}$ in approximations to the prime-counting function.
$E_1(x)$ is also the upper incomplete gamma value $\Gamma(0, x)$ at $a = 0$.
To handle the logarithmic singularity at the origin, exponential growth or decay at large arguments, and cancellation near real zeros, `scioxide-special` selects rational approximations, series, continued fractions, and Chebyshev expansions by interval and computes with the exponential factor removed.
This page explains definitions and properties, interval selection and evaluation methods, accuracy guarantees, and API choices; a cell checks relative accuracy near the zero of $\operatorname{Ei}$.
[Trigonometric, hyperbolic, and Fresnel integrals](../trigonometric-integrals-and-fresnel/) covers sine and cosine integrals, hyperbolic integrals, and Fresnel integrals.

## Definition and properties

### Exponential integrals

For integer $n \ge 0$ and $x > 0$, define the generalized exponential integral as follows (DLMF §8.19).

$$
E_n(x) = \int_1^\infty \frac{e^{-xt}}{t^n}\,dt, \qquad E_1(x) = \int_x^\infty \frac{e^{-t}}{t}\,dt
$$

The order-zero value is $E_0(x) = e^{-x}/x$; for $n \ge 2$, $E_n(0) = 1/(n-1)$ is finite.
$E_1$ has the logarithmic singularity $E_1(x) = -\gamma - \ln x + x - x^2/4 + \cdots$ at the origin ($\gamma = 0.57721\ldots$ is Euler's constant).
Integration by parts gives the recurrence $n E_{n+1}(x) = e^{-x} - x E_n(x)$.
As $x \to \infty$, $E_n(x)$ decreases with $e^{-x}$, and the scaled value $e^{x} E_n(x)$ lies between $1/(x+n)$ and $1/(x+n-1)$ (DLMF §8.19).

The exponential integral $\operatorname{Ei}$ is defined by the following Cauchy principal value (DLMF §6.2).

$$
\operatorname{Ei}(x) = \mathrm{PV}\!\int_{-\infty}^{x} \frac{e^{t}}{t}\,dt = \gamma + \ln|x| + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^k}{k\,k!}
$$

At $x < 0$, $\operatorname{Ei}(x) = -E_1(-x)$.
$\operatorname{Ei}$ grows as $e^{x}/x$ for $x \to \infty$ and has a unique positive real zero $x_0 = 0.37250\,74107\,81366\,63446\ldots$ (DLMF §6.13).

For $x < 0$, `expint_e1(x)` and `expint_en(1, x)` return the real principal value $-\operatorname{Ei}(-x)$ rather than a complex value.
This is the real part of $E_1$ on either side of the cut.
$E_n$ with $n \ne 1$ does not accept $x < 0$.

### Logarithmic integrals li and Li

The logarithmic integral is $\operatorname{li}(x) = \mathrm{PV}\!\int_0^x dt/\ln t = \operatorname{Ei}(\ln x)$ ($x \ge 0$; DLMF §6.2).
It satisfies $\operatorname{li}(0) = 0$, has a logarithmic singularity at $x = 1$, and a unique zero $\mu = e^{x_0} = 1.45136\,92348\ldots$, Soldner's constant.
The offset logarithmic integral $\operatorname{Li}(x) = \operatorname{li}(x) - \operatorname{li}(2)$ approximates $\pi(x)$, the number of primes at most $x$.
Here $\operatorname{li}(2) = 1.04516\,37801\,17492\ldots$ and $\operatorname{Li}(2) = 0$.

### Cubic exponential integral

`expint_3(x)` is the cubic exponential integral $\int_0^x e^{-t^3}\,dt = \gamma(1/3, x^3)/3$ ($x \ge 0$).
$\gamma(a, z)$ is the lower incomplete gamma function; as $x \to \infty$, the value approaches $\Gamma(4/3) = 0.89297\,95115\ldots$.
Despite the similar name, it differs from $E_3(x)$ (`expint_en(3, x)`).

## Interval selection and evaluation methods

Different methods suit different intervals, so no single method covers the entire domain.
An origin series converges rapidly for small $x$, but at large $x$ its intermediate terms grow to order $e^{x}$ before cancelling, leaving rounding error.
Continued fractions and asymptotic expansions need few terms for large $x$, but converge slowly or lack accuracy for small $x$.
Intermediate intervals use fixed-degree rational approximations $P(t)/Q(t)$ with precomputed coefficients chosen to reduce maximum relative error, or Chebyshev expansions $\sum_k c_k T_k(t)$.
Chebyshev expansion maps the interval to $t \in [-1, 1]$ and sums with Clenshaw's recurrence; work is proportional to the coefficient count and independent of the input.

For exponentially growing or decaying functions, compute a scaled value with the exponential factor removed, then multiply by that factor only once at the end.
This avoids intermediate underflow and overflow; even when an ordinary value is unrepresentable, options-form `scaled()` (significand and power of 2) and `logarithmic()` retain values.

### Evaluation of E1 and Ei

For $E_1$ at $x > 0$, use Boost.Math rational approximations for binary64 (Maddock et al.).
For $x \le 1$, the form $E_1(x) = -\ln x + x - c + R(x)$ (constant $c$, rational function $R$) separates the logarithmic singularity $-\ln x$ from the rational approximation.
For $x > 1$, approximate the scaled value as $e^{x}E_1(x) = (1 + R(1/x))/x$.

$\operatorname{Ei}$ uses rational approximations on $(0, 6]$, $(6, 10]$, $(10, 20]$, $(20, 40]$, and $(40, \infty)$; $x < 0$ is routed to $-E_1(-x)$.
On $(0, 6]$, retain the zero $x_0$ in two words (the sum of two binary64 values with about 106 bits) and evaluate the following form.

$$
\operatorname{Ei}(x) = \ln\frac{x}{x_0} + (x - x_0)\,R\!\left(\frac{x}{3} - 1\right)
$$

Subtract $x - x_0$ in two words; for $|x - x_0| < 0.1$, evaluate $\ln(x/x_0)$ as $\ln(1 + (x - x_0)/x_0)$ with `ln_1p`, avoiding cancellation near the zero and preserving small relative error.
Intervals with $x > 1$ return the scaled value $e^{-x}\operatorname{Ei}(x)$, multiplying by the exponential factor only at the end.
The value of $\operatorname{Ei}$ exceeds binary64 range beyond $x \approx 716$; standard forms return `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`), while options forms retain values in `scaled()` and `logarithmic()`.

### Evaluation of En for orders at least 2

For $n \ge 2$, `expint_en` switches methods by $n$ and $x$ as follows, always forming the scaled value $e^{x}E_n(x)$.

| Condition | Method |
|---|---|
| $x = 0$ | $1/(n-1)$ |
| $x \le 1$ | Series (DLMF 8.19.8) with binary64 terms and a double-word sum |
| $1 < x \le 2$, $n \le 32$ | Upward recurrence $E_{m+1} = (e^{-x} - xE_m)/m$ from $E_1$ |
| $2 < x \le 8$, $n \le 32$ | Series (DLMF 8.19.8) summed in double-word arithmetic |
| Otherwise | Continued-fraction convergents (DLMF 8.19.17) from a three-term recurrence |

The series is $E_n(x) = \frac{(-x)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}(\psi(n) - \ln x) - \sum_{k \ne n-1} \frac{(-x)^k}{k!(1 - n + k)}$, where $\psi(n) = 1 + 1/2 + \cdots + 1/(n-1) - \gamma$ is the digamma function.
Since the series alternates, its absolute term sum grows to roughly $e^{x}/x$ while its sum $E_n(x)$ is only of order $e^{-x}/(x+n)$, losing about $2x\log_{10}e$ digits to cancellation.
At $x \le 8$, about 7 digits are lost; double-word arithmetic, with about 32 digits, absorbs this loss.
Each upward-recurrence step multiplies absolute error by $x/m$; for $x \le 2$ the first step amplifies it by at most 2, and steps with $m \ge 2$ do not amplify it.
All partial numerators and denominators of the continued fraction $e^{x}E_n(x) = \cfrac{1}{x + \cfrac{n}{1 + \cfrac{1}{x + \cfrac{n+1}{1 + \cdots}}}}$ are positive, so the three-term recurrences for convergent numerators and denominators require no subtraction.
When the denominator exceeds $2^{500}$, scale all four state values by $2^{-512}$; stop when successive convergents differ by at most relative $\varepsilon/2$ ($\varepsilon = 2^{-52}$ is binary64 machine epsilon).
The first convergent $1/(x+n)$ has relative correction at most $n/(x+n)^2$; for large $x$ where this is below $\varepsilon$, return $1/(x+n)$ without iteration.

### Cubic exponential and logarithmic integrals

`expint_3` uses Chebyshev expansions on $[0, 1]$, $[1, 2]$, $[2, 3]$, and $[3, 4]$ multiplied by $x$; for $x > 4$, return the rounded limit $\Gamma(4/3)$ and include the omitted-tail bound $e^{-x^3}/(3x^2)$ in the estimated error.
`logarithmic_integral` passes $\ln x$ to the $\operatorname{Ei}$ kernel; for $|x - 1| < 1/2$, form $\ln x$ with `ln_1p(x - 1)`.
Since $\ln x$ retains relative accuracy near the logarithmic singularity at $x = 1$, the small-argument $\operatorname{Ei}$ kernel works directly.
For $[1.5, 2.5]$, `logarithmic_integral_offset` uses the Chebyshev expansion $\operatorname{Li}(x) = (x - 2)\,T(x)$ about $x = 2$, avoiding cancellation in $\operatorname{li}(x) - \operatorname{li}(2)$.
For other $x$, subtract $\operatorname{li}(2)$ from $\operatorname{li}(x)$.

### Work budget and computational cost

`IntegralOptions` contains only a work budget, defaulting to 512 units.
Trigonometric, hyperbolic, and Fresnel integrals use the same `IntegralOptions`.
Rational approximations and Chebyshev expansions use one unit per coefficient; series and continued fractions use one unit per term.
Evaluations finish in a few dozen to about a hundred units; larger counts occur when $E_n$ needs more series or continued-fraction terms (103 units at $n = 3$, $x = 7.9$).
On reaching the budget, options forms return termination status `IterationLimit` and the value at truncation, with the estimated error inflated to the error model's limit.
Standard forms return `ConvergenceFailure` on reaching the budget.

## Accuracy guarantees and estimated errors

The following accuracy targets are checked against independently generated high-precision references with 160 and 240 digits.

| Function | binary64 target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `expint_e1`, `expint_e2`, `expint_en`, `expint_ei`, `expint_3` | 8 ULP | Assessed by absolute error near zeros |
| `expint_e1_scaled`, `expint_e2_scaled`, `expint_en_scaled`, `expint_ei_scaled` | No separate target | Comparison tests also check `expint_ei_scaled` and `expint_en_scaled` within 8 ULP |
| `logarithmic_integral`, `logarithmic_integral_offset` | No uniform target | Values and estimated errors tested in binary32 and binary64 |

ULP (unit in the last place) measures error in units of the spacing between adjacent floating-point numbers in the result type.
The 8 ULP target applies to binary64; binary32 has no ULP target.
Near a zero, the function value approaches 0 and relative error loses meaning; the specification becomes an absolute-error bound.
Near the zero $x_0$ of $\operatorname{Ei}$, explicit subtraction of the zero also keeps relative error small, as checked in the cell below.
For functions without uniform targets, comparison tests check binary64 within relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ (absolute $5 \times 10^{-13}$ for small values) and binary32 within relative $2 \times 10^{-4}$ (absolute $5 \times 10^{-6}$), and verify that estimates cover actual errors.

Options-form estimated errors use interval-specific error models.
For kernels $E_1$, $E_n$, and $\operatorname{Ei}$, add relative $2\varepsilon$ to the kernel error as an estimate checked for coverage against reference data (`EstimateReliability::StatisticallyCalibrated`).
Other functions and evaluations stopped at the budget use model-based estimates (`ModelBased`), adding relative $32\varepsilon$.
Neither is an interval with guaranteed containment.

## API

| Function | Computes | Domain and main errors | Options form | Scoped API |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `expint_e1` | $E_1(x)$; $-\operatorname{Ei}(-x)$ for $x < 0$ | $x = 0$ gives `Pole` | `expint_e1_with_options` | None |
| `expint_e1_scaled` | $e^{x}E_1(x)$ | $x = 0$ gives `Pole` | `expint_e1_scaled_with_options` | None |
| `expint_e2` | $E_2(x)$ | $x < 0$ gives `OutsideDomain` | `expint_e2_with_options` | `expint_e2_scoped` |
| `expint_e2_scaled` | $e^{x}E_2(x)$ | $x < 0$ gives `OutsideDomain` | `expint_e2_scaled_with_options` | `expint_e2_scaled_scoped` |
| `expint_en` | $E_n(x)$, `n: u32` | $x = 0$ with $n \le 1$ gives `Pole`; $x < 0$ with $n \ne 1$ gives `OutsideDomain` | `expint_en_with_options` | `expint_en_scoped` |
| `expint_en_scaled` | $e^{x}E_n(x)$ | Same as `expint_en` | `expint_en_scaled_with_options` | `expint_en_scaled_scoped` |
| `expint_ei` | $\operatorname{Ei}(x)$ | $x = 0$ gives `Pole` | `expint_ei_with_options` | None |
| `expint_ei_scaled` | $e^{-x}\operatorname{Ei}(x)$ | $x = 0$ gives `Pole` | `expint_ei_scaled_with_options` | None |
| `expint_3` | $\int_0^x e^{-t^3}dt$ | $x < 0$ gives `OutsideDomain` | `expint_3_with_options` | None |
| `logarithmic_integral` | $\operatorname{li}(x)$ | $x = 1$ gives `Pole`; $x < 0$ gives `OutsideDomain` | `logarithmic_integral_with_options` | None |
| `logarithmic_integral_offset` | $\operatorname{Li}(x) = \operatorname{li}(x) - \operatorname{li}(2)$ | Same as `logarithmic_integral` | `logarithmic_integral_offset_with_options` | None |

Errors are returned as `SciOxideError`; when `kind()` is `DomainError`, details in `view()` are `Pole` or `OutsideDomain`.
Nonfinite inputs give `NonFinite`; ordinary values exceeding the type's range give `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
All functions accept only real arguments (`RealScalar`); complex exponential integrals are not provided.
For complex $E_1(z)$, evaluate $\Gamma(0, z)$ using the complex form of `gamma_upper`.

Standard forms `f(x)` return only values.
Options forms `f_with_options(x, options)` accept `IntegralOptions` and return `SpecialFunctionResult` containing ordinary values `value()`, power-of-two representations `scaled()`, logarithmic forms `logarithmic()`, and derivatives `derivative()` with respect to $x$, each with estimated errors, plus termination status, work counts, and algorithm identifiers.
Options-form ordinary values are bitwise equal to standard-form values.
Scoped APIs `f_scoped(x, options, scope)` compute the same results as options forms while counting work in the caller's `SolveScope` and checking cancellation and deadlines.
For functions without a scoped API (None in the table), use the options form.

Use scaled forms when the value itself exceeds the representable range.
In radiative transfer with optical depth $\tau$ above a few hundred, $E_n(\tau)$ becomes 0 in binary64, but `expint_en_scaled(n, tau)` returns a value of order $1/(\tau + n)$, allowing the exponential factor $e^{-\tau}$ to be retained separately and combined logarithmically.
Large arguments of $\operatorname{Ei}$ instead cause overflow; use `expint_ei_scaled`.
Named scaled forms (`_scaled`) are separate functions with the exponential factor removed; options-form `scaled()` represents the same value using powers of 2.

## Relative accuracy near the zero of Ei

The next cell checks relative error of `expint_ei` at $x = x_0 + \delta$, displaced by $\delta$ from the zero $x_0$ of $\operatorname{Ei}$.
The reference sums the Taylor series $\operatorname{Ei}(x_0 + d) = e^{x_0}\int_0^d e^{u}/(x_0 + u)\,du$ about $x_0$ using standard f64 functions.
$x_0$ is stored as high and low f64 words from published digits; $d$ represents displacement from the true zero.
For comparison, also show relative error from directly summing the defining series $\gamma + \ln x + \sum x^k/(k\,k!)$ in f64 and the relative difference between `logarithmic_integral(exp(x))` and $\operatorname{Ei}(x)$.
The inputs select the sign of $\delta$ and the minimum $|\delta|$.

```rust
//| id: special-expint-ei-li-identity
//| ref: special-expint-ei-li-identity
//| title: "Relative error near the zero of Ei and li(eˣ) = Ei(x)"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   side: {"type":"select","label":"Direction of displacement from the zero","options":[{"label":"Above x₀ (δ > 0)","value":"plus"},{"label":"Below x₀ (δ < 0)","value":"minus"}],"value":"plus"}
//|   smallest_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Exponent p of minimum |δ| (|δ| = 10^p)","description":"Choose how closely to approach the zero.","min":-15,"max":-4,"step":1,"value":-12}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"below-root","label":"δ < 0, |δ| ≥ 10⁻¹⁵","description":"Ei is negative to the left of the zero; approach as closely as |δ| = 10⁻¹⁵.","inputs":{"side":"minus","smallest_exponent":-15}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"ei-zero","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ei-zero","path":"/rows/11/1","approx":{"value":3.896e-12,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ei-zero","path":"/rows/11/2","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ei-zero","path":"/rows/11/4","range":{"min":1e-6}}
//|   - case: below-root
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"ei-zero","path":"/rows/14/1","range":{"max":0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ei-zero","path":"/rows/14/2","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ei-zero","path":"/rows/14/4","range":{"min":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_special::{expint_ei, expint_ei_with_options, logarithmic_integral};

// Ei zero x₀ = 0.37250 74107 81366 63446 19918 66580… (DLMF §6.13).
// root_hi is the nearest f64 to x₀; root_lo = x₀ − root_hi is the remainder obtained from published digits.
let root_hi = 0.372_507_410_781_366_63_f64;
let root_lo = 1.314_018_341_438_602_8e-17;
let euler_gamma = 0.577_215_664_901_532_9_f64;
let sign = if side == "minus" { -1.0 } else { 1.0 };
let decades = (-smallest_exponent).round() as i32;

// Reference (standard f64 functions): integrate e^u/(x₀ + u) = Σₘ cₘ uᵐ term by term.
// cₘ = Σ_{i+j=m} (1/i!)(−1)ʲ/x₀^(j+1). The convergence radius is the distance x₀ to the singularity at 0; 60 terms suffice for |d| ≤ 0.1.
let reference = |d: f64| {
    let mut total = 0.0;
    let mut power = d;
    for m in 0..60 {
        let mut coefficient = 0.0;
        let mut inverse_factorial = 1.0;
        for i in 0..=m {
            let j = (m - i) as i32;
            coefficient += inverse_factorial * (-1.0_f64).powi(j) / root_hi.powi(j + 1);
            inverse_factorial /= (i + 1) as f64;
        }
        total += coefficient * power / (m + 1) as f64;
        power *= d;
    }
    root_hi.exp() * total
};
// Comparison: sum the defining series γ + ln x + Σ xᵏ/(k·k!) directly in f64 (standard f64 functions).
let naive = |x: f64| {
    let mut sum = euler_gamma + x.ln();
    let mut term = 1.0;
    for k in 1..60 {
        term *= x / k as f64;
        sum += term / k as f64;
    }
    sum
};

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for k in 1..=decades {
    let x = root_hi + sign * 10.0_f64.powi(-k);
    // x − root_hi is exact by Sterbenz's lemma. Subtract root_lo to obtain d, the displacement from the true zero.
    let d = (x - root_hi) - root_lo;
    let exact = reference(d);
    let value = expint_ei(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let detailed = expint_ei_with_options(x, Default::default()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let estimate = detailed.value().and_then(|v| v.relative_error());
    let li = logarithmic_integral(RealScalar::exp(x)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        x - root_hi,
        value,
        ((value - exact) / exact).abs(),
        estimate,
        ((naive(x) - exact) / exact).abs(),
        ((li - value) / value).abs(),
    ));
}

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "δ = x − x₀ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Ei(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Ei relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Estimated relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Naive-series relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative difference between li(eˣ) and Ei(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ei-zero" })
);
```

For the default inputs, `expint_ei` has relative error at most $5 \times 10^{-16}$ in every row down to $|\delta| = 10^{-12}$; even $\operatorname{Ei}(x_0 + 10^{-12}) = 3.896 \times 10^{-12}$ has relative error $2 \times 10^{-16}$.
The estimated relative error is $8\varepsilon = 1.78 \times 10^{-15}$ in every row and does not grow near the zero.
The interval's error model is proportional to the value (the kernel's $6\varepsilon|\operatorname{Ei}(x)|$ plus the calibration term $2\varepsilon$); two-word subtraction of the zero and evaluation of the logarithm using `ln_1p` keep actual errors within this model as well.
The naive series' relative error grows inversely with $|\delta|$, reaching $3.2 \times 10^{-6}$ at $|\delta| = 10^{-12}$.
Both $\gamma + \ln x$ and the series sum have magnitude about 0.41; rounding of order $10^{-17}$ in their subtraction becomes relative error against a value of only $3.9|\delta|$.

The last column shows that the identity $\operatorname{li}(e^{x}) = \operatorname{Ei}(x)$ does not hold to small relative error in this region.
Rounding $e^{x}$ to binary64 shifts the recovered $\ln$ argument by about $\varepsilon$ in absolute terms, changing the value by this amount times $\operatorname{Ei}'(x_0) = e^{x_0}/x_0$, so relative differences are of order $\varepsilon/|\delta|$.
Rows with 0 occur when rounding of $e^{x}$ happens to recover the original $x$ through $\ln$.
This is not an error in `logarithmic_integral`.
Near the zero $\mu$ of $\operatorname{li}$, rounding of input $x$ alone produces relative errors of this size because the condition number is large.
On the $\delta < 0$ side (`below-root`), values are negative; even at $|\delta| = 10^{-15}$, relative error of `expint_ei` remains $2 \times 10^{-16}$, whereas the naive series differs by 1.6%.

## Summary

$E_1$, $E_n$, $\operatorname{Ei}$, and `expint_3` target 8 ULP in binary64, assessed by absolute error near zeros; explicit zero subtraction also preserves small relative error near the zero of $\operatorname{Ei}$.
$\operatorname{li}$ and $\operatorname{Li}$ have no uniform ULP target; inspect options-form estimated errors when accuracy matters.
For large arguments whose values leave binary64 range, use `expint_en_scaled`, `expint_ei_scaled`, or options-form `scaled()` and `logarithmic()`.
`expint_3` is $\int_0^x e^{-t^3}dt$; call `expint_en(3, x)` when $E_3$ is needed.
The final table column shows that near the zero of $\operatorname{li}$, which uses the same $\operatorname{Ei}$ kernel, input rounding alone gives relative errors of order $\varepsilon/|\delta|$.
