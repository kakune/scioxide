Counting electrons, energy, and heat capacity in metals and semiconductors with the Fermi–Dirac distribution $1/(e^{t-x} + 1)$ produces semi-infinite integrals weighted by $t^j$.
For large negative $x = \mu/k_BT$ ($\mu$ is chemical potential, $k_B$ Boltzmann's constant, and $T$ temperature), the integral is exponentially small; for large positive values it grows as the power $x^{j+1}$.
A single formula inevitably leaves regions where a series converges slowly or an asymptotic series is unusable.
scioxide-special evaluates this integral using series, Chebyshev expansions, Sommerfeld expansions, and integer-order inversion formulas selected by argument region.
This page explains normalization, the two limits, evaluation in each region, accuracy guarantees, and APIs, and checks values using defining-integral quadrature and the derivative relation.
[Chemical potential of an electron gas](../../examples/fermi-gas-chemical-potential/) shows how to find chemical potential from electron density.

## Definition and conventions

Complete Fermi–Dirac integrals in scioxide-special are normalized by the gamma function (DLMF §25.12(iii)).

$$
F_j(x) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(j+1)} \int_0^\infty \frac{t^j}{e^{t-x} + 1}\,dt = -\mathrm{Li}_{j+1}(-e^x), \qquad j > -1
$$

$\mathrm{Li}_s$ is the polylogarithm.
With this normalization, differentiation with respect to $x$ lowers the order by one.

$$
\frac{d}{dx} F_j(x) = F_{j-1}(x)
$$

At $j = -1$, analytic continuation through this relation yields the logistic function $F_{-1}(x) = 1/(1 + e^{-x})$.
At $j = 0$, the value is $F_0(x) = \ln(1 + e^x)$.
For integer orders $j < -1$, the functions are derivatives of order $(-1-j)$ of the logistic function and are rational functions of $e^x$.
At the origin, the Dirichlet eta function $\eta(s) = (1 - 2^{1-s})\zeta(s)$ ($\zeta$ is the Riemann zeta function) gives $F_j(0) = \eta(j+1)$.

Some physics references call the unnormalized integral $\mathcal{F}_j(x) = \int_0^\infty t^j/(e^{t-x}+1)\,dt$, without $1/\Gamma(j+1)$, the Fermi–Dirac integral.
Convert using $\mathcal{F}_j = \Gamma(j+1)\,F_j$ in that convention.
For example, $\mathcal{F}_{1/2} = (\sqrt{\pi}/2)\,F_{1/2}$; omitting the conversion changes electron density by about 11%.

## Nondegenerate and degenerate limits

For $x \to -\infty$ (the nondegenerate limit), expand the integrand's denominator as a geometric series in $e^{x-t}$ and integrate term by term to obtain
$$
F_j(x) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty (-1)^{k+1} \frac{e^{kx}}{k^{j+1}} = e^x - \frac{e^{2x}}{2^{j+1}} + \cdots
$$


The leading term $e^x$ is the Boltzmann value; the second term is the first correction from Pauli exclusion.

For $x \to +\infty$ (the strongly degenerate limit), the distribution changes between 0 and 1 only near $t = x$, giving the following Sommerfeld expansion.

$$
F_j(x) = \frac{x^{j+1}}{\Gamma(j+2)} \left[ 1 + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 2\eta(2k)\,\frac{(j+1)j\cdots(j+2-2k)}{x^{2k}} \right] + O(e^{-x})
$$


The numerator product contains $2k$ factors descending from $j+1$ in steps of 1.
Since $2\eta(2) = \pi^2/6$, the first correction is $(\pi^2/6)\,j(j+1)/x^2$.
For integer orders, the product eventually vanishes and the bracket becomes a finite sum; for half-integer orders, it is a divergent asymptotic series.

For integer orders $j \ge 0$, the polylogarithm inversion formula (DLMF §25.12) gives the following identity, including the exponentially small term.

$$
F_j(x) = 2\sum_{p} \eta(j+1-p)\,\frac{x^p}{p!} + (-1)^j F_j(-x)
$$


$p$ ranges over $0 \le p \le j + 1$ of the same parity as $j + 1$, so coefficients involve only $\eta(0) = 1/2$ and eta values at even arguments, rational multiples of $\pi^{2k}$.
At $j = 0$ it gives $F_0(x) - F_0(-x) = x$; at $j = 1$ it gives $F_1(x) + F_1(-x) = x^2/2 + \pi^2/6$.

## Evaluation of Fermi–Dirac integrals

| Order $j$ | Argument range | Method | Method identifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| $-1$, $0$ | Entire domain | Closed forms $1/(1+e^{-x})$, $\max(x, 0) + \ln(1 + e^{-\lvert x\rvert})$ | `fermi_algebraic` |
| Integer $-101 \le j \le -2$ | Entire domain | Rational function of $e^{-\lvert x\rvert}$ with Eulerian-number coefficients; pole sum for $-j \ge 11$ and $\lvert x\rvert \le -1-j$ | `polylog_eulerian_rational`, `polylog_integer_poles`, etc. |
| $j \ge -1/2$ | $x < -1$ | Series $e^x \sum_{k \ge 1} (-1)^{k+1} e^{(k-1)x}/k^{j+1}$ | `fermi_fugacity_series` |
| $-1/2, 1/2, 3/2, 1, 2$ | $-1 \le x < 30$ | Chebyshev expansions on four intervals $[-1,1)$, $[1,4)$, $[4,10)$, $[10,30)$ (multiply by $x^{j+1}$ on the last) | `fermi_central_expansion` |
| $-1/2, 1/2, 3/2$ | $x \ge 30$ | Sommerfeld expansion truncated at the smallest term | `fermi_sommerfeld` |
| Integer $j \ge 1$ | $x > 0$ ($x \ge 30$ for $j \le 2$) | Inversion formula | `fermi_integer_inversion` |
| Integer $j \ge 3$ | $-1 \le x \le 0$ | Alternating series accelerated by Euler transformation | `fermi_euler_series` |

The series for $x < -1$ alternates with geometric ratio $e^x < e^{-1}$; even at $x = -1$, it reaches relative $\varepsilon$ (double-precision machine epsilon) in at most 35 terms.
Factoring out $e^x$ before summation retains the options-form logarithm $\ln F_j(x) = x + \ln(\cdots)$ even at $x < -745$, where $e^x$ falls below the ordinary-value range.

On $-1 \le x < 30$, the series ratio approaches 1 while Sommerfeld terms are not yet small enough.
scioxide-special covers this region with Chebyshev expansions on four intervals for each of the five supported orders.
Chebyshev expansions are polynomial approximations with nearly uniform error over an interval; fixed coefficient counts give fixed work.
On the last interval $[10, 30)$, expand $F_j(x)/x^{j+1}$ and multiply by $x^{j+1}$ so the coefficients need not represent the growth of the leading Sommerfeld term.

For half-integer orders at $x \ge 30$, sum Sommerfeld terms until their magnitudes stop decreasing or fall below relative $\varepsilon$.
Term magnitudes behave roughly as $(2k)!/x^{2k}$, reaching a minimum at $2k \approx x$; smaller $x$ leaves a larger minimum term.
The estimated error includes four times the first omitted term and the bound $4e^{-x}$ on the ignored $O(e^{-x})$ contribution.
At the boundary $x = 30$, estimated relative error is about $4 \times 10^{-13}$, and the difference from the reference for $F_{-1/2}$ is $5 \times 10^{-15}$ (enter $x = 30$ in the cell below to check).

For integer orders $j \ge 1$ at $x > 0$, sum the inversion formula's polynomial part logarithmically from low degree upward, then add the correction $F_j(-x)$ by series.
The correction is exponentially small and the polynomial part is finite, leaving no asymptotic truncation error.
When $x$ becomes small relative to $p$, omit remaining terms if their sum bound is below relative $\varepsilon$.
The polynomial has at most $(j+1)/2 + 1$ terms; large order and $x$ can exceed the work budget, giving `ConvergenceFailure` (with the default budget this occurs at $j = 2 \times 10^5$, $x = 10^5$).

Integer orders $j \ge 3$ have no central Chebyshev expansion on $-1 \le x \le 0$.
Here the terms of $F_j(x) = e^x \sum_{k \ge 0} (-1)^k e^{kx}/(k+1)^{j+1}$ form a completely monotone sequence, so sum the alternating series with Euler transformation.
Euler transformation sums the difference sequence $\Delta^n a_0$ with weights $2^{-(n+1)}$; row $n$ contributes at most $2^{-n} a_0$, reaching relative $\varepsilon$ in about 53 rows.
Rebuilding the difference table each row makes work proportional to the square of the row count, which never exceeds 64.

For negative integer order $j = -1 - n$ ($n \ge 1$), evaluate $F_j(x) = -\mathrm{Li}_{-n}(-e^x)$ using the polylogarithm kernel.
Evaluate $\mathrm{Li}_{-n}(z) = z\,A_n(z)/(1 - z)^{n+1}$ ($A_n$ has Eulerian-number coefficients $A(n, k)$), using $F_j(-x) = (-1)^{n+1} F_j(x)$ to stay on the side $\lvert z\rvert = e^{-\lvert x\rvert} \le 1$.
For $n \ge 10$ and $\lvert x\rvert \le n$, polynomial terms suffer substantial cancellation; instead sum the poles $\mathrm{Li}_{-n}(e^\mu) = n! \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} (2\pi i k - \mu)^{-n-1}$ ($\mu = x + i\pi$).

## Errors and value range

Nonfinite $x$ gives `NonFinite`.
When the ordinary value $F_j(x) \approx x^{j+1}/\Gamma(j+2)$ of order $j \ge 1/2$ exceeds the scalar range, standard forms return `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`), while options forms retain logarithmic and scaled values.
`fermi_dirac_half` with `f64` retains ordinary values up to $x \approx 4 \times 10^{205}$.
`fermi_dirac_mhalf`, `fermi_dirac_m1`, and `fermi_dirac_0` do not overflow for finite $x$.
For large negative $x$, the options-form logarithm remains available even when the ordinary value underflows.
`fermi_dirac_int` treats $j < -101$ as `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).

## Order-zero incomplete integral

Only the order-zero incomplete integral is provided.

$$
F_0(x, b) = \int_b^\infty \frac{dt}{e^{t-x} + 1} = \ln\bigl(1 + e^{x-b}\bigr), \qquad b \ge 0
$$

`fermi_dirac_inc_0(x, b)` forms $x - b$ and uses the same closed form as the complete integral $F_0$ (method identifier `fermi_algebraic`); at $b = 0$ it agrees with `fermi_dirac_0`.
$b < 0$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`); nonfinite $x$ and $b$ give `NonFinite`.
If $x - b$ overflows to $-\infty$, return the limit 0 with termination status `Limit`.
Incomplete integrals of other orders are not provided; integrate their defining integrands using scioxide-integrate quadrature when needed.

## Accuracy guarantees

Fermi–Dirac integrals have no uniform ULP target.
Comparison tests against independently generated high-precision references check the following.

- Value error is at most relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ plus absolute $5 \times 10^{-13}$ for `f64`, and relative $2 \times 10^{-4}$ plus absolute $5 \times 10^{-6}$ for `f32`, including incomplete integrals.
- Options-form estimated errors cover actual errors, allowing for reference rounding.
  Logarithmic and scaled error estimates are checked in the same way.
- For inputs producing subnormal results, differences from references stay within 8 ULP (16 ULP for `f32`).

Test tolerances are specification limits; typical errors are much smaller, as measured in the cell below.
Options-form estimates are derived from truncation and rounding analysis (`ModelBased`), not intervals with guaranteed containment.
Users assess whether the estimate meets their requirements using methods such as `relative_error()`.

## API

All functions accept only real arguments and return `f32` or `f64` values.
Standard forms return values only; options forms `*_with_options(.., options)` return `SpecialFunctionResult` with estimated errors, logarithmic and scaled forms, termination status, and algorithm identifier.
Scoped APIs `*_scoped(.., options, scope)` evaluate within a parent solve, reporting work as nested counts ([Three calling forms](oxref:special-api-call-forms)).

| Standard form | Options form | Scoped API | Computes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fermi_dirac_m1` | `fermi_dirac_m1_with_options` | None | $F_{-1}(x) = 1/(1+e^{-x})$ |
| `fermi_dirac_mhalf` | `fermi_dirac_mhalf_with_options` | `fermi_dirac_mhalf_scoped` | $F_{-1/2}(x)$ |
| `fermi_dirac_0` | `fermi_dirac_0_with_options` | None | $F_0(x) = \ln(1+e^x)$ |
| `fermi_dirac_half` | `fermi_dirac_half_with_options` | `fermi_dirac_half_scoped` | $F_{1/2}(x)$ |
| `fermi_dirac_1` | `fermi_dirac_1_with_options` | `fermi_dirac_1_scoped` | $F_1(x)$ |
| `fermi_dirac_3half` | `fermi_dirac_3half_with_options` | `fermi_dirac_3half_scoped` | $F_{3/2}(x)$ |
| `fermi_dirac_2` | `fermi_dirac_2_with_options` | `fermi_dirac_2_scoped` | $F_2(x)$ |
| `fermi_dirac_int(j, x)` | `fermi_dirac_int_with_options` | `fermi_dirac_int_scoped` | $F_j(x)$ for integer order $j \ge -101$ |
| `fermi_dirac_inc_0(x, b)` | `fermi_dirac_inc_0_with_options` | None | Incomplete integral $F_0(x, b)$ |

The options type `FermiDiracOptions` contains only the work budget `Budget` (default 65536), not an accuracy request.
The budget counts series, expansion, and polynomial terms and Chebyshev coefficients.
If insufficient, options forms return either a result with termination status `IterationLimit` or an error `ConvergenceFailure`; standard forms return `ConvergenceFailure` in either case.
$F_{-1}$, $F_0$, and $F_0(x, b)$ use closed forms with argument-independent work and have no scoped APIs.
A fixed-order `fermi_dirac_1(x)` and `fermi_dirac_int(1, x)` use the same method and return the same quantity.
Only the half-integer orders $-1/2$, $1/2$, and $3/2$ are provided; other noninteger orders are unsupported.
Alternatives are $-\mathrm{Li}_{j+1}(-e^x)$ through the real-order polylogarithm `polylog` (for $x < 709$ where $e^x$ does not overflow; [Polylogarithms of general order and the Lerch transcendent](../polylog-and-lerch/)) or quadrature of the defining integral.

## Checking Fermi–Dirac integrals

The next cell uses composite Boole quadrature of the defining integral as an independent reference.
Composite Boole quadrature divides the interval into $m$ equal subintervals, with the count a multiple of 4, sums point values with weights $7, 32, 12, 32, 14, 32, 12, 32, 14, \dots, 32, 7$, and multiplies by $2/45$ times the step $h$.
This equals one Richardson extrapolation of composite Simpson rules with steps $h$ and $2h$, reducing error as $h^6$ for smooth integrands.
To reduce rounding in long sums, use Neumaier compensated summation, retaining low-order digits lost in each addition in a separate variable and restoring them at the end.
The following definitions block contains this helper.

```rust
//| id: special-fermi-dirac-boole
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Composite Boole quadrature and compensated summation"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
/// Independent reference for ∫ₐᵇ g(t) dt using composite Boole quadrature. m is the subinterval count, a multiple of 4.
/// Use Neumaier compensated summation and propagate failures of g unchanged.
pub(crate) fn boole(
    mut g: impl FnMut(f64) -> Result<f64, String>,
    a: f64,
    b: f64,
    m: usize,
) -> Result<f64, String> {
    let h = (b - a) / m as f64;
    let (mut sum, mut compensation) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for i in 0..=m {
        let weight = if i == 0 || i == m {
            7.0
        } else if i % 2 == 1 {
            32.0
        } else if i % 4 == 2 {
            12.0
        } else {
            14.0
        };
        let term = weight * g(a + i as f64 * h)?;
        let next = sum + term;
        compensation += if sum.abs() >= term.abs() {
            (sum - next) + term
        } else {
            (term - next) + sum
        };
        sum = next;
    }
    Ok((sum + compensation) * 2.0 * h / 45.0)
}
```

At input $x$, the cell evaluates $F_j(x)$ of order $j = -1, -1/2, 0, 1/2, 1, 3/2, 2, 3$ with options forms, displaying relative estimated errors and method identifiers.
The reference substitutes $t = u^2$ to remove the endpoint $t^{-1/2}$ and evaluates the defining integral $\int_0^U 2u^{2j+1}/(e^{u^2 - x} + 1)\,du$ ($U^2 = \max(x, 0) + 50$) by composite Boole quadrature with 20000 subintervals, then divides by $\Gamma(j+1)$.
The contribution beyond the upper limit is negligible, about relative $e^{-50} \approx 2 \times 10^{-22}$.
Since the function at $j = -1$ has no integral representation here, use the logistic expression with `f64` intrinsic methods as the reference.
The final column checks the derivative relation $F_j' = F_{j-1}$ by comparing the centered difference $(F_j(x+h) - F_j(x-h))/(2h)$ ($h = 10^{-4}$) with scioxide-special's $F_{j-1}$.
Different orders use different methods, so this relation checks neighboring-order implementations against each other.
Centered differences retain truncation error $h^2 F_j'''/6$ and rounding error $\varepsilon F_j/h$; divide the discrepancy by the larger of $F_{j-1}$ and $F_j$.
$F_{-3/2}$ is not provided, leaving the $j = -1/2$ row empty.

    fermi_dirac_mhalf_with_options,

let order_3 = |x: f64, options: FermiDiracOptions| {
    scioxide_special::fermi_dirac_int_with_options(3, x, options)
};
let sqrt_pi = std::f64::consts::PI.sqrt();

let orders: [(f64, Detailed, f64, Option<usize>); 8] = [
    (-1.0, fermi_dirac_m1_with_options, 1.0, None),
    (-0.5, fermi_dirac_mhalf_with_options, sqrt_pi, None),
    (0.0, fermi_dirac_0_with_options, 1.0, Some(0)),
    (0.5, fermi_dirac_half_with_options, sqrt_pi / 2.0, Some(1)),
    (1.0, fermi_dirac_1_with_options, 1.0, Some(2)),
    (1.5, fermi_dirac_3half_with_options, 0.75 * sqrt_pi, Some(3)),
    (2.0, fermi_dirac_2_with_options, 2.0, Some(4)),
    (3.0, order_3, 6.0, Some(6)),

        (-1.0, _) => Some(fermi_dirac_int(-2, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?),

For the default $x = 2$, $F_{-1}$ and $F_0$ use closed forms, $F_{-1/2}$ through $F_2$ use central Chebyshev expansions, and $F_3$ uses inversion; relative differences from references are at most $2.1 \times 10^{-16}$.
Estimated relative errors range from $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ for closed forms to $9.6 \times 10^{-14}$ for inversion, one to three orders of magnitude above actual differences.
At $x = -8$ (`boltzmann`), evaluations with $j \ge -1/2$ switch to the series.
The values for every order lie slightly below $e^{-8} = 3.3546 \times 10^{-4}$, approaching $e^x$ as order increases, as predicted by $F_j \approx e^x - e^{2x}/2^{j+1}$.
At $x = -0.5$ (`euler`), only $F_3$ switches to Euler transformation, with relative reference difference $1.9 \times 10^{-16}$.
At $x = 40$ (`degenerate`), half-integer orders switch to `fermi_sommerfeld` and integer orders to `fermi_integer_inversion`, with relative reference differences at most $1.1 \times 10^{-15}$.
Estimated relative errors for these methods range from $4 \times 10^{-14}$ to $2.4 \times 10^{-13}$, above the $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ of closed forms $F_{-1}$ and $F_0$.

## Summary

scioxide-special normalizes Fermi–Dirac integrals with $1/\Gamma(j+1)$ to obtain $F_j' = F_{j-1}$; multiply by $\Gamma(j+1)$ when comparing with references using unnormalized $\mathcal{F}_j$.
Evaluation uses series for $x < -1$, Chebyshev expansions for $-1 \le x < 30$, and Sommerfeld expansions for $x \ge 30$; integer orders use inversion's finite sum for $x > 0$ and Euler transformation for $-1 \le x \le 0$ at $j \ge 3$.
There is no uniform ULP specification; comparison tests check tolerances of about relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ in `f64` and estimated-error coverage, so inspect options-form relative estimated errors when accuracy matters.
Use options-form logarithms when large $x$ causes overflow or large negative values cause underflow.
See [Chemical potential of an electron gas](../../examples/fermi-gas-chemical-potential/) for passing $F_{1/2}$ and its derivative $F_{-1/2}$ to root finding, and [Debye functions, transport integrals, and synchrotron functions](../thermal-and-radiation-integrals/) for integrals with the same statistical weights.
