The gamma function $\Gamma(x)$ extends factorials to real and complex numbers and appears in distribution normalization constants, statistical-mechanical partition functions, and hypergeometric-series coefficients.
Its value grows faster than exponentially with the argument, has poles at nonpositive integers, and alternates sign between intervals on the negative real axis.
Its logarithmic derivatives, digamma and polygamma functions, appear in series sums and expectations of statistics; the Pochhammer symbol, a gamma-function ratio, gives hypergeometric-series coefficients.
This page explains these functions' definitions and properties, the region-dependent evaluation methods in `scioxide-special`, accuracy guarantees, and API choices.
[Incomplete gamma functions](../incomplete-gamma/) covers integrals truncated at an intermediate point and gamma-distribution cumulative probabilities and quantiles.

## Definition and basic properties

For $\operatorname{Re} z > 0$, the gamma function is defined by Euler's integral of the second kind (DLMF 5.2.1).

$$
\Gamma(z) = \int_0^\infty t^{z-1} e^{-t} \, dt
$$

Integration by parts gives the fundamental recurrence $\Gamma(z+1) = z\,\Gamma(z)$; together with $\Gamma(1) = 1$, it yields $\Gamma(n+1) = n!$ for nonnegative integer $n$.
Applying the recurrence leftward analytically continues $\Gamma$ throughout the complex plane except for simple poles at $z = 0, -1, -2, \dots$.
$\Gamma$ has neither zeros nor branch cuts.

Euler's reflection formula (DLMF 5.5.3) relates values on the negative side to those on the positive side.

$$
\Gamma(z)\,\Gamma(1-z) = \frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)} \qquad (z \notin \mathbb{Z})
$$

On the real interval $(-n, -n+1)$ ($n = 1, 2, \dots$), the sign of $\Gamma(x)$ is $(-1)^n$.
`gamma` returns values with this sign for negative noninteger $x$ and returns `DomainError` (`Pole`) at nonpositive integers.
NaN and infinite inputs give `NonFinite`.

The binary64 maximum is about $1.80 \times 10^{308}$; $\Gamma(x)$ exceeds it at $x \approx 171.62$ ($x \approx 35.04$ for binary32).
Beyond this, `gamma` returns `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`), but the options form `gamma_with_options` retains a signed logarithmic form and a power-of-two-scaled value ([First computation](oxref:special-index-first-computation)).

## Log-gamma function

Calculations involving ratios of large gamma values, such as likelihoods and partition functions, work directly with logarithms.
For real $x$, `ln_gamma(x)` returns $\ln|\Gamma(x)|$, discarding the sign.
The values satisfy $\ln\Gamma(1) = \ln\Gamma(2) = 0$; evaluation preserves relative accuracy even near these two zeros, as explained below.
To obtain both sign and logarithm, extract `sign()` and `log_magnitude()` from the `LogValue` returned by `gamma_with_options(x, options)?.logarithmic()`.

For complex $z$, `ln_gamma` returns the analytic $\log\Gamma(z)$ that is real on the positive real axis.
Its cut is $(-\infty, 0]$; imaginary parts $+0$ and $-0$ select the upper and lower sides.
The imaginary part is not reduced to $(-\pi, \pi]$, so the returned logarithm can differ from the principal logarithm of $\Gamma(z)$ by an integer multiple of $2\pi i$.
For example, the imaginary part of $\log\Gamma(-2.5 + 0i)$ is $-3\pi$.

## Digamma and polygamma functions

The logarithmic derivative of the gamma function is the digamma function (DLMF 5.2.2).

$$
\psi(z) = \frac{d}{dz}\ln\Gamma(z) = \frac{\Gamma'(z)}{\Gamma(z)}, \qquad \psi(1) = -\gamma
$$

$\gamma \approx 0.5772$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant.
$\psi$ satisfies the recurrence $\psi(z+1) = \psi(z) + 1/z$ (DLMF 5.5.2) and reflection formula $\psi(1-z) - \psi(z) = \pi\cot(\pi z)$ (DLMF 5.5.4), with simple poles at $z = 0, -1, -2, \dots$.
It has one positive real zero $x_0 \approx 1.4616$ and, on the negative real axis, one zero between each pair of adjacent poles.

The polygamma function $\psi^{(n)}$ is the derivative of order $n$ of digamma; for $n \ge 1$ it can be expressed using the Hurwitz zeta function $\zeta(s, z) = \sum_{k \ge 0} (k + z)^{-s}$ (DLMF 5.15.1, 25.11.12).

$$
\psi^{(n)}(z) = \frac{d^{n+1}}{dz^{n+1}}\ln\Gamma(z) = (-1)^{n+1}\, n!\, \zeta(n+1, z)
$$

$\psi^{(n)}$ has poles of order $n+1$ at nonpositive integers, with magnitudes growing on the scale of $n!$.
`order` in `polygamma(order, z)` is $n$ (`usize`); `order = 0` returns the same value as `digamma`.
On the negative real axis, use the reflection formula $\psi^{(n)}(1-z) + (-1)^{n+1}\psi^{(n)}(z) = (-1)^n \pi \frac{d^n}{dz^n}\cot(\pi z)$ (DLMF 5.15.6).

## Pochhammer symbol

The rising factorial (Pochhammer symbol) is defined as a ratio of gamma functions (DLMF 5.2.5).

$$
(a)_x = \frac{\Gamma(a+x)}{\Gamma(a)}
$$

For nonnegative integer $x = n$, it is the polynomial $(a)_n = a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)$, with $(a)_0 = 1$.
For negative integer $x = -n$, it becomes $(a)_{-n} = 1/[(a-1)(a-2)\cdots(a-n)]$.
Where only $\Gamma(a)$ has a pole ($a$ is a nonpositive integer and $a + x$ is not), the value is 0.
If both $a$ and $a + x$ are nonpositive integers, poles cancel; for positive integer $x$ or negative integer shifts satisfying $\lvert x\rvert \le 128$, return the product above.
Other points where $a + x$ is a nonpositive integer give `DomainError` (`Pole`).

For integer shifts $\lvert x\rvert \le 128$, `pochhammer(a, x)` computes the value as a product in double-word arithmetic, with about 106 significand bits.
For other shifts, form the gamma-function ratio logarithmically.
If the shift $x$ is at most 1% of the distance from $a$ to the nearest pole, capped at 1, compute $\ln\Gamma(a+x) - \ln\Gamma(a) = \int_a^{a+x} \psi(t)\,dt$ by 8-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature.
This form retains $x$ even for $x$ so small that $a + x$ rounds back to $a$.
For real $a$ and $a + x$ both at least 32, rearrange the Stirling-series difference into $x\ln a + (a + x - \tfrac{1}{2})\operatorname{log1p}(x/a) - x$ plus a correction difference, treating shift $x$ separately from base point $a$.
Directly subtracting $\ln\Gamma(a+x) - \ln\Gamma(a)$ for large $a$ and small $x$ would subtract nearly equal large logarithms and lose digits.
For other real arguments, evaluate and subtract $\ln\lvert\Gamma\rvert$ in double-word arithmetic, retaining negative-side signs separately.
For complex arguments with small real part, first shift by recurrence to at least $4.5$, then use differences of complex Lanczos approximations or the quadrature above.

As $x \to 0$, $(a)_x \to 1$, so forming $((a)_x - 1)/x$ by subtraction loses meaningful digits to cancellation.
`pochhammer_relative(a, x)` computes this quantity without cancellation using differences of $\exp$ evaluated with $\operatorname{expm1}$ and similar methods, returning the limit $\psi(a)$ at $x = 0$.
Unlike other gamma-family functions, these two options forms accept `CombinatorialOptions`, shared with combinatorial functions.
Neither real nor complex arguments have a uniform ULP specification; options forms return estimated errors.

## Evaluation of real gamma functions

Real `gamma`, `ln_gamma`, and `digamma` use fixed-work kernels evaluated in binary64 and rounded once to the result type.
binary32 arguments are also evaluated in binary64.
These functions have no scoped APIs because their work is fixed.

`gamma` selects methods by argument region.

| Argument range | Method |
|---|---|
| Integer $x = 1, \dots, 21$ | Exact values from a factorial table |
| $0 < x < 0.5$ | Recurrence $\Gamma(x) = \Gamma(x+1)/x$ |
| $0.5 \le x < 16$ | Shift to $[1, 2]$ by recurrence and evaluate the Cephes rational approximation |
| $16 \le x \le 171.625$ | Stirling series, using a degree-18 polynomial for the correction $\exp(\cdot) - 1$; the first omitted term is below $2 \times 10^{-23}$ |
| $x > 171.625$ | Overflow |
| $-0.5 < x < 0$ | Recurrence $\Gamma(x) = \Gamma(x+1)/x$ |
| $-16 < x \le -0.5$ | Reflection formula $\Gamma(x) = \pi / [\sin(\pi x)\,(-x)\,\Gamma(-x)]$ |
| $-200 \le x \le -16$ | Reflection formula; express $\Gamma(-x)$ in rational Lanczos form and form powers and exponentials as ratios of products scaled by powers of 2 |
| $x < -200$ | Value falls below binary64 range and becomes signed zero |

`ln_gamma` selects regions by zero locations rather than value magnitude.
For $x \ge 16$, use the Stirling series.
After recurrence reduction to $1 \le x < 3$, use rational approximations on $[1, 1.5]$, $(1.5, 2)$, and $(2, 3)$, factoring out the factors for nearby zeros ($(x-1)(x-2)$ on $[1, 2]$, $x - 2$ on $(2, 3)$) to preserve relative accuracy.
For $0.5 \le x < 1$, evaluate the near-1 approximation directly in $x - 1$; use $\ln\Gamma(x+1) - \ln x$ for $x < 0.5$ and $-\ln x$ for $x < 10^{-16}$.
For negative $x$, use binary64 reflection only when $x \le -8$ and cancellation among the three reflection terms is small.
Otherwise use double-word arithmetic; for $x > -32$, shift by recurrence to $[0, 1)$, and farther left form the reflection formula's $\sin$ using a series.
$\ln|\Gamma(x)|$ also has negative-real zeros, so this avoids subtraction in the reflection formula near them.

For $x \ge 16$, `digamma` uses eight terms of the asymptotic expansion $\psi(x) \sim \ln x - \frac{1}{2x} - \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{B_{2k}}{2k\,x^{2k}}$ (DLMF 5.11.2).
For smaller positive $x$, shift by recurrence to $[1, 2]$, then evaluate a rational approximation factoring out the displacement $x - x_0$ from the positive zero $x_0 \approx 1.4616$.
Subtract $x_0$ using a three-word constant, retaining relative accuracy near the zero.
For negative $x$, use reflection; its error is proportional to the term magnitudes $|\psi(1-x)| + |\pi\cot(\pi x)|$, rather than the value of $\psi$.
Relative accuracy therefore deteriorates near negative-real zeros.

For positive $x$, `polygamma` directly sums the initial Hurwitz-series terms and completes the tail with Euler–Maclaurin summation, both in double-word arithmetic.
For $n \le 4$, directly sum $\lceil 16 - x\rceil$ terms until the argument reaches 16; otherwise sum $n + 16$ terms.
Including multiplication by power-of-two-scaled $n!$, work grows proportionally to order $n$ and counts toward the `GammaOptions` budget.
For negative $x$, form the reflection term $\frac{d^n}{dz^n}\cot(\pi z)$ as a polynomial in $\cot$ for $n \le 20$ and as paired partial-fraction sums toward the poles for $n > 20$.
Both use workspaces whose size is independent of $n$.

### Stirling series

The Stirling series used for $x \ge 16$ is an asymptotic expansion derived from Euler–Maclaurin summation (DLMF 5.11.1).

$$
\ln \Gamma(x) \sim \left( x - \frac{1}{2} \right) \ln x - x + \frac{1}{2} \ln(2\pi) + \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k-1)\, x^{2k-1}}
$$

$B_{2k}$ are Bernoulli numbers ($B_2 = 1/6$, $B_4 = -1/30$, $B_6 = 1/42$, …), with the following initial corrections.

$$
\ln \Gamma(x) = \left( x - \frac{1}{2} \right) \ln x - x + \frac{1}{2} \ln(2\pi) + \frac{1}{12x} - \frac{1}{360x^3} + \frac{1}{1260x^5} - \cdots
$$

This series diverges.
For fixed $x$, successive terms initially decrease and then begin growing.
For real $x > 0$, the truncation error is smaller than the first omitted term and has the same sign (DLMF 5.11(ii)).
Larger $x$ needs fewer terms for binary64 accuracy, so the implementation uses this series only for $x \ge 16$ and shifts smaller arguments by recurrence.
Shifting $x$ rightward by recurrence adds some multiplication rounding but rapidly reduces series truncation error.

The next cell manually computes Stirling partial sums and plots errors using `ln_gamma` as the reference.
The input is the starting argument $x_0$; the horizontal logarithmic axis runs from $x_0$ to $x_0 + 40$.
The table compares partial-sum errors at $x = x_0, 2x_0, 4x_0, 8x_0$ with the magnitude of the first omitted term.
Errors smaller than the first omitted term agree with the error estimate above.

```rust
//| id: special-gamma-stirling
//| ref: special-gamma-stirling
//| title: "Compare Stirling partial sums with ln Γ(x)"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   minimum_x: {"type":"range","label":"Starting x₀ [-]","description":"Move the argument range for partial-sum comparison to the right.","min":1,"max":20,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"handoff","label":"x₀ = 16","description":"Compare from the point where the implementation switches to Stirling's series.","inputs":{"minimum_x":16}}
//|   - {"id":"small","label":"x₀ = 1","description":"For small x, adding corrections reduces error slowly.","inputs":{"minimum_x":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.7678e-2,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":9.9852e-5,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/3/5","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|   - case: handoff
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1.3e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":7.5688e-10,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: small
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"errors","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":8.1061e-2,"rtol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_special::ln_gamma;

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let half_log_two_pi = 0.5 * RealScalar::ln(2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI);
// Correction coefficients B₂ₖ/(2k(2k−1)) = 1/12, −1/360, 1/1260, −1/1680.
let coefficients = [1.0 / 12.0, -1.0 / 360.0, 1.0 / 1260.0, -1.0 / 1680.0];
// Partial sum with `terms` corrections (manual calculation, compared with ln_gamma).
let partial = |x: f64, terms: usize| -> f64 {
    let mut value = (x - 0.5) * RealScalar::ln(x) - x + half_log_two_pi;
    for (k, c) in coefficients.iter().take(terms).enumerate() {
        value += c / RealScalar::powi(x, 2 * k as i32 + 1);
    }
    value
};
// Magnitude of the first omitted term.
let omitted = |x: f64, terms: usize| -> f64 {
    coefficients[terms].abs() / RealScalar::powi(x, 2 * terms as i32 + 1)
};

let names = ["Leading part only", "Through 1/(12x)", "Through 1/(360x³)"];
let mut series = Vec::new();
let mut dropped = 0; // Number of exactly zero errors that cannot be plotted logarithmically
for terms in 0..3 {
    let mut measured = Vec::new();
    let mut theory = Vec::new();
    for index in 0..=64 {
        let x = minimum_x + 40.0 * f64::from(index) / 64.0;
        let error = (partial(x, terms) - ln_gamma(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?).abs();
        if error > 0.0 {
            measured.push([x, error]);
        } else {
            dropped += 1;
        }
        theory.push([x, omitted(x, terms)]);
    }
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": names[terms], "points": measured }));
    series.push(serde_json::json!({
        "name": format!("Omitted term ({})", names[terms]),
        "points": theory,
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Argument x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Difference from ln Γ(x) [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for scale in [1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 8.0] {
    let x = minimum_x * scale;
    let reference = ln_gamma(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let error = |terms: usize| (partial(x, terms) - reference).abs();
    rows.push((
        x,
        error(0),
        omitted(x, 0),
        error(1),
        omitted(x, 1),
        error(2),
        omitted(x, 2),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "x [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 3 } },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Leading-part error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "1/(12x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "One-correction error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "1/(360x³) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Two-correction error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "1/(1260x⁵) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "errors" })
);
emit_text!(format!("Points omitted from the chart because error is exactly 0: {dropped}"));
```

For the default $x_0 = 3$, the leading-part error $2.77 \times 10^{-2}$ is nearly $1/(12x) = 2.78 \times 10^{-2}$; adding $1/(12x)$ lowers it to $9.99 \times 10^{-5}$, below $1/(360x^3) = 1.03 \times 10^{-4}$.
In every table column, error is smaller than the first omitted term; doubling $x$ reduces the three errors by factors of about 1/2, 1/8, and 1/32.
This agrees with truncation errors proportional to $x^{-1}$, $x^{-3}$, and $x^{-5}$.

Starting at $x_0 = 16$, the two-correction error $5.68 \times 10^{-14}$ at $x = 128$ exceeds the omitted term $2.31 \times 10^{-14}$.
This error is 1 ULP ($2^{-44} \approx 5.68 \times 10^{-14}$) at $\ln\Gamma(128) \approx 491.6$; the rounding difference between the partial sum and `ln_gamma` exceeds truncation error.
At large $x$, the curves also flatten at this rounding level.
For $x_0 = 1$, the two-correction error $5.06 \times 10^{-4}$ at $x = 1$ is only about 1/160 of the leading-part error $8.11 \times 10^{-2}$.
At small $x$, extra terms do not reduce error enough, so the implementation shifts to $x \ge 16$ by recurrence before using the series.

## Complex gamma functions and the Lanczos approximation

Complex `gamma`, `ln_gamma`, and `digamma` evaluate the Lanczos approximation logarithmically for $\operatorname{Re} z \ge 1/2$.
Set $y = z - 1$; with $g = 6.024680040776729583740234375$ and 13 coefficients $c_0, \dots, c_{12}$, evaluate

$$
\log\Gamma(y+1) = \tfrac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi) + \left(y + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\log\left(y + g + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) - \left(y + g + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) + \log\left[c_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{12}\frac{c_k}{y + k}\right]
$$


This $g$ equals that of Boost.Math's 13-term approximation (`lanczos13m53`); coefficients $c_k$ come from its rational function's partial-fraction decomposition divided by $\sqrt{2\pi}$.
The real kernel's reflection formula ($x \le -16$) and incomplete-gamma prefactors use the same approximation in rational form.
For $\operatorname{Re} z < 1/2$, use reflection, retaining the continuous branch of $\log\sin(\pi z)$ without reducing to a principal value.
Using a principal logarithm would lose the $2\pi$ rotations of $\log\Gamma$ along the negative axis.
`gamma` returns $\exp(\log\Gamma(z))$, and `digamma` returns the derivative of the same approximation.
Complex `polygamma` uses the Hurwitz zeta function ([Riemann and Hurwitz zeta functions and the Dirichlet eta function](../zeta-functions/)).
Complex arguments have no uniform ULP specification; options forms return estimated errors.

## Accuracy guarantees

For real arguments, the following ULP targets are checked against independently generated high-precision reference data.
ULP is the spacing between adjacent floating-point values at the result; targets apply over the tested range.

| Function (real) | binary64 | binary32 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gamma`, `ln_gamma`, `digamma`, `polygamma` | 4 ULP | 1 ULP | Maxima in a 4 160-point sample: 3.7, 1.9, 2.1, 0.7 ULP. `ln_gamma` includes neighborhoods of zeros |
| `digamma`, `polygamma` for $x < 0$ | 4 units of term magnitude | — | Absolute error relative to the sum of absolute reflection terms; relative accuracy deteriorates near zeros |
| `pochhammer`, `pochhammer_relative` | No uniform specification | No uniform specification | Tested against independent references and identities; options forms return estimated errors |

The maxima in the 4 160-point sample are measurements over the tested range and do not extend the target range.
[Incomplete gamma functions](oxref:special-incgamma-accuracy) gives targets for incomplete gamma functions and their inverses.
Complex arguments have no uniform ULP specification; use options-form estimated errors (the error in `ValueWithError`, not an enclosure).

## API

| Function | Standard-form arguments | Options form | Scoped API | Complex | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gamma` | `x` | `gamma_with_options` | None | Yes | $\Gamma(x)$ |
| `ln_gamma` | `x` | `ln_gamma_with_options` | None | Yes | $\ln\lvert\Gamma(x)\rvert$; $\log\Gamma(z)$ for complex arguments |
| `digamma` | `x` | `digamma_with_options` | None | Yes | $\psi(x)$ |
| `polygamma` | `order: usize, z` | `polygamma_with_options` | `polygamma_scoped` | Yes | $\psi^{(n)}(z)$ |
| `pochhammer` | `a, x` | `pochhammer_with_options` | None | Yes | $(a)_x$ |
| `pochhammer_relative` | `a, x` | `pochhammer_relative_with_options` | None | Yes | $((a)_x - 1)/x$ |

Standard forms return only values; options forms accept options and return `SpecialFunctionResult`.
Options results contain ordinary values and estimated errors, scaled and logarithmic values, termination status, algorithm identifiers, and work counts; ordinary values agree bitwise with standard forms.
Scoped APIs return the same results as options forms, report work to the parent execution context as nested counts, and follow the parent's cancellation and deadline.
Fixed-work functions `gamma`, `ln_gamma`, `digamma`, `pochhammer`, and `pochhammer_relative` have no scoped APIs.

Options forms use `GammaOptions` for gamma and polygamma functions and `CombinatorialOptions` for Pochhammer symbols.
Both contain only a work budget (`Budget`), defaulting to 65 536 units and changed with `GammaOptions::new(budget)` or `with_iteration_budget`.
The budget is not an accuracy request; evaluations reaching it give `ConvergenceFailure`.
Fixed-work functions also return this error without starting evaluation if their known work exceeds the budget.

## Summary and further reading

$\Gamma(x)$ overflows beyond $x \approx 171.62$ in binary64; for large arguments and ratios, use `ln_gamma` or the signed logarithmic form in an options result.
Real gamma-family functions select rational approximations, Stirling series, and reflection formulas around zeros and poles, targeting 4 ULP in binary64 over the tested range.
On the negative real axis, cancellation between reflection terms degrades digamma and polygamma relative accuracy near zeros; interpret estimated errors as absolute errors scaled to term magnitudes.
The Pochhammer symbol forms gamma ratios logarithmically without rounding $a + x$; as $x \to 0$, compute the difference $((a)_x - 1)/x$ with `pochhammer_relative`.
See [Incomplete gamma functions](../incomplete-gamma/) for truncated integrals and gamma quantiles, [Beta and regularized incomplete beta functions](../beta-and-error-functions/) for beta functions, and [Factorials and binomial coefficients](../combinatorial-numbers/) for integer-argument factorials and binomial coefficients.
