Hypergeometric series contain many functions as special cases, from exponentials, logarithms, and inverse trigonometric functions to Legendre and Bessel functions and incomplete beta and gamma functions.
Many special-function formulas can be expressed hypergeometrically, so use this page to check values from other pages or evaluate combinations without dedicated functions.
Defining series converge only in limited regions, converge slowly, or suffer cancellation; implementations divide the domain using transformations, connection formulas, and numerical continuation along differential equations.
This page explains hypergeometric-series structure, region selection for each function, accuracy guarantees, and APIs; two cells examine the regions of $_2F_1$ and cancellation in $_1F_1$.
[Riemann and Hurwitz zeta functions and the Dirichlet eta function](../zeta-functions/) covers Riemann and Hurwitz zeta functions.

## Generalized hypergeometric series

The generalized hypergeometric function $_pF_q$ is defined by a series using Pochhammer symbols (rising factorials) $(a)_k = a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1) = \Gamma(a+k)/\Gamma(a)$ (DLMF 16.2.1).

$$
{}_pF_q(a_1, \ldots, a_p; b_1, \ldots, b_q; z) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(a_1)_k \cdots (a_p)_k}{(b_1)_k \cdots (b_q)_k}\,\frac{z^k}{k!}
$$

$a_i$ are numerator parameters and $b_j$ denominator parameters.
Successive terms have the following rational ratio, allowing each term to be formed from its predecessor with $O(p + q)$ work.

$$
\frac{t_{k+1}}{t_k} = \frac{(a_1 + k)\cdots(a_p + k)}{(b_1 + k)\cdots(b_q + k)}\,\frac{z}{k + 1}
$$

As $k \to \infty$ the term ratio behaves as $z\,k^{p-q-1}$; nonterminating series therefore converge for every $z$ when $p \le q$, converge for $|z| < 1$ when $p = q + 1$, and diverge at $z \ne 0$ when $p > q + 1$.
A nonpositive integer numerator parameter $-n$ makes $(a_i)_k$ zero at $k > n$, terminating the series as a polynomial of degree $n$.
A nonpositive integer denominator parameter $-m$ eventually makes $(b_j)_k$ zero, leaving an undefined term and giving `DomainError` (`Pole`).
However, if a nonpositive integer numerator parameter $-n$ exceeds $-m$ and terminates the series before the zero denominator, evaluate the polynomial.

`hyp_pfq(a, b, z)` generates terms by forward recurrence and accumulates them with Kahan compensated summation, stopping when a remainder bound from the term-ratio bound falls below the rounding level.
The error estimate combines the truncated tail with rounding proportional to the sum of absolute term values $\sum_k |t_k|$.
When terms cancel to a sum many orders smaller than the terms, the relative error estimate grows accordingly.
For a nonterminating series, $p = q + 1$ with $|z| \ge 1$, or $p > q + 1$ (except $_2F_0$), returns `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
Forms $_0F_1$, $_1F_1$, $_2F_1$, and $_2F_0$ use the same continuation as the named functions below, allowing evaluation outside the series convergence region.

## Named functions and identities

The following low-order families have dedicated evaluation and analytic continuation.

| Function | Definition and main relations | Convergence region and cut |
|---|---|---|
| `hyp0f1(b, z)` | $_0F_1(;b;z)$; $J_\nu(x) = \frac{(x/2)^\nu}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\,{}_0F_1(;\nu+1;-x^2/4)$ (DLMF 10.16.9) | Entire, no cut |
| `hyp1f1(a, b, z)` | Kummer function $M(a, b, z) = {}_1F_1(a;b;z)$; Kummer transformation $M(a,b,z) = e^{z}M(b-a,b,-z)$ (DLMF 13.2.39) | Entire, no cut |
| `hyperu(a, b, z)` | Tricomi function $U(a,b,z) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_0^\infty e^{-zt}t^{a-1}(1+t)^{b-a-1}dt$ (DLMF 13.4.4); $U \sim z^{-a}$ | Cut $(-\infty, 0]$ |
| `hyp2f0(a, b, z)` | $_2F_0(a,b;;z) = (-1/z)^{a}U(a, 1+a-b, -1/z)$ (DLMF 13.6.21) | Asymptotic series; real types require $z < 0$ |
| `hyp2f1(a, b, c, z)` | Gauss function $_2F_1(a,b;c;z)$; at $z = 1$, Gauss summation $\frac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c-a-b)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}$ ($c - a - b > 0$) | $\lvert z\rvert < 1$, cut $[1, \infty)$ |
| `hyp2f1_regularized(a, b, c, z)` | $_2F_1(a,b;c;z)/\Gamma(c)$; entire in $c$ | Same as `hyp2f1` |

$U$ solves Kummer's equation $zw'' + (b - z)w' - aw = 0$ with behavior $z^{-a}$ as $z \to +\infty$ and pairs with $M$ to form the general solution.
Whereas $M$ grows as $e^{z}z^{a-b}$, $U$ grows or decays only algebraically, so it serves as the decaying, minimal solution on the positive real axis.
$U(a, b, 0)$ equals $\Gamma(1-b)/\Gamma(a-b+1)$ at $\operatorname{Re} b < 1$; for $\operatorname{Re} b \ge 1$, the origin is singular.

The regularized form of $_2F_1$ remains finite even at $c = 0, -1, -2, \ldots$.
For $c = -m$, coefficients $1/\Gamma(c + k)$ of the first $m + 1$ terms vanish, giving the following evaluation formula (DLMF §15.2).

$$
\frac{{}_2F_1(a, b; -m; z)}{\Gamma(-m)} = \frac{(a)_{m+1}(b)_{m+1}}{(m+1)!}\,z^{m+1}\,{}_2F_1(a+m+1, b+m+1; m+2; z)
$$

## Regions of Gauss's hypergeometric function

The series for $_2F_1$ converges for $|z| < 1$, but its ratio approaches 1 and convergence slows as $z$ approaches 1; for $|z| \ge 1$ it diverges.
Map to an argument near the origin by transformation, connect to a pair of series in $1/z$, or carry values by numerically solving the differential equation.

Pfaff's transformation (DLMF 15.8.1) maps the argument to $w = z/(z-1)$.

$$
{}_2F_1(a, b; c; z) = (1-z)^{-a}\,{}_2F_1\!\left(a, c-b; c; \frac{z}{z-1}\right) = (1-z)^{-b}\,{}_2F_1\!\left(c-a, b; c; \frac{z}{z-1}\right)
$$

It maps $z \in [-9, -1/2)$ to $w \in [1/3, 9/10)$.
As $z$ becomes more negative, $w$ approaches 1 and the transformed series also slows.
For $z < -9$, use a connection formula splitting the result into two series in $1/z$ (DLMF 15.8.2).

$$
\frac{{}_2F_1(a,b;c;z)}{\Gamma(c)} = \frac{\Gamma(b-a)\,(-z)^{-a}}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-a)}\,\frac{{}_2F_1(a, a-c+1; a-b+1; 1/z)}{\Gamma(a-b+1)} + (a \leftrightarrow b)
$$

Since $|1/z| < 1/9$, the series converge quickly, but when $b - a$ is near an integer, poles of $\Gamma(b-a)$ and $\Gamma(a-b+1)$ approach and the two terms become a difference of large values.
When $b - a$ is exactly an integer $m$, use a logarithmic limiting formula with poles cancelled analytically (DLMF 15.8.8), pairing $\ln(-z)$ and digamma terms with reciprocal-gamma zeros.

Near $z = 1$, the term ratio approaches 1; small $c - a - b$ also makes the value at $z = 1$ large.
Real types handle this region by Taylor-method numerical continuation of the hypergeometric differential equation $z(1-z)w'' + (c - (a+b+1)z)w' - abw = 0$, rather than connection formulas in $1 - z$.
Evaluate the series and derivative (contiguous relation $\frac{d}{dz}{}_2F_1 = \frac{ab}{c}\,{}_2F_1(a+1,b+1;c+1;z)$) at $z_0 = \pm 1/4$ with the same sign as the target $z$; carry value and derivative through several double-word Taylor-series steps to the target $z$.
Steps shrink near the singularity $z = 1$, so work increases as $1 - z$ decreases.

Real `hyp2f1` selects methods as follows.
Identifiers are the names returned by options-form `algorithm().method()`.

| Condition | Method | Identifier |
|---|---|---|
| $a$ or $b$ is a nonpositive integer | Polynomial (compensated kernel if cancellation remains unresolved) | `real_series` |
| $a = c$ or $b = c$ | $(1-z)^{-b}$ or $(1-z)^{-a}$ | `parameter_identity` |
| $a = b = 1$, $c = 2$, $z < 1$ | Form $-\ln(1-z)/z$ with `ln_1p` | `logarithmic_identity` |
| $-1/2 \le z \le 0.9$ | Sum series in double-word arithmetic | `real_series` |
| $-9 \le z < -1/2$ | Series after Pfaff transformation | `real_pfaff_series` |
| $z < -9$, integer $b - a$ | Logarithmic $1/z$ connection (DLMF 15.8.8) | `real_integer_reciprocal` |
| $z < -9$, $\lvert\sin \pi(b-a)\rvert > 0.01$ | $1/z$ connection (DLMF 15.8.2) | `real_reciprocal_connection` |
| $z < -9$, noninteger $b - a$ near an integer | Differential-equation Taylor continuation | `real_taylor_continuation` |
| $0.9 < z < 1$ | Differential-equation Taylor continuation | `real_taylor_continuation` |
| $z = 1$, $c - a - b > 0$ | Gauss summation | `real_gauss_endpoint` |
| $z = 1$, $c - a - b \le 0$ | `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) | None |
| $z > 1$, nonterminating series | `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) | None |

Of the two Pfaff forms, choose the one with the smaller initial transformed term ratio, using a terminating form when available.
Sum initial series terms in double-word arithmetic; when rounding in the remaining terms can no longer affect the result, switch to binary64 and sum to a relative tail of $2^{-57}$.
If double-word arithmetic cannot resolve cancellation, reevaluate the region with a compensated kernel that also carries parameter directions in double-word arithmetic.

To obtain values at $z > 1$ unavailable in real types, call with a complex type.
Complex $z$ continues to the plane cut along $[1, \infty)$; signed zero in the imaginary part selects the side ($2 + 0i$ and $2 - 0i$ give opposite imaginary signs).
Complex evaluation uses $1 - z$ connections for $|1 - z| \le 0.1$ (logarithmic limits for integer $c - a - b$), $1/z$ connections for $|z| > 4$ when $b - a$ is away from integers, series for $|z| < 0.7$, and differential-equation continuation elsewhere.

## Confluent functions

### 1F1

`hyp1f1` returns $e^{z}$ for $a = b$; otherwise, sum the series in double-word arithmetic.
For $z < -1$, terms of the $M(a,b,z)$ series alternate, growing to order $e^{|z|}$ before cancelling.
Use Kummer's transformation $M(a,b,z) = e^{z}M(b-a,b,-z)$ to obtain a positive-argument series before summation.
For $b > a > 0$, every transformed-series term is positive, avoiding cancellation.

For $|z| > 40 + 2(|a| + |b|)$, the compensated kernel tries asymptotic expansions (exponential and algebraic terms on the positive side; algebraic terms after Kummer transformation on the negative side), accepting them when the estimated error is at most $8\varepsilon|M|$.
If double-word arithmetic cannot resolve cancellation, resum using expansions of 4, 8, then 16 words; if still unresolved, return `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).

### U and 2F0

Real `hyperu` selects methods in the following order.
For nonpositive integer $a$, $U$ is a polynomial in $z$.
$z = 0$ returns the origin value for $\operatorname{Re} b < 1$; otherwise it gives `OutsideDomain`.
For $b = a + 1$, return $U = z^{-a}$ (DLMF 13.6.4).
For large $z$, accept the asymptotic expansion $U \sim z^{-a}\sum_n (a)_n(a-b+1)_n(-1/z)^n/n!$ (DLMF 13.7.3) when its smallest term is sufficiently small.
For $z < 2$ and noninteger $b$, try the connection $U = \frac{\Gamma(1-b)}{\Gamma(a-b+1)}M(a,b,z) + \frac{\Gamma(b-1)}{\Gamma(a)}z^{1-b}M(a-b+1,2-b,z)$ through two $M$ series (DLMF 13.2.42), accepting it when the sum of their absolute values is at most four times $|U|$, indicating limited cancellation.
Otherwise, use Kummer's transformation $U(a,b,z) = z^{1-b}U(1+a-b, 2-b, z)$ (DLMF 13.2.40) to reach $b \ge 1$, run the three-term recurrence in $a$ backward (DLMF 13.3.7) for the minimal-solution ratio $U(a+1)/U(a)$, and normalize with the Wronskian of $M$ and $U$ (DLMF 13.2.34).
Forward recurrence mixes in the growing solution $M$ and amplifies errors; backward recurrence is stable because the minimal solution becomes dominant.
For $z < 2$, carry the value and derivative toward the origin using the differential equation's Taylor method.
Inputs outside recurrence and connection ranges, such as $|a| > 64$, $b > 12$, and $z < 2^{-40}$, use the complex kernel, including numerical quadrature of integral representations.

`hyp2f0` sums a polynomial if $a$ or $b$ is a nonpositive integer; for $z < 0$, it computes $(-1/z)^{a}U(a, 1+a-b, -1/z)$.
Estimate the value changes caused by rounding in $-1/z$ and $1 + a - b$ using first-order derivative sensitivities, and add them to the error estimate.
For negative arguments with $|z|$ below the reciprocal of `f64::MAX`, $-1/z$ overflows; use the initial series terms $1 + abz$, returning `LossOfSignificance` for parameters where these do not suffice.

### 0F1

`hyp0f1` sums its series for $z \ge -128$; for negative arguments with unresolved cancellation, use the Bessel relation $_0F_1(;b;z) = \Gamma(b)(-z)^{(1-b)/2}J_{b-1}(2\sqrt{-z})$.
For $b < 1/2$, reflection expresses negative orders as combinations of $J$ and $Y$.
Rounding $\sqrt{-z}$ shifts the oscillatory phase; correct the rounding residual of $\sqrt{-z}$ using Bessel derivatives before forming the value.
This path's error estimate is checked for coverage against Bessel-kernel reference data (`StatisticallyCalibrated`).

### Complex arguments

For complex $z$, $_0F_1$ integrates the differential equation when $\operatorname{Re} z < -8$ or $|\operatorname{Im} z| > 12$, and otherwise sums the series.
$_1F_1$ uses asymptotic expansion for large $|z|$, Kummer transformation for $\operatorname{Re} z < -8$, series for $|z| < 8$ or near the positive real axis, and differential-equation continuation elsewhere.
Parameters may remain real while only $z$ is complex (`impl Parameter<T>`).

## Accuracy guarantees and estimated errors

The following accuracy targets are checked against independently generated high-precision references.

| Function | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Real `hyp0f1`, `hyp1f1`, `hyp2f1`, `hyperu` | 64 ULP in binary32 and binary64 | Absolute-error bounds near zeros. $_1F_1$ has relative error below $10^{-12}$ for inputs with condition number at most 1000 |
| `hyp_pfq` (excluding forms above), `hyp2f0`, `hyp2f1_regularized` | No uniform target | Compare estimated errors against independent high-precision references |
| Complex arguments, parameter derivatives | No uniform target | Test $_1F_1$ over all phases through $\lvert z\rvert = 10^4$, and $_2F_1$ down to distances of $10^{-15}$ from $z = 1$ |

ULP measures error in units of adjacent floating-point spacing in the result type; near zeros, relative error loses meaning, so use absolute-error bounds.
Estimated errors combine series truncation, double-word rounding models, and errors of connection-formula terms; they are not intervals with guaranteed containment.
Cancellation unresolved by double-word or expansion arithmetic gives `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`), rather than an inaccurate value.

## Hypergeometric APIs

| Item | Kind | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `hyp0f1`, `hyp1f1`, `hyp2f1`, `hyp2f1_regularized`, `hyp2f0`, `hyperu` | Functions | Standard forms; parameters use `impl Parameter<T>` and arguments use `T: NumericScalar` |
| `hyp0f1_with_options`, `hyp1f1_with_options`, `hyp2f1_with_options`, `hyp2f1_regularized_with_options`, `hyp2f0_with_options`, `hyperu_with_options` | Functions | Options forms accepting `HypergeometricOptions`, returning values, estimated errors, and derivatives with respect to $z$ |
| `hyp0f1_scoped`, `hyp1f1_scoped`, `hyp2f1_scoped`, `hyp2f1_regularized_scoped`, `hyp2f0_scoped`, `hyperu_scoped` | Functions | Scoped APIs counting work within `SolveScope` |
| `hyp_pfq`, `hyp_pfq_with_options`, `hyp_pfq_scoped` | Functions | General $_pF_q$; pass parameters as `&[T]` slices. Options forms have no derivatives |
| `HypergeometricFunction` | Struct | Retain checked parameters for evaluation at many $z$; add regularized $_pF_q$ and first derivatives |
| `HypergeometricWorkspace` | Struct | $O(p+q)$ workspace used by `evaluate_scoped` |
| `HypergeometricFamily` | Enum | `Pfq` or `U`; returned by `HypergeometricFunction::family()` |
| `HypergeometricDerivative` | Enum | `None`, `Argument`, `Numerator(i)`, `Denominator(i)` (zero-based indices) |
| `HypergeometricOptions` | Struct | Work budget, default 65536 units |

For named functions, options forms `f_with_options(.., options)` add estimated errors, termination status, work counts, and algorithm identifiers to the same standard-form value, separately computing a derivative with respect to $z$.
For real arguments, derivatives use contiguous relations ($\frac{\prod a_i}{\prod b_j}\,{}_pF_q(a+1;b+1;z)$ for $_pF_q$, $-aU(a+1,b+1,z)$ for $U$); for complex arguments, the value kernel also carries the $z$ derivative direction.
A derivative's domain can be narrower than the value's: the derivative of $_2F_1$ at $z = 1$ requires $c - a - b > 1$.
Scoped APIs `f_scoped(.., options, scope)` perform the same computation as options forms within the caller's `SolveScope`, checking cancellation and deadlines.

Call general $_pF_q$ one evaluation at a time through `hyp_pfq(&a, &b, z)`, without constructing a plan.
Use `HypergeometricFunction` for many $z$ with the same parameters, regularized $_pF_q$, or parameter derivatives.
`HypergeometricFunction::pfq(&numerator, &denominator, regularized, options)` only checks parameters and retains them in $O(p+q)$ memory; it performs no other precomputation.
`HypergeometricFunction::u(a, b, options)` retains Tricomi's $U$.
`evaluate(z, derivative)` allocates workspace per call; passing `HypergeometricWorkspace` from `workspace()` to `evaluate_scoped(z, derivative, &mut workspace, scope)` avoids allocation.
Parameter derivatives are analytic derivatives carried with the value in the same computation along one direction (forward automatic differentiation), rather than finite differences.
Regularization replaces denominator $(b_j)_k$ with $\Gamma(b_j + k)$, dividing the value by $\prod_j \Gamma(b_j)$ and retaining finiteness for nonpositive integer $b_j$.
Derivative indices at least the parameter count (indices are zero-based), or workspace sizes inconsistent with the parameter count, return `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).

## Gauss 2F1 regions and closed forms

For two parameter sets with closed forms, the next cell varies $z$ from $-10^6$ to just below 1 and lists `hyp2f1_with_options` values, selected methods, estimated errors, and work counts.
The first set is $_2F_1(\frac12, 1; \frac32; z)$, giving $\arctan x / x$ for $z = -x^2$ and $\operatorname{artanh} x / x$ for $z = x^2$ (DLMF 15.4.2, 15.4.3).
Since $b - a = 1/2$, $z < -9$ selects the ordinary $1/z$ connection.
The second set is $_2F_1(1, 2; 3; z) = 2(-\ln(1-z) - z)/z^2$; integer $b - a = 1$ selects a logarithmic connection.
Both have $c - a - b = 0$, making $z = 1$ outside the domain; the last row shows this error.
Closed forms use standard f64 functions, forming $1 - z$ exactly from the input to avoid rounding $\ln(1-z)$ when $z$ is near 1.

```rust
//| id: special-hyp2f1-connection-regions
//| ref: special-hyp2f1-connection-regions
//| title: "Methods by Gauss 2F1 region and differences from closed forms"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   family: {"type":"select","label":"Parameter set","options":[{"label":"(1/2, 1; 3/2): arctan and artanh","value":"arctan"},{"label":"(1, 2; 3): logarithm","value":"log"}],"value":"arctan"}
//|   nearest_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Exponent p of minimum 1 − z (1 − z = 10^p)","description":"Choose how closely the last row's z approaches 1.","min":-12,"max":-2,"step":1,"value":-9}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"logarithmic","label":"(1, 2; 3)","description":"Integer b − a selects a logarithmic connection for z < −9.","inputs":{"family":"log"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"real_reciprocal_connection"}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/5/1","equals":"real_pfaff_series"}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/13/1","equals":"real_taylor_continuation"}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/13/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/14/1","equals":"DomainError（OutsideDomain）"}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/14/2","equals":null}
//|   - case: logarithmic
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"real_integer_reciprocal"}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/13/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::SciOxideErrorView;
use scioxide_special::hyp2f1_with_options;

let (a, b, c) = if family == "log" {
    (1.0, 2.0, 3.0)
} else {
    (0.5, 1.0, 1.5)
};
// Closed form (reference using standard f64 functions). w = 1 − z is exact for z ∈ [1/2, 2] by Sterbenz's lemma.
let closed_form = |z: f64, w: f64| {
    if family == "log" {
        2.0 * (-w.ln() - z) / (z * z)
    } else if z < 0.0 {
        let t = (-z).sqrt();
        t.atan() / t
    } else {
        // artanh(s) = (ln(1 + s) − ln(1 − s))/2, 1 − s = (1 − z)/(1 + s).
        let s = z.sqrt();
        0.5 * (s.ln_1p() - (w / (1.0 + s)).ln()) / s
    }
};

let last = 10.0_f64.powf(nearest_exponent);
let mut points = vec![
    -1e6, -1e3, -50.0, -9.5, -9.0, -3.0, -0.75, -0.3, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99,
];
points.push(1.0 - last);

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for z in points {
    let result =
        hyp2f1_with_options(a, b, c, z, Default::default()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let value = result.value().ok_or("Value outside ordinary range")?;
    let exact = closed_form(z, 1.0 - z);
    rows.push((
        z,
        result.algorithm().method().to_string(),
        Some(*value.value()),
        Some(exact),
        Some(((value.value() - exact) / exact).abs()),
        Some(value.absolute_error() / value.value().abs()),
        Some(result.counts().iterations() as f64),
    ));
}
// z = 1 is outside the domain because c − a − b = 0. Retain the error classification in the table.
let edge = match hyp2f1_with_options(a, b, c, 1.0, Default::default()) {
    Ok(_) => return Err("A value was returned at z = 1".to_string()),
    Err(e) => match e.view() {
        SciOxideErrorView::DomainError { reason, .. } => format!("{:?}（{reason:?}）", e.kind()),
        _ => return Err(e.to_string()),
    },
};
rows.push((1.0, edge, None, None, None, None, None));

let sig17 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 17 });
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let count = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 0 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "z [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig17 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "2F1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Estimated relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "None" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Work [units]", "type": "number", "format": count, "nullText": "None" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "regions" })
);
```

For the default set $(\frac12, 1; \frac32)$, relative differences from closed forms are at most $1.5 \times 10^{-16}$ in every row; accuracy is maintained across method switches from $z = -10^6$ to $z = 1 - 10^{-9}$.
The method column shows `real_reciprocal_connection` at $z \le -9.5$, `real_pfaff_series` at $[-9, -0.75]$, `real_series` at $[-0.3, 0.9]$, and `real_taylor_continuation` at $z \ge 0.95$, matching the boundaries in the table above.
Estimated relative errors are $2.3 \times 10^{-15}$ for series and Pfaff transformation, $3.2 \times 10^{-15}$ for differential-equation continuation, and $1.0$–$1.4 \times 10^{-14}$ for $1/z$ connections, exceeding actual differences in every row.
Work counts show the computational cost differences.
The series takes 63 units at $z = 0.3$ but 705 units at $z = 0.9$ as the term ratio approaches 1.
The $1/z$ connection has short series, but double-word gamma and log-gamma evaluations require about 4200 units; changing $|z|$ to $10^6$ barely changes work.
Differential-equation continuation uses smaller steps as $1 - z$ shrinks, requiring 7607 units at $z = 1 - 10^{-9}$.
The final row shows that $c - a - b = 0$ makes $z = 1$ give `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).

For `logarithmic` ($(1, 2; 3)$), integer $b - a = 1$ changes the method at $z \le -9.5$ to `real_integer_reciprocal`.
The ordinary $1/z$ connection is unusable at the pole of $\Gamma(b - a)\Gamma(a - b + 1)$; the logarithmic limiting formula keeps relative differences at most $2.2 \times 10^{-16}$.
The relative difference $1.4 \times 10^{-15}$ at $z = 0.3$ reflects about one digit lost by subtraction in the reference closed form $2(-\ln(1-z) - z)/z^2$.

## Cancellation in 1F1 and Kummer's transformation

The defining series for $M(a, b, -x)$ ($x > 0$) alternates; its largest term is of order $e^{x}$ while the sum is only of order $x^{-a}$.
Naive binary64 summation gives relative error of about $\varepsilon\sum_k|t_k|/|M|$.
This ratio $\sum_k|t_k|/|M|$ is the cancellation factor.

The next cell selects parameters $a$ and $b$ and computes values and derivatives with respect to $z$ using `HypergeometricFunction` at $z = -1, -5, -10, -20, -30, -40$.
The reference sums the right side of Kummer's transformation $M(a,b,z) = e^{z}M(b-a,b,-z)$ in f64 (for $b > a$, all terms are positive, avoiding cancellation).
The derivative reference also sums the differentiated transformation $e^{z}[M(b-a,b,-z) - \frac{b-a}{b}M(b-a+1,b+1,-z)]$ in f64, independently of SciOxide's contiguous-relation derivative.
`HypergeometricFunction` checks and retains parameters once, creates one workspace, and reuses it through `evaluate_scoped`, avoiding per-point allocation.

```rust
//| id: special-hyp1f1-kummer-cancellation
//| ref: special-hyp1f1-kummer-cancellation
//| title: "Cancellation at negative 1F1 arguments and Kummer's transformation"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   a: {"type":"range","label":"Numerator parameter a","min":0.25,"max":3,"step":0.25,"value":0.5}
//|   b: {"type":"range","label":"Denominator parameter b","min":0.5,"max":6,"step":0.5,"value":1.5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"b-below-a","label":"a = 3, b = 0.5","description":"For b < a, leading transformed-series terms change sign, but cancellation stays small.","inputs":{"a":3,"b":0.5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"kummer","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kummer","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"min":1e-2}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kummer","path":"/rows/5/4","range":{"min":1e15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kummer","path":"/rows/5/5","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: b-below-a
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"kummer","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"kummer","path":"/rows/5/5","range":{"max":1e-11}}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_special::{HypergeometricDerivative, HypergeometricFunction};

// Sum Σ (α)ₖ yᵏ/((β)ₖ k!) directly in f64, returning the sum and the absolute term sum for reference and comparison.
let series = |alpha: f64, beta: f64, y: f64| {
    let (mut term, mut sum, mut magnitude) = (1.0_f64, 1.0_f64, 1.0_f64);
    for k in 0..400 {
        let k = k as f64;
        term *= (alpha + k) * y / ((beta + k) * (k + 1.0));
        sum += term;
        magnitude += term.abs();
        if term.abs() <= 1e-18 * sum.abs() && k > y {
            break;
        }
    }
    (sum, magnitude)
};

let function = HypergeometricFunction::pfq(&[a], &[b], false, Default::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut workspace = function.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for x in [1.0_f64, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 40.0] {
    let z = -x;
    let result = function
        .evaluate_scoped(
            z,
            HypergeometricDerivative::Argument,
            &mut workspace,
            ExecutionContext::sequential(),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let value = *result.value().ok_or("Missing value")?.value();
    let slope = *result.derivative().ok_or("Missing derivative")?.value();
    // Reference: right side of Kummer's transformation (standard f64 functions).
    let reference = z.exp() * series(b - a, b, x).0;
    let reference_slope =
        z.exp() * (series(b - a, b, x).0 - (b - a) / b * series(b - a + 1.0, b + 1.0, x).0);
    // Comparison: directly sum the defining series in f64.
    let (naive, magnitude) = series(a, b, z);
    rows.push((
        z,
        value,
        ((value - reference) / reference).abs(),
        ((naive - reference) / reference).abs(),
        magnitude / reference.abs(),
        ((slope - reference_slope) / reference_slope).abs(),
    ));
}

let sig8 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 8 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "z [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "M(a, b, z) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig8 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative reference difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Naive-series relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Cancellation factor [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "dM/dz relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "kummer" })
);
```

For the defaults $a = 1/2$ and $b = 3/2$, SciOxide's relative difference from the reference stays at most $8 \times 10^{-16}$ through $z = -40$.
The naive-series relative error grows roughly as the cancellation factor times $\varepsilon$ (the factor is $2.1 \times 10^{16}$ at $z = -40$), reaching $5.5 \times 10^{-10}$ at $z = -20$ and an 18% discrepancy at $z = -40$.
This factor explains why `hyp1f1` switches to Kummer transformation at $z < -1$.
The derivative's relative difference grows to $5.6 \times 10^{-14}$ at $z = -40$ because reference subtraction $M(b-a,b,-z) - \frac{b-a}{b}M(b-a+1,b+1,-z)$ suffers roughly a $|z|$-fold loss of precision; for the set `b-below-a`, it remains $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ at the same point.
For `b-below-a` ($a = 3$, $b = 0.5$), the value changes sign; the cancellation factor reaches $6 \times 10^{25}$ at $z = -40$, leaving no meaningful digits in naive summation.
SciOxide's relative differences stay at most $3.9 \times 10^{-16}$ in every row.

## Summary

Real `hyp0f1`, `hyp1f1`, `hyp2f1`, and `hyperu` target 64 ULP; general `hyp_pfq`, `hyp2f0`, regularized forms, and complex arguments have no uniform target, so inspect options-form estimated errors.
$_2F_1$ uses series for $z \in [-1/2, 0.9]$, Pfaff transformation for $[-9, -1/2)$, $1/z$ connections for $z < -9$ (logarithmic for integer $b - a$, differential-equation continuation near integers), and Taylor continuation for $0.9 < z < 1$; options-form `algorithm().method()` identifies the selected method.
For values at $z > 1$ outside the real-type domain, call with a complex type, choosing the cut side with signed imaginary zero.
At negative $_1F_1$ arguments, the defining series suffers cancellation by a factor of about $e^{|z|}$; `hyp1f1` uses Kummer transformation to obtain a positive-term series before summation.
Use `HypergeometricFunction` and a workspace for many $z$ with the same parameters, regularized forms, or parameter derivatives.

See [Cylindrical Bessel and Hankel functions](../airy-bessel-and-hankel/) to examine recurrence directions and Miller's method behind $_0F_1$ through its Bessel relation.
[Riemann and Hurwitz zeta functions and the Dirichlet eta function](../zeta-functions/) illustrates slow-series convergence handled by Euler–Maclaurin summation and functional equations; the API guide's [Hypergeometric functions](../../api/hypergeometric/) gives individual specifications.
