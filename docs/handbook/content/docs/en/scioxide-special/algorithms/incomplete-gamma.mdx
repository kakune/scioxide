Incomplete gamma functions split the defining gamma integral at $x$ and give cumulative probabilities for gamma, chi-squared, and Poisson distributions.
The probability forms $P$ and $Q$ sum to 1, but subtraction such as $1 - P$ loses significant digits in the smaller probability, requiring direct evaluation of the smaller tail.
This page explains definitions and domains, how `scioxide-special` selects methods using shape $a$ and ratio $x/a$, unregularized and logarithmic forms, inverses, complex arguments, accuracy guarantees, and API choices.
It assumes the complete gamma and log-gamma evaluation methods for $\Gamma(a)$ and $\ln\Gamma(a)$ in [Gamma functions, polygamma functions, and the Pochhammer symbol](../gamma-family/).

## Definitions and domains

Splitting the integration interval at $x$ gives the lower incomplete gamma function $\gamma(a, x)$ and upper incomplete gamma function $\Gamma(a, x)$ (DLMF 8.2.1, 8.2.2).
$a$ is the shape parameter.

$$
\gamma(a, x) = \int_0^x t^{a-1} e^{-t} \, dt, \qquad \Gamma(a, x) = \int_x^\infty t^{a-1} e^{-t} \, dt, \qquad \gamma(a, x) + \Gamma(a, x) = \Gamma(a)
$$

Probability calculations use the regularized incomplete gamma functions $P$ and $Q$, obtained by dividing the incomplete gamma functions by $\Gamma(a)$ (DLMF 8.2.4).

$$
P(a, x) = \frac{\gamma(a, x)}{\Gamma(a)}, \qquad Q(a, x) = \frac{\Gamma(a, x)}{\Gamma(a)}, \qquad P + Q = 1
$$

$P(a, x)$ is the cumulative distribution function of a gamma distribution with shape $a$ and scale 1; $Q(a, x)$ is its survival function, or upper probability.

Real `gamma_p` and `gamma_q` accept $a \ge 0$ and $x \ge 0$.
$a < 0$ or $x < 0$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`); NaN and infinity give `NonFinite`.
At $x = 0$, return $P = 0$ and $Q = 1$ with termination status `Exact`.
For fixed $x > 0$, return the $a \to 0$ limits $P(0, x) = 1$ and $Q(0, x) = 0$ with termination status `Limit`.
At the origin $(a, x) = (0, 0)$, the limit depends on the approach, giving `DomainError` (`Undefined`).

The unregularized `gamma_lower(a, x)` returns $\gamma(a, x)$, and `gamma_upper(a, x)` returns $\Gamma(a, x)$.
`gamma_lower` requires real $a > 0$; $a = 0$ gives `DomainError` (`Pole`), while $a < 0$ and $x < 0$ give `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
`gamma_upper` also accepts $a = 0$, returning $\Gamma(0, x) = E_1(x)$ (exponential integral, DLMF 8.4.4) with the same evaluation as the exponential integral ([Exponential and logarithmic integrals](../exponential-and-trigonometric-integrals/)).
$\Gamma(0, 0)$ gives `DomainError` (`Pole`).

## Why evaluate the smaller tail directly

Although $P + Q = 1$ is an identity, subtracting $Q = 1 - P$ in the region $Q \ll 1$ causes cancellation.
Rounding error in $P$ is of order $2^{-53}$ relative to the magnitude 1 of $P$; at $Q = 10^{-10}$, relative error in $1 - P$ is of order $10^{-6}$, and the subtraction gives 0 at $Q < 10^{-16}$.
Real `gamma_p` and `gamma_q` therefore use a common evaluator to compute one tail directly and form only the other by $1 - (\text{directly evaluated tail})$.
Each method directly evaluates the tail it can compute accurately; method regions are chosen so this is always the smaller tail.
The larger tail is near 1, so this subtraction loses no accuracy.
The options-form algorithm identifier identifies the method; all methods except Temme's expansion have a fixed directly evaluated tail.
Temme's expansion directly evaluates $P$ for $x < a$ and $Q$ for $x \ge a$.

## Method selection

The evaluator checks conditions in the following order and uses the first matching method.
Parentheses give the suffix of the identifier retained by options-form `algorithm()`.

1. For $a = 1$, use the closed forms $P = -\operatorname{expm1}(-x)$ and $Q = e^{-x}$ (`unit_exponential`).
2. For $a = 1/2$, use $P = \operatorname{erf}\sqrt{x}$ and $Q = \operatorname{erfc}\sqrt{x}$ (`half_integer_erf_erfc`).
3. For $a < 1$, $x \le 2$, and $x^a > 1/2$, compute $Q$ using a series that analytically cancels the pole of $1/a$ (`small_shape_upper_series`; a rearrangement of DLMF 8.7.3).

   $$
   \Gamma(a, x) = \frac{\Gamma(1+a) - x^a}{a} - x^a \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{(-x)^k}{k!\,(a+k)}
   $$

   As $a \to 0$, $\Gamma(a)$ and $x^a/a$ both diverge as $1/a$ and cancel; form the difference $\Gamma(1+a) - x^a$ before dividing by $a$.
4. If $2a$ is an integer at most 65 (integers and half-integers of $a \le 32.5$) and $a \le x \le -\ln(\text{MIN\_POSITIVE}) \approx 708.4$ ($e^{-x}$ is normal), compute $Q$ by a finite sum (`integer_upper_finite`, `half_integer_upper_finite`; DLMF §8.4 and recurrence 8.8.2).

   $$
   Q(n, x) = e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^k}{k!}, \qquad Q\left(n + \tfrac{1}{2}, x\right) = \operatorname{erfc}\sqrt{x} + e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{x^{k+1/2}}{\Gamma\left(k + \tfrac{3}{2}\right)}
   $$

   All terms are positive, so cancellation does not occur regardless of the magnitude of $Q$.
   Skip this method if the remaining work budget cannot cover the term count.
5. For $a > 20$ in the transition band where $x$ is near $a$, compute the smaller tail using Temme's uniform asymptotic expansion (`temme_transition`; DLMF 8.12).
   The band is $-\ell a < x - a < 0.4a$, with $\ell$ equal to $0.25$ for $a < 100$ and $0.4$ for $a \ge 100$.
6. For remaining $x < a$, compute $P$ by summing a power series with Kahan compensated addition (`lower_compensated_series`; a form of DLMF 8.7.1).

   $$
   P(a, x) = \frac{x^a e^{-x}}{\Gamma(a)} \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{x^n}{a(a+1)\cdots(a+n)}
   $$

7. For remaining $x \ge a$, compute $Q$ by forward modified-Lentz evaluation of the even contraction of Legendre's continued fraction (`upper_scaled_lentz`; DLMF 8.9.2).

   $$
   Q(a, x) = \frac{x^a e^{-x}}{\Gamma(a)} \cdot \cfrac{1}{x + 1 - a - \cfrac{1\cdot(1-a)}{x + 3 - a - \cfrac{2\cdot(2-a)}{x + 5 - a - \cdots}}}
   $$

The power-series term ratio is $x/(a+n)$, so terms decrease from the start for $x < a$.
The continued fraction converges rapidly for $x \ge a$.
Using only these two methods for large shapes at $a \approx x$ would require term counts proportional to $\sqrt{a}$; Temme's expansion handles this region.
Temme's expansion uses $\lambda = x/a$ and $\eta = \operatorname{sign}(\lambda - 1)\sqrt{2(\lambda - 1 - \ln\lambda)}$ to express the tail through $\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(\lvert\eta\rvert\sqrt{a/2})$ plus a correction series.
The implementation uses $\operatorname{erfcx}$ scaled by $e^{a\eta^2/2}$ and Taylor polynomials for correction coefficients $c_k(\eta)$, truncating at 10 powers of $1/a$.
Narrowing the band's lower boundary to $x - a > -0.25a$ for $a < 100$ keeps coefficient Taylor truncation within $\lvert\eta\rvert < 0.28$.
For $a > 20$, outside the band the power series converges in about 170 terms and the continued fraction in about 20 steps, so work does not grow with $a$.

For the power-series and continued-fraction prefactor $x^a e^{-x}/\Gamma(a)$, express $\Gamma(a)$ in rational Lanczos form $L_g(a)$, set $t = a + g - 1/2$, and take the logarithm of the following expression before exponentiating.

$$
\frac{x^a e^{-x}}{\Gamma(a)} = \left(\frac{x}{t}\right)^a e^{t - x} \frac{\sqrt{t}}{L_g(a)}
$$


$g$ is the same constant as in the complete-gamma Lanczos approximation; $L_g(a)$ is a rational function satisfying $\Gamma(a) = L_g(a)\,t^{a-1/2}e^{-t}$.
Computing and subtracting $a\ln x$ and $\ln\Gamma(a)$ separately loses digits for large $a$ through subtraction of large logarithms; taking the logarithm of the ratio $x/t$ avoids cancellation.
Take the logarithm in binary64 only for $a \le 32$ with $a\lvert\ln(x/t)\rvert \le 16$; otherwise use double-word floating-point arithmetic.

Modified Lentz evaluation replaces near-zero denominators by a small value ($\text{MIN\_POSITIVE}/\varepsilon$) to avoid division by zero, stopping when successive approximations have ratio within $\varepsilon/2$ of 1.
The power series stops when the term is at most $\varepsilon$ times the sum and a geometric-series bound on the remaining terms is also sufficiently small.

Terms and steps count as work; exceeding the `GammaOptions` budget (default 65 536) gives `ConvergenceFailure`.
Options-form `algorithm()` records the method with an identifier such as `gamma/upper_scaled_lentz@1`; `counts().iterations()` exposes work counts.

## Checking method selection

The next cell selects shape $a$ and tabulates the method selected by `gamma_q_with_options`, work counts, $Q$, and $\ln Q$ at nine points from $x = 0.2a$ to $5a$.
As an independent check across method boundaries, it reports the relative residual of the following recurrence (DLMF 8.8.6).

$$
Q(a + 1, x) = Q(a, x) + \frac{x^a e^{-x}}{\Gamma(a+1)}
$$


$Q(a+1, x)$ and $Q(a, x)$ often use different methods, so an error in either appears in the residual.
The second term on the right is computed manually as $\exp(a\ln x - x - \ln\Gamma(a+1))$ using `ln_gamma`; rounding each exponent term can introduce relative error up to about $\varepsilon(\lvert a\ln x\rvert + x + \ln\Gamma(a+1))$, and the residual may grow to this scale.
The chart uses $x/a$ horizontally and $Q$ on a logarithmic vertical axis, grouping points by method to show switch locations.

For the default $a = 40$, $x/a = 0.2, 0.5, 0.7$ uses the power series, $0.95$ through $1.3$ use Temme's expansion, and $2$ and $5$ use the continued fraction; $Q$ falls from near 1 to $4.63 \times 10^{-44}$.
At $x/a \le 0.7$, the power series computes $P$ directly and $Q$ is its complement.
For the power series and continued fraction, work counts terms and steps; for Temme's expansion, it counts the 10 retained terms.
All relative recurrence residuals are at most $1.4 \times 10^{-14}$; $Q(a, x)$ and $Q(a+1, x)$ evaluated across method boundaries also agree at the rounding level.
Residuals grow with $x/a$ from about $10^{-17}$ to $10^{-14}$ because the magnitude of the check exponent $a\ln x - x - \ln\Gamma(a+1)$ grows and rounding in the manually computed term increases.

At $a = 4$, the five points with $x \ge a$ use a four-term finite sum (`gamma/integer_upper_finite@1`).
At $a = 0.5$, every point uses $\operatorname{erf}$ and $\operatorname{erfc}$.
At $a = 150$, Temme's lower band boundary extends to $x - a > -0.4a$, so $x/a = 0.7$ also uses its expansion.
The residual in the $x = 750$ row then grows to $5.5 \times 10^{-14}$ because the absolute terms in the check exponent sum to $2.3 \times 10^3$; it remains below the estimate $\varepsilon \cdot 2.3 \times 10^3 \approx 5 \times 10^{-13}$.
Residuals orders of magnitude above this estimate suggest an error in method selection or the check formula.

## Unregularized forms

`gamma_lower` and `gamma_upper` add $\ln\Gamma(a)$ to the regularized-tail logarithm $\ln P$ or $\ln Q$ in double-word arithmetic, then exponentiate once at the end.
Even for large $a$ where $\Gamma(a)$ exceeds binary64 range, they return ordinary values if the values themselves fit.
Tail logarithms use a shared logarithmic-tail procedure separate from the evaluator above.
This procedure uses Temme's expansion within its band, a power series for $x < a + 1$ outside it ($\ln P$), and a continued fraction elsewhere ($\ln Q$).
It does not use finite-sum or closed-form shortcuts.
If the opposite tail is evaluated, switch sides using $\ln(1 - e^{\ell})$ without cancellation.

There are three exceptions.
At $x = 0$, return $\gamma(a, 0) = 0$ and $\Gamma(a, 0) = \Gamma(a)$.
For $a \le 1/2$ and $x \le 1$ with `gamma_upper`, compute $\Gamma(a, x)$ directly using the same pole-cancelled $1/a$ series as method 3 above (`upper_small_shape_series`).
$a = 0$ at `gamma_upper` is $E_1(x)$ as described above.

Values exceeding binary64 range give `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
Options forms still return results: ordinary-value projection has overflow status, while `logarithmic()` retains $\ln\Gamma(a, x)$.

## Logarithmic forms and inverses

For probabilities below binary64 range, use `gamma_log_p(a, x)` and `gamma_log_q(a, x)`, which return logarithms without forming $P$ or $Q$.
Both first try evaluating the requested tail as an ordinary binary64 probability.
This is restricted to $a < 128$ and $0 < x \le 708$ with a normal prefactor $x^a e^{-x}/\Gamma(a)$.
For integer and half-integer shapes ($a \le 32.5$) at $x \ge a$, use the finite sum above; for other $x < a + 1$, use a positive-term power series, returning its logarithm if the error estimate meets the acceptance criterion.
For other shapes and ranges, use $P$ and $Q$ from the evaluator above, take the logarithm of the smaller tail, and compute the logarithm of the larger tail as $\ln(1 - T)$ with $\operatorname{log1p}$ without cancellation.
When $P$ or $Q$ falls below the normal range, use the same double-word logarithmic-tail procedure as the unregularized forms.
Options-form identifiers begin with `probability`, as in `probability/upper_linear_tail@1`.
$\ln P(a, 0) = -\infty$ and $\ln Q(0, x) = -\infty$ are returned as values, not errors.

Find quantiles using `gamma_p_inv(a, p)` to solve $P(a, x) = p$ or `gamma_q_inv(a, q)` to solve $Q(a, x) = q$.
The logarithmic inverses `gamma_p_inv_log(a, ln p)` and `gamma_q_inv_log(a, ln q)` handle $p$ or $q$ too small to represent in binary64.
All require $a > 0$; $a \le 0$ and out-of-range probabilities (outside $[0, 1]$, or positive logarithms) give `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`); NaN and infinity, including logarithmic $-\infty$, give `NonFinite`.
$p = 0$ and $p = 1$ return $x = 0$ and $x = +\infty$.

The inverses solve as follows.
For $p > 1/2$, solve for the opposite tail, targeting $1 - p$, which is exact in binary64.
For logarithmic inputs, switch to the opposite tail similarly when $\ln p > -\ln 2$.
When $a > 1$ and the target probability exceeds $e^{-36}$, initialize with the DiDonato–Morris (1986) asymptotic formula based on normal quantiles; otherwise solve the leading tail term for an initial value.
Then apply Halley's method in $\ln x$, safeguarded by a root bracket.
Final steps evaluate tails in double-word arithmetic and stop at the spacing of adjacent floating-point values in the result type.
For $a = 1$, use the closed form $x = -\ln(1 - p)$.
[Invert waiting-time distribution quantiles](../../examples/gamma-quantiles/) demonstrates their use.

## Complex arguments

`gamma_p`, `gamma_q`, `gamma_lower`, and `gamma_upper` also accept complex arguments; shape $a$ may remain real while $z$ is complex.
$z^a$ uses principal powers with a negative-real-axis cut; the imaginary sign selects the side.
The directly evaluated tail is chosen using $\lvert z\rvert$ and $\lvert a\rvert$.
For $\lvert z\rvert \le \lvert a\rvert + 1$ and for $\lvert z\rvert \le 40 + 2\lvert a\rvert$ with $\operatorname{Re} z \le 0$, evaluate the lower tail by power series.
When terms grow large at $\operatorname{Re} z < 0$, switch to a Kummer-transformed series to avoid cancellation among terms of order $e^{\lvert z\rvert}$.
Outside this region, evaluate the upper tail with Legendre's continued fraction in the right half-plane when $\lvert z\rvert > 1$ or $\lvert a\rvert > 1$, and with Tricomi's confluent hypergeometric function $U$ otherwise.
Form the other tail by complementation if subtraction amplifies cancellation by at most a factor of 2; otherwise evaluate it directly.
For $z \ne 0$ with $a = 0, -1, -2, \dots$, return the limits $P = 1$ and $Q = 0$.
At $z = 0$, $\operatorname{Re} a \le 0$ gives `DomainError` (`Undefined`), except that $\Gamma(0, 0)$ gives `DomainError` (`Pole`).
Logarithmic forms and inverses accept only real arguments.

## Accuracy guarantees

For real arguments, the following ULP targets are checked against independently generated high-precision reference data.
ULP is the spacing between adjacent floating-point values at the result; targets apply over the tested range.

| Function (real) | binary64 | binary32 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gamma_p`, `gamma_q`, `gamma_lower`, `gamma_upper`, `gamma_log_p`, `gamma_log_q` | 32 ULP | 1 ULP | Includes deep tails at $20 < a \le 10^{10}$. In a broader 4 160-point sample, maxima for $P$ and $Q$ reach 90 ULP |
| `gamma_p_inv`, `gamma_q_inv`, `gamma_p_inv_log`, `gamma_q_inv_log` | 32 ULP | 1 ULP | Includes $20 < a \le 10^{10}$ and endpoints of the output floating-point grid |

The 4 160-point maxima do not extend the target range; the 90 ULP for `gamma_p` and `gamma_q` shows that points outside the tested target range can exceed it.
binary32 arguments are evaluated in binary64 and rounded once.
Complex arguments have no uniform ULP specification; use options-form estimated errors (the error in `ValueWithError`, not an enclosure).

| Function | Standard-form arguments | Options form | Scoped API | Complex | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gamma_lower` | `a, x` | `gamma_lower_with_options` | `gamma_lower_scoped` | Yes | $\gamma(a, x)$ |
| `gamma_upper` | `a, x` | `gamma_upper_with_options` | `gamma_upper_scoped` | Yes | $\Gamma(a, x)$ |
| `gamma_p` | `a, x` | `gamma_p_with_options` | `gamma_p_scoped` | Yes | $P(a, x)$ |
| `gamma_q` | `a, x` | `gamma_q_with_options` | `gamma_q_scoped` | Yes | $Q(a, x)$ |
| `gamma_log_p` | `a, x` | `gamma_log_p_with_options` | `gamma_log_p_scoped` | No | $\ln P(a, x)$ |
| `gamma_log_q` | `a, x` | `gamma_log_q_with_options` | `gamma_log_q_scoped` | No | $\ln Q(a, x)$ |
| `gamma_p_inv` | `a, p` | `gamma_p_inv_with_options` | `gamma_p_inv_scoped` | No | $x$ satisfying $P(a, x) = p$ |
| `gamma_q_inv` | `a, q` | `gamma_q_inv_with_options` | `gamma_q_inv_scoped` | No | $x$ satisfying $Q(a, x) = q$ |
| `gamma_p_inv_log` | `a, ln p` | `gamma_p_inv_log_with_options` | `gamma_p_inv_log_scoped` | No | $x$ satisfying $\ln P(a, x) = \ln p$ |
| `gamma_q_inv_log` | `a, ln q` | `gamma_q_inv_log_with_options` | `gamma_q_inv_log_scoped` | No | $x$ satisfying $\ln Q(a, x) = \ln q$ |

Standard forms return only values; options forms accept `GammaOptions` and return `SpecialFunctionResult`.
Options results contain ordinary values and estimated errors, scaled and logarithmic values, termination status, algorithm identifiers, and work counts; ordinary values agree bitwise with standard forms.
Every function has a scoped API because evaluation work depends on the arguments.
Scoped APIs return the same results as options forms, report work to the parent execution context as nested counts, and follow the parent's cancellation and deadline.

`GammaOptions` contains only a work budget (`Budget`), defaulting to 65 536 units and changed with `GammaOptions::new(budget)` or `with_iteration_budget`.
The budget is not an accuracy request; evaluations reaching it give `ConvergenceFailure`.
`a` in `gamma_lower`, `gamma_upper`, `gamma_p`, and `gamma_q` uses `impl Parameter<T>`, allowing a real shape with complex `z`.
Logarithmic forms and inverses accept only the real type `S: RealScalar`.

## Summary and further reading

Incomplete gamma functions select closed forms, finite sums, Temme expansions, power series, and continued fractions by shape $a$ and ratio $x/a$, evaluating one tail directly and obtaining the other by complementation.
Compute small upper probabilities directly with `gamma_q` or `gamma_log_q`, avoiding $1 - P$.
Even for large shapes where $\Gamma(a)$ overflows, `gamma_lower` and `gamma_upper` undo normalization logarithmically and return ordinary values when representable; otherwise use options-form logarithms.
Find quantiles with `gamma_p_inv` and `gamma_q_inv`, passing probabilities below binary64 range to logarithmic inverses.
See [Invert waiting-time distribution quantiles](../../examples/gamma-quantiles/) for quantile use and rounding in $1 - q$, [Beta and regularized incomplete beta functions](../beta-and-error-functions/) for the same problem in beta distributions, and [Gamma functions, polygamma functions, and the Pochhammer symbol](../gamma-family/) for complete-gamma evaluation.
