Nonlinear pendulum angles, free Duffing oscillations, and periodic KdV solutions (cnoidal waves) have closed forms in Jacobi elliptic functions.
Jacobi elliptic functions invert incomplete elliptic integrals of the first kind, reducing to trigonometric functions at parameter $m = 0$ and hyperbolic functions at $m = 1$.
This page explains definitions and periods, evaluation using the arithmetic–geometric mean (AGM) and descending Landen transformations, real parameters outside $[0, 1]$, complex arguments, accuracy, and calling forms.
[Definitions of elliptic integrals and complete elliptic integrals K and E](../elliptic-and-legendre/) covers the parameter convention and complete integral $K(m)$; [Carlson symmetric forms and incomplete elliptic integrals](../carlson-symmetric-integrals/) covers evaluation of the incomplete first-kind integral $F(\phi \mid m)$.

## Definitions and periods

Solving the incomplete first-kind integral $u = F(\phi \mid m)$ for $\phi$ gives the amplitude $\phi = \operatorname{am}(u \mid m)$; Jacobi elliptic functions are then defined as follows (DLMF §22.2, §22.16).

$$
\operatorname{sn}(u \mid m) = \sin\phi, \qquad
\operatorname{cn}(u \mid m) = \cos\phi, \qquad
\operatorname{dn}(u \mid m) = \sqrt{1 - m \sin^2\phi}
$$


DLMF writes the modulus $k$ as the argument; `scioxide-special` functions accept the parameter $m = k^2$.
At $m = 0$, the functions become $\operatorname{sn} = \sin u$, $\operatorname{cn} = \cos u$, $\operatorname{dn} = 1$, and $\operatorname{am} = u$; at $m = 1$ they become $\operatorname{sn} = \tanh u$, $\operatorname{cn} = \operatorname{dn} = \operatorname{sech} u$, and $\operatorname{am} = \operatorname{gd} u$.
The definitions give the following identities.

$$
\operatorname{sn}^2 + \operatorname{cn}^2 = 1, \qquad \operatorname{dn}^2 + m \operatorname{sn}^2 = 1, \qquad
\frac{d}{du}\operatorname{sn}(u \mid m) = \operatorname{cn}(u \mid m)\operatorname{dn}(u \mid m)
$$


On the real axis for $0 \le m < 1$, $\operatorname{sn}$ and $\operatorname{cn}$ have period $4K(m)$ and $\operatorname{dn}$ has period $2K(m)$; their quarter-period values are $\operatorname{sn}(K \mid m) = 1$, $\operatorname{cn}(K \mid m) = 0$, and $\operatorname{dn}(K \mid m) = \sqrt{1 - m}$.
For $m \le 1$, $\operatorname{am}$ is the continuous increasing real branch satisfying $\operatorname{am}(u + 2K \mid m) = \operatorname{am}(u \mid m) + \pi$.
`jacobi_sn`, `jacobi_cn`, `jacobi_dn`, and `jacobi_amplitude` return these functions.

## Evaluation by AGM and descending Landen transformations

For real $0 < m < 1$, advance the AGM of $a_0 = 1$ and $b_0 = \sqrt{1 - m}$ ([Arithmetic–geometric mean](oxref:special-elliptic-agm)) until $|c_N| \le 2\varepsilon a_N$, retaining intermediate means ($\varepsilon$ is binary64 machine epsilon).
Because convergence is quadratic, the iteration count $N$ is only a few; exceeding 32 gives `NumericalInstability`.
Since $2K = \pi/a_N$, reduce $u$ by multiples of the half-period $2K$, reversing the signs of $\operatorname{sn}$ and $\operatorname{cn}$ after an odd number of reductions.
Starting from the reduced angle $a_N u$, reconstruct $\operatorname{sn}$, $\operatorname{cn}$, and $\operatorname{dn}$ by descending Landen transformations through the saved means in reverse order.
Each step recovers the amplitude with the inverse-sine formula of DLMF 22.20.
Reflect the half-period argument further about $K/2$, recovering small $\operatorname{cn}$ from complementary values.
Construct the amplitude as $j\pi + \operatorname{atan2}(\operatorname{sn}, \operatorname{cn})$ from the reduction count $j$ and $\operatorname{atan2}(\operatorname{sn}, \operatorname{cn})$.
For $m = 0$ and $m = 1$, use the closed forms above; for $|u| < 10^{-154}$, use $\operatorname{sn} = u$.
AGM and descent use binary64; binary32 arguments are widened to binary64 for evaluation.

### All real parameters

All real parameters are accepted.
Transform $m < 0$ and $m > 1$ to parameters in $[0, 1]$ using the real transformations of DLMF §22.17.
Set $\mu = -m/(1 - m)$; for $m < 0$, the transformation is

$$
\operatorname{sn}(u \mid m) = \frac{\operatorname{sn}(v \mid \mu)}{\sqrt{1 - m}\,\operatorname{dn}(v \mid \mu)}, \quad
\operatorname{cn}(u \mid m) = \frac{\operatorname{cn}(v \mid \mu)}{\operatorname{dn}(v \mid \mu)}, \quad
\operatorname{dn}(u \mid m) = \frac{1}{\operatorname{dn}(v \mid \mu)}, \qquad v = \sqrt{1 - m}\,u
$$

and for $m > 1$, set $w = \sqrt{m}\,u$ to obtain

$$
\operatorname{sn}(u \mid m) = \frac{\operatorname{sn}(w \mid 1/m)}{\sqrt{m}}, \qquad
\operatorname{cn}(u \mid m) = \operatorname{dn}(w \mid 1/m), \qquad
\operatorname{dn}(u \mid m) = \operatorname{cn}(w \mid 1/m)
$$


Thus, for $m > 1$, the real period of $\operatorname{sn}$ is $4K(1/m)/\sqrt{m}$, the maximum of $\operatorname{sn}$ is $1/\sqrt{m}$, and $\operatorname{dn}$ changes sign.
For $m > 1$, $\operatorname{am}$ is the real branch $\arcsin(\operatorname{sn})$ from this transformation, remaining within $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$.

### Complex arguments

For complex $u$ and real $m$, evaluate the real part with parameter $m$ and the imaginary part with complementary parameter $1 - m$, combining them through addition theorems (DLMF §22.8) and Jacobi's imaginary transformation.
For complex $m$, reduce the parameter by modular transformations, then evaluate quotients of Jacobi theta functions (DLMF §20.7, §22.2).
As functions of complex $u$, $\operatorname{sn}$, $\operatorname{cn}$, and $\operatorname{dn}$ have poles at $u = 2jK + (2l + 1)iK'$ ($K' = K(1 - m)$; $j, l$ are integers).
At or near poles, failure to determine a value gives `DomainError` (`Pole`) or `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).
Every finite real $u$ and $m$ is accepted.

## Error and computational cost

Work for real arguments sums AGM and descent steps, approximately twice the AGM step count.
Parameters outside $[0, 1]$ add one transformation unit.
A few to a dozen or so steps suffice, far below the default `EllipticOptions` budget of 65536 units.
Error estimates combine period-reduction error propagated through derivatives with a rounding model proportional to the descent step count (confidence `ModelBased`); they are not enclosures.
For large $|u|$, estimated error grows in proportion to the number of reduced periods.

## Accuracy guarantees

| Function (real arguments) | binary64 | binary32 | Test findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| `jacobi_sn`, `jacobi_cn`, `jacobi_dn`, `jacobi_amplitude` | 64 ULP | 64 ULP | Measured binary64 median for `jacobi_sn`: at most 1.2 ULP (not a maximum) |

Accuracy targets apply over the range tested against independently generated high-precision reference data.
Complex arguments have no uniform ULP specification; use options-form estimated errors.

## Functions and calling forms

All functions accept `NumericScalar` (`f32`, `f64`, `Complex<f32>`, `Complex<f64>`), using the same names for real and complex arguments.
Standard forms return only the value; options forms `*_with_options(.., options)` return `SpecialFunctionResult`, and scoped APIs `*_scoped(.., options, scope)` return the same result within a solve scope.

| Function | Standard form | Options form | Scoped API | Domain (real types) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\operatorname{sn}$, $\operatorname{cn}$, $\operatorname{dn}$, $\operatorname{am}$ | `jacobi_sn(u, m)`, `jacobi_cn(u, m)`, `jacobi_dn(u, m)`, `jacobi_amplitude(u, m)` | `jacobi_sn_with_options`, `jacobi_cn_with_options`, `jacobi_dn_with_options`, `jacobi_amplitude_with_options` | `jacobi_sn_scoped`, `jacobi_cn_scoped`, `jacobi_dn_scoped`, `jacobi_amplitude_scoped` | All finite real values |

Options forms and scoped APIs accept `EllipticOptions`, containing only a work budget `Budget`, defaulting to 65536 units.
Reaching the budget gives `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`) without an intermediate value.
Options results contain an estimated error, termination status (`SpecialFunctionTermination`), work counts, and algorithm identifier.
The parameter may remain real even for complex argument $u$ (`m: impl Parameter<T>`).
The four functions perform the same AGM and descent separately, so computing $\operatorname{sn}$, $\operatorname{cn}$, and $\operatorname{dn}$ at the same $(u, m)$ triples the work.

## Jacobi periods and parameter transformations

The next cell varies parameter $m$ from $-3$ to $3$, plotting $\operatorname{sn}$, $\operatorname{cn}$, and $\operatorname{dn}$ across one period $P$ of $\operatorname{sn}$.
$P$ is $4K(m)$ for $m < 1$ and $4K(1/m)/\sqrt{m}$ for $m > 1$, obtained from `elliptic_k`.
At $m = 1$ there is no period, so plot $0 \le u \le 8$.
Vertical lines mark $P/4$, $P/2$, and $3P/4$.
The table compares values at $u = P/4$ with closed forms ($\operatorname{sn} = 1$, $\operatorname{cn} = 0$, $\operatorname{dn} = \sqrt{1 - m}$ for $m \le 1$; $1/\sqrt{m}$, $\sqrt{1 - 1/m}$, $0$ for $m > 1$), also checking $\operatorname{am}(P/2 \mid m) = \pi$ for $m \le 1$.
$P$ is derived from fixed-work $K$ approximations, so this checks AGM evaluation against a different algorithm.
The remaining rows show maximum identity residuals over 401 points and the maximum difference between centered differences of $\operatorname{sn}$ (step $10^{-5}$) and $\operatorname{cn}\,\operatorname{dn}$.
Centered-difference error combines truncation $h^2$ and rounding $\varepsilon/h$, so a final-row difference of about $10^{-10}$ is expected.

```rust
//| id: special-jacobi-periods
//| ref: special-jacobi-periods
//| title: "Check Jacobi elliptic periods and identities for each parameter"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   parameter: {"type":"range","label":"Parameter m [-]","description":"Negative values and values above 1 use real-parameter transformations.","min":-3,"max":3,"step":0.05,"value":0.9}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"negative","label":"m = −3","description":"Imaginary-modulus transformation. dn increases from 1 to 2.","inputs":{"parameter":-3}}
//|   - {"id":"hyperbolic","label":"m = 1","description":"No period; sn = tanh and cn = dn = sech.","inputs":{"parameter":1}}
//|   - {"id":"reciprocal","label":"m = 3","description":"Reciprocal-parameter transformation. The maximum of sn drops to 1/√3.","inputs":{"parameter":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":10.312368453392693,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/4/3","range":{"min":-1e-14,"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/5/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/7/1","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|   - case: negative
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":2,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/6/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: hyperbolic
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"max":1e-9}}
//|   - case: reciprocal
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.5773502691896258,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
use scioxide_special::{elliptic_k, jacobi_amplitude, jacobi_cn, jacobi_dn, jacobi_sn};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let m = parameter;
let digits16 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 16 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
// Real period P of sn: 4K(m) for m ≤ 1, 4K(1/m)/√m for m > 1 (reciprocal-parameter transformation).
// At m = 1 there is no period; plot [0, 8].
let period = if m < 1.0 {
    Some(4.0 * elliptic_k(m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
} else if m > 1.0 {
    Some(4.0 * elliptic_k(1.0 / m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? / m.sqrt())
} else {
    None
};
let span = period.unwrap_or(8.0);
let (mut sn_points, mut cn_points, mut dn_points) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut pythagoras, mut modulus, mut derivative) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
let step = 1e-5;
for index in 0..=400 {
    let u = span * f64::from(index) / 400.0;
    let sn = jacobi_sn(u, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let cn = jacobi_cn(u, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let dn = jacobi_dn(u, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    pythagoras = pythagoras.max((sn * sn + cn * cn - 1.0).abs());
    modulus = modulus.max((dn * dn + m * sn * sn - 1.0).abs());
    // Check d sn/du = cn dn using centered differences (error O(h²)).
    let ahead = jacobi_sn(u + step, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let behind = jacobi_sn(u - step, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    derivative = derivative.max(((ahead - behind) / (2.0 * step) - cn * dn).abs());
    sn_points.push([u, sn]);
    cn_points.push([u, cn]);
    dn_points.push([u, dn]);
}
let quarters: Vec<_> = (1..4)
    .filter_map(|q| period.map(|p| p * f64::from(q) / 4.0))
    .map(|u| serde_json::json!({ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": u }))
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "sn(u|m)", "points": sn_points }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "cn(u|m)", "points": cn_points }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "dn(u|m)", "points": dn_points }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "u [-]",
        "yLabel": "Jacobi elliptic functions [-]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": span,
        "yMin": -2.0,
        "yMax": 2.0,
        "annotations": quarters
    })
);

// Quarter-period values. Expected values are closed forms for each parameter range.
let mut rows: Vec<(String, f64, Option<f64>, Option<f64>)> = Vec::new();
if let Some(p) = period {
    let quarter = p / 4.0;
    let expected_sn = if m > 1.0 { 1.0 / m.sqrt() } else { 1.0 };
    let expected_cn = if m > 1.0 { (1.0 - 1.0 / m).sqrt() } else { 0.0 };
    let expected_dn = if m > 1.0 { 0.0 } else { (1.0 - m).sqrt() };
    for (name, value, expected) in [
        ("Period P", p, None),
        (
            "sn(P/4|m)",
            jacobi_sn(quarter, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
            Some(expected_sn),
        ),
        (
            "cn(P/4|m)",
            jacobi_cn(quarter, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
            Some(expected_cn),
        ),
        (
            "dn(P/4|m)",
            jacobi_dn(quarter, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
            Some(expected_dn),
        ),
    ] {
        rows.push((name.into(), value, expected, expected.map(|x| value - x)));
    }
    if m <= 1.0 {
        // am(2K|m) = π (continuous real branch for m ≤ 1).
        let am = jacobi_amplitude(p / 2.0, m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        rows.push(("am(P/2|m)".into(), am, Some(PI), Some(am - PI)));
    }
}
rows.push(("max |sn² + cn² − 1|".into(), pythagoras, None, None));
rows.push(("max |dn² + m sn² − 1|".into(), modulus, None, None));
rows.push(("max |Centered difference − cn dn|".into(), derivative, None, None));
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "SciOxide value", "type": "number", "format": digits16 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form", "type": "number", "format": digits16, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
```

For the default $m = 0.9$, $P = 10.31236845339269$; $\operatorname{sn}$ has a nearly trapezoidal shape, while $\operatorname{dn}$ falls to $\sqrt{0.1} \approx 0.316$ and returns to 1 at $P/2$.
Quarter-period differences are at most $1.5 \times 10^{-16}$; $\operatorname{am}(P/2 \mid m)$ differs from $\pi$ by $8.9 \times 10^{-16}$, identity residuals are at most $6.7 \times 10^{-16}$, and centered-difference discrepancy is $7.2 \times 10^{-11}$.
For $m = -3$, $\operatorname{dn}$ grows from 1 to $\sqrt{1 - m} = 2$; for $m = 3$, the maximum of $\operatorname{sn}$ falls to $1/\sqrt{3} \approx 0.577$ and $\operatorname{dn}$ changes sign, taking the role of $\operatorname{cn}$.
At $m = 1$, $\operatorname{sn} = \tanh u$ approaches 1 and $\operatorname{cn}$ and $\operatorname{dn}$ share the curve $\operatorname{sech} u$.
The residual of $\operatorname{dn}^2 + m\operatorname{sn}^2 = 1$ is at most $1.4 \times 10^{-15}$ for every $m$, showing that parameter transformations keep the three functions consistent.

## Summary and further reading

Pass parameter $m = k^2$ to Jacobi elliptic functions; all finite real $u$ and $m$ are accepted.
$0 < m < 1$ uses AGM and descending Landen transformations; $m < 0$ and $m > 1$ are mapped into $[0, 1]$ using DLMF §22.17 transformations.
For $m > 1$, the maximum of $\operatorname{sn}$ drops to $1/\sqrt{m}$ and $\operatorname{am}$ uses the branch $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$, behaving differently from the continuous amplitude for $m \le 1$.
Real arguments target 64 ULP; for complex arguments use options-form estimated errors.
See [Compute the period of a large-amplitude pendulum](../../examples/pendulum-period/) for a physical application, [Carlson symmetric forms and incomplete elliptic integrals](../carlson-symmetric-integrals/) for the inverse incomplete integrals, and [Elliptic integral APIs](../../api/elliptic/) for individual specifications.
