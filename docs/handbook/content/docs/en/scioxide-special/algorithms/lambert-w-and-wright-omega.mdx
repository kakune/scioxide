The Lambert W function solves the equation $w e^w = z$ for $w$.
Equations in which the unknown appears both in an exponent and as a coefficient, such as $x = a + b e^{cx}$, reduce to this form by a change of variables.
Wien's displacement law determining the peak wavelength of blackbody radiation, the current through a diode with a series resistor, the characteristic roots $\lambda = W_k(a\tau)/\tau$ of the delay differential equation $y'(t) = a\,y(t - \tau)$, and the final size of an epidemic in the SIR model all have closed forms in terms of Lambert W.
The Wright ω function solves $\omega + \ln\omega = z$ and provides a way to compute $W_0(e^x)$ without forming $e^x$.

This page explains the branches and cuts of both functions, the difficulties near the branch point $-1/e$, the initial approximations and Halley iteration used by scioxide-special, endpoint conventions, and APIs.
Three cells examine self-consistency and conditioning of the real branches, endpoints and complex branches, and the relationship between Wright ω and $W_0(e^x)$.

## Definitions and branches

### Real branches

$f(w) = w e^w$ attains its minimum $-1/e$ at $w = -1$, decreases for $w < -1$, and increases for $w > -1$.
Thus, for real $x$, the equation $w e^w = x$ has no real solution for $x < -1/e$, two for $-1/e < x < 0$, and one for $x \ge 0$ (DLMF §4.13).
The solution on the $w \ge -1$ side is called the principal branch $W_0(x)$, and the solution on the $w \le -1$ side is called $W_{-1}(x)$.

$W_0$ is defined for $x \ge -1/e$, satisfies $W_0(-1/e) = -1$, $W_0(0) = 0$, and $W_0(e) = 1$, and increases monotonically.
$W_{-1}$ is defined for $-1/e \le x < 0$, decreases monotonically from $W_{-1}(-1/e) = -1$, and diverges to $-\infty$ as $x \to 0^-$.
The two branches meet at the branch point $x = -1/e$.

Implicit differentiation gives the following derivative.

$$
W'(x) = \frac{W(x)}{x\,(1 + W(x))} = \frac{1}{e^{W(x)}\,(1 + W(x))}
$$

At the branch point, $1 + W = 0$, so the derivative diverges.

### Complex branches

For complex $z$, the equation $w e^w = z$ has infinitely many solutions, divided into branches $W_k(z)$ indexed by integers $k$ (the numbering of Corless et al. 1996; DLMF §4.13).
The cut of $W_0$ is $(-\infty, -1/e]$, and the cut of every other branch is $(-\infty, 0]$.
On a cut, the signed zero of the imaginary part ($+0$ or $-0$) selects the value on the upper or lower bank.
$W_0(0) = 0$, and all branches with $k \ne 0$ diverge logarithmically at $z = 0$.

The real function $W_{-1}(x)$ ($-1/e \le x < 0$) equals the value of complex branch $k = -1$ on the upper bank of the cut (imaginary part $+0$).
For large $|z|$, set $L_1 = \ln z + 2\pi i k$ and $L_2 = \ln L_1$; the following asymptotic expansion holds (Corless et al. 1996, Eq. 4.19).

$$
W_k(z) = L_1 - L_2 + \frac{L_2}{L_1} + \frac{L_2(L_2 - 2)}{2L_1^2} + O\!\left(\left(\frac{L_2}{L_1}\right)^3\right)
$$

This expansion shows that, for large $\lvert z\rvert$, the imaginary part of branch $k$ approaches $2\pi k + \arg z$.

### Wright ω function

The Wright ω function solves $\omega + \ln\omega = z$ and can be written in terms of Lambert W as follows (Corless–Jeffrey 2002).

$$
\omega(z) = W_{K(z)}(e^z), \qquad K(z) = \left\lceil \frac{\operatorname{Im} z - \pi}{2\pi} \right\rceil
$$

$K(z)$ is called the unwinding number; it selects the branch by restoring the multiple of $2\pi$ lost in the argument of $e^z$.
For real $x$, $K = 0$ and $\omega(x) = W_0(e^x)$; the value is positive and increases monotonically.
As $x \to -\infty$, $\omega(x) \approx e^x$; as $x \to +\infty$, $\omega(x) \approx x - \ln x$.

Complex $\omega$ is single-valued but is not entire.
Its value changes discontinuously across the two half-lines $\operatorname{Im} z = \pm\pi$, $\operatorname{Re} z \le -1$; on them, the signed zero of the imaginary part selects the bank.
The endpoints $z = -1 \pm i\pi$ are branch points with $\omega = -1$.
Elsewhere, $\omega$ is continuous and satisfies $\omega + \ln\omega = z$ with the principal logarithm.

## Conditioning near the branch point

A condition number measures the sensitivity of a function value to small changes in its input.
Measured as the ratio of relative output error to relative input error, the condition number of Lambert W is as follows.

$$
\kappa(x) = \left\lvert \frac{x\,W'(x)}{W(x)} \right\rvert = \frac{1}{\lvert 1 + W(x)\rvert}
$$

When $W$ is away from $-1$, $\kappa$ is of order 1, but it diverges near the branch point.
Near the branch point, call $q = 1 + e\,x$ the branch coordinate and set $p = \pm\sqrt{2q}$; the following Puiseux series holds (Corless et al. 1996, §4).

$$
W(x) = -1 + p - \frac{p^2}{3} + \frac{11}{72}p^3 - \frac{43}{540}p^4 + \cdots
$$

$W_0$ lies on the $p > 0$ side, and $W_{-1}$ on the $p < 0$ side.
Since $W + 1$ is proportional to $\sqrt{q}$, at a point where $x$ is a distance $\delta$ from $-1/e$, $W + 1 \approx \sqrt{2e\delta}$.
For example, when $x$ is one double-precision ULP ($5.6 \times 10^{-17}$) away from $-1/e$, $W + 1$ is $1.7 \times 10^{-8}$.

The difficulty is that evaluating $q = 1 + e\,x$ directly in double precision produces an error in $e\,x$ (about $5 \times 10^{-17}$) from the rounding error in $e$ (about $1.4 \times 10^{-16}$) that is as large as $q$ itself next to the branch point.
scioxide-special stores $e$ as the sum of high and low double-precision parts, adds the product of $x$ and the high part to 1 with a fused multiply-add (adding to 1 without rounding the product), then adds the product with the low part to obtain $q$.
This gives $q$ for the binary value $x$ with small relative error even at inputs next to the branch point, allowing $W(x)$ to be computed from it.
However, if $x$ itself contains rounding from another calculation, that error is amplified by $\kappa$ in $W$.

## Evaluation methods

### Real branches

The real functions `lambert_w0` and `lambert_wm1` choose an initial approximation according to the argument and refine it by Halley iteration.
Halley iteration repeatedly applies the following update to the equation $f(w) = 0$; near a simple root, the number of correct digits roughly triples at each step (cubic convergence).

$$
w_{n+1} = w_n - \frac{f(w_n)}{f'(w_n) - \dfrac{f(w_n)\,f''(w_n)}{2 f'(w_n)}}
$$

Compared with Newton's method (quadratic convergence), it additionally evaluates $f''$, but for Lambert W, $f''$ has almost the same expression as $f'$, so the extra work per step is small.

The expression $w e^w - x$ is not used directly for $f$.
This is because $e^w$ overflows for large $w$ and $e^w$ underflows for large negative $w$.
The implementation uses the following forms according to the argument range.

| Range | Initial approximation | Equation for iteration |
|---|---|---|
| $W_0$ with $\lvert x\rvert < 10^{-8}$ | No iteration | $W_0(x) \approx x - x^2 + \frac{3}{2}x^3$ (omitted terms are smaller than the unit roundoff) |
| Branch coordinate $q = 1 + e\,x \le 0.2$ ($x \le -0.294$) | Nine terms of the Puiseux series | $(u - 1)e^u + 1 - q = 0$ in $u = W + 1$. Evaluate the left-hand side with 23 terms of $u^2\sum_{n \ge 2}(n-1)u^{n-2}/n!$ to avoid subtracting quantities of the same order as $q$ |
| Other $W_0$ | $x(1 - x + \frac{3}{2}x^2)$ for $x < 1/2$, $0.567 + 0.36(x - 1)$ for $x < 2$, and $\ln x - \ln\ln x$ above that | Exponential residual $w - x e^{-w} = 0$ |
| Other $W_{-1}$ | $\ell - \ln(-\ell)$, $\ell = \ln(-x)$ | Logarithmic residual $w + \ln(-w) - \ell = 0$ |

Iteration stops when the correction $\lvert f/f'\rvert$ at the new iterate is at most $\varepsilon\lvert W\rvert/2$ for real values or $8\varepsilon\lvert W\rvert$ for complex values ($\varepsilon$ is machine epsilon and $W$ is the current function value).
If rounding returns the iteration to either of the preceding two iterates, the new iterate, current iterate, and preceding two iterates are compared, and the one with the smallest residual is selected.
If its correction has not fallen to at most $8\varepsilon\lvert W\rvert$, evaluation stops with `ConvergenceFailure` (`Stagnation`).
The estimated error in the form with options is the sum of the last correction, an estimate of residual rounding divided by $\lvert f'\rvert$, and $16\varepsilon$ times the magnitude of the value.

One Halley step and one term of the Puiseux series each count as one work unit; evaluation stops when the work limit in `LambertOptions` (65536 by default) is exhausted.
The form with options returns the last iterate with termination status `IterationLimit` (or `ConvergenceFailure` if no iteration has completed); the standard form returns `ConvergenceFailure`.
Inputs of type `f32` are also widened to double precision, evaluated by the same kernel, and rounded at the end.

### Endpoint conventions

Since $-1/e$ is not representable in binary floating point, the value $x_e$ rounded for each type serves as the endpoint.
At $x = x_e$, both `lambert_w0` and `lambert_wm1` return exactly $-1$, and the derivative in the form with options is non-finite.
The representable number immediately below $x_e$ is rejected with `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`), regardless of its position relative to the true $-1/e$.
From the number immediately above $x_e$ onward, evaluation proceeds normally using the branch-coordinate calculation above.

Complex `lambert_w_complex` does not use this convention and evaluates $x_e$ as its exact binary value.
Since double-precision $x_e$ is slightly below the true $-1/e$, $W_0(x_e + 0i)$ is $-1$ with an imaginary part of order $10^{-8}$.

### Complex branches and Wright ω

For branches adjacent to the branch point ($k = 0$ and $k = \mp 1$ according to the bank of the cut), `lambert_w_complex(z, k)` uses the same iteration in $u = W + 1$ as the real functions when $\lvert q\rvert \le 0.2$.
Elsewhere on the principal branch, the initial approximation comes from a table of local linear approximations for moderate $\lvert z\rvert$ and from the asymptotic expansion above outside that range.
Other branches start from the Puiseux series near the branch point and from the first two asymptotic terms $L_1 - \ln L_1$ elsewhere.
Iteration uses the exponential residual $w - z e^{-w}$ on the principal branch (when $\lvert\operatorname{Re} w\rvert < 500$), and the logarithmic residual $w + \ln w - (\ln z + 2\pi i k)$ on other branches and when $\lvert\operatorname{Re} w\rvert \ge 500$.
The residual of $w e^w = z$ alone cannot distinguish branches, so after convergence, $w + \ln w - L_1$ is checked for closeness to 0; convergence to the wrong branch returns `NumericalInstability`.

Real `wright_omega` returns $\omega = e^x$ for $x \le -36$ (the next term $e^{2x}$ is smaller than the unit roundoff), iterates from $t = e^x$ with the exponential residual $w - t e^{-w}$ for $x < 0$, and uses the logarithmic residual $w + \ln w - x$ for $x \ge 0$.
None of these paths forms $e^x$ for large $x$ where $e^x$ would overflow.
When $x$ is so small that $\omega$ underflows, the logarithmic form with options retains the finite value $\ln\omega = x$.
Complex `wright_omega` uses branch-coordinate iteration within a distance of 0.05 from the branch points $-1 \pm i\pi$.
For $-700 < \operatorname{Re} z < 600$ and $\lvert\operatorname{Im} z\rvert < 10^8$, it evaluates $W_K(e^z)$, selected by the unwinding number, with the complex-branch kernel above.
For $\operatorname{Re} z \le -700$ and $\lvert\operatorname{Im} z\rvert \le \pi$, it returns $\omega = e^z$; elsewhere outside the central range, it iterates from $z - \ln z$ using the logarithmic residual.

## Accuracy guarantees

| Function | Real arguments | Complex arguments |
|---|---|---|
| `lambert_w0`, `lambert_wm1` | Target 64 ULP for both `f32` and `f64`, checked at every reference-data point. Return an absolute error bound reflecting the condition number near the branch point | Not applicable |
| `lambert_w_complex` | Not applicable | No uniform ULP bound; return an estimated error derived from the correction and residual |
| `wright_omega` | Target 64 ULP for both `f32` and `f64`, checked at every reference-data point | No uniform bound; the estimated error includes propagation of rounding to the output type through $\omega + \ln\omega$ |

The reference data consists of independently computed arbitrary-precision reference values.
Reference tests check that the three real functions have errors within 3 ULP for `f64` and 1 ULP for `f32` at randomly selected points, and that the actual error does not exceed the estimated error from the form with options at any tested point, including near the branch point.
Complex arguments are tested against independent references within the larger of relative $2 \times 10^{-12}$ and absolute $2 \times 10^{-14}$ (`f64`).
The estimated error is not an enclosing interval and need not contain the true value at points outside the reference data.
The iteration count depends on the argument and the quality of the initial approximation, so the cells below show measured work units.

## API

| Function | Scalar | Form with options | Scoped API | Errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lambert_w0(x)` | `RealScalar` only | `lambert_w0_with_options` | `lambert_w0_scoped` | $x < x_e$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) |
| `lambert_wm1(x)` | `RealScalar` only | `lambert_wm1_with_options` | `lambert_wm1_scoped` | $x = 0$ gives `DomainError` (`Pole`); $x > 0$ and $x < x_e$ give `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) |
| `lambert_w_complex(z, branch)` | `Complex<S>` (`S: RealScalar`) and branch index `branch: i32` | `lambert_w_complex_with_options` | `lambert_w_complex_scoped` | $z = 0$, $k \ne 0$ give `DomainError` (`Pole`). A failed branch check gives `NumericalInstability` |
| `wright_omega(x)` | `NumericScalar` (real and complex) | `wright_omega_with_options` | `wright_omega_scoped` | No domain errors |

All functions use `LambertOptions`, which contains only a work limit (iteration count).
Non-finite inputs give `NonFinite`; on reaching the limit, the standard form gives `ConvergenceFailure`.
Scoped APIs pass the iteration count as a nested count within the parent solve and obey the parent's cancellation and deadline.

The real branches are separate functions, `lambert_w0` and `lambert_wm1`, and do not accept complex inputs.
Use `lambert_w_complex` for complex values or branches with $k \ne 0, -1$.
To obtain the real value of $W_{-1}$ for real $-1/e < x < 0$ through the complex function, pass `Complex::new(x, 0.0)` with imaginary part $+0$ and `branch = -1`.
When $W_0(e^x)$ is needed at $x$ where $e^x$ falls outside the representable range, use `wright_omega(x)` rather than `lambert_w0(x.exp())`.

## Self-consistency and conditioning of the real branches

The next cell forms $x = t e^t$ from parameter $t$ and checks whether $W_0(x)$ for $t \ge -1$ or $W_{-1}(x)$ for $t < -1$ recovers $t$.
Alongside the input $t$, it lists points on both sides of the branch point $t = -1$ and points with large $\lvert t\rvert$.
Since computing $x$ includes double-precision rounding, the recovery error can be amplified by the condition number $\kappa = 1/\lvert 1 + t\rvert$.
The table also shows the estimated error from the form with options, work units, and selected method (algorithm identifier).

```rust
//| id: special-lambert-self-consistency
//| ref: special-lambert-self-consistency
//| title: "Recover t from t e^t using the real Lambert W branches"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   t: {"type":"range","label":"Parameter t [-]","description":"Recover with W₀ for t ≥ −1 and W₋₁ for t < −1. Appears in the first table row.","min":-30,"max":30,"step":0.1,"value":1.0}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"lower-branch","label":"t = −3","description":"Logarithmic-residual path for W₋₁.","inputs":{"t":-3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"recover","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"recover","path":"/rows/0/7","equals":1}
//|       - {"artifact":"recover","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"max":1e-10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"recover","path":"/rows/3/8","equals":"real_branch_point_halley"}
//|       - {"artifact":"recover","path":"/rows/5/4","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"recover","path":"/rows/8/4","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: lower-branch
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"recover","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"W₋₁"}
//|       - {"artifact":"recover","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-15}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_special::{LambertOptions, lambert_w0_with_options, lambert_wm1_with_options};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let options = LambertOptions::default();
// List the input t, points on both sides of the branch point t = −1, and large |t|.
let points = [
    t,
    -1.0 + 1e-3,
    -1.0 + 1e-6,
    -1.0 - 1e-6,
    -1.0 - 1e-3,
    -20.0,
    0.5,
    10.0,
    300.0,
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for point in points {
    let x = point * RealScalar::exp(point);
    let (branch, result) = if point >= -1.0 {
        ("W₀", lambert_w0_with_options(x, options))
    } else {
        ("W₋₁", lambert_wm1_with_options(x, options))
    };
    let result = result.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let value = result.value().ok_or("Missing value")?;
    let w = *value.value();
    rows.push((
        branch,
        point,
        x,
        w,
        (w - point).abs() / point.abs().max(1.0),
        1.0 / (1.0 + point).abs(),
        value.absolute_error(),
        result.counts().iterations(),
        result.algorithm().method().to_string(),
    ));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Branch" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "t [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "x = t e^t [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "W(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 16 } },
    { "key": "4", "label": "|W − t|/max(|t|, 1) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Condition number 1/|1 + t| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Estimated error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Work [units]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "Method" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "recover" })
);
```

For the default $t = 1$, $x = e$ recovers $W_0(e) = 1$ exactly with one work unit (one Halley step).
$t = 0.5$, $10$, $300$, and $-20$ are also recovered with zero relative difference, using 2–3 work units.
Even at $x = 300\,e^{300} \approx 5.8 \times 10^{132}$, exponential-residual iteration avoids forming $e^w$, so no intermediate overflow occurs.

The four rows near the branch point ($t = -1 \pm 10^{-3}$, $-1 \pm 10^{-6}$) use the branch-coordinate path (`real_branch_point_halley`); their ten work units comprise nine Puiseux terms and one Halley step.
In these four rows, $\lvert W - t\rvert$ is $7 \times 10^{-14}$ and $3 \times 10^{-11}$, much larger than the estimated error $3.6 \times 10^{-15}$.
This is the result of amplifying double-precision rounding in $x = t e^t$ by condition numbers $10^3$ and $10^6$, rather than function evaluation error.
Indeed, $t = -1 + 10^{-6}$ and $t = -1 - 10^{-6}$ round to the same double-precision $x$, and $W_0$ and $W_{-1}$ return the two solutions for that same $x$.
The estimated error concerns the given binary value $x$; account separately for the effect of input error by multiplying that error by the condition number.

## Endpoint conventions and complex branches

The first table in the next cell lists real-branch results for the rounded endpoint $x_e$ in double and single precision, its two neighbors, and inputs outside the domain.
Calls that fail show the `kind()` classification and `view()` reason instead of a value.
The final two rows of the same table check that complex `lambert_w_complex` evaluates $x_e$ as its binary value and that $k = -1$ on the bank with imaginary part $+0$ agrees with real $W_{-1}$.

## Wright ω and W₀(e^x)

The first table in the next cell compares `wright_omega(x)` and `lambert_w0(e^x)` at the input $x$ and several fixed points, and shows the residual of the defining equation $\omega + \ln\omega = x$.
For $x > 709.78$, $e^x$ exceeds the double-precision range, so `lambert_w0` rejects it as a non-finite input and the comparison column is blank.
The second table evaluates complex $\omega$ above and below the cut $\operatorname{Im} z = \pi$, $\operatorname{Re} z \le -1$ ($\operatorname{Re} z = -2$), above and below the uncut region $\operatorname{Re} z = 0$, and near the branch point $-1 + i\pi$.
The upper and lower points are $\operatorname{Im} z = \pi \pm 10^{-9}$.

The final row uses $-1 + i\pi$ formed with double-precision $\pi$; the result $\omega = -1 + 1.1 \times 10^{-8}i$ differs from $-1$ by order $\sqrt{\delta}$.
This results from the difference $1.2 \times 10^{-16}$ between double-precision $\pi$ and the true $\pi$ being amplified by conditioning at the branch point.

## Summary

Real Lambert W has two functions, `lambert_w0` ($W \ge -1$) and `lambert_wm1` ($W \le -1$), both accepting only `RealScalar`; use `lambert_w_complex(z, k)` for complex values and other branches.
Evaluation uses Halley iteration from an initial approximation selected by argument range; near the branch point, the branch coordinate $q = 1 + e\,x$ is computed with small relative error using fused multiply-add and the low part of $e$, and iteration proceeds in $u = W + 1$.
At the rounded endpoint $x_e$, both real branches return exactly $-1$ and reject the number immediately below with `OutsideDomain`, whereas the complex function evaluates $x_e$ as its binary value.
Near the branch point, the error in the result is dominated by input rounding multiplied by the condition number $1/\lvert 1 + W\rvert$ rather than the function's estimated error, so check the input precision first.
Compute $W_0(e^x)$ at $x$ where $e^x$ falls outside the representable range using `wright_omega(x)`, and remember that complex $\omega$ has cuts $\operatorname{Im} z = \pm\pi$, $\operatorname{Re} z \le -1$.
