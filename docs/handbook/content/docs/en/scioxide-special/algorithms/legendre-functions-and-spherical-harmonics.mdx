Legendre functions appear in multipole expansions of gravitational and electrostatic fields, angular components of spherically symmetric problems, and Gauss–Legendre quadrature nodes as solutions of Laplace's equation separated in spherical coordinates.
At degrees above several thousand, the associated functions $P_l^m$ leave the binary64 range as the order $m$ increases, while Legendre polynomials $P_l$ become sums with substantial cancellation near their zeros.
This page explains definitions and conventions for Legendre functions of the first and second kinds, associated Legendre functions, and radial eigenfunctions $H_l$ on hyperbolic space, the evaluation recurrences and their stability, work limits, accuracy, and calling forms.
[Spherical harmonics](../spherical-harmonics/) covers associated functions normalized on the unit sphere, while [Angular momentum coupling coefficients and Wigner symbols](../angular-momentum-coupling/) covers angular momentum coupling coefficients.

## Definitions of Legendre functions

Legendre's differential equation is

$$
(1 - x^2) y'' - 2x y' + l(l + 1) y = 0
$$

and has the polynomial solution $P_l(x)$ for integer degree $l \ge 0$.
$P_l$ is defined by Rodrigues' formula $P_l(x) = \frac{1}{2^l l!}\frac{d^l}{dx^l}(x^2 - 1)^l$, normalized to $P_l(1) = 1$ and $P_l(-1) = (-1)^l$ (DLMF §18.5).
Its orthogonality relation on $[-1, 1]$ is $\int_{-1}^1 P_l P_k \, dx = \frac{2}{2l + 1}\delta_{lk}$.
`legendre_p(l, x)` returns $P_l(x)$ for every real $x$, including $|x| > 1$.

As another independent solution, `legendre_q(l, x)` returns the Ferrers function of the second kind on $-1 \le x \le 1$, with starting values (DLMF §14.5):

$$
Q_0(x) = \operatorname{artanh} x = \frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1 + x}{1 - x}, \qquad Q_1(x) = x \operatorname{artanh} x - 1
$$


$Q_l$ has logarithmic singularities at $x = \pm 1$ and returns $+\infty$ at $x = 1$; at $x = -1$, it returns $-\infty$ for even $l$ and $+\infty$ for odd $l$.
$|x| > 1$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
The Legendre function of the second kind used for $|x| > 1$ ($Q_l$ rather than a Ferrers function) is not provided.
$P_l$ and $Q_l$ satisfy the same three-term recurrence (Bonnet's recurrence):

$$
(l + 1) F_{l+1}(x) = (2l + 1) x F_l(x) - l F_{l-1}(x)
$$

The Casorati determinant of the two solutions is $P_l Q_{l-1} - P_{l-1} Q_l = 1/l$ (DLMF §14.10).

### Associated Legendre functions

For integer degree $l \ge 0$ and order $|m| \le l$, `legendre_p_assoc(l, m, x)` returns the Ferrers function of the first kind on $-1 \le x \le 1$ (DLMF §14.6, §14.9):

$$
P_l^m(x) = (-1)^m (1 - x^2)^{m/2}\frac{d^m}{dx^m}P_l(x) \quad (0 \le m \le l), \qquad
P_l^{-m}(x) = (-1)^m \frac{(l - m)!}{(l + m)!} P_l^m(x)
$$


The factor $(-1)^m$ is the Condon–Shortley phase, which `legendre_p_assoc` includes.
For example, $P_1^1(x) = -\sqrt{1 - x^2}$.
When comparing with conventions that omit the Condon–Shortley phase (some geophysics references, or the direct use of `(1 − x²)^{m/2} dᵐP_l/dxᵐ`), reverse the sign for odd $m$.
For $m \ne 0$, the value is zero at $x = \pm 1$.
For fixed order $m$, the orthogonality relation is

$$
\int_{-1}^1 P_l^m(x) P_k^m(x)\, dx = \frac{2}{2l + 1}\frac{(l + m)!}{(l - m)!}\delta_{lk}
$$


### Radial eigenfunctions on hyperbolic space

The radial eigenfunctions $H_l(\lambda, \eta)$ of the Laplace operator on three-dimensional hyperbolic space appear in field expansions in an open Robertson–Walker universe.
For nonnegative integer $l$, real wavenumber $\lambda$, and radial coordinate $\eta \ge 0$, `legendre_h3d(l, lambda, eta)` returns the function normalized by

$$
H_0 = \frac{\sin(\lambda\eta)}{\lambda \sinh\eta}, \qquad
H_1 = \frac{\coth\eta \, H_0 - \cos(\lambda\eta)/\sinh\eta}{\sqrt{1 + \lambda^2}},
$$

$$
\sqrt{\lambda^2 + (l + 1)^2}\, H_{l+1} = (2l + 1)\coth\eta\, H_l - \sqrt{\lambda^2 + l^2}\, H_{l-1}
$$


With $\lambda\eta$ fixed, $\eta \to 0$ gives $H_l(\lambda, \eta) \to j_l(\lambda\eta)$ (the spherical Bessel function); up to a factor depending only on $\lambda$ and $l$, it equals the conical function $(\sinh\eta)^{-1/2} P^{-1/2-l}_{-1/2+i\lambda}(\cosh\eta)$ (DLMF §14.20).
$H_l$ is even in $\lambda$, takes the continuous limit at $\lambda = 0$, and at $\eta = 0$ has $H_0 = 1$ and $H_l = 0$ for $l \ge 1$.
$\eta < 0$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`), while overflow in $\lambda\eta$ gives `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).

## Evaluation by recurrence and stability

### Forward recurrence for Legendre functions

A three-term recurrence has growing and decaying solutions; when the desired solution decays faster than the other solution (is recessive), forward recurrence amplifies rounding error.
For $|x| < 1$, both $P_l$ and $Q_l$ oscillate with amplitude $O(l^{-1/2})$, and neither is recessive, so recurrence in increasing $l$ does not produce exponential error growth.
`legendre_p` advances Bonnet's recurrence by $l - 1$ steps in binary64 from $P_0 = 1$ and $P_1 = x$.
At each step, fused multiply-add (FMA) extracts product and division rounding errors and propagates them with the value through the same linear recurrence (residual correction).
Near zeros, the correction supplies most of the answer, so evaluation switches to double-word arithmetic, representing one value with two double-precision numbers.
For $|x| > 1$, $P_l$ grows exponentially with $l$; evaluation advances in double-word arithmetic while scaling the state by powers of two, and returns `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`) if the result exceeds the binary64 range.
`legendre_q` forms $Q_0$ with a compensated logarithm and advances the same recurrence from $Q_1 = xQ_0 - 1$ in double-word arithmetic.
$P_l$ at $x = \pm 1$ has a closed form and requires zero work.

To keep the recurrence coefficient $2l - 1$ exact as a binary64 integer, $l > 2^{52}$ gives `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`), except for $P_l$ at $x = \pm 1$.
One step consumes one unit of the work limit in `LegendreOptions`; the default limit of 65536 permits $l$ up to about 65537.
For higher degrees, increase the limit with `LegendreOptions::new(Budget::try_from(n)?)`.
If the limit is insufficient, the recurrence returns `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`) without an intermediate value.

### Associated functions and scaling

`legendre_p_assoc` computes the diagonal value $P_m^m(x) = (-1)^m (2m - 1)!!\,(1 - x^2)^{m/2}$ with compensated arithmetic and, starting from $P_{m+1}^m = (2m + 1) x P_m^m$, advances the recurrence

$$
(l - m + 1) P_{l+1}^m = (2l + 1) x P_l^m - (l + m) P_{l-1}^m
$$

in increasing $l$.
Since $(2m - 1)!!$ grows rapidly with $m$ and $(1 - x^2)^{m/2}$ decays rapidly, the state is stored as a significand and a separate power-of-two exponent, then rounded to an ordinary value only once at the end.
Values outside the binary64 range remain available as the `scaled()` value (with a separate power-of-two exponent) and logarithmic form in the form with options; the standard form gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
Negative orders are constructed from $m > 0$ using the relation above, dividing in the factors of $(l - m)!/(l + m)!$ one at a time.
The work is $l$ steps for $m \ge 0$ ($m$ diagonal steps and $l - m$ degree-raising steps), plus $2|m|$ steps for negative orders; $l + |m| > 2^{53}$ gives `SizeOverflow`.
$x = \pm 1$ and $l = 0$ have closed-form values with termination status `Exact`.
The estimated error in the form with options is a rounding model: the sum of recurrence-term magnitudes multiplied by $64\varepsilon(1 + l + 2|m|)^2$; it is not an enclosing interval.

### Recurrence direction for hyperbolic-space functions

The recurrence for $H_l$ is in the oscillatory region for $l \le |\lambda|\sinh\eta$, where forward recurrence is stable; at larger $l$, $H_l$ becomes recessive.
`legendre_h3d` sums a hypergeometric series when $\eta \le 1$ and $|\lambda|\eta \le 1$; elsewhere, it uses forward recurrence from $H_0$ and $H_1$ when $l \le |\lambda|\sinh\eta$ or $\eta > \ln(l + 1) + 2$, and Miller backward recurrence otherwise (recurring backward from a large degree and renormalizing with the known $H_0$).
Work is proportional to $l$, with the limit set by `SphericalBesselOptions` (65536 units by default).
The estimated error from Miller backward recurrence is empirical (confidence `Heuristic`).

## Accuracy guarantees

| Function (real arguments) | binary64 | binary32 | Test findings |
|---|---|---|---|
| `legendre_p` | 64 ULP | 64 ULP | Maximum measured binary64 error: 0.5 ULP. Representative grids from degree 0 to 10000, endpoints, near zeros, and reference data at degree 20000 |
| `legendre_q` | 64 ULP | 64 ULP | Reference data through degree 20000 |
| `legendre_p_assoc` | 64 ULP | 64 ULP | Associated-function reference data |
| `legendre_h3d` | Unspecified | Unspecified | Compare values and estimated errors with reference data; use absolute error near zeros |

Near zeros, an absolute error bound replaces 64 ULP.
Reference data for `legendre_p` consists of values computed independently at 160-digit and 240-digit precision.
The forms of `legendre_p` and `legendre_q` with options return a 64 ULP envelope as the estimated error.
The estimated error in the form of `legendre_p_assoc` with options uses the rounding model above and can exceed the 64 ULP specification.

## Functions and calling forms

All functions accept real types (`f32`, `f64`) and return real values.
The degree and order types are determined for each function.

| Function | Arguments | Form with options | Scoped API | Options |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `legendre_p` | `degree: usize`, `x: S` | `legendre_p_with_options` | `legendre_p_scoped` | `LegendreOptions` |
| `legendre_q` | `degree: usize`, `x: S` | `legendre_q_with_options` | `legendre_q_scoped` | `LegendreOptions` |
| `legendre_p_assoc` | `degree: usize`, `order: i32`, `x: S` | `legendre_p_assoc_with_options` | `legendre_p_assoc_scoped` | `LegendreOptions` |
| `legendre_h3d` | `order: u32`, `lambda: S`, `eta: S` | `legendre_h3d_with_options` | `legendre_h3d_scoped` | `SphericalBesselOptions` |

The form with options `*_with_options(.., options)` returns `SpecialFunctionResult`, containing an estimated error, termination status, work counts, and algorithm identifier.
The form of `legendre_p_assoc` with options retains a `scaled()` value and logarithmic form even when the value is outside the binary64 range.
The scoped API `*_scoped(.., options, scope)` returns the same result as the form with options within a solve scope.
`LegendreOptions` and `SphericalBesselOptions` contain only a work limit (`Budget`); they do not change accuracy.
To evaluate at many points, map the function and collect the results.
No sequence function returns multiple degrees together.

## Associated-function orthogonality and phase conventions

The next cell selects order $m$ and checks the orthogonality of $P_l^m$ for $l = m, \dots, m + 4$ using the composite Simpson rule ($N$ subintervals, computed manually).
Since $P_l^m P_k^m$ is the product of $(1 - x^2)^m$ and a polynomial, it is a polynomial in $x$, so Simpson-rule error decreases as $N^{-4}$.
The first table shows the relative difference between $\int (P_l^m)^2 dx$ and the normalization constant $N_l = \frac{2}{2l+1}\frac{(l+m)!}{(l-m)!}$, and the maximum of the off-diagonal integrals after division by $\sqrt{N_l N_k}$.
The second table compares $P_1^1$, $P_2^1$, $P_2^2$, $P_1^{-1}$, $Q_0$, and $Q_2$ at $x = 0.5$ with closed forms to check the Condon–Shortley phase and negative-order convention.

```rust
//| id: special-legendre-orthogonality-check
//| ref: special-legendre-orthogonality-check
//| title: "Check associated Legendre orthogonality and phase conventions"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   order: {"type":"range","label":"Order m","description":"Order of P_l^m. Examine degrees l = m, …, m + 4.","min":0,"max":8,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   intervals: {"type":"range","label":"Simpson subinterval count N","description":"Vary quadrature spacing 2/N. Error decreases as N⁻⁴.","min":200,"max":4000,"step":200,"value":2000}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"high-order","label":"m = 8","description":"Relative differences remain at rounding level even when the normalization constant exceeds 10¹⁵.","inputs":{"order":8}}
//|   - {"id":"coarse","label":"N = 200","description":"Reducing the subinterval count by a factor of ten increases quadrature error by about 10⁴.","inputs":{"intervals":200}}

//|   - case: default
//|     expect:

//|       - {"artifact":"conventions","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-0.8660254037844386,"rtol":1e-15}}

//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gram","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":8109673360588800,"rtol":1e-14}}

//|   - case: coarse
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"gram","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":2.5e-9,"rtol":0.01}}

// Values of P_l^m at Simpson nodes on [−1, 1] (rows correspond to degrees).
let h = 2.0 / n as f64;
let mut values = Vec::new();
for &l in &degrees {
    let column = (0..=n)
        .map(|i| legendre_p_assoc(l, m, -1.0 + h * i as f64))
        .collect::<Result<Vec<f64>, _>>()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    values.push(column);
}
// Independent check: ∫ P_l^m P_k^m dx by the composite Simpson rule (computed manually).
let integral = |a: &[f64], b: &[f64]| {
    let mut sum = a[0] * b[0] + a[n] * b[n];
    for i in 1..n {
        sum += if i % 2 == 1 { 4.0 } else { 2.0 } * a[i] * b[i];
    }
    sum * h / 3.0
};
// Normalization constant N_l = 2/(2l + 1) · (l + m)!/(l − m)!.
let norm = |l: usize| {
    let ratio: f64 = ((l - order as usize + 1)..=(l + order as usize))
        .map(|k| k as f64)
        .product();
    2.0 / (2.0 * l as f64 + 1.0) * ratio
};

for (a, &l) in degrees.iter().enumerate() {
    let diagonal = integral(&values[a], &values[a]);
    let off_diagonal = degrees
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .filter(|&(b, _)| b != a)
        .map(|(b, &k)| (integral(&values[a], &values[b]) / (norm(l) * norm(k)).sqrt()).abs())
        .fold(0.0, f64::max);
    rows.push((l, diagonal, norm(l), diagonal / norm(l) - 1.0, off_diagonal));

    { "key": "2", "label": "Normalization constant N_l", "type": "number", "format": digits16 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative difference", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Maximum off-diagonal (divided by N)", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);

    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "gram" })
);

// Phase conventions: compare at x = 0.5 with closed forms (reference values computed with f64 intrinsic methods).
let x = 0.5_f64;
let s = (1.0 - x * x).sqrt();
let p = |l: usize, m: i32| legendre_p_assoc(l, m, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string());
let q = |l: usize| legendre_q(l, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string());
let atanh = 0.5 * ((1.0 + x) / (1.0 - x)).ln();
let conventions = vec![
    ("P_1^1(x) = −√(1 − x²)", p(1, 1)?, -s),
    ("P_2^1(x) = −3x√(1 − x²)", p(2, 1)?, -3.0 * x * s),
    ("P_2^2(x) = 3(1 − x²)", p(2, 2)?, 3.0 * s * s),
    ("P_1^(−1)(x) = ½√(1 − x²)", p(1, -1)?, 0.5 * s),
    ("Q_0(x) = artanh x", q(0)?, atanh),
    (
        "Q_2(x) = P_2(x) artanh x − 3x/2",
        q(2)?,
        0.5 * (3.0 * x * x - 1.0) * atanh - 1.5 * x,
    ),
];
let conventions: Vec<_> = conventions
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, value, exact)| (name, value, exact, value - exact))
    .collect();
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Function (x = 0.5)" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "SciOxide value", "type": "number", "format": digits16 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form", "type": "number", "format": digits16 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    conventions,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "conventions" })
);
```
For the defaults $m = 2$ and $N = 2000$, relative differences increase from $2.5 \times 10^{-13}$ at $l = 2$ to $5.9 \times 10^{-10}$ at $l = 6$, while off-diagonal values are at most $1.6 \times 10^{-10}$.
Higher degrees have larger higher derivatives of the integrand, increasing Simpson-rule error.
Setting $N$ to 200 increases the relative difference by about $10^4$ ($N^{-4}$), while 4000 reduces it by about a factor of 16, so the difference is quadrature error rather than error in `legendre_p_assoc`.
For $m = 8$, the normalization grows to $N_{12} \approx 8.1 \times 10^{15}$, but relative differences remain of order $10^{-15}$.
Differences in the second table are at most $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$, and the negative sign of $P_1^1 = -\sqrt{1 - x^2}$ demonstrates the Condon–Shortley phase.

## High degrees and hyperbolic-space functions

The next cell selects degree $l$ and angle $\theta$ ($x = \cos\theta$) and compares $P_l$ and $Q_l$ with the large-$l$ asymptotic forms (DLMF §14.15):

$$
P_l(\cos\theta) \approx \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi l \sin\theta}}\cos\Bigl(\bigl(l + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)\theta - \frac{\pi}{4}\Bigr), \qquad
Q_l(\cos\theta) \approx -\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2 l \sin\theta}}\sin\Bigl(\bigl(l + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)\theta - \frac{\pi}{4}\Bigr)
$$


The asymptotic forms have relative error $O(1/l)$, so their error determines the differences in this row.
The Casorati determinant $l(P_l Q_{l-1} - P_{l-1} Q_l) = 1$ combines $P$ and $Q$ computed with separate recurrences and should equal 1 to rounding accuracy.
$P_l^0$ is $P_l$ computed through the separate associated-function path.
The second table compares $H_0$ and $H_1$ at $\lambda = 1.5$, $\eta = 0.5$ (the hypergeometric-series region) with closed forms, and $H_2$ with the recurrence; it also compares $H_3$ at $\eta = 10^{-3}$ with fixed $\lambda\eta = 1.5$ against the spherical Bessel function $j_3(1.5)$ (`spherical_jn`).
The final-row difference retains the approach to the limit, $O(\eta^2)$.
The third table shows `legendre_p_with_options` with work limits $l - 1$ and $(l - 1)/2$.

## Summary and further reading

`legendre_p` is evaluated for real arguments including $|x| > 1$, while `legendre_q` and `legendre_p_assoc` are evaluated on $[-1, 1]$, and the associated functions include the Condon–Shortley phase.
$P_l$, $Q_l$, and $P_l^m$ use forward recurrence, with a 64 ULP specification over the tested range (maximum measured error for $P_l$: 0.5 ULP).
Each recurrence step counts as one work unit, so increase the `LegendreOptions` limit above degree 65537; an insufficient limit returns no value and gives `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).
Receive associated-function values outside the binary64 range through the `scaled()` value and logarithmic form in the form with options.
See [Spherical harmonics](../spherical-harmonics/) for normalized multipole bases, [Angular momentum coupling coefficients and Wigner symbols](../angular-momentum-coupling/) for integrals of spherical-harmonic products (Gaunt coefficients) and angular momentum addition, and the [Legendre, angular momentum, and spectral function APIs](../../api/legendre-angular-spectral/) for individual specifications.
