Separating the two-dimensional wave equation in elliptic cylindrical coordinates on a domain with an elliptical cross section gives Mathieu equations in the angular and radial directions.
This equation describes natural oscillations of elliptical membranes and waveguides, scattering by elliptic cylinders, and stability of periodically driven oscillators ([Paul trap stability regions](../../examples/paul-trap-stability/)).
The Fourier coefficients of periodic solutions satisfy a three-term recurrence, and characteristic values are eigenvalues of a symmetric tridiagonal matrix for those coefficients.
`scioxide-special` truncates and solves this eigenproblem to construct a plan, `MathieuPlan`, that retains the coefficients.
The truncation procedure (spectral-method settings) is shared with `SpheroidalPlan`, the plan for [spheroidal wave functions](../spheroidal-wave-functions/) expanded in Ferrers functions, and is explained on this page.
This page covers the equation and conventions, reduction to an eigenproblem and choice of truncation, angular and radial evaluation, accuracy guarantees, and APIs, in that order.

## Mathieu equation and characteristic values

Mathieu's equation (DLMF 28.2.1) is the linear equation with periodic coefficients and real parameters $q$ and $a$:

$$
w''(z) + (a - 2q\cos 2z)\,w(z) = 0
$$


Since the coefficients have period $\pi$, Floquet's theorem expresses solutions in terms of a pair satisfying $w(z+\pi) = e^{i\nu\pi} w(z)$ ($\nu$ is the characteristic exponent).
Solutions of period $\pi$ or $2\pi$ exist only at discrete values of $a$ determined for each $q$.
These are called characteristic values, denoted $a_n(q)$ ($n \ge 0$) for even solutions $\operatorname{ce}_n(z,q)$ and $b_n(q)$ ($n \ge 1$) for odd solutions $\operatorname{se}_n(z,q)$.

At $q = 0$, the equation becomes $w'' + a w = 0$, with $a_n(0) = b_n(0) = n^2$, $\operatorname{ce}_n(z,0) = \cos nz$ ($n \ge 1$), $\operatorname{ce}_0(z,0) = 1/\sqrt2$, and $\operatorname{se}_n(z,0) = \sin nz$.
For small $q$, characteristic values have power series in $q$ (DLMF 28.6.1–28.6.5).

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_0(q) &= -\tfrac{1}{2}q^2 + \tfrac{7}{128}q^4 - \tfrac{29}{2304}q^6 + \tfrac{68687}{18874368}q^8 + O(q^{10}), \\
a_1(q) &= 1 + q - \tfrac{1}{8}q^2 - \tfrac{1}{64}q^3 - \tfrac{1}{1536}q^4 + \tfrac{11}{36864}q^5 + \tfrac{49}{589824}q^6 + O(q^7), \\
b_1(q) &= 1 - q - \tfrac{1}{8}q^2 + \tfrac{1}{64}q^3 - \tfrac{1}{1536}q^4 - \tfrac{11}{36864}q^5 + \tfrac{49}{589824}q^6 + O(q^7), \\
a_2(q) &= 4 + \tfrac{5}{12}q^2 - \tfrac{763}{13824}q^4 + \tfrac{1002401}{79626240}q^6 + O(q^8), \\
b_2(q) &= 4 - \tfrac{1}{12}q^2 + \tfrac{5}{13824}q^4 - \tfrac{289}{79626240}q^6 + O(q^8).
\end{aligned}
$$

These series have finite radii of convergence and cannot be used when $q$ is much greater than 1.
For $q > 0$, the ordering is $a_0 < b_1 < a_1 < b_2 < a_2 < \cdots$; as $q \to \infty$, $a_n$ and $b_{n+1}$ approach $-2q + 2(2n+1)\sqrt q$ and become exponentially close to each other (DLMF 28.8.1).
Values at $q < 0$ reduce to $q > 0$ through $a_{2r}(-q) = a_{2r}(q)$, $a_{2r+1}(-q) = b_{2r+1}(q)$, and $b_{2r+2}(-q) = b_{2r+2}(q)$ (DLMF 28.2.26).

Normalization follows DLMF §28.2, with $\int_0^{2\pi}\operatorname{ce}_n(z,q)^2\,dz = \int_0^{2\pi}\operatorname{se}_n(z,q)^2\,dz = \pi$.
The sign is chosen to connect continuously to $\cos nz$ and $\sin nz$ as $q \to 0$.
The implementation takes the lowest-frequency coefficient positive for $q > 0$; for $q < 0$, it takes it negative when the index $r$ within the class (next section) is odd.

## Three-term Fourier recurrence and eigenproblem

Expanding periodic solutions in Fourier series divides $\operatorname{ce}_n$ and $\operatorname{se}_n$ into four classes, each with a fixed frequency parity.

$$
\operatorname{ce}_{2r} = \sum_{k\ge0} A_{2k}\cos 2kz,\quad
\operatorname{ce}_{2r+1} = \sum_{k\ge0} A_{2k+1}\cos(2k+1)z,\quad
\operatorname{se}_{2r+1} = \sum_{k\ge0} B_{2k+1}\sin(2k+1)z,\quad
\operatorname{se}_{2r+2} = \sum_{k\ge0} B_{2k+2}\sin(2k+2)z.
$$

Substitution into the equation gives the three-term coefficient recurrence (DLMF 28.4.5–28.4.8):

$$
(a - f_k^2)\,c_k = q\,(c_{k-1} + c_{k+1}),\qquad f_k = 2k + p
$$


$p$ is the lowest frequency: 0 for $\operatorname{ce}_{2r}$, 1 for $\operatorname{ce}_{2r+1}$ and $\operatorname{se}_{2r+1}$, and 2 for $\operatorname{se}_{2r+2}$.
Only the first few rows differ: for $\operatorname{ce}_{2r}$, $c_0$ has a factor of 2 in row $k = 1$; for $\operatorname{ce}_{2r+1}$ and $\operatorname{se}_{2r+1}$, $+q$ and $-q$ are added to the diagonal.
After substituting $\tilde c_0 = \sqrt2\,c_0$ for $\operatorname{ce}_{2r}$, every class becomes the eigenproblem $T\boldsymbol c = a\boldsymbol c$ for a symmetric tridiagonal matrix $T$ with diagonal $f_k^2$ (and $\pm q$ in the first row) and off-diagonal $q$ ($\sqrt2\,q$ only at the first position for $\operatorname{ce}_{2r}$).
The characteristic value of index $r$ within the class is the $r$th smallest eigenvalue of $T$ (counting from 0).

The infinite matrix cannot be handled directly, so it is truncated at $N$ rows.
Truncation works because the coefficients decay rapidly.
In rows where $f_k^2 \gg |a|, |q|$, the recurrence gives $c_{k+1}/c_k \approx -q/f_{k+1}^2$, and $|c_k|$ decays roughly as $(|q|/4)^k/(k!)^2$, faster than exponentially.
Decay starts where $f_k^2$ exceeds $|q|$, so the required row count grows in proportion to $\sqrt{|q|}$.

### Computing coefficients

For each truncated matrix, `MathieuPlan::new` computes only the requested eigenvalue by Sturm-sequence bisection, then locates the largest eigenvector component by inverse iteration (`SpheroidalPlan::new` uses the same procedure).
Each bisection step and each inverse iteration require $O(N)$ operations and $O(N)$ memory.
The coefficients themselves are reconstructed by inserting the computed eigenvalue into the recurrence and using scaled arithmetic from both ends toward the largest component.
Beyond the largest component, the desired solution has rapidly decreasing coefficients (the minimal solution), but forward recurrence picks up the growing solution (the dominant solution) and is unstable.
Backward recurrence makes the minimal solution dominant, avoiding growth of relative error.
The reconstructed coefficients are normalized to unit Euclidean norm and assigned the sign required by the convention.

`SpectralNormalization::Standard` selects the normalization $\int\operatorname{ce}_n^2 = \pi$ above, while `SpectralNormalization::UnitL2` selects unit $L^2$ norm on $[0, 2\pi]$.
The two differ only by the constant factor $1/\sqrt\pi$.
At $q = 0$, the matrix is diagonal, so no eigenproblem is solved; the result is $a = n^2$ with unit-vector coefficients.

### Truncation doubling and acceptance criteria

The truncation row count is set by `SpectralTerms` in `SpectralOptions` (default minimum 16, maximum 256).
The rules in this section are shared by `MathieuPlan` and `SpheroidalPlan`; for spheroidal functions, replace $a$ with $\lambda_{mn}$ and $q$ with $c$.
The initial row count is $\max(\text{minimum}, j + 8)$, where $j$ is the index within the class ($r$ for Mathieu functions and $\lfloor (n - m)/2 \rfloor$ for spheroidal functions).
The problem is solved again with successive row-count doublings and accepted when all four conditions below hold relative to the preceding truncation.

1. The change in the characteristic value is at most $\max(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}|a|)$.
2. The residual of the truncated eigenproblem (including the first omitted row) is at most $\sqrt{\varepsilon}\max(|a|, 1)$.
   Here $\varepsilon$ is the scalar type's machine epsilon; exceeding this threshold indicates a breakdown unexplained by matrix rounding.
3. The maximum coefficient change is at most $\max(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}})$.
4. The estimated sum of absolute omitted coefficients is at most $\max(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}})$.

The tolerances $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}$ are set with `SpectralOptions::with_tolerance` and default to $10^{-13}$ for both `f64` and `f32`.
Coefficients and characteristic values are computed in `f64` for either scalar type, and conditions 1, 3, and 4 are independent of the scalar type.
Only $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ in condition 2 is larger for `f32`, so a `f32` plan is accepted with the same or fewer rows.
Since a preceding truncation is needed for comparison, at least one doubling is required when $q \ne 0$.

The omitted-coefficient estimate uses the fact that the diagonal in omitted rows is at least $f_k^2$ and the off-diagonal at most $|q|$ (for spheroidal functions, diagonal lower and off-diagonal upper bounds are built from matrix elements of $l(l+1)$ and $x^2$).
Let $G$ be a lower bound for the diagonal minus the characteristic value and $E$ an upper bound for the off-diagonal; when $t = E/G < 1/2$, the ratio of adjacent minimal-solution coefficients is at most $\rho = 2t/(1 + \sqrt{1 - 4t^2})$ (the smaller solution of $\rho = t(1+\rho^2)$).
The sum of omitted coefficients is estimated by a geometric series, multiplying the last retained coefficient by $\rho/(1-\rho)$.
This is an estimate, not a rigorous upper bound.

If acceptance fails at the maximum row count, evaluation returns `ConvergenceFailure` (`TruncationLimit`) with the residual and work counts.
The maximum row count does not grow automatically with degree or parameters; in this case, increase the maximum with `SpectralTerms::new(minimum, maximum)` and pass it through `SpectralOptions::with_terms`.
For $q \ne 0$, room for comparing two truncations requires $\max(\text{minimum}, j + 8) < \text{maximum}$; indices violating this condition give `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`) before preparation.
Preparation memory is checked in advance against an upper bound of $32N_{\max}^2 + 512N_{\max} + 4096$ bytes for maximum row count $N_{\max}$; exceeding `SpectralOptions::with_preparation_bytes` (64 MiB by default) returns `ResourceLimit`.
Preparation and each evaluation are separately limited by `SpectralOptions::with_iteration_budget` (2,097,152 units by default); exceeding the limit gives `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).

`SpectralDiagnostics`, returned by `MathieuPlan::diagnostics` and `SpheroidalPlan::diagnostics`, contains the number of retained coefficients (`terms`), doubling count (`refinements`), eigenproblem residual (`eigen_residual`), coefficient change between the final two truncations (`coefficient_change`), omitted-coefficient estimate (`truncation_bound`), preparation work (`work`), and upper bound on memory use (`preparation_bytes`).
Residuals, changes, and estimates are absolute values computed in `f64` regardless of the plan's scalar type.

## Evaluating angular and radial functions

`MathieuPlan::angular(z)` sums the Fourier series using retained coefficients and returns $\operatorname{ce}_n(z,q)$ or $\operatorname{se}_n(z,q)$ with its derivative in $z$.
$z$ is any finite real or complex number (in radians); the angular functions are entire.
Phase advances by multiplication by $e^{2iz}$ rather than evaluating $\cos 2z$ or $\sin 2z$ term by term.
Forming $2z$ loses digits for large $|z|$, so increments are formed from $\sin z$ and $\cos z$.
Estimated errors include rounding in phase recurrence, coefficient error, and the omitted-coefficient estimate.
For complex $z$, a term of frequency $\nu$ grows by $e^{\nu|\operatorname{Im}z|}$, and the omitted-coefficient contribution grows by the same factor.
When the omitted-coefficient ratio $\rho$ times $e^{2|\operatorname{Im}z|}$ reaches 1, the series does not converge, so evaluation returns `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).
Work is one unit per coefficient.

The radial (modified) Mathieu equation is $w'' - (a - 2q\cosh 2z)\,w = 0$ after substituting $z \to iz$ (DLMF 28.20.1) and appears in the radial direction of elliptic cylindrical coordinates.
Its solutions $\operatorname{Mc}_n^{(j)}(z,q)$ and $\operatorname{Ms}_n^{(j)}(z,q)$ are computed using series of Bessel-function products with the same coefficients as the angular functions (DLMF 28.24.1–28.24.4):

$$
\operatorname{Mc}_{2r}^{(j)}(z,q) = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_s A_{2s}}\sum_{k\ge0} (-1)^{r+k} A_{2k}\left[J_{k-s}(u)\,\mathcal C^{(j)}_{k+s}(v) + J_{k+s}(u)\,\mathcal C^{(j)}_{k-s}(v)\right],\qquad u = \sqrt q\,e^{-z},\ v = \sqrt q\,e^{z}
$$

The other three classes have the same form.
$\mathcal C^{(1)} = J$, $\mathcal C^{(2)} = Y$, $\mathcal C^{(3)} = H^{(1)}$, and $\mathcal C^{(4)} = H^{(2)}$ are selected by `First`, `Second`, `Third`, and `Fourth` in `RadialKind`.
$s$ is an arbitrary index; the implementation chooses the largest absolute coefficient to minimize error from division by $A_{2s}$ ($\varepsilon_0 = 2$, and 1 otherwise).
This series determines radial normalization, so the radial functions are unchanged by the choice of angular normalization.
The third and fourth kinds are first kind $\pm i \cdot$ second kind, representing outgoing and incoming waves for large real part of $z$.
For $q < 0$, $\sqrt q$ uses the principal square root, so values are complex even for real $z$.
At $q = 0$, the first kind is 1 for $n = 0$ and zero otherwise; kinds two through four diverge and return `DomainError` (`Pole`).
Work per coefficient is two pairs of Bessel values and derivatives; omitted-term contributions are estimated by multiplying the coefficient estimate by Bessel-function growth with order and coordinate.
This is also an estimate, not an upper bound.

The Wronskian of the first and second kinds is independent of $q$ and $z$ and equals

$$
\operatorname{Mc}_n^{(1)}\,\frac{d}{dz}\operatorname{Mc}_n^{(2)} - \frac{d}{dz}\operatorname{Mc}_n^{(1)}\,\operatorname{Mc}_n^{(2)} = \frac{2}{\pi}
$$

(DLMF 28.20.21; the same holds for $\operatorname{Ms}$).
This checks the independently summed results of the two series.

```rust
use std::f64::consts::{FRAC_1_SQRT_2, PI};

let n = order as u32;
let parity = if odd {
    MathieuParity::Odd
} else {
    MathieuParity::Even
};
let normalization = SpectralNormalization::Standard;
let plan = MathieuPlan::new(n, parity, q, normalization, SpectralOptions::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let angular = |z: f64| {
    plan.angular(z)
        .and_then(|r| r.try_value())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
// Radial function value and derivative in z
let radial = |z: f64, kind| {
    plan.radial(z, kind)
        .and_then(|r| Ok((r.try_value()?, r.try_derivative()?)))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let mut curve = Vec::new();
let mut limit = Vec::new();
for i in 0..=120 {
    let z = PI * f64::from(i) / 120.0;
    curve.push([z, angular(z)?]);
    // q = 0 limit (reference values from f64 standard functions)
    let reference = match (odd, n) {
        (true, _) => (f64::from(n) * z).sin(),
        (false, 0) => FRAC_1_SQRT_2,
        (false, _) => (f64::from(n) * z).cos(),
    };
    limit.push([z, reference]);
}
// ∫₀^{2π} w² dz by the trapezoidal rule (independent quadrature)
let mut norm = 0.0;
for i in 0..64 {
    norm += angular(2.0 * PI * f64::from(i) / 64.0)?.powi(2) * 2.0 * PI / 64.0;
}
let mut rows = vec![("∫₀^{2π} w² dz − π", None, norm - PI)];
for z in [0.0, 0.5, 1.5] {
    let (m1, d1) = radial(z, RadialKind::First)?;
    let (m2, d2) = radial(z, RadialKind::Second)?;
    let (m3, _) = radial(z, RadialKind::Third)?;
    let wronskian = (m1 * d2 - d1 * m2).re;
    rows.push(("W{M⁽¹⁾, M⁽²⁾} − 2/π", Some(z), wronskian - 2.0 / PI));
    let combined = m3 - (m1 + m2 * Complex::new(0.0, 1.0));
    rows.push(("|M⁽³⁾ − (M⁽¹⁾ + iM⁽²⁾)|", Some(z), combined.norm()));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Check" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Radial coordinate z [-]", "type": "number", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Plan angular function", "points": curve }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "q = 0 limit", "points": limit, "style": { "lineType": "dashed" } })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({ "xLabel": "Angle z [rad]", "yLabel": "Angular function w(z) [-]" })
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
```

For the defaults $\operatorname{ce}_2$ and $q = 5$, the value at $z = 0$ decreases from $\cos 0 = 1$ to 0.735, peaks at 0.92 at $z \approx 0.21\pi$, and is $-0.72$ at $z = \pi/2$.
Writing the equation as $w'' + (a - V(z))w = 0$ with $V(z) = 2q\cos 2z$, the potential $V$ for $q > 0$ is minimized at $z = \pi/2$, so increasing $q$ concentrates the function there.
In the case $\operatorname{se}_3$, $q = 30$, the value at $z = \pi/8$ is only 0.06, and the region $|w| > 0.5$ is confined between $z = 0.23\pi$ and $0.77\pi$.
The trapezoidal-rule value of $\int_0^{2\pi} w^2\,dz$ differs from $\pi$ by at most $4 \times 10^{-16}$, confirming that normalization `Standard` follows the DLMF convention.
The radial Wronskian differs from $2/\pi$ by at most $4 \times 10^{-16}$ at each of $z = 0$, 0.5, and 1.5.
The first and second kinds use separate Bessel series, so this agreement checks both series and their coefficients.
The difference between the third kind and first kind $+ i\cdot$ second kind is also at rounding level.


## Accuracy guarantees

Mathieu functions have no uniform ULP specification across the full family.
Tolerances determine truncation acceptance criteria and do not promise output accuracy.
Residuals in `SpectralDiagnostics` and estimated errors returned by individual evaluations (errors in `SpecialFunctionResult`) are estimates describing the computation, not enclosing intervals.

Reference tests check differences from independently generated reference values within the following tolerances.
For five modes with $q = 1$, characteristic values agree with reference values within $2 \times 10^{-12}$.
Over $q = 10^{-4}$ through $1000$ and $q = -1$, angular and radial (first and second kinds) values and derivatives at real and complex $z$ agree with independent references within the larger of relative $5 \times 10^{-9}$ and absolute $5 \times 10^{-12}$ for `f64`, or relative $10^{-3}$ and absolute $2 \times 10^{-5}$ for `f32`.
The Wronskian is tested against $2/\pi$ within absolute $10^{-8}$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, 6$ with $q = 2$ and both parities.
Plans with `f32` also compute internally in `f64` and round to `f32` at the end.

## API

| Item | Role |
|---|---|
| `MathieuPlan::new(order, parity, q, normalization, options)` | Prepare characteristic values and Fourier coefficients. `build(.., scope)` performs the same preparation within a scope |
| `MathieuPlan::characteristic_value`, `diagnostics` | $a_n(q)$ or $b_n(q)$, truncation diagnostics |
| `MathieuPlan::angular(z)`, `angular_scoped(z, scope)` | Detailed results for $\operatorname{ce}_n$, $\operatorname{se}_n$, and derivatives |
| `MathieuPlan::radial(z, kind)`, `radial_scoped(z, kind, scope)` | Detailed results for $\operatorname{Mc}_n^{(j)}$, $\operatorname{Ms}_n^{(j)}$, and derivatives (complex) |
| `MathieuPlan::coefficient(k)`, `coefficient_count` | Fourier coefficients (scaled values) in the selected normalization and their count |
| `MathieuPlan::order`, `parity`, `parameter`, `normalization` | Values used for preparation |
| `mathieu_a(n, q)`, `mathieu_b(n, q)` | One-shot characteristic-value functions |
| `mathieu_ce(n, q, z)`, `mathieu_se(n, q, z)` | One-shot angular functions (real or complex `z`) |
| `mathieu_mc(n, q, z, kind)`, `mathieu_ms(n, q, z, kind)` | One-shot radial functions (complex values) |
| `MathieuParity` | `Even` ($\operatorname{ce}$, $\operatorname{Mc}$) and `Odd` ($\operatorname{se}$, $\operatorname{Ms}$) |
| `RadialKind` | Radial kinds `First`, `Second`, `Third`, `Fourth` (shared with spheroidal functions) |
| `SpectralNormalization` | Angular normalization `Standard`, `UnitL2` (shared with spheroidal functions) |
| `SpectralOptions` | Tolerance (`with_tolerance`), truncation range (`with_terms`), work limit (`with_iteration_budget`), memory limit (`with_preparation_bytes`) |
| `SpectralTerms::new(minimum, maximum)` | Minimum and maximum truncation row counts. Unless $2 \le$ minimum $\le$ maximum, give `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`) |
| `SpectralDiagnostics` | `terms`, `refinements`, `eigen_residual`, `coefficient_change`, `truncation_bound`, `work`, `preparation_bytes` |

Spheroidal wave plans and functions are listed in the [Spheroidal wave function APIs](oxref:special-spheroidal-api).

Construct a plan when evaluating at many points or when estimated errors, derivatives, or termination statuses are needed.
Plans are immutable; repeated evaluations never rebuild their coefficients.
One-shot functions such as `mathieu_a`, `mathieu_ce`, and `mathieu_mc` construct one internal plan with default `SpectralOptions` and normalization `Standard`, evaluate it once, and return an ordinary value.
Preparation and evaluation share one work limit (2,097,152 units), and the result is bitwise identical to evaluation of a plan with the same arguments.
One-shot functions have no form with options `*_with_options` or scoped API `*_scoped`; use the plan's `build` and `*_scoped` methods for those purposes.
[Detailed results and options](../../api/results-and-options/) explains scoped evaluation semantics (cancellation, deadlines, and addition to parent counts).

Errors are as follows.
NaN or infinite $q$ gives `NonFinite`; $n = 0$ for odd functions or indices outside the truncation range give `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
For one-shot functions, the out-of-range indices are $n \ge 496$ for even functions and $n \ge 497$ for odd functions (512 and 513 respectively when $q = 0$).
Radial kinds two through four at $q = 0$ give `DomainError` (`Pole`).
Evaluation returns `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`) when matrix entries or coefficients leave the `f64` range, when $|\operatorname{Im} z|$ is too large for the angular series to converge, when $\sqrt q\,e^{\pm z}$ overflows or becomes zero, or when the radial series fails to converge with the retained coefficients.
A one-shot value exceeding the scalar range gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`); the plan's `characteristic_value` gives `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).

## Summary

Mathieu characteristic values are eigenvalues of a symmetric tridiagonal matrix for Fourier coefficients; the truncation row count is determined by comparing two truncations and estimating omitted coefficients.
The coefficient and doubling counts in `SpectralDiagnostics` show how large the truncation became for $q$; if `TruncationLimit` occurs, increase the maximum in `SpectralTerms`.
Construct a plan to evaluate many points at the same $q$; use a one-shot function when only one value is needed.
Estimated errors are not enclosing intervals, so check important values using independent relations such as Wronskians or small-$q$ series.

For spheroidal functions, which solve a Ferrers expansion with the same truncation rules, see [Spheroidal wave functions](../spheroidal-wave-functions/) to examine the spherical limit of characteristic values and radial-function connections.
[Paul trap stability regions](../../examples/paul-trap-stability/) applies characteristic values to stability diagrams.
