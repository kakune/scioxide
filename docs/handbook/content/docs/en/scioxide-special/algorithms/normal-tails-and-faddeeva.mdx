The standard normal cumulative distribution function $\Phi(x)$ and upper tail $Q(x) = 1 - \Phi(x)$ appear in hypothesis-test $p$-values, confidence intervals, the Black–Scholes formula, and extreme-value statistics.
For large $x$, $Q(x)$ decreases like $e^{-x^2/2}$; in binary64, $\Phi(x)$ rounds to 1 at $x > 8.29$, so subtraction $1 - \Phi(x)$ loses the tail value.
This page explains the normal-distribution functions in `scioxide-special` (CDF, upper tail, their logarithmic forms, quantiles, and inversion from log probabilities) and Owen's $T$ function for bivariate normal quadrant probabilities.
[Error functions, Dawson's integral, and inverse error functions](../error-functions/) explains the scaled complementary error function $\operatorname{erfcx}(x) = e^{x^2}\operatorname{erfc}(x)$ kernel and inverse error functions used by the normal functions.
ULP is the floating-point spacing at the true value, and $\varepsilon = 2^{-52}$ is binary64 machine epsilon.

## Standard normal CDF and tails

With standard normal density $\varphi(x) = e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$, the CDF and upper tail are as follows.

$$
\Phi(x) = \int_{-\infty}^x \varphi(t)\,dt = \tfrac12 \operatorname{erfc}\!\left(-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right), \qquad
Q(x) = 1 - \Phi(x) = \tfrac12 \operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \Phi(-x).
$$

`normal_cdf` returns $\Phi(x)$ and `normal_sf` returns $Q(x)$; both accept every finite real (`RealScalar`) $x$ and have no error conditions.
$Q(x)$ falls below the normal range and becomes subnormal at $x > 37.52$, then rounds to zero at $x > 38.48$.
`normal_sf` returns zero without evaluation for $\lvert x \rvert > 39$ (`normal_cdf` returns zero for $x < -39$).

### Direct tail evaluation

`normal_sf` computes both tails with the following formula, without subtracting from 1.

$$
Q(\lvert x \rvert) = \tfrac12 \operatorname{erfcx}\!\left(\frac{\lvert x \rvert}{\sqrt{2}}\right) e^{-x^2/2}.
$$

$\operatorname{erfcx}$ varies slowly, so rounding its argument $\lvert x \rvert/\sqrt{2}$ has little effect on the result.
In contrast, the exponent $x^2/2$ reaches 722 at $x = 38$, and its rounding error $\delta$ becomes relative error $\delta$ in the result ($\mathrm{ulp}(722) = 1.1 \times 10^{-13}$).
Therefore $x^2/2 = s + e$ is split exactly with an error-free product (a product split into two words), and a correction for the low word $e$ is applied using $e^{-s}(1 - e)$.
For $x \ge 0$, $\Phi(x)$ is formed as $1 - Q(x)$; for $x < 0$, $\Phi(x)$ directly returns $Q(-x)$.
The operation count is constant, and `normal_cdf` and `normal_sf` have no scoped APIs.

Over $-38 \le x \le 38$, checked against independently generated reference values, accuracy is 8 ULP for binary64 and 1 ULP for binary32 (computed in binary64 with a single final rounding).
The estimated errors returned by the forms with options `normal_cdf_with_options` and `normal_sf_with_options` are 64-unit envelopes of the value ($64\varepsilon\lvert v\rvert$), larger than the ULP targets.

### Mills ratio and asymptotic expansion

The upper tail divided by the density, $R(x) = Q(x)/\varphi(x)$, is called the Mills ratio.
Using $\operatorname{erfcx}$ gives $R(x) = \sqrt{\pi/2}\,\operatorname{erfcx}(x/\sqrt{2})$, which contains no $e^{-x^2/2}$ and remains representable even when the tail falls below the type's range.
For large $x$, DLMF 7.12.1 gives the following asymptotic expansion.

$$
R(x) \sim \frac{1}{x}\left(1 - \frac{1}{x^2} + \frac{1 \cdot 3}{x^4} - \frac{1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5}{x^6} + \cdots\right) = \frac{1}{x}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty} (-1)^m \frac{(2m - 1)!!}{x^{2m}}.
$$

This series diverges: its terms reach a minimum near $m \approx x^2/2$ and then grow.
The error of a truncated sum is smaller than the first omitted term, and larger $x$ gives greater accuracy with fewer terms.
For $x > 0$, Gordon's inequality (1941), $x/(1 + x^2) < R(x) < 1/x$, also holds, and $R(x)$ approaches the lower bound as $x \to \infty$.

### Logarithmic forms

Use logarithmic forms for tails where $Q(x)$ falls below the type's range, and for likelihood calculations that turn products of probabilities into sums.
`normal_log_cdf` computes $\ln\Phi(x)$ using $\ln\bigl(\tfrac12 \operatorname{erfcx}(-x/\sqrt{2})\bigr) - x^2/2$ for $x \le 0$ (forming $x^2/2$ exactly in two words), and $\operatorname{ln1p}(-Q(x))$ for $x > 0$, without forming the probability itself.
`normal_log_sf(x)` is $\ln Q(x) = \ln\Phi(-x)$ and performs the same calculation at $-x$.
The only error occurs when $x^2/2$ overflows ($x < -1.9 \times 10^{154}$ for `normal_log_cdf` and $x > 1.9 \times 10^{154}$ for `normal_log_sf`), returning `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
In the opposite tail, $\ln(1 - Q)$ merely approaches zero, so no error occurs.
Accuracy matches the CDF: 8 ULP for binary64 and 1 ULP for binary32 over $-38 \le x \le 38$.

Relative accuracy is limited when converting a logarithmic value back to a probability.
Absolute error $\delta$ in $\ln Q$ becomes relative error $\delta$ in $Q$; merely representing $\ln Q = -804.6$ ($x = 40$) in binary64 introduces rounding of $\mathrm{ulp}(804.6)/2 = 5.7 \times 10^{-14}$.
In deep tails, expect relative probability errors of order $\varepsilon \lvert \ln Q \rvert$.

### Quantiles and inversion from log probabilities

`normal_quantile` returns the quantile $\Phi^{-1}(p)$, and `normal_isf` returns the upper quantile $Q^{-1}(p) = -\Phi^{-1}(p)$.
Both compute $\Phi^{-1}(p) = -\sqrt{2}\operatorname{erfcinv}(2p)$ by doubling the smaller tail $\min(p, 1 - p)$ (doubling is exact) and passing it to a rational approximation of the inverse error function, without iteration.
Even for positive subnormal $p$, errors remain within 4 ULP for binary64 and 1 ULP for binary32.
`normal_quantile` returns $-\infty$ at $p = 0$ and $+\infty$ at $p = 1$; outside $[0, 1]$, it gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).

For probabilities below the type's smallest subnormal value, use `normal_inv_log_cdf` ($x$ satisfying $\ln\Phi(x) = \ln p$) or `normal_inv_log_sf` ($x$ satisfying $\ln Q(x) = \ln q$, the negative of the former); these accept the log probability $\ln p$.
The method depends on the magnitude of $\ln p$.

- For $\tfrac14 \le p \le 2^{-1/2}$, form $1 - 2p$ using $-\operatorname{expm1}(\ln p + \ln 2)$ and pass it to `erfinv`.
  This avoids losing small deviations of $1 - 2p$ to rounding near the median.
- For $p > 2^{-1/2}$, form $\ln(1 - p) = \ln(1 - e^{\ln p})$ and switch to the opposite tail.
- For $-36 < \ln p < \ln\tfrac14$, use $-\sqrt{2}\operatorname{erfcinv}(2e^{\ln p})$.
- For $\ln p \le -36$, apply Newton's method to $\ln\Phi(x) - \ln p = 0$ from the asymptotic initial approximation $x_0 = -\sqrt{-2\ln p}$.
  The derivative is $\varphi(x)/\Phi(x) = \sqrt{2/\pi}/\operatorname{erfcx}(-x/\sqrt{2})$; iteration stops when the update is at most 2 ULP.
  For $\ln p < -\varepsilon^{-2}$, the correction to the initial approximation is below 1 ULP, so return that approximation.

Each Newton step counts against the work limit in `ProbabilityOptions` (65536 by default), so only these two functions have scoped APIs, `normal_inv_log_cdf_scoped` and `normal_inv_log_sf_scoped`.
Positive $\ln p$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`), while $\ln p = -\infty$ gives `NonFinite` (use `normal_quantile(0)` for the zero-probability limit).
At $\ln p = 0$, `normal_inv_log_cdf` returns $+\infty$ and `normal_inv_log_sf` returns $-\infty$.
These two have no ULP target, and their forms with options return a 64-unit estimated error.

## Normal upper tail and Mills ratio

The next cell computes `normal_sf`, `1.0 - normal_cdf(x)`, and `normal_log_sf` at nine points from $x = 1$ to 40.
It forms the Mills ratio as $R(x) = \sqrt{\pi/2}\operatorname{erfcx}(x/\sqrt{2})$ using `erfcx` and reports its position $(R - L)/(U - L)$ between Gordon's lower and upper bounds ($L = x/(1 + x^2)$, $U = 1/x$).
A position between zero and one satisfies the inequality.
As an independent check, it forms $\ln\varphi(x) + \ln R_N(x)$ in f64 from the Mills asymptotic series truncated at $N$ terms and reports its difference from `normal_log_sf`.
The last column is the difference after recovering $x$ from $\ln Q(x)$ using `normal_inv_log_sf`.

```rust
//| id: special-normal-log-sf-mills-check
//| ref: special-normal-log-sf-mills-check
//| title: "Compare the normal upper tail with the Mills asymptotic expansion"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   terms: {"type":"range","label":"Asymptotic term count N","min":1,"max":8,"step":1,"value":4}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"one-term","label":"N = 1","description":"Leading term 1/x only. The ln Q difference is of order 1/x², leaving 6 × 10⁻⁴ even at x = 40.","inputs":{"terms":1}}
//|   - {"id":"eight-terms","label":"N = 8","description":"For x ≥ 20, differences reach the rounding level of ln Q; for x ≤ 2, the truncated sum is negative and comparison is unavailable.","inputs":{"terms":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":6.220960574271784e-16,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/3/2","range":{"min":6.6e-16,"max":6.7e-16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/4/2","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/7/1","approx":{"value":2.8854283600687843e-316,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/3","approx":{"value":-804.6084420137538,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/4","approx":{"value":0.024984404205720571,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/5","range":{"min":0,"max":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/6","range":{"min":1e-11,"max":3e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/7","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: one-term
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/6","range":{"min":6e-4,"max":6.5e-4}}
//|   - case: eight-terms
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/1/6","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/6","range":{"max":2e-13}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_special::{erfcx, normal_cdf, normal_inv_log_sf, normal_log_sf, normal_sf};
use std::f64::consts::{FRAC_2_SQRT_PI, PI, SQRT_2};

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let n_terms = terms as u32;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for x in [1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 8.0, 10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 38.0, 40.0] {
    let tail = normal_sf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let naive = 1.0 - normal_cdf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let log_tail = normal_log_sf(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Mills ratio R = Q/φ = √(π/2) erfcx(x/√2). Here √(π/2) = 1/(FRAC_2_SQRT_PI/√2).
    let mills = erfcx(x / SQRT_2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? * SQRT_2 / FRAC_2_SQRT_PI;
    // Position within Gordon's (1941) inequality x/(1 + x²) < R < 1/x (between zero and one).
    let (low, high) = (x / (1.0 + x * x), 1.0 / x);
    let position = (mills - low) / (high - low);
    // Independent check: R ~ (1/x) Σ_{m<N} (−1)^m (2m − 1)!! / x^{2m}. Compare with ln Q.
    let mut term = 1.0;
    let mut sum = 0.0;
    for m in 0..n_terms {
        sum += term;
        term *= -(2.0 * f64::from(m) + 1.0) / (x * x);
    }
    let log_phi = -0.5 * x * x - 0.5 * RealScalar::ln(2.0 * PI);
    // Do not compare when the truncated sum is nonpositive (small x).
    let difference = (sum > 0.0).then(|| (log_tail - log_phi - RealScalar::ln(sum / x)).abs());
    let back = normal_inv_log_sf(log_tail).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - x;
    rows.push((x, tail, naive, log_tail, mills, position, difference, back));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "x [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Q(x)（normal_sf） [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "1 − Φ(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "ln Q(x)（normal_log_sf） [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Mills ratio R(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Position within Gordon bounds [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Difference from asymptotic expansion [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "Negative sum" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Inverse recovery difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "tails" })
);
```

For the default $N = 4$, `normal_sf` returns $6.221 \times 10^{-16}$ at $x = 8$ and the subnormal value $2.885 \times 10^{-316}$ at $x = 38$, then zero at $x = 40$.
`1.0 - normal_cdf(x)` is $6.66 \times 10^{-16}$ at $x = 8$ (7% relative error) and zero for $x \ge 10$.
`normal_log_sf` returns $\ln Q = -804.6084$ even at $x = 40$; the largest recovery difference through `normal_inv_log_sf` is $1.8 \times 10^{-15}$ at $x = 8$.
The Mills ratio position lies between zero and one in every row and approaches zero as $2/x^2$ for increasing $x$ (0.00125 at $x = 40$).
Differences from the asymptotic expansion are 0.18 at $x = 2$, $9.7 \times 10^{-7}$ at $x = 10$, and $1.6 \times 10^{-11}$ at $x = 40$, matching the size of the first omitted term $105/x^8$.
At $x = 1$, the truncated sum $1 - 1 + 3 - 15$ is negative, so no comparison is made.
Selecting $N = 8$ reduces the differences at $x \ge 20$ to values ranging from $9 \times 10^{-15}$ to $7 \times 10^{-14}$, stopping at the rounding level of $\ln Q$ ($\mathrm{ulp}(726.6) = 1.1 \times 10^{-13}$ at $x = 38$).
At $N = 1$, only the leading term $1/x$ remains, leaving a difference $6.2 \times 10^{-4} \approx 1/x^2$ even at $x = 40$.

## Owen's T function

Owen's $T$ function is defined by the following integral (Owen 1956).

$$
T(h, a) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^a \frac{\exp\!\left(-\tfrac12 h^2(1 + t^2)\right)}{1 + t^2}\,dt.
$$

$T$ is even in $h$ and odd in $a$, and satisfies $T(0, a) = \arctan(a)/(2\pi)$, $T(h, 0) = 0$, and $T(h, 1) = \tfrac12\Phi(h)(1 - \Phi(h))$, with $T(h, \infty) = \tfrac12 Q(h)$ for $h \ge 0$.
For the standard bivariate normal distribution $(X, Y)$ with correlation $\rho$, the quadrant probability for $h, k \ne 0$ is given by (Owen 1956):

$$
P(X > h, Y > k) = \tfrac12 Q(h) + \tfrac12 Q(k) - T(h, a_h) - T(k, a_k) - \beta, \qquad
a_h = \frac{k - \rho h}{h\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}, \quad a_k = \frac{h - \rho k}{k\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}}
$$


$\beta$ is zero for $hk > 0$ and $\tfrac12$ for $hk < 0$.
Bivariate normal probabilities over angular sectors can be expressed with the same $T$.

`owens_t` uses method T5 of Patefield and Tandy (2000).
For $\lvert a \rvert \le 1$, it performs one 32-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature on $[0, \min(\lvert a \rvert, 12/\lvert h \rvert)]$.
The integrand poles $t = \pm i$ lie outside the quadrature convergence region (the Bernstein ellipse), and the Gaussian tail beyond $ht > 12$ is below $2 \times 10^{-33}$, so truncation and quadrature errors are below binary64 rounding.
For $\lvert a \rvert > 1$, with $h, a \ge 0$, the following identity reduces the problem

$$
T(h, a) + T(ah, 1/a) = \tfrac12\bigl(Q(h) + Q(ah)\bigr) - Q(h)\,Q(ah)
$$

to $1/a \le 1$.
At $h = 0$, it directly returns $\arctan(a)/(2\pi)$; for values of $h$ where $e^{-h^2/2}$ becomes zero, it returns zero.
The 32 nodes count against the work limit, but work has a bound independent of the arguments, so no scoped API is provided.
It accepts all finite $h, a$ and has no error conditions; binary32 is evaluated in binary64 with one final rounding.
There is no uniform ULP target; the form with options `owens_t_with_options` returns an estimated error combining quadrature and normal-tail errors.

The quadrant-probability formula loses relative accuracy through subtraction when the result is smaller than the individual terms.
For example, the probability $1.8 \times 10^{-8}$ at $\rho = -0.9$, $h = 3$, and $k = -1$ is a difference of terms such as $\tfrac12 Q(k) \approx 0.42$; even an absolute error of $2 \times 10^{-18}$ gives relative error $10^{-10}$ (the negative-correlation case in the cell below).

## Bivariate normal quadrant probabilities

The next cell computes $P(X > h, Y > k)$ for a bivariate normal distribution with correlation $\rho$ using Owen's formula and compares it with numerical integration of another formula.
The check uses $P = \int_h^\infty \varphi(x)\,Q\bigl((k - \rho x)/\sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\bigr)\,dx$, integrating $[h, \max(h, 0) + 12]$ with the composite Simpson rule over 10000 subintervals (`normal_sf` supplies $Q$, but the $T$ function is not used).
The last column is the residual of the identity $T(h, 1) = \tfrac12\Phi(h)(1 - \Phi(h))$.
$h$ takes seven values from $-2$ to 3; rows where $h$ and $k$ have opposite signs include $\beta = \tfrac12$.

```rust
//| id: special-owens-t-identity
//| ref: special-owens-t-identity
//| title: "Compute bivariate normal quadrant probabilities with Owen's T function"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   rho: {"type":"range","label":"Correlation coefficient ρ","min":-0.9,"max":0.9,"step":0.1,"value":0.5}
//|   k: {"type":"select","label":"Threshold k for Y","options":["-1","0.5","1.5"],"value":"1.5"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"negative","label":"ρ = −0.9, k = −1","description":"Strong negative correlation. In rows with h > 0, larger X implies smaller Y, and probability falls rapidly.","inputs":{"rho":-0.9,"k":"-1"}}
//|   - {"id":"positive","label":"ρ = 0.9, k = 0.5","description":"Strong positive correlation. For h ≤ −1, X > h is almost certain, so probability approaches Q(0.5) = 0.3085.","inputs":{"rho":0.9,"k":"0.5"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/4/1","approx":{"value":0.03241757863227539,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/6/3","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/5/4","range":{"min":-1e-16,"max":1e-16}}
//|   - case: negative
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":1.784813386146e-8,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/4/3","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: positive
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.3085375384652758,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orthant","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"min":-1e-13,"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_special::{normal_cdf, normal_sf, owens_t};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let k: f64 = k.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot parse threshold")?;
let root = (1.0 - rho * rho).sqrt();
// Owen (1956): P(X > h, Y > k) = ½Q(h) + ½Q(k) − T(h, a_h) − T(k, a_k) − β（h, k ≠ 0）.
let orthant = |h: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let a_h = (k - rho * h) / (h * root);
    let a_k = (h - rho * k) / (k * root);
    let beta = if h * k > 0.0 { 0.0 } else { 0.5 };
    let q =
        normal_sf(h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? + normal_sf(k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let t = owens_t(h, a_h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        + owens_t(k, a_k).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(0.5 * q - t - beta)
};
// Independent check: P = ∫_h^∞ φ(x) Q((k − ρx)/√(1 − ρ²)) dx by the composite Simpson rule (10000 subintervals).
let quadrature = |h: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let (end, n) = (h.max(0.0) + 12.0, 10000);
    let step = (end - h) / n as f64;
    let mut sum = 0.0;
    for i in 0..=n {
        let x = h + step * i as f64;
        let weight = if i == 0 || i == n {
            1.0
        } else if i % 2 == 1 {
            4.0
        } else {
            2.0
        };
        let density = (-0.5 * x * x).exp() / (2.0 * PI).sqrt();
        sum += weight * density * normal_sf((k - rho * x) / root).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
    Ok(sum * step / 3.0)
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for h in [-2.0, -1.0, -0.5, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0] {
    let owen = orthant(h)?;
    let reference = quadrature(h)?;
    // Residual of T(h, 1) = ½ Φ(h)(1 − Φ(h)) (Owen 1956).
    let p = normal_cdf(h).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let identity = owens_t(h, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - 0.5 * p * (1.0 - p);
    rows.push((h, owen, reference, owen - reference, identity));
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "h [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "P(X > h, Y > k) (Owen's formula) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Simpson value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "T(h, 1) − ½Φ(h)(1 − Φ(h)) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orthant" })
);
```

For the defaults $\rho = 0.5$ and $k = 1.5$, Owen's formula and Simpson's rule differ by at most $10^{-15}$ in all seven rows.
Rows with $h < 0$ include $\beta = \tfrac12$, and the probability approaches $Q(1.5) = 0.0668$ as $h$ decreases.
The residual of the identity $T(h, 1) = \tfrac12\Phi(h)(1 - \Phi(h))$ is at most $1.4 \times 10^{-17}$.
Selecting $\rho = -0.9$ and $k = -1$ increases the difference to $1.6 \times 10^{-14}$, reflecting Simpson-rule error from the rapidly varying integrand.
In this case, the probability $1.7848133860 \times 10^{-8}$ at $h = 3$ differs relatively by $9.6 \times 10^{-11}$ from $1.7848133861 \times 10^{-8}$ computed by multiprecision quadrature (40 digits), consistent with the loss of relative accuracy through subtraction described above.
Against the same multiprecision values, Owen's formula has relative errors of order $5 \times 10^{-16}$ at both $h = 0.5$ and $h = 1$, so the table difference $10^{-14}$ comes from Simpson's rule.
For $\rho = 0.9$ and $k = 0.5$, rows with $h \le -1$ approach $Q(0.5) = 0.3085$.

## API

The functions in this family are listed below.
All functions are real-only (`RealScalar`).
All options use `ProbabilityOptions`, containing only a work budget (`Budget`, default 65536) and no accuracy request.
Options forms return `SpecialFunctionResult`, containing an estimated error, termination status, work counts, and algorithm identifier.

| Standard form | Computes | Form with options | Scoped API |
|---|---|---|---|
| `normal_cdf` | $\Phi(x)$ | `normal_cdf_with_options` | None |
| `normal_sf` | $Q(x) = 1 - \Phi(x)$ | `normal_sf_with_options` | None |
| `normal_log_cdf` | $\ln\Phi(x)$ | `normal_log_cdf_with_options` | None |
| `normal_log_sf` | $\ln Q(x)$ | `normal_log_sf_with_options` | None |
| `normal_quantile` | $x$ satisfying $\Phi(x) = p$ | `normal_quantile_with_options` | None |
| `normal_isf` | $x$ satisfying $Q(x) = p$ | `normal_isf_with_options` | None |
| `normal_inv_log_cdf` | $x$ satisfying $\ln\Phi(x) = \ln p$ | `normal_inv_log_cdf_with_options` | `normal_inv_log_cdf_scoped` |
| `normal_inv_log_sf` | $x$ satisfying $\ln Q(x) = \ln q$ | `normal_inv_log_sf_with_options` | `normal_inv_log_sf_scoped` |
| `owens_t` | $T(h, a)$ | `owens_t_with_options` | None |

Accuracy targets are summarized below.
ULP values apply to ranges checked against independently generated references, and estimated errors are returned by the forms with options; neither is an interval with guaranteed enclosure.

| Function | binary64 | binary32 | Estimated error with options |
|---|---|---|---|
| `normal_cdf`, `normal_sf`, `normal_log_cdf`, `normal_log_sf` | 8 ULP on $-38 \le x \le 38$ | 1 ULP | 64 units |
| `normal_quantile`, `normal_isf` | 4 ULP (including subnormal $p$) | 1 ULP | 64 units |
| `normal_inv_log_cdf`, `normal_inv_log_sf` | No target | No target | 64 units |
| `owens_t` | No target | No target | Derived from quadrature and tail errors |

Iterations reaching the budget give `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`) in standard forms; options forms and scoped APIs return results with termination status `IterationLimit` if an estimate is available.

## Summary

Compute normal tails directly with `normal_sf` and `normal_log_sf`; avoid subtraction $1 - \Phi(x)$, which becomes zero at $x > 8.29$.
Use logarithmic forms for the range where $Q(x)$ is subnormal at $x > 37.52$ and for likelihood calculations, expecting relative accuracy of order $\varepsilon\lvert\ln Q\rvert$ for probabilities recovered from them.
`normal_quantile` and `normal_isf` compute quantiles without iteration to a 4 ULP target; use log-probability inverses for probabilities below the type's range.
Owen's $T$ function gives bivariate normal quadrant probabilities, but subtraction reduces relative accuracy when the result is smaller than its individual terms.
