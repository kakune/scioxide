Separating problems involving a harmonic-oscillator potential with a uniform field or waves crossing a parabolic barrier in parabolic cylinder coordinates gives Weber's equation.
Its solutions are parabolic cylinder functions; quantum harmonic-oscillator eigenfunctions (Hermite functions) are the special case of nonnegative integer order.

This page covers the parabolic cylinder functions $D_\nu$, $V_\nu$, and $W(a, x)$, and $D_n$ for complex arguments.
After definitions and properties, it explains how and why `scioxide-special` selects evaluation methods by order and argument region, accuracy, and APIs; the final cell checks values with Wronskians ($fg' - f'g$ for two solutions $f$ and $g$) and Hermite polynomials.
Struve functions, in the same family of Bessel-type equations with inhomogeneous terms or quadratic potentials, are covered in [Struve functions and Bessel–Struve integrals](../struve-parabolic-and-bessel-integrals/).

## Weber's equation and parabolic cylinder functions

For Weber's equation (DLMF 12.2.2),

$$
w'' - \left(\frac{x^2}{4} + a\right)w = 0
$$

the solution decaying as $x \to +\infty$ is denoted $U(a, x)$, and the growing solution $V(a, x)$ (DLMF §12.2).
`scioxide-special` uses Whittaker's notation $D_\nu(x) = U(-\nu - 1/2, x)$, and `order` in `parabolic_d(order, x)` means $\nu$.
$D_\nu$ satisfies $y'' + (\nu + 1/2 - x^2/4)\,y = 0$ and has $D_\nu(x) \sim x^\nu e^{-x^2/4}$ as $x \to +\infty$ (DLMF 12.9.1).
It is entire in $\nu$ and $x$, so any finite values are accepted.
For nonnegative integer $n$, it can be written using the Hermite polynomial $\mathrm{He}_n$ as $D_n(x) = e^{-x^2/4}\mathrm{He}_n(x)$ (DLMF 12.7.2), the quantum harmonic-oscillator eigenfunction.

`parabolic_v(order, x)` returns $V_\nu(x) = V(-\nu - 1/2, x)$ in the same notation.
It grows as $V_\nu(x) \sim \sqrt{2/\pi}\,x^{-\nu-1}e^{x^2/4}$; values at the origin are as follows.

$$
D_\nu(0) = \frac{2^{\nu/2}\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma((1 - \nu)/2)}, \qquad
V_\nu(0) = -\frac{2^{-\nu/2}\sin(\pi\nu/2)}{\Gamma(1 + \nu/2)}, \qquad
V_\nu'(0) = \frac{2^{(1-\nu)/2}\cos(\pi\nu/2)}{\Gamma((\nu + 1)/2)}.
$$

Here, $1/\Gamma$ is taken as zero at poles of $\Gamma$.
The Wronskians of the two solutions are constant (DLMF 12.2.11, 12.2.20).

$$
\mathscr{W}\{D_\nu(x), D_\nu(-x)\} = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\Gamma(-\nu)}, \qquad
\mathscr{W}\{D_\nu(x), V_\nu(x)\} = \sqrt{2/\pi}.
$$

For nonnegative integer $\nu$, $1/\Gamma(-\nu) = 0$, and $D_n(x)$ and $D_n(-x)$ are linearly dependent ($D_n(-x) = (-1)^n D_n(x)$).
Therefore values at negative $x$ are constructed from $D_\nu$ and $V_\nu$, which are linearly independent for every $\nu$, using connection formulas (DLMF 12.2.15 and the $V$ formula in §12.2, rewritten in $\nu$ notation).

$$
D_\nu(-x) = \cos(\pi\nu)\,D_\nu(x) + \frac{\pi}{\Gamma(-\nu)}V_\nu(x), \qquad
V_\nu(-x) = -\cos(\pi\nu)\,V_\nu(x) - \frac{\sin(\pi\nu)}{\Gamma(\nu + 1)}D_\nu(x).
$$

`parabolic_w(order, x)` returns the real solution $W(a, x)$ of the other equation $w'' + (x^2/4 - a)\,w = 0$ (DLMF §12.14).
Its `order` means the coefficient $a$ rather than $\nu$.
$W(a, x)$ describes waves crossing the parabolic barrier $-x^2/4$, with the following normalization at the origin.

$$
W(a, 0) = 2^{-3/4}\left\lvert\frac{\Gamma(1/4 + ia/2)}{\Gamma(3/4 + ia/2)}\right\rvert^{1/2}, \qquad
W'(a, 0) = -\frac{1}{2W(a, 0)}.
$$

`parabolic_d_complex(n, z)` returns $D_n(z) = e^{-z^2/4}\mathrm{He}_n(z)$ for nonnegative integer order $n$ (`u32`) and complex $z$.
Use `parabolic_d` for real noninteger orders; the combination of complex $z$ and noninteger order is not provided.

## Evaluating parabolic cylinder functions

$D_\nu$ switches methods according to the region of $\nu$ and $x$.
The algorithm identifier in the form with options shows the method: origin series, large-argument asymptotic expansion, or recurrence and integral representation.

- At $x = 0$, compute the origin value $D_\nu(0)$ and slope $D_\nu'(0)$ directly from Gamma functions.
- For $\lvert\nu\rvert \le 2$ and $0 < \lvert x\rvert \le 3$, except nonnegative integer $\nu$, sum the even and odd power series about the origin (DLMF 12.4) with compensated summation (accumulating rounding errors separately).
  Cancellation between the even and odd series is small inside this rectangle and grows outside it.
- For $\lvert x\rvert \ge \max(32, 2(\lvert\nu\rvert + 1))$, sum the asymptotic expansion (DLMF 12.9.1).
  At this boundary, terms decrease sufficiently to the rounding level of `f64`, so truncation error is not an issue.
  Return an error if terms stop decreasing prematurely.

In the intervening region, for $\nu < 0$, apply quadrature to the integral representation (DLMF 12.5.1):

$$
D_{-\alpha}(x) = \frac{e^{-x^2/4}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^\infty t^{\alpha - 1}e^{-t^2/2 - xt}\,dt, \qquad \alpha = -\nu > 0
$$


For $0 < \alpha < 1$, substitute $t = u^4$ to smooth the origin singularity, subtract the density $t^{\alpha-1}e^{-t}$ whose integral is $\Gamma(\alpha)$, then apply composite Simpson quadrature with successive spacing halvings.
For $\alpha \ge 1$, use a logarithmic variable centered on the integrand peak (saddle point) before applying the same quadrature, and bound the discarded tails using log-concavity of the integrand.
Both stop when the difference between successive spacing halvings falls to rounding level.

For $\nu > 0$, advance the order recurrence (DLMF 12.8.1 in $\nu$ notation) upward from negative orders at least 1 below $\nu$ (for integer $\nu$, use $D_0 = e^{-x^2/4}$ and $D_1 = xe^{-x^2/4}$):

$$
D_{\nu+1}(x) = x\,D_\nu(x) - \nu\,D_{\nu-1}(x)
$$


Well beyond the turning point $2\sqrt{\nu + 1/2}$ in $x$, $D_\nu(x)$ is the dominant recurrence solution (being multiplied by about $x$ per step while the other solution is multiplied by $\nu/x$); inside the turning point, both solutions oscillate with comparable magnitudes, so upward recurrence does not cause exponential error growth.
Work is about $\nu$ steps; under the default work limit (262144 units), very large positive orders can be evaluated only in the asymptotic region $\lvert x\rvert \ge 2(\nu + 1)$.
For negative $x$, use the connection formula above, also computing $V_\nu(\lvert x\rvert)$.

$V_\nu$ sums an asymptotic expansion for $\lvert x\rvert \ge \max(32, 2(\lvert\nu\rvert + 1))$; for smaller $x$, it continues the solution from the origin values to $x$ by Taylor-series steps of the differential equation.
Continuation advances $e^{-x^2/4}V_\nu$ with the growth factor removed, keeping the factor $e^{x^2/4}$ separately as a logarithmic scale.
The step size from position $x_0$ is at most $0.5/(1 + x_0 + \sqrt{\lvert\nu\rvert})$, and each step sums through the $h^{25}$ term.
For negative $x$, use the connection formula with $D_\nu$.

$W(a, x)$ computes $W(a, 0)$ with 64-term Chebyshev expansions over five intervals of $\lvert a\rvert$ (boundaries 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3.5, 5), then continues to $x$ with Taylor-series steps of the same form.
Each step advances two unit solutions to form a transition matrix, which also propagates the accumulated error.
Matching the range used to construct the Chebyshev expansions, only $\lvert a\rvert \le 5$ and $\lvert x\rvert \le 5$ are accepted.

`parabolic_d_complex` advances the three-term recurrence $\mathrm{He}_{k+1}(z) = z\,\mathrm{He}_k(z) - k\,\mathrm{He}_{k-1}(z)$ with intermediate rescaling, then multiplies by $e^{-z^2/4}$ with its phase $-\operatorname{Im}(z^2)/4$ split into high and low parts.
Work is $n + 1$ steps.

Every method stores $D_\nu$ and $V_\nu$ as “significand × $e^{\text{scale}}$.”
Even when large $\lvert x\rvert$ makes the value exceed the type's range, the form with options returns scaled and logarithmic forms, setting projection to an ordinary value to `Overflow` (the standard form returns an error).
The form of `parabolic_d_complex` with options also returns a scaled form when the value overflows.
The ordinary value is formed as $e^{\ln\lvert\text{significand}\rvert + \text{scale}}$, so when the exponent has magnitude $L$, its rounding transfers to relative error of order $L\varepsilon$ in the value ($\varepsilon$ is machine epsilon).
Estimated errors are heuristic for every function and are not enclosing intervals.

## Accuracy of parabolic cylinder functions

Parabolic cylinder functions have no uniform ULP specification (ULP is the floating-point spacing at the exact result).
Reference tests for $D_\nu$ and $V_\nu$ compare independently generated references with `f32` and `f64` values, derivatives, and estimated errors over $\lvert\nu\rvert \le 20$ and $\lvert x\rvert \le 10^4$, including overflow cases where only scaled and logarithmic forms remain.
Near zeros, comparisons use absolute error.
References for $W(a, x)$ cover the full accepted rectangle in $a$ and $x$ (a grid of $a = -5, \ldots, 5$ and $x = -5, \ldots, 5$).
Tests for `parabolic_d_complex` are limited to comparisons with low-order closed forms $e^{-z^2/4}\mathrm{He}_n(z)$ and checks for finite results at sampled points in the complex plane.
Check individual accuracy using estimated errors in the form with options (the error in `ValueWithError`) and identities such as those in the cell below.

## API

Every function has a standard form `f(..)` returning only the value, a form with options `f_with_options(.., options)` returning `SpecialFunctionResult`, and a scoped API `f_scoped(.., options, scope)` accepting a solve scope.
The options `ParabolicCylinderOptions` contain only a work limit (`Budget`, not an accuracy request), defaulting to 262144 units.
Arguments are real (`f32`, `f64`); only `parabolic_d_complex` accepts `Complex<S>`.
The forms with options for the three real functions return derivatives, scaled forms, and logarithmic forms in addition to the value.

| Standard form | Form with options | Scoped API | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| `parabolic_d` | `parabolic_d_with_options` | `parabolic_d_scoped` | $D_\nu(x)$ |
| `parabolic_v` | `parabolic_v_with_options` | `parabolic_v_scoped` | $V_\nu(x)$ |
| `parabolic_w` | `parabolic_w_with_options` | `parabolic_w_scoped` | $W(a, x)$（$\lvert a\rvert, \lvert x\rvert \le 5$） |
| `parabolic_d_complex` | `parabolic_d_complex_with_options` | `parabolic_d_complex_scoped` | $D_n(z)$ ($n$ is `u32`, $z$ is complex) |

Classify errors using `kind()` from `SciOxideError`.
Non-finite inputs give `NonFinite`.
`parabolic_w` returns `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) identifying argument `order` for $\lvert a\rvert > 5$, or argument `x` for $\lvert x\rvert > 5$.
When the value exceeds the type's range, the standard form returns `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`); stopping at the work limit returns `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).
If asymptotic terms stop decreasing or the origin series fails to converge, evaluation returns `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).
`parabolic_d_complex` returns `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`) when $z^2/4$ cannot be represented in `f64` (around $\lvert z\rvert > 10^{154}$) and the result does not underflow to zero.
The termination status (`SpecialFunctionTermination`) of a successful form with options is `Converged`.

## Parabolic cylinder Wronskians and Hermite polynomials

The next cell selects order $\nu$, forms two Wronskians from the values and derivatives returned by the forms of $D_\nu(\pm x)$ and $V_\nu(x)$ with options, and compares them with constants $\sqrt{2\pi}/\Gamma(-\nu)$ and $\sqrt{2/\pi}$.
Evaluation points $x = 0.5, 2$ use the origin series (when $\lvert\nu\rvert \le 2$ and the order is not a nonnegative integer), $x = 3.5, 5, 10, 20$ use integral quadrature or recurrence, and $x = 40$ lies in the asymptotic region.
Since $D_\nu(-x)$ is constructed from $D_\nu(x)$ and $V_\nu(x)$ by a connection formula, the first Wronskian also checks that formula.
A Wronskian is a difference of products of values and derivatives, so error is measured relative to the product magnitude $\lvert D_\nu(x)D_\nu'(-x)\rvert + \lvert D_\nu'(x)D_\nu(-x)\rvert$ (in units of $\varepsilon$).
For nonnegative integer $\nu$, the cell also shows relative errors against $e^{-x^2/4}\mathrm{He}_n(x)$ computed manually with the three-term Hermite recurrence (blank for other $\nu$).

```rust
//| id: special-parabolic-d-hermite-check
//| ref: special-parabolic-d-hermite-check
//| title: "Check parabolic cylinder functions with Wronskians and Hermite polynomials"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   order: {"type":"range","label":"Order ν [-]","description":"For nonnegative integers, also compare with Hermite polynomials.","min":-4,"max":8,"step":0.1,"value":0.3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"negative","label":"ν = −2.5","description":"Negative order using integral quadrature outside the origin-series region.","inputs":{"order":-2.5}}
//|   - {"id":"integer","label":"ν = 3","description":"1/Γ(−ν) = 0 makes D_ν(x) and D_ν(−x) linearly dependent. Compare with Hermite polynomials.","inputs":{"order":3}}
//|   - {"id":"recurrence","label":"ν = 6.7","description":"Construct by seven upward recurrence steps from negative orders.","inputs":{"order":6.7}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.8788645270258308,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/3/2","range":{"max":64}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":64}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/6/2","range":{"max":512}}
//|   - case: negative
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"max":64}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/6/3","range":{"max":512}}
//|   - case: integer
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/2/2","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"max":16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":64}}
//|   - case: recurrence
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"max":64}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskians","path":"/rows/4/3","range":{"max":64}}
use scioxide_special::{
    ParabolicCylinderOptions, SpecialFunctionResult, gamma, parabolic_d_with_options,
    parabolic_v_with_options,
};

let options = ParabolicCylinderOptions::default();
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let eps = f64::EPSILON;
let nu = (order * 10.0).round() / 10.0;
let integer = nu >= 0.0 && nu == nu.round();
// √(2π)/Γ(−ν). For nonnegative integers, 1/Γ(−ν) = 0.
let w_dd = if integer {
    0.0
} else {
    (2.0 * pi).sqrt() / gamma(-nu).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
};
let w_dv = (2.0 / pi).sqrt();
let pair = |r: &SpecialFunctionResult<f64>| -> Result<(f64, f64), String> {
    let v = r.try_value().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let d = r.try_derivative().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok((v, d))
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for &x in &[0.5_f64, 2.0, 3.5, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0, 40.0] {
    let plus = parabolic_d_with_options(nu, x, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let minus = parabolic_d_with_options(nu, -x, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let v = parabolic_v_with_options(nu, x, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let ((d, dp), (m, mp), (vv, vp)) = (pair(&plus)?, pair(&minus)?, pair(&v)?);
    // d/dx D_ν(−x) = −D_ν′(−x).
    let first = d * (-mp) - dp * m;
    let first_scale = (d * mp).abs() + (dp * m).abs();
    let second = d * vp - dp * vv;
    let second_scale = (d * vp).abs() + (dp * vv).abs();
    // For nonnegative integer n, compute e^{−x²/4} He_n(x) manually by three-term recurrence (independent check).
    let hermite = integer.then(|| {
        let (mut previous, mut current) = (0.0_f64, 1.0_f64);
        for k in 0..(nu as u32) {
            let next = x * current - f64::from(k) * previous;
            previous = current;
            current = next;
        }
        let exact = (-0.25 * x * x).exp() * current;
        (d - exact).abs() / (eps * exact.abs())
    });
    rows.push((
        x,
        d,
        (!integer).then(|| (first - w_dd).abs() / (eps * first_scale)),
        (second - w_dv).abs() / (eps * second_scale),
        hermite,
        plus.counts().iterations(),
    ));
}
let sci6 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 6 });
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "x [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "D_ν(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "W{D_ν(x), D_ν(−x)} error [ε units]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "W{D_ν, V_ν} error [ε units]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative error against Hermite [ε units]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "D_ν(x) work [units]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "wronskians" })
);
```

For the default $\nu = 0.3$, errors in both Wronskians are at most 15 $\varepsilon$ units at $x \le 10$, 73–75 at $x = 20$, and 97–98 $\varepsilon$ units at asymptotic point $x = 40$.
For $\nu = -2.5$, errors are at most 17 $\varepsilon$ units for $x \le 20$, but 199–260 $\varepsilon$ units at $x = 40$.
At $x = 40$, products combine $D_\nu(40) \approx 6 \times 10^{-174}$ with $D_\nu(-40)$ of order $10^{171}$, but storing values as “significand × $e^{\text{scale}}$” keeps the products correctly of order 1.
The scale here is about $\pm 400$, and exponent rounding on conversion to ordinary values can transfer relative error of order $400\varepsilon$ to the values.
The increase to several hundred $\varepsilon$ units at large $x$ is within this rounding range.

The work column shows the different methods.
The origin series ($x \le 2$ at $\nu = 0.3$) uses 11–24 units, the asymptotic expansion 5–8, saddle-point quadrature at $\nu = -2.5$ 1990, and negative-order quadrature plus recurrence at $\nu = 0.3$ and $6.7$ 961–4039.
For nonnegative integer $\nu$, the Wronskian constant is zero, so that column is blank and comparison uses relative error against Hermite polynomials.
At $\nu = 3$, two recurrence steps from $D_0$ and $D_1$ require two work units; relative errors against Hermite are at most 5 $\varepsilon$ units for $x \le 10$ and 90 $\varepsilon$ units at $x = 40$.
The error at $x = 40$ comes from exponent rounding on conversion from scale $e^{-400}$ to an ordinary value, rather than from the recurrence.
If errors substantially exceed this level, check whether the arguments lie inside the reference-test range ($\lvert\nu\rvert \le 20$, $\lvert x\rvert \le 10^4$) and inspect the estimated error in the form with options.

## Summary

The parabolic cylinder function $D_\nu$ uses power series in the origin rectangle $\lvert\nu\rvert \le 2$, $\lvert x\rvert \le 3$, asymptotic expansion for $\lvert x\rvert \ge \max(32, 2(\lvert\nu\rvert + 1))$, and negative-order integral quadrature and positive-order upward recurrence in between; negative $x$ uses a connection formula with $V_\nu$.
$V_\nu$ and $W(a, x)$ use Taylor continuation from origin values, and $W$ accepts only $\lvert a\rvert, \lvert x\rvert \le 5$.
Values are stored as “significand × $e^{\text{scale}}$,” so values beyond the `f64` range remain usable through scaled and logarithmic forms in the form with options.
There is no uniform ULP specification and estimated errors are heuristic, so check important values using identities such as Wronskians or Hermite polynomials.
See [Kelvin, Struve, and parabolic cylinder functions](../../api/kelvin-struve-parabolic/) for order conventions and individual specifications, and [Struve functions and Bessel–Struve integrals](../struve-parabolic-and-bessel-integrals/) for solutions of the inhomogeneous Bessel equation.
