The dilogarithm $\operatorname{Li}_2(z)$, as the value of the logarithmic integral $-\int_0^z \ln(1 - t)/t\,dt$, appears in Feynman loop integrals, lattice models of statistical mechanics, and calculations of special zeta values.
The Spence function expresses the same function at argument $1 - z$ and is used in formulas for radiative corrections, among others.
The Clausen function $\operatorname{Cl}_2(\theta)$ is the imaginary part of the dilogarithm on the unit circle and appears in hyperbolic volumes and lattice sums.

This page explains definitions and cuts of the three functions, the functional equations and series used by scioxide-special, accuracy guarantees, and API selection.
All three use a fixed number of terms, so work barely varies with the argument, and they have no scoped APIs.
The general-order $s$ polylogarithm $\operatorname{Li}_s(z)$ and Lerch transcendent use other methods whose work varies substantially with the argument; see [General-order polylogarithms and the Lerch transcendent](../polylog-and-lerch/).
The final two cells numerically check dilogarithm functional equations and Clausen special values.

## Definitions and cuts

### Dilogarithm

The order-$s = 2$ case of the polylogarithm $\operatorname{Li}_s(z) = \sum_{k \ge 1} z^k/k^s$ is called the dilogarithm (DLMF 25.12.1–25.12.2).

$$
\operatorname{Li}_2(z) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{z^k}{k^2} = -\int_0^z \frac{\ln(1 - t)}{t} \, dt, \qquad \lvert z\rvert \le 1
$$

Analytic continuation of the integral representation in $z$ makes $\operatorname{Li}_2$ single-valued on the plane cut along $[1, \infty)$.
On a cut, the signed zero of the imaginary part ($+0$ or $-0$) selects the value on the upper or lower bank.

$\operatorname{Li}_2(0) = 0$, $\operatorname{Li}_2(1) = \pi^2/6$, and $\operatorname{Li}_2(-1) = -\pi^2/12$.
Real $x > 1$ lies on the cut, with imaginary parts $\pm\pi \ln x$ on its two banks.
In this case, scioxide-special's real `dilog` returns the real part of the principal value, $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Li}_2(x)$.

The dilogarithm has functional equations mapping arguments to other regions.
In the following formulas, $z$ is complex and lies in the range indicated to the right of each formula.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Li}_2(z) + \operatorname{Li}_2(1 - z) &= \frac{\pi^2}{6} - \ln z \, \ln(1 - z) && \text{(reflection formula, DLMF 25.12.6)} \\
\operatorname{Li}_2(z) + \operatorname{Li}_2(1/z) &= -\frac{\pi^2}{6} - \frac{1}{2}\ln^2(-z) && z \notin [0, \infty) \text{(inversion formula, DLMF 25.12.4)} \\
\operatorname{Li}_2(z) + \operatorname{Li}_2\!\left(\frac{z}{z - 1}\right) &= -\frac{1}{2}\ln^2(1 - z) && z \notin [1, \infty) \text{(Landen's formula, DLMF 25.12.3)}
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the inversion formula to real $x > 1$, substituting $\ln(-1/x) = -\ln x \pm i\pi$, and taking real parts gives $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Li}_2(x) + \operatorname{Li}_2(1/x) = \pi^2/3 - \frac{1}{2}\ln^2 x$.
The right-hand side decreases with $x$, so $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Li}_2(x)$ crosses zero at $x \approx 12.595$ and becomes negative.

### Spence function

The Spence function is the dilogarithm evaluated at $1 - z$.

$$
\operatorname{Li}_2(1 - z) = \int_1^z \frac{\ln t}{1 - t} \, dt
$$

`spence(x)` returns this value, $\operatorname{Li}_2(1 - x)$, equal to $\pi^2/6$ at $x = 0$ and zero at $x = 1$.
Some references call $\operatorname{Li}_2(x)$ itself or a sign-reversed version the Spence function, so align definitions when comparing libraries.
Real `spence` accepts only $x \ge 0$.
For complex inputs, the cut moves to $(-\infty, 0]$; since $z \mapsto 1 - z$ reverses the imaginary sign, signed-zero bank selection is opposite to `dilog`.

### Clausen function

The second-order Clausen function is defined by the following series and integral (Abramowitz–Stegun §27.8).

$$
\operatorname{Cl}_2(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin k\theta}{k^2} = -\int_0^{\theta} \ln\left\lvert 2 \sin\frac{t}{2} \right\rvert \, dt
$$

$\theta$ is in radians; $\operatorname{Cl}_2$ is odd, has period $2\pi$, is continuous, and has no poles.
Its relation to the dilogarithm on the unit circle is $\operatorname{Cl}_2(\theta) = \operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{i\theta})$.
It attains its maximum $1.0149416064\ldots$ at $\theta = \pi/3$, where the derivative $-\ln\lvert 2\sin(\theta/2)\rvert$ vanishes; this equals the volume of a regular ideal tetrahedron in hyperbolic space.
At $\theta = \pi/2$, it equals Catalan's constant $G = 0.9159655941\ldots$, and the duplication formula $\operatorname{Cl}_2(2\theta) = 2\operatorname{Cl}_2(\theta) - 2\operatorname{Cl}_2(\pi - \theta)$ holds.
Near the origin, $\operatorname{Cl}_2(\theta) \approx \theta(1 - \ln\lvert\theta\rvert)$, with a logarithmically divergent slope.

## Evaluation methods

### Real dilogarithm and Spence function

Real `dilog` and `spence` reduce arguments to $[-1, 1/2]$ with functional equations, then evaluate a series in $w = -\ln(1 - x)$.
Substituting $t = 1 - e^{-u}$ into the integral representation of $\operatorname{Li}_2$ gives $\operatorname{Li}_2(x) = \int_0^{w} u/(e^u - 1)\, du$.
Termwise integration of the Bernoulli expansion $u/(e^u - 1) = \sum_{n \ge 0} B_n u^n/n!$ of the integrand gives the following series.

$$
\operatorname{Li}_2(x) = w - \frac{w^2}{4} + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{B_{2k}}{(2k+1)\,(2k)!}\, w^{2k+1}
$$

$B_{2k}$ are Bernoulli numbers.
The series converges for $|w| < 2\pi$, and $[-1, 1/2]$ gives $|w| \le \ln 2$, so terms decay roughly as $(\ln 2/2\pi)^{2k}$.
The implementation truncates after 13 terms through $k = 13$, with truncation error below $8 \times 10^{-29}$.
The defining power series $\sum x^k/k^2$ needs about 50 terms to reach double-precision rounding level even at $x = 1/2$, and converges still more slowly as $x \to 1$.

Intervals are assigned as follows.
$-1 \le x \le 1/2$ uses the series directly; $1/2 < x < 2$ uses reflection; $x < -1$ and $x \ge 2$ use inversion to map to $[-1, 1/2]$.
For reflection at $1 < x < 2$, use $\ln\lvert 1 - x\rvert$ instead of $\ln(1 - x)$ to obtain the real part.
In reflection and inversion, the constant $\pi^2/6$ or $\pi^2/3$, a product of logarithms, and the series value may cancel, so their sum is assembled in double-double arithmetic (representing one value as a sum of two double-precision numbers).
For $12 \le x \le 13$, containing the zero $x \approx 12.595$ of $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Li}_2(x)$, sum the defining series at $1/x$ in double-double arithmetic; for $8 \le x \le 24$, also compute $\ln x$ in double-double arithmetic to keep absolute error at rounding level near the zero.
`spence` uses the same kernel, assigning $x < 1/2$ to reflection, $1/2 \le x \le 2$ to the series, and $x > 2$ to inversion.

Work is one unit for interval selection and one per Bernoulli-series term, totaling 14 units for most arguments.
For $12 \le x \le 13$, summing up to 32 defining-series terms instead of the Bernoulli series takes about 30 units; exact values such as $x = 0$ return with zero work.
Work stays in this range regardless of argument, so `dilog` and `spence` have no scoped APIs.
Inputs of type `f32` are also widened to double precision and evaluated by the same kernel, with one final rounding.

### Complex dilogarithm

Complex `dilog` uses the same transformations.
For $|z| > 1$, inversion maps inside the unit circle; points with $|z| \le 1$ and $\operatorname{Re} z > 1/2$ map to $1 - z$ by reflection; remaining points use the same Bernoulli series.
When $|z| \le 1$ and $\operatorname{Re} z > 1/2$, $\lvert 1 - z\rvert < 1$, so at most two transformations suffice, and the transformed $w$ lies in the series convergence disk $|w| < 2\pi$.
Work is one unit per transformation plus 13 series units, totaling 14–16 units.
The form with options estimates error from the last term and sum magnitude, adding rounding from logarithmic terms in the transformations.
Complex `spence(z)` computes `dilog(1 − z)` with the same kernel.

### Clausen function

`clausen` uses the argument directly for $\lvert\theta\rvert \le \pi/2$; otherwise, full-range argument reduction computes $\sin\theta$ and $\cos\theta$, and $\operatorname{atan2}$ extracts signed distance $u$ ($|u| \le \pi/2$) from the nearest multiple of $2\pi$ or odd multiple of $\pi$.
Since it does not subtract $2\pi k$ from $\theta$, reduction avoids loss of digits even for $\theta$ near the largest finite value.

Near multiples of $2\pi$, use the form $\operatorname{Cl}_2(u) = u\,(c(u) - \ln|u|)$ separating the logarithmic singularity, evaluating its regular part $c(u)$ with a 15-term Chebyshev expansion in $t = 2(u/\pi)^2 - 1$.
Near odd multiples of $\pi$, $\operatorname{Cl}_2$ is regular, so use the 32-term power series $u\,(\ln 2 - \sum_{k=1}^{32} c_k\,(u/\pi)^{2k})$.
Both have fixed term counts and no scoped API.
The form with options consumes one work unit in `ClausenOptions` per evaluated coefficient; the default limit 128 exceeds the longest expansion of 32 terms.
Setting a limit below the term count makes the form with options return a partially summed value with termination status `IterationLimit` and estimated error based on omitted terms.
The standard form `clausen(x)` uses the default limit, so this truncation does not occur.

## Errors and accuracy guarantees

Read accuracy in three categories.
The first is the ULP target tested at reference-data points (independent high-precision reference values).
The second is the estimated error returned by the form with options, derived from truncation and rounding but not an enclosing interval (an interval guaranteed to contain the true value).
The third is a range with no uniform bound, where the estimated error should be inspected.

| Function | Real arguments | Complex arguments |
|---|---|---|
| `dilog`, `spence` | Target 64 ULP for both `f32` and `f64`, checked at every reference-data point | No uniform ULP bound; return an estimated error |
| `clausen` | No uniform ULP bound; the form with options returns a derived error estimate | Not provided |

Tests at randomly selected points check `spence` in `f64` within 2.8 ULP, `dilog` in `f64` within 31 ULP at 99% of points, and both in `f32` within 1 ULP.
The same tests also check that actual errors never exceed the estimated errors from the forms with options at any tested point, including near boundaries.
Complex arguments are tested against independent references within the larger of relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ and absolute $5 \times 10^{-13}$ (`f64`).
`clausen` is tested for values and error estimates with `f32`, `f64`, and subnormal arguments.

Near zero function values, ULP errors may become large.
Near the zero $x \approx 12.595$ of $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Li}_2(x)$, reference tests check absolute errors at most $5 \times 10^{-16}$.
Near $\theta = \pi$ for $\operatorname{Cl}_2$, values approach zero, so relative errors may also become large.

## API

| Function | Scalar | Form with options | Scoped API | Options | Domain errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dilog(x)` | `NumericScalar` (real and complex) | `dilog_with_options` | None | `PolylogOptions` | None |
| `spence(x)` | `NumericScalar` | `spence_with_options` | None | `PolylogOptions` | Real $x < 0$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) |
| `clausen(x)` | `RealScalar` only | `clausen_with_options` | None | `ClausenOptions` | None |

Every function gives `NonFinite` for non-finite inputs.
The default work limit is 65536 units for `PolylogOptions` and 128 for `ClausenOptions`; neither is an accuracy request.
If the limit in `dilog_with_options` or `spence_with_options` is below the work needed for evaluation (about 14–30 units above), evaluation returns `ConvergenceFailure` without a value, even for real arguments.
As described above, `clausen_with_options` returns a truncated value with `IterationLimit`.

The order-2 `polylog(2.0, x)` uses the same kernel as `dilog(x)`, returning values under the same real-part convention even for real $x > 1$.
A complex Clausen function is not provided.
For complex $\theta$, evaluate the right side of $\operatorname{Cl}_2(\theta) = \frac{1}{2i}\left[\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{i\theta}) - \operatorname{Li}_2(e^{-i\theta})\right]$, valid for real $\theta$, using complex `dilog`.
Higher-order Clausen functions $\operatorname{Cl}_n$ are also not provided; compute $\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_n(e^{i\theta})$ for even $n$ or $\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Li}_n(e^{i\theta})$ for odd orders using complex `polylog`.

## Checking dilogarithm functional equations

The next cell takes input $x$ with $0 < x < 1$ and compares both sides of reflection, Landen's formula, and two inversion formulas.
The left side sums `dilog` values; the right side is an elementary expression containing only logarithms and $\pi^2$.
At $x = 0.3$, $1 - x$ uses reflection, $x/(x - 1)$ and $x$ use the series, and $-1/x$ and $1/x$ use inversion, so one $x$ exercises all three intervals.
The Spence row checks that `spence(1 − x)` equals `dilog(x)`.
The second table shows results of the form with options `dilog_with_options`: estimated error, termination status, and work units.

## Clausen special values and the dilogarithm on the unit circle

At the input angle $\theta$, the next cell compares `clausen` with the imaginary part $\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_2(e^{i\theta})$ of complex `dilog` and checks the duplication formula.
The following three rows use angle-independent special values, checking that $\operatorname{Cl}_2(\pi/2)$, $\Phi(-1, 2, 1/2)/4$ from the Lerch transcendent, and $\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{Li}_2(i)$ all equal Catalan's constant $G$.
$\Phi(-1, 2, 1/2)/4 = \sum_{n \ge 0} (-1)^n/(2n+1)^2 = G$ is the Dirichlet beta value $\beta(2)$; [General-order polylogarithms and the Lerch transcendent](../polylog-and-lerch/) explains evaluation of `lerch_phi`.
References for $G$ and the maximum $\operatorname{Cl}_2(\pi/3)$ are constants with at least 18 significant digits from a separate arbitrary-precision computation.
The chart shows $\operatorname{Cl}_2$ on $[0, 2\pi]$, with vertical lines at the selected $\theta$ and the maximum location $\pi/3$.

## Summary

The dilogarithm and Spence function map arguments to $[-1, 1/2]$ through reflection and inversion and evaluate 13 Bernoulli-series terms in $w = -\ln(1 - x)$, so work is nearly constant and no scoped API is provided; real arguments meet the 64 ULP target on the reference data.
For real `dilog`, $x > 1$ returns the real part of the principal value; for imaginary parts, use a complex type and select the cut bank with signed zero.
Definitions of the Spence function differ among references, so check that `spence(x)` means $\operatorname{Li}_2(1 - x)$ before comparing values.
`clausen` accepts only real inputs and uses full-range argument reduction and fixed-term expansions; substitute complex `dilog` and `polylog` for complex angles or higher-order $\operatorname{Cl}_n$.

See [General-order polylogarithms and the Lerch transcendent](../polylog-and-lerch/) for evaluation methods and work for general orders.
See [Lambert W and Wright ω functions](../lambert-w-and-wright-omega/) for solving $w e^w = z$ on each branch.
