Separating the Helmholtz equation $\nabla^2\psi + k^2\psi = 0$ in spherical coordinates expresses the radial functions in terms of half-integer-order Bessel functions.
These are spherical Bessel functions $j_n$ and $y_n$, their modified counterparts $i_n$ and $k_n$, and spherical Hankel functions $h_n^{(1)}$ and $h_n^{(2)}$ describing traveling waves; they appear in scattering partial-wave expansions, acoustic and electromagnetic multipole expansions, and Mie scattering.
Riccati–Bessel functions, obtained by multiplying the radial functions by $x$, solve the radial Schrödinger equation in one-dimensional form.
This page covers definitions and properties, evaluation in `scioxide-special`, accuracy, and APIs, then numerically checks recurrence direction and Wronskians.
It uses cylindrical Bessel functions and stable recurrence directions described in [Cylindrical Bessel and Hankel functions](../airy-bessel-and-hankel/).

## Spherical Bessel and spherical Hankel functions

After separation as $\psi = R(r)\,Y_\ell^m(\theta, \phi)$, $R$ as a function of $x = kr$ satisfies the spherical Bessel equation (DLMF 10.47.1).

$$
x^2 w'' + 2x w' + \left[x^2 - n(n+1)\right]w = 0, \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots
$$

Solutions are expressed in half-integer-order cylindrical Bessel functions (DLMF §10.47).

$$
j_n(z) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\,J_{n+1/2}(z), \qquad
y_n(z) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\,Y_{n+1/2}(z), \qquad
h_n^{(1,2)}(z) = j_n(z) \pm \mathrm{i}\,y_n(z).
$$

As solutions of the modified equation $x^2 w'' + 2x w' - [x^2 + n(n+1)]w = 0$, `scioxide-special` defines $i_n$ and $k_n$ with the DLMF §10.47 convention.

$$
i_n(z) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\,I_{n+1/2}(z), \qquad
k_n(z) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}\,K_{n+1/2}(z).
$$

Normalization of $k_n$ varies between references, some using $\sqrt{2/(\pi z)}\,K_{n+1/2}$, so check $k_0$ when comparing implementations.
Half-integer-order Bessel functions have elementary closed forms; low-order values are as follows (DLMF §10.49).

$$
j_0(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x}, \quad j_1(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x^2} - \frac{\cos x}{x}, \quad
y_0(x) = -\frac{\cos x}{x}, \quad i_0(x) = \frac{\sinh x}{x}, \quad k_0(x) = \frac{\pi}{2x}e^{-x}.
$$

$j_n$ and $i_n$ are entire, with $j_0(0) = i_0(0) = 1$ and zero at the origin for $n \ge 1$, and satisfy $j_n(-z) = (-1)^n j_n(z)$.
$y_n$, $k_n$, and $h_n^{(1,2)}$ have poles at the origin.
$y_n$ and $h_n^{(1,2)}$ also accept negative real arguments ($y_n(-z) = (-1)^{n+1}y_n(z)$), while real $k_n$ accepts only $x > 0$.

$j_n$, $y_n$, and $h_n^{(1,2)}$ satisfy the three-term recurrence and derivative formulas (DLMF 10.51.1–2).

$$
f_{n-1}(z) + f_{n+1}(z) = \frac{2n+1}{z}f_n(z), \qquad
f_n'(z) = f_{n-1}(z) - \frac{n+1}{z}f_n(z).
$$

The Wronskian is $\mathscr{W}\{j_n, y_n\}(z) = j_n y_n' - j_n' y_n = z^{-2}$ (DLMF 10.50.1), used for numerical checks in the cell below.
As $x \to \infty$, $j_n(x) \sim \sin(x - n\pi/2)/x$ and $h_n^{(1)}(x) \sim (-\mathrm{i})^{n+1}e^{\mathrm{i}x}/x$; $h_n^{(1)}(kr)$ describes an outgoing spherical wave under the time factor $e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}$.
$e^{-\mathrm{i}x}h_n^{(1)}(x)$ is a degree-$n+1$ polynomial in $1/x$, so the scaled form `spherical_hankel_1_scaled` does not oscillate on the real axis.

## Evaluating spherical Bessel functions

For real arguments, $j_n$ and $i_n$ sum the defining series when $\lvert x\rvert \le 1$.
Elsewhere, recurrence advances the order from closed forms $f_0$ and $f_1$.
As in the discussion of [recurrence direction](oxref:special-bessel-recurrence-stability), $j_n$ is the minimal solution for $n > x$, so forward recurrence cannot be used.
Thus $j_n$ ($n > x$) and $i_n$ ($x < 4(n+1)^2$) use Miller backward recurrence, normalized with the larger of closed forms $f_0$ and $f_1$.
$y_n$ and $k_n$ are dominant solutions and always use forward recurrence.
Modified-function recurrences are scaled throughout by an exponential factor, avoiding intermediate overflow.
Work is proportional to order $n$; exceeding the work limit in `SphericalBesselOptions` (65536 units by default) gives `ConvergenceFailure`.

For complex arguments, sum the series for $\lvert z\rvert < 1/4$; elsewhere, use cylindrical Bessel functions of order $n + 1/2$.
The square root $\sqrt{z}$ and cylindrical functions use principal branches.
Off the real axis, spherical Hankel functions are computed from modified functions of order $n + 1/2$.

Exponentially varying functions have scaled forms.
`spherical_in_scaled` returns $e^{-\lvert\operatorname{Re}z\rvert}i_n(z)$, and `spherical_kn_scaled` returns $e^{z}k_n(z)$.
`spherical_hankel_1_scaled` returns $e^{-\mathrm{i}z}h_n^{(1)}(z)$ and `spherical_hankel_2_scaled` returns $e^{\mathrm{i}z}h_n^{(2)}(z)$, remaining in range for large negative or positive $\operatorname{Im}z$ respectively.
Derivatives in the forms with options are derivatives of the unscaled functions multiplied by the same factor; the factor itself is not differentiated.

## Riccati–Bessel functions

For a spherically symmetric potential, substituting $u(r) = r R(r)$ puts the radial Schrödinger equation in the one-dimensional form $u'' + [k^2 - \ell(\ell+1)/r^2 - 2mV/\hbar^2]u = 0$.
The free solutions at $V = 0$ are Riccati–Bessel functions; `scioxide-special` provides the following two for real arguments.

$$
\texttt{riccati\_jn}(n, x) = x\,j_n(x), \qquad \texttt{riccati\_yn}(n, x) = x\,y_n(x).
$$

Both satisfy $u'' + [1 - n(n+1)/x^2]u = 0$, with $x\,j_0(x) = \sin x$ and $x\,y_0(x) = -\cos x$.
The sign is opposite to Abramowitz–Stegun's $C_n(x) = -x\,y_n(x)$, so check sign conventions in Mie scattering formulas.

`riccati_jn` accepts all finite $x$ and sums a series (DLMF 10.53.1) for $\lvert x\rvert \le 1$.
Elsewhere, it advances order from closed forms $\sin x$ and $\sin x/x - \cos x$, using forward recurrence for $n \le \lvert x\rvert$ or Miller backward recurrence normalized by the larger closed form for $n > \lvert x\rvert$.
`riccati_yn` uses forward recurrence from closed forms $-\cos x$ and $-\cos x/x - \sin x$.
At $x = 0$, `riccati_yn` returns the limiting value $-1$ if $n = 0$ and `DomainError` (`Pole`) if $n \ge 1$.
When $n$ is large relative to $\lvert x\rvert$, $\lvert x\,y_n(x)\rvert$ overflows, giving `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).

## Assessing accuracy

Spherical Bessel, spherical Hankel, and Riccati–Bessel functions have no uniform ULP specification.
For real arguments, reference tests compare values and derivatives with independently generated `f32` and `f64` references, measuring error in mixed absolute-relative units $\varepsilon\max(1, \lvert\text{reference}\rvert)$ to remain meaningful near zeros.
At reference-data points, tests check that `f64` errors are within 1024 such units and that estimated errors in the forms with options (the error in `ValueWithError`, not an enclosing interval) cover actual errors.
This measured limit applies to reference-data points, not every accepted argument; inspect options-form estimates when accuracy matters.
Closed-form and forward-recurrence error estimates are derived from rounding-error propagation (`Derived`); Miller backward-recurrence estimates are empirical differences between results at two truncation depths (`Heuristic`) and are not upper bounds.

## API

Every function has standard, options, and scoped forms.
Spherical Bessel and Hankel functions accept real and complex inputs through `NumericScalar`; Riccati–Bessel functions accept only real inputs through `RealScalar`.
The order `order` is a nonnegative `u32` integer.
Options use `SphericalBesselOptions`, containing only a work budget (`Budget`, not an accuracy request; default 65536 units).

| Standard form | Form with options | Scoped API | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| `spherical_jn`, `spherical_yn` | `spherical_jn_with_options`, `spherical_yn_with_options` | `spherical_jn_scoped`, `spherical_yn_scoped` | $j_n(z)$, $y_n(z)$ |
| `spherical_in`, `spherical_kn` | `spherical_in_with_options`, `spherical_kn_with_options` | `spherical_in_scoped`, `spherical_kn_scoped` | $i_n(z)$, $k_n(z)$ |
| `spherical_in_scaled`, `spherical_kn_scaled` | `spherical_in_scaled_with_options`, `spherical_kn_scaled_with_options` | `spherical_in_scaled_scoped`, `spherical_kn_scaled_scoped` | $e^{-\lvert\operatorname{Re}z\rvert}i_n(z)$, $e^{z}k_n(z)$ |
| `spherical_hankel_1`, `spherical_hankel_2` | `spherical_hankel_1_with_options`, `spherical_hankel_2_with_options` | `spherical_hankel_1_scoped`, `spherical_hankel_2_scoped` | $h_n^{(1)}(z)$, $h_n^{(2)}(z)$ (complex) |
| `spherical_hankel_1_scaled`, `spherical_hankel_2_scaled` | `spherical_hankel_1_scaled_with_options`, `spherical_hankel_2_scaled_with_options` | `spherical_hankel_1_scaled_scoped`, `spherical_hankel_2_scaled_scoped` | $e^{-\mathrm{i}z}h_n^{(1)}(z)$, $e^{\mathrm{i}z}h_n^{(2)}(z)$ (complex) |
| `riccati_jn`, `riccati_yn` | `riccati_jn_with_options`, `riccati_yn_with_options` | `riccati_jn_scoped`, `riccati_yn_scoped` | $x\,j_n(x)$, $x\,y_n(x)$ |

Non-finite inputs return `NonFinite`.
$y_n$, $h_n^{(1,2)}$, and `riccati_yn` for $n \ge 1$ return `DomainError` (`Pole`) at $x = 0$; $k_n$ returns `DomainError` (`Pole`) at real $x = 0$ and `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) at $x < 0$.
Evaluations stopped at the work limit return partial results with termination `IterationLimit` in forms with options, or `ConvergenceFailure` if no estimate exists.
[Spherical harmonics](../spherical-harmonics/) covers spherical harmonic functions.

## Spherical Bessel recurrence and Wronskian

The next cell selects argument $x$ and maximum order $N$, computes $\lvert j_n(x)\rvert$ and $\lvert y_n(x)\rvert$ for $n = 0, \dots, N$ with `spherical_jn` and `spherical_yn`, and plots them on a logarithmic axis.
For comparison, it also plots $j_n$ computed by forward recurrence from closed forms $j_0$ and $j_1$.
This forward recurrence is computed manually to demonstrate that $j_n$ is minimal for $n > x$.
Every fifth order, the table shows the API's $j_n$, relative error of the forward recurrence, Wronskian residual $\lvert x^2(j_n y_n' - j_n' y_n) - 1\rvert$, and relative differences of `riccati_jn` and `riccati_yn` from $x\,j_n$ and $x\,y_n$.
Derivatives are formed from API values using $f_n' = f_{n-1} - (n+1)f_n/x$ ($f_0' = -f_1$ for $n = 0$).

```rust
//| id: special-spherical-bessel-wronskian
//| ref: special-spherical-bessel-wronskian
//| title: "Spherical Bessel recurrence direction and Wronskian"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   argument: {"type":"range","label":"Argument x [-]","description":"For n > x, j_n is minimal and y_n dominant.","min":0.5,"max":20,"step":0.5,"value":5}
//|   max_order: {"type":"range","label":"Maximum order N [-]","description":"Maximum plotted order.","min":5,"max":40,"step":5,"value":25}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"large-argument","label":"x = 20, N = 15","description":"All orders lie in the oscillatory region n < x, where forward recurrence also retains accuracy.","inputs":{"argument":20,"max_order":15}}
//|   - {"id":"small-argument","label":"x = 0.5, N = 40","description":"|y_40| exceeds 10^71 and j_40 falls below 10^−72, while the Wronskian is preserved.","inputs":{"argument":0.5,"max_order":40}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-0.1917848549326277,"rtol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/2","range":{"min":1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/5/4","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: large-argument
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: small-argument
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/8/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/8/2","range":{"min":1e100}}
use scioxide_special::{riccati_jn, riccati_yn, spherical_jn, spherical_yn};

let x = argument;
let n_max = max_order as u32;
let mut j = Vec::new();
let mut y = Vec::new();
for n in 0..=n_max + 1 {
    j.push(spherical_jn(n, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
    y.push(spherical_yn(n, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}

// Forward recurrence f_{n+1} = (2n+1)/x f_n − f_{n−1} from closed forms j_0, j_1 (computed manually with f64 standard functions).
let mut forward = vec![x.sin() / x, x.sin() / (x * x) - x.cos() / x];
for n in 1..n_max as usize {
    forward.push((2 * n + 1) as f64 / x * forward[n] - forward[n - 1]);
}

let points = |values: &[f64]| -> Vec<[f64; 2]> {
    values[..=n_max as usize]
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(n, v)| [n as f64, v.abs()])
        .filter(|p| p[1] > 0.0)
        .collect()
};
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "|j_n(x)|（API）", "points": points(&j) }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "|y_n(x)|（API）", "points": points(&y) }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "|j_n(x)| (forward recurrence)",
            "points": points(&forward),
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Degree n [-]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute value [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 40.0,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": x, "label": "n = x" }]
    })
);

// f_n' = f_{n−1} − (n+1)/x f_n, f_0' = −f_1.
let derivative = |f: &[f64], n: usize| {
    if n == 0 {
        -f[1]
    } else {
        f[n - 1] - (n + 1) as f64 / x * f[n]
    }
};
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for n in (0..=n_max).step_by(5) {
    let k = n as usize;
    let wronskian = x * x * (j[k] * derivative(&y, k) - derivative(&j, k) * y[k]) - 1.0;
    let rj = riccati_jn(n, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let ry = riccati_yn(n, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let riccati = ((rj - x * j[k]) / (x * j[k]))
        .abs()
        .max(((ry - x * y[k]) / (x * y[k])).abs());
    rows.push((
        n,
        j[k],
        ((forward[k] - j[k]) / j[k]).abs(),
        wronskian.abs(),
        riccati,
    ));
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Degree n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "j_n(x) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Forward recurrence relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "|x²W − 1| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative difference from Riccati [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })
);
```

At the default $x = 5$, all three curves overlap for $n < x$; for $n > x$, $\lvert j_n\rvert$ decreases by almost one decimal order per order, and $\lvert y_n\rvert$ increases at the same rate.
The forward-recurrence $j_n$ departs from the API values starting at $n \approx 17$, growing in proportion to $\lvert y_n\rvert$ and reaching relative error $3 \times 10^{13}$ at $n = 25$.
The component of dominant solution $y_n$ in the rounding error is amplified; this is why `spherical_jn` uses Miller backward recurrence for $n > x$.
For the default inputs, Wronskian residuals and relative differences from Riccati–Bessel functions are of order $10^{-15}$ at every order.
Keeping $x = 5$ and setting $N = 40$ increases Wronskian residuals and relative differences from Riccati–Bessel functions to order $10^{-14}$ (maximum $5 \times 10^{-14}$) for $n = 35, 40$, where $j_n$ is about $10^{-27}$ or smaller.
Relative errors in minimal solutions computed by backward recurrence can grow to this level, and their estimated errors are empirical.

## Summary

Spherical Bessel functions are half-integer-order cylindrical Bessel functions, computed from closed forms $f_0$ and $f_1$ using Miller backward recurrence for minimal solutions $j_n$ ($n > x$) and $i_n$, and forward recurrence for $y_n$ and $k_n$.
The conventions are $k_n$ as $\sqrt{\pi/(2z)}\,K_{n+1/2}$ and `riccati_yn` as $x\,y_n$, so check normalization and signs at $k_0$ and $x\,y_0 = -\cos x$ when comparing references or implementations.
Compare exponentially varying $i_n$, $k_n$, and $h_n^{(1,2)}$ at large $\lvert z\rvert$ using scaled forms.
These families have no uniform ULP specification, so check accuracy with estimated errors in the forms with options and identities such as Wronskians, interpreting Miller-method estimates as empirical.
See [Legendre functions](../legendre-functions-and-spherical-harmonics/) and [Spherical harmonics](../spherical-harmonics/) for the angular part of partial-wave expansions, and [Coulomb wave functions and hydrogenic radial states](../coulomb-and-hydrogenic/) for radial scattering functions with a Coulomb potential.
