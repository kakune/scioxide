Separating the three-dimensional Helmholtz equation $\nabla^2 u + k^2 u = 0$ in spheroidal coordinates gives spheroidal wave equations for both angular and radial factors.
This equation describes acoustic and electromagnetic scattering by spheroids, natural oscillations of spheroidal cavities, and one-electron states of diatomic molecules.
`scioxide-special` expands angular functions in normalized Ferrers functions, truncates and solves the symmetric tridiagonal eigenproblem for their coefficients, and constructs a plan `SpheroidalPlan` retaining those coefficients.
Eigenproblem truncation is shared with Mathieu functions, so reading [Mathieu functions and spectral methods](../mathieu-and-spheroidal/) first makes the procedure easier to follow.
This page covers the equation and conventions, Ferrers expansion, angular and radial evaluation, accuracy guarantees, and APIs, in that order.

## Spheroidal wave equation and conventions

After separation in spheroidal coordinates, both angular and radial factors satisfy the following equation (Flammer's 1957 convention):

$$
(1 - x^2)\,w'' - 2x\,w' + \left(\lambda_{mn} - s\,c^2x^2 - \frac{m^2}{1 - x^2}\right) w = 0
$$


$m \ge 0$ is the azimuthal degree, $n \ge m$ the degree, $c$ the real size parameter given by half the interfocal distance times the wavenumber, and $\lambda_{mn}$ the characteristic value.
$s = +1$ denotes prolate (`SpheroidalKind::Prolate`, cigar-shaped), and $s = -1$ oblate (`SpheroidalKind::Oblate`, disk-shaped).
The relation to DLMF 30.2.1 notation is $\gamma^2 = s\,c^2$ and $\lambda_{mn} = \lambda_n^m(\gamma^2) + \gamma^2$.

The angular function $S_{mn}(c, x)$ is the solution bounded on $[-1, 1]$.
At $c = 0$, the equation becomes the associated Legendre differential equation, $S_{mn}$ becomes the Ferrers function $P_n^m(x)$ (including the Condon–Shortley phase), and the characteristic value is $\lambda_{mn}(0) = n(n+1)$.
The radial function $R_{mn}^{(j)}(c, x)$ solves the same equation; its physical domain is $x \ge 1$ for prolate and $x \ge 0$ for oblate functions.
$j = 1, 2, 3, 4$ denotes kinds one through four, selected by `First`, `Second`, `Third`, and `Fourth` in `RadialKind`.

`SpectralNormalization::Standard` selects the same normalization as Ferrers functions, $\int_{-1}^1 S_{mn}^2\,dx = 2(n+m)!/((2n+1)(n-m)!)$; `UnitL2` selects unit $L^2$ norm on $[-1, 1]$.
The sign is chosen to connect continuously to $P_n^m$ as $c \to 0$.
Radial normalization is determined by the expansion in the next section and does not depend on angular normalization.

## Ferrers expansion and eigenproblem

Expand the angular function in Ferrers functions.
Set $p = (n - m) \bmod 2$ and let $\bar P_l^m$ be the Ferrers function of unit $L^2$ norm on $[-1, 1]$; write

$$
S_{mn}(c, x) \propto \sum_{k\ge0} d_k\,\bar P_{m+2k+p}^m(x)
$$


Since $x^2 \bar P_l^m$ is a sum of $\bar P_{l-2}^m$, $\bar P_l^m$, and $\bar P_{l+2}^m$, substitution into the equation gives a three-term recurrence for coefficients $d_k$.
Let $(x^2)_{ll}$ and $(x^2)_{l,l+2}$ be matrix elements of $x^2$ in normalized Ferrers functions; then $\boldsymbol d$ is an eigenvector of the symmetric tridiagonal matrix with diagonal $l(l+1) + \gamma^2 (x^2)_{ll}$ and off-diagonal $\gamma^2 (x^2)_{l,l+2}$ ($l = m + 2k + p$), with eigenvalue $\lambda_{mn}$ (DLMF §30.8).
$\lambda_{mn}$ is the $\kappa = \lfloor (n - m)/2 \rfloor$th smallest eigenvalue of this matrix (counting from 0).

Eigenvalue computation, coefficient reconstruction, truncation doubling and acceptance, and interpretation of `SpectralOptions` and `SpectralDiagnostics` are shared with Mathieu functions; see [Computing coefficients](oxref:special-mathieu-coefficients) and [Truncation doubling and acceptance criteria](oxref:special-mathieu-truncation).
Coefficients start decaying where the diagonal $l(l+1)$ exceeds $c^2$, so they spread further as $c$ grows, with the required row count growing roughly in proportion to $c$.
At $c = 0$, the matrix is diagonal, so no eigenproblem is solved; the result is $\lambda_{mn} = n(n+1)$ with unit-vector coefficients.
Besides the eigenproblem, preparation computes $2N$ pairs of Legendre recurrence coefficients for evaluation and the constant normalizing the radial expansion.
Preparation requires $O(N + m)$ operations and $O(N)$ memory, where $N \le 256$ is the accepted coefficient count.

In the small-$c$ limit, first-order perturbation gives

$$
\lambda_{mn}(c) = n(n+1) + s\,c^2\,\frac{2n(n+1) - 2m^2 - 1}{(2n-1)(2n+3)} + O(c^4)
$$


The fraction on the right is the expectation $(x^2)_{nn}$ of $x^2$ in normalized Ferrers functions, identical to DLMF 30.3.8 rewritten as $\lambda_{mn} = \lambda_n^m + \gamma^2$.
The $c^4$ term is proportional to $\gamma^4 = c^4$, so its coefficient is the same for prolate and oblate functions at the same $(m, n)$; signs differ in the $c^2$ and $c^6$ terms.

## Angular evaluation

`SpheroidalPlan::angular(x)` sums the expansion while generating $\bar P_l^m(x)/(1 - x^2)^{m/2}$ by upward recurrence with stored Legendre coefficients, then multiplies by $(1 - x^2)^{m/2}$ and the normalization constant to return the value and derivative in $x$.
Upward recurrence in Ferrers degree is stable on $[-1, 1]$.
Work is one unit per coefficient and per degree step.
`SpheroidalPlan::coefficient(k)` returns normalized-Ferrers coefficients $d_k$ with unit Euclidean norm; the normalization constant is applied during evaluation.

Real $x$ is restricted to $[-1, 1]$; outside, it gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
For complex $x$, it returns analytic continuation using the principal square root in factor $(1 - x^2)^{m/2}$.
Ferrers functions then grow like $\rho^l$ according to the size $\rho = \max|x \pm \sqrt{x^2 - 1}|$ of the Bernstein ellipse through $x$ (foci $\pm1$), so omitted contributions are estimated by multiplying the omitted-coefficient ratio by $\rho^2$.
When that product reaches 1, the expansion fails to converge, returning `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).
At $x = \pm1$, values are finite, but the derivative diverges for $m = 1$, so only the value is returned and the derivative omitted.

The dashed line uses Ferrers functions from `legendre_p_assoc`, a separate implementation from the spheroidal plan.
The table shows the relative difference between $\int_{-1}^1 S_{mn}^2\,dx$ computed by Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature (interpolatory quadrature at Chebyshev nodes, converging exponentially for smooth functions) and normalization $2(n+m)!/((2n+1)(n-m)!)$, and the difference between the plan at $c = 0$ and `legendre_p_assoc`.
The integrand $S_{mn}^2$ is a product of $(1 - x^2)^m$ and a polynomial series, smooth even at endpoints, so Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature over 128 subintervals reaches rounding level.

//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"oblate","label":"Oblate, c = 4","description":"For oblate functions, zeros and peaks move toward the endpoints x = ±1.","inputs":{"kind":"Oblate"}}

//|   - case: default

    SpectralNormalization, SpectralOptions, SpheroidalKind, SpheroidalPlan, legendre_p_assoc,
};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let spheroid = match kind.as_str() {
    "Oblate" => SpheroidalKind::Oblate,
    _ => SpheroidalKind::Prolate,
};
let (m, n) = (m as u32, (m + degree_offset) as u32);
let prepare = |size: f64| {
    let normalization = SpectralNormalization::Standard;
    SpheroidalPlan::new(
        spheroid,
        m,
        n,
        size,
        normalization,
        SpectralOptions::default(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let plan = prepare(c)?;
let angular = |x: f64| {
    plan.angular(x)
        .and_then(|r| r.try_value())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
// Ferrers function Pₙᵐ (c = 0 limit, separate implementation)
let ferrers = |x: f64| legendre_p_assoc(n as usize, m as i32, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string());
let mut curve = Vec::new();
let mut limit = Vec::new();
for i in 0..=100 {
    let x = -1.0 + 2.0 * f64::from(i) / 100.0;
    curve.push([x, angular(x)?]);
    limit.push([x, ferrers(x)?]);
}

let points = 128;
let mut integral = 0.0;
for j in 0..=points {
    let theta = PI * j as f64 / points as f64;
    let mut weight = 1.0;
    for k in 1..=points / 2 {
        let b = if 2 * k == points { 1.0 } else { 2.0 };
        weight -= b * (2.0 * k as f64 * theta).cos() / (4.0 * (k * k) as f64 - 1.0);
    }
    let end = if j == 0 || j == points { 1.0 } else { 2.0 };
    integral += end / points as f64 * weight * angular(theta.cos())?.powi(2);
}
// Normalization value 2(n+m)!/((2n+1)(n−m)!)
let mut norm = 2.0 / (2.0 * f64::from(n) + 1.0);
for factor in (n - m + 1)..=(n + m) {
    norm *= f64::from(factor);
}
let x0 = 0.3;
let sphere = prepare(0.0)?;
let s0 = sphere
    .angular(x0)
    .and_then(|r| r.try_value())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let p0 = ferrers(x0)?;
let rows = vec![
    ("∫₋₁¹ S² dx", integral, norm, (integral - norm).abs() / norm),
    ("S(0.3) at c = 0", s0, p0, (s0 - p0).abs()),
];
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Sₘₙ(c, x)", "points": curve }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Pₙᵐ(x)（c = 0）", "points": limit, "style": { "lineType": "dashed" } })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({ "xLabel": "Angular coordinate x [-]", "yLabel": "Angular function [-]" })
);
let fixed12 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 12 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Check" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Plan value", "type": "number", "format": fixed12 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Reference value", "type": "number", "format": fixed12 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference (relative in first row)", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "angular" })
);

In the chart, zeros move inward from $\pm0.447$ at $P_3^1$ to near $\pm0.35$, peaks on both sides move inward from $\pm0.86$ to $\pm0.78$, and the value at $x = 0$ decreases from 1.5 to 1.39.
Writing the equation as $-((1 - x^2)w')' + (m^2/(1 - x^2) + s\,c^2x^2)\,w = \lambda_{mn}w$, the prolate term $+c^2x^2$ acts like a potential energy growing with $|x|$, pushing the function toward $x = 0$.
For oblate functions, the term becomes $-c^2x^2$ and the direction reverses: zeros move outward to near $\pm0.61$, peaks to $\pm0.92$, and the function spreads toward endpoints $x = \pm1$.
At $c = 0$, the curve overlaps the dashed $P_3^1$, with difference $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$ at $x = 0.3$.

## Radial evaluation

`SpheroidalPlan::radial(x, kind)` evaluates radial functions using a product expansion of Ferrers and spherical Bessel functions (DLMF §30.11) with the same coefficients $d_k$ as the angular functions.
Choose an angular point $\eta$ and set $r = \sqrt{x^2 - s(1 - \eta^2)}$ and $w = \eta x/r$; then

$$
R_{mn}^{(j)}(c, x) = \frac{1}{\tilde S(\eta)}\left(\frac{x^2 - s}{r^2}\right)^{m/2}\sum_{k\ge0} (-1)^{k-\kappa}\,d_k\,\tilde P_{m+2k+p}(w)\,z_{m+2k+p}^{(j)}(c\,r)
$$


$\tilde P_l = \bar P_l^m/(1 - x^2)^{m/2}$ is the polynomial obtained by removing the endpoint factor from a Ferrers function, $\tilde S(\eta) = \sum_k d_k\,\tilde P_{m+2k+p}(\eta)$ is the angular value with the same factor removed, and $\kappa = \lfloor (n - m)/2 \rfloor$.
$z^{(1)} = j_l$, $z^{(2)} = y_l$, $z^{(3)} = h_l^{(1)}$, and $z^{(4)} = h_l^{(2)}$ are spherical Bessel functions; the third and fourth kinds are first kind $\pm i \cdot$ second kind and describe outgoing and incoming waves.
As $x \to \infty$, $r \to x$ and $w \to \eta$, so the sum divides exactly by $\tilde S(\eta)$, giving $R_{mn}^{(1)} \sim \cos(cx - (n+1)\pi/2)/(cx)$.
This asymptotic form determines radial normalization, independently of the choice of $\eta$ and angular normalization.
If $n - m$ is odd and $\eta = 0$ is selected, $\tilde S(0) = 0$, so use the formula differentiated on both sides with respect to $\eta$.

During preparation, $\eta$ is chosen from $0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 1$ to minimize the estimated-error-to-value ratio for $\tilde S(\eta)$; it is available through `SpheroidalPlan::radial_normalization_point`.
For the first kind at points with $|r| \le 0.2$, reselect $\eta$ as 0 or 1 to preserve expansion convergence.
The expansions for kinds two through four contain $y_l$ growing with order and estimate omitted-term ratios as $1/|r|^2$, so these expansions cannot be used for $|r| \le 1$.
Thus for kinds two through four, when $|r| < 1.5$ (also when $\operatorname{Re} x < 0$ for oblate functions), start from values at $x = 3$ and integrate the radial equation with Taylor series to reach the target.
Continuation paths avoid the foci $x = \pm1$ (prolate) and $x = \pm i$ (oblate), with steps at most 0.2 times the distance to the nearest focus.
Kinds two through four diverge at the foci, returning `DomainError` (`Pole`) there.
At $c = 0$, the first kind is 1 for $n = 0$ and zero otherwise; kinds two through four give `DomainError` (`Pole`).

The Wronskian of the first and second kinds is

$$
R_{mn}^{(1)}\,\frac{dR_{mn}^{(2)}}{dx} - \frac{dR_{mn}^{(1)}}{dx}\,R_{mn}^{(2)} = \frac{1}{c\,(x^2 - s)}
$$

($1/(c(x^2-1))$ for prolate and $1/(c(x^2+1))$ for oblate functions).
The two kinds use separate Bessel series or Taylor continuation, so this relation checks both expansion and continuation.

### Checking the spherical limit and Wronskian

The next cell selects prolate or oblate, $m$, $n$, and $c$, computes characteristic values with successive halvings of $c$, and shows the difference from first-order perturbation above divided by $c^4$.
If this value approaches a constant, the difference is decreasing as $O(c^4)$.
At the selected $c$, the second table compares radial Wronskians at $x = 1.5$ for prolate or $x = 0.5$ for oblate functions with theoretical values.

```rust
//| id: special-spheroidal-sphere-limit
//| ref: special-spheroidal-sphere-limit
//| title: "Spherical limit of spheroidal characteristic values and radial Wronskians"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   kind: {"type":"select","label":"Spheroid","options":["Prolate","Oblate"],"value":"Prolate"}
//|   m: {"type":"integer","label":"Degree m","min":0,"max":4,"value":1}
//|   degree_offset: {"type":"integer","label":"n − m","min":0,"max":6,"value":1}
//|   c: {"type":"range","label":"Size parameter c","min":0.1,"max":8,"step":0.1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"oblate","label":"Oblate, m = 0, n = 0","description":"The first-order term reverses sign.","inputs":{"kind":"Oblate","m":0,"degree_offset":0}}
//|   - {"id":"prolate-ground","label":"Prolate, m = 0, n = 0","description":"The c⁴ coefficient matches the oblate case.","inputs":{"m":0,"degree_offset":0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"limit","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":7.653149562003568,"rtol":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"limit","path":"/rows/4/3","approx":{"value":-0.003887,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskian","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: oblate
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"limit","path":"/rows/4/3","approx":{"value":-0.014822,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskian","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: prolate-ground
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"limit","path":"/rows/4/3","approx":{"value":-0.014822,"rtol":2e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wronskian","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_special::{
    RadialKind, SpectralNormalization, SpectralOptions, SpheroidalKind, SpheroidalPlan,
};

let (spheroid, s, x) = match kind.as_str() {
    "Oblate" => (SpheroidalKind::Oblate, -1.0, 0.5),
    _ => (SpheroidalKind::Prolate, 1.0, 1.5),
};
let (m, n) = (m as u32, (m + degree_offset) as u32);
let (mf, nf) = (f64::from(m), f64::from(n));
// Expectation of x² in normalized Ferrers functions (first-order perturbation coefficient)
let moment =
    (2.0 * nf * (nf + 1.0) - 2.0 * mf * mf - 1.0) / ((2.0 * nf - 1.0) * (2.0 * nf + 3.0));
let prepare = |size: f64| {
    let normalization = SpectralNormalization::Standard;
    SpheroidalPlan::new(
        spheroid,
        m,
        n,
        size,
        normalization,
        SpectralOptions::default(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
// The plan for the selected c is also used for the Wronskian check
let plan = prepare(c)?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for step in 0..5 {
    let size = c * 0.5_f64.powi(step);
    let lambda = if step == 0 {
        plan.characteristic_value()
    } else {
        prepare(size)?.characteristic_value()
    }
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let first_order = nf * (nf + 1.0) + s * size * size * moment;
    let scaled_difference = (lambda - first_order) / size.powi(4);
    rows.push((size, lambda, first_order, scaled_difference));
}
// Radial values and derivatives in x at the selected c
let radial = |kind| {
    plan.radial(x, kind)
        .and_then(|r| Ok((r.try_value()?, r.try_derivative()?)))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let (r1, d1) = radial(RadialKind::First)?;
let (r2, d2) = radial(RadialKind::Second)?;
let wronskian = (r1 * d2 - d1 * r2).re;
let expected = 1.0 / (c * (x * x - s));
let fixed10 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "c [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Characteristic value λₘₙ", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "First-order perturbation", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Difference / c⁴", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "limit" })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Radial coordinate x [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "R⁽¹⁾", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "R⁽²⁾", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Wronskian", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Relative difference", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Coefficient count N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Normalization point η", "type": "number" }
]);
let relative = (wronskian - expected).abs() / expected.abs();
let terms = plan.diagnostics().terms() as u32;
let eta = plan.radial_normalization_point();
let row = (x, r1.re, r2.re, wronskian, relative, terms, eta);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "wronskian" })
);
```

For the default prolate case, $m = 1$, $n = 2$, and $c = 2$, the difference divided by $c^4$ approaches a constant, from $-0.00382$ at $c = 2$ to $-0.003887$ at $c = 0.125$.
The difference decreases proportionally to $c^4$, showing consistency between the first-order perturbation coefficient $3/7$ and the plan's characteristic values.
For oblate $m = n = 0$, the first-order term reverses sign and $\lambda_{00}$ moves toward negative values.
The $c^4$ coefficient is then $-0.01482$, matching $-0.01481$ for prolate $m = n = 0$, consistent with the $\gamma^4 = c^4$ term being independent of $s$.
At $c = 8$, first-order perturbation (33.4) differs substantially from the characteristic value (23.4), so the perturbation expansion is unusable, while the coefficient count remains 32.
Radial Wronskians agree with theoretical values $1/(2 \cdot 1.25) = 0.4$ at prolate $x = 1.5$ and $1/(2 \cdot 1.25) = 0.4$ at oblate $x = 0.5$ to relative differences of order $10^{-15}$.
Both points have product-expansion radius $|r| < 1.5$, so the second kind uses Taylor continuation from $x = 3$; this agreement also checks continuation.
Even for combinations such as $m = 1$ and $n = 7$, where second-kind values are large ($R^{(2)} \approx -88$), relative differences remain at $8 \times 10^{-15}$.
The normalization point $\eta$ is selected between 0.25 and 0.9 for each combination and appears in the final table column.

## Accuracy guarantees

Spheroidal wave functions have no uniform ULP specification across the full family.
Tolerances in `SpectralOptions` determine truncation acceptance criteria and do not promise output accuracy.
Residuals in `SpectralDiagnostics` and estimated errors returned by individual evaluations (errors in `SpecialFunctionResult`) are estimates describing the computation, not enclosing intervals.
For complex coordinates, omitted-coefficient contributions and continuation errors may grow.

Reference tests check differences from independently generated reference values within the following tolerances.
At exterior points (prolate $x > 1$, oblate $x > 0$), characteristic values agree with reference values within $10^{-12}(1 + |\lambda|)$, and first- and second-kind values and derivatives within $2 \times 10^{-9}(1 + |R|)$.
Against independent references at real and complex coordinates, angular and radial kinds one through four, including derivatives, agree within $2 \times 10^{-9}(1 + |\text{reference}|)$ for `f64` and $2 \times 10^{-5}(1 + |\text{reference}|)$ for `f32`.
At interior points (prolate $x = 1.1, 1.5$, oblate $x = 0, 0.5$), Wronskians are tested within relative $10^{-8}$.
Plans with `f32` also compute internally in `f64` and round to `f32` at the end.

Construct a plan to evaluate many points with the same $(m, n, c)$ or when estimated errors, derivatives, or termination statuses are needed.
Plans are immutable; repeated evaluations never rebuild their coefficients.
One-shot functions construct one internal plan with default `SpectralOptions` and normalization `Standard`, evaluate it once, and return an ordinary value.
Preparation and evaluation share one work limit (2,097,152 units), and the result is bitwise identical to evaluation of a plan with the same arguments.
One-shot functions have no form with options `*_with_options` or scoped API `*_scoped`; use the plan's `build` and `*_scoped` methods for those purposes.

Errors are as follows.
NaN or infinite $c$ gives `NonFinite`; $n < m$ or indices outside the truncation range give `InvalidInput` (`OutOfRange`).
For one-shot functions, this range is $n - m \ge 496$ (512 when $c = 0$).
Evaluation returns `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`) when $c^2$ or matrix entries exceed `f64`, when no radial normalization point can be resolved, or when expansion or continuation cannot resolve $x$.
A one-shot value exceeding the scalar range gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`); the plan's `characteristic_value` gives `NumericalInstability` (`LossOfSignificance`).

## Summary

Spheroidal characteristic values $\lambda_{mn}$ and angular functions come from the symmetric tridiagonal eigenproblem for normalized Ferrers expansion coefficients; truncation follows the same doubling and acceptance rules as Mathieu functions.
Larger $c$ needs more coefficients; inspect `SpectralDiagnostics::terms` and increase the maximum in `SpectralTerms` if `TruncationLimit` occurs.
Radial functions use product expansions of Ferrers and spherical Bessel functions; near foci, kinds two through four use Taylor continuation from $x = 3$.
Estimated errors are not enclosing intervals, so check important values with independent relations such as small-$c$ perturbation, angular normalization, and radial Wronskians.

See [Mathieu functions and spectral methods](../mathieu-and-spheroidal/) for detailed acceptance criteria and diagnostic interpretation.
For Laplace's equation on an ellipsoid with three unequal axes, see [Ellipsoidal harmonics and Lamé functions](../ellipsoidal-harmonics/) for Lamé classification and quadrature of exterior solutions.
[Legendre functions](../legendre-functions-and-spherical-harmonics/) explains the Ferrers functions themselves in the $c = 0$ limit.
