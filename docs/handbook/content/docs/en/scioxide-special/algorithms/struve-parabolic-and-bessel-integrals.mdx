Adding a right-hand side to the cylindrical Bessel equation gives solutions that are sums of Bessel and Struve functions.
When a circular piston of radius $a$ in a planar baffle radiates sound of wavenumber $k$, the real and imaginary parts of radiation impedance are proportional to $1 - 2J_1(2ka)/(2ka)$ and $2\mathbf{H}_1(2ka)/(2ka)$, with the Struve function $\mathbf{H}_1$ appearing in the imaginary part.
Closed forms for diffraction and heat-conduction solutions retain Bessel integrals such as $\int_0^x J_0(t)\,dt$.

This page covers the Struve function $\mathbf{H}_\nu$, modified Struve function $\mathbf{L}_\nu$, and integrals of order-zero Bessel and Struve functions.
For each family, it explains definitions and properties, how and why `scioxide-special` selects evaluation methods by argument region, accuracy guarantees, and APIs; the final two cells check values against elementary functions and Struve-based closed forms.
See [Cylindrical Bessel and Hankel functions](../airy-bessel-and-hankel/) for Bessel definitions and evaluation, and [Parabolic cylinder functions](../parabolic-cylinder-functions/) for solutions of Weber's equation with a quadratic potential.

## Struve and modified Struve functions

The Struve function $\mathbf{H}_\nu(x)$ is a particular solution of the inhomogeneous Bessel equation (DLMF 11.2.7),

$$
w'' + \frac{1}{x}w' + \left(1 - \frac{\nu^2}{x^2}\right)w = \frac{(x/2)^{\nu - 1}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\nu + 1/2)}
$$

defined by the following power series (DLMF 11.2.1).

$$
\mathbf{H}_\nu(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k (x/2)^{\nu + 2k + 1}}{\Gamma(k + 3/2)\,\Gamma(k + \nu + 3/2)}.
$$

The modified Struve function $\mathbf{L}_\nu(x) = -i e^{-i\nu\pi/2}\mathbf{H}_\nu(ix)$ removes the sign $(-1)^k$ from the same series (DLMF 11.2.2), making all terms have the same sign.
Terms for which $\Gamma(k + \nu + 3/2)$ has a pole are taken as zero.
$\nu$ may be any finite real number.

$x < 0$ is defined only for integer order $n$, using $\mathbf{H}_n(-x) = (-1)^{n+1}\mathbf{H}_n(x)$ and $\mathbf{L}_n(-x) = (-1)^{n+1}\mathbf{L}_n(x)$.
At $x = 0$, values are zero for $\nu > -1$ and $\nu = -3/2, -5/2, \ldots$, and $2/\pi$ for $\nu = -1$; all other orders have a pole.
Half-integer orders have elementary expressions (DLMF §11.4).
For example, $\mathbf{H}_{1/2}(x) = \sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\,(1 - \cos x)$ and $\mathbf{L}_{1/2}(x) = \sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\,(\cosh x - 1)$; for $\nu = -(n + 1/2)$, $\mathbf{H}_\nu(x) = (-1)^n J_{n+1/2}(x)$ and $\mathbf{L}_\nu(x) = I_{n+1/2}(x)$ (DLMF 11.4.4, 11.4.5).

At large $x$, Struve functions split into a Bessel function plus a slowly varying function (DLMF 11.6.1, 11.6.2).

$$
\mathbf{H}_\nu(x) = Y_\nu(x) + \mathbf{K}_\nu(x), \qquad
\mathbf{L}_\nu(x) = I_\nu(x) + \mathbf{M}_\nu(x),
$$

$$
\mathbf{K}_\nu(x) \sim \frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{\Gamma(k + 1/2)\,(x/2)^{\nu - 2k - 1}}{\Gamma(\nu + 1/2 - k)}.
$$

$\mathbf{M}_\nu$ uses the same sum with term signs $(-1)^{k+1}$.
$\mathbf{H}_\nu$ superposes $\mathbf{K}_\nu \approx (x/2)^{\nu-1}/(\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\nu + 1/2))$ on the oscillation of $Y_\nu$, while $\mathbf{L}_\nu$, like $I_\nu$, grows as $e^x/\sqrt{2\pi x}$.

### Choosing series or asymptotic expansions

`struve` and `modstruve` try an asymptotic expansion for $x \ge 20$, summing the defining series if it is not accepted.
Orders $\nu = -(n + 1/2)$ use the Bessel representations above.

The asymptotic expansion sums at least $\nu - 1/2$ terms of $\mathbf{K}_\nu$, then truncates when terms begin growing or become negligible relative to the sum.
For real $\nu$ and $x > 0$, the remainder of $\mathbf{K}_\nu$ after more than $\nu - 1/2$ terms has the sign of the first omitted term and is smaller in magnitude (DLMF §11.6(i)).
The implementation adds half that term to the sum, making truncation error at most half the omitted term.
It accepts when this half-term is at most $\varepsilon/2$ times the larger of the $Y_\nu$ amplitude $\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}$ and $\lvert\mathbf{K}_\nu\rvert$ ($\varepsilon$ is machine epsilon for `f64`), then adds $Y_\nu$.
It also rejects when $\lvert Y_\nu\rvert + \lvert\mathbf{K}_\nu\rvert$ exceeds 64 times the larger of the sum and amplitude (cancellation occurring when $\nu$ exceeds $x$), reverting to the series.
The smallest term is about $e^{-x}$ times the leading term, so acceptance for general orders starts only when $x$ exceeds about 26–35 (earlier at larger orders); below that, the series is used even for $x \ge 20$.
For half-integer orders $\nu = 1/2, 3/2, \ldots$, the sum for $\mathbf{K}_\nu$ terminates ($\Gamma(\nu + 1/2 - k)$ reaches a pole), leaving no truncation error and allowing acceptance from $x = 20$.

DLMF gives no remainder bound for the $\mathbf{L}_\nu$ expansion.
Therefore, estimate truncation error as $x$ times the first omitted term and add an estimate accounting for the DLMF formula not distinguishing $\mathbf{L}_\nu - I_\nu$ from $\mathbf{L}_\nu - I_{-\nu}$ (their difference $I_\nu - I_{-\nu}$ is about $e^{-2x}$ times $I_\nu$); accept only when the sum is at most $\varepsilon\lvert I_\nu\rvert/16$.
Since $\mathbf{M}_\nu$ is smaller than $I_\nu$ by about $e^{-x}$, the $\mathbf{L}_\nu$ expansion is accepted at smaller $x$ than $\mathbf{H}_\nu$.

Series are summed in double-word arithmetic (about 106 bits represented by two `f64` values).
The $\mathbf{H}_\nu$ series alternates; as $x$ grows, term magnitudes reach the scale of $\mathbf{L}_\nu \sim e^x/\sqrt{2\pi x}$, while the sum is orders of magnitude smaller.
For a sum of order $\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}$, cancellation loses a factor of about $e^x/2$ in precision: 28 bits at $x = 20$ and 50 at $x = 35$, where asymptotic acceptance begins, leaving the 53 bits of `f64` even after subtraction from the 106 double-word bits.
Asymptotic expansions are first tried at $x \ge 20$ because cancellation between $Y_\nu$ and $\mathbf{K}_\nu$, or $I_\nu - I_{-\nu}$, cannot be neglected at smaller $x$.
The coefficient $(x/2)^{\nu+1}/\Gamma(\nu + 3/2)$ is stored logarithmically if it would overflow, with a single final projection to an ordinary value.
Once term ratios fall below 1, the series remainder is bounded by a geometric series.

The forms with options `struve_with_options` and `modstruve_with_options` return derivatives $\mathbf{H}_\nu'(x)$ and $\mathbf{L}_\nu'(x)$ as well as values and estimated errors (omitting derivatives at $x = 0$ when $-1 < \nu < 0$, where they are infinite).
Value error estimates are derived from truncation and rounding; paths using Bessel functions (asymptotic expansions and $\nu = -(n + 1/2)$) add the error range measured in Bessel reference tests.
Derivative error estimates are heuristic.

### Struve accuracy

The `f64` target checked with independent references is 64 ULP over $\nu \in [-5, 10]$ and $x \in [10^{-3}, 100]$ (ULP is floating-point spacing at the exact result).
Near zeros of $\mathbf{H}_\nu$, relative error is meaningless, so the target is absolute error $4\varepsilon\max(1, \lvert\mathbf{H}_\nu(x)\rvert)$.
Outside this range and for `f32`, there is no uniform ULP specification; tests check reference differences and estimated errors against family-specific limits.
In those ranges, inspect the estimated error in the form with options.

## Bessel and Struve integrals

The following eleven integrals of order-zero Bessel and Struve functions are provided as functions.
Integrals involving $Y_0$ and $K_0$, whose integrands have an origin singularity like $\ln t$, or weights $1/t$ are made finite by removing a constant or using a tail form.

| Function | Value | Domain | Properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bessel_j0_integral` | $\int_0^x J_0(t)\,dt$ | All $x$ | Odd; tends to 1 as $x \to \infty$ |
| `bessel_j0_complement_integral` | $\int_0^x \frac{1 - J_0(t)}{t}\,dt$ | All $x$ | Even; grows like $\ln(x/2) + \gamma$ as $x \to \infty$ |
| `bessel_y0_integral` | $\int_0^x Y_0(t)\,dt$ | $x \ge 0$ | Tends to zero as $x \to \infty$ |
| `bessel_y0_tail_integral` | $\int_x^\infty \frac{Y_0(t)}{t}\,dt$ | $x > 0$ | Diverges as $x \to 0^+$ |
| `bessel_i0_integral` | $\int_0^x I_0(t)\,dt$ | All $x$ | Odd; grows like $e^{\lvert x\rvert}/\sqrt{2\pi\lvert x\rvert}$ |
| `bessel_i0_remainder_integral` | $\int_0^x \frac{I_0(t) - 1}{t}\,dt$ | All $x$ | Even; grows like $e^{\lvert x\rvert}/(\lvert x\rvert\sqrt{2\pi\lvert x\rvert})$ |
| `bessel_k0_integral` | $\int_0^x K_0(t)\,dt$ | $x \ge 0$ | Tends to $\pi/2$ as $x \to \infty$ |
| `bessel_k0_tail_integral` | $\int_x^\infty \frac{K_0(t)}{t}\,dt$ | $x > 0$ | Decays like $e^{-x}/x^{3/2}$ |
| `struve_h0_integral` | $\int_0^x \mathbf{H}_0(t)\,dt$ | All $x$ | Even |
| `struve_h0_tail_integral` | $\int_x^\infty \frac{\mathbf{H}_0(t)}{t}\,dt$ | All $x$ | $\pi/2$ at $x = 0$; $\int_{-x}^\infty = \pi - \int_x^\infty$ |
| `struve_l0_integral` | $\int_0^x \mathbf{L}_0(t)\,dt$ | All $x$ | Even; grows like $e^{\lvert x\rvert}/\sqrt{2\pi\lvert x\rvert}$ |

$\gamma$ is Euler's constant.
In `bessel_j0_complement_integral`, “complement” refers to integrand $1 - J_0$; in `bessel_i0_remainder_integral`, “remainder” refers to $I_0 - 1$; neither is a tail $\int_x^\infty$.
For the tail $\int_x^\infty J_0(t)\,dt$, use $1 - $ `bessel_j0_integral(x)`, but at large $x$ this subtracts a number close to 1 and increases relative error.

### Evaluating the integrals

For $\lvert x\rvert \le 12$, sum the termwise integral of the integrand's power series.
The series for $Y_0$ and $K_0$ contain $\ln(x/2)$; integration constants follow from finite parts of Mellin transforms (DLMF §10.22).
For $12 < \lvert x\rvert < 32$, evaluate precomputed 64-term Chebyshev expansions on $[12, 16]$, $[16, 24]$, and $[24, 32]$ by Clenshaw's method.
For $\lvert x\rvert \ge 32$, integrals involving $J_0$ and $Y_0$ subtract asymptotic tail expansions $\int_x^\infty$ from their limits (Hankel-type expansions separating oscillations into $\cos x$ and $\sin x$ phases); Struve integrals add the termwise integrated asymptotic expansion of $\mathbf{H}_0 - Y_0$.
The integral of $K_0$ subtracts its asymptotic tail from $\pi/2$; tail integrals use that expansion directly.
Integrals involving $I_0$ and $\mathbf{L}_0$ sum asymptotic expansions of the integrals themselves, proportional to $e^x$.

Series for $J_0$, $Y_0$, and $\mathbf{H}_0$ alternate; at $x = 12$, the sum of absolute terms is of order $\int_0^{12} I_0(t)\,dt \approx 2.0 \times 10^4$.
Values are of order 1 and sums use `f64`, so cancellation loses about four decimal digits.
This explains the switch to Chebyshev expansions at $x = 12$; just below the boundary, errors exceed `f64` rounding (checked in the cell below).
Integrals involving $I_0$ and $\mathbf{L}_0$ have same-sign terms without cancellation and use series throughout $\lvert x\rvert < 32$.

Negative $x$ is mapped to positive $x$ by parity.
Every form with options returns the derivative in $x$: the integrand for $\int_0^x$ forms, and the negated integrand for tails $\int_x^\infty$.
At $x = 0$, `bessel_y0_integral` and `bessel_k0_integral` have infinite integrands, so derivatives are omitted.
Value error estimates are derived from truncation and rounding; derivative error estimates are heuristic.

### Integral accuracy

Bessel and Struve integrals have no uniform ULP specification.
Tests compare values and estimated errors against independent references in `f32` and `f64`, controlling absolute error near zeros on the scale $\varepsilon\max(1, \lvert\text{reference}\rvert)$.
Read individual accuracy from estimated errors in the forms with options (the error in `ValueWithError`, not an enclosing interval).

[Normalized J and weighted integrals](oxref:special-bessel-lambda-weighted) covers weighted integrals $\int_0^1 t^\mu J_\nu(2at)\,dt$ (`bessel_weighted_integral`) and Jahnke–Emde functions $\Lambda_\nu(x)$ (`bessel_lambda`).

## API

Every function has a standard form `f(..)` returning only the value, a form with options `f_with_options(.., options)` returning `SpecialFunctionResult`, and a scoped API `f_scoped(.., options, scope)` accepting a solve scope.
Options contain only a work limit (`Budget`, not an accuracy request).
Defaults are 262144 units for `StruveOptions` and 65536 for `BesselIntegralOptions`.
Arguments are real (`f32`, `f64`).

Classify errors using `kind()` from `SciOxideError`.
Non-finite inputs give `NonFinite`.
`DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`) occurs for negative $x$ at noninteger orders (Struve functions), and negative $x$ (`bessel_y0_integral`, `bessel_k0_integral`, `bessel_y0_tail_integral`, `bessel_k0_tail_integral`).
Origin poles of Struve functions ($\nu < -1$ with $\nu \ne -3/2, -5/2, \ldots$) and $x = 0$ for the two tail integrals return `DomainError` (`Pole`).
When values exceed `f64`, evaluation returns `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`); Struve functions in `f32` give a type-conversion error when values exceed `f32`.
Stopping at the work limit without a value estimate returns `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).
Successful forms with options have termination status (`SpecialFunctionTermination`) `Converged`, or `Exact` when returning an exact value without computation (such as an origin value).

## Switching from series to asymptotic expansions

The next cell evaluates $\mathbf{H}_\nu$ and $\mathbf{L}_\nu$ at half-integer order $\nu = 1/2, 3/2$ from $x = 1$ to $60$ and compares with elementary closed forms.
The closed forms below follow from DLMF §11.4 half-integer expressions and recurrence (DLMF 11.4.23), providing references computed with `f64` standard functions.

$$
\mathbf{H}_{3/2}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\left(\frac{1 - \cos x}{x} - \sin x\right) + \sqrt{\frac{x}{2\pi}}, \qquad
\mathbf{L}_{3/2}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\left(\sinh x - \frac{\cosh x - 1}{x}\right) - \sqrt{\frac{x}{2\pi}}.
$$

Measure error as $\lvert\Delta\rvert / (\varepsilon\max(1, \lvert\text{reference}\rvert))$ so that it remains meaningful near zeros.
Since $\mathbf{L}_\nu$ exceeds 1, this equals relative error divided by $\varepsilon$.
For half-integer orders, $\mathbf{K}_\nu$ has a finite sum, so $x < 20$ uses the series and $x \ge 20$ the asymptotic expansion.
The table splits the domain into two intervals and shows maximum error, estimated error, and work (units counted against the work limit) from the form with options.

```rust
//| id: special-struve-series-asymptotic
//| ref: special-struve-series-asymptotic
//| title: "Compare Struve series and asymptotic expansions with closed forms"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   function: {"type":"select","label":"Function","options":["H_ν（Struve）","L_ν (modified Struve)"],"value":"H_ν（Struve）"}
//|   order: {"type":"select","label":"Order ν","options":["1/2","3/2"],"value":"3/2"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"modified","label":"L_{3/2}","description":"Modified Struve function with same-sign terms. Even values exceeding 10²⁴ at x = 60 have relative error of only a few ε.","inputs":{"function":"L_ν (modified Struve)"}}
//|   - {"id":"half","label":"H_{1/2}","description":"Zeros at x = 2πk. The absolute-error scale avoids increased errors near zeros.","inputs":{"order":"1/2"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":4,"max":64}}
//|   - case: modified
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":4}}
//|   - case: half
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/0/1","range":{"max":4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"regions","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":4}}
use scioxide_special::{StruveOptions, modstruve_with_options, struve_with_options};

let modified = function != "H_ν（Struve）";
let nu = if order == "1/2" { 0.5 } else { 1.5 };
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let eps = f64::EPSILON;
let options = StruveOptions::default();
// Half-integer closed forms (references from f64 standard functions).
let reference = |x: f64| {
    let a = (2.0 / (pi * x)).sqrt();
    let b = (x / (2.0 * pi)).sqrt();
    match (modified, order == "1/2") {
        (false, true) => a * (1.0 - x.cos()),
        (false, false) => a * ((1.0 - x.cos()) / x - x.sin()) + b,
        (true, true) => a * (x.cosh() - 1.0),
        (true, false) => a * (x.sinh() - (x.cosh() - 1.0) / x) - b,
    }
};
let mut points = Vec::new();
// [Maximum error, maximum estimated error, maximum work] by interval.
let mut summary = [[0.0_f64; 3]; 2];
for index in 0..=236 {
    let x = 1.0 + 0.25 * f64::from(index);
    let result = if modified {
        modstruve_with_options(nu, x, options)
    } else {
        struve_with_options(nu, x, options)
    }
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let value = result.value().ok_or("No ordinary value")?;
    let exact = reference(x);
    let scale = eps * exact.abs().max(1.0);
    let error = (value.value() - exact).abs() / scale;
    points.push([x, error]);
    let row = &mut summary[usize::from(x >= 20.0)];
    row[0] = row[0].max(error);
    row[1] = row[1].max(value.absolute_error() / scale);
    row[2] = row[2].max(result.counts().iterations() as f64);
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "|Δ| / (ε max(1, |reference|))", "points": points })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Argument x [-]",
        "yLabel": "Error [ε units]",
        "xMin": 1.0,
        "xMax": 60.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": 20.0, "label": "Try asymptotic expansion at x = 20" }]
    })
);
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Interval" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Maximum error [ε units]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Maximum estimated error [ε units]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Maximum work [units]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let rows: Vec<(&str, f64, f64, f64)> = vec![
    ("1 ≤ x < 20", summary[0][0], summary[0][1], summary[0][2]),
    ("20 ≤ x ≤ 60", summary[1][0], summary[1][1], summary[1][2]),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "regions" })
);
```

For the default $\mathbf{H}_{3/2}$, maximum errors are 1.2 on $x < 20$ and 1.8 $\varepsilon$ units on $x \ge 20$, with no jump at $x = 20$.
Series and asymptotic expansions return equally accurate values on both sides of the switch.
Estimated errors of 6–13 $\varepsilon$ units exceed actual errors, conservatively estimating rounding and truncation.
Maximum work is 41 units in the series interval and 209 in the asymptotic interval, which evaluates Bessel functions including $Y_{\nu+1}$ for derivatives.
For general orders (such as $\nu = 0.3$), asymptotic expansions are accepted only above $x$ of 30, so series term counts and work grow between $x = 20$ and 30.
Maximum errors are at most 2 $\varepsilon$ units for both $\mathbf{L}_{3/2}$ and $\mathbf{H}_{1/2}$.
If errors exceed several tens of $\varepsilon$ at some points, suspect reference cancellation (small $x$ in $\mathbf{H}_{3/2}$) or evaluation error, and compare with estimated errors.

## Checking integrals with Struve functions

$\int_0^x J_0(t)\,dt$ and $\int_0^x Y_0(t)\,dt$ have closed forms in Bessel and Struve functions (using $\nu = 0$ and $\mathbf{H}_{-1} = 2/\pi - \mathbf{H}_1$ in DLMF 10.22.2).

$$
\int_0^x \mathscr{C}_0(t)\,dt = x\,\mathscr{C}_0(x) + \frac{\pi x}{2}\left(\mathscr{C}_1(x)\,\mathbf{H}_0(x) - \mathscr{C}_0(x)\,\mathbf{H}_1(x)\right), \qquad \mathscr{C} = J, Y.
$$

The next cell compares `bessel_j0_integral` and `bessel_y0_integral` with these formulas.
The right sides use `bessel_j`, `bessel_y`, and `struve`, separate from the integral implementations.
Evaluation points cover all three method intervals (series $x \le 12$, Chebyshev expansion $12 < x < 32$, and asymptotic expansion $x \ge 32$), including both sides of boundaries.
The table's “Difference from derivative” is the difference between the derivative returned by the form with options and integrand $\mathscr{C}_0(x)$.

```rust

//| crates: [scioxide-special]

//|   - {"id":"y0","label":"Y₀","description":"Integral of Y₀ with an origin logarithmic singularity. Approaches zero as x → ∞.","inputs":{"integrand":"Y₀"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default

//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.4896805066460451,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/4/4","range":{"min":100,"max":10000}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/4/5","range":{"min":10000}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/5/4","range":{"max":16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/8/4","range":{"max":16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/10/4","range":{"max":16}}
//|   - case: y0
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-0.5617954559146402,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/4/5","range":{"min":10000}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/5/4","range":{"max":16}}
//|       - {"artifact":"identity","path":"/rows/10/4","range":{"max":16}}
use scioxide_special::{
    BesselIntegralOptions, bessel_j, bessel_j0_integral_with_options, bessel_y,
    bessel_y0_integral_with_options, struve,
};

let first_kind = integrand == "J₀";
let options = BesselIntegralOptions::default();
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
let eps = f64::EPSILON;
let cylinder = |order: f64, x: f64| {
    if first_kind {
        bessel_j(order, x)
    } else {
        bessel_y(order, x)
    }
};
let points = [
    0.5_f64, 3.0, 8.0, 11.5, 11.9, 12.1, 20.0, 31.9, 32.1, 60.0, 300.0,
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for &x in &points {
    let result = if first_kind {
        bessel_j0_integral_with_options(x, options)
    } else {
        bessel_y0_integral_with_options(x, options)
    }
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let value = result.value().ok_or("No ordinary value")?;
    let c0 = cylinder(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let c1 = cylinder(1.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let h0 = struve(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let h1 = struve(1.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Closed form from DLMF 10.22.2 (ν = 0).
    let closed = x * c0 + 0.5 * pi * x * (c1 * h0 - c0 * h1);
    let scale = eps * closed.abs().max(1.0);
    let slope = result.derivative().ok_or("Missing derivative")?;
    let method = if x <= 12.0 {
        "Series"
    } else if x < 32.0 {
        "Chebyshev expansion"
    } else {
        "Asymptotic expansion"
    };
    rows.push((
        x,
        method,
        *value.value(),
        closed,
        (value.value() - closed).abs() / scale,
        value.absolute_error() / scale,
        (slope.value() - c0).abs() / (eps * c0.abs().max(1.0)),
    ));
}
let sci10 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 10 });
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "x [-]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Method" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Integral [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci10 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Difference [ε units]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Estimated error [ε units]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Difference from derivative [ε units]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "identity" })
);
```

Differences use the scale $\varepsilon\max(1, \lvert\text{closed form}\rvert)$; the closed forms themselves contain rounding from four function values, so differences of a few $\varepsilon$ count as agreement.
Differences for $J_0$ increase to 58 at $x = 8$ and 1743 $\varepsilon$ units at $x = 11.9$, returning to 0.5 at $x = 12.1$ above the boundary.
$Y_0$ also reaches 2249 $\varepsilon$ units at $x = 11.9$.
This is the series cancellation discussed above.
At the same points, estimated errors in the forms with options are $5.8 \times 10^5$ $\varepsilon$ units, larger still than actual differences and conservatively reporting the degradation.
The absolute differences are about $4 \times 10^{-13}$, harmless for many uses, but check estimated errors when `f64` rounding-level accuracy is needed.
Within the Chebyshev and asymptotic intervals, differences are at most 10 $\varepsilon$ units.

## Summary

Struve functions $\mathbf{H}_\nu$ and $\mathbf{L}_\nu$ use double-word series or a Bessel function plus asymptotic expansion when accepted for $x \ge 20$.
For general orders, asymptotic acceptance starts above $x$ around 30, and for half-integer orders from $x = 20$.
For `f64`, $\mathbf{H}_\nu$ and $\mathbf{L}_\nu$ target 64 ULP on $\nu \in [-5, 10]$ and $x \in [10^{-3}, 100]$; outside those ranges and for `f32`, inspect the estimated error in the form with options.
Order-zero Bessel and Struve integrals switch among series, Chebyshev, and asymptotic expansions at $x = 12$ and $32$; just below $x = 12$, cancellation increases errors to about $10^3\varepsilon$.
`complement` and `remainder` integrate an integrand with a constant removed rather than a tail; use `_tail_integral` functions or differences from limiting values when a tail is needed.
See [Coulomb wave functions and hydrogenic radial states](../coulomb-and-hydrogenic/) for scattering with a long-range Coulomb potential, [Parabolic cylinder functions](../parabolic-cylinder-functions/) for quadratic-potential solutions, and [Kelvin, Struve, and parabolic cylinder functions](../../api/kelvin-struve-parabolic/) for individual specifications.
