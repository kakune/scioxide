Lattice heat capacity of solids and electrical resistivity of metals involve definite integrals over $[0, x]$ weighted by the Bose distribution $1/(e^t - 1)$; relativistic electron radiation spectra involve integrals of modified Bessel functions.
The integrands are smooth, but integral values vary as powers or exponentials at the argument extremes, so one formula across the whole domain causes cancellation or values outside the range.
scioxide-special evaluates these integrals by selecting Chebyshev expansions, tail subtraction from complete integrals, or scaled asymptotic expansions according to the argument region.
This page summarizes definitions and conventions, evaluation, accuracy guarantees, and APIs for Debye functions, transport integrals, and synchrotron functions, checking values with defining integrals and identities.
[Fermi–Dirac integrals](../fermi-dirac-integrals/) covers integrals weighted by the Fermi distribution $1/(e^{t-x} + 1)$.

## Debye functions

### Definition and properties

For integer $n = 1, \dots, 6$ and $x \ge 0$, scioxide-special uses the following normalized Debye function.

$$
D_n(x) = \frac{n}{x^n} \int_0^x \frac{t^n}{e^t - 1}\,dt
$$

The prefactor $n/x^n$ gives $D_n(0) = 1$, and $D_n$ decreases monotonically in $x$.
Some references call the unnormalized integral $\int_0^x t^n/(e^t - 1)\,dt$ the Debye function; multiply by $x^n/n$ when comparing with those values.

Near the origin, termwise integration of the Bernoulli expansion of $t/(e^t - 1)$ gives (Abramowitz–Stegun §27.1):

$$
D_n(x) = 1 - \frac{n x}{2(n+1)} + n \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{B_{2k}\, x^{2k}}{(2k+n)\,(2k)!}, \qquad \lvert x \rvert < 2\pi
$$


$B_{2k}$ are Bernoulli numbers; apart from the linear term, this is even in $x$.
As $x \to \infty$, the complete integral $\int_0^\infty t^n/(e^t - 1)\,dt = n!\,\zeta(n+1)$ with upper limit $\infty$ dominates, giving

$$
D_n(x) = \frac{n \cdot n!\,\zeta(n+1)}{x^n} \bigl(1 - R_n(x)\bigr), \qquad
R_n(x) = \frac{1}{n!\,\zeta(n+1)} \int_x^\infty \frac{t^n}{e^t - 1}\,dt
$$


$\zeta$ is the Riemann zeta function, and the relative tail $R_n(x)$ approaches zero at rate $x^n e^{-x}$.
In the three-dimensional Debye solid model, set $x = \Theta_D/T$ ($\Theta_D$ is the Debye temperature); lattice internal energy is $3Nk_BT\,D_3(x)$ ([Heat capacity in the Debye model](../../examples/debye-heat-capacity/)).

### Evaluating Debye functions

For $0 < x \le 4$, evaluate the even part $D_n(x) + n x/(2(n+1))$ with a Chebyshev expansion in $x^2/8 - 1 \in (-1, 1]$, then subtract the linear term.
A Chebyshev expansion is a polynomial approximation with nearly uniform error across the interval; its fixed coefficient count makes evaluation work fixed as well.
The Bernoulli series above converges with ratio only $(4/2\pi)^2 \approx 0.41$ at $x = 4$ and needs too many terms, so it is not used.

For $x > 4$, subtract the tail from the complete integral.
Termwise integration of $1/(e^t - 1) = \sum_{k \ge 1} e^{-kt}$ gives the tail

$$
\int_x^\infty \frac{t^n}{e^t - 1}\,dt = \sum_{k=1}^\infty e^{-kx} \sum_{m=0}^n \frac{n!}{m!}\,\frac{x^m}{k^{\,n-m+1}}
$$

with the sum over $k$ converging at ratio $e^{-x} < e^{-4} \approx 0.018$.
The implementation assembles each term logarithmically as a relative value divided by $n \cdot n!\,\zeta(n+1)$, without forming $x^n$ itself.
Thus, even at large $x$ where $D_n(x)$ falls below the ordinary range, the form with options retains its value in logarithmic and scaled forms (`ScaledValue`, a significand paired with a power-of-two exponent).
Tail sums need fewer terms as $x$ grows: 8–9 at $x = 4$ and 1–2 at $x = 20$; truncation occurs when the geometric sum of remaining terms falls below relative $\varepsilon$ (double-precision machine epsilon).

Negative $x$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`); NaN and infinity give `NonFinite`.
The limiting value zero as $x \to \infty$ is not assigned to infinite inputs.

## Transport integrals

### Definition and relation to Debye functions

For $n = 2, 3, 4, 5$ and $x \ge 0$, the transport integral is

$$
J_n(x) = \int_0^x \frac{t^n e^t}{(e^t - 1)^2}\,dt
$$


The integrand behaves as $t^{n-2}$ at the origin, so for small $x$, $J_n(x) \approx x^{n-1}/(n-1)$.
As $x \to \infty$, it approaches $J_n(\infty) = n!\,\zeta(n)$ ($J_2(\infty) = \pi^2/3$, $J_4(\infty) = 4\pi^4/15$).

Integration by parts using $e^t/(e^t - 1)^2 = -\tfrac{d}{dt}\,(e^t - 1)^{-1}$ gives the relation to Debye functions:

$$
J_n(x) = \frac{n}{n-1}\,x^{n-1} D_{n-1}(x) - \frac{x^n}{e^x - 1}
$$


This gives two expressions for constant-volume heat capacity in the Debye model: $C_V/(3Nk_B) = 3J_4(x)/x^3 = 4D_3(x) - 3x/(e^x - 1)$.
The Bloch–Grüneisen formula $\rho(T) \propto (T/\Theta_R)^5 J_5(\Theta_R/T)$ for metallic resistivity also uses this function.

### Evaluating transport integrals

For $0 < x \le 4$, evaluate $J_n(x)/x^{n-1}$ with a Chebyshev expansion in $x^2/8 - 1$, then multiply by $x$ $n - 1$ times.
Avoiding division or separately rounded $x^{n-1}$ preserves values even at small $x$ near the subnormal boundary.
For $x > 4$, as with Debye functions, subtract the exponentially convergent tail $\sum_k x^n e^{-kx}\,(\text{polynomial in }1/(kx))$ from limit $n!\,\zeta(n)$, stopping when the remaining tail falls below $\varepsilon$ times the limit.
The default work limit 128 suffices in both regions.
Negative $x$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`); non-finite inputs give `NonFinite`.

## Synchrotron functions

### Definition of synchrotron functions

For radiation from an ultrarelativistic electron moving in a circle in a magnetic field, power per frequency is a function of $x = \omega/\omega_c$, frequency divided by the critical frequency $\omega_c$ (Rybicki & Lightman 1979, Chapter 6).

$$
F(x) = x \int_x^\infty K_{5/3}(t)\,dt, \qquad G(x) = x\,K_{2/3}(x), \qquad x \ge 0
$$

$K_\nu$ is the modified Bessel function of the second kind.
$F$ gives the total radiation-power spectrum, and $G$ the difference between two polarization components.
For small $x$, $F(x) \approx \tfrac{3}{2}\,2^{2/3}\,\Gamma(5/3)\,x^{1/3} \approx 2.149\,x^{1/3}$ and $G(x) \approx 2^{-1/3}\,\Gamma(2/3)\,x^{1/3} \approx 1.075\,x^{1/3}$.
For large $x$, both approach $\sqrt{\pi x/2}\,e^{-x}$.
$F(0) = G(0) = 0$; in `f64`, ordinary values underflow once $x$ exceeds about 745.

### Evaluation and scaled forms

For $0 < x \le 1$, construct fractional powers from the cube root $z = x^{1/3}$ through products such as $x z^2$.
Evaluate the two functions $A$ and $B$ of $x^2$ (with coefficients specific to each synchrotron function) with Chebyshev expansions in $x^2/8 - 1$, then assemble $F(x) = x^{1/3} A - x^{11/3} B - (\pi/\sqrt{3})\,x$ and $G(x) = x^{1/3} A - x^{5/3} B$.
Fractional powers are not formed with `powf`, avoiding its rounding.
For $1 < x \le 4$, evaluate $e^x F(x)$ and $e^x G(x)$ with Chebyshev expansions.
For $x > 4$, factor out $\sqrt{\pi x/2}$ and evaluate the remainder with a Chebyshev expansion in $1/(x + c)$.
Every region has a fixed coefficient count and constant work, so no scoped API is provided.

Scaled forms `synchrotron_1_scaled` and `synchrotron_2_scaled` return $e^x F(x)$ and $e^x G(x)$; they grow like $\sqrt{\pi x/2}$ at large $x$ and do not underflow.
For $x > 1$, standard forms multiply the scaled value by $e^{-x/2}$ twice, avoiding premature rounding of $e^{-x}$ to zero.
The logarithmic form with options is formed from $\ln(e^x F) - x$, surviving after ordinary values underflow.
Negative $x$ gives `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`); non-finite inputs give `NonFinite`.

## Accuracy guarantees

These families have no uniform ULP target.
Against independent high-precision references, tests check the following tolerances and that estimated errors cover actual errors (including the reference rounding width).

| Function family | `f64` tolerance | `f32` tolerance | Additional checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Debye functions | Sum of relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ and absolute $5 \times 10^{-13}$ | Sum of relative $2 \times 10^{-4}$ and absolute $5 \times 10^{-6}$ | Estimated errors of logarithmic and scaled forms; subnormal results within 8 ULP (16 ULP for `f32`) |
| Transport integrals, synchrotron functions | Larger of relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ and absolute $5 \times 10^{-13}$ | Larger of relative $2 \times 10^{-4}$ and absolute $5 \times 10^{-6}$ | Logarithmic forms, subnormal results within 8 ULP (16 ULP for `f32`), agreement of scaled and standard forms |

Test tolerances are specification limits; typical errors are much smaller, as measured in the cell below.
Options-form estimates are derived from truncation and rounding analysis (`ModelBased`), not intervals with guaranteed containment.
Users assess whether the estimate meets their requirements using methods such as `relative_error()`.

## API

All functions accept only real arguments and return `f32` or `f64` values.
Standard forms return only a value; forms with options `*_with_options(.., options)` return `SpecialFunctionResult` with estimated error, logarithmic form, scaled form, termination status, and method identifier.
Scoped APIs `*_scoped(.., options, scope)` evaluate within a parent solve, reporting work as nested counts ([Three calling forms](oxref:special-api-call-forms)).

| `debye_2` | `debye_2_with_options` | `debye_2_scoped` | $D_2(x)$ | `DebyeOptions` |
| `debye_3` | `debye_3_with_options` | `debye_3_scoped` | $D_3(x)$ | `DebyeOptions` |
| `debye_4` | `debye_4_with_options` | `debye_4_scoped` | $D_4(x)$ | `DebyeOptions` |
| `debye_5` | `debye_5_with_options` | `debye_5_scoped` | $D_5(x)$ | `DebyeOptions` |
| `debye_6` | `debye_6_with_options` | `debye_6_scoped` | $D_6(x)$ | `DebyeOptions` |
| `transport_2` | `transport_2_with_options` | `transport_2_scoped` | $J_2(x)$ | `TransportOptions` (default 128) |

| `synchrotron_2_scaled` | `synchrotron_2_scaled_with_options` | None | $e^x G(x)$ | `SynchrotronOptions` |

Option types `DebyeOptions`, `TransportOptions`, and `SynchrotronOptions` contain only a work limit `Budget`, not an accuracy request.
Limits count series terms, tail terms, or Chebyshev coefficients.
If insufficient, options forms return either a result with termination status `IterationLimit` or an error `ConvergenceFailure`; standard forms return `ConvergenceFailure` in either case.
Synchrotron functions use fixed coefficients and argument-independent work, so no scoped API is provided.
Only $n = 1, \dots, 6$ for Debye functions and $n = 2, \dots, 5$ for transport integrals are provided; other orders are not.
For other orders, apply scioxide-integrate quadrature to the defining integral or construct $J_n$ from $D_{n-1}$ using the integration-by-parts relation above.

## Checks

Both cells use composite Boole quadrature of defining integrals as independent references.
Composite Boole quadrature divides the interval into $m$ equal subintervals, with the count a multiple of 4, sums point values with weights $7, 32, 12, 32, 14, 32, 12, 32, 14, \dots, 32, 7$, and multiplies by $2/45$ times the step $h$.
This equals one Richardson extrapolation of composite Simpson rules with steps $h$ and $2h$, reducing error as $h^6$ for smooth integrands.
To reduce rounding in long sums, use Neumaier compensated summation, retaining low-order digits lost in each addition in a separate variable and restoring them at the end.
The helper below is placed in a definitions block shared by both cells.

```rust
//| id: special-thermal-boole
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Composite Boole quadrature and compensated summation"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
/// Independent reference for ∫ₐᵇ g(t) dt using composite Boole quadrature. m is the subinterval count, a multiple of 4.
/// Use Neumaier compensated summation and propagate failures of g unchanged.
pub(crate) fn boole(
    mut g: impl FnMut(f64) -> Result<f64, String>,
    a: f64,
    b: f64,
    m: usize,
) -> Result<f64, String> {
    let h = (b - a) / m as f64;
    let (mut sum, mut compensation) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for i in 0..=m {
        let weight = if i == 0 || i == m {
            7.0
        } else if i % 2 == 1 {
            32.0
        } else if i % 4 == 2 {
            12.0
        } else {
            14.0
        };
        let term = weight * g(a + i as f64 * h)?;
        let next = sum + term;
        compensation += if sum.abs() >= term.abs() {
            (sum - next) + term
        } else {
            (term - next) + sum
        };
        sum = next;
    }
    Ok((sum + compensation) * 2.0 * h / 45.0)
}
```

### Checking Debye functions and transport integrals

The next cell evaluates $D_1, \dots, D_6$ and $J_2, \dots, J_5$ at input $x$ and compares them with two independent methods.
Debye functions are compared with reference values from composite Boole quadrature of their defining integrals (20000 equal subintervals), alongside estimated relative errors and method identifiers from the forms with options.
Transport integrals are compared with the integration-by-parts relation $J_n = \tfrac{n}{n-1} x^{n-1} D_{n-1}(x) - x^n/(e^x - 1)$ using scioxide-special's `debye_*` values.
They use separate coefficient tables and tail sums, so agreement checks both implementations.


At the default $x = 2.5$, all six Debye functions use Chebyshev expansions (`debye_origin_expansion`), with relative differences from Boole references at most $6 \times 10^{-16}$.
Estimated relative errors range from $8 \times 10^{-15}$ to $2 \times 10^{-14}$, one to two decimal orders larger than actual differences.
Relative differences between transport integrals and values from $D_{n-1}$ are at most $1.1 \times 10^{-15}$.
At $x = 12$ (`tail`), the method switches to `debye_exponential_tail`; ratios to asymptotic forms range from 0.99995 for $D_1$ to 0.955 for $D_6$, moving away from 1 as $n$ grows.
The relative tail $R_n(x)$ has magnitude $x^n e^{-x}$, so convergence to the asymptotic form is slower at larger $n$.
For the same reason, $J_n(x)/n!\,\zeta(n)$ falls from 0.9997 for $n = 2$ to 0.980 for $n = 5$.
At $x = 50$ (`far`), ratios to the asymptotic forms agree with 1 within $5 \times 10^{-15}$.
A row whose relative difference greatly exceeds its estimated relative error suggests an implementation or reference error.

### Checking synchrotron functions

The next cell plots $F$ and $G$ on a logarithmic grid $10^{-3} \le x \le 30$, overlaying the small-$x$ power $2.149\,x^{1/3}$ and large-$x$ asymptotic form $\sqrt{\pi x/2}\,e^{-x}$ as dashed lines.
At input $x$, the table compares $G(x)$ with $x K_{2/3}(x)$ using scioxide-special's `bessel_k`, and $F(x)$ with quadrature of $x\int_x^{x+40} K_{5/3}(t)\,dt$.
The quadrature substitutes $t = x + c\,(e^s - 1)$ ($c = \min(x, 1)$), using composite Boole quadrature with fine spacing near $t = x$ and coarse spacing farther away, evaluating `bessel_k` 401 times.
Contributions beyond the upper limit $x + 40$ are negligible, of relative order $e^{-40} \approx 4 \times 10^{-18}$.
Scaled forms are compared with $e^x$ times standard forms.

## Summary

Debye functions are normalized by $n/x^n$ to give $D_n(0) = 1$, so multiply by $x^n/n$ when comparing references that use the unnormalized integral.
Debye functions and transport integrals cover $x \le 4$ with fixed-term Chebyshev expansions and evaluate $x > 4$ by subtracting tails from complete integrals $n!\,\zeta(\cdot)$; the integration-by-parts identity $J_n = \tfrac{n}{n-1}x^{n-1}D_{n-1} - x^n/(e^x - 1)$ checks them against each other.
Synchrotron functions use three Chebyshev intervals with constant work; where $x$ exceeds about 745 and ordinary values underflow, use scaled forms `synchrotron_1_scaled` and `synchrotron_2_scaled`.
There is no uniform ULP specification; comparison tests check tolerances of about relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ in `f64` and estimated-error coverage, so inspect options-form relative estimated errors when accuracy matters.
For physical applications, [Heat capacity in the Debye model](../../examples/debye-heat-capacity/) checks two heat-capacity expressions using $D_3$ and $J_4$; [Fermi–Dirac integrals](../fermi-dirac-integrals/) covers integrals weighted by the Fermi distribution.
