Sine, cosine, and Fresnel integrals are defined by integrals of oscillatory elementary functions such as $\sin t/t$, $\cos t/t$, and $\cos(\pi t^2/2)$.
$\operatorname{Si}$ and $\operatorname{Ci}$ appear in antenna theory and the Gibbs phenomenon of Fourier series; Fresnel integrals appear in wave-optical diffraction and transition curves (clothoids) for roads and railways.
The hyperbolic sine integral $\operatorname{Shi}$ and hyperbolic cosine integral $\operatorname{Chi}$ replace integrand factors $\sin$ and $\cos$ with $\sinh$ and $\cosh$, and can be expressed as sums and differences of exponential integrals $\operatorname{Ei}$ and $E_1$.
At large arguments, values approach limits while oscillating, so `scioxide-special` separates oscillations with auxiliary functions, using Chebyshev expansions near the origin and asymptotic expansions farther away.
This page explains definitions and properties, interval selection and evaluation, accuracy guarantees, and API selection; two cells examine the square-wave Gibbs phenomenon and straight-edge diffraction.
[Exponential and logarithmic integrals](../exponential-and-trigonometric-integrals/) covers evaluation of $\operatorname{Ei}$ and $E_1$ used by $\operatorname{Shi}$ and $\operatorname{Chi}$.

## Definition and properties

### Sine and cosine integrals

Define sine and cosine integrals as follows (DLMF §6.2).

$$
\operatorname{Si}(x) = \int_0^x \frac{\sin t}{t}\,dt, \qquad
\operatorname{Ci}(x) = \gamma + \ln x + \int_0^x \frac{\cos t - 1}{t}\,dt \quad (x > 0)
$$

$\gamma = 0.57721\ldots$ is Euler's constant.
$\operatorname{Si}$ is odd and entire and approaches $\pi/2$ as $x \to \infty$.
Its maximum is $\operatorname{Si}(\pi) = 1.85193\,70519\ldots$ at $x = \pi$, determining the Gibbs overshoot.
$\operatorname{Ci}$ diverges like $\gamma + \ln x$ at the origin and approaches zero with oscillations as $x \to \infty$.
Its positive zeros start at $0.61650\ldots$, $3.38418\ldots$, and $6.42704\ldots$, gradually approaching $k\pi$.

For large $x$, auxiliary functions $f$ and $g$ separate the oscillation (DLMF §6.2, §6.12).

$$
\operatorname{Si}(x) = \frac{\pi}{2} - f(x)\cos x - g(x)\sin x, \qquad
\operatorname{Ci}(x) = f(x)\sin x - g(x)\cos x
$$

Here, $f(x) \sim \frac{1}{x}\sum_k (-1)^k \frac{(2k)!}{x^{2k}}$ and $g(x) \sim \frac{1}{x^2}\sum_k (-1)^k \frac{(2k+1)!}{x^{2k}}$ decrease smoothly, and the series are asymptotic expansions.
Asymptotic expansions diverge, but truncation at the smallest term gives errors on the scale of that term.

### Hyperbolic sine and cosine integrals

For $x > 0$, the hyperbolic sine and cosine integrals are as follows (DLMF §6.2).

$$
\operatorname{Shi}(x) = \int_0^x \frac{\sinh t}{t}\,dt = \frac{\operatorname{Ei}(x) + E_1(x)}{2}, \qquad
\operatorname{Chi}(x) = \gamma + \ln x + \int_0^x \frac{\cosh t - 1}{t}\,dt = \frac{\operatorname{Ei}(x) - E_1(x)}{2}
$$

$\operatorname{Shi}$ is odd and entire; $\operatorname{Chi}$ has an origin logarithmic singularity; both grow like $e^{x}/(2x)$.

### Inverse tangent integral

The inverse tangent integral is $\operatorname{Ti}_2(x) = \int_0^x \arctan t / t\,dt$.
It is odd, and $\operatorname{Ti}_2(1)$ equals Catalan's constant $G = 0.91596\,55941\ldots$.
For $x > 0$, the inversion formula $\operatorname{Ti}_2(x) = \operatorname{Ti}_2(1/x) + \frac{\pi}{2}\ln x$ holds (Lewin 1981).

### Fresnel integrals

Define Fresnel integrals as follows (DLMF 7.2.7, 7.2.8).

$$
C(z) = \int_0^z \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi t^2}{2}\right)dt, \qquad S(z) = \int_0^z \sin\!\left(\frac{\pi t^2}{2}\right)dt
$$

Both are odd and entire and approach $1/2$ as $x \to +\infty$.
The curve traced by the point $(C(x), S(x))$ is the Cornu spiral (clothoid), whose curvature grows proportionally to arc length $x$.
For large $x$, auxiliary functions separate oscillation (DLMF §7.5).

$$
C(x) = \frac12 + f(x)\sin\frac{\pi x^2}{2} - g(x)\cos\frac{\pi x^2}{2}, \qquad
S(x) = \frac12 - f(x)\cos\frac{\pi x^2}{2} - g(x)\sin\frac{\pi x^2}{2}
$$

Since $f(x) \sim 1/(\pi x)$ and $g(x) \sim 1/(\pi^2 x^3)$, the spiral winds into the center $(1/2, 1/2)$ at radius about $1/(\pi x)$.
For complex $z$, the error-function relation $C(z) + iS(z) = \frac{1+i}{2}\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}(1-i)z\right)$ holds (DLMF §7.5).

## Interval selection and evaluation methods

Origin series converge rapidly for small $x$, but at large $x$, terms grow before cancelling and leave rounding error.
Asymptotic expansions need few terms for large $x$, but at small $x$, the smallest term is too large for accuracy.
Intermediate intervals use fixed-degree rational approximations $P(t)/Q(t)$ with precomputed coefficients chosen to reduce maximum relative error, or Chebyshev expansions $\sum_k c_k T_k(t)$.
Chebyshev expansion maps the interval to $t \in [-1, 1]$ and sums with Clenshaw's recurrence; work is proportional to the coefficient count and independent of the input.

### Si and Ci

For $|x| \le 4$, use Chebyshev expansions $T_{\mathrm{Si}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{Ci}}$ in $t = (x^2 - 8)/8$, forming values as follows.

$$
\operatorname{Si}(x) = x\left(\frac34 + T_{\mathrm{Si}}(t)\right), \qquad
\operatorname{Ci}(x) = \ln x - \frac12 + T_{\mathrm{Ci}}(t)
$$

The logarithmic singularity of $\operatorname{Ci}$ is kept outside the expansion as $\ln x$; the expansion approximates only a smooth function of $x^2$.
Near the two zeros where $|\operatorname{Ci}(x)| < 1/4$, resummation uses double-word arithmetic including low coefficient digits (about 106 bits as a sum of two binary64 values).
There, split the logarithm as $\ln x = \ln c + \ln(1 + (x - c)/c)$ using a nearby center $c = 3/4$ or $c = 3$ to reduce rounding error.

For $4 < x < 48$, approximate auxiliary functions $f$ and $g$ rationally in $1/x^2$ (different coefficients for $(4, 8)$ and $[8, 48)$); for $x \ge 48$, sum asymptotic expansions.
For $x \ge 48$, the smallest asymptotic term is below binary64 rounding, so stopping when a term falls below $\varepsilon/4$ suffices ($\varepsilon = 2^{-52}$ is binary64 machine epsilon).
Here, $\operatorname{Ci}$ is the difference of $f\sin x$ and $g\cos x$, so estimated absolute error is about a dozen times $\varepsilon/x$, with large relative errors near zeros.

### Shi and Chi

For $|x| \le 1$, sum origin series (adding $\gamma + \ln x$ for $\operatorname{Chi}$).
For $|x| > 1$, combine scaled kernels of $\operatorname{Ei}$ and $E_1$ while keeping exponential factors removed, as in $e^{-x}\operatorname{Shi}(x) = \frac12\left(e^{-x}\operatorname{Ei}(x) + e^{-2x}\,e^{x}E_1(x)\right)$.
[Evaluating E1 and Ei](oxref:special-expint-e1-ei) explains these kernels.
`shi_scaled` is $e^{-|x|}\operatorname{Shi}(x)$ and `chi_scaled` is $e^{-x}\operatorname{Chi}(x)$; both approach $1/(2x)$ for large $x$.
Ordinary $\operatorname{Shi}$ and $\operatorname{Chi}$ overflow beyond $|x| \approx 717$.

### Inverse tangent integral

`atanint` uses a Chebyshev expansion on $[0, 1]$ multiplied by $t$, mapping $|x| > 1$ to $1/|x|$ through inversion.
By odd symmetry, negative $x$ returns the negated value at $|x|$.

### Evaluating Fresnel integrals

For real $x$, Chebyshev expansions on $[0, 1]$, $[1, 2]$, $[2, 4]$, and $[4, 8]$ use the forms $C(x) = x\,T_C(x)$ and $S(x) = x^3\,T_S(x)$, factoring out origin behavior $C \approx x$ and $S \approx \pi x^3/6$.
For $x > 8$, sum asymptotic expansions of $f$ and $g$ until terms start growing or fall below $\varepsilon$.

Forming phase $\pi x^2/2$ directly in binary64 produces rounding error growing as $\varepsilon x^2$, making $\sin$ and $\cos$ meaningless at large $x$.
The implementation splits $x \cdot (x/2)$ exactly into high and low binary64 parts with fused multiply-add, reduces their $\sin(\pi h)$ and $\cos(\pi h)$ phases exactly as multiples of $\pi$, then combines them with angle-addition formulas.
For $|x| \ge 2^{53}$, all representable $x$ are even, so $x^2/2$ is even and the phase is a multiple of $2\pi$.

For complex $z$, sum origin series when $|z| \le 1.5$; otherwise evaluate the error-function relation with the complex error-function kernel.
Values growing like $\exp(\pi|\operatorname{Im} z^2|/2)$ are retained in scaled form by the forms with options.
However, if phase $\pi z^2/2$ or its sine and cosine exceed binary64, evaluation returns `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).

### Work limits

`IntegralOptions` contains only a work budget, defaulting to 512 units.
Rational and Chebyshev approximations consume one unit per coefficient, while series and asymptotic expansions consume one per term; all functions on this page finish within tens of units.
Work limits are shared with exponential integrals; see [Work limits and computational cost](oxref:special-expint-budget).

## Accuracy guarantees and estimated errors

The following accuracy targets are checked against independently generated high-precision references with 160 and 240 digits.

| Function | binary64 target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `si`, `ci` | 8 ULP | Use absolute error near zeros satisfying $\lvert\operatorname{Ci}(x)\rvert < 1/(8(1+x))$ |
| `shi`, `chi`, `shi_scaled`, `chi_scaled`, `atanint` | No uniform target | Values and estimated errors tested in binary32 and binary64 |
| `fresnel_c`, `fresnel_s` (real and complex) | No uniform target | Values and estimated errors tested in binary32 and binary64 |

ULP (unit in the last place) measures error in units of the spacing between adjacent floating-point numbers in the result type.
The 8 ULP target applies to binary64; binary32 has no ULP target.
Near a zero, the function value approaches 0 and relative error loses meaning; the specification becomes an absolute-error bound.
Near the zeros of $\operatorname{Ci}$ in $[0, 4]$, double-word reevaluation reduces absolute error, but no relative-error target applies.
For functions without uniform targets, comparison tests check binary64 within relative $5 \times 10^{-11}$ (absolute $5 \times 10^{-13}$ for small values) and binary32 within relative $2 \times 10^{-4}$ (absolute $5 \times 10^{-6}$), and verify that estimates cover actual errors.

Options-form estimated errors use interval-specific error models.
The $\operatorname{Si}$ and $\operatorname{Ci}$ kernels add relative $2\varepsilon$ to their kernel errors, yielding estimates whose coverage is checked on reference data (`EstimateReliability::StatisticallyCalibrated`).
Other functions and evaluations stopped at the budget use model-based estimates (`ModelBased`), adding relative $32\varepsilon$.
Neither is an interval with guaranteed containment.

## API

| Function | Computes | Domain and main errors | Options form | Scoped API |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `si` | $\operatorname{Si}(x)$ | Every finite $x$ | `si_with_options` | `si_scoped` |
| `ci` | $\operatorname{Ci}(x)$ | $x = 0$ gives `Pole`; $x < 0$ gives `OutsideDomain` | `ci_with_options` | `ci_scoped` |
| `shi` | $\operatorname{Shi}(x)$ | Every finite $x$ | `shi_with_options` | `shi_scoped` |
| `shi_scaled` | $e^{-\lvert x\rvert}\operatorname{Shi}(x)$ | Every finite $x$ | `shi_scaled_with_options` | `shi_scaled_scoped` |
| `chi` | $\operatorname{Chi}(x)$ | $x = 0$ gives `Pole`; $x < 0$ gives `OutsideDomain` | `chi_with_options` | `chi_scoped` |
| `chi_scaled` | $e^{-x}\operatorname{Chi}(x)$ | Same as `chi` | `chi_scaled_with_options` | `chi_scaled_scoped` |
| `atanint` | $\operatorname{Ti}_2(x)$ | Every finite $x$ | `atanint_with_options` | None |
| `fresnel_c` | $C(z)$, real and complex | Complex values exceeding range give `Overflow` | `fresnel_c_with_options` | `fresnel_c_scoped` |
| `fresnel_s` | $S(z)$, real and complex | Complex values exceeding range give `Overflow` | `fresnel_s_with_options` | `fresnel_s_scoped` |

Errors are returned as `SciOxideError`; when `kind()` is `DomainError`, details in `view()` are `Pole` or `OutsideDomain`.
Nonfinite inputs give `NonFinite`; ordinary values exceeding the type's range give `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
Only `fresnel_c` and `fresnel_s` accept `NumericScalar`; the others accept only real inputs (`RealScalar`).

Standard forms `f(x)` return only values.
Options forms `f_with_options(x, options)` accept `IntegralOptions` and return `SpecialFunctionResult` containing ordinary values `value()`, power-of-two representations `scaled()`, logarithmic forms `logarithmic()`, and derivatives `derivative()` with respect to $x$, each with estimated errors, plus termination status, work counts, and algorithm identifiers.
Options-form ordinary values are bitwise equal to standard-form values.
Scoped APIs `f_scoped(x, options, scope)` compute the same results as options forms while counting work in the caller's `SolveScope` and checking cancellation and deadlines.
`atanint` has no scoped API, so use the form with options.

For large arguments, $\operatorname{Shi}$ and $\operatorname{Chi}$ overflow; use `shi_scaled` and `chi_scaled`.
Named scaled forms (`_scaled`) are separate functions with the exponential factor removed; options-form `scaled()` represents the same value using powers of 2.

## Square-wave Gibbs phenomenon and Si(π)

The Fourier partial sum of square wave $\operatorname{sgn}(\sin x)$ truncated to $M$ odd harmonics is as follows.

$$
S_M(x) = \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{\sin((2k-1)x)}{2k-1}
$$

Since $S_M'(x) = \frac{2\sin(2Mx)}{\pi\sin x}$, its first maximum to the right of discontinuity $x = 0$ is at $x_M = \pi/(2M)$.
Viewing the sum as a midpoint rule for $t_k = (2k-1)x$ with spacing $2x$ gives $S_M(x) \approx \frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Si}(2Mx)$; as $M \to \infty$, the maximum approaches $\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Si}(\pi) = 1.17897\ldots$.
For a jump of size 2, the overshoot is about $0.179$, or about 9% of the jump, and does not decrease with more terms.
This is the Gibbs phenomenon.
The leading midpoint-rule error $-\frac{h^2}{24}(f'(\pi) - f'(0))$ ($f(t) = \sin t/t$, $h = \pi/M$, $f'(\pi) = -1/\pi$) also gives $S_M(x_M) - \frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Si}(\pi) \approx \frac{1}{12M^2}$.

The next cell sums $S_M(x_M)$ directly with f64 standard functions and checks that its difference from the limit formed with `si(π)`, multiplied by $12M^2$, approaches 1.
This checks $\operatorname{Si}(\pi)$ to $O(M^{-4})$ using a sum independent of `si`.
The chart overlays the partial sum for input $M$ and $\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Si}(2Mx)$ (dashed) near the discontinuity.

```rust
//| id: special-si-gibbs-overshoot
//| ref: special-si-gibbs-overshoot
//| title: "Gibbs phenomenon of Fourier square-wave partial sums and Si(π)"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   harmonics: {"type":"range","label":"Odd harmonic count M","description":"Number of sine terms in the partial sum. The table has four rows: M, 4M, 16M, and 64M.","min":2,"max":64,"step":1,"value":8}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"few-terms","label":"M = 2","description":"With few terms, 12M² × difference is farther from 1.","inputs":{"harmonics":2}}

    let sum: f64 = (1..=m)

            let n = (2 * k - 1) as f64;
            (n * x).sin() / n
        })
        .sum();

};

        peak,

        limit,
        gap,

}
```

At the default $M = 8$, maximum $S_8(x_8) = 1.18028$ exceeds limit $\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Si}(\pi) = 1.1789797444721672$ by $1.3 \times 10^{-3}$, and $12M^2 \times$ times the difference is 1.0018.
Each quadrupling of $M$ reduces the difference by about 1/16; at $M = 512$, $12M^2 \times$ times the difference is $1 + 4.4 \times 10^{-7}$.
After subtracting $1/(12M^2)$ from the difference, the remainder is $1.4 \times 10^{-13}$ at $M = 512$, on the scale of the next midpoint-rule term $O(M^{-4})$, so `si(π)` is consistent with the independent direct sum to this precision.
The direct sum and dashed $\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Si}(2Mx)$ almost overlap, with the first maximum reaching the upper reference line $\frac{2}{\pi}\operatorname{Si}(\pi)$.
With few terms such as $M = 2$, their separation is visible, and the first row's $12M^2 \times$ times the difference departs to 1.03.
Conversely, selecting $M = 64$ makes the final row 4096 terms; direct-sum rounding of order $10^{-15}$ affects the difference $5 \times 10^{-9}$, moving $12M^2 \times$ times the difference below 1 to $1 - 3.3 \times 10^{-7}$.

## Straight-edge Fresnel diffraction

For a plane wave (wavelength $\lambda$) incident on a half-plane screen edge, intensity on an observation plane a distance $z$ away follows this formula after converting position $x$ from the edge to dimensionless Fresnel variable $v = x\sqrt{2/(\lambda z)}$ (Born & Wolf 1999, §8.7).

$$
\frac{I(v)}{I_0} = \frac12\left[\left(C(v) + \frac12\right)^2 + \left(S(v) + \frac12\right)^2\right]
$$

$I_0$ is intensity without the screen; $v < 0$ is the geometric shadow and $v > 0$ the illuminated side.
Directly above the edge, $v = 0$, $I/I_0 = 1/4$ holds exactly.
The first illuminated-side maximum is the first positive root of $dI/dv = (C + \frac12)\cos\frac{\pi v^2}{2} + (S + \frac12)\sin\frac{\pi v^2}{2} = 0$, with intensity about 1.37 times $I_0$ (Born & Wolf 1999).

The next cell selects wavelength $\lambda$ and distance $z$, plots intensity on a fixed observation-position axis $x \in [-2, 4]$ mm, and finds the first maximum by bisection.
The second chart is the Cornu spiral $(C(v), S(v))$; the final table column shows the distance from center $(1/2, 1/2)$ times $\pi v$ approaching 1 for large $v$ (checking auxiliary function $f \sim 1/(\pi v)$).

```rust
//| id: special-fresnel-clothoid-limit
//| ref: special-fresnel-clothoid-limit
//| title: "Straight-edge Fresnel diffraction and Cornu spiral"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   wavelength_nm: {"type":"range","label":"Wavelength λ [nm]","min":400,"max":700,"step":1,"value":633}
//|   distance_m: {"type":"range","label":"Edge-to-observation distance z [m]","min":0.1,"max":5,"step":0.1,"value":1}
//|   spiral_end: {"type":"range","label":"Maximum |v| for the spiral","min":2,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":4}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"blue-near","label":"λ = 400 nm, z = 0.1 m","description":"Smaller λz narrows fringe spacing. Intensity values do not change.","inputs":{"wavelength_nm":400,"distance_m":0.1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"edge","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1.2172,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edge","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.6848,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edge","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.3704,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edge","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.25,"atol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edge","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: blue-near
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"edge","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.1721,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"edge","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.3704,"atol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_special::{fresnel_c, fresnel_s};
use std::f64::consts::PI;

// Conversion factor √(λz/2) between position x [m] and Fresnel variable v.
let scale = (wavelength_nm * 1e-9 * distance_m / 2.0).sqrt();
let intensity = |v: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let c = fresnel_c(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? + 0.5;
    let s = fresnel_s(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? + 0.5;
    Ok(0.5 * (c * c + s * s))
};
// dI/dv (phase cosine and sine use f64 standard functions).
let slope = |v: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let phase = 0.5 * PI * v * v;
    let c = fresnel_c(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? + 0.5;
    let s = fresnel_s(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? + 0.5;
    Ok(c * phase.cos() + s * phase.sin())
};

// Find the first maximum by bisection. dI/dv is positive at v = 1 and negative at v = 1.5.
let (mut lo, mut hi) = (1.0_f64, 1.5_f64);
for _ in 0..60 {
    let mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi);
    if slope(mid)? > 0.0 {
        lo = mid;
    } else {
        hi = mid;
    }
}
let v_peak = 0.5 * (lo + hi);
// Distance from center (1/2, 1/2) × πv (checks large-v auxiliary functions).
let far = 50.0;
let dc = fresnel_c(far).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - 0.5;
let ds = fresnel_s(far).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - 0.5;
let rows = vec![(
    v_peak,
    v_peak * scale * 1e3,
    intensity(v_peak)?,
    intensity(0.0)?,
    PI * far * (dc * dc + ds * ds).sqrt(),
)];
```

For the defaults $\lambda = 633$ nm and $z = 1$ m, the first maximum is at $v = 1.2172$, corresponding to $0.685$ mm from the edge, with intensity $I/I_0 = 1.3704$.
This agrees with Born & Wolf's factor of about 1.37, and intensity above the edge agrees with 0.25 within rounding.
The value of $v$ and intensity do not depend on $\lambda$ or $z$; only position moves proportionally to $\sqrt{\lambda z/2}$.
At `blue-near` ($\lambda = 400$ nm, $z = 0.1$ m), the maximum position contracts to $0.172$ mm while intensity remains 1.3704.
The final column is $1 - 4 \times 10^{-8}$ at $v = 50$, showing the distance follows the asymptotic form $1/(\pi v)$ to this accuracy.
In a correct plot, intensity decreases monotonically toward zero on the shadow side ($x < 0$), while oscillations around 1 decay on the illuminated side.
If illuminated-side oscillations are not centered on 1, suspect signs of $C$ and $S$ or phase handling.

## Summary

`si` and `ci` target 8 ULP in binary64, using absolute error near zeros of $\operatorname{Ci}$.
$\operatorname{Shi}$, $\operatorname{Chi}$, inverse tangent, and Fresnel integrals have no uniform ULP target; inspect estimated errors in the forms with options when accuracy matters.
For $\operatorname{Shi}$ and $\operatorname{Chi}$ exceeding binary64 at large arguments, use `shi_scaled` and `chi_scaled` or `scaled()` and `logarithmic()` in the forms with options.
Fresnel integrals reduce phase $\pi x^2/2$ accurately; the Cornu spiral radius $1/(\pi v)$ checks that $C$ and $S$ oscillate correctly around $1/2$ even at large $x$.

Return to [Exponential and logarithmic integrals](../exponential-and-trigonometric-integrals/) to examine region selection and accuracy near zeros for $\operatorname{Ei}$ and $E_1$ used by $\operatorname{Shi}$ and $\operatorname{Chi}$.
See [Faddeeva function and Voigt profile](../faddeeva-and-voigt/) for complex error and Faddeeva functions used by complex Fresnel integrals.
