The Riemann zeta function is known for describing the distribution of primes; in physics, it appears in Bose–Einstein statistics, blackbody integrals ($\int_0^\infty x^3/(e^x - 1)\,dx = \pi^4/15 = 6\zeta(4)$), and regularization of the Casimir effect.
The Hurwitz zeta function generalizes it by shifting the starting point of the sum and is used in polygamma functions $\psi^{(m)}(q) = (-1)^{m+1} m!\,\zeta(m+1, q)$, lattice sums, and tail sums of discrete distributions.
The defining Dirichlet series converges only for $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$ and is slow even there, so `scioxide-special` does not sum it directly; it selects interval-specific Chebyshev expansions, functional equations, Euler–Maclaurin summation, or Taylor series about $q = 1$.
This page explains definitions and properties, evaluation, accuracy guarantees, and APIs; a cell examines slow convergence of the defining series and the effect of Euler–Maclaurin corrections.
[General-order polylogarithms and the Lerch transcendent](../polylog-and-lerch/) covers generalizations of the zeta functions.

## Definition and properties

### Riemann zeta function

The Riemann zeta function is defined as a Dirichlet series for $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$ (DLMF 25.2.1).

$$
\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s}
$$

It has a simple pole of residue 1 at $s = 1$ and continues meromorphically over the rest of the complex plane.
The continuation satisfies the functional equation $\zeta(s) = 2^{s}\pi^{s-1}\sin(\pi s/2)\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(1-s)$ (DLMF §25.4) and has trivial zeros at $s = -2, -4, \ldots$.
Known values include $\zeta(2) = \pi^2/6$, $\zeta(0) = -1/2$, and $\zeta(-1) = -1/12$.
`riemann_zeta(1.0)` returns `DomainError` (`Pole`), and negative even integers return exactly zero.

### Dirichlet eta function

The alternating Dirichlet eta function is defined as follows (DLMF §25.2).

$$
\eta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^s} = \left(1 - 2^{1-s}\right)\zeta(s)
$$

The alternating series converges for $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$, and $\eta$ continues to an entire function.
At $s = 1$, the pole of $\zeta$ and zero of $1 - 2^{1-s}$ cancel, giving $\eta(1) = \ln 2$.
`dirichlet_eta` has no poles and returns $\eta(0) = 1/2$.

### Hurwitz zeta function

The Hurwitz zeta function is defined below for $\operatorname{Re} s > 1$ and $q > 0$ (DLMF 25.11.1), and continues meromorphically with a pole at $s = 1$.

$$
\zeta(s, q) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(n+q)^{s}}
$$

$\zeta(s, 1) = \zeta(s)$ and $\zeta(s, 1/2) = (2^s - 1)\zeta(s)$, with shift recurrence $\zeta(s, q) = \zeta(s, q+1) + q^{-s}$.
At $s = 0$ and $s = -1$, Bernoulli polynomials give the closed forms $\zeta(0, q) = 1/2 - q$ and $\zeta(-1, q) = -(q^2 - q + 1/6)/2$ (DLMF 25.11.14).

## Evaluation methods

### Real Riemann zeta and eta functions

Real `riemann_zeta` uses the following interval-specific methods rather than the defining series.

| Range of $s$ | Method |
|---|---|
| $0 < s \le 2$ | Divide a Chebyshev expansion of pole-removed $(s-1)\zeta(s)$ by $s - 1$ |
| $2 < s \le 24$ | Chebyshev expansion of $\zeta(s) - 1$ ($[2, 4]$, $[4, 8]$, $[8, 16]$, $[16, 24]$) |
| $s > 24$ | $1 + 2^{-s} + 3^{-s} + 4^{-s}$. The omitted tail is at most $4^{1-s}/(s-1)$ |
| $s = 0$ | $-1/2$ |
| $-1 \le s < 0$ | Chebyshev expansion |
| $s < -1$ | Sum logarithms of functional-equation factors in double-word arithmetic (about 106 bits as a sum of two binary64 values), then exponentiate. Negative even integers give zero |

For $0 < s \le 2$, approximating $(s-1)\zeta(s)$ factors the pole $1/(s-1)$ out of the expansion, leaving a smooth function of $s$ that needs few coefficients.
For $s < -1$, combining functional-equation factors logarithmically allows a representable product even when $\Gamma(1-s)$ and $(2\pi)^{s}$ separately exceed the range.
At large negative $s$, $\Gamma(1-s)$ dominates and $|\zeta(s)|$ grows; in binary64, $s$ below roughly $-260$ gives `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`), except at negative even integers.

Real `dirichlet_eta` uses dedicated Chebyshev expansions on $0 < s \le 2$ and $-1 \le s < 0$; for $s > 2$, it forms $1 - 2^{1-s}$ with `expm1` and multiplies by $\zeta(s)$; for $s < -1$, it adds $\ln(2^{1-s} - 1)$ to the logarithmic functional equation.
$\eta$ is about $2^{1-s}$ times larger than $\zeta$, so in binary64 it overflows when $s$ is below roughly $-218$.
For binary32 inputs with $s \le 0$, evaluation is performed in binary64 and rounded.

### Complex Riemann zeta function

Complex $\zeta(s)$ uses Euler–Maclaurin summation for $\operatorname{Re} s \ge -1/2$ and the functional equation with accurately reduced phase $\pi s/2$ for $\operatorname{Re} s < -1/2$.
The functional-equation error estimate includes rounding from logarithmic assembly of its factors.
Complex $\eta(s)$ multiplies $\zeta(s)$ by $-\operatorname{expm1}((1-s)\ln 2)$.

### Euler–Maclaurin summation

Euler–Maclaurin summation directly adds the first $N$ terms and replaces the remaining tail by an integral and corrections involving Bernoulli numbers $B_{2k}$ (DLMF 2.10.1).

$$
\sum_{n=N}^{\infty} f(n) = \int_N^\infty f(x)\,dx + \frac{f(N)}{2} - \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!} f^{(2k-1)}(N) + R_K
$$

For $f(x) = (x + q)^{-s}$, $f^{(2k-1)}$ has a closed form, and corrections decrease faster at larger $N$.
However, the correction series itself diverges, so stop before terms begin growing and use the last term's magnitude to estimate remainder $R_K$.

### Real Hurwitz zeta function

For $s > 1$, real `hurwitz_zeta` factors out the leading $q^{-s}$ and applies Euler–Maclaurin summation to the normalized sum.
All terms are positive for $s > 1$, so `hurwitz_zeta_log` obtains $\ln\zeta(s, q)$ from the same sum without forming $\zeta(s, q)$.
Even when values underflow, as in $\zeta(1000, 2) \approx 2^{-1000}$, logarithms remain representable.

For $s < 1$, real `hurwitz_zeta` selects methods in the following order.
At $q = 1$, it returns the same value as `riemann_zeta`; at $s = 0$ and $s = -1$, it uses the closed forms above.
If $s > -23$ and $q \ge \max(16, 2|s|)$, the tail integral dominates without cancellation, so use Euler–Maclaurin summation without subtracting the leading term.
For $s \le -8$, use Hurwitz's absolutely convergent Fourier series (DLMF 25.11.9).
Otherwise, use the Taylor series about $q = 1$ (DLMF 25.11.10), $\zeta(s, 1 + \delta) = \sum_k \binom{-s}{k}\zeta(s+k)\delta^k$ ($|\delta| \le 1/2$), then shift back to $q$ with the shift recurrence.
Estimated error is a derived absolute error including truncation, errors in individual components, and cancellation.

### Complex Hurwitz zeta function

Complex `hurwitz_zeta` uses the real path when $s$ and $q$ are both real and $q > 0$.
For $\operatorname{Re} s \le 0$, use the same Taylor series if shifted $q$ lies at a distance less than 0.75 from 1; otherwise integrate Hermite's representation (DLMF 25.11.29) by adaptive Gauss–Legendre subdivision.
For $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$, use Euler–Maclaurin summation.
Powers use the principal value $(n + q)^{-s} = \exp(-s\ln(n + q))$, so the negative real axis of $q$ is a cut, with the imaginary signed zero selecting the side, and $q = 0, -1, -2, \ldots$ are poles.

## Accuracy guarantees and estimated errors

Accuracy targets checked against independent high-precision references (160-digit and 240-digit reference data) are as follows.

| Function | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Real `riemann_zeta`, `dirichlet_eta` | 64 ULP in binary32 and binary64 | Reference data: $s \in [-31.5, 64]$ for $\zeta$, $s \in [-12.5, 20]$ for $\eta$ |
| Real `hurwitz_zeta` ($s > 1$) | 64 ULP in binary32 and binary64 | Reference data: $q \in [0.1, 64]$ |
| Real `hurwitz_zeta` ($s < 1$) | Derived absolute error bound | Tested over $s \in [-60, 1)$, $q \in (0, 1000]$. Relative errors grow near zeros |
| `hurwitz_zeta_log` | No separate target | Return absolute error $\varepsilon(64 + \lvert\ln\zeta(s,q)\rvert)$ in natural-log units |
| Complex `riemann_zeta`, `dirichlet_eta`, `hurwitz_zeta` | No uniform target | Large $\lvert\operatorname{Im} s\rvert$ can make estimated errors comparable to values |

ULP (unit in the last place) measures error in units of the spacing between adjacent floating-point numbers in the result type.
For real functions with a 64 ULP target, the forms with options estimate error as 64 ULP of the value (coverage checked on reference data, `StatisticallyCalibrated`).
For $s < 1$, Hurwitz zeta returns an absolute error derived from truncation and rounding (error-model estimate, `ModelBased`); large relative error near a zero is not treated as failure.
None of the estimated errors is an interval with guaranteed enclosure.

## API

| Function | Computes | Domain and main errors | Options form | Scoped API |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `riemann_zeta` | $\zeta(s)$, real and complex | $s = 1$ gives `Pole`; large negative $s$ gives `Overflow` | `riemann_zeta_with_options` | `riemann_zeta_scoped` |
| `dirichlet_eta` | $\eta(s)$, real and complex | Large negative $s$ gives `Overflow` | `dirichlet_eta_with_options` | `dirichlet_eta_scoped` |
| `hurwitz_zeta` | $\zeta(s, q)$, real and complex | $s = 1$ and $q = 0$ give `Pole`; real $q < 0$ gives `OutsideDomain`; complex $q = -1, -2, \ldots$ gives `Pole` | `hurwitz_zeta_with_options` | `hurwitz_zeta_scoped` |
| `hurwitz_zeta_log` | $\ln\zeta(s, q)$, real $s > 1$, $q > 0$ | $s = 1$ and $q = 0$ give `Pole`; $s < 1$ and $q < 0$ give `OutsideDomain` | `hurwitz_zeta_log_with_options` | `hurwitz_zeta_log_scoped` |

Errors are returned as `SciOxideError`; when `kind()` is `DomainError`, details in `view()` are `Pole` or `OutsideDomain`.
Non-finite inputs give `NonFinite`; values beyond the type's range give `NumericalInstability` (`Overflow`).
`ZetaOptions` contains only a work limit, defaulting to 65536 units.
At the limit, both standard and options forms return `ConvergenceFailure`.
The arguments of `riemann_zeta` and `dirichlet_eta` are named `x` in the signatures.
The $q$ of `hurwitz_zeta` is `impl Parameter<T>`, allowing real $q$ with complex $s$.

Standard forms `f(..)` return only values.
The form with options `f_with_options(.., options)` adds estimated error, termination status, work counts, and algorithm identifier to the same value.
Scoped APIs `f_scoped(.., options, scope)` perform the same computation as options forms within the caller's `SolveScope`, checking cancellation and deadlines.
Use `hurwitz_zeta_log` for Hurwitz zeta with $s > 1$ whose values underflow, or when logarithms are needed, such as likelihood calculations.

## Slow defining-series convergence and Euler–Maclaurin corrections

The defining series converges slowly.
The error of partial sum $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^{N} n^{-s}$ is the tail $\sum_{n > N} n^{-s} \approx N^{1-s}/(s-1)$; even at $s = 2$, $N = 10^6$ terms leave $10^{-6}$.
Adding the first three Euler–Maclaurin terms (integral, half endpoint, and $B_2$ correction) gives $S_N + \frac{N^{1-s}}{s-1} - \frac12 N^{-s} + \frac{s}{12}N^{-s-1}$, leaving error only on the scale of the next correction $-\frac{s(s+1)(s+2)}{720}N^{-s-3}$.

The next cell selects $s$ and plots partial-sum errors for $N = 1, 2, 4, \ldots, 32768$ as differences from `riemann_zeta(s)`.
The dashed curve is the integral tail estimate $N^{1-s}/(s-1)$; another series shows errors after the three Euler–Maclaurin terms.
Partial sums and corrections use f64 standard functions, so reaching rounding level after correction independently checks `riemann_zeta`.
As $s$ approaches pole $s = 1$, the estimated tail barely decreases.

```rust
//| id: special-zeta-partial-sum
//| ref: special-zeta-partial-sum
//| title: "Convergence of the zeta defining series and Euler–Maclaurin corrections"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   exponent_s: {"type":"range","label":"Exponent s","description":"Vary the distance from the pole s = 1.","min":1.1,"max":4,"step":0.1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"near-pole","label":"s = 1.1","description":"Near the pole, partial-sum errors barely decrease.","inputs":{"exponent_s":1.1}}
//|   - {"id":"steep","label":"s = 4","description":"The tail is small, and partial-sum errors stop at addition rounding.","inputs":{"exponent_s":4}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"partial","path":"/rows/5/1","approx":{"value":3.0517e-5,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"partial","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: near-pole
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"partial","path":"/rows/5/1","range":{"min":3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"partial","path":"/rows/5/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: steep
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"partial","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"partial","path":"/rows/5/2","approx":{"value":9.4739e-15,"rtol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_special::riemann_zeta;

let s = exponent_s;
let reference = riemann_zeta(s).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut plain, mut tail, mut corrected) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let (mut sum, mut previous) = (0.0_f64, 0_usize);
for power in 0..=15 {
    let terms = 1_usize << power;
    // Sum partial sums in increasing n (f64 standard functions).
    for n in previous + 1..=terms {
        sum += (n as f64).powf(-s);
    }
    previous = terms;
    let big_n = terms as f64;
    // Integral tail estimate and three Euler–Maclaurin terms (f64 standard functions).
    let integral = big_n.powf(1.0 - s) / (s - 1.0);
    let euler_maclaurin =
        sum + integral - 0.5 * big_n.powf(-s) + s / 12.0 * big_n.powf(-s - 1.0);
    let plain_error = (reference - sum).abs();
    let corrected_error = (reference - euler_maclaurin).abs();
    // Omit zero-error points from the logarithmic chart and show “0” in the table.
    if plain_error > 0.0 {
        plain.push([big_n, plain_error]);
    }
    if corrected_error > 0.0 {
        corrected.push([big_n, corrected_error]);
    }
    tail.push([big_n, integral]);
    if power % 3 == 0 {
        rows.push((
            terms as u32,
            Some(plain_error).filter(|e| *e > 0.0),
            integral,
            Some(corrected_error).filter(|e| *e > 0.0),
        ));
    }
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Partial-sum error", "points": plain }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "Integral tail estimate",
            "points": tail,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Error after three Euler–Maclaurin terms", "points": corrected })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Term count N [-]",
        "yLabel": "Absolute difference from ζ(s) [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "N", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Partial-sum error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "0" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "N^(1−s)/(s−1) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Corrected error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "0" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "partial" })
);
```

At $s = 2$, partial-sum errors follow a straight line of slope $-1$, overlapping the dashed tail estimate $N^{-1}$ and leaving $3.05 \times 10^{-5}$ even at $N = 32768$.
Adding three Euler–Maclaurin terms reduces errors as $N^{-s-3}$, reaching rounding level $6.7 \times 10^{-16}$ at $N = 512$, so `riemann_zeta(2)` agrees with this independent sum to $10^{-15}$ precision.
Beyond that, rounding in the partial sum itself remains ($9.8 \times 10^{-15}$ at $N = 4096$).
At `near-pole` ($s = 1.1$), the tail estimate $N^{-0.1}/0.1$ barely decreases, leaving partial-sum error 3.5 even at $N = 32768$.
Near the pole, summing the defining series alone cannot determine the value; adding corrections gives error $8.7 \times 10^{-14}$ at $N = 512$.
At `steep` ($s = 4$), the tail $N^{-3}/3$ is small, and the partial-sum error $2.8 \times 10^{-13}$ at $N = 32768$ exceeds the tail estimate $9.5 \times 10^{-15}$.
This is accumulated rounding from summing $n^{-s}$ in descending term magnitude, rather than series truncation error.
The table's “0” marks errors exactly zero in binary64; those points are omitted from the logarithmic chart.

## Summary

Real `riemann_zeta` and `dirichlet_eta`, and `hurwitz_zeta` with $s > 1$, target 64 ULP in binary32 and binary64; `hurwitz_zeta` with $s < 1$ returns a derived absolute error estimate.
Zeta functions use interval-specific Chebyshev expansions, functional equations, and Euler–Maclaurin summation rather than the defining series, allowing evaluation even near pole $s = 1$.
At large negative $s$, values exceed binary64: $\zeta$ beyond $s \approx -260$ and $\eta$ beyond $s \approx -218$ give `Overflow`.
For Hurwitz zeta with $s > 1$ whose values underflow, use `hurwitz_zeta_log`.
The cell shows that direct defining-series summation yields no digits near the pole even after tens of thousands of terms, while three Euler–Maclaurin corrections reach rounding level with hundreds.
