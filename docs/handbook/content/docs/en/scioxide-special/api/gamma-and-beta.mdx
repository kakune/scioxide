These tables cover complete Gamma $\Gamma$ and Beta $B$ functions, regularized incomplete forms $P(a,x)$, $Q(a,x)$, and $I_x(a,b)$, and their logarithmic forms and inverses.
Functions accepting `NumericScalar` use the same names for real (`f32`, `f64`) and complex (`Complex<f32>`, `Complex<f64>`) arguments; functions accepting only `RealScalar` are real-only.
Each row lists the standard form, the form with options `*_with_options` that returns `SpecialFunctionResult`, and the scoped API `*_scoped(.., options, scope)` where available ([three calling forms](oxref:special-api-call-forms)).
Scoped APIs are provided only for functions whose computational cost varies with the arguments.
The option types, including incomplete forms, logarithmic forms, and inverses, are `GammaOptions` for the Gamma family and `BetaOptions` for the Beta family; both contain only a work limit (`Budget`) ([Result types and options](../results-and-options/)).

All functions return `NonFinite` for non-finite input and `NumericalInstability` for unrepresentable results or computational breakdown.
For iterative functions, a computation that does not finish within the limit produces `ConvergenceFailure` in the standard form; forms with options and scoped APIs return termination status `IterationLimit` when an estimate remains available (otherwise, `ConvergenceFailure`).

## Complete Gamma function and logarithmic form

Real Gamma, log Gamma, digamma, and polygamma functions target 4 ULP for binary64 and 1 ULP for binary32 on comparison data (independently generated reference values).
Complex analytic continuation has no fixed ULP specification.
Gamma, log Gamma, and digamma have fixed operation counts independently of their arguments and no scoped APIs.
Polygamma has a scoped API because its computational cost grows in proportion to the order.

## Incomplete Gamma functions

Real evaluation accepts finite $a \ge 0$ and $x \ge 0$; negative $a$ or $x < 0$ produces `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
Treatment of shape $a = 0$ depends on the function.
`gamma_lower` requires $a > 0$ and reports $a = 0$ as `DomainError` (`Pole`); the others return the limits $P(0, x) = 1$, $Q(0, x) = 0$, and $\Gamma(0, x) = E_1(x)$ for $x > 0$.
At the origin $(a, x) = (0, 0)$, regularized and log-regularized forms are not uniquely determined and produce `DomainError` (`Undefined`); `gamma_upper` treats it as a pole, producing `DomainError` (`Pole`).
Complex evaluation uses principal powers of $z$, with a cut on the negative real axis and the side selected by signed zero of the imaginary part.
At $z = 0$, $\operatorname{Re} a \le 0$ produces `DomainError` (`Undefined`) ($a = 0$ in `gamma_upper` is a pole).
`gamma_lower`, `gamma_upper`, `gamma_p`, and `gamma_q` accept shape $a$ through `Parameter<T>`, so real $a$ may be passed with complex $z$.
Logarithmic forms `gamma_log_p` and `gamma_log_q` are real-only.
Real incomplete forms target 32 ULP for binary64 and 1 ULP for binary32 on boundary comparison data including shapes $20 < a \le 10^{10}$ and deep tails (the maximum error in a broader binary64 sample of 4,160 points is 90 ULP).
Complex incomplete forms have no uniform ULP specification; forms with options return estimated errors.
All four complex forms with options include a derivative with respect to $z$ in `derivative()`; real forms with options have no derivative.

## Inverses of incomplete Gamma functions

Inverses are real-only (`RealScalar`), with argument order `(a, p)`.
$a \le 0$ and out-of-range $p$ produce `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
Starting from an asymptotic initial estimate, bracketed Halley iteration proceeds in the coordinate $\ln x$; the final few steps evaluate tails in double-double precision and stop at scalar precision.
$a = 1$ is solved in closed form.
The targets on comparison data including shapes $20 < a \le 10^{10}$ are 32 ULP for binary64 and 1 ULP for binary32.

## Incomplete Beta functions and tails

Real evaluation accepts finite $a, b \ge 0$ and $0 \le x \le 1$; negative shapes or $x$ outside the interval produce `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
Zero shapes are treated as limits: $a = 0$, $b > 0$, and $x > 0$ return $I_x = 1$; $b = 0$, $a > 0$, and $x < 1$ return $I_x = 0$.
Both shapes zero, and the corners $(a, x) = (0, 0)$ and $(b, x) = (0, 1)$, do not determine a unique value and produce `DomainError` (`Undefined`).
Tail and logarithmic functions follow the same rules.
Only `beta_inc` accepts complex arguments; it accepts shapes $a$ and $b$ through `Parameter<T>`.
Tails and logarithmic forms are real-only.
Real incomplete Beta and its tails and logarithmic forms target 32 ULP for binary64 and 1 ULP for binary32 on the comparison data; binary64 estimated errors are also returned as 32 units (the maximum error in a broader binary64 sample of 4,160 points is 12 ULP).
The finite sums for integer shapes switch to the general algorithm before the leading power loses accuracy within the normal range, preserving representable subnormal tails.

## Inverses of incomplete Beta functions

Inverses are real-only (`RealScalar`), with argument order `(a, b, p)`.
$a \le 0$, $b \le 0$, and out-of-range $p$ produce `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`).
Starting from an asymptotic initial estimate, bracketed Halley iteration proceeds in the coordinate $\ln(x/(1-x))$; the final few steps evaluate tails in double-double precision and stop at scalar precision.
The targets on the comparison data are 32 ULP for binary64 and 1 ULP for binary32.
