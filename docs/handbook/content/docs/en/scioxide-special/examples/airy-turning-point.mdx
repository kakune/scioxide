Quantum bound states occur in Earth's gravitational field as well as inside atoms.
A neutron falling onto a horizontal mirror is confined to a well bounded below by the mirror and above by gravity, giving discrete energies for its vertical motion.
Nesvizhevsky et al. (2002) passed ultracold neutrons (neutrons traveling at a few m/s) at the Institut Laue-Langevin through a gap between a mirror and an absorber and observed evidence of these quantum states.
The Schrödinger equation for the linear potential $mgz$ reduces to the Airy equation, so energy levels are determined by zeros of the Airy function $\operatorname{Ai}$.
This example uses values and derivatives returned by `airy_ai_with_options` and `airy_bi_with_options` in `scioxide-special` to compute zeros, wavefunctions, normalization, and the Wronskian, comparing them with the paper's values and the WKB approximation.
Definitions and evaluation methods are explained in [Airy functions](../../algorithms/airy-functions/).

## Gravitational well and the Airy equation

Take the $z$ axis vertically upward and place the mirror at $z = 0$.
For a neutron of mass $m$, the potential is $V(z) = mgz$ for $z > 0$, with an infinite wall at the mirror.
The mirror reflects the neutron completely, so the wavefunction satisfies $\psi(0) = 0$.
For $z > 0$, the stationary Schrödinger equation takes the form:

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\,\psi''(z) + mgz\,\psi(z) = E\,\psi(z)
$$


Nondimensionalize length and energy using

$$
\ell = \left(\frac{\hbar^2}{2m^2g}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
\varepsilon = mg\ell = \left(\frac{\hbar^2mg^2}{2}\right)^{1/3}
$$

and set $\zeta = z/\ell - E/\varepsilon$; the equation becomes the Airy equation $\psi''(\zeta) = \zeta\,\psi(\zeta)$.
Using the CODATA 2018 values $\hbar = 1.054571817 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, neutron mass $m = 1.67492749804 \times 10^{-27}$ kg, and standard gravity $g = 9.80665$ m/s² gives $\ell \approx 5.87$ μm and $\varepsilon \approx 0.602$ peV ($1\ \text{peV} = 10^{-12}$ eV).

Only $\operatorname{Ai}$ remains bounded as $z \to \infty$; $\operatorname{Bi}$ grows exponentially and is excluded.
The mirror condition $\psi(0) = 0$ becomes $\operatorname{Ai}(-E/\varepsilon) = 0$; numbering the zeros of $\operatorname{Ai}$ as $0 > a_1 > a_2 > \cdots$ gives:

$$
E_n = \varepsilon\,|a_n|,\qquad z_n = \frac{E_n}{mg} = \ell\,|a_n|,\qquad
\psi_n(z) = \frac{\operatorname{Ai}(z/\ell + a_n)}{\sqrt{\ell}\,|\operatorname{Ai}'(a_n)|}
$$


$z_n$ is the classical turning-point height; above it, the wavefunction decays exponentially.
The normalization constant follows from the antiderivative $x\operatorname{Ai}(x)^2 - \operatorname{Ai}'(x)^2$ of $\operatorname{Ai}^2$ (DLMF §9.11), which gives $\int_{a_n}^\infty \operatorname{Ai}(x)^2dx = \operatorname{Ai}'(a_n)^2$.

## Computing zeros and the WKB approximation

Adding the phase shifts $\pi$ from reflection at the mirror and $\pi/2$ from the linear turning point to Bohr–Sommerfeld quantization gives

$$
\int_0^{z_n}\sqrt{2m(E_n - mgz)}\,dz = \pi\hbar\left(n - \tfrac{1}{4}\right)
$$

and hence the WKB approximation for the zeros:

$$
|a_n| \approx t_n^{2/3},\qquad t_n = \frac{3\pi}{8}(4n - 1)
$$


This is the leading term of the asymptotic expansion $|a_n| = t_n^{2/3}\left(1 + \tfrac{5}{48}t_n^{-2} - \cdots\right)$ in DLMF 9.9.6; its relative error approaches $\tfrac{5}{48}t_n^{-2}$ as $n$ increases.

The cells on this page find zeros by a few Newton iterations $x \leftarrow x - \operatorname{Ai}(x)/\operatorname{Ai}'(x)$ starting from the WKB approximation.
Both $\operatorname{Ai}$ and $\operatorname{Ai}'$ come from one evaluation of `airy_ai_with_options` (`try_value` and `try_derivative`).
Real $\operatorname{Ai}$ is within 64 ULP over the tested range, with absolute-error tests near zeros.
For systematic computation of numbered zeros, use `special_zeros_with_options` (family `SpecialZeroFamily::AiryAi`) from [Special function zeros](../../../scioxide-rootfind/algorithms/special-function-zeros/) in `scioxide-rootfind`.
This function starts from tabulated values or asymptotic expansions in the zero index, refines with Newton's method safeguarded by bisection in a sign-changing interval, and checks each zero in the original equation.

## Levels, wavefunctions, and the Wronskian

The next cell selects a celestial body (gravitational acceleration $g$) and the number of levels to plot, and computes zeros, energies, and turning-point heights for $n = 1, \dots, 5$.
The table shows differences between Newton zeros and DLMF Table 9.9.1, residuals of $\operatorname{Ai}$ at the zeros, $E_n$ and the theoretical values 1.41, 2.46, 3.32, and 4.08 peV reported by Nesvizhevsky et al. (2002), turning-point heights, differences between the Wronskian $\operatorname{Ai}\operatorname{Bi}' - \operatorname{Ai}'\operatorname{Bi}$ and $1/\pi$, and $\int_0^\infty|\psi_n|^2dz$ computed by the trapezoidal rule.
The Wronskian uses only the four values and derivatives returned by the APIs, checking values and derivatives together.
The plot shows normalized wavefunctions $\psi_n(z)$ [μm⁻¹ᐟ²], with vertical lines at turning-point heights.
Changing $g$ to the lunar value of 1.62 m/s² lowers the levels and spreads the wavefunctions to greater heights according to $\ell \propto g^{-1/3}$ and $\varepsilon \propto g^{2/3}$.

```rust
//| id: special-airy-neutron-gravity-quantization
//| ref: special-airy-neutron-gravity-quantization
//| title: "Levels, wavefunctions, and the Wronskian of gravitationally bound neutrons"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   body: {"type":"select","label":"Celestial body (gravitational acceleration)","options":["Earth (9.80665 m/s²)","Mars (3.721 m/s²)","Moon (1.62 m/s²)"],"value":"Earth (9.80665 m/s²)"}
//|   levels: {"type":"integer","label":"Number of levels to plot","min":1,"max":5,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"moon","label":"Lunar g = 1.62 m/s²","description":"Levels fall to 0.30 times their Earth values, and turning-point heights increase by a factor of 1.82.","inputs":{"body":"Moon (1.62 m/s²)"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-2.338107410459767,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.41,"atol":0.005}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.4067188095476253,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":13.72,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/2/7","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/0/8","approx":{"value":1,"atol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: moon
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.42351635595277426,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"levels","path":"/rows/0/5","equals":null}
use scioxide_special::{AiryOptions, airy_ai_with_options, airy_bi_with_options};

let gravity: f64 = match body.as_str() {
    "Mars (3.721 m/s²)" => 3.721,
    "Moon (1.62 m/s²)" => 1.62,
    _ => 9.80665,
};
// CODATA 2018: ħ [J s], neutron mass [kg], elementary charge [C]
let (hbar, mass, charge) = (1.054571817e-34, 1.67492749804e-27, 1.602176634e-19);
let length = (hbar * hbar / (2.0 * mass * mass * gravity)).cbrt(); // ℓ [m]
let energy = mass * gravity * length / charge * 1e12; // ε [peV]
let ai = |x: f64| {
    airy_ai_with_options(x, AiryOptions::default())
        .and_then(|r| Ok((r.try_value()?, r.try_derivative()?)))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let bi = |x: f64| {
    airy_bi_with_options(x, AiryOptions::default())
        .and_then(|r| Ok((r.try_value()?, r.try_derivative()?)))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
// DLMF Table 9.9.1 zeros and theoretical values from Nesvizhevsky et al. (2002) [peV]
let table = [
    -2.338107410459767,
    -4.087949444130971,
    -5.520559828095551,
    -6.786708090071759,
];
let paper = [1.41, 2.46, 3.32, 4.08];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut curves = Vec::new();
let mut marks = Vec::new();
for n in 1..=5_usize {
    // Find Ai zeros by Newton iteration from the WKB approximation
    let t = 3.0 * std::f64::consts::PI / 8.0 * (4.0 * n as f64 - 1.0);
    let mut zero = -t.powf(2.0 / 3.0);
    for _ in 0..4 {
        let (value, slope) = ai(zero)?;
        zero -= value / slope;
    }
    let (residual, slope) = ai(zero)?;
    let (b, b_slope) = bi(zero)?;
    let wronskian = residual * b_slope - slope * b - 1.0 / std::f64::consts::PI;
    // Wavefunction ψₙ(z) = Ai(z/ℓ + aₙ)/(√ℓ |Ai′(aₙ)|) (z in μm, ψ in μm^(−1/2))
    let norm = 1.0 / ((length * 1e6).sqrt() * slope.abs());
    // Stretch Earth's 80 μm plotting range by g^(−1/3); compute ∫|ψ|² dz by the trapezoidal rule (independent check)
    let top = 80.0 * (9.80665 / gravity).cbrt();
    let dz = top / 600.0;
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    let mut probability = 0.0;
    for i in 0..=600 {
        let z = dz * f64::from(i);
        let psi = norm * ai(z * 1e-6 / length + zero)?.0;
        let weight = if i == 0 || i == 600 { 0.5 } else { 1.0 };
        probability += weight * psi * psi * dz;
        points.push([z, psi]);
    }
    let height = length * zero.abs() * 1e6; // zₙ [μm]
    if n <= levels as usize {
        curves.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": format!("n = {n}"), "points": points }));
        marks.push(serde_json::json!({ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": height, "label": format!("z{n}") }));
    }
    let difference = table.get(n - 1).map(|reference| zero - reference);
    let energy_n = energy * zero.abs();
    // The paper's values apply only to Earth's gravity
    let reference = paper.get(n - 1).copied().filter(|_| gravity == 9.80665);
    rows.push((
        n as u32,
        zero,
        difference,
        residual,
        energy_n,
        reference,
        height,
        wronskian,
        probability,
    ));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &curves,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Height above the mirror z [μm]",
        "yLabel": "Wavefunction ψₙ(z) [μm^(−1/2)]",
        "annotations": marks
    })
);
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let fixed12 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 12 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Zero aₙ", "type": "number", "format": fixed12 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from DLMF table", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Ai(aₙ)", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Eₙ [peV]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Paper Eₙ [peV]", "type": "number", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Turning point zₙ [μm]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Wronskian − 1/π", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "8", "label": "∫|ψₙ|² dz", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "levels" })
);
```

For Earth's gravity, $\ell = 5.869$ μm and $\varepsilon = 0.6016$ peV; the levels are $E_1 = 1.407$, $E_2 = 2.460$, $E_3 = 3.321$, and $E_4 = 4.083$ peV, agreeing to the displayed digits with 1.41, 2.46, 3.32, and 4.08 peV in Nesvizhevsky et al. (2002).
The turning-point heights are $z_1 = 13.7$ μm, $z_2 = 24.0$ μm, and $z_3 = 32.4$ μm.
In the paper, almost no neutrons passed when the mirror–absorber gap was narrower than approximately 15 μm; transmission began above that gap.
This is slightly larger than $z_1$ because the wavefunction penetrates exponentially into the forbidden region above the turning point.

The Newton zeros agree with the DLMF table at $n = 1, 3, 4$ and differ by $9 \times 10^{-16}$ at $n = 2$.
Residuals of $\operatorname{Ai}$ at the zeros are at most $3 \times 10^{-16}$; differences between the Wronskian and $1/\pi$ are at most $6 \times 10^{-17}$.
Trapezoidal estimates of $\int|\psi_n|^2dz$ differ from 1 by at most $4 \times 10^{-13}$ for $n = 1$ through 4, confirming the normalization constant $1/(\sqrt\ell|\operatorname{Ai}'(a_n)|)$.
For $n = 5$, the difference increases to $1.0 \times 10^{-10}$ because a tail that has not fully decayed above the turning point $z_5 = 46.6$ μm remains beyond the integration endpoint of 80 μm.
For lunar gravity (1.62 m/s²), levels are $(1.62/9.80665)^{2/3} = 0.30$ times and turning-point heights $(9.80665/1.62)^{1/3} = 1.82$ times their Earth values.
The paper's values correspond to Earth's gravity and are omitted from the table in this case.


## Accuracy of the WKB approximation

The next cell finds zeros for $n = 1, \dots, N$ by the same method and plots the relative error of the WKB approximation on logarithmic axes.
The dashed line is $\tfrac{5}{48}t_n^{-2}$, predicted by the next term in DLMF 9.9.6.
Agreement between the two indicates consistency between zero computation and the asymptotic expansion.

```rust
//| id: special-airy-wkb-zeros
//| ref: special-airy-wkb-zeros
//| title: "Relative errors of Airy zeros and the WKB approximation"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   count: {"type":"integer","label":"Number of zeros N","min":5,"max":200,"value":40}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"many","label":"N = 200","description":"At n = 200, relative error falls to approximately 10⁻⁷.","inputs":{"count":200}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"wkb","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.007637209338536672,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"wkb","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":2.9687e-6,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|   - case: many
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"wkb","path":"/rows/2/3","approx":{"value":1.17563e-7,"rtol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_special::{AiryOptions, airy_ai_with_options};

let ai = |x: f64| {
    airy_ai_with_options(x, AiryOptions::default())
        .and_then(|r| Ok((r.try_value()?, r.try_derivative()?)))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
};
let mut errors = Vec::new();
let mut predicted = Vec::new();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for n in 1..=count as usize {
    let t = 3.0 * std::f64::consts::PI / 8.0 * (4.0 * n as f64 - 1.0);
    let wkb = t.powf(2.0 / 3.0);
    let mut zero = -wkb;
    for _ in 0..4 {
        let (value, slope) = ai(zero)?;
        zero -= value / slope;
    }
    let relative = (wkb - zero.abs()).abs() / zero.abs();
    // Prediction from the next term in DLMF 9.9.6 (independent check)
    let next_term = 5.0 / 48.0 / (t * t);
    errors.push([n as f64, relative]);
    predicted.push([n as f64, next_term]);
    if n == 1 || n == 4 || n == count as usize {
        rows.push((n as u32, zero, wkb, relative, next_term));
    }
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "WKB relative error", "points": errors }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "(5/48) tₙ⁻²", "points": predicted, "style": { "lineType": "dashed" } })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Zero index n [-]",
        "yLabel": "Relative error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let fixed10 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 10 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Zero aₙ", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "WKB |aₙ|", "type": "number", "format": fixed10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative error", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "(5/48) tₙ⁻²", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(columns, rows, metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "wkb" }));
```

The WKB relative error decreases from $7.6 \times 10^{-3}$ at $n = 1$ to $3.3 \times 10^{-4}$ at $n = 4$ and $3.0 \times 10^{-6}$ at $n = 40$.
Its ratio to the prediction $\tfrac{5}{48}t_n^{-2}$ is 0.92 at $n = 1$ and 0.99996 at $n = 40$; the next term alone explains the error increasingly well as $n$ grows.
The two plotted curves overlap along a straight line of slope $-2$.
At $n = 200$, relative error falls to $1.18 \times 10^{-7}$; four Newton iterations from the WKB initial estimate find zeros to the rounding level.


## Summary

For bound states in a linear potential, zeros $a_n$ of the Airy function $\operatorname{Ai}$ determine energies $E_n = \varepsilon|a_n|$ and turning-point heights $z_n = \ell|a_n|$.
`airy_ai_with_options` returns a value and derivative together, allowing Newton zeros, the normalization constant $|\operatorname{Ai}'(a_n)|$, and the Wronskian check to use the same evaluation.
For Earth's gravity, $E_1 = 1.41$ peV and $z_1 = 13.7$ μm agree with Nesvizhevsky et al. (2002).
Even at $n = 1$, the WKB relative error is below 0.8%; it decreases in proportion to $t_n^{-2}$, providing a sufficient initial estimate for large $n$.

For a general method to find many numbered zeros, see [Special function zeros](../../../scioxide-rootfind/algorithms/special-function-zeros/).
[Paul trap stability regions and Mathieu functions](../paul-trap-stability/) gives an example of confinement and stability under periodic driving.
