For two positively charged nuclei to react, they must penetrate the barrier of Coulomb repulsion between them.
In stellar hydrogen burning and controlled fusion, reactions occur at energies one to three orders of magnitude below the barrier height; their rates are largely determined by the tunneling probability.
Gamow (1928) showed that this probability is governed by $e^{-2\pi\eta}$ (the Gamow factor), explaining the extreme sensitivity of $\alpha$-decay lifetimes to decay energy.
This example evaluates Coulomb wave functions at the nuclear surface radius to compute barrier penetrability, then checks it against the low-energy limiting formula, the Gamow factor, the neutron spherical Bessel formula, and the Wronskian.
Definitions and evaluation methods appear in [Coulomb waves and hydrogen-like radial states](../../algorithms/coulomb-and-hydrogenic/).

## Model and units

Consider relative motion of two nuclei with mass numbers $A_1$ and $A_2$ and charges $Z_1e$ and $Z_2e$.
Approximate the reduced mass by $\mu c^2 = A_1A_2/(A_1 + A_2) \cdot uc^2$ (replacing nuclear masses by mass number times the atomic mass unit introduces a difference of approximately 0.1%); measure center-of-mass energy $E$ in MeV and length in fm.
Use the CODATA 2018 recommended constants (Tiesinga et al., 2021): $\hbar c = 197.3269804$ MeV fm, $e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0) = \alpha\hbar c = 1.439964548$ MeV fm, and $uc^2 = 931.49410242$ MeV.

The wavenumber $k$, Sommerfeld parameter $\eta$, and dimensionless radius $\rho = kR$ are given by:

$$
k = \frac{\sqrt{2\mu c^2 E}}{\hbar c}, \qquad
\eta = \frac{Z_1Z_2\,\alpha\hbar c\,\mu c^2}{(\hbar c)^2 k} = Z_1Z_2\,\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\mu c^2}{2E}}, \qquad
\rho = kR.
$$

Take the nuclear-force radius as $R = r_0(A_1^{1/3} + A_2^{1/3})$, with $r_0$ as an input.
The Coulomb barrier height at radius $R$ is $B = Z_1Z_2\,\alpha\hbar c/R$, with $E/B = \rho/(2\eta)$.
$\eta\rho = Z_1Z_2\,\alpha\hbar c\,\mu c^2 R/(\hbar c)^2$ is independent of energy and depends only on $R$ and the system.

## Penetrability

Only the Coulomb force acts outside $R$, so the radial function of partial wave $l$ is a linear combination of $F_l$ and $G_l$.
The ratio of outgoing-wave flux for $H_l^+ = G_l + iF_l$ to its squared amplitude at $R$ defines the barrier penetrability (the penetration factor of R-matrix theory, Lane and Thomas, 1958):

$$
P_l(E) = \frac{\rho}{F_l(\eta, \rho)^2 + G_l(\eta, \rho)^2}.
$$

Above the barrier ($E \gg B$), $F_l^2 + G_l^2 \to 1$ and $P_l \approx \rho$.
Below the barrier, $G_l$ grows exponentially and $P_l$ decreases rapidly.
For neutrons ($Z_1 = 0$), $\eta = 0$; $F_l = \rho j_l(\rho)$ and $G_l = -\rho y_l(\rho)$ give $P_l = 1/(\rho(j_l^2 + y_l^2))$, leaving only the centrifugal barrier, as in $P_0 = 1$ and $P_1 = \rho^2/(1 + \rho^2)$.

### Low-energy limit and the Gamow factor

In the limit $\eta \to \infty$ with $\eta\rho$ fixed (decreasing energy at fixed $R$), Coulomb wave functions can be expressed with modified Bessel functions (DLMF 33.10.3):

$$
G_l(\eta, \rho) \sim \frac{2(2\eta)^l}{(2l+1)!\,C_l(\eta)}\sqrt{2\eta\rho}\;K_{2l+1}\!\left(\sqrt{8\eta\rho}\right).
$$

In the same limit, $F_l$ becomes small in proportion to $C_l(\eta)$, so penetrability is approximated by $P_l \approx \rho/G_l^2$.
For $l = 0$, using $C_0(\eta)^2 \approx 2\pi\eta\,e^{-2\pi\eta}$ gives:

$$
P_0 \approx \frac{\pi}{4K_1\!\left(\sqrt{8\eta\rho}\right)^2}\,e^{-2\pi\eta}.
$$

Since $\sqrt{8\eta\rho}$ depends only on $R$ and the system, decreasing energy at fixed $R$ puts all the energy dependence of $P_0$ into the Gamow factor $e^{-2\pi\eta}$.
This is why astrophysical reaction cross sections $\sigma(E)$ are converted to $S(E) = \sigma(E)\,E\,e^{2\pi\eta}$ (the astrophysical S factor) and extrapolated as smooth functions.

## Energy dependence of penetrability

The next cell selects a system, orbital angular momentum $l$, and radius coefficient $r_0$, then evaluates `coulomb_wave` at 201 points from $E = 0.01B$ to $2B$ and plots $P_l(E)$ (for neutrons, 1 MeV replaces $B$ as the reference energy).
The dashed reference is the DLMF 33.10.3 limiting formula for charged particles or the spherical Bessel formula $1/(\rho(j_l^2 + y_l^2))$ for neutrons; these are independent references computed using `coulomb_normalization`, `bessel_k`, `spherical_jn`, and `spherical_yn`.
At the five points $E/B = 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1$, the table shows $\eta$, $\rho$, $P_l$, the ratio to the reference formula, $P_l\,e^{2\pi\eta}$ after multiplication by the Gamow factor, and the difference between the Wronskian and 1.

```rust
//| id: special-coulomb-barrier-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Two-body kinematics and penetrability"
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
use scioxide_special::{bessel_k, coulomb_normalization, coulomb_wave, spherical_jn, spherical_yn};

/// ħc [MeV fm], αħc = e²/(4πε₀) [MeV fm], uc² [MeV]（CODATA 2018）.
pub(crate) const HBAR_C: f64 = 197.326_980_4;
pub(crate) const ALPHA_HBAR_C: f64 = 1.439_964_548;
pub(crate) const AMU: f64 = 931.494_102_42;

/// Charges and mass numbers of two nuclei.
pub(crate) struct Pair {
    pub(crate) z: f64,
    pub(crate) mass: f64,
    pub(crate) radius: f64,
}

/// Construct charge product Z₁Z₂, reduced mass μc² [MeV], and radius R = r₀(A₁^⅓ + A₂^⅓) [fm] from the system name.
pub(crate) fn pair(name: &str, r0: f64) -> Pair {
    let (z1, a1, z2, a2) = match name {
        "d + t" => (1.0, 2.0, 1.0, 3.0),
        "α + ²⁰⁸Pb" => (2.0, 4.0, 82.0, 208.0),
        "n + ¹²C（η = 0）" => (0.0, 1.0, 6.0, 12.0),
        _ => (1.0, 1.0, 6.0, 12.0),
    };
    Pair {
        z: z1 * z2,
        mass: a1 * a2 / (a1 + a2) * AMU,
        radius: r0 * (f64::cbrt(a1) + f64::cbrt(a2)),
    }
}

impl Pair {
    /// Barrier height B [MeV]. For neutrons, returns the reference value 1 MeV.
    pub(crate) fn scale(&self) -> f64 {
        if self.z == 0.0 {
            1.0
        } else {
            self.z * ALPHA_HBAR_C / self.radius
        }
    }

    /// (η, ρ) at energy E [MeV].
    pub(crate) fn kinematics(&self, energy: f64) -> (f64, f64) {
        let k = (2.0 * self.mass * energy).sqrt() / HBAR_C;
        let eta = self.z * ALPHA_HBAR_C * self.mass / (HBAR_C * HBAR_C * k);
        (eta, k * self.radius)
    }
}

/// Penetrability P_l = ρ/(F² + G²) and Wronskian F′G − FG′.
pub(crate) fn penetrability(l: f64, eta: f64, rho: f64) -> Result<(f64, f64), String> {
    let [f, g, fp, gp] = coulomb_wave(l, eta, rho).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok((rho / (f * f + g * g), fp * g - f * gp))
}

/// Independent reference: 1/(ρ(j_l² + y_l²)) for η = 0, or ρ/G_l² using G_l from DLMF 33.10.3 for η > 0.
pub(crate) fn reference(l: u32, eta: f64, rho: f64) -> Result<f64, String> {
    if eta == 0.0 {
        let j = spherical_jn(l, rho).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let y = spherical_yn(l, rho).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        return Ok(1.0 / (rho * (j * j + y * y)));
    }
    let lf = f64::from(l);
    let c = coulomb_normalization(lf, eta).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let x = (8.0 * eta * rho).sqrt();
    let k = bessel_k(2.0 * lf + 1.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let factorial: f64 = (1..=2 * l + 1).map(f64::from).product();
    let g = 2.0 * (2.0 * eta).powi(l as i32) / (factorial * c) * (2.0 * eta * rho).sqrt() * k;
    Ok(rho / (g * g))
}
```

For the default p + ¹²C ($R = 3.95$ fm, $B = 2.19$ MeV), $P_0$ falls by 14 orders of magnitude, from $0.68$ at $E = B$ to $2.3 \times 10^{-15}$ at $E = 0.01B$.
The ratios to the reference formula are $0.986$ at $E/B = 0.01$, $0.86$ at $0.1$, and $0.25$ at $1$, showing that the limiting formula approaches penetrability at low energy.
Over the same range, $P_0\,e^{2\pi\eta}$ takes values $127$, $111$, and $32$, approaching the constant $\pi/(4K_1(\sqrt{8\eta\rho})^2) \approx 128$ ($\eta\rho = 0.753$) at low energy.
Thus $e^{-2\pi\eta}$ accounts for most of the 14-order variation.
The Wronskian differs from 1 by at most 0.5 $\varepsilon$ units at every point.

For α + ²⁰⁸Pb, the barrier is $B = 26.2$ MeV; penetrability is $P_0 = 2.1 \times 10^{-16}$ at $E = 7.86$ MeV ($0.3B$), near $\alpha$-decay energies, and falls to $10^{-237}$ at $E = 0.01B$.
Here $\eta\rho = 200$ is large and $\rho$ also exceeds 1, so even at $E = 0.01B$ the ratio to the limiting formula is only $0.87$.
The limiting formula is a guide for $\rho \ll 1$; accurate penetrability uses $F_l$ and $G_l$ themselves.
For neutron $l = 1$, the ratio is 1 to rounding accuracy and $P_1 = \rho^2/(1 + \rho^2)$ is reproduced at every energy.

## Penetrability by partial wave

Below the barrier, the centrifugal barrier $l(l+1)/\rho^2$ adds to the Coulomb barrier, so higher partial waves have lower penetrability.
The next cell selects a system and $E/B$, constructs `[F, G, F′, G′]` for $l = 0, \ldots, 6$ at $r_0 = 1.2$ fm together using `coulomb_wave_sequence`, and shows $P_l$ and $P_l/P_0$.
The sequence uses downward order recurrence for $F$ and upward recurrence for $G$; relative differences from separate calls to `coulomb_wave` at each order are also shown, checking recurrence accuracy together with the Wronskian.

    "E = {energy:.4} MeV, R = {:.3} fm, η = {eta:.4}, ρ = {rho:.4}",

For the default α + ²⁰⁸Pb at $E = 0.3B = 7.86$ MeV, $\rho = 11$ is large, so $P_l/P_0$ is $0.034$ even at $l = 6$, and the centrifugal barrier has a gradual effect.
For p + ¹²C at $E = 0.1B$, $\rho = 0.39$ is small; $P_l/P_0$ falls from $0.099$ at $l = 1$ to $1.2 \times 10^{-13}$ at $l = 6$.
This is why low-energy reactions need only $s$ waves (sometimes also $p$ waves).
The Wronskian differs from 1 by at most 1 $\varepsilon$ unit, and relative differences from `coulomb_wave` at each order are at most 1.5 $\varepsilon$ units; the sequence recurrence preserves scalar-evaluation accuracy.

## Summary

Barrier penetrability is computed as $P_l = \rho/(F_l^2 + G_l^2)$ from $F_l$ and $G_l$ returned by `coulomb_wave` at nuclear surface radius $\rho = kR$, becoming exponentially smaller as $E/B$ decreases.
In the low-energy limit at fixed $R$, the modified Bessel formula in DLMF 33.10.3 approximates $P_l$; the energy dependence of $P_0$ is concentrated in the Gamow factor $e^{-2\pi\eta}$.
For neutrons, $\eta = 0$ agrees with the spherical Bessel formula, and only the centrifugal barrier reduces penetrability.
When many partial waves are needed, `coulomb_wave_sequence` computes them together with the same accuracy as separate evaluations at each order.
See [Coulomb wave functions and hydrogenic radial states](../../algorithms/coulomb-and-hydrogenic/) for evaluation methods and accuracy guarantees, and [Hydrogenic radial wavefunctions and expectation values](../hydrogen-radial-states/) for bound-state computations.
