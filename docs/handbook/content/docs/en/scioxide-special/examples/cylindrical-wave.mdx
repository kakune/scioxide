Cylindrically symmetric waves are expressed by cylindrical Bessel functions, just as plane waves in Cartesian coordinates are expressed by trigonometric functions.
Standing waves in domains containing the origin and aperture diffraction patterns use Bessel functions of the first kind $J_\nu$; traveling waves propagating to infinity use Hankel functions of the first kind $H_\nu^{(1)}$.
This example computes intensity and encircled energy of the Fraunhofer diffraction pattern (Airy disk) from a telescope's circular aperture, checking dark-ring positions and resolution.
It then compares the amplitude of cylindrical waves from a line sound source in air with Hankel asymptotic forms and checks radius-independent energy flux through the Wronskian.
Definitions and evaluation methods are explained in [Cylindrical Bessel and Hankel functions](../../algorithms/airy-bessel-and-hankel/).
Natural frequencies of a circular membrane (zeros of $J_\nu$) are covered in [Circular membrane natural frequencies and Bessel zeros](../../../scioxide-rootfind/examples/circular-membrane/).

## Circular-aperture diffraction pattern

A plane wave of wavelength $\lambda$ strikes a circular aperture of diameter $D$ (radius $a = D/2$) normally; the diffraction pattern is observed sufficiently far away (or in the focal plane).
The Fraunhofer amplitude is the phase factor integrated over the aperture; using polar coordinates $(\rho, \varphi)$ in the aperture and diffraction angle $\theta$ gives:

$$
U(\theta) \propto \int_0^a \int_0^{2\pi} e^{-\mathrm{i}k\rho\sin\theta\cos\varphi}\,d\varphi\,\rho\,d\rho
= 2\pi\int_0^a J_0(k\rho\sin\theta)\,\rho\,d\rho
= 2\pi a^2\,\frac{J_1(x)}{x}.
$$

where $k = 2\pi/\lambda$ and $x = ka\sin\theta = \pi D\sin\theta/\lambda$.
The first equality uses the integral representation $J_0(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}z\cos\varphi}\,d\varphi$ of $J_0$ (DLMF §10.9); the second uses $(tJ_1(t))' = tJ_0(t)$ (DLMF §10.6).
Intensity divided by the central intensity $I_0$ is:

$$
\frac{I(x)}{I_0} = \left[\frac{2J_1(x)}{x}\right]^2 = \Lambda_1(x)^2, \qquad
\frac{2J_1(x)}{x} = 1 - \frac{x^2}{8} + \frac{x^4}{192} - \cdots
$$


$\Lambda_1$ is the Jahnke–Emde Lambda function; `bessel_lambda(1.0, x)` returns $2J_1(x)/x$ even at $x = 0$ without division by 0.

Dark rings occur at positive zeros $x = j_{1,n}$ of $J_1$; the first four are $j_{1,1} = 3.8317060$, $j_{1,2} = 7.0155867$, $j_{1,3} = 10.1734681$, and $j_{1,4} = 13.3236919$ (Abramowitz and Stegun, 1964, Table 9.5).
To compute zeros to any desired number of digits, use `special_zeros` from `scioxide-rootfind`, as described in [Circular membrane natural frequencies and Bessel zeros](../../../scioxide-rootfind/examples/circular-membrane/).
The angular radius $\theta_1$ of the first dark ring satisfies

$$
\sin\theta_1 = \frac{j_{1,1}}{\pi}\,\frac{\lambda}{D} = 1.2197\,\frac{\lambda}{D}
$$

and Rayleigh resolution $\theta_R \approx 1.22\lambda/D$ is the criterion that two point-source images are resolved when their centers are separated by $\theta_1$.
For a 100 mm telescope at $\lambda = 550$ nm, $\theta_1 = 6.71 \times 10^{-6}$ rad $= 1.38$ arcsec.

## Encircled energy

Encircled energy is the fraction of light inside a circle of angular radius $x$ about the center.
Integrating intensity over image-plane area with weight $x\,dx$ gives (Rayleigh, 1881):

$$
E(x) = \frac{\int_0^x [2J_1(t)/t]^2\,t\,dt}{\int_0^\infty [2J_1(t)/t]^2\,t\,dt} = 2\int_0^x \frac{J_1(t)^2}{t}\,dt = 1 - J_0(x)^2 - J_1(x)^2
$$


The final equality follows because $J_0' = -J_1$ and $J_1' = J_0 - J_1/x$ (DLMF 10.6.2) give $\frac{d}{dx}[1 - J_0^2 - J_1^2] = 2J_1^2/x$ and both sides vanish at $x = 0$.
At dark rings, $J_1 = 0$, so $E(j_{1,n}) = 1 - J_0(j_{1,n})^2$; approximately 84% of the total lies inside the first dark ring (the Airy disk).

## Computing the diffraction pattern

The next cell selects wavelength $\lambda$ [nm] and aperture diameter $D$ [mm], varies diffraction angle $\theta$ from 0 to 6 arcsec, and plots relative intensity $\Lambda_1(x)^2$ and encircled energy $1 - J_0^2 - J_1^2$.
The two plots share horizontal coordinate $\theta$ and are linked; a vertical line marks Rayleigh resolution $1.22\lambda/D$.
Intensity varies from 1 to below $10^{-4}$ and is plotted on a logarithmic axis, with dark rings appearing as troughs.

For each dark ring, the first table shows the reference zero $j_{1,n}$, the sign-changing interval of $J_1$ found by scanning $x$ in steps of 0.05, $\lvert J_1\rvert$ at the reference zero, encircled energy, and angular radius $\theta_n$.
The second table provides independent checks: the exact $\theta_1$ and $1.22\lambda/D$, the difference between Simpson integration of $2\int_0^{j_{1,1}} J_1(t)^2/t\,dt$ and the closed form $1 - J_0^2$, and the difference between $\Lambda_1$ and the series $1 - x^2/8 + x^4/192$ at $x = 0.1$.
The latter difference is approximately the next omitted term $x^6/9216 \approx 1.09 \times 10^{-10}$.
Simpson's rule and the series are manual computations for verification; integrand values come from the APIs.

```rust
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":1.085e-10,"rtol":2e-3}}
//|   - case: large-aperture
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rings","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":0.34591558898547825,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: red-light
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"rings","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":3.52204963347619,"rtol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_special::{bessel_j, bessel_lambda};

// x = πD sinθ/λ. Convert D [mm] and λ [nm] to m.
let arcsec = std::f64::consts::PI / (180.0 * 3600.0);
let scale = std::f64::consts::PI * (diameter * 1e-3) / (wavelength * 1e-9);
let x_of = |theta_arcsec: f64| scale * RealScalar::sin(theta_arcsec * arcsec);
let encircled = |x: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let j0 = bessel_j(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let j1 = bessel_j(1.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(1.0 - j0 * j0 - j1 * j1)
};

let mut intensity = Vec::new();
let mut energy = Vec::new();
for index in 0..=600 {
    let theta = 6.0 * f64::from(index) / 600.0;
    let x = x_of(theta);
    // Λ₁(x) = 2J₁(x)/x is defined even at x = 0.
    let amplitude = bessel_lambda(1.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if amplitude != 0.0 {
        intensity.push([theta, amplitude * amplitude]);
    }
    energy.push([theta, encircled(x)?]);
}
let rayleigh = 1.22 * (wavelength * 1e-9) / (diameter * 1e-3) / arcsec;
let marker = serde_json::json!([{
    "kind": "line",
    "axis": "x",
    "value": rayleigh,
    "label": "1.22λ/D"
}]);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "I/I₀ = Λ₁(x)²", "points": intensity })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "airy-angle" },
        "xLabel": "Diffraction angle θ [arcsec]",
        "yLabel": "Relative intensity I/I₀ [-]",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 6.0,
        "yMin": 1e-6,
        "yMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": marker
    })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "1 − J₀² − J₁²", "points": energy })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "airy-angle" },
        "xLabel": "Diffraction angle θ [arcsec]",
        "yLabel": "Encircled energy E [-]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 6.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": marker
    })
);

// Dark rings: reference zeros (Abramowitz and Stegun, Table 9.5) and sign changes found by scanning.
let zeros: [f64; 4] = [
    3.8317059702075123,
    7.0155866698156188,
    10.173468135062722,
    13.323691936314223,
];
let mut brackets = Vec::new();
let mut previous = (0.05, bessel_j(1.0, 0.05).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
for step in 2..=300 {
    let x = 0.05 * f64::from(step);
    let value = bessel_j(1.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if value.signum() != previous.1.signum() {
        brackets.push((previous.0, x));
    }
    previous = (x, value);
}
let mut rings = Vec::new();
for (n, (&zero, &(left, right))) in zeros.iter().zip(&brackets).enumerate() {
    let residual = bessel_j(1.0, zero).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.abs();
    let theta = RealScalar::asin(zero / scale) / arcsec;
    rings.push((n + 1, zero, left, right, residual, encircled(zero)?, theta));
}
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let sig5 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 5 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Dark ring n", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Reference zero j₁,ₙ [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Sign-change left endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Sign-change right endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "|J₁(j₁,ₙ)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Encircled energy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Angular radius θₙ [arcsec]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rings,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "rings" })
);

// Check 1: Simpson's rule for 2∫₀^{j₁,₁} J₁(t)²/t dt = ∫ t Λ₁(t)²/2 dt (manual computation).
let (upper, panels) = (zeros[0], 2000);
let h = upper / f64::from(panels);
let mut simpson = 0.0;
for i in 0..=panels {
    let t = h * f64::from(i);
    let lambda = bessel_lambda(1.0, t).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let weight = if i == 0 || i == panels {
        1.0
    } else if i % 2 == 1 {
        4.0
    } else {
        2.0
    };
    simpson += weight * 0.5 * t * lambda * lambda;
}
simpson *= h / 3.0;
// Check 2: series 1 − x²/8 + x⁴/192 at x = 0.1. The next omitted term is x⁶/9216 ≈ 1.09 × 10⁻¹⁰.
let small = 0.1_f64;
let series = 1.0 - small * small / 8.0 + small.powi(4) / 192.0;
let lambda_small = bessel_lambda(1.0, small).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let exact_theta = rings[0].6;
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "θ₁ (exact) [arcsec]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "1.22λ/D [arcsec]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Simpson E(j₁,₁) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig5 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Closed-form discrepancy [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Difference from series at x = 0.1 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        exact_theta,
        rayleigh,
        simpson,
        (simpson - encircled(upper)?).abs(),
        (lambda_small - series).abs(),
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);
```

With defaults $\lambda = 550$ nm and $D = 100$ mm, the first dark ring lies at $\theta_1 = 1.3837$ arcsec.
The relative difference $3 \times 10^{-4}$ from $1.22\lambda/D = 1.3840$ arcsec comes from rounding $j_{1,1}/\pi = 1.2197$ to the coefficient 1.22.
All four scanned sign-changing intervals contain the reference zeros; $\lvert J_1\rvert$ at those zeros is of order $10^{-16}$.
Encircled energy increases in steps at the dark rings to 0.838, 0.910, 0.938, and 0.952; each bright ring between them carries a few percent.
The difference between Simpson's rule and the closed form is $3.5 \times 10^{-14}$, numerically confirming that $1 - J_0^2 - J_1^2$ equals the intensity integral.
The series difference is $1.08 \times 10^{-10}$, matching the size of the omitted term.
Quadrupling diameter divides every angle by four; intensity and encircled energy keep the same shapes as functions of $x$.

## Cylindrical waves from a line sound source

Next consider sound pressure spreading through air from an infinitely long line source oscillating at angular frequency $\omega$.
Pressure $p(r, t) = \operatorname{Re}[P\,H_0^{(1)}(kr)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}]$ satisfies the two-dimensional Helmholtz equation; with time factor $e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}$, $H_0^{(1)}(kr)$ describes an outgoing wave (Sommerfeld radiation condition).
The wavenumber is $k = \omega/c = 2\pi f/c$; take sound speed $c = 343$ m/s (air at 20 °C).

In the far field, where $kr$ is large, the Hankel asymptotic expansion applies (DLMF 10.17.5):

$$
H_0^{(1)}(x) \sim \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\,e^{\mathrm{i}(x - \pi/4)}\left(1 - \frac{\mathrm{i}}{8x} - \frac{9}{128x^2} + \cdots\right).
$$

Amplitude decreases as $\sqrt{2/(\pi kr)}$, or $r^{-1/2}$; relative error decreases as $1/(8x)$ with only the leading term and as $x^{-3}$ with three terms.

The $r^{-1/2}$ amplitude decay follows from energy conservation.
Particle velocity is $v = (\partial p/\partial r)/(\mathrm{i}\omega\rho)$ ($\rho$ is air density), giving time-averaged intensity $\bar{I} = \frac{\lvert P\rvert^2 k}{2\omega\rho}\operatorname{Im}[\overline{H_0^{(1)}}\,H_0^{(1)\prime}](kr)$.
Substituting $H_0^{(1)} = J_0 + \mathrm{i}Y_0$ makes the imaginary part equal to the Wronskian $J_0Y_0' - J_0'Y_0 = 2/(\pi kr)$.
Therefore, under the condition

$$
F(r) = kr\,\operatorname{Im}\left[\overline{H_0^{(1)}(kr)}\,H_0^{(1)\prime}(kr)\right] = \frac{2}{\pi}, \qquad
2\pi r\,\bar{I} = \frac{2\lvert P\rvert^2}{\omega\rho}
$$

The power per unit length through a cylindrical surface of radius $r$ is therefore independent of $r$.
This flux is conserved even where near-field amplitude departs from $r^{-1/2}$.

## Computing cylindrical waves

The next cell selects frequency $f$ [Hz], computes $\lvert H_0^{(1)}(kr)\rvert$ from $r = 0.02$ m to 20 m using `hankel_1`, and compares it with the leading amplitude $\sqrt{2/(\pi kr)}$ on logarithmic axes.
The second plot shows relative errors between $e^{-\mathrm{i}kr}H_0^{(1)}(kr)$ returned by `hankel_1_scaled` and the leading and three-term asymptotic approximations.
The plots share horizontal coordinate $r$ and are linked.
At $r = 0.1, 1, 10$ m, the table shows $kr$, $\lvert H_0^{(1)}\rvert$, the two relative errors, relative differences between flux $F$ and $2/\pi$, and relative Wronskian residuals using `bessel_j_derivative` and `bessel_y_derivative`.
Flux is computed from `hankel_1` and `hankel_1_derivative`.

```rust
//| id: special-hankel-cylindrical-radiation
//| ref: special-hankel-cylindrical-radiation
//| title: "Line-source cylindrical-wave amplitude and energy flux"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   frequency: {"type":"range","label":"Frequency f [Hz]","description":"Vary the wavenumber k = 2πf/c.","min":100,"max":2000,"step":50,"value":500}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"low-frequency","label":"f = 100 Hz","description":"At wavelength 3.4 m, even 1 m is in the near field, with leading-term error above 5%.","inputs":{"frequency":100}}
//|   - {"id":"high-frequency","label":"f = 2000 Hz","description":"At wavelength 17 cm, leading-term error is within 1% beyond 0.35 m.","inputs":{"frequency":2000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":9.159162255363828,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.2634477792037513,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.01359,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/1/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: low-frequency
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"min":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: high-frequency
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"radiation","path":"/rows/2/4","range":{"max":1e-8}}
use scioxide_core::{Complex, RealScalar};
use scioxide_special::{
    bessel_j, bessel_j_derivative, bessel_y, bessel_y_derivative, hankel_1,
    hankel_1_derivative, hankel_1_scaled,
};

let sound_speed = 343.0;
let k = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * frequency / sound_speed;
let pi = std::f64::consts::PI;
// Leading and three-term asymptotic approximations (DLMF 10.17.5), with e^{ix} removed.
let leading = |x: f64| Complex::new(1.0, -1.0).scale((1.0 / (pi * x)).sqrt());
let three_terms =
    |x: f64| leading(x) * Complex::new(1.0 - 9.0 / (128.0 * x * x), -1.0 / (8.0 * x));

let mut amplitude = Vec::new();
let mut envelope = Vec::new();
let mut first_error = Vec::new();
let mut third_error = Vec::new();
for index in 0..=300 {
    // Logarithmically spaced from r = 0.02 m to 20 m.
    let r = 0.02 * RealScalar::powf(1000.0_f64, f64::from(index) / 300.0);
    let x = k * r;
    let h = hankel_1(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let scaled = hankel_1_scaled(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    amplitude.push([r, h.norm()]);
    envelope.push([r, (2.0 / (pi * x)).sqrt()]);
    first_error.push([r, (scaled - leading(x)).norm() / scaled.norm()]);
    third_error.push([r, (scaled - three_terms(x)).norm() / scaled.norm()]);
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "|H₀⁽¹⁾(kr)|", "points": amplitude }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "√(2/(πkr))",
            "points": envelope,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "radial-distance" },
        "xLabel": "Distance r [m]",
        "yLabel": "Pressure amplitude |p|/|P| [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 0.02,
        "xMax": 20.0,
        "yMin": 0.01,
        "yMax": 10.0
    })
);
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Leading term", "points": first_error }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Three-term sum", "points": third_error }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "link": { "group": "radial-distance" },
        "xLabel": "Distance r [m]",
        "yLabel": "Asymptotic relative error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 0.02,
        "xMax": 20.0,
        "yMin": 1e-12,
        "yMax": 10.0
    })
);

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for r in [0.1, 1.0, 10.0] {
    let x = k * r;
    let h = hankel_1(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let dh = hankel_1_derivative(0.0, x, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let scaled = hankel_1_scaled(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Energy flux F = x Im(conj(H) H') equals 2/π.
    let flux = x * (h.conj() * dh).im;
    let j = bessel_j(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let y = bessel_y(0.0, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let dj = bessel_j_derivative(0.0, x, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let dy = bessel_y_derivative(0.0, x, 1).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let wronskian = (j * dy - dj * y) * pi * x / 2.0 - 1.0;
    rows.push((
        r,
        x,
        h.norm(),
        (scaled - leading(x)).norm() / scaled.norm(),
        (scaled - three_terms(x)).norm() / scaled.norm(),
        (flux * pi / 2.0 - 1.0).abs(),
        wronskian.abs(),
    ));
}
let sig4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Distance r [m]", "type": "number" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "kr [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "|H₀⁽¹⁾(kr)| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Leading-term relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Three-term relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "|F/(2/π) − 1| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
```

At default $f = 500$ Hz (wavelength 0.69 m), $r = 1$ m gives $kr = 9.16$ and $\lvert H_0^{(1)}\rvert = 0.264$, with leading-term relative error $1/(8kr) \approx 1.4 \times 10^{-2}$.
On logarithmic axes, the far-field amplitude follows a straight line of slope $-1/2$; near-field values lie below it.
As $kr \to 0$, $\lvert H_0^{(1)}\rvert$ grows only logarithmically like $\lvert Y_0(kr)\rvert \approx (2/\pi)\lvert\ln(kr/2) + \gamma\rvert$ ($\gamma$ is Euler's constant), slower than $(kr)^{-1/2}$.
In the relative-error plot, leading-term error decreases with slope $-1$ and three-term error with slope $-3$; neither expansion applies in the near field where $kr$ is small.
Relative residuals of flux $F$ and the Wronskian are at most $5 \times 10^{-16}$ in both near and far fields, demonstrating flux conservation even where near-field amplitude departs from $r^{-1/2}$.
Changing frequency only shifts the plots horizontally to positions with the same $kr$; error magnitude depends only on $kr$.

## Summary

Circular-aperture diffraction amplitude is $2J_1(x)/x$; using `bessel_lambda(1.0, x)` lets you compute intensity $\Lambda_1(x)^2$ without special treatment of $x = 0$.
Encircled energy has closed form $1 - J_0^2 - J_1^2$; approximately 84% lies inside the first dark ring $j_{1,1} = 3.8317$, whose angular radius $1.22\lambda/D$ gives Rayleigh resolution.
When zero values themselves are needed, compute them with `special_zeros` in `scioxide-rootfind`; within the cell, check sign changes and small $\lvert J_1\rvert$.
The far-field amplitude of a cylindrical wave $H_0^{(1)}(kr)$ approaches $\sqrt{2/(\pi kr)}$, with leading-term error approximately $1/(8kr)$; in the near field, use `hankel_1` values instead of the asymptotic form.
Energy flux $kr\operatorname{Im}[\overline{H}H'] = 2/\pi$ is the Wronskian itself, providing a check of consistency between values and derivatives.
See [Spherical Bessel, spherical Hankel, and Riccati–Bessel functions](../../algorithms/spherical-bessel-kelvin-and-riccati/) for three-dimensional spherical waves, and [Neutron quantization in gravity and Airy functions](../airy-turning-point/) for waves with turning points.
