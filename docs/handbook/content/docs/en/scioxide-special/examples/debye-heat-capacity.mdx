The molar heat capacity of a monatomic solid at room temperature is approximately $3R \approx 24.94$ J/(mol·K) (the Dulong–Petit law).
The classical equipartition theorem predicts this value at every temperature, but heat capacity decreases toward 0 at low temperature, and hard, light crystals such as diamond remain far below $3R$ even at room temperature.
Debye (1912) quantized lattice vibrations as a collection of sound waves with a maximum-frequency cutoff and expressed the temperature dependence of heat capacity using a single function $D_3$.
This example evaluates Debye-model heat capacity using both `debye_3` and `transport_4` in scioxide-special and numerically checks the temperature ranges in which the two limiting laws and the high-temperature expansion apply.

## Heat capacity in the Debye model

Treat the lattice vibrations of a crystal containing $N$ atoms as a collection of harmonic oscillators with frequencies $\omega$.
The Debye model takes the frequency distribution (density of states) to be that of sound waves in a continuum, $g(\omega) \propto \omega^2$, and truncates it at a frequency $\omega_D$ that gives a total of $3N$ degrees of freedom.

$$
g(\omega) = \frac{9N}{\omega_D^3}\,\omega^2 \quad (0 \le \omega \le \omega_D), \qquad \int_0^{\omega_D} g(\omega)\,d\omega = 3N
$$

Define the Debye temperature $\Theta_D$ by $k_B\Theta_D = \hbar\omega_D$, where $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant and $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant.
The mean thermal energy of an oscillator with frequency $\omega$ is $\hbar\omega/(e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT} - 1)$, so setting $t = \hbar\omega/k_BT$ and $x_D = \Theta_D/T$ gives the lattice internal energy as

$$
U(T) = \int_0^{\omega_D} \frac{\hbar\omega\, g(\omega)}{e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT} - 1}\,d\omega
= 9Nk_BT \left(\frac{T}{\Theta_D}\right)^3 \int_0^{x_D} \frac{t^3}{e^t - 1}\,dt
= 3Nk_BT\,D_3(x_D)
$$


The zero-point energy $\tfrac{9}{8}Nk_B\Theta_D$ is omitted because it is independent of temperature.
Here $D_3(x) = (3/x^3)\int_0^x t^3/(e^t - 1)\,dt$ is the third-order Debye function; scioxide-special's `debye_3` returns this normalization, with $D_3(0) = 1$ ([Debye functions](oxref:special-thermal-debye)).

Using $D_3'(x) = -3D_3(x)/x + 3/(e^x - 1)$, the constant-volume heat capacity $C_V = dU/dT$ becomes

$$
\frac{C_V}{3Nk_B} = D_3(x_D) - x_D D_3'(x_D) = 4D_3(x_D) - \frac{3x_D}{e^{x_D} - 1}
$$


Differentiating $U$ with respect to $T$ before integrating gives the same quantity, which can also be expressed using the transport integral $J_4(x) = \int_0^x t^4 e^t/(e^t - 1)^2\,dt$ as

$$
\frac{C_V}{3Nk_B} = \frac{3J_4(x_D)}{x_D^3}
$$


Integration by parts relates these two expressions identically, but scioxide-special implements `debye_3` and `transport_4` using separate coefficient tables and tail sums, so agreement between them checks the implementations.
For 1 mol of atoms, $3Nk_B = 3R$, where $R = 8.314462618$ J/(mol·K) is the gas constant.

## The two limits

At high temperature ($x_D \to 0$), substituting the series for $D_3$ gives

$$
\frac{C_V}{3R} = 1 - \frac{x_D^2}{20} + \frac{x_D^4}{560} - \cdots
$$


The limit 1 gives the Dulong–Petit law $C_V = 3R$, and the series converges for $x_D < 2\pi$.

At low temperature ($x_D \to \infty$), $J_4(\infty) = 4!\,\zeta(4) = 4\pi^4/15$ gives

$$
\frac{C_V}{3R} \approx \frac{4\pi^4}{5x_D^3}, \qquad C_V \approx \frac{12\pi^4}{5}\,R \left(\frac{T}{\Theta_D}\right)^3 \approx 1943.8 \left(\frac{T}{\Theta_D}\right)^3 \text{ J/(mol·K)}
$$

which is Debye's $T^3$ law.
The omitted tail $\int_{x_D}^\infty t^4 e^t/(e^t - 1)^2\,dt$ decreases like $x_D^4 e^{-x_D}$, so the relative error of the $T^3$ law decreases exponentially as temperature decreases.

In metals, there is a low-temperature range in which the conduction-electron heat capacity (proportional to temperature) exceeds the lattice heat capacity $\propto T^3$; this example models only the lattice contribution.

## Debye temperatures of materials

The values of $\Theta_D$ below come from the table in Chapter 5 of Kittel, *Introduction to Solid State Physics* (8th ed., 2005), and were determined from low-temperature heat-capacity measurements.

| Material | $\Theta_D$ [K] | $x_D$ at room temperature, 300 K |
|---|---|---|
| Lead Pb | 105 | 0.35 |
| Copper Cu | 343 | 1.14 |
| Aluminum Al | 428 | 1.43 |
| Diamond C | 2230 | 7.43 |

The frequency distribution of a real crystal differs from $\omega^2$, so the value of $\Theta_D$ that fits its heat capacity changes slightly with temperature.
Applying a value of $\Theta_D$ determined at low temperature to room temperature can produce a departure from measured heat capacity.
This example examines the properties of the Debye model itself, defined by a single $\Theta_D$.

## Heat-capacity curves and two evaluations

The next cell plots $C_V(T)$ for the four materials from 1 to 1000 K and tabulates each material's $C_V$ at the input temperature $T$.
The table compares the two values $4D_3 - 3x_D/(e^{x_D} - 1)$ (`debye_3`) and $3J_4/x_D^3$ (`transport_4`), their relative difference, the $T^3$ law, and the three-term high-temperature expansion $1 - x_D^2/20 + x_D^4/560$.
The high-temperature expansion is meaningless outside its convergence radius $2\pi$, so rows with $x_D \ge 2\pi$ leave that entry blank.

```rust
//| id: special-debye-heat-capacity-materials
//| ref: special-debye-heat-capacity-materials
//| title: "Evaluate Debye heat capacity for four materials using debye_3 and transport_4"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   temperature: {"type":"range","label":"Temperature T [K]","description":"Temperature used for the table comparison, shown by a vertical line in the chart.","min":1,"max":1000,"step":1,"value":300}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"cold","label":"T = 20 K","description":"Except for Pb, x_D ≥ 17 and the relative difference from the T³ law is at most 2×10⁻⁴. Pb has x_D = 5.25 and departs substantially from the T³ law.","inputs":{"temperature":20}}
//|   - {"id":"hot","label":"T = 1000 K","description":"Except for diamond, the relative difference from the three-term high-temperature expansion is at most 10⁻⁶, and C_V exceeds 99 % of 3R.","inputs":{"temperature":1000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":23.39,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/3/3","approx":{"value":4.1332,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/3/7","equals":null}
//|   - case: cold
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/1/3","approx":{"value":0.385289,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/3/3","approx":{"value":1.40223e-3,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/3/6","approx":{"value":1.40223e-3,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|   - case: hot
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/0/8","approx":{"value":0.99945,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"heat","path":"/rows/3/3","approx":{"value":19.695,"atol":1e-3}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_special::{debye_3, transport_4};

let gas_constant = 8.314462618; // R [J/(mol·K)]
let three_r = 3.0 * gas_constant;
// Debye temperatures [K] (table in Chapter 5 of Kittel 2005).
let materials = [
    ("Lead Pb", 105.0),
    ("Copper Cu", 343.0),
    ("Aluminum Al", 428.0),
    ("Diamond C", 2230.0),
];
// C_V/(3R) = 4D₃(x) − 3x/(eˣ − 1). Evaluate x/(eˣ − 1) with exp_m1 to avoid cancellation.
let ratio = |x: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let d3 = debye_3(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(4.0 * d3 - 3.0 * x / RealScalar::exp_m1(x))
};
let mut series = Vec::new();
for (name, theta) in materials {
    let mut points = Vec::new();
    for i in 0..=200 {
        let t = 1.0 + 999.0 * i as f64 / 200.0;
        points.push([t, three_r * ratio(theta / t)?]);
    }
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points }));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Temperature T [K]",
        "yLabel": "Constant-volume molar heat capacity C_V [J/(mol·K)]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 1000.0,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 27.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": three_r, "label": "3R (Dulong–Petit)" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": temperature, "label": "Input T" }
        ]
    })
);

let pi4 = std::f64::consts::PI.powi(4);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, theta) in materials {
    let x = theta / temperature;
    let via_debye = three_r * ratio(x)?;
    // Independent route: C_V/(3R) = 3J₄(x)/x³ (implemented with separate coefficient tables and tail sums).
    let via_transport = three_r * 3.0 * transport_4(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? / x.powi(3);
    // References: the low-temperature T³ law and three-term high-temperature expansion (manual formulas).
    let cubic = three_r * 4.0 * pi4 / (5.0 * x.powi(3));
    let high = (x < 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI)
        .then(|| three_r * (1.0 - x * x / 20.0 + x.powi(4) / 560.0));
    rows.push((
        name,
        theta,
        x,
        via_debye,
        via_transport,
        (via_debye - via_transport).abs() / via_debye,
        cubic,
        high,
        via_debye / three_r,
    ));
}
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sig6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Material" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Θ_D [K]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 0 } },
    { "key": "2", "label": "x_D = Θ_D/T [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "C_V (debye_3)[J/(mol·K)]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "C_V (transport_4)[J/(mol·K)]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative difference between evaluations", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "T³ law [J/(mol·K)]", "type": "number", "format": sig6 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Three-term high-temperature expansion [J/(mol·K)]", "type": "number", "format": sig6, "nullText": "x_D ≥ 2π" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "C_V/3R [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 4 } }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "heat" })
);
```

The four curves have the same shape and are stretched horizontally in proportion to $\Theta_D$.
At the default 300 K, $C_V$ is 24.79 J/(mol·K) for lead (99.4 % of $3R$), 23.39 (93.8 %) for copper, 22.58 (90.5 %) for aluminum, and 4.13 (16.6 %) for diamond.
The relative difference between the values from `debye_3` and `transport_4` is at most $5 \times 10^{-16}$ in every row: they agree at the level of double-precision rounding.
For diamond, even at $x_D = 7.43$, the $T^3$-law value 4.73 exceeds the model value by 14 %, so room temperature is still outside the range of the $T^3$ law.

At 20 K (`cold`), copper's $C_V = 0.3853$ J/(mol·K) differs from the $T^3$ law by a relative $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$, while diamond's $1.402 \times 10^{-3}$ J/(mol·K) agrees with the $T^3$ law at the rounding level.
Lead, with its low $\Theta_D$, has $x_D = 5.25$; the $T^3$-law value (13.4) exceeds the model value (8.48) by 58 %.
At 1000 K (`hot`), $C_V/3R$ exceeds 0.99 for lead, copper, and aluminum, while diamond reaches only 0.790; even the three-term high-temperature expansion differs by 0.75 % for diamond ($x_D = 2.23$).
The two evaluations use the same $x_D$, so disagreement beyond rounding indicates an implementation error.
Their agreement cannot detect input errors such as confusing $x_D = \Theta_D/T$ with $T/\Theta_D$, so also compare against the $T^3$ law and the $3R$ limit.

## Temperature ranges for the limiting laws

Since $C_V/3R$ depends only on $T/\Theta_D$, the same curve applies to every material.
The next cell varies $T/\Theta_D$ on a logarithmic grid from $10^{-2}$ to $10$ and plots on logarithmic axes the departures of the $T^3$ law, the three-term high-temperature expansion, and the Dulong–Petit value 1 from the value given by `debye_3`.
For the input relative tolerance, it finds the endpoints of the $T/\Theta_D$ ranges where each approximation meets the tolerance by linear interpolation on the grid and lists them in a table.
The table also gives the maximum relative difference between `debye_3` and `transport_4` on the same grid.

```rust
//| id: special-debye-heat-capacity-limits
//| ref: special-debye-heat-capacity-limits
//| title: "Temperature ranges for the T³ law, high-temperature expansion, and Dulong–Petit law"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   tolerance_percent: {"type":"select","label":"Relative tolerance","description":"Allowed relative departure of the approximation from the Debye-model value.","options":[{"label":"10 %","value":"10"},{"label":"1 %","value":"1"},{"label":"0.1 %","value":"0.1"}],"value":"1"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"loose","label":"10 %","description":"The T³ law applies up to T/Θ_D ≈ 0.12, and the Dulong–Petit value applies from T/Θ_D ≈ 0.72.","inputs":{"tolerance_percent":"10"}}
//|   - {"id":"strict","label":"0.1 %","description":"The T³-law range shrinks to T/Θ_D ≈ 0.068; the Dulong–Petit value does not meet the tolerance until T/Θ_D reaches about 7.","inputs":{"tolerance_percent":"0.1"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"ranges","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0869,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ranges","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":2.24,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ranges","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: loose
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"ranges","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.1246,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"ranges","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":0.717,"atol":0.005}}
//|   - case: strict
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"ranges","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0680,"atol":5e-4}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_special::{debye_3, transport_4};

let tolerance = tolerance_percent
    .parse::<f64>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    / 100.0;
let pi4 = std::f64::consts::PI.powi(4);
let mut cubic_error = Vec::new();
let mut series_error = Vec::new();
let mut classical_error = Vec::new();
let mut identity = 0.0_f64;
let mut exact_zeros = 0;
for i in 0..=300 {
    let reduced = 1e-2 * 1e3_f64.powf(i as f64 / 300.0); // T/Θ_D
    let x = 1.0 / reduced;
    let exact = 4.0 * debye_3(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - 3.0 * x / RealScalar::exp_m1(x);
    let via_transport = 3.0 * transport_4(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? / x.powi(3);
    identity = identity.max((exact - via_transport).abs() / exact);
    // Relative differences between reference approximations (manual formulas) and the Debye-model value.
    // Points with exactly zero relative difference cannot appear on a logarithmic axis, so omit and count them.
    let cubic = (4.0 * pi4 / (5.0 * x.powi(3)) - exact).abs() / exact;
    if cubic > 0.0 {
        cubic_error.push([reduced, cubic]);
    } else {
        exact_zeros += 1;
    }
    if x < 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI {
        let series = 1.0 - x * x / 20.0 + x.powi(4) / 560.0;
        series_error.push([reduced, (series - exact).abs() / exact]);
    }
    classical_error.push([reduced, (1.0 - exact).abs() / exact]);
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "T³ law", "points": cubic_error }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Three-term high-temperature expansion", "points": series_error }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Dulong–Petit (1)", "points": classical_error })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "T/Θ_D [-]",
        "yLabel": "Relative difference from Debye-model value [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "yMin": 1e-16,
        "yMax": 1e5,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": tolerance, "label": "Relative tolerance" }]
    })
);

// Find T/Θ_D where the relative difference crosses the tolerance by linear interpolation between adjacent grid points.
let crossing = |points: &[[f64; 2]]| {
    points.windows(2).find_map(|pair| {
        let ([x0, e0], [x1, e1]) = (pair[0], pair[1]);
        ((e0 - tolerance) * (e1 - tolerance) <= 0.0)
            .then(|| x0 + (tolerance - e0) * (x1 - x0) / (e1 - e0))
    })
};
let rows = vec![
    ("Upper endpoint where the T³ law meets the tolerance", crossing(&cubic_error)),
    ("Lower endpoint where the three-term high-temperature expansion meets the tolerance", crossing(&series_error)),
    (
        "Lower endpoint where Dulong–Petit meets the tolerance",
        crossing(&classical_error),
    ),
    ("Maximum relative difference between debye_3 and transport_4", Some(identity)),
    (
        "Number of points omitted because the T³-law relative difference is zero",
        Some(exact_zeros as f64),
    ),
];
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 3 }, "nullText": "No crossing within the grid" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "ranges" })
);
```

At the default relative tolerance of 1 %, the $T^3$ law meets the tolerance for $T/\Theta_D \le 0.087$, the three-term high-temperature expansion for $T/\Theta_D \ge 0.43$, and the Dulong–Petit value for $T/\Theta_D \ge 2.24$.
For copper ($\Theta_D = 343$ K), the $T^3$ law applies up to about 30 K, and the Dulong–Petit value from about 770 K.
The plotted relative difference of the $T^3$ law decreases exponentially as $T/\Theta_D$ decreases and levels off at the rounding level of about $10^{-16}$ below $T/\Theta_D \approx 0.02$.
Points whose relative difference is exactly 0 cannot appear on a logarithmic axis, so they are omitted and counted in the table (the count depends on rounding in the execution environment and is 17 in browser wasm).
The relative difference between `debye_3` and `transport_4` is at most $1.2 \times 10^{-15}$ at all 301 grid points.

Relaxing the tolerance to 10 % (`loose`) expands the upper endpoint for the $T^3$ law to 0.125 and the lower endpoint for Dulong–Petit to 0.717; tightening it to 0.1 % (`strict`) narrows the $T^3$-law range to 0.068 and the Dulong–Petit range to 7.07 and above.
The Dulong–Petit lower endpoint depends strongly on the tolerance because the high-temperature difference decreases only as a power, like $x_D^2/20 = (\Theta_D/T)^2/20$.
The upper endpoint for the $T^3$ law moves less because its error varies exponentially, like $x_D^4 e^{-x_D}$.

## Summary

The constant-volume heat capacity in the Debye model is $C_V/3R = 4D_3(\Theta_D/T) - 3x_D/(e^{x_D} - 1)$; `debye_3` and `exp_m1` alone evaluate it at every temperature, and $3J_4(x_D)/x_D^3$ (`transport_4`) provides a cross-check.
Heat capacity depends only on $T/\Theta_D$, so differences between materials appear through $\Theta_D$; at room temperature, lead is close to $3R$, while diamond reaches only about one sixth of $3R$.
At a relative tolerance of 1 %, the $T^3$ law applies for $T \lesssim 0.087\,\Theta_D$ and the Dulong–Petit value for $T \gtrsim 2.2\,\Theta_D$; temperatures between them require the Debye-function value itself.
See [Debye functions, transport integrals, and synchrotron functions](../../algorithms/thermal-and-radiation-integrals/) for evaluation methods and accuracy guarantees, and scioxide-integrate's [blackbody radiation example](../../../scioxide-integrate/examples/blackbody-radiation/) for integrating the same integrand over a semi-infinite interval.
