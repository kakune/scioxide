Conduction electrons in metals and electrons in heavily doped semiconductors fill levels up to high energies even at low temperature because of the Pauli exclusion principle: they are degenerate.
Given the electron number density $n$ and temperature $T$, the chemical potential $\mu$ of the Fermi–Dirac distribution is determined by requiring that the electron count integrated over the distribution equal $n$.
This condition is an equation containing the Fermi–Dirac integral $F_{1/2}$ and has no closed-form solution for $\mu$.
This example passes scioxide-special's `fermi_dirac_half` and its derivative `fermi_dirac_mhalf` to scioxide-rootfind's safeguarded Newton method to find $\mu$, then checks it against the low-temperature Sommerfeld expansion and the high-temperature classical limit.

## Particle-number condition and Fermi–Dirac integrals

The three-dimensional density of states per unit volume for free electrons of mass $m$ and spin $1/2$ is $g(\epsilon) = \dfrac{1}{2\pi^2}\left(\dfrac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}\epsilon^{1/2}$, including both spin states.
Counting occupations with the Fermi–Dirac distribution $f(\epsilon) = 1/(e^{(\epsilon - \mu)/k_BT} + 1)$ at temperature $T$ and chemical potential $\mu$, and setting $t = \epsilon/k_BT$ and $\eta = \mu/k_BT$, gives

$$
n = \int_0^\infty g(\epsilon) f(\epsilon)\,d\epsilon
= \frac{1}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{2mk_BT}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2} \int_0^\infty \frac{t^{1/2}}{e^{t-\eta} + 1}\,dt
$$


scioxide-special normalizes its Fermi–Dirac integrals by $\Gamma(j+1)$ as $F_j(\eta) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(j+1)}\int_0^\infty t^j/(e^{t-\eta}+1)\,dt$ ([Fermi–Dirac integral definitions and conventions](oxref:special-fermi-dirac-definition)).
Using $\Gamma(3/2) = \sqrt{\pi}/2$ and the thermal de Broglie wavelength $\lambda = h/\sqrt{2\pi m k_BT}$ simplifies the density equation to

$$
n = \frac{2}{\lambda^3}\,F_{1/2}(\eta)
$$


The factor 2 is the spin degeneracy.
Confusing the unnormalized integral $\int_0^\infty t^{1/2}/(e^{t-\eta}+1)\,dt = (\sqrt{\pi}/2)\,F_{1/2}(\eta)$ with the value of `fermi_dirac_half` shifts the right-hand side by about 11 % and gives an incorrect $\mu$.

At $T = 0$, the distribution is a step cut off at $\epsilon = \mu$, and states are filled up to the Fermi energy $E_F = \dfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}(3\pi^2 n)^{2/3}$.
Using the dimensionless temperature $\theta = k_BT/E_F$ expresses the density condition in the material-independent form

$$
F_{1/2}(\eta) = \frac{n\lambda^3}{2} = \frac{4}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\,\theta^{-3/2}
$$


The right-hand side $n\lambda^3/2$ estimates the number of electrons in a cube with sides equal to the thermal wavelength: a value much greater than 1 indicates degeneracy, while a value much less than 1 indicates a classical gas.
Solving this equation for $\eta$ gives $\mu/E_F = \theta\,\eta(\theta)$.

## The two limits

For $\theta \ll 1$ (a strongly degenerate gas), substituting the Sommerfeld expansion $F_{1/2}(\eta) = \frac{4}{3\sqrt\pi}\eta^{3/2}\bigl[1 + \frac{\pi^2}{8}\eta^{-2} + \frac{7\pi^4}{640}\eta^{-4} + \cdots\bigr]$ of $F_{1/2}$ into the equation and solving for $\eta$ as a series in $\theta$ gives

$$
\frac{\mu}{E_F} = 1 - \frac{\pi^2}{12}\,\theta^2 - \frac{\pi^4}{80}\,\theta^4 + O(\theta^6)
$$


For $\theta \gg 1$ (a classical gas), $\eta \to -\infty$; using $F_{1/2}(\eta) = e^\eta - e^{2\eta}/2^{3/2} + \cdots$ and setting $y = \frac{4}{3\sqrt\pi}\theta^{-3/2}$ gives

$$
\eta = \ln y + \frac{y}{2^{3/2}} + O(y^2), \qquad \frac{\mu}{E_F} = \theta\,\eta
$$


The leading term $\eta = \ln y$ is the chemical potential of the Boltzmann distribution, and $y/2^{3/2}$ is the first quantum correction due to the Pauli exclusion principle.

At densities $n \gtrsim 6 \times 10^{35}$ m⁻³, as inside white dwarfs, the Fermi momentum $\hbar(3\pi^2 n)^{1/3}$ exceeds $m_ec$ and requires a relativistic dispersion relation, so this model no longer applies.

## Root finding with safeguarded Newton iteration

The differentiation relation $F_j' = F_{j-1}$ for normalized Fermi–Dirac integrals gives the derivative of the residual $\Phi(\eta) = F_{1/2}(\eta) - y$ as $\Phi'(\eta) = F_{-1/2}(\eta)$, supplied directly by scioxide-special's `fermi_dirac_mhalf`.
Since $F_{1/2}$ increases monotonically, the root is unique, and the following two inequalities give a bracket.
First, $1/(e^{t-\eta}+1) < e^{\eta - t}$ implies $F_{1/2}(\eta) < e^\eta$ everywhere, so the lower endpoint $\eta_- = \ln y$ satisfies $\Phi(\eta_-) < 0$.
Second, $F_{1/2}(\eta) > \frac{4}{3\sqrt\pi}\eta^{3/2}$ holds for $\eta > 0$, so $\eta_+ = \max\bigl((\tfrac{3\sqrt\pi}{4}y)^{2/3}, 0\bigr) + 1$ satisfies $\Phi(\eta_+) > 0$.
This inequality follows because the occupation left by the distribution $1/(e^{t-\eta}+1)$ at $t = \eta + s$ equals the missing occupation at $t = \eta - s$, while the upper weight $(\eta + s)^{1/2}$ exceeds the lower weight $(\eta - s)^{1/2}$.

scioxide-rootfind's `SafeguardedNewton` takes this bracket and an initial guess, bisects the bracket if the Newton update leaves it or makes slow progress, and shrinks the bracket at each evaluation ([safeguarding with a bracket](oxref:rootfind-safeguarded-newton)).
The initial guess is the classical limit $\ln y$ for $y < 1$ and the degenerate limit $(\tfrac{3\sqrt\pi}{4}y)^{2/3}$ for $y \ge 1$.
Iteration stops when the bracket width meets the default coordinate tolerances (both relative and absolute $4\varepsilon$), giving termination `BracketTolerance`, or when the evaluated residual is exactly 0, giving `ResidualTolerance`.
The following definitions block shares this root solve and the right-hand side $y(\theta)$ of the density condition between two cells.

```rust
//| id: special-fermi-gas-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Solve the density condition F_{1/2}(η) = y with safeguarded Newton iteration"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, Interval, RealScalar};
use scioxide_rootfind::{
    RootResult, SafeguardedInitialGuess, SafeguardedNewton, ScalarFn, ScalarRootOptions,
    ScalarRootSolver,
};
use scioxide_special::{fermi_dirac_half, fermi_dirac_mhalf};

/// Right-hand side of the density condition: y = nλ³/2 = (4/(3√π)) θ^(−3/2), where θ = k_BT/E_F.
pub(crate) fn degeneracy(theta: f64) -> f64 {
    4.0 / (3.0 * RealScalar::sqrt(std::f64::consts::PI)) * RealScalar::powf(theta, -1.5)
}

/// Solve F_{1/2}(η) = y for η = μ/k_BT. The derivative is F_{1/2}' = F_{−1/2}.
pub(crate) fn solve_eta(y: f64) -> Result<RootResult<f64>, String> {
    let value = move |eta: f64| -> Result<f64, SciOxideError> { Ok(fermi_dirac_half(eta)? - y) };
    let residual = ScalarFn::new(value).with_derivative(|eta: f64| fermi_dirac_mhalf(eta));
    // Bracket: F_{1/2}(η) < e^η gives the lower endpoint ln y; F_{1/2}(η) > (4/(3√π)) η^(3/2) gives the upper endpoint.
    let lower = RealScalar::ln(y);
    let degenerate = RealScalar::powf(0.75 * RealScalar::sqrt(std::f64::consts::PI) * y, 2.0 / 3.0);
    let upper = degenerate.max(0.0) + 1.0;
    let guess = if y < 1.0 { lower } else { degenerate };
    let interval = Interval::new(lower, upper).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let start = SafeguardedInitialGuess::new(interval, guess).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    SafeguardedNewton::default()
        .solve(&residual, start, &ScalarRootOptions::default())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
}
```

## Temperature dependence of the chemical potential

The next cell varies $\theta$ on a logarithmic grid from $10^{-2}$ to $10$, computes $\mu/E_F$, and plots it together with the two limiting formulas.
At representative $\theta$, the table lists the computed $\eta$ and $\mu/E_F$, differences from the limiting formulas, and the root solver's termination and iteration count.
Selecting the number of terms in the limiting formulas lets you check that the Sommerfeld-expansion error changes from $O(\theta^4)$ to $O(\theta^6)$ and the classical-limit error from $O(y)$ to $O(y^2)$.

```rust
//| id: special-fermi-gas-chemical-potential
//| ref: special-fermi-gas-chemical-potential
//| title: "Compute electron-gas chemical potential μ/E_F as a function of θ = k_BT/E_F"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [special-fermi-gas-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   terms: {"type":"select","label":"Terms in limiting formulas","description":"Use only the leading term or include the next correction.","options":[{"label":"Leading term only","value":"leading"},{"label":"Include correction","value":"corrected"}],"value":"corrected"}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"leading","label":"Leading term only","description":"Compare using only 1 − (π²/12)θ² for Sommerfeld and θ ln y for the classical limit.","inputs":{"terms":"leading"}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"potential","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":0.99164124,"atol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"potential","path":"/rows/1/4","approx":{"value":1.23e-5,"rtol":0.02}}
//|       - {"artifact":"potential","path":"/rows/4/2","approx":{"value":-0.0214608,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"potential","path":"/rows/6/5","approx":{"value":1.12e-4,"rtol":0.02}}
//|   - case: leading
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"potential","path":"/rows/1/4","approx":{"value":1.341e-4,"rtol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"potential","path":"/rows/6/5","approx":{"value":0.1188,"rtol":0.01}}
use std::f64::consts::PI;

let corrected = terms == "corrected";
// Limiting formulas (manual reference calculations), returning μ/E_F.
let sommerfeld = |theta: f64| {
    let quartic = if corrected {
        PI.powi(4) / 80.0 * theta.powi(4)
    } else {
        0.0
    };
    1.0 - PI * PI / 12.0 * theta * theta - quartic
};
let classical = |theta: f64| {
    let y = degeneracy(theta);
    let correction = if corrected {
        y / 2.0_f64.powf(1.5)
    } else {
        0.0
    };
    theta * (y.ln() + correction)
};
let mut exact = Vec::new();
let mut low = Vec::new();
let mut high = Vec::new();
for i in 0..=120 {
    let theta = 1e-2 * 1e3_f64.powf(i as f64 / 120.0);
    let eta = solve_eta(degeneracy(theta))?.point();
    exact.push([theta, theta * eta]);
    if theta <= 1.0 {
        low.push([theta, sommerfeld(theta)]);
    }
    if theta >= 0.5 {
        high.push([theta, classical(theta)]);
    }
}
let dashed = serde_json::json!({ "lineType": "dashed" });
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "μ/E_F from root finding", "points": exact }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Sommerfeld expansion", "points": low, "style": dashed }),
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "Classical limit", "points": high, "style": dashed })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "θ = k_BT/E_F [-]",
        "yLabel": "μ/E_F [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yMin": -40.0,
        "yMax": 2.0,
        "annotations": [{ "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.0, "label": "μ = 0" }]
    })
);

let mut rows = Vec::new();
for theta in [0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0] {
    let result = solve_eta(degeneracy(theta))?;
    let mu = theta * result.point();
    // Compare each limiting formula only at θ on its corresponding side of the range.
    let low_error = (theta <= 0.5).then(|| (mu - sommerfeld(theta)).abs());
    let high_error = (theta >= 1.0).then(|| (mu - classical(theta)).abs());
    rows.push((
        theta,
        result.point(),
        mu,
        sommerfeld(theta),
        low_error,
        high_error,
        format!("{:?}", result.termination()),
        result.counts().iterations(),
    ));
}
let sig8 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 8 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "θ [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 2 } },
    { "key": "1", "label": "η = μ/k_BT [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig8 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "μ/E_F [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig8 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Sommerfeld value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig8 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "|μ/E_F − Sommerfeld| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "|μ/E_F − classical limit| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination status" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Iterations [count]", "type": "integer" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "potential" })
);
```

The plotted $\mu/E_F$ decreases from 1 in proportion to $\theta^2$ as $\theta \to 0$, crosses 0 at $\theta \approx 0.989$, and grows in magnitude on the negative side like $-\tfrac{3}{2}\theta\ln\theta$ at high temperature.
The zero crossing agrees with $\theta_0 = \bigl(4/(3\sqrt\pi\,F_{1/2}(0))\bigr)^{2/3} = 0.98873$, obtained by substituting $F_{1/2}(0) = (1 - 2^{-1/2})\zeta(3/2) = 0.765147$ into the density condition.
With the default correction terms, the difference from the Sommerfeld expansion is $1.5 \times 10^{-7}$ at $\theta = 0.05$ and $1.2 \times 10^{-5}$ at $\theta = 0.1$.
Halving $\theta$ reduces the difference by about a factor of 80, broadly consistent with the remaining terms decreasing as $\theta^6$ (a factor of 64).
The differences from the classical limit at $\theta = 1, 2, 5$ are $2.7 \times 10^{-3}$, $6.9 \times 10^{-4}$, and $1.1 \times 10^{-4}$, decreasing as $y$ decreases.

With only leading terms (`leading`), the difference from Sommerfeld at $\theta = 0.1$ becomes $1.34 \times 10^{-4}$, almost equal to the omitted term $\pi^4\theta^4/80 = 1.22 \times 10^{-4}$.
At $\theta = 5$, the difference from the classical limit is 0.119, almost equal to the omitted correction $\theta y/2^{3/2} = 0.119$.
At $\theta = 0.5$, even the two-term Sommerfeld expansion differs by 0.025, and at $\theta = 1$, even the corrected classical limit differs by $2.7 \times 10^{-3}$, so $0.5 \lesssim \theta \lesssim 2$ requires a numerical solution.
The root solve finishes in 3–5 iterations for every $\theta$, with termination `BracketTolerance` or `ResidualTolerance`.

## Chemical potential from density and temperature

The next cell takes electron number density $n$ [m⁻³] and temperature $T$ [K], computes $E_F$, $T_F = E_F/k_B$, the thermal wavelength $\lambda$, and the degeneracy indicator $n\lambda^3/2$, then finds $\mu$ in eV.
It uses CODATA 2018 physical constants: $h$, $k_B$, and the elementary charge have defined values, and the electron mass is $9.1093837015 \times 10^{-31}$ kg.
The default $n = 10^{26}$ m⁻³ gives $T_F \approx 900$ K and partial degeneracy at room temperature.
The copper case uses the conduction-electron density $n = 8.47 \times 10^{28}$ m⁻³ (table in Chapter 1 of Ashcroft & Mermin 1976), treating each atom as contributing one free electron.
The root solver's termination is the conclusion of this cell and appears in the final outcome.

```rust
//| id: special-fermi-gas-physical
//| ref: special-fermi-gas-physical
//| title: "Compute chemical potential μ [eV] from electron density and temperature"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [special-fermi-gas-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-rootfind, scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   log10_density: {"type":"range","label":"log₁₀ n (n [m⁻³])","description":"Base-10 logarithm of the electron number density.","min":22,"max":30,"step":0.001,"value":26}
//|   temperature: {"type":"range","label":"Temperature T [K]","min":1,"max":5000,"step":1,"value":300}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"copper","label":"Conduction electrons in copper, 300 K","description":"At n = 8.47×10²⁸ m⁻³, E_F ≈ 7.0 eV and θ ≈ 0.0037. μ is below E_F by a relative 10⁻⁵.","inputs":{"log10_density":28.928,"temperature":300}}
//|   - {"id":"dilute","label":"n = 10²² m⁻³, 1000 K","description":"A classical gas with nλ³/2 ≈ 10⁻⁴. μ is negative and nearly matches the Boltzmann formula k_BT ln(nλ³/2).","inputs":{"log10_density":22,"temperature":1000}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"state","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0785604,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"state","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":0.0703487,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"state","path":"/rows/7/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: copper
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"state","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":7.03402,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"state","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":7.03394,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"state","path":"/rows/8/1","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: dilute
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"state","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":-0.830171,"rtol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"state","path":"/rows/9/1","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use std::f64::consts::PI;

// CODATA 2018 physical constants (SI).
let planck = 6.62607015e-34; // h [J·s]
let hbar = planck / (2.0 * PI);
let boltzmann = 1.380649e-23; // k_B [J/K]
let electron_volt = 1.602176634e-19; // [J]
let mass = 9.1093837015e-31; // Electron mass m_e [kg]

let density = RealScalar::powf(10.0, log10_density);
let fermi_energy =
    hbar * hbar / (2.0 * mass) * RealScalar::powf(3.0 * PI * PI * density, 2.0 / 3.0);
let thermal = boltzmann * temperature;
let theta = thermal / fermi_energy;
let wavelength = planck / RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 * PI * mass * thermal);
let y = density * RealScalar::powi(wavelength, 3) / 2.0;
let result = solve_eta(y)?;
let eta = result.point();
let mu = eta * thermal / electron_volt;
// Checks (manual calculations): agreement of the two formulas for y, the low-temperature Sommerfeld expansion, and the high-temperature classical limit.
let consistency = (y - degeneracy(theta)).abs() / y;
let sommerfeld = 1.0 - PI * PI / 12.0 * theta * theta - PI.powi(4) / 80.0 * theta.powi(4);
let classical = theta * (y.ln() + y / 2.0_f64.powf(1.5));
let rows = vec![
    ("E_F [eV]", Some(fermi_energy / electron_volt)),
    ("T_F = E_F/k_B [K]", Some(fermi_energy / boltzmann)),
    ("θ = k_BT/E_F [-]", Some(theta)),
    ("Thermal wavelength λ [nm]", Some(wavelength * 1e9)),
    ("nλ³/2 = F_{1/2}(η) [-]", Some(y)),
    ("η = μ/k_BT [-]", Some(eta)),
    ("μ [eV]", Some(mu)),
    ("Relative difference between the two formulas for y [-]", Some(consistency)),
    (
        "|μ/E_F − Sommerfeld| (θ ≤ 0.5)[-]",
        (theta <= 0.5).then(|| (theta * eta - sommerfeld).abs()),
    ),
    (
        "|μ/E_F − classical limit| (θ ≥ 1) [-]",
        (theta >= 1.0).then(|| (theta * eta - classical).abs()),
    ),
];
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 6 }, "nullText": "Not applicable" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "state" })
);

let termination = result.termination();
let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Solved F_{1/2}(η) = nλ³/2 to the stopping criterion")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Root finding stopped before meeting the criterion. The last point is an estimate")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{termination:?}"))
        .diagnostic("Residual F_{1/2}(η) − y", result.residual(), None)
        .diagnostic("Iteration count", result.counts().iterations() as f64, None)
);
```

At the default $n = 10^{26}$ m⁻³, $E_F = 0.0786$ eV and $T_F = 912$ K; at 300 K, $\theta = 0.329$ and $n\lambda^3/2 = 3.98$.
Root finding gives $\eta = 2.72$ and $\mu = 0.0703$ eV, with $\mu$ about 10 % below $E_F$.
The difference from the two-term Sommerfeld expansion is $1.2 \times 10^{-3}$ in $\mu/E_F$, revealing the effect of truncating the expansion at this temperature.
The relative difference between $n\lambda^3/2$ calculated directly from physical constants and $(4/(3\sqrt\pi))\theta^{-3/2}$ is $3 \times 10^{-15}$, checking the unit conversion.

For copper (`copper`), $E_F = 7.034$ eV, $T_F = 8.16 \times 10^4$ K, and $\theta = 0.0037$; $\mu = 7.03394$ eV is below $E_F$ by a relative $1.1 \times 10^{-5}$.
The difference from the Sommerfeld expansion is $2 \times 10^{-14}$, at the level of double-precision rounding.
For the dilute gas (`dilute`), $\theta = 509$ and $n\lambda^3/2 = 6.5 \times 10^{-5}$; $\mu = -0.830$ eV agrees with the corrected classical limit to within $1.1 \times 10^{-8}$.
Every case has outcome satisfied, an exactly zero residual (termination `ResidualTolerance`), and 2–5 iterations.
At the input endpoint $n = 10^{30}$ m⁻³ and 300 K ($\eta \approx 1410$), the iteration count rises to 37, but termination is `BracketTolerance` and the outcome remains satisfied.
If the iteration count approaches the limit of 100 or the outcome becomes partial, review the bracket endpoints and how the derivative is supplied.

## Summary

The free-electron density equation is $n = (2/\lambda^3)F_{1/2}(\mu/k_BT)$ using scioxide-special's Gamma-normalized Fermi–Dirac integral; mixing it with the unnormalized integral changes the density by a factor of $\sqrt{\pi}/2$.
For normalized integrals, the derivative is $F_{1/2}' = F_{-1/2}$, so passing `fermi_dirac_mhalf` as the derivative to `SafeguardedNewton` and bracketing the root between $\ln y$ and an endpoint built from the leading Sommerfeld term gives the same procedure from degeneracy to the classical regime.
The computed $\mu/E_F$ agrees with the two-term Sommerfeld expansion to about $10^{-5}$ or better for $\theta \le 0.1$ and with the corrected classical limit to about $10^{-3}$ or better for $\theta \ge 2$; the intermediate range $0.5 \lesssim \theta \lesssim 2$ requires a numerical solution.
Conduction electrons in metals have $\theta \sim 10^{-3}$ at room temperature, allowing $\mu \approx E_F$, while the chemical potential $\mu$ of electrons in low-density semiconductors varies substantially with temperature.
See [Fermi–Dirac integrals](../../algorithms/fermi-dirac-integrals/) for evaluating $F_{1/2}$ and its accuracy, and scioxide-rootfind's [open scalar methods](../../../scioxide-rootfind/algorithms/open-scalar-methods/) for choosing between bracketing and Newton methods.
