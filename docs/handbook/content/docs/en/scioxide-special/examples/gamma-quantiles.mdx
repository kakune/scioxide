When a service receives requests irregularly and accumulates $a$ requests for batch processing, the waiting time for a complete batch follows a Gamma distribution.
The wait for the $a$th radioactive decay in radiation counting, or for a component to stop at its $a$th failure, follows the same distribution.
Choosing $t$ to meet a target such as “99.9 % of batches are complete within $t$ seconds” requires a quantile calculation: invert the cumulative distribution function to obtain time from probability $p$.
This example derives the waiting-time distribution, computes quantiles with `gamma_p_inv` and `gamma_q_inv`, and checks the results using finite Poisson sums, rounding analysis for upper-tail probabilities, and chi-square closed forms.

## Poisson arrivals and the Gamma distribution

Consider a Poisson process in which requests arrive independently at a mean rate $\lambda$ [requests/s].
The count $N(t)$ arriving during $[0, t]$ has a Poisson distribution with mean $\lambda t$.

$$
\Pr[N(t) = k] = e^{-\lambda t}\frac{(\lambda t)^k}{k!}
$$

If $T_a$ is the time of the $a$th arrival, then $T_a \le t$ and $N(t) \ge a$ are the same event.
Thus, using dimensionless time $x = \lambda t$ gives

$$
\Pr[T_a \le t] = 1 - e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}\frac{x^k}{k!} = P(a, x)
$$

so the waiting-time cumulative distribution function is the regularized lower incomplete Gamma function $P(a, x)$ ([incomplete Gamma functions](oxref:special-incgamma-definition)).
For integer $a$, the sum on the right agrees with the finite sum $Q(a, x) = e^{-x}\sum_{k<a} x^k/k!$ in DLMF 8.4.10.
Differentiating with respect to $x$ gives the density

$$
f(x) = \frac{x^{a-1}e^{-x}}{\Gamma(a)}
$$

whose mean and variance are both $a$.
Returning to seconds gives mean waiting time $a/\lambda$ and standard deviation $\sqrt{a}/\lambda$.

The quantile $t_p$ at probability $p$ solves the nonlinear equation $P(a, \lambda t_p) = p$.

$$
x_p = P^{-1}(a, p), \qquad t_p = \frac{x_p}{\lambda}
$$

## Quantile sensitivity

The derivative of the inverse is the reciprocal density $dx/dp = 1/f(x)$.
The condition number measuring the amplification of a relative change in $p$ into a relative change in $x_p$ is

$$
\kappa_p = \left|\frac{p}{x}\frac{dx}{dp}\right| = \frac{p}{x\, f(x)}
$$

while specifying the upper-tail probability $q = 1 - p$ gives condition number $\kappa_q = q/(x f(x))$.
In the right tail, both $f(x)$ and $q$ decrease exponentially, but when $x$ is much greater than $a$, the ratio $q/f(x)$ stays near 1, so $\kappa_q$ is small, of order $1/x$.
When $p$ is close to 1, however, $\kappa_p \approx 1/(x f(x))$ becomes large, and a small change in $p$ moves the quantile substantially.

This difference directly affects whether to supply the target as $p$ or $q$.
Forming $p = 1 - q$ in binary64 introduces an absolute rounding error in $q$ of order $\delta \le 2^{-53}$.
This becomes a relative error $\delta/q$ in $q$, of order $10^{-7}$ for $q = 10^{-9}$.
`gamma_p_inv(a, p)` internally converts $p > 1/2$ exactly to $1 - p$ and solves the upper tail, but cannot remove this error because the supplied $p$ is already rounded.
When the target is specified as an upper-tail probability, pass $q$ directly to `gamma_q_inv(a, q)`.
If $q$ is too small to represent in binary64, pass its logarithm $\ln q$ to `gamma_q_inv_log`.

The inverse starts from an asymptotic initial guess and applies Halley iteration in $\ln x$ with a safeguarding bracket, stopping at the precision of the result type.
The accuracy target over the tested range is 32 ULP in binary64.
See [logarithmic forms and inverses](oxref:special-incgamma-log-inverse) for method details.

## Quantiles of batch waiting times

The next cell computes waiting-time quantiles from batch size $a$ [requests], arrival rate $\lambda$ [requests/s], and target probability $p$.
The chart shows the quantile curve $t_p$ for $p \in [0.01, 0.99]$, with reference lines marking the target probability and quantile.
The table lists the mean $a/\lambda$, median, target quantile $t_p$, condition number $\kappa_p$, and round-trip error $|P(a, x_p) - p|$.
As an independent check, it manually sums the finite Poisson expression $1 - e^{-x}\sum_{k<a} x^k/k!$ derived above using standard `f64` functions and reports its difference from the target $p$.
The round-trip error only checks consistency between the inverse and `gamma_p`, so the finite sum from a separate formula is the main independent check.

```rust
//| id: special-gamma-quantiles
//| ref: special-gamma-quantiles
//| title: "Compute quantiles of the wait for a complete batch"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   batch_size: {"type":"range","label":"Batch size a [requests]","description":"Change which arrival completes the batch.","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":20}
//|   arrival_rate: {"type":"range","label":"Arrival rate λ [requests/s]","description":"Mean number of requests arriving per second.","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":10}
//|   target_probability: {"type":"range","label":"Target probability p [-]","description":"Find the time by which the batch is complete with this probability.","min":0.5,"max":0.999,"step":0.001,"value":0.999}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"median","label":"p = 0.5","description":"The median is slightly shorter than the mean a/λ.","inputs":{"target_probability":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"single","label":"a = 1","description":"The wait for the first arrival is exponential, with x_p = −ln(1 − p).","inputs":{"batch_size":1}}
//|   - {"id":"large","label":"a = 100","description":"For larger batches, the distribution concentrates around its mean and relative variability decreases.","inputs":{"batch_size":100}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":1.966767242,"rtol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":3.670097876,"rtol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":53.72,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: median
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.966767242,"rtol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|   - case: single
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":0.690775527898214,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: large
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":13.377026391,"rtol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"quantiles","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-15}}
use scioxide_special::{gamma_p, gamma_p_inv, ln_gamma};

let a = batch_size;
let lambda = arrival_rate;
let p = target_probability;
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 5 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });

// Quantile curve, converted back to time and plotted in seconds.
let curve = (1..=99)
    .map(|index| {
        let probability = f64::from(index) / 100.0;
        gamma_p_inv(a, probability).map(|x| [probability, x / lambda])
    })
    .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let median = gamma_p_inv(a, 0.5).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let x = gamma_p_inv(a, p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let roundtrip = (gamma_p(a, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - p).abs();

// Independent check: finite Poisson sum 1 − e^(−x) Σ_{k<a} x^k / k! (manual calculation with standard f64 functions).
let mut term = (-x).exp();
let mut below = term;
for k in 1..a as usize {
    term *= x / k as f64;
    below += term;
}
let poisson_error = ((1.0 - below) - p).abs();
// Condition number κ_p = p / (x f(x)), f(x) = x^(a−1) e^(−x) / Γ(a) (manual calculation with standard f64 functions except ln Γ).
let log_density = (a - 1.0) * x.ln() - x - ln_gamma(a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let condition = p / (x * log_density.exp());

emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "Quantile t_p", "points": curve })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Probability p [-]",
        "yLabel": "Waiting time t [s]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 1.0,
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": p, "label": "Target probability" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": x / lambda, "label": "Target quantile" },
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": a / lambda, "label": "Mean a/λ" }
        ]
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "a [requests]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Mean a/λ [s]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Median [s]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Quantile t_p [s]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Condition number κ_p [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Round-trip error |P − p| [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Difference from Poisson sum [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![(
        a as u32,
        a / lambda,
        median / lambda,
        x / lambda,
        condition,
        roundtrip,
        poisson_error
    )],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "quantiles" })
);
```

With the default $a = 20$ requests and $\lambda = 10$ requests/s, the mean waiting time is 2 s, the median is 1.967 s, and 99.9 % of batches are complete within 3.670 s.
Increasing the target from 0.5 to 0.999 less than doubles the probability but multiplies the quantile by 1.87, while condition number $\kappa_p$ rises from 0.28 to 53.7.
For $a = 1$, the distribution is exponential and the 99.9 % quantile is $x = -\ln 0.001 = 6.908$, or 0.691 s, with a condition number as large as 145.
For $a = 100$, the 99.9 % quantile is 13.38 s against a mean of 10 s; its ratio to the mean decreases from 1.84 for $a = 20$ to 1.34.
This happens because the standard deviation $\sqrt{a}/\lambda$ grows more slowly than the mean $a/\lambda$.

Both the round-trip error and the difference from the Poisson sum are at most $10^{-15}$ for every input, at the level of binary64 rounding.
If the difference exceeds $10^{-12}$, check for confusion between dimensionless $x$ before dividing by the arrival rate and $t$ in seconds.

## Supply an upper-tail probability

The next cell selects batch size $a$, varies the upper-tail probability $q = 10^{-k}$ from $k = 1$ to $400$, and computes quantile $x_q$ in three ways.
They are `gamma_q_inv(a, q)`, `gamma_p_inv(a, p)` supplied with rounded $p = 1 - q$, and `gamma_q_inv_log(a, ln q)` supplied with $\ln q = -k \ln 10$.
Since $q = 10^{-400}$ is not representable in binary64, only the logarithmic form solves that row.
The table compares the relative difference of the second method with the relative difference $\delta/(x f(x))$ predicted by rounding analysis.
Here $\delta = (1 - p) - q$ is the error introduced in $q$ by rounding $p$; for $p \ge 1/2$, $1 - p$ is computed exactly, so $\delta$ is also obtained exactly in binary64.
Agreement between prediction and measurement shows that the second method's difference comes from input rounding rather than inverse-function error.
The final column gives the difference between $\ln Q(a, x_q)$ recovered by forward evaluation with `gamma_log_q` and $\ln q$.

```rust
//| id: special-gamma-quantile-tails
//| ref: special-gamma-quantile-tails
//| title: "Compute quantiles from upper-tail probabilities and examine rounding of 1 − q"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   batch_size: {"type":"range","label":"Batch size a [requests]","description":"Change the distribution shape.","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":20}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"single","label":"a = 1","description":"For the exponential distribution, the quantile has closed form x_q = −ln q.","inputs":{"batch_size":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/4/3","approx":{"value":6.935e-10,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/4/4","approx":{"value":6.935e-10,"rtol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/7/2","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/6","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/9/1","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/9/6","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: single
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/8/1","approx":{"value":690.7755278982137,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tails","path":"/rows/9/5","approx":{"value":921.0340371976183,"rtol":1e-14}}
use scioxide_special::{gamma_log_q, gamma_p_inv, gamma_q_inv, gamma_q_inv_log, ln_gamma};

let a = batch_size;
let sig10 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let log_gamma_a = ln_gamma(a).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut direct_points = Vec::new();
let mut rounded_points = Vec::new();
for k in [1_u32, 2, 3, 6, 9, 12, 16, 17, 300, 400] {
    let log_q = -f64::from(k) * std::f64::consts::LN_10;
    // 10^(−400) is outside the binary64 range, so use None.
    let q = (k <= 300).then(|| 10.0_f64.powi(-(k as i32)));
    let from_log = gamma_q_inv_log(a, log_q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let direct = match q {
        Some(q) => Some(gamma_q_inv(a, q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?),
        None => None,
    };
    // Supply rounded p = 1 − q. If 1 − q rounds to 1, the quantile becomes +∞.
    let rounded = match q {
        Some(q) => {
            Some(gamma_p_inv(a, 1.0 - q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?).filter(|x| x.is_finite())
        }
        None => None,
    };
    let rounded_error = match (direct, rounded) {
        (Some(x), Some(y)) => Some((y - x).abs() / x),
        _ => None,
    };
    // Rounding prediction: δ / (x f(x)), δ = (1 − p) − q (1 − p is exact for p ≥ 1/2).
    let predicted = match (q, direct) {
        (Some(q), Some(x)) if 1.0 - q < 1.0 => {
            let delta = (1.0 - (1.0 - q)) - q;
            let log_density = (a - 1.0) * x.ln() - x - log_gamma_a;
            Some(delta.abs() / (x * log_density.exp()))
        }
        _ => None,
    };
    let back = gamma_log_q(a, from_log).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    if let Some(x) = direct {
        direct_points.push([f64::from(k), x]);
    }
    if let Some(y) = rounded {
        rounded_points.push([f64::from(k), y]);
    }
    rows.push((
        k,
        direct,
        rounded,
        rounded_error,
        predicted,
        from_log,
        (back - log_q).abs() / log_q.abs(),
    ));
}

emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({ "name": "gamma_q_inv(a, q)", "points": direct_points }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "gamma_p_inv(a, 1 − q)",
            "points": rounded_points,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        })
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "k (q = 10^−k)[-]",
        "yLabel": "Quantile x_q [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log"
    })
);
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "k (q = 10^−k)", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "gamma_q_inv [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig10, "nullText": "q is not representable" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "gamma_p_inv(1 − q) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig10, "nullText": "1 − q = 1" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Rounding prediction [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "gamma_q_inv_log [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig10 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Relative residual in ln Q [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "tails" })
);
```

With the default $a = 20$, all three methods agree for $k \le 3$.
The value of `gamma_p_inv(a, 1 − q)` starts to depart at $k = 6$, with relative difference increasing from $9.5 \times 10^{-13}$ at $k = 6$ to $6.9 \times 10^{-10}$ at $k = 9$ and $4.4 \times 10^{-7}$ at $k = 12$.
In rows from $k = 6$ to $12$, the rounding prediction $\delta/(x f(x))$ agrees with the measured relative difference to at least 3 digits, showing that the departure comes from rounding when forming $p = 1 - q$ rather than inverse-function error.
At $k = 16$, $1 - q$ rounds to the predecessor of 1, $1 - 2^{-53}$, changing $q$ by 11 % and producing a relative difference of $1.7 \times 10^{-3}$.
The change in $q$ is large in this row, so the result departs slightly from the linear prediction $1.8 \times 10^{-3}$.
At $k = 17$, $1 - q$ rounds to 1 and `gamma_p_inv` returns $+\infty$ (blank in the table).

`gamma_q_inv` and `gamma_q_inv_log` agree through $k = 300$; for $q = 10^{-400}$, only the logarithmic form returns $x = 1013.2$.
Every relative residual of $\ln Q$ recovered with `gamma_log_q` is at most $10^{-15}$.
For $a = 1$, $x_q = k\ln 10$, giving 690.78 at $k = 300$ and 921.03 at $k = 400$, in agreement with the closed form.

## Check: chi-square quantiles

The chi-square distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom is a Gamma distribution with shape $\nu/2$ and scale 2, so its quantile is $\chi^2_p = 2P^{-1}(\nu/2, p)$.
For three choices of degrees of freedom, a formula separate from `gamma_p_inv` checks the quantile.
The case $\nu = 2$ is exponential and has the closed form $\chi^2_p = -2\ln(1 - p)$.
For $\nu = 1$, $P(1/2, x) = \operatorname{erf}\sqrt{x}$ gives $\chi^2_p = 2\,[\operatorname{erfc}^{-1}(1 - p)]^2$, which can be computed with a separate function, the inverse complementary error function `erfcinv`.
The case $\nu = 10$ has integer shape 5; substitute the quantile into the finite Poisson sum above and compare with $p$.
The 95 % quantile for $\nu = 1$ is $3.84146$, the square of the normal distribution's two-sided 95 % critical value $1.95996$.

```rust
//| id: special-gamma-chi-square
//| ref: special-gamma-chi-square
//| title: "Check chi-square quantiles against closed forms and a separate function"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"chi-square","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":3.841458820694124,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"chi-square","path":"/rows/0/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"chi-square","path":"/rows/3/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"chi-square","path":"/rows/8/4","range":{"max":1e-14}}
use scioxide_special::{erfcinv, gamma_p_inv};

let sig10 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for nu in [1_u32, 2, 10] {
    for p in [0.95, 0.99, 0.999] {
        let chi_square =
            2.0 * gamma_p_inv(f64::from(nu) / 2.0, p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        // Independent check: alternative quantile formulas for ν = 1, 2; the difference between finite-sum P and p for ν = 10.
        let (method, reference, error) = match nu {
            1 => {
                let root = erfcinv(1.0 - p).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
                let value = 2.0 * root * root;
                (
                    "2 erfcinv(1 − p)²",
                    Some(value),
                    (chi_square - value).abs() / value,
                )
            }
            2 => {
                // Closed form −2 ln(1 − p) (reference value from standard f64 functions).
                let value = -2.0 * (-p).ln_1p();
                (
                    "−2 ln(1 − p)",
                    Some(value),
                    (chi_square - value).abs() / value,
                )
            }
            _ => {
                // P(5, x) = 1 − e^(−x) Σ_{k<5} x^k / k! (manual calculation).
                let x = chi_square / 2.0;
                let mut term = (-x).exp();
                let mut below = term;
                for k in 1..5 {
                    term *= x / f64::from(k);
                    below += term;
                }
                ("P − p from Poisson sum", None, ((1.0 - below) - p).abs())
            }
        };
        rows.push((nu, p, chi_square, reference, error, method));
    }
}
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Degrees of freedom ν", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Probability p [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 3 } },
    { "key": "2", "label": "2 gamma_p_inv(ν/2, p) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Alternative formula value [-]", "type": "number", "format": sig10, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Check method" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "chi-square" })
);
```

The 95 % quantile for $\nu = 1$ is 3.841459, agreeing with $1.95996^2$.
In the 6 rows for $\nu = 1, 2$, the relative difference from the alternative formula is at most $4.1 \times 10^{-16}$.
In the 3 rows for $\nu = 10$, the difference between the Poisson-sum $P$ and $p$ is at most $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$.
All differences are at the binary64 rounding level, far below the inverse's target of 32 ULP (about $7 \times 10^{-15}$ relative error).

## Summary and further reading

The wait for the $a$th Poisson arrival has a Gamma distribution with shape $a$; its quantile is $t_p = P^{-1}(a, p)/\lambda$, computed directly by `gamma_p_inv`.
In the right tail with $p$ close to 1, condition number $\kappa_p$ is large, so a small change in the target probability substantially changes the quantile.
If the target is an upper-tail probability $q$, pass $q$ directly to `gamma_q_inv(a, q)` without forming $1 - q$.
For tails where $q$ is too small to represent in binary64, pass $\ln q$ to `gamma_q_inv_log`.
See [incomplete Gamma functions](../../algorithms/incomplete-gamma/) for evaluation and inverse methods, and [Gamma and Beta functions and probability tails](../../api/gamma-and-beta/) for individual function specifications.
