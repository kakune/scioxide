Systems with one electron around a nucleus of charge $Ze$ (hydrogen, He⁺, Li²⁺, etc.)
have closed-form Schrödinger solutions.
The wavefunction separates into radial function $R_{nl}(r)$ and spherical harmonics; atomic size, transition strengths, hyperfine structure, and other quantities follow from integrals of $R_{nl}$.
This example evaluates $R_{nl}$ with `hydrogenic_radial`, plots radial probability densities, and checks normalization, closed-form expectations, and node counts/positions using independent numerical integration.
Definitions and evaluation methods appear in [Coulomb waves and hydrogen-like radial states](oxref:special-coulomb-hydrogenic).

## Model and units

Treat the nuclear mass as infinite and consider an electron in potential $V(r) = -Ze^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 r)$.
Measuring lengths in Bohr radii $a_0 = 4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar^2/(m_e e^2) = 5.29177210903 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}$ (CODATA 2018 recommendation, Tiesinga et al. 2021) gives bound energies $E_n = -Z^2/(2n^2)$ Hartree (1 Hartree $= 27.211$ eV) and radial functions

$$
R_{nl}(r) = \left(\frac{2Z}{n}\right)^{3/2}\sqrt{\frac{(n - l - 1)!}{2n\,(n + l)!}}\;e^{-\rho/2}\rho^l\,L_{n-l-1}^{(2l+1)}(\rho), \qquad \rho = \frac{2Zr}{n}.
$$

where $L_k^{(\alpha)}$ is a generalized Laguerre polynomial; its properties and three-term recurrence appear in [Orthogonal polynomial families](../../../scioxide-poly/algorithms/orthogonal-families/).
`hydrogenic_radial(n, l, charge, r)` accepts $r$ in units of $a_0$ and returns values normalized to $\int_0^\infty R_{nl}^2r^2\,dr = 1$.
In real hydrogen, reduced mass $\mu = m_em_p/(m_e + m_p)$ multiplies lengths by $m_e/\mu \approx 1.000545$; this example neglects that correction.

$P_{nl}(r) = r^2R_{nl}(r)^2$ is the probability density for finding the electron in the spherical shell between radii $r$ and $r + dr$.
The radial function has $n - l - 1$ nodes (points where $R_{nl} = 0$) in $0 < r < \infty$ and behaves as $R_{nl} \sim r^l$ at the origin.
Only $l = 0$ is nonzero at the origin, with $R_{n0}(0) = 2(Z/n)^{3/2}$.

## Closed-form expectation values

Expectations of powers of $r$, $\langle r^k\rangle = \int_0^\infty r^k P_{nl}(r)\,dr$, have the following closed forms (standard quantum-mechanics results, including Bethe and Salpeter 1957).

$$
\langle r\rangle = \frac{3n^2 - l(l+1)}{2Z}, \qquad
\langle r^2\rangle = \frac{n^2\left(5n^2 + 1 - 3l(l+1)\right)}{2Z^2},
$$

$$
\langle r^{-1}\rangle = \frac{Z}{n^2}, \qquad
\langle r^{-2}\rangle = \frac{Z^2}{n^3(l + 1/2)}.
$$

Their units are respectively $a_0$, $a_0^2$, $a_0^{-1}$, and $a_0^{-2}$.
$\langle r^{-1}\rangle$ expresses the same result as virial theorem $\langle V\rangle = 2E_n$ and depends only on the principal quantum number.
At fixed $n$, $\langle r\rangle$ decreases as $l$ grows; states with $l = n - 1$ have a single peak without nodes.
Changing $Z$ multiplies all lengths by $1/Z$, so $\langle r\rangle$ of He⁺ ($Z = 2$) in $1s$ is $0.75\,a_0$, half hydrogen's value.

## Checking radial densities and expectations

The following cell selects $n$, $l$, and $Z$, evaluates `hydrogenic_radial` at 8001 equally spaced points over $0 \le r \le r_{\max}$, and plots $P_{nl}(r)$.
At $r_{\max} = (n/Z)(2n + 40)$, $e^{-\rho}$ is as small as $e^{-4n-80}$, making omitted-tail error negligible.
From the same point values, it manually computes normalization and four expectations with composite Simpson's rule (an independent check), comparing against closed forms.
The integrands $rR^2$ and $R^2$ for $\langle r^{-1}\rangle$ and $\langle r^{-2}\rangle$ remain finite at the origin, allowing the same rule.
Nodes are counted from sign changes of $R_{nl}$, with locations linearly interpolated between adjacent points.
Since `hydrogenic_radial` rejects combinations with $l \ge n$ using `DomainError` (`OutsideDomain`), the cell limits $l$ to $n - 1$ before calling and shows the actual $(n, l)$ in the node table.

```rust
//| id: special-hydrogen-radial-states
//| ref: special-hydrogen-radial-states
//| title: "Hydrogen-like radial probability densities and expectations"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   n: {"type":"range","label":"Principal quantum number n","min":1,"max":6,"step":1,"value":3}
//|   l: {"type":"range","label":"Orbital quantum number l (l < n)","min":0,"max":5,"step":1,"value":0}
//|   charge: {"type":"range","label":"Nuclear charge Z","min":1,"max":10,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"circular","label":"3d（n = 3, l = 2）","description":"The l = n − 1 state has no nodes and a single peak at r = n² = 9 a₀.","inputs":{"l":2}}
//|   - {"id":"helium-ion","label":"He⁺ 2s","description":"For Z = 2, all lengths halve and the node moves to r = 1 a₀.","inputs":{"n":2,"charge":2}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/3/3","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/4/3","range":{"max":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"nodes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"nodes","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.9019237886466844,"atol":1e-4}}
//|   - case: circular
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/4/3","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"nodes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"nodes","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":null}
//|   - case: helium-ion
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":3.0,"rtol":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"moments","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.5,"rtol":1e-7}}
//|       - {"artifact":"nodes","path":"/rows/0/3","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-4}}
use scioxide_special::hydrogenic_radial;

// Inputs are integer-valued f64. Pass quantum numbers to the API as u32.
// The API rejects l ≥ n with DomainError, so limit l to n − 1 before calling.
let n = n as u32;
let (l, z) = ((l as u32).min(n - 1), charge);
let (nf, lf) = (f64::from(n), f64::from(l));
let r_max = nf / z * (2.0 * nf + 40.0);
const INTERVALS: usize = 8000;
let h = r_max / INTERVALS as f64;
let mut radial = Vec::with_capacity(INTERVALS + 1);
for i in 0..=INTERVALS {
    let r = h * i as f64;
    radial.push((r, hydrogenic_radial(n, l, z, r).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?));
}
// Composite Simpson's rule (independent hand-computed check). k is the power of r.
let moment = |k: i32| {
    let mut sum = 0.0;
    for (i, &(r, value)) in radial.iter().enumerate() {
        let weight = match i {
            0 | INTERVALS => 1.0,
            _ if i % 2 == 1 => 4.0,
            _ => 2.0,
        };
        // Form r^k · r² R² in a form finite at the origin even for k < 0.
        sum += weight * r.powi(k + 2) * value * value;
    }
    sum * h / 3.0
};
let quantities = [
    ("∫ P(r) dr [-]", moment(0), 1.0),
    (
        "⟨r⟩ [a₀]",
        moment(1),
        (3.0 * nf * nf - lf * (lf + 1.0)) / (2.0 * z),
    ),
    (
        "⟨r²⟩ [a₀²]",
        moment(2),
        nf * nf * (5.0 * nf * nf + 1.0 - 3.0 * lf * (lf + 1.0)) / (2.0 * z * z),
    ),
    ("⟨1/r⟩ [1/a₀]", moment(-1), z / (nf * nf)),
    (
        "⟨1/r²⟩ [1/a₀²]",
        moment(-2),
        z * z / (nf.powi(3) * (lf + 0.5)),
    ),
];
let rows: Vec<(&str, f64, f64, f64)> = quantities
    .iter()
    .map(|&(name, numeric, exact)| (name, numeric, exact, (numeric - exact).abs() / exact))
    .collect();
// Nodes: excluding the origin, linearly interpolate intervals with sign changes of R.
let mut nodes = Vec::new();
for pair in radial[1..].windows(2) {
    let ((r0, v0), (r1, v1)) = (pair[0], pair[1]);
    if v0 * v1 < 0.0 {
        nodes.push(r0 - v0 * (r1 - r0) / (v1 - v0));
    }
}
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let sig10 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Simpson's rule", "type": "number", "format": sig10 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form", "type": "number", "format": sig10 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Relative difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "moments" })
);
let node_rows: Vec<(String, usize, u32, Option<f64>)> = vec![(
    format!("{n},{l}"),
    nodes.len(),
    n - l - 1,
    nodes.first().copied(),
)];
let node_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "State (n, l)" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Counted nodes [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "n − l − 1 [count]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "First node [a₀]", "type": "number", "format": sig10, "nullText": "No nodes" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    node_columns,
    node_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "nodes" })
);
let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = radial
    .iter()
    .step_by(10)
    .map(|&(r, value)| [r, r * r * value * value])
    .collect();
let mean = rows[1].2;
let mut marks =
    vec![serde_json::json!({ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": mean, "label": "⟨r⟩" })];
for &node in &nodes {
    marks
        .push(serde_json::json!({ "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": node, "label": "Node" }));
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "P(r) = r² R(r)²", "points": points })],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Radius r [a₀]",
        "yLabel": "Radial probability density P(r) [1/a₀]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 2.5 * mean + 2.0 / z,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "annotations": marks
    })
);
```

For default $3s$ ($n = 3$, $l = 0$, $Z = 1$), relative discrepancies in normalization and all four expectations are at most $7 \times 10^{-9}$, with `hydrogenic_radial` values matching closed forms.
This discrepancy is Simpson truncation error $O(h^4)$ (step $h \approx 0.017\,a_0$), remaining for $l = 0$ because the integrand has a nonzero third derivative at the origin.
For $l \ge 1$, as in $3d$, the integrand flattens near the origin as $r^{2l+2}$, reducing relative discrepancies to rounding level $10^{-14}$.
Two nodes are counted.
The exact $3s$ nodes are $r = (3 \mp \sqrt{3})\,n/(2Z) = 1.901924\ldots$ and $7.098076\ldots$ from roots $\rho = 3 \mp \sqrt{3}$ of $\rho^2 - 6\rho + 6 = 0$; the table's first node $1.901954$ agrees within linear-interpolation error $3 \times 10^{-5}$.
$\langle r\rangle = 13.5\,a_0 \approx 714$ pm is nine times the ground-state $1.5\,a_0$, demonstrating spread proportional to $n^2$.

Switching to $3d$ removes nodes, gives $\langle r\rangle = 10.5\,a_0$, and produces one peak closer to the origin than $3s$.
Larger $l$ gives a centrifugal barrier $l(l+1)/r^2$ pushing electrons away from the origin, but also shortens the outer tail, reducing mean radius.
For He⁺ $2s$, the node moves from hydrogen's $2\,a_0$ to $1\,a_0$ ($1.0000045$ in the table), while $\langle 1/r\rangle$ changes from $1/4$ to $1/2$, scaling by a factor of $Z$.
Since $r_{\max}$ and step size scale with $n/Z$, changing $Z$ leaves relative discrepancies unchanged.
For $n = 6$ in state $s$, the step grows to $0.039\,a_0$ and the relative discrepancy in $\langle 1/r^2\rangle$ rises to $1.2 \times 10^{-7}$.
If relative discrepancies exceed $10^{-6}$, increase point counts and check whether Simpson errors decrease as $h^4$ before suspecting `hydrogenic_radial`.

## Summary

`hydrogenic_radial` returns normalized $R_{nl}$ at $r$ in Bohr-radius units, rejecting $l \ge n$ and $Z \le 0$ with `DomainError`.
Numerically integrating radial probability density $r^2R_{nl}^2$ reproduces normalization and closed forms for $\langle r^k\rangle$ ($k = -2, -1, 1, 2$) within quadrature error, with node count $n - l - 1$.
Lengths scale by $1/Z$ and mean radii with $n^2$, allowing other $Z$ or $n$ values to be estimated by unit conversion.
See the [scioxide-integrate chapter](../../../scioxide-integrate/) for expectations using adaptive quadrature, and [Coulomb barrier penetration and the Gamow factor](../coulomb-barrier-penetration/) for positive-energy Coulomb scattering.
