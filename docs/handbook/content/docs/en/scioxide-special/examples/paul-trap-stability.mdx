Applying direct and radio-frequency voltages to quadrupole electrodes produces alternating restoring and outward forces proportional to distance from the axis; under suitable conditions, they confine ions near the axis.
This is the principle of the Paul trap (radio-frequency ion trap), underlying linear Paul traps and quadrupole mass spectrometers (mass filters) (Paul 1990).
Nondimensionalizing the ion's equation of motion gives the Mathieu equation, and whether the motion remains bounded depends only on two parameters $(q, a)$.
This example uses the Mathieu characteristic values $a_r(q)$ and $b_r(q)$ in `scioxide-special` to draw a stability diagram and find the apex of the first stability region, then checks the results using Floquet analysis from one-period numerical integration.
See [Mathieu functions and spectral methods](../../algorithms/mathieu-and-spheroidal/) for the characteristic-value algorithm.

## Quadrupole field and the Mathieu equation

For four electrodes along the $z$ axis, applying direct voltage $U$ [V] and radio-frequency voltage with amplitude $V$ [V] and angular frequency $\Omega$ [rad/s] gives the approximate potential near the axis

$$
\Phi(x, y, t) = \frac{U + V\cos\Omega t}{2r_0^2}\,(x^2 - y^2)
$$


Here $r_0$ [m] is the distance from the axis to the electrodes.
An ion of charge $e$ and mass $m$ obeys $m\ddot x = -e\,\partial_x\Phi$ and $m\ddot y = -e\,\partial_y\Phi$, with separable $x$ and $y$ motion.
Using dimensionless time $\tau = \Omega t/2$ and shifting the radio-frequency phase origin by half a period gives

$$
\frac{d^2u}{d\tau^2} + (a_u - 2q_u\cos 2\tau)\,u = 0,\qquad
a_x = -a_y = \frac{4eU}{mr_0^2\Omega^2},\qquad
q_x = -q_y = \frac{2eV}{mr_0^2\Omega^2}
$$

where $u = x, y$.
This is the Mathieu equation; below, write $a = a_x$ and $q = q_x \ge 0$.
There is no force in the $z$ direction, so confinement is a two-direction problem in $x$ and $y$.

## Floquet's theorem and stability

The coefficients of the Mathieu equation have period $\pi$.
For the two solutions $u_1$ and $u_2$ starting at $(u, u') = (1, 0)$ and $(0, 1)$, define the trace of the one-period propagation map (monodromy matrix) as

$$
\Delta(a, q) = u_1(\pi) + u_2'(\pi)
$$

Since its determinant is 1 by conservation of the Wronskian, its eigenvalues are the roots of $\lambda^2 - \Delta\lambda + 1 = 0$.
If $|\Delta| < 2$, the eigenvalues form a conjugate pair $e^{\pm i\pi\nu}$ on the unit circle, and all solutions are bounded (stable).
If $|\Delta| > 2$, one eigenvalue has magnitude greater than 1, and solutions grow exponentially from period to period (unstable).
The characteristic exponent $\nu$ is obtained from $\cos\pi\nu = \Delta/2$.

At the boundary $|\Delta| = 2$, there are solutions of period $\pi$ or $2\pi$, and their $a$ values are the characteristic values $a_r(q)$ and $b_r(q)$.
For $q > 0$, the characteristic values are ordered $a_0 < b_1 < a_1 < b_2 < a_2 < \cdots$, and motion in the $x$ direction is stable for $a$ in the intervals

$$
a_0(q) < a < b_1(q),\qquad a_1(q) < a < b_2(q),\qquad a_2(q) < a < b_3(q),\ \dots
$$


Shifting $\tau$ by $\pi/2$ transforms the equation for $-q$ back into that for $q$, so stability depends only on $|q|$.
Thus motion in the $y$ direction ($a_y = -a$, $q_y = -q$) is stable when $-a$ lies in one of these intervals.
An ion is confined when both $x$ and $y$ are stable.

## The first stability region and its apex

The first stability region, touching the origin, is the intersection of $a_0(q) < a < b_1(q)$ and $a_0(q) < -a < b_1(q)$.
For $a \ge 0$, the upper boundary is the smaller of $b_1(q)$ and $-a_0(q)$, whose intersection forms the apex.
For small $q$, the series $a_0(q) = -q^2/2 + O(q^4)$ and $b_1(q) = 1 - q - q^2/8 + O(q^3)$ (DLMF 28.6.1, 28.6.3) describe the boundary, but near the apex $q$ is close to 0.7, where truncating these series is unsuitable.
The apex coordinates $(q, a) \approx (0.706, 0.237)$ are widely used in the quadrupole mass-spectrometry literature; the next cell reproduces them by solving $b_1(q) + a_0(q) = 0$ using characteristic values.

A mass filter sweeps the voltages while keeping the ratio $U/V$ fixed.
Then $a/q = 2U/V$ is constant, and ions of every mass lie on the same line through the origin (scan line) in the $(q, a)$ plane.
Choosing $U/V$ so that the scan line passes just below the apex narrows the $q$ interval through the stable region, allowing only a narrow mass range to pass.
The scan line through the apex has slope $a/q \approx 0.336$, or $U/V \approx 0.168$.

## Compute the stability diagram and apex

At each grid point in $q \in [0, q_{\max}]$ and $a \in [-a_{\max}, a_{\max}]$, the next cell determines which of $x$ and $y$ are stable.
For each $q$, it obtains $a_0, b_1, \dots, a_3, b_4$ with `mathieu_a` and `mathieu_b`, then counts whether $a$ and $-a$ lie in stable intervals.
The heatmap gives the number of stable directions: 2 means confinement, 1 means stability in only one direction, and 0 means instability in both.
It finds the apex of the first stability region by bisecting $q \in [0.5, 0.9]$ to follow the sign change of $b_1(q) + a_0(q)$ (using `scioxide-special` characteristic values at each point).
The table gives the apex coordinates, scan-line slope, voltages placing an ion of mass $M$ [u] at the apex in a mass filter with $r_0$ = 4 mm and $\Omega/2\pi$ = 1 MHz, and a check that $a_r, b_r \to r^2$ as $q \to 0$.

```rust
//| id: special-paul-trap-mathieu-stability
//| ref: special-paul-trap-mathieu-stability
//| title: "Paul-trap stability diagram and apex of the first stability region"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   q_max: {"type":"range","label":"Upper limit of q","min":1,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":2}
//|   a_max: {"type":"range","label":"Upper limit of |a|","min":0.5,"max":8,"step":0.5,"value":1.5}
//|   mass: {"type":"range","label":"Ion mass M [u]","min":10,"max":500,"step":10,"value":100}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"wide","label":"q ≤ 8, |a| ≤ 8","description":"Bands stable in only one direction extend to high a; very few points are stable in both directions.","inputs":{"q_max":8,"a_max":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"apex","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.706,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"apex","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.237,"atol":5e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"apex","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.7059960697133707,"rtol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"apex","path":"/rows/6/1","approx":{"value":-8.333333312293689e-8,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: wide
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"apex","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.23699399311247382,"rtol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_special::{mathieu_a, mathieu_b};

// Characteristic values at q: [a₀, b₁, a₁, b₂, a₂, b₃, a₃, b₄] (including every band in |a| ≤ 8)
let characteristic = |q: f64| -> Result<[f64; 8], String> {
    let a = |r| mathieu_a(r, q).map_err(|e| e.to_string());
    let b = |r| mathieu_b(r, q).map_err(|e| e.to_string());
    Ok([a(0)?, b(1)?, a(1)?, b(2)?, a(2)?, b(3)?, a(3)?, b(4)?])
};
// Test membership in any stable interval (a_r, b_{r+1})
let stable = |c: &[f64; 8], value: f64| c.chunks(2).any(|w| w[0] < value && value < w[1]);
let mut map = Vec::new();
for i in 0..=80 {
    let q = q_max * f64::from(i) / 80.0;
    let c = characteristic(q)?;
    for j in 0..=80 {
        let a = a_max * (2.0 * f64::from(j) / 80.0 - 1.0);
        let count = u8::from(stable(&c, a)) + u8::from(stable(&c, -a));
        map.push([q, a, f64::from(count)]);
    }
}
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "heatmap",
    "data": map,
    "xLabel": "Radio-frequency parameter q [-]",
    "yLabel": "Direct-voltage parameter a [-]",
    "title": "Number of stable directions (2 means confinement)",
    "colorMin": 0.0,
    "colorMax": 2.0,
    "tooltip": true
}));

// Apex: solve b₁(q) + a₀(q) = 0 by bisection
let gap = |q: f64| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let c = characteristic(q)?;
    Ok(c[1] + c[0])
};
let (mut low, mut high) = (0.5, 0.9);
for _ in 0..50 {
    let middle = 0.5 * (low + high);
    if gap(middle)? > 0.0 {
        low = middle;
    } else {
        high = middle;
    }
}
let q_apex = 0.5 * (low + high);
let a_apex = mathieu_b(1, q_apex).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Voltages placing the ion at the apex in a mass filter (r₀ = 4 mm, Ω/2π = 1 MHz)
let (charge, unit_mass) = (1.602176634e-19, 1.66053906660e-27);
let omega = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * 1.0e6;
let scale = mass * unit_mass * 4.0e-3_f64.powi(2) * omega * omega / charge;
let rows = vec![
    ("q at apex", q_apex, Some(0.706)),
    ("a at apex", a_apex, Some(0.237)),
    ("Scan-line slope a/q", a_apex / q_apex, None),
    ("Radio-frequency amplitude V [V]", q_apex * scale / 2.0, None),
    ("Direct voltage U [V]", a_apex * scale / 4.0, None),
    (
        "a₁(10⁻³) − 1",
        mathieu_a(1, 1e-3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - 1.0,
        Some(1e-3),
    ),
    (
        "b₂(10⁻³) − 4",
        mathieu_b(2, 1e-3).map_err(|e| e.to_string())? - 4.0,
        Some(-1e-6 / 12.0),
    ),
];
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Computed value", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Literature value or leading series term", "type": "number", "format": fixed6, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "apex" })
);
```

The heatmap's value-2 confinement region extends along $a = 0$ from the origin to $q \approx 0.91$, forming an approximately triangular shape with upper and lower apexes at $a = \pm 0.237$.
Along $a = 0$, when $q$ exceeds 0.908, $b_1(q) < 0$ makes the $x$ direction unstable and the value drops to 0.
Value-1 bands are stable in only one direction: the $x$ bands extend diagonally along characteristic-value curves on the $a > 0$ side, and the $y$ bands on the $a < 0$ side.
Expanding to $q, |a| \le 8$ reveals one-direction stable bands up to high $a$, but only 13 of the 6561 grid points are stable in both directions.
The second and higher confinement regions are too narrow to resolve on this grid.

The tabulated apex $(q, a) = (0.705996, 0.236994)$ agrees to the displayed digits with the commonly cited $(0.706, 0.237)$.
The scan-line slope is $a/q = 0.3357$, corresponding to $U/V = 0.168$.
Placing a 100 u ion at the apex in a 1 MHz mass filter with $r_0$ = 4 mm requires $V \approx 231$ V and $U \approx 38.8$ V.
Both $V$ and $U$ are proportional to mass, so sweeping the voltages at fixed ratio moves ions through the apex in mass order.
At $q = 10^{-3}$, the characteristic values give $a_1 - 1 = 0.00099987$ (differing from the leading term $q$ by $-q^2/8$) and $b_2 - 4 = -8.3333 \times 10^{-8}$ (leading term $-q^2/12$), approaching $r^2$ as $q \to 0$.


## Check with Floquet analysis

Since the stability diagram relies on characteristic-value ordering, check it with a separate method.
The next cell integrates the Mathieu equation for one period $[0, \pi]$ at the selected $(q, a)$ using classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta and computes the monodromy-matrix trace $\Delta$.
This integration is an independent check implemented manually within the cell, without `scioxide-special`.
The table also classifies the same point using characteristic-value intervals and shows whether the two classifications agree.
For stable motion, it computes the characteristic exponent $\nu = \arccos(\Delta/2)/\pi$ and compares it with the small-$a$, small-$q$ approximation $\nu \approx \sqrt{a + q^2/2}$ (Dehmelt's approximation).
The exponent $\nu$ gives the angular frequency $\omega = \nu\Omega/2$ of the ion's secular motion, a slow oscillation below the radio frequency.
The chart shows the trajectory $x(\tau)$ integrated over 10 periods: the amplitude remains bounded for stable motion and grows exponentially for unstable motion.
General-purpose ODE integration methods are covered in the `scioxide-ode` chapter.

```rust
//| id: special-paul-trap-floquet
//| ref: special-paul-trap-floquet
//| title: "Compare one-period Floquet analysis with characteristic-value intervals"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-special]
//| inputs:
//|   q: {"type":"range","label":"q","min":0,"max":1.2,"step":0.01,"value":0.4}
//|   a: {"type":"range","label":"a","min":-0.5,"max":0.5,"step":0.01,"value":0.05}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"near-apex","label":"q = 0.70, a = 0.23","description":"Both x and y are stable just inside the apex.","inputs":{"q":0.7,"a":0.23}}
//|   - {"id":"unstable","label":"q = 1.0, a = 0","description":"The x direction becomes unstable when q exceeds 0.908.","inputs":{"q":1.0,"a":0.0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"floquet","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":true}
//|       - {"artifact":"floquet","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":true}
//|       - {"artifact":"floquet","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.3751312339480702,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|   - case: near-apex
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"floquet","path":"/rows/1/2","equals":true}
//|       - {"artifact":"floquet","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":true}
//|   - case: unstable
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"floquet","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":false}
//|       - {"artifact":"floquet","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":false}
//|       - {"artifact":"floquet","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":-2.8333904963392245,"rtol":1e-6}}
use scioxide_special::{mathieu_a, mathieu_b};

// Integrate u'' = −(a − 2q cos 2τ)u with fourth-order Runge–Kutta (independent check)
let force = |t: f64, u: f64, a: f64| -(a - 2.0 * q * (2.0 * t).cos()) * u;
let propagate = |state: [f64; 2], start: f64, steps: usize, a: f64| -> [f64; 2] {
    let h = std::f64::consts::PI / 2000.0;
    let [mut u, mut v] = state;
    for k in 0..steps {
        let t = start + h * k as f64;
        let (k1u, k1v) = (v, force(t, u, a));
        let (k2u, k2v) = (v + 0.5 * h * k1v, force(t + 0.5 * h, u + 0.5 * h * k1u, a));
        let (k3u, k3v) = (v + 0.5 * h * k2v, force(t + 0.5 * h, u + 0.5 * h * k2u, a));
        let (k4u, k4v) = (v + h * k3v, force(t + h, u + h * k3u, a));
        u += h / 6.0 * (k1u + 2.0 * k2u + 2.0 * k3u + k4u);
        v += h / 6.0 * (k1v + 2.0 * k2v + 2.0 * k3v + k4v);
    }
    [u, v]
};
let characteristic = [
    mathieu_a(0, q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    mathieu_b(1, q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    mathieu_a(1, q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    mathieu_b(2, q).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
];
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (direction, value) in [("x", a), ("y", -a)] {
    let first = propagate([1.0, 0.0], 0.0, 2000, value);
    let second = propagate([0.0, 1.0], 0.0, 2000, value);
    let trace = first[0] + second[1];
    let by_trace = trace.abs() < 2.0;
    let by_values = characteristic
        .chunks(2)
        .any(|w| w[0] < value && value < w[1]);
    let nu = by_trace.then(|| (trace / 2.0).acos() / std::f64::consts::PI);
    let dehmelt = (value + q * q / 2.0 > 0.0).then(|| (value + q * q / 2.0).sqrt());
    rows.push((direction, trace, by_trace, by_values, nu, dehmelt));
}
// Plot the x(τ) trajectory for 10 periods (initial x = 1, x' = 0)
let mut orbit = vec![[0.0, 1.0]];
let mut state = [1.0, 0.0];
for k in 0..400 {
    let start = std::f64::consts::PI * f64::from(k) / 40.0;
    state = propagate(state, start, 50, a);
    orbit.push([start + std::f64::consts::PI / 40.0, state[0]]);
}
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![serde_json::json!({ "name": "x(τ)", "points": orbit })],
    options =
        &serde_json::json!({ "xLabel": "Dimensionless time τ = Ωt/2 [-]", "yLabel": "Displacement x/x₀ [-]" })
);
let fixed6 = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 6 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Direction" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Trace Δ", "type": "number", "format": fixed6 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "|Δ| < 2", "type": "boolean" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Stable by characteristic-value intervals", "type": "boolean" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Characteristic exponent ν", "type": "number", "format": fixed6, "nullText": "Unstable" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "√(a + q²/2)", "type": "number", "format": fixed6, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "floquet" })
);
```

At the default $q = 0.4$ and $a = 0.05$, the trace is $\Delta = 0.765$ in the $x$ direction and $1.701$ in the $y$ direction; both satisfy $|\Delta| < 2$ and agree with the characteristic-interval classification.
The characteristic exponents are $\nu = 0.3751$ for $x$ and $0.1762$ for $y$, differing from Dehmelt's approximations $0.3606$ and $0.1732$ by 4% for $x$ and 2% for $y$.
The approximation retains only the leading $q^2$ term and departs further as $q$ increases.
Just inside the apex, at $q = 0.70$ and $a = 0.23$, the $x$ trace is $-1.929$ and the $y$ trace is $1.961$, both close to their respective boundaries $\mp 2$.
The approximate $\nu$ is 0.69 for $x$ (0.915 by integration) and 0.12 for $y$ (0.063 by integration), a substantial departure, so use characteristic values or integration near the apex.
At $q = 1.0$ and $a = 0$, the trace is $-2.833$ with $|\Delta| > 2$, and both classifications indicate instability.
The plotted $x(\tau)$ oscillates with a constant envelope at stable points and grows by a factor $|\lambda| \approx 2.4$ per period at unstable points.


## Summary

Ion motion in a linear quadrupole follows the Mathieu equation; confinement requires $(q, a)$ to lie in characteristic-value stability intervals for both $x$ and $y$.
The characteristic values $a_r(q)$ and $b_r(q)$ come from `mathieu_a` and `mathieu_b`, and their ordering alone determines the stability diagram.
The apex of the first stability region solves $b_1(q) = -a_0(q)$, giving $(q, a) = (0.706, 0.237)$ and fixing the mass-filter voltage ratio $U/V \approx 0.168$.
Characteristic-value classification agrees with the one-period monodromy-trace criterion $|\Delta| < 2$; the two classifications agree at points away from the boundaries.
