For models without closed-form conjugate posteriors, Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) draws posterior samples to estimate expectations and quantiles.
MCMC samples are states of a Markov chain constructed to have the posterior as its stationary distribution; they are not independent.
Samples alone must be used to examine whether initial-state effects have dissipated and stationarity has been reached, and how many independent samples' worth of information remains after accounting for correlation.
This page derives posterior summaries and convergence diagnostics (R̂, effective sample size, and Monte Carlo standard error) computed by `posterior_summary` in `scioxide-stats` and explains their inputs and outputs.
Closed-form conjugate posteriors appear in [Conjugate priors and Bayesian updating](../bayesian-conjugate-updates/).

## Diagnosing and summarizing posterior MCMC chains

### Available and unavailable capabilities

`scioxide-stats` provides no MCMC samplers (Metropolis–Hastings, Gibbs, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, or NUTS).
Construct chains yourself from `scioxide-random` generators and distributions, or with external probabilistic-programming tools, and pass them as `scioxide-linalg` matrix views.
`scioxide-stats` summarizes and diagnoses chains after warmup (burn-in) has been removed.
Automatic warmup detection and removal, thinning, highest posterior density (HPD) intervals, quantile Monte Carlo standard errors, and multivariate R̂ and effective sample sizes are not provided.
To inspect the autocorrelation function itself, apply the autocorrelation estimates in [Time-series analysis](../time-series/) to each chain.

### Monte Carlo standard error and effective sample size

When a stationary chain $x_1, \dots, x_N$ estimates posterior mean $E[\theta]$ by its mean $\bar x$, independent samples give $\mathrm{Var}(\bar x) = \sigma^2/N$, while correlated samples give

$$
\mathrm{Var}(\bar x) \approx \frac{\sigma^2}{N}\,\tau, \qquad
\tau = 1 + 2\sum_{t=1}^{\infty} \rho_t
$$


$\sigma^2$ is posterior variance, $\rho_t$ is lag-$t$ autocorrelation, and $\tau$ is the integrated autocorrelation time.
Effective sample size $\mathrm{ESS} = N/\tau$ is the number of independent samples giving equal precision, with Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE) $\sqrt{\sigma^2/\mathrm{ESS}}$.
For example, an AR(1) process with coefficient $\phi$ has $\rho_t = \phi^t$ and thus $\tau = (1+\phi)/(1-\phi)$; at $\phi = 0.81$, $\tau \approx 9.5$, so ten samples give about one independent sample's information.

### Split chains and R̂

Run $M$ chains from different initial values and split each chain's $D$ samples into first and second halves of $\lfloor D/2 \rfloor$ (discarding the middle sample if $D$ is odd).
Treat the $2M$ split chains as length-$n = \lfloor D/2 \rfloor$ chains and form the following from mean within-chain variance $W$ and variance of chain means $B/n$:

$$
\hat V = \frac{n-1}{n} W + \frac{B}{n}, \qquad \hat R = \sqrt{\hat V / W}
$$


If all chains sample the same stationary distribution, $\hat V$ and $W$ approach $\sigma^2$ and $\hat R \to 1$.
Remaining initial-state effects or chains trapped in different distribution regions disperse chain means and give $\hat R > 1$.
Splitting chains allows drift within a single chain (nonstationarity) to be detected as between-chain differences.

Vehtari et al. (2021) apply this $\hat R$ after two transformations.
Rank normalization replaces average rank $r$ among all $S = 2Mn$ split samples with $z = \Phi^{-1}\!\left((r - 3/8)/(S + 1/4)\right)$ ($\Phi^{-1}$ is the standard-normal quantile function).
This defines $\hat R$ even for heavy-tailed distributions or those without a variance, allowing location-shift detection.
Folding replaces samples $x$ by distances from the median $|x - \mathrm{median}|$ before rank normalization, detecting scale (spread) differences.
The reported $\hat R$ is the larger of the two values; values below 1 are not rounded to 1.
The same paper recommends $\hat R < 1.01$ and at least 400 bulk and tail effective samples each across at least four chains as guidelines for using posterior summaries.
These are diagnostics: $\hat R$ near 1 and a large effective sample size do not prove convergence.
If every chain visits only the same part of the distribution, none of these diagnostics can detect the problem.

### Autocorrelation and Geyer's initial monotone sequence

Autocorrelation is estimated from mean lag-$t$ autocovariance $\bar c_t$ over split chains as

$$
\hat\rho_t = 1 - \frac{W - \bar c_t}{\hat V}
$$

(the same estimator as Stan and ArviZ).
At large lags, noise dominates $\hat\rho_t$, so the sum is truncated.
Geyer's (1992) initial positive sequence stops before adjacent-pair sums $P_k = \hat\rho_{2k} + \hat\rho_{2k+1}$ become negative; the initial monotone sequence additionally smooths $P_k$ to be nonincreasing.
The resulting $\hat\tau$ has lower bound $1/\log_{10} S$, so negatively correlated (antithetic) chains may have effective sample sizes above $S$.
For maximum lag $L$, cost is $O(SL)$ and memory is $O(MD)$.

### Posterior-summary outputs

`PosteriorSummary` has the following public fields for one real parameter.

| Field | Contents |
|---|---|
| `mean` | Mean of all $N = MD$ samples |
| `variance` | Sample variance of all samples (divisor $N-1$) |
| `quantiles` | 5%, 50%, and 95% quantiles of all samples (Hyndman–Fan type 7) |
| `rhat` | Maximum rank-normalized and folded split $\hat R$ |
| `bulk_ess` | Bulk effective sample size: ESS of rank-normalized split chains |
| `tail_ess` | Tail effective sample size: minimum ESS of indicators $\mathbf 1\{x \le q_{0.05}\}$ and $\mathbf 1\{x \le q_{0.95}\}$ |
| `mean_mcse` | Mean Monte Carlo standard error $\sqrt{\mathrm{variance}/\mathrm{ESS}}$, using ESS from untransformed split chains |
| `chains`, `draws_per_chain` | Number of chains $M$ and samples per chain $D$ |

`rhat`, `bulk_ess`, `tail_ess`, and `mean_mcse` are `Option<f64>`, becoming `None` when undefined because the samples they depend on do not vary (such as zero within-chain variance).
Means, variances, and quantiles use all samples; diagnostics use split chains, omitting middle samples when $D$ is odd.
Outputs use `f64` regardless of input type.

### Inputs and call forms

Supply chains as matrix view `MatrixView`, where row $i$ contains the $D$ post-warmup samples of chain $i$.
Fewer than two chains or four samples per chain returns `InvalidInput`; NaN or infinite samples return `NonFinite`.

`PosteriorOptions` has three limits, all unlimited by default.
`with_max_workspace_bytes` limits workspace memory excluding inputs and results; exceeding it returns `ResourceLimit`.
`with_max_lag` is the maximum lag used for ESS; 0 uses all lags.
A limit truncates Geyer's sequence at that lag, while ESS normalization still uses the total split-sample count.
`with_max_lag_work` limits autocorrelation product evaluations; the computation requires $5 \cdot 2M\lfloor D/2\rfloor\,(L+1)$.
Exceeding this returns `ConvergenceFailure` (`EvaluationLimit`) before computation starts.

The one-shot `posterior_summary(chains)` uses default `PosteriorOptions`; the detailed form `posterior_summary_with_options(chains, options)` accepts limits.
The borrowed form `posterior_summaries_into(parameters, options, output, context)` accepts a slice of per-parameter matrix views and writes summaries to `output[i]`.
Parameters run in parallel if the execution context permits; results are independent of worker count.
The memory limit applies to the sum of concurrently active workers' workspaces.
An `output` length differing from the parameter count returns `InvalidInput`; cancellation returns `ExecutionStopped`.

## Gibbs sampling of a bivariate normal distribution

Sample a standard bivariate normal distribution $(x, y)$ with correlation $\rho$ by Gibbs sampling, alternating draws from conditional distributions

$$
x \mid y \sim N(\rho y,\; 1 - \rho^2), \qquad y \mid x \sim N(\rho x,\; 1 - \rho^2)
$$

(Gelman et al., BDA, 3rd ed., §11.1).
The $x$ sequence is the AR(1) process $x_{t+1} = \rho^2 x_t + (\text{independent noise})$ with coefficient $\phi = \rho^2$, giving theoretical values

$$
\mathrm{ESS}_{\text{theory}} = N \frac{1 - \rho^2}{1 + \rho^2}, \qquad
\mathrm{MCSE}_{\text{theory}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \cdot \frac{1 + \rho^2}{1 - \rho^2}}
$$

These check estimated ESS and MCSE (marginal variance is 1).
The $y$ sequence follows the same AR(1) process.
As correlation $\rho$ approaches 1, conditional width $\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ narrows and the chain moves slowly through the distribution.

The next cell runs four chains with initial $y$ values $+s$, $-s$, $+s$, and $-s$, then uses `posterior_summaries_into` to summarize $x$ and $y$ together without removing warmup.
Inputs are correlation $\rho$, samples per chain $D$, initial-state displacement $s$, and random seed.
The table's $z$ is $\bar x / \mathrm{MCSE}$, measuring deviation from true mean 0 in estimated MCSE units.
The chart shows traces of the four $x$ chains; remaining initial-state effects appear as separated chains.
The computation is expensive, so it runs on a button.

```rust
//| id: stats-bayesian-conjugate-updates-api-6
//| ref: stats-bayesian-conjugate-updates-api-6
//| title: "Compare R̂ and effective sample size with theory using bivariate-normal Gibbs sampling"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-random, scioxide-stats]
//| inputs:
//|   rho: {"type":"range","label":"Correlation ρ [-]","min":0,"max":0.95,"step":0.01,"value":0.9}
//|   draws: {"type":"range","label":"Samples per chain D","min":100,"max":500,"step":50,"value":500}
//|   start: {"type":"range","label":"Initial-state displacement s [-]","min":0,"max":10,"step":0.5,"value":0}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"independent","label":"ρ = 0","description":"Samples are independent and ESS approaches total sample count.","inputs":{"rho":0}}
//|   - {"id":"stuck","label":"ρ = 0.95, s = 10, D = 100","description":"Initial-state effects remain, making R̂ far above 1.","inputs":{"rho":0.95,"start":10,"draws":100}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":1.02196207,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/6","approx":{"value":220.151447,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":150,"max":300}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/8","approx":{"value":0.0675258953,"rtol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/2/6","approx":{"value":209.944751381215,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/2/8","approx":{"value":0.0690156351469494,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: independent
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"min":1800,"max":2200}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/2/6","equals":2000}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1.01}}
//|   - case: stuck
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"min":1.1}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"min":2}}
use scioxide_core::{ExecutionContext, RealScalar};
use scioxide_linalg::MatrixView;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, Quantile, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};
use scioxide_stats::{PosteriorOptions, posterior_summaries_into};

let (chains, length) = (4, draws as usize);
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let noise = Normal::new(0.0, RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 - rho * rho)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut xs = vec![0.0; chains * length];
let mut ys = vec![0.0; chains * length];
for chain in 0..chains {
    // Start y at ±s for each chain and alternately draw x and y from conditional distributions
    let mut y = if chain % 2 == 0 { start } else { -start };
    for step in 0..length {
        let x = rho * y + noise.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        y = rho * x + noise.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        xs[chain * length + step] = x;
        ys[chain * length + step] = y;
    }
}
let views = [
    MatrixView::from_slice(chains, length, &xs).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
    MatrixView::from_slice(chains, length, &ys).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?,
];
let mut output = [None, None];
let context = ExecutionContext::sequential();
posterior_summaries_into(&views, PosteriorOptions::default(), &mut output, context)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// AR(1) theory (coefficient ρ²)
let total = (chains * length) as f64;
let phi = rho * rho;
let ess_theory = total * (1.0 - phi) / (1.0 + phi);
let mcse_theory = RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 / ess_theory);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
for (name, summary) in ["x", "y"].iter().zip(output) {
    let s = summary.ok_or("Summary is absent")?;
    let z = s.mean_mcse.map(|mcse| s.mean / mcse);
    rows.push((
        *name,
        s.mean,
        s.variance,
        s.quantiles[0],
        s.quantiles[2],
        s.rhat,
        s.bulk_ess,
        s.tail_ess,
        s.mean_mcse,
        z,
    ));
}
let z95 = Normal::new(0.0, 1.0)
    .and_then(|law| law.quantile(0.95))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
rows.push((
    "Theoretical value",
    0.0,
    1.0,
    -z95,
    z95,
    Some(1.0),
    Some(ess_theory),
    None,
    Some(mcse_theory),
    None,
));
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed0 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 0 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Parameter" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Variance [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "5% quantile [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "95% quantile [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "R̂ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "－" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Bulk ESS", "type": "number", "format": fixed0, "nullText": "－" },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Tail ESS", "type": "number", "format": fixed0, "nullText": "－" },
    { "key": "8", "label": "MCSE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "－" },
    { "key": "9", "label": "z = mean/MCSE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "－" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
let series: Vec<_> = (0..chains)
    .map(|chain| {
        let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = (0..length)
            .map(|step| [step as f64, xs[chain * length + step]])
            .collect();
        serde_json::json!({ "name": format!("Chain {}", chain + 1), "points": points })
    })
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({ "xLabel": "Iteration t [-]", "yLabel": "x sample [-]" })
);
```

At the defaults ($\rho = 0.9$, $D = 500$, $s = 0$), bulk ESS for $x$ is 220, within 5% of theoretical 210; MCSE 0.0675 is also close to theoretical 0.0690.
The total 2000 samples contain only about 200 independent samples' information.
Mean $-0.067$ has standardized value $z = -1.0$ in MCSE units, consistent with the true 0.
However, $\hat R = 1.022$ exceeds Vehtari et al.'s guideline 1.01 and ESS is below 400, indicating that these chains should be extended before using their summaries.
Tail ESS of 450 exceeds bulk ESS because the 5% and 95% quantile indicators' autocorrelations decay faster.
With “ρ = 0”, samples are independent, ESS is 2025 (theory 2000), and $\hat R = 0.9998$.
$\hat R$ may be below 1 and is not rounded to 1.
With “ρ = 0.95, s = 10, D = 100”, initial-state effects from $\pm 10$ remain after 100 iterations, traces separate above and below, and $\hat R = 1.29$.
Variance increases to 5.18, five times the true 1, and estimated MCSE 0.63 is three times theoretical 0.22; these chain summaries cannot be used.
Removing warmup and extending chains makes $\hat R$ approach 1.

## Summary

`scioxide-stats` provides no MCMC samplers; construct chains yourself with `scioxide-random` or other tools, remove warmup, and pass matrix views with chains as rows and samples as columns to `posterior_summary`.
Before using summaries, require `rhat` below 1.01 and sufficiently large `bulk_ess` and `tail_ess` (at least 400 each across four chains); otherwise extend the chains.
Measure posterior-mean uncertainty with `mean_mcse`; only 5%, 50%, and 95% quantiles are returned, so compute other quantiles or HPD intervals yourself from samples.
Summarize multiple parameters together with `posterior_summaries_into` and use `PosteriorOptions` to bound memory and autocorrelation work for long chains.
These diagnostics cannot detect chains all visiting only the same part of a distribution; check multiple chains with dispersed initial values.
