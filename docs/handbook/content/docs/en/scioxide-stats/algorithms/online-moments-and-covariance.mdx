Updating means and variances as observations arrive one by one is called **online aggregation**.
It retains no samples, so memory remains constant regardless of sample count.
Separately computed aggregates can also be merged later.
This page explains why textbook variance formulas fail in floating-point arithmetic and derives Welford recurrences, their merge generalization, third and fourth central moments, and bivariate covariance.
It then explains the `OnlineMoments` and `OnlineCovariance` accumulators and `reduce_*` functions aggregating matrix views in batches.

## Cancellation in the naive variance formula

For sample $x_1, \dots, x_n$, mean $\bar x$, and sum of squared deviations $S = \sum_i (x_i - \bar x)^2$, the sample variance is $s^2 = S/(n-1)$.
Expanding $S$ gives the following formula computable in one pass over the data.

$$
S = \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 - \frac{1}{n}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\Bigr)^2
$$

This formula subtracts two large numbers.
When mean $\mu$ is orders of magnitude greater than standard deviation $\sigma$, the terms are nearly equal and their leading digits cancel on subtraction.
This is called **cancellation**.

Chan, Golub, and LeVeque (1983) express the condition number of $S$ under data perturbations as

$$
\kappa = \frac{\lVert x \rVert_2}{\sqrt S} = \sqrt{1 + \frac{\mu^2}{\sigma^2}}, \qquad \sigma^2 = \frac{S}{n}
$$


Here, $\mu$ is the sample mean and $\lVert x \rVert_2$ is the 2-norm of the data vector.
For unit roundoff $u = 2^{-53} \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-16}$, approximate relative-error bounds for each algorithm are as follows.

| Algorithm | Approximate relative-error bound |
|---|---|
| One-pass formula (difference of sums of squares) | $n \kappa^2 u$ |
| Two passes (compute the mean, then sum deviations) | $n u + n^2 \kappa^2 u^2$ |
| Online updates (Welford) | $n \kappa u$ |
| Pairwise merging of partial aggregates | $\kappa u \log_2 n$ |

The one-pass bound is proportional to $\kappa^2$; at $\mu/\sigma \approx 10^8$ it exceeds 1, so every significant digit may be lost.
The computed variance can even be negative.
These are worst-case bounds; actual errors may be much smaller for particular data.
Two-pass algorithms remain accurate for large $\kappa$ but read data twice, making them unsuitable for streams.

## Welford recurrence

Let $\bar x_{n-1}$ be the mean through observation $n-1$ and $M_{2,n-1}$ the sum of squared deviations; write the difference from the new observation $x_n$ as $\delta_n = x_n - \bar x_{n-1}$.
The mean updates as follows.

$$
\bar x_n = \bar x_{n-1} + \frac{\delta_n}{n}
$$

Square the deviation $x_i - \bar x_n = (x_i - \bar x_{n-1}) - \delta_n/n$ and sum over $i < n$.
Since $\sum_{i<n} (x_i - \bar x_{n-1}) = 0$, the cross term vanishes, giving $\sum_{i<n}(x_i - \bar x_n)^2 = M_{2,n-1} + (n-1)\delta_n^2/n^2$.
Adding the contribution $((n-1)\delta_n/n)^2$ of $x_n$ gives the recurrence (Welford, 1962).

$$
M_{2,n} = M_{2,n-1} + \frac{n-1}{n}\delta_n^2 = M_{2,n-1} + (x_n - \bar x_{n-1})(x_n - \bar x_n)
$$

Only products of deviations are accumulated, avoiding differences between large sums of squares.
Substituting $n_B = 1$ and $M_{2,B} = 0$ into the next section's merge formula gives this recurrence.

## Merging partial aggregates

For subsets $A$ and $B$, let sizes be $n_A$ and $n_B$, means $\bar x_A$ and $\bar x_B$, squared-deviation sums $M_{2,A}$ and $M_{2,B}$, and set $n = n_A + n_B$ and $\Delta = \bar x_B - \bar x_A$.
Reexpressing each group's deviations relative to the overall mean and using zero within-group deviation sums gives the following (Chan, Golub, LeVeque, 1979).

$$
\bar x_{AB} = \bar x_A + \frac{n_B}{n}\Delta, \qquad
M_{2,AB} = M_{2,A} + M_{2,B} + \frac{n_A n_B}{n}\Delta^2
$$

The last term is variation between group means.
Simply averaging group variances omits this term and underestimates overall variance.
Parallel and streaming computations pairwise merge partition aggregates $(n_k, \bar x_k, M_{2,k})$ into final statistics.
Binary-tree merging makes the error estimate proportional to $\log_2 n$ instead of $n$, as in the last row of the preceding table.

Merging is mathematically associative, but floating-point rounding depends on merge order.
Different partitions and merge orders can therefore change trailing result digits even for identical data.
The [Merge order and rounding](#merge-order-and-rounding) cell measures these differences.

## Accumulator state and API

`OnlineMoments::new(order)` creates an empty accumulator; `observe(x)` treats an observation as a size-one aggregate and merges it into the current state with the formula above.
`merge(&other)` merges accumulators from disjoint partitions.
Both cost $O(1)$ operations per call and allocate nothing.
`from_slice(values, order)` constructs an accumulator from a whole slice.
If every value is within the ordinary f64 range, it uses a compensated first pass for a shifted-origin mean and a second pass summing centered powers.
Trailing digits may therefore differ from adding the same values individually with `observe`.

Internal state contains count, mean, minimum and maximum, and central moments divided by count $M_k/n$.
The mean is retained as a compensated sum of high and low parts; updates and merges preserve the low part.
Mean differences $\Delta$ for merging subtract high and low parts separately before recombining, cancelling large common offsets first.
Values outside the ordinary f64 range switch state to an extended-range representation with a separate binary exponent (`scioxide_core::Scaled`), continuing aggregation without overflow.

`observe` rejects nonfinite values with `NonFinite` and count overflow with `SizeOverflow`.
`merge` rejects differing retained moment orders with `InvalidInput`; merging an empty accumulator changes nothing.
If merging produces negative even-order $M_2$ or $M_4$, it returns `NumericalInstability` for cancellation.
Any failure leaves accumulator state unchanged.

`snapshot()` only returns a state copy `MomentsSnapshot` and cannot fail.
Each estimate is rounded to `S` on every query and returned as `Result<Option<S>, SciOxideError>`.
Undefined estimates (empty-sample means or one-point sample variances) become `Ok(None)`; a value unrepresentable in `S` causes only its own query to return `Err`.
For example, for the two points $\pm 10^{154}$, sample variance $2 \times 10^{308}$ overflows f64 and returns `Err`, while mean 0 and standard error $10^{154}$ remain available.
Primary queries are `mean`, `population_variance` (divisor $n$), `sample_variance` (divisor $n-1$), `variance(ddof)`, `variance_of_sample_mean` ($s^2/n$), `standard_error_of_sample_mean` ($s/\sqrt n$), `minimum`, and `maximum`.
`scaled_variance_of_sample_mean` reads the variance of the mean without rounding to `S`; its use in intervals appears in [Mathematics of weighted estimation, standard errors, and confidence intervals](oxref:stats-weighted-mean-confidence-interval).

## Higher central moments, skewness, and kurtosis

Third and fourth central-moment sums $M_3 = \sum (x_i - \bar x)^3$ and $M_4 = \sum (x_i - \bar x)^4$ merge similarly by binomial expansion (Pébay, 2008; Pébay et al., 2016).

$$
M_{3,AB} = M_{3,A} + M_{3,B} + \Delta^3 \frac{n_A n_B (n_A - n_B)}{n^2} + 3\Delta \frac{n_A M_{2,B} - n_B M_{2,A}}{n}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{4,AB} ={}& M_{4,A} + M_{4,B} + \Delta^4 \frac{n_A n_B (n_A^2 - n_A n_B + n_B^2)}{n^3} \\
&+ 6\Delta^2 \frac{n_A^2 M_{2,B} + n_B^2 M_{2,A}}{n^2} + 4\Delta \frac{n_A M_{3,B} - n_B M_{3,A}}{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

Higher orders require more operations per update; choose the accumulator's maximum retained order (`Second`, `Third`, or `Fourth`) with `MomentOrder` in advance.
Querying an unretained order returns `NotImplemented`.
`central_moment(k)` returns population central moments $m_k = M_k / n$ ($m_0 = 1$, $m_1 = 0$).

From $m_k$, compute sample skewness $g_1 = m_3 / m_2^{3/2}$ and excess kurtosis $g_2 = m_4/m_2^2 - 3$.
`skewness(BiasCorrection::None)` and `kurtosis(BiasCorrection::None, …)` return these $g_1$ and $g_2$.
`BiasCorrection::Corrected` applies independent-sample corrections ($G_1$ and $G_2$ in Joanes and Gill, 1998).

$$
G_1 = \frac{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}{n-2}\, g_1 \quad (n \ge 3), \qquad
G_2 = \frac{n-1}{(n-2)(n-3)}\bigl((n+1) g_2 + 6\bigr) \quad (n \ge 4)
$$

$G_1$ and $G_2$ are ratios of k-statistics.
The k-statistic $k_r$ is an unbiased estimator of the order-$r$ cumulant $\kappa_r$, but ratios are generally biased.
`k_statistic(r)` ($r = 1, \dots, 4$) returns $k_r$ itself; `k_statistic_variance(r)` ($r = 1, 2$) returns an unbiased estimator of its variance.
The variance estimate for $r = 2$ can be negative in some samples.
`KurtosisConvention::Fisher` returns excess kurtosis (0 for a normal distribution); `Pearson` adds 3.
Empty or constant samples, or counts insufficient for the correction, return `None`.

## Bivariate covariance and correlation

The cross-deviation sum $C = \sum (x_i - \bar x)(y_i - \bar y)$ of pairs $(x_i, y_i)$ also updates and merges using $\delta_{x,n} = x_n - \bar x_{n-1}$, $\Delta_x = \bar x_B - \bar x_A$, and $\Delta_y = \bar y_B - \bar y_A$ as follows.

$$
C_n = C_{n-1} + \delta_{x,n}\,(y_n - \bar y_n), \qquad
C_{AB} = C_A + C_B + \frac{n_A n_B}{n}\Delta_x \Delta_y
$$

Sample covariance is $C/(n-1)$; Pearson correlation is $r = C/\sqrt{M_{2,x} M_{2,y}}$.

`OnlineCovariance` treats each pair as an independent estimation unit, updating both marginal moments and cross-deviation sums from the same pairs.
`from_slices(x, y, order)` rejects mismatched input lengths with `DimensionMismatch` and recomputes with exact products if the cross-deviation sum suffers severe cancellation.
`CovarianceSnapshot` returns `population_covariance`, `sample_covariance`, covariance between means `covariance_of_sample_means` ($C/(n(n-1))$), and marginal `marginal_x` and `marginal_y`.
`correlation` computes correlation from marginal variances before rounding and clips to $[-1, 1]$.
If unclipped $|r|$ exceeds $1 + 128\varepsilon$, the cross-deviation sum is considered to have lost accuracy to cancellation, returning `NumericalInstability`.

Covariance and correlation definedness are reported separately.
`CovarianceValidity` returned by `validity()` distinguishes no pairs (`Empty`), only population covariance defined (`PopulationOnly`), and sample covariance also defined (`SampleDefined`).
`CorrelationValidity` returned by `correlation_validity()` distinguishes defined (`Defined`), insufficient pairs (`InsufficientPairs`), and which coordinates are constant (`ConstantX`, `ConstantY`, `ConstantBoth`).
When correlation is undefined, `correlation` returns `Ok(None)`.

For covariance matrices with more than two variables, use matrix-based `sample_covariance` and related APIs ([Multivariate statistics](../multivariate-statistics/)).
For frequency tables whose rows have integer counts, passing counts as `Weights::<_, Frequency>` to `frequency_covariance` computes means and covariance without expanding rows.

## Batched matrix-view reductions

To aggregate multidimensional-array rows or columns as separate samples, wrap the array in `BatchView` and pass it to `reduce_*` functions.
The `axis` of `BatchView::new(&values, layout, axis)` is the sample axis; each position on the remaining axes is a **batch** (an independent sample, called a unit of batch processing in the API guide).
Output contains one snapshot per batch, each holding the same quantities as feeding that batch's observations into an accumulator individually.

| Default form | Settings form | Workspace form | Output per batch | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `reduce_moments` | `reduce_moments_with_options` | `reduce_moments_into` | `MomentsSnapshot` | Means, variances, higher moments |
| `reduce_trimmed_moments` | `reduce_trimmed_moments_with_options` | `reduce_trimmed_moments_into` | `MomentsSnapshot` | Moments of observations within the value range `TrimLimits` |
| `reduce_weighted_moments` | `reduce_weighted_moments_with_options` | `reduce_weighted_moments_into` | `WeightedMomentsSnapshot` | Moments using view weights (frequency, reliability, analytical) |
| `reduce_covariance` | `reduce_covariance_with_options` | `reduce_covariance_into` | `CovarianceSnapshot` | Pairwise covariance of two identically shaped views |
| `reduce_weighted_covariance` | `reduce_weighted_covariance_with_options` | `reduce_weighted_covariance_into` | `WeightedCovarianceSnapshot` | Weighted pairwise covariance |
| `reduce_moment` | `reduce_moment_with_options` | `reduce_moment_into` | `Option<S>` | Moments of arbitrary order $k$ around a specified center |

As shown, each function has three forms.
Unsuffixed forms (such as `reduce_moments(view)`) use defaults; `_with_options` forms accept `StatisticsOptions`.
Both allocate workspace and output per call and execute sequentially on the current thread.
`_into` forms (such as `reduce_moments_into(view, options, output, workspace, context)`) write caller-provided output slices and workspace and accept execution context `ExecutionContext`.
Query workspace sizes with `ReductionWorkspace::workspace_requirements(view, options, context)`.
The returned `ReductionRequirements` contains batch count `batches()`, partitions per batch `workers()`, and workspace bytes `required_bytes::<S>()`.
Construct with `ReductionWorkspace::workspace(&req)` or enlarge existing workspace with `try_reserve(&req)`; check sufficiency with `check(&req)`.
For repeated reductions of identically shaped views, reuse `_into` forms and workspace to avoid allocation after the first call.

`StatisticsOptions` controls missing and nonfinite values, merge order, and retained moment order.
By default, observations marked missing in a validity array (`BatchView::with_validity`) (`MissingPolicy::Reject`), and NaN or infinity (`NonFinitePolicy::Reject`), cause errors; `Skip` omits them from estimation.
`with_moment_order` selects the retained order for `reduce_moments` and related results (default `MomentOrder::Second`); `with_max_bytes` selects the workspace limit.
`MergePolicy::InputOrder` (the default) traverses each batch once in input order.
`MergePolicy::StablePartitions` divides each batch into execution-plan partitions, processes them in parallel, and merges them in fixed partition order.
Both are independent of thread completion order, but their results may differ by rounding.
scioxide-stats tests compare partition merging with one-pass accumulation within $128\,\varepsilon\,(\text{tree depth}+1)$ times a scale (value magnitude for means, squared range for variances, and the product of coordinate ranges for covariance).
Against exact rational reference values, differences normalized by the corresponding power of spread are checked within $512\,\varepsilon$ (plus one output subnormal unit).
These are tested ranges, not forward-error guarantees for every ill-conditioned dataset.

`reduce_moment(view, k, center)` returns the following quantity per batch.

$$
\mu_k = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - c)^k
$$

When `center` is `Some(c)`, moments are about $c$; with `None`, they are about the batch mean.
Moments about the origin (raw moments) use `Some(0.0)`.
Moments about the mean use two passes: one for the mean and one for powers.
Empty batches return `None`; $k = 0$ is exactly 1 and mean-centered $k = 1$ exactly 0.
`reduce_trimmed_moments(view, limits)` first validates observations under missing-data and nonfinite-value policies, then removes observations outside `TrimLimits` before computing moments.
`TrimLimits` is a value range, distinct from trimmed means removing endpoint fractions ([Order statistics, extrema, and robust location estimators](oxref:stats-robust-breakdown-trimmed-mean)).

## Variance with an increasing offset

Five values formed by adding offset $10^p$ to deviations $[-2, -1, 0, 1, 2]$ have sample variance exactly 2.5 regardless of offset.
Since $10^{15.5} < 2^{52}$, values $10^p + d$ are exactly representable in f64; all differences between computed variance and 2.5 come from algorithmic rounding.
The next cell varies the offset from $10^0$ to $10^{15.5}$ and plots differences from 2.5 for `OnlineMoments` sample variance and a comparison one-pass formula on logarithmic axes.
A vertical line marks the offset for the selected exponent $p$, and the values at that point appear in the table.
Exactly zero differences cannot be plotted on log axes, so the table counts them.

```rust
//|   offset_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Offset exponent p (offset 10^p)","description":"Changes the offset added to the same deviations −2…2.","min":0,"max":15,"step":1,"value":10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"small-offset","label":"p = 3","description":"κ² u remains small and the one-pass formula is still close to 2.5.","inputs":{"offset_exponent":3}}
//|   - {"id":"huge-offset","label":"p = 15","description":"The one-pass formula gives meaningless values while online updates retain 2.5.","inputs":{"offset_exponent":15}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":2.5,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-2.5,"atol":1e-12}}

//|   - case: huge-offset
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":-1e10}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":2.5,"atol":1e-14}}

use scioxide_stats::{MomentOrder, OnlineMoments};

// Deviations [-2, -1, 0, 1, 2] have sample variance exactly 2.5 regardless of offset.
let deviations = [-2.0_f64, -1.0, 0.0, 1.0, 2.0];
let exact = 2.5_f64;
let evaluate = |offset: f64| -> Result<(f64, f64), String> {
    let values: Vec<f64> = deviations.iter().map(|&d| offset + d).collect();
    // Main computation: scioxide-stats online updates.
    let mut moments = OnlineMoments::new(MomentOrder::Second);
    for &v in &values {
        moments.observe(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
    let stable = moments
        .snapshot()
        .sample_variance()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .ok_or("Sample variance is undefined")?;
    // Comparison: one-pass difference-of-squares formula (not a scioxide-stats computation).
    let n = values.len() as f64;
    let sum: f64 = values.iter().sum();
    let squares: f64 = values.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum();
    Ok(((squares - sum * sum / n) / (n - 1.0), stable))
};

        naive_curve.push([offset, dn]);
    } else {
        naive_zero += 1;
    }
    if ds > 0.0 {
        stable_curve.push([offset, ds]);
    } else {
        stable_zero += 1;
    }
}
let offset = RealScalar::powf(10.0_f64, offset_exponent);
let (naive, stable) = evaluate(offset)?;

    &vec![

            "style": {"showSymbol": true}
        }),
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": "OnlineMoments (online updates)",
            "points": stable_curve,
            "style": {"showSymbol": true, "symbol": "diamond"}
        }),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Reference value [-]",
        "yLabel": "Difference from exact sample variance 2.5 [-]",

    })

let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });

    { "key": "0", "label": "Algorithm", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample variance [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from exact 2.5 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Points with zero difference [count/63]", "type": "integer" }

    ("One-pass formula", naive, naive - exact, naive_zero),
    ("OnlineMoments", stable, stable - exact, stable_zero),

    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "variance" })
```

The one-pass difference grows proportionally to squared offset: $7.1 \times 10^{-15}$ at offset 5.6 and $6.2 \times 10^{-2}$ at $1.8 \times 10^7$.
The sample's population variance is $\sigma^2 = 2$, giving $\kappa \approx 10^p/\sqrt 2$; difference growth matches proportionality to $\kappa^2$ in the approximate bound $n\kappa^2 u$ above.
At offset $10^8$, the difference reaches 2.5 (variance becomes 0); larger offsets return 0 or meaningless values such as difference $-1.4 \times 10^{14}$ at $10^{15}$.
The table shows the one-pass formula returning 0 at the default $p = 10$.

`OnlineMoments` differs from 2.5 at only two of 63 points, offsets 3.2 and 32, by at most $5.3 \times 10^{-15}$.
The other 61 points return 2.5 exactly.
Small integer deviations also make update products exactly representable; this does not imply zero error for general data.

## Merge order and rounding

Measure how partial-aggregate merge order affects rounding using data with an exact reference.
The next cell uses the random seed to generate $n = 4096$ deviations $d_i \in [-8, 8]$ in integer multiples of $1/64$, then adds offset $10^p$.
Since $10^{13} \cdot 64 < 2^{53}$, values $10^p + d_i$ ($p \le 13$) are exactly representable in f64.
Deviation sum $\sum d_i$ and squared sum $\sum d_i^2$ are exact in f64; the exact sample variance is

$$
s^2 = \frac{n\sum d_i^2 - \bigl(\sum d_i\bigr)^2}{n(n-1)}
$$

Its numerator can be computed exactly, with a single rounding in division (independent check).
Compare sample-variance relative errors for four computation orders on these data.
The orders are individual updates with `observe`, two passes with `from_slice`, left-to-right `merge` of partition accumulators, and binary-tree pairwise `merge` of partition accumulators.
Partition count $k$ is adjustable.
The one-pass formula's relative error is also plotted for comparison.
The horizontal axis uses the same offset as the preceding cell, linking both charts' zooming and panning.

```rust
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/3/1","range":{"max":4e-16}}
//|   - case: small-offset
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":24.2515,"rtol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"orders","path":"/rows/4/1","range":{"max":1e-13}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_stats::{MomentOrder, OnlineMoments};

const N: usize = 4096;
// Generate deviations d ∈ [-8, 8] on a 1/64 grid with a linear congruential method (input generation).
let mut state = sampling_seed as u64;
let deviations: Vec<f64> = (0..N)
    .map(|_| {
        state = state
            .wrapping_mul(6364136223846793005)
            .wrapping_add(1442695040888963407);
        ((state >> 40) % 1025) as f64 / 64.0 - 8.0
    })
    .collect();
// Independent check: deviation sums and squared sums are exact in f64, so the numerator is exact and only division rounds.
let n = N as f64;
let sum: f64 = deviations.iter().sum();
let squares: f64 = deviations.iter().map(|d| d * d).sum();
let exact = (n * squares - sum * sum) / (n * (n - 1.0));
let parts = 1_usize << (partition_exponent as u32);

let variance = |m: &OnlineMoments<f64>| -> Result<f64, String> {
    let s2 = m.snapshot().sample_variance().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(s2.ok_or("Sample variance is undefined")?)
};
let evaluate = |offset: f64| -> Result<[f64; 5], String> {
    let values: Vec<f64> = deviations.iter().map(|&d| offset + d).collect();
    let mut sequential = OnlineMoments::new(MomentOrder::Second);
    for &v in &values {
        sequential.observe(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
    let slice =
        OnlineMoments::from_slice(&values, MomentOrder::Second).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Construct an accumulator for each partition using observe.
    let mut chunks = Vec::with_capacity(parts);
    for chunk in values.chunks(N / parts) {
        let mut m = OnlineMoments::new(MomentOrder::Second);
        for &v in chunk {
            m.observe(v).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        }
        chunks.push(m);
    }
    let mut left = OnlineMoments::new(MomentOrder::Second);
    for m in &chunks {
        left.merge(m).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    }
    // Binary tree: repeatedly merge adjacent pairs to halve the count.
    while chunks.len() > 1 {
        let mut next = Vec::with_capacity(chunks.len() / 2);
        for pair in chunks.chunks(2) {
            let mut m = pair[0];
            m.merge(&pair[1]).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            next.push(m);
        }
        chunks = next;
    }
    // Comparison: one-pass formula (not a scioxide-stats computation).
    let (s, q) = values
        .iter()
        .fold((0.0, 0.0), |(s, q), v| (s + v, q + v * v));
    let naive = (q - s * s / n) / (n - 1.0);
    let out = [
        variance(&sequential)?,
        variance(&slice)?,
        variance(&left)?,
        variance(&chunks[0])?,
        naive,
    ];
    Ok(out.map(|v| (v - exact).abs() / exact))
};

let names = [
    "observe (one at a time)",
    "from_slice (two passes)",
    "Left-to-right merge",
    "Binary-tree merge",
    "One-pass formula",
];
let mut curves: Vec<Vec<[f64; 2]>> = vec![Vec::new(); 5];
let mut zeros = [0_u32; 5];
for p in 0..=26 {
    let offset = RealScalar::powf(10.0_f64, 0.5 * f64::from(p));
    for (j, e) in evaluate(offset)?.into_iter().enumerate() {
        if e > 0.0 {
            curves[j].push([offset, e]);
        } else {
            zeros[j] += 1;
        }
    }
}
let series: Vec<_> = names
    .iter()
    .zip(curves)
    .map(|(name, points)| {
        serde_json::json!({
            "name": name,
            "points": points,
            "style": {"showSymbol": true}
        })
    })
    .collect();
let chosen = RealScalar::powf(10.0_f64, offset_exponent);
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Reference value [-]",
        "yLabel": "Relative sample-variance error [-]",
        "xType": "log",
        "yType": "log",
        "xMin": 1.0,
        "xMax": 4.0e15,
        "link": {"group": "stats-offset"},
        "annotations": [
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": chosen, "label": "Offset shown in table"},
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": f64::EPSILON / 2.0, "label": "Unit roundoff u"}
        ]
    })
);
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Computation order", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative error / u [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Offsets with zero error [count/27]", "type": "integer" }
]);
let errors = evaluate(chosen)?;
let rows: Vec<_> = (0..5)
    .map(|j| {
        (
            names[j],
            errors[j],
            errors[j] / (f64::EPSILON / 2.0),
            zeros[j],
        )
    })
    .collect();
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "orders" })
);
```

At the default seed 2024 and $k = 16$, relative errors are $2.7 \times 10^{-15}$ (about $24u$) for `observe`, $6.7 \times 10^{-16}$ (about $6u$) for left-to-right `merge`, and $3.4 \times 10^{-16}$ (about $3u$) for binary-tree `merge`; `from_slice` gives 0.
All four orders give the same relative errors across offsets from $10^0$ to $10^{13}$.
Compensated high and low mean parts are subtracted separately, cancelling the offset first and rounding only the deviation part.
Over the same range, one-pass relative error grows from 0 at $p = 0$ to $1.6 \times 10^3$ at $p = 10$ and $6.5 \times 10^9$ at $p = 13$.

At $k = 1$ there is one partition, so left-to-right and binary-tree merges use the same order as `observe` and give the same error $24u$.
At $k = 256$, binary-tree error is 0 and left-to-right error is about $3u$.
With seed 1, `observe` gives about $33u$, left-to-right merging about $4.5u$, and binary-tree merging about $6.0u$.
One dataset cannot establish the best order, but every error is within tens of $u$, far below the approximate bound $n\kappa u$ above (of order $10^{10}$ times at $p = 10$).
Trailing-digit changes from merge order provide a basis for choosing tolerances when comparing parallel aggregates.

## Summary

One-pass variance error grows proportionally to squared condition number $\kappa^2 \approx (\mu/\sigma)^2$; when the mean exceeds standard deviation by about eight orders of magnitude, no meaningful digits remain.
Welford recurrences and their merge generalization accumulate only deviations, giving an error estimate proportional to $\kappa$.
`OnlineMoments` and `OnlineCovariance` use the same merge formula for updates and partial aggregates, retaining compensated means to preserve variance digits even with large offsets.
Merge order changes trailing digits; fix the order with `MergePolicy` when reproducibility is required.
Choose retained order with `MomentOrder` in advance and specify skewness/kurtosis corrections and conventions through `BiasCorrection` and `KurtosisConvention`.
Undefined statistics return `Ok(None)`; unrepresentable values and detected cancellation return `Err`.
Handle these separately.
