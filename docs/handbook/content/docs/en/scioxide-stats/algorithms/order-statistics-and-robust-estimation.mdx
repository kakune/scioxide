Sample means and sample variances are optimal estimators for normally distributed measurements.
For heavy-tailed or outlier-contaminated data, however, squared-error estimators can be moved greatly by a few extreme values.
Order-based estimators (medians and trimmed means) and estimators bounding extreme-observation influence (Huber M-estimators) reduce this effect.
This page explains order-statistic distributions, breakdown points, trimmed means, Huber estimators, multivariate scatter, sigma clipping, and rolling-window statistics.
It then experimentally compares estimator bias and variance while varying outlier magnitude and contamination proportion.

## Order statistics and mode

Sorting independent identically distributed continuous variables $X_1, \dots, X_n$ into ascending order $X_{(1)} \le X_{(2)} \le \dots \le X_{(n)}$ gives **order statistics**.
For population distribution function $F$ and density $f$, order statistic $X_{(k)}$ at position $k$ has density

$$
f_{(k)}(x) = \frac{n!}{(k-1)!\,(n-k)!} \, F(x)^{k-1} \, \bigl(1 - F(x)\bigr)^{n-k} \, f(x)
$$

This counts the probability of $k-1$ observations below $x$, one near $x$, and $n-k$ above $x$.
$U_{(k)} = F(X_{(k)})$ follows the beta distribution $\mathrm{Beta}(k, n-k+1)$, whose expectation $k/(n+1)$ gives type-6 quantile plotting positions.
Minimum and maximum distribution functions are $F_{(1)}(x) = 1 - (1 - F(x))^n$ and $F_{(n)}(x) = F(x)^n$.
The maximum distribution moves farther into the tail as $n$ grows, so interpret extreme statistics with an explicit sample size.

Select scioxide-stats order-based estimators with `Statistic` constructors and evaluate them with `statistic(values, weights, request)`.
The result `StatisticValue::Order` contains enum `OrderResult`, distinguishing quantiles (`QuantileResult`), modes (`ModeResult`), expectiles, interquartile ranges, percentile positions, and tie correction factors.
`StatisticValue::scalar()` returns a real value in every case.
`ModeResult` returned by `Statistic::mode()` contains the most frequent value `value` and its count `count`.
Ties in frequency return the smallest value; $-0.0$ and $+0.0$ count as one value ($+0.0$).
Compute minima and maxima with `Statistic::minimum()` and `maximum()`, and medians with `Statistic::quantile(QuantileProbability::new(0.5)?, rule)`.
Quantiles and interquartile ranges use selection without full sorting, with average cost $O(n)$ ([Computing quantiles by selection](oxref:stats-quantile-selected-computation)).
Mode sorts to find equal-value runs, costing $O(n \log n)$.

## Breakdown points and trimmed means

A **breakdown point** measures estimator robustness.
The finite-sample breakdown point is the smallest fraction $m/n$ of $n$ observations that, when $m$ are replaced arbitrarily, can make the estimate arbitrarily large or small.
Moving one observation to infinity moves the mean to infinity, so its breakdown point is $1/n$, tending to 0 as $n \to \infty$.
The median remains bounded until nearly half the observations are replaced, giving a breakdown point near 1/2.

A **trimmed mean** removes a fraction $\alpha$ of observations at each end of a sorted sample and averages the remainder.
Removing $\lfloor \alpha n \rfloor$ per side eliminates up to $\lfloor \alpha n \rfloor$ upper outliers; the breakdown point is $(\lfloor \alpha n \rfloor + 1)/n$, asymptotically $\alpha$.
The limit $\alpha \to 1/2$ is the median.
For normal data, the trimmed mean's asymptotic variance is as follows, with $\xi = \Phi^{-1}(1 - \alpha)$.

$$
n\,\mathrm{Var}(\bar X_\alpha) \to \frac{\int_{-\xi}^{\xi} x^2\,\varphi(x)\,dx + 2\alpha\xi^2}{(1 - 2\alpha)^2}
$$

The numerator is the variance of the distribution replacing endpoint observations by $\pm\xi$ (Winsorizing).
At $\alpha = 0.2$ this is about 1.14, giving relative efficiency about 0.87 under normality.

Select a trimmed mean in scioxide-stats with `Statistic::trimmed_mean(proportion, side)`.
`proportion` is a fraction in $[0, 1)$; `TrimSide` selects the trimmed ends.
`Both` removes $\lfloor \text{proportion} \cdot n \rfloor$ from each end, `Lower` only from the lower end, and `Upper` only from the upper end.
An out-of-range fraction returns `InvalidInput`; fractions removing all observations also cause errors.
For the trimmed sample itself, `StatisticsWorkspace::trim_into(values, proportion, side, output)` sorts retained observations into the start of the output and returns their count.
Winsorized means (replacing endpoint observations by boundary values instead of removing them) are not provided.
If needed, replace endpoints of the sorted sample with boundary values before averaging.

Use `TrimLimits` to remove observations by a value range rather than a fraction.
`TrimLimits::new(lower, upper)` accepts lower and upper limits (`None` means unbounded) and retains observations $\text{lower} \le x \le \text{upper}$.
Bounds are closed by default; each end can be opened (`Exclusive`), as in `with_inclusive(TrimInclusion::Exclusive, TrimInclusion::Inclusive)`.
NaN or infinite bounds are rejected with `NonFinite`; a lower bound exceeding the upper is rejected with `InvalidInput`.
`reduce_trimmed_moments` computes moments of in-range observations for each matrix-view batch ([Batched matrix-view reductions](oxref:stats-online-matrix-view-reduction)).

Robust scale estimators include median absolute deviation `Statistic::median_absolute_deviation(method, scale)` and interquartile range `Statistic::iqr(method)`.
Median absolute deviation is $\mathrm{median}_i |x_i - \mathrm{median}(x)| / \text{scale}$, with raw values at `scale = 1`.
To match a normal standard deviation, pass $\Phi^{-1}(3/4) \approx 0.6745$ to `scale`.
Median absolute deviation has breakdown point about 1/2; the interquartile range has about 1/4.

## Huber M-estimators and robust scatter

Trimmed means discard information from removed observations.
Huber's (1964) **M-estimator** retains observations while smoothly reducing large-residual influence.
For location $\mu$ and scale $\sigma$, let standardized residuals be $u_i = (x_i - \mu)/\sigma$.
The Huber location estimate is $\mu$ minimizing the sum of losses $\rho_c$ below.

$$
\rho_c(u) = \begin{cases}
\frac{1}{2} u^2 & (|u| \le c) \\
c |u| - \frac{1}{2} c^2 & (|u| > c)
\end{cases}
$$

$\rho_c$ equals squared loss within $|u| \le c$ and absolute loss outside, continuously differentiable at the boundary.
Its derivative $\psi_c(u) = \rho_c'(u) = \max(-c, \min(c, u))$ is called the **influence function** ($\psi$ function).
$\psi_c$ equals $u$ within $|u| \le c$ and saturates at $\pm c$ outside, bounding any one observation's influence.
The estimating equation $\sum_i \psi_c((x_i - \mu)/\sigma) = 0$ has no closed-form solution.
With weights $w_i = \psi_c(u_i)/u_i = \min(1, c/|u_i|)$, the equation becomes $\sum_i w_i (x_i - \mu) = 0$; solve it by repeatedly fixing weights, computing a weighted mean, and recomputing weights from new residuals.
This is **iteratively reweighted least squares** (IRLS).
Since $\rho_c$ is convex, a fixed scale gives a unique solution and IRLS converges.

Normal asymptotic variance is $E[\psi_c^2]/(E[\psi_c'])^2$; at $c = 1.345$, efficiency relative to the sample mean is 95%.
It approaches the sample mean as $c \to \infty$ and the median as $c \to 0$.
Choosing scale from a breakdown-1/2 estimator such as MAD also gives the location estimator breakdown point about 1/2.

scioxide-stats `huber_location(&x)` performs this iteration with default tuning constant 1.345 and at most 100 weighted least-squares solves.
The first iteration is unweighted least squares, the sample mean.
Each iteration sets scale $\hat\sigma$ to residual MAD divided by 0.6745 and solves the next weighted least-squares problem with weights $\min(1, c\hat\sigma/|r_i|)$.
For location estimation, residual MAD equals data MAD independently of $\mu$, so scale stays fixed across iterations.
Coefficient changes within the `scioxide_core::StepTolerance` tolerance stop with `Converged`.
Residuals all zero within rounding stop with scale 0 and `ExactFit`.
When MAD is 0 but residuals are not (such as more than half the observations equal), weights cannot be determined and `InvalidInput` is returned.
The one-shot `huber_location` returns no value after an iteration limit, instead returning `ConvergenceFailure` (`IterationLimit`).

Pass `HuberOptions` to `huber_location_with_options(&x, options)` to change settings.
Starting from `HuberOptions::default()`, change settings with `with_tuning(c)`, `with_tolerance(step_tolerance)`, `with_iterations(budget)` (weighted least-squares solve limit), `with_factor_iterations(budget)` (iteration limit for each SVD), and `with_max_bytes(bytes)`.
The detailed form returns the last accepted iteration in `HuberFit` even at a limit, setting termination status `termination()` to `BudgetExhausted`.
`HuberFit` contains coefficients `coefficients()` (one component for location), residuals `residuals()`, final weights `weights()`, scale `scale()`, solve count `iterations()`, and report `report()`.
`HuberReport` contains termination status, iteration and evaluation counts `counts()`, and accepted iteration count `accepted_iterations()`.
Assess termination status `StatsTermination` with `is_success()` (true for `Completed`, `Converged`, and `ExactFit`).
`PreparedHuber` estimates regression coefficients with the same iteration and is explained in [Regression, ANOVA, and generalized linear models](../regression-anova-and-glm/).

For multivariate data $\mathbf x_i \in \mathbb R^p$, `PreparedHuberScatter` provides Huber-type M-estimates of location and scatter (Maronna, 1976).
Starting from sample mean and covariance, each iteration computes Mahalanobis distances $d_i = \sqrt{(\mathbf x_i - \boldsymbol\mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (\mathbf x_i - \boldsymbol\mu)}$ and weights $w_i = \min(1, c/d_i)$, updating estimates by the following formulas.

$$
\boldsymbol\mu = \frac{\sum_i w_i \mathbf x_i}{\sum_i w_i}, \qquad
\Sigma = \frac{\sum_i w_i (\mathbf x_i - \boldsymbol\mu)(\mathbf x_i - \boldsymbol\mu)^\top}{\sum_i w_i}
$$

Convergence occurs when the maximum component change in $\boldsymbol\mu$ and $\Sigma$, divided by the maximum absolute component value (at least 1), is within tolerance.
Each iteration costs one symmetric eigendecomposition $O(p^3)$ plus $O(np^2)$ operations for distances and new scatter.
Scatter is normalized by total weight without a normal-consistency correction.
This estimator lacks a high breakdown point (falling to order $1/(p+1)$ as dimension $p$ grows); high-breakdown estimators such as minimum covariance determinant (MCD) are not provided.

`PreparedHuberScatter::new(data, options)` accepts an $n \times p$ matrix, rejecting no columns or rows no greater than columns with `InvalidInput` and nonfinite values with `NonFinite`.
`HuberScatterOptions` defaults to threshold $c = 2$, relative-change tolerance $\sqrt\varepsilon$, and 100 iterations.
`fit()` executes sequentially; `fit_scoped(scope)` accepts an execution context; both return `HuberScatterFit`.
`fit_into(&mut output, &mut workspace, scope)` reuses `HuberScatterOutput` and `HuberScatterWorkspace` constructed with `output()` and `workspace()`, returning `HuberScatterReport` (termination status, iteration count, final relative change, and counts).
`workspace()` returns `InvalidInput` when sample covariance's smallest eigenvalue is at most $p\varepsilon\lambda_{\max}$ (numerically singular).
Stopping at iteration limits, cancellation, or deadlines still returns `Ok` with status `BudgetExhausted` or `Stopped` and retains the last accepted estimate (possibly the initial sample mean and covariance).
`HuberScatterOutput::fit()` returns `HuberScatterFit` from the last successful call (location `location`, scatter `scatter`, iteration count, relative change, and status).
`HuberScatterWorkspace::last_accepted()` returns the preceding call's last accepted estimate as `HuberScatterAccepted`; `last_attempt()` returns attempt records including failed calls.

## Sigma clipping and rolling-window statistics

**Sigma clipping** removes outliers in astronomical photometry and signal processing.
Compute the remaining observations' mean $\bar x$ and standard deviation $s$ and remove observations outside $[\bar x - c_{\mathrm{low}} s,\ \bar x + c_{\mathrm{high}} s]$.
Recompute $\bar x$ and $s$ after removal and repeat until no observations are removed or the iteration limit is reached.
Large outliers pull initial $\bar x$ and $s$ toward themselves, enlarging $s$ and hiding smaller outliers.
This is **masking**; later iterations remove the hidden outliers.

`sigma_clip_into(values, low, high, options, output, indices, workspace, scope)` uses the current observations' mean and population standard deviation (divisor $n$) each iteration.
`SigmaClipOptions::new()` defaults to 100 iterations, adjustable with `with_max_iterations`.
Remaining observations are written in input order at the start of `output`; their input indices go at the start of `indices`.
The result `SigmaClipResult` contains retained count `retained()`, completed passes `iterations()`, final-pass boundaries `lower()` and `upper()`, and termination status `termination()`.
Removing nothing in a pass gives `Converged`; reaching the limit gives `BudgetExhausted`, with the final pass's result still usable.
Negative or nonfinite `low` or `high` returns `InvalidInput`; a pass removing every observation returns `InvalidInput`.
Query and reserve workspace with `StatisticsWorkspace::scalar_requirements(n, options)`.
Clipping centered and scaled by median and MAD is not provided.
If needed, compute center and scale with `Statistic::quantile` and `Statistic::median_absolute_deviation` and remove out-of-range observations.

Use rolling-window statistics for local time-series or stream summaries.
`RollingWindow::new(left, right)` represents a window from `left` observations before to `right` after each point; `RollingBoundary` selects endpoint handling.
`Truncate` (the default) shortens windows by excluding points outside the series; `Repeat` repeats the first or last observation; `ZeroPad` pads zeros (unweighted views only).
`rolling_statistics(view, statistic, window)` uses defaults; `rolling_statistics_with_options` accepts `StatisticsOptions`.
`rolling_statistics_into` writes preallocated output and workspace without allocation.
Unweighted means, variances, and standard deviations update windows with compensated sums at amortized cost $O(1)$ per window.
Windows are recomputed directly when estimated rounding error exceeds $64\varepsilon$ and every window width $w$.
Unweighted medians retain a sorted window at cost $O(w)$ per window.
Other statistics are computed directly for each window.

## Outlier magnitude and estimators

Add one observation $x_{\mathrm{out}}$ to nine approximately normal observations $[-1.35, -0.92, -0.55, -0.18, 0.05, 0.28, 0.62, 0.88, 1.17]$ (mean 0).
The next cell varies $x_{\mathrm{out}}$ from 0 to 50, computing sample means, medians (type 7), 20% trimmed means, and Huber estimates ($c = 1.345$) with `statistic` and `huber_location_with_options`.
This is Tukey's **sensitivity curve**, showing how much one observation can move an estimate.
The table reports estimates at selected $x_{\mathrm{out}}$, finite-sample breakdown points at $n = 10$, and Huber termination status and solve count.

```rust
//| id: stats-robust-location-bias
//| ref: stats-robust-location-bias
//| title: "Vary outlier magnitude to compare means, medians, trimmed means, and Huber estimates"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-stats]
//| inputs:
//|   outlier_magnitude: {"type":"range","label":"Outlier magnitude x_out [-]","description":"Changes the tenth observation.","min":0,"max":50,"step":1,"value":10}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"no-outlier","label":"x_out = 0","description":"With no outlier, all four estimates are near 0.","inputs":{"outlier_magnitude":0}}
//|   - {"id":"far-outlier","label":"x_out = 50","description":"Only the sample mean moves proportionally to the outlier.","inputs":{"outlier_magnitude":50}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.165,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":0.18333333333333332,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.16875,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/3/3","equals":"Converged"}
//|   - case: no-outlier
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.020384,"atol":1e-5}}
//|   - case: far-outlier
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":5.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"estimates","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.16875,"atol":1e-6}}
use scioxide_stats::{
    HuberOptions, QuantileMethod, QuantileProbability, QuantileRule, Statistic, TrimSide,
    Unweighted, huber_location_with_options, statistic,
};

let base = [-1.35_f64, -0.92, -0.55, -0.18, 0.05, 0.28, 0.62, 0.88, 1.17];
let half = QuantileProbability::new(0.5).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Compute four estimators and Huber status for ten samples including outlier out.
let estimate = |out: f64| -> Result<([f64; 4], String, usize), String> {
    let mut x = base.to_vec();
    x.push(out);
    let value = |request: Statistic<f64>| -> Result<f64, String> {
        statistic(&x, Unweighted, request)
            .and_then(|v| v.scalar())
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
    };
    let mean = value(Statistic::mean())?;
    let median = value(Statistic::quantile(
        half,
        QuantileRule::new(QuantileMethod::Linear),
    ))?;
    let trimmed = value(Statistic::trimmed_mean(0.2, TrimSide::Both))?;
    let fit =
        huber_location_with_options(&x, HuberOptions::default()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let huber = fit.coefficients().as_slice()[0];
    Ok((
        [mean, median, trimmed, huber],
        format!("{:?}", fit.termination()),
        fit.iterations(),
    ))
};
let mut curves = vec![Vec::new(); 4];
for i in 0..=50 {
    let out = f64::from(i);
    let (values, _, _) = estimate(out)?;
    for (curve, value) in curves.iter_mut().zip(values) {
        curve.push([out, value]);
    }
}
let names = [
    "Sample mean",
    "Median (type 7)",
    "20% trimmed mean",
    "Huber (c = 1.345)",
];
let series: Vec<_> = names
    .iter()
    .zip(&curves)
    .map(|(name, points)| serde_json::json!({"name": name, "points": points}))
    .collect();
emit_line_chart!(
    &series,
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Outlier magnitude x_out [-]",
        "yLabel": "Location estimate [-]",
        "xMin": 0.0,
        "xMax": 50.0,
        "annotations": [
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": outlier_magnitude, "label": "Selected x_out"},
            {"kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": 0.0, "label": "Mean 0 of nine observations"}
        ]
    })
);
let (values, termination, iterations) = estimate(outlier_magnitude)?;
// Finite-sample breakdown points at n = 10 (formulas in the text).
let breakdown = [0.1, 0.5, 0.3, 0.5];
let rows: Vec<_> = (0..4)
    .map(|j| {
        let huber = j == 3;
        (
            names[j],
            values[j],
            breakdown[j],
            huber.then(|| termination.clone()),
            huber.then_some(iterations),
        )
    })
    .collect();
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed1 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 1 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Estimator", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Estimate [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Breakdown point (n = 10) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed1 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Termination", "type": "string", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Solve count", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "estimates" })
);
```

With outlier 0, all four estimators are near 0 (mean 0, median 0.025, trimmed mean 0.037, Huber 0.020).
At the default $x_{\mathrm{out}} = 10$ the mean moves to $(0 + 10)/10 = 1.0$; at $x_{\mathrm{out}} = 50$ it becomes 5.0.
The median (0.165) and trimmed mean (0.183) stop moving once the outlier becomes the largest observation ($x_{\mathrm{out}} > 1.17$).
The outlier affects only rank, not through its magnitude.
The Huber estimate increases smoothly until $x_{\mathrm{out}}$ exceeds residual threshold $c\hat\sigma$, then stays at 0.16875.
The observation's $\psi$ saturates at $c$ beyond the threshold, bounding influence.
Huber takes eight solves at $x_{\mathrm{out}} = 0$ and eleven at 10 and 50, all stopping with `Converged`.
Breakdown points at $n = 10$ are 1/10 for the mean, $(\lfloor 0.2 \cdot 10 \rfloor + 1)/10 = 0.3$ for 20% trimming, and 5/10 for the median and Huber (using MAD scale).

## Bias and variance under contamination

The preceding section examined one outlier.
Next compare bias and variance under random contamination.
Consider a contamination model drawing $k$ of $n = 20$ observations from $N(\delta, 1)$ and the remainder from $N(0, 1)$.
The true location is 0, so the mean estimate is bias and root mean squared error (RMSE) combines bias and variance.

The next cell generates $R = 2000$ samples and computes all four estimators' bias, standard deviation, and RMSE.
Bias Monte Carlo standard error is $\mathrm{SD}/\sqrt R$.
At $k = 0$, compare variance multiplied by $n$ with normal asymptotic variance.
Asymptotic variances are 1 for the mean, $\pi/2$ for the median, the preceding formula for 20% trimming, and $E[\psi_c^2]/(E[\psi_c'])^2$ for Huber.
Compute $E[\psi_c'] = 2\Phi(c) - 1$ and $E[\psi_c^2] = 2\Phi(c) - 1 - 2c\varphi(c) + 2c^2(1 - \Phi(c))$ in the Huber formula using `scioxide_special::erf` and `erfc`.
The standard error of variance multiplied by $n$ uses the normal approximation $n\,\mathrm{Var}\sqrt{2/(R-1)}$.

```rust
//| ref: stats-robust-contamination

//| crates: [scioxide-stats, scioxide-random, scioxide-special, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   contaminated: {"type":"range","label":"Outlier count k (out of n = 20)","description":"Observations drawn from N(δ, 1).","min":0,"max":8,"step":1,"value":2}
//|   shift: {"type":"range","label":"Outlier center δ [-]","description":"Mean of the outlier distribution.","min":2,"max":20,"step":1,"value":8}
//|   sampling_seed: {"type":"integer","label":"Random seed","min":0,"max":100000,"value":31}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"clean","label":"k = 0","description":"Without contamination, n times variance can be compared with asymptotic variance.","inputs":{"contaminated":0}}
//|   - {"id":"heavy","label":"k = 8","description":"40% contamination exceeds the trimmed mean's breakdown point.","inputs":{"contaminated":8}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.80,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.138,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/3/4","approx":{"value":0.311,"atol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/3/7","approx":{"value":1.0526312911880376,"rtol":1e-9}}

//|   - case: clean
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/0/5","approx":{"value":1.0,"atol":0.07}}
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/1/5","approx":{"value":1.5708,"atol":0.15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/3/5","approx":{"value":1.0526,"atol":0.1}}
//|   - case: heavy
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/2/1","range":{"min":2.0}}
//|       - {"artifact":"contamination","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"max":1.2}}

    Unweighted, huber_location_with_options, statistic,

const N: usize = 20;
const REPLICATES: usize = 2000;
let k = contaminated as usize;
let half = QuantileProbability::new(0.5).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let standard = Normal::new(0.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(sampling_seed as u64);
let (mut sums, mut squares) = ([0.0_f64; 4], [0.0_f64; 4]);
let mut not_converged = 0_usize;
let mut x = [0.0_f64; N];
for _ in 0..REPLICATES {
    for (i, xi) in x.iter_mut().enumerate() {
        let z = standard.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        *xi = if i < k { shift + z } else { z };
    }
    let value = |request: Statistic<f64>| -> Result<f64, String> {
        statistic(&x, Unweighted, request)
            .and_then(|v| v.scalar())
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
    };
    let fit =
        huber_location_with_options(&x, HuberOptions::default()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    not_converged += usize::from(!fit.termination().is_success());
    let estimates = [
        value(Statistic::mean())?,
        value(Statistic::quantile(
            half,
            QuantileRule::new(QuantileMethod::Linear),
        ))?,
        value(Statistic::trimmed_mean(0.2, TrimSide::Both))?,
        fit.coefficients().as_slice()[0],
    ];
    for (j, e) in estimates.into_iter().enumerate() {
        sums[j] += e;
        squares[j] += e * e;
    }
}
// Normal asymptotic variance n·Var (formulas in the text).
let c = 1.345_f64;
let sqrt2 = RealScalar::sqrt(2.0_f64);
let pdf =
    |x: f64| RealScalar::exp(-0.5 * x * x) / RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 * core::f64::consts::PI);
let central = scioxide_special::erf(c / sqrt2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let upper_tail = 0.5 * scioxide_special::erfc(c / sqrt2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let huber_variance =
    (central - 2.0 * c * pdf(c) + 2.0 * c * c * upper_tail) / (central * central);
let xi = 0.8416212335729143_f64; // Φ⁻¹(0.8)
let winsorized = 0.6 - 2.0 * xi * pdf(xi) + 0.4 * xi * xi;
let asymptotic = [
    1.0,
    core::f64::consts::FRAC_PI_2,
    winsorized / 0.36,
    huber_variance,
];
let names = [
    "Sample mean",
    "Median (type 7)",
    "20% trimmed mean",
    "Huber (c = 1.345)",
];
let r = REPLICATES as f64;
let rows: Vec<_> = (0..4)
    .map(|j| {
        let bias = sums[j] / r;
        let variance = (squares[j] / r - bias * bias) * r / (r - 1.0);
        let sd = RealScalar::sqrt(variance);
        let scaled = N as f64 * variance;
        let rmse = RealScalar::sqrt(bias * bias + variance);
        (
            names[j],
            bias,
            sd / RealScalar::sqrt(r),
            sd,
            rmse,
            scaled,
            scaled * RealScalar::sqrt(2.0 / (r - 1.0)),
            asymptotic[j],
        )
    })
    .collect();
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Estimator", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Bias [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Bias standard error [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Standard deviation [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "RMSE [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "n × variance [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Standard error of n × variance [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Asymptotic variance (k = 0) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "contamination" })
);
emit_text!(format!(
    "Samples whose Huber iterations reached the limit: {not_converged} / {REPLICATES}"
));
```

Without contamination at $k = 0$ (seed 31), variance multiplied by $n$ is 1.035 for means, 1.513 for medians, 1.187 for trimming, and 1.112 for Huber; these agree within two standard errors (0.03–0.05) with asymptotic variances 1, 1.571, 1.145, and 1.053.
For normal data the mean is most efficient, followed by Huber, trimming, and the median in increasing variance order.
All four biases are 0 within standard errors.

At the defaults $k = 2$ and $\delta = 8$ (10% contamination), mean bias is 0.80 as predicted by $k\delta/n = 0.8$, dominating RMSE 0.83.
Median bias is 0.14, trimming 0.16, and Huber 0.19, with RMSE limited to 0.30–0.32.
Increasing $\delta$ to 20 barely changes these three biases and RMSEs, but mean bias grows to 2.0.
At $k = 8$ (40% contamination), 20% trimming removes only four points at each end, leaving four outliers and giving bias 2.77.
Huber bias also grows to 2.47; only the median remains relatively small at 0.95.
Breakdown points concern outliers moving to infinity; for finite $\delta$, bias depends on both contamination fraction and outlier location.
No Huber iteration reached its limit under any condition.

## Sigma-clipping iterations and masking

Apply sigma clipping to a sample of 33 observations: 30 observations from $N(10, 1)$ plus outliers 16, 17.5, and 25.
The large outlier 25 increases first-pass $s$, hiding 16 and 17.5 inside the bounds.
The next cell increases iteration limits from one to the selected count, calling `sigma_clip_into` and tabulating boundaries and retained counts after each pass.
Termination status is the cell's conclusion, so an outcome shows `SigmaClipResult::termination()` at the selected limit.

```rust
//| id: stats-sigma-clip-passes
//| ref: stats-sigma-clip-passes
//| title: "Vary iteration limits to track sigma-clipping bounds and retained observations"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-stats, scioxide-random, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   clip_multiplier: {"type":"range","label":"Clipping factor c [-]","description":"Factor c for both lower and upper bounds (x̄ ± c s).","min":1.5,"max":4,"step":0.1,"value":2.5}
//|   max_passes: {"type":"range","label":"Iteration limit [iterations]","description":"Iteration limit passed to sigma_clip_into.","min":1,"max":10,"step":1,"value":5}
//|   sampling_seed: {"type":"integer","label":"Random seed","min":0,"max":100000,"value":5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"one-pass","label":"One-iteration limit","description":"Only 25 is removed on the first pass, then execution reaches its limit.","inputs":{"max_passes":1}}
//|   - {"id":"loose","label":"c = 4","description":"Wide bounds retain masked outliers.","inputs":{"clip_multiplier":4}}
//|   - {"id":"tight","label":"c = 1.5","description":"Bounds are too narrow and keep removing normal observations.","inputs":{"clip_multiplier":1.5,"max_passes":10}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"passes","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":32}
//|       - {"artifact":"passes","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":2}
//|       - {"artifact":"passes","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"passes","path":"/rows/2/6","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: one-pass
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"partial"}
//|       - {"artifact":"passes","path":"/rows/0/6","equals":"BudgetExhausted"}
//|   - case: loose
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"passes","path":"/rows/4/3","equals":2}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/1/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: tight
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"passes","path":"/rows/9/2","equals":18}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};
use scioxide_stats::{SigmaClipOptions, StatisticsOptions, StatisticsWorkspace, sigma_clip_into};

let law = Normal::new(10.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(sampling_seed as u64);
let mut values = Vec::with_capacity(33);
for _ in 0..30 {
    values.push(law.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}
values.extend_from_slice(&[16.0, 17.5, 25.0]);
let n = values.len();
let mut workspace = StatisticsWorkspace::default();
let requirements = StatisticsWorkspace::scalar_requirements(n, StatisticsOptions::new())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
workspace
    .try_reserve(&requirements)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut output, mut indices) = (vec![0.0_f64; n], vec![0_usize; n]);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut last = None;
for passes in 1..=max_passes as usize {
    let options = SigmaClipOptions::new().with_max_iterations(passes);
    let result = sigma_clip_into(
        &values,
        clip_multiplier,
        clip_multiplier,
        options,
        &mut output,
        &mut indices,
        &mut workspace,
        ExecutionContext::sequential(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Count remaining outliers (indices 30, 31, 32).
    let outliers_left = indices[..result.retained()]
        .iter()
        .filter(|&&i| i >= 30)
        .count();
    rows.push((
        passes as u32,
        result.iterations() as u32,
        result.retained() as u32,
        outliers_left as u32,
        result.lower(),
        result.upper(),
        format!("{:?}", result.termination()),
    ));
    last = Some(result);
}
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Limit [iterations]", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Completed passes", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Retained count", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Retained outliers", "type": "integer" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Lower bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Upper bound [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Termination", "type": "string" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "passes" })
);
let result = last.ok_or("Iteration limit is 0")?;
let outcome = if result.termination().is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("No observations were removed on the last pass; clipping converged")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("The iteration limit was reached while removals remained; the last pass's result is usable")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{:?}", result.termination()))
        .diagnostic("Completed passes", result.iterations() as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Retained count", result.retained() as f64, None)

```

At the default $c = 2.5$ (seed 5), first-pass boundaries are $[2.74, 18.66]$, removing only 25 and retaining 16 and 17.5.
The value 25 raised mean and standard deviation, hiding the other outliers inside the bounds.
The second pass narrows bounds to $[5.34, 15.16]$ and removes 16 and 17.5, leaving 30 normal observations.
The third pass removes nothing and stops with `Converged`, with boundaries $[7.22, 12.41]$.
A one-iteration limit stops with `BudgetExhausted` while removals remain, giving a partial outcome.

At $c = 4$, second-pass bounds $[2.39, 18.11]$ contain 16 and 17.5, so convergence retains both outliers.
At $c = 1.5$, normal observations continue being removed after outliers are gone; convergence occurs at pass six with 18 left.
For a normal distribution, about 13% lie outside $\pm 1.5 s$ and each removal reduces $s$.
Too small a factor loses normal observations; too large retains masked outliers.
Choose about 2.5–3 and inspect retained counts and bounds.

## Summary

The mean's breakdown point is $1/n$, so one observation can move it arbitrarily far.
Trimming endpoint fractions $\alpha$ raises breakdown to about $\alpha$, while the median raises it to about 1/2, at the cost of normal-data efficiency.
Huber bounds influence at $\pm c$ without discarding observations; $c = 1.345$ combines 95% normal-data efficiency with breakdown near 1/2.
Select estimators using both `huber_location_with_options` termination status and contamination-experiment bias and RMSE.
Use `PreparedHuberScatter` for multivariate scatter, iterative sigma clipping for outlier removal, and `RollingWindow` rolling windows for local summaries; none replaces high-breakdown multivariate estimation.
