Resampling approximates a statistic's sampling distribution by repeatedly recomputing it from the available sample or from samples generated under the null hypothesis, when the sampling distribution has no explicit formula or the normal approximation is doubtful.
This page covers four resampling procedures in scioxide-stats.
The bootstrap draws with replacement from a sample to construct confidence intervals; the jackknife removes one observation at a time to estimate bias and standard error.
Permutation tests construct a null distribution by rearranging group assignments of observations, while Monte Carlo tests generate samples from a probability model under the null hypothesis.
Finally, this page describes multiscale graph correlation (MGC), an independence test whose p-value comes from a permutation test.

All four procedures use a single Plan type, [`ResamplingPlan`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingPlan.html).
The Plan accepts group sizes and a method ([`ResamplingMethod`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/enum.ResamplingMethod.html)); supply the statistic as a function returning a value from a sequence of observation indices.
The caller always supplies the random number generator; the library never chooses a seed internally.

## The idea of resampling

Suppose observations $x_1,\dots,x_n$ are independent draws from an unknown distribution $F$; write the quantity of interest as $\theta=t(F)$ and its estimator as $\hat\theta=T(x_1,\dots,x_n)$.
The standard error and confidence interval of $\hat\theta$ are determined by the sampling distribution of $\hat\theta$ under $F$.
The bootstrap principle (plug-in principle) replaces the unknown $F$ with the empirical distribution $\hat F_n$ (a discrete distribution assigning probability $1/n$ to each observation), draws a sample $x^{*}_1,\dots,x^{*}_n$ of size $n$ with replacement from $\hat F_n$, and examines the distribution of $\hat\theta^{*}=T(x^{*})$.
The distribution of $\hat\theta^{*}$ under $\hat F_n$ theoretically has finitely many values, but the number of combinations $\binom{2n-1}{n}$ is large, so it is approximated by $B$ randomly generated resamples.

Resampling results therefore have two kinds of error.
One is the statistical error from replacing $F$ with $\hat F_n$; it cannot decrease without increasing $n$.
The other is Monte Carlo error from approximation with $B$ resamples; it decreases at $O(B^{-1/2})$ as $B$ increases.
scioxide-stats attaches the magnitude of the latter to the result (`mean_monte_carlo_error`, `tail_uncertainty`); the former is discussed here through the accuracy order of the interval construction.

Permutation and Monte Carlo tests construct null distributions rather than sampling distributions.
Permutation tests use exchangeability of observation assignments under the null hypothesis; Monte Carlo tests generate samples from a probability model completely specified by the null hypothesis.

## Bootstrap confidence intervals (percentile, basic, BCa)

Write the empirical quantile of the statistics $\hat\theta^{*}_1,\dots,\hat\theta^{*}_B$ from $B$ replicates as $q(p)$.
scioxide-stats uses Hyndman–Fan type 7 for $q(p)$ (linear interpolation between adjacent order statistics).
For confidence level $1-\alpha$, the three intervals in [`BootstrapMethod`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/enum.BootstrapMethod.html) are as follows.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Percentile:}\quad & [\,q(\alpha/2),\ q(1-\alpha/2)\,] \\
\text{Basic:}\quad & [\,2\hat\theta-q(1-\alpha/2),\ 2\hat\theta-q(\alpha/2)\,] \\
\text{BCa (Bca):}\quad & [\,q(\alpha_1),\ q(\alpha_2)\,]
\end{aligned}
$$

The percentile method treats the distribution of $\hat\theta^{*}$ directly as the distribution of $\hat\theta$.
The basic method approximates the distribution of the error $\hat\theta-\theta$ by that of $\hat\theta^{*}-\hat\theta$ and inverts it.
Because the basic method reflects skewness to the opposite side of $\hat\theta$, its interval can extend beyond the range of bounded quantities such as correlation coefficients (as checked in the cell below).

BCa (bias-corrected and accelerated) adjusts quantile probabilities using a model in which, for some monotone transformation $\phi=m(\theta)$, $\hat\phi$ follows a normal distribution $N(\phi-z_0\sigma_\phi,\ \sigma_\phi^2)$ with $\sigma_\phi=1+a\phi$.
The adjusted probabilities are given below, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function and $z^{(\alpha/2)}=\Phi^{-1}(\alpha/2)$.

$$
\alpha_1=\Phi\!\left(z_0+\frac{z_0+z^{(\alpha/2)}}{1-a\,(z_0+z^{(\alpha/2)})}\right),\qquad
\alpha_2=\Phi\!\left(z_0+\frac{z_0+z^{(1-\alpha/2)}}{1-a\,(z_0+z^{(1-\alpha/2)})}\right)
$$

The bias correction $z_0$ comes from the proportion of replicates below $\hat\theta$.
scioxide-stats assigns half weight to replicates equal to $\hat\theta$.

$$
z_0=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\#\{\hat\theta^{*}_b<\hat\theta\}+\tfrac12\#\{\hat\theta^{*}_b=\hat\theta\}}{B}\right)
$$

The acceleration $a$ describes how rapidly the standard error of $\hat\theta$ changes with $\theta$; it is computed from the jackknife influence values $U_i=\bar\theta_{(\cdot)}-\hat\theta_{(i)}$ as $a=\sum U_i^3/\{6(\sum U_i^2)^{3/2}\}$.
Here $\hat\theta_{(i)}$ is the statistic with observation $i$ removed, and $\bar\theta_{(\cdot)}$ is its average.
When $z_0=a=0$, the BCa interval equals the percentile interval.
For the sample mean, $\hat\theta_{(i)}=(n\bar x-x_i)/(n-1)$ gives $U_i=(x_i-\bar x)/(n-1)$, yielding the closed form $a=g_1/(6\sqrt n)$ ($g_1=m_3/m_2^{3/2}$ is sample skewness with divisor $n$).

Compare interval accuracy through the order of the coverage error at a one-sided endpoint.
The percentile and basic methods have one-sided coverage error $O(n^{-1/2})$ (first-order accuracy), while BCa has $O(n^{-1})$ (second-order accuracy; Efron 1987, Hall 1988).
For two-sided intervals, leading error terms at the two ends cancel, so the difference is less pronounced than for one-sided intervals.
The percentile and BCa methods commute with monotone transformations: transforming an interval for $\phi=m(\theta)$ back with $m^{-1}$ gives the interval for $\theta$.
The basic method does not have this property.

BCa computation fails in two cases.
If all jackknife statistics are equal, $\sum U_i^2=0$ and $a$ is undefined; [`ResamplingPlan::evaluate_into`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingPlan.html#method.evaluate_into) returns `NumericalInstability`.
If all replicates lie on one side of $\hat\theta$, then $z_0=\pm\infty$, the adjusted probabilities stick at 0 or 1, or $1-a(z_0+z)\le0$; this also produces `NumericalInstability`.
Neither case returns an interval collapsed to a point as a substitute for BCa.
When all replicates are equal, `degenerate` on [`BootstrapInterval`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.BootstrapInterval.html) is true and the percentile and basic intervals collapse to a point.

In addition to interval `bounds`, `BootstrapInterval` contains the bootstrap standard error `standard_error`, the sample standard deviation of replicates (divisor $B-1$), bias estimate `bias` ($\bar\theta^{*}-\hat\theta$), and BCa quantities `bias_correction` ($z_0$) and `acceleration` ($a$).
`mean_monte_carlo_error` is $s/\sqrt B$, the Monte Carlo error of the replicate mean and `bias`; it is not the error of the interval endpoints.
Endpoints are tail quantiles and have greater Monte Carlo error than the mean.
Efron–Tibshirani (1993) suggest $B=50$–$200$ for standard errors and $B\ge1000$ for quantile-based intervals as rough guidelines.

### Sampling designs (BootstrapDesign)

Pass data in one or more groups and select group treatment and the resampling unit with [`BootstrapDesign`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/enum.BootstrapDesign.html).
Every design preserves the size of each group in every resample.

| Design | Sampling unit | Conditions | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Independent` | Resample observations independently within each group | None | One independent sample, difference between two independent samples |
| `Paired` | Resample row indices and use the same rows across all groups | Equal group lengths | Paired measurements, $(x_i,y_i)$ for the same individual |
| `Stratified` | Resample each stratum independently as with `Independent` | None | Stratified samples with fixed stratum sizes; weight the statistic by stratum sizes |
| `MovingBlock { length }` | Uniformly select contiguous blocks of length `length` from $n-\ell+1$ starting positions and concatenate them | One group, $1\le\ell\le n$ | Stationary time series with autocorrelation |
| `CircularBlock { length }` | As for `MovingBlock`, but blocks can start at any of $n$ positions and wrap from the end to the beginning | One group, $1\le\ell\le n$ | End observations are selected as frequently as others |

If block length $\ell$ is too short, it destroys autocorrelation; if too long, fewer blocks increase variance.
For variance estimation, the $\ell$ minimizing mean squared error is proportional to $n^{1/3}$ (Hall, Horowitz and Jing 1995).
Block designs cannot be used with BCa or jackknife; combining them makes [`ResamplingPlan::new`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingPlan.html#method.new) return `InvalidInput`.

## Jackknife

For a sample of size $n$, let $\hat\theta_{(i)}$ be the statistic with observation $i$ removed and $\bar\theta_{(\cdot)}$ its average.
The jackknife (Quenouille 1949, Tukey 1958) estimates bias and standard error as follows.

$$
\widehat{\mathrm{bias}}=(n-1)\,(\bar\theta_{(\cdot)}-\hat\theta),\qquad
\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}^2=\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n(\hat\theta_{(i)}-\bar\theta_{(\cdot)})^2
$$

The bias-corrected estimate is $\hat\theta-\widehat{\mathrm{bias}}$.
The factor $n-1$ is chosen so that bias of the form $b_1/n+O(n^{-2})$ becomes $O(n^{-2})$ after correction.
For the sample mean, the standard error equals $\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}=s/\sqrt n$ exactly ($s$ is the sample standard deviation with divisor $n-1$), and the bias estimate is zero.

For multiple groups, remove observations from group $g$ one at a time and sum the group contributions.

$$
\widehat{\mathrm{bias}}=\sum_g(n_g-1)\,(\bar\theta_g-\hat\theta),\qquad
\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}^2=\sum_g\frac{n_g-1}{n_g}\sum_{i}(\hat\theta_{(g,i)}-\bar\theta_g)^2
$$

The `Paired` design treats rows as a single group, removing the same row from every group at each deletion.
Read results from `bias`, `bias_corrected`, and `standard_error` on [`JackknifeEstimate`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.JackknifeEstimate.html).
Computation requires $n$ statistic evaluations and uses no random numbers.

The jackknife assumes that the statistic is smooth (differentiable) in the observations.
For nonsmooth statistics such as the sample median, its standard error estimate is inconsistent (Efron–Tibshirani 1993, Section 11.6).
Use the bootstrap standard error in that case.
The delete-$d$ jackknife, which removes $d$ observations at once, is not provided.

## Comparing intervals for law-school correlations

The next three blocks use data from 15 law schools in Table 3.1 of Efron–Tibshirani (1993): mean LSAT scores and mean undergraduate GPAs of entering students.
The quantity of interest is the correlation coefficient $r(\text{LSAT},\text{GPA})=0.7764$; the mean GPA is also selectable for comparison because it has closed forms.
The first block contains definitions shared by two cells: `LSAT` and `GPA` hold the data, and `theta(rows, correlation)` computes the statistic $\hat\theta$ from rows indexed by `rows`.
Because indices refer to rows, resampling rows preserves $(\text{LSAT}_i,\text{GPA}_i)$ pairs.

```rust
//| id: stats-resampling-law-school
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Law-school data and statistic θ"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

/// Mean LSAT scores of entering students at 15 schools (Efron–Tibshirani, Table 3.1).
pub(crate) const LSAT: [f64; 15] = [
    576.0, 635.0, 558.0, 578.0, 666.0, 580.0, 555.0, 661.0, 651.0, 605.0, 653.0, 575.0, 545.0,
    572.0, 594.0,
];
/// Mean undergraduate GPAs at the same 15 schools.
pub(crate) const GPA: [f64; 15] = [
    3.39, 3.30, 2.81, 3.03, 3.44, 3.07, 3.00, 3.43, 3.36, 3.13, 3.12, 2.74, 2.76, 2.88, 2.96,
];

/// Correlation r(LSAT, GPA) or mean GPA for the selected rows.
pub(crate) fn theta(rows: &[usize], correlation: bool) -> f64 {
    let m = rows.len() as f64;
    let mx = rows.iter().map(|&i| LSAT[i]).sum::<f64>() / m;
    let my = rows.iter().map(|&i| GPA[i]).sum::<f64>() / m;
    if !correlation {
        return my;
    }
    let (mut sxy, mut sxx, mut syy) = (0.0, 0.0, 0.0);
    for &i in rows {
        let (dx, dy) = (LSAT[i] - mx, GPA[i] - my);
        sxy += dx * dy;
        sxx += dx * dx;
        syy += dy * dy;
    }
    sxy / RealScalar::sqrt(sxx * syy)
}
```

Because the basic method reflects distribution skewness to the opposite side of $\hat\theta$, it is unsuitable for long-tailed statistics such as this example.
The bootstrap standard error at $B=2000$ is 0.139; setting $B=9999$ gives 0.133, approaching the large-$B$ value (about 0.13) reported by Efron–Tibshirani (1993) for the same data.
Under bivariate normal theory for correlation, the approximate standard error is $(1-r^2)/\sqrt{n-3}=0.115$, smaller than the resampling value for these data.
Selecting mean GPA gives small $|a|$ and $|z_0|$, and the three intervals nearly overlap.

```rust
    design: BootstrapDesign::Independent,

    .evaluate(
        ResamplingProblem::Indexed(&indexed),
        ReplicateOptions::sequential(&mut rng, sampling),

    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;

    return Err("No jackknife inference".into());
};

    .map(|k| {
        let rows: Vec<usize> = all.iter().copied().filter(|&i| i != k).collect();

    .collect();

    let m3 = GPA.iter().map(|v| (v - observed).powi(3)).sum::<f64>() / n as f64;
```

## Permutation tests

Permutation tests use exchangeability of group assignments under the null hypothesis.
Under the null hypothesis of equal distributions for two groups, pooling and reassigning observations to two groups of the original sizes makes every assignment as likely as the observed one.
Computing the statistic for every assignment therefore gives its exact null distribution conditional on the observations.
This p-value assumes no particular shape for the population distribution.

Select the type of exchangeability with [`PermutationKind`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/enum.PermutationKind.html).
Let $k$ groups have sizes $n_1,\dots,n_k$, with $N=n_1+\dots+n_k$.

| Type | Permutation | Number of assignments | Conditions | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Independent` | Reassign all observations to groups of the original sizes, ignoring within-group order | $N!/(n_1!\cdots n_k!)$ | At least two groups | Independent two-sample and $k$-sample comparisons |
| `PairedLabels` | Permute group labels within each row | $(k!)^n$ | At least two groups, all of length $n$ | Paired comparisons, repeated measurements |
| `Pairings` | Permute rows independently within each group, keeping observations in their own groups | $(n!)^k$ | All groups of length $n$ | Association tests (breaking pairings) |

`Exact` in [`PermutationMode`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/enum.PermutationMode.html) enumerates every assignment once.
If the assignment count exceeds the replicate count in [`ResamplingOptions`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingOptions.html), Plan construction produces `ConvergenceFailure`.
`Randomized` draws $B$ assignments uniformly with replacement.
The observed assignment itself may be selected.

Let the observed statistic be $T$, replicate statistics $T^{*}_b$, the count at or below $T$ be $L$, and the count at or above $T$ be $G$.
Ties satisfy $|T^{*}-T|\le\mathrm{atol}+\mathrm{rtol}\,|T|$ and are counted on both sides.
The default tolerances are $\mathrm{atol}=0$ and $\mathrm{rtol}=100\varepsilon$; change them with `with_tie_tolerance`.
The one-sided probabilities and p-value are as follows ([`ResamplingTest`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingTest.html)).

$$
p_-=\frac{L+c}{B+c},\qquad p_+=\frac{G+c}{B+c},\qquad
p_{\text{two-sided}}=\min\{1,\ 2\min(p_-,p_+)\}
$$

For `Exact`, $c=0$ and $B$ is the total number of assignments.
Because enumeration includes the observed assignment itself, the p-value is at least $1/B$.
For `Randomized`, $c=1$; counting the observation as a replicate prevents zero p-values and keeps the test size at or below the nominal level (Phipson and Smyth 2010).
In [`TestAlternative`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/enum.TestAlternative.html), `Less` returns $p_-$, `Greater` returns $p_+$, and `TwoSided` returns twice the smaller one-sided probability.
This two-sided p-value differs in definition from the proportion satisfying $|T^{*}|\ge|T|$, and the values differ for asymmetric null distributions.

Besides the p-value, `ResamplingTest` contains $L$ (`less_equal`), $G$ (`greater_equal`), whether enumeration is exhaustive (`exact`), and the one-sided p-value resolution `resolution` ($1/N$ for exhaustive enumeration, $1/(B+1)$ for randomized sampling).
For randomized sampling, `tail_uncertainty` returns 95% Wilson score intervals for $L/B$ and $G/B$, indicating the p-value's Monte Carlo error.
For $p\approx0.05$ and $B=9999$, the standard error of a one-sided probability is about 0.0022.

## Difference in means between treatment and control groups

### Replacing classical-test p-values with permutation p-values

[`classical_permutation_test_into`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/fn.classical_permutation_test_into.html) replaces the p-value in a [classical test](../classical-tests/) result `ClassicalTestResult` with one computed from the permutation distribution of the same statistic.
The supplied statistic function must return exactly `base.statistic()` under the identity permutation; otherwise, the call produces `InvalidInput`.
Use this to retain a statistic (such as Welch's $t$ or Baumgartner–Weiß–Schindler (BWS)) while computing only its p-value from a permutation distribution without distributional-shape assumptions.
For omnibus statistics such as BWS, use an upper-tail (`TestAlternative::Greater`) Plan.
The resulting `ClassicalPermutationResult` returns a test result `test()` with the permutation p-value and a resampling result `resampling()` containing termination status, $L$, and $G$; `test()` is `None` if execution stops early.

## Monte Carlo tests

A Monte Carlo test (Barnard 1963, Hope 1968) constructs an empirical null distribution by generating $B$ samples of size $n$ from the observation distribution completely specified by the null hypothesis.
Its p-values are $p_-=(L+1)/(B+1)$ and $p_+=(G+1)/(B+1)$, as in randomized permutation testing; if $\alpha(B+1)$ is an integer, its size is exactly $\alpha$ regardless of $B$ (Davison and Hinkley 1997, Chapter 4).
Increasing $B$ raises power and reduces the p-value's Monte Carlo error.

If the null hypothesis includes unknown parameters estimated from observations and used to generate samples (a parametric bootstrap test), the test size is no longer exact and is only asymptotically correct.
For goodness-of-fit tests with estimated parameters, [Distribution goodness-of-fit tests](../distribution-and-variance-tests/) calibrate the estimation step within the same framework.

Construct the Plan with `ResamplingMethod::MonteCarlo { alternative }` and only one group.
Pass the problem through `ResamplingProblem::MonteCarlo { observed, sampler, statistic }`.
`sampler` is a function of type [`NullSampler`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/type.NullSampler.html) that writes a null-hypothesis sample into the output array using only the supplied random number generator.
Unwritten elements, NaN, or infinities produce `NonFinite`.
`statistic` is a [`SampleStatistic`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/type.SampleStatistic.html) returning the statistic from the sample array.

## Testing the mean interarrival time

The next cell uses interarrival times at a radiation detector.
The null hypothesis is “arrivals form a Poisson process with mean interval 1 s”; interarrival times then independently follow an exponential distribution with mean 1 s.
Generate $n=20$ observed intervals from an exponential distribution with true mean interval $\mu$, then perform a Monte Carlo test with $B=9999$, using the mean interval as the statistic and a mean-1-s exponential sampler.
As an independent check, the mean of $n$ intervals under the null follows a gamma distribution with shape $n$ and scale $1/n$ exactly; compute the exact two-sided p-value $2\min\{F(\bar x),1-F(\bar x)\}$ from its cumulative distribution function for comparison.
Setting input $\mu$ to 1 makes the null true; the p-value can then lie anywhere from 0 to 1.

```rust
};
let xbar = observed.iter().sum::<f64>() / n as f64;
// Independent check: under the null, x̄ ~ Gamma(shape n, scale 1/n)
let law = Gamma::<f64>::new(n as f64, 1.0 / n as f64).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let lower = law.cdf(xbar).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let exact = (2.0 * lower.min(1.0 - lower)).min(1.0);
let [low_tail, high_tail] = test.tail_uncertainty().ok_or("No Wilson interval")?;
let rows = vec![
    ("Observed mean x̄ [s]", xbar),
    ("Monte Carlo two-sided p-value", test.p_value()),
    ("Exact gamma two-sided p-value", exact),
    ("Wilson lower limit for lower-tail probability L/B", low_tail.lower()),
    ("Wilson upper limit for lower-tail probability L/B", low_tail.upper()),
    ("Wilson lower limit for upper-tail probability G/B", high_tail.lower()),
    ("Wilson upper limit for upper-tail probability G/B", high_tail.upper()),
    ("Exact lower-tail probability F(x̄)", lower),
];
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "fractionDigits": 4 } }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "monte-carlo" })
);
```

With the default inputs ($\mu=2$ s, seed 11), the observed mean is $\bar x=1.630$ s; the Monte Carlo two-sided p-value 0.0156 is close to the exact gamma value 0.0144.
The exact lower-tail probability $F(\bar x)=0.9928$ lies in the 95% Wilson interval $[0.9904, 0.9938]$ for the lower-tail probability $L/B$.
Repeated failure to fall within the Wilson interval indicates too few $B$ or an error in the sampler or statistic.
At $\mu=0.6$ s, the observed mean is 0.489 s, giving a p-value of 0.0054 in the lower tail (exact value 0.0054).
At $\mu=1$ s, the null is true; this seed gives p-value 0.43 (exact value 0.42), and changing the seed produces approximately uniform p-values.

## Multiscale graph correlation (MGC)

The correlation coefficient captures only linear relationships and is near zero for relationships such as $y=x^2$.
Distance correlation (Székely, Rizzo and Bakirov 2007) takes an inner product of doubly centered pairwise distance matrices for two variables; its population value is zero if and only if the variables are independent.
Multiscale graph correlation (MGC; Vogelstein et al. 2019, Shen, Priebe and Vogelstein 2020) computes local distance correlations by restricting the distance-correlation sums to each point's $k$ neighbors in $x$ and $l$ neighbors in $y$ for every neighborhood size $(k,l)$, and uses a smoothed maximum of the local-correlation map as its statistic.
Taking all points as neighbors, $(k,l)=(n,n)$, gives distance correlation; nearly linear relationships maximize at the global scale, while nonlinear local structure maximizes at smaller scales.

Prepare [`MgcPlan`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.MgcPlan.html) with `from_samples` (two matrices with observations in rows and dimensions in columns, using Euclidean distance) or `from_distances` (distance matrices).
Preparation computes and ranks distance matrices in $O(n^2d+n^2\log n)$ time and $O(n^2)$ memory; each statistic evaluation costs $O(n^2)$ time.
At least five observations are required, and computation uses `f64`.
`statistic` returns [`MgcStatistic`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.MgcStatistic.html): `correlation` is the statistic in $[-1,1]$, and `scale` is the selected neighborhood size $(k,l)$ (counted from 1).
`map_shape` is the local-correlation map size; equal distances are grouped into the same neighborhood size.

The p-value comes from an upper-tail permutation test that permutes rows of $y$.
`permutation_plan` creates a Plan permuting row pairings in $y$ (reassigning group labels for `two_sample`); `test` (allocating output) or `test_into` (writing into caller storage) returns `ClassicalPermutationResult`.
`Exact` enumerates all $n!$ pairings, so it is limited to small samples for which $n!$ fits within the replicate limit ($n\le7$ with the default 9999).
`two_sample` tests equality of two sample distributions as independence from group labels.

## Detecting a quadratic relationship

The next cell places 40 equally spaced $x$ points on $[-1,1]$ and generates $y=x^2+\sigma\varepsilon$ ($\varepsilon$ is standard normal).
Compare the MGC permutation test ($B=999$) with the correlation coefficient's $t$ test (`pearson_test` in [Classical tests](../classical-tests/)).
Because $y$ is an even function of $x$, the population correlation is zero and the correlation test does not reject the null.
Increasing noise $\sigma$ eventually prevents MGC from detecting the relationship as well.

```rust
//| id: stats-resampling-mgc
//| ref: stats-resampling-mgc
//| title: "Multiscale graph correlation and correlation testing for y = x² + noise"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-linalg, scioxide-random, scioxide-stats]
//| inputs:
//|   noise: {"type":"range","label":"Noise standard deviation σ [-]","min":0.05,"max":1.0,"step":0.05,"value":0.1}
//|   sampling_seed: {"type":"integer","label":"Random seed","min":0,"max":100000,"value":1}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"noisy","label":"σ = 1","description":"Noise obscures the relationship, the MGC p-value increases, and the selected scale becomes global (40, 40).","inputs":{"noise":1.0}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"dependence","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.4194153994239032,"rtol":1e-09}}
//|       - {"artifact":"dependence","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.001,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"dependence","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":6}
//|       - {"artifact":"dependence","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"min":0.9}}
//|   - case: noisy
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"dependence","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.427,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"dependence","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":40}
//|       - {"artifact":"dependence","path":"/rows/0/4","equals":40}
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_random::{
    Distribution, Normal, OutputPolicy, SamplingPolicy, SeedableRng, SplitMix64,
};
use scioxide_stats::{
    ClassicalOptions, MgcPlan, PermutationMode, ReplicateOptions, ResamplingOptions,
    pearson_test,
};

let n = 40;
let normal = Normal::<f64>::new(0.0, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(sampling_seed as u64);
let x: Vec<f64> = (0..n)
    .map(|i| -1.0 + 2.0 * i as f64 / (n - 1) as f64)
    .collect();
let mut y = Vec::with_capacity(n);
for &v in &x {
    y.push(v * v + noise * normal.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}
// Rows are observations and columns are dimensions (one dimension here)
let xm = Matrix::from_vec(n, 1, x.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let ym = Matrix::from_vec(n, 1, y.clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mgc = MgcPlan::from_samples(xm.view(), ym.view(), ClassicalOptions::default())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut scratch = mgc.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let statistic = mgc
    .statistic(None, &mut scratch)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = ResamplingOptions::default().with_replicates(999);
let plan = mgc
    .permutation_plan(PermutationMode::Randomized, options)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut perm_rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(sampling_seed as u64 + 1);
let sampling = SamplingPolicy::rollback(OutputPolicy::Atomic);
let result = mgc
    .test(&plan, ReplicateOptions::sequential(&mut perm_rng, sampling))
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let test = result.test().ok_or("Permutation test stopped early")?;
let pearson = pearson_test(&x, &y).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rows = vec![
    (
        "MGC",
        statistic.correlation,
        test.p_value(),
        Some(statistic.scale[0]),
        Some(statistic.scale[1]),
    ),
    (
        "Correlation (t test)",
        pearson.statistic(),
        pearson.p_value(),
        None,
        None,
    ),
];
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Test" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Statistic [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "p value [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "x neighborhood k", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "y neighborhood l", "type": "integer", "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "dependence" })
);
let points: Vec<[f64; 2]> = x.iter().zip(&y).map(|(&a, &b)| [a, b]).collect();
let curve: Vec<[f64; 2]> = x.iter().map(|&a| [a, a * a]).collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "xy",
    "xLabel": "x [-]",
    "yLabel": "y [-]",
    "dataZoom": true,
    "tooltip": true,
    "layers": [
        { "id": "data", "kind": "scatter", "name": "Observations", "points": points },
        {
            "id": "truth",
            "kind": "line",
            "name": "y = x²",
            "points": curve,
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        }
    ]
}));
```

With the default inputs ($\sigma=0.1$), the MGC p-value is the permutation-test minimum $1/(B+1)=0.001$; the selected neighborhood is small at $x$ ($k=6$), while the correlation-test p-value is 0.98.
Local correlations at small $k$ capture the local structure in which nearby $x$ points also have nearby $y$ values.
At $\sigma=1$, noise obscures the quadratic relationship (amplitude 1) and the MGC p-value also increases.
The selected scale is then global at $(40,40)$, and the statistic equals distance correlation.

## Plan, workspace, and result types

[`ResamplingPlan::new`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingPlan.html#method.new) checks group sizes `lengths`, the method, and `ResamplingOptions`, precomputing group boundaries, the exhaustive assignment count, jackknife deletion count, and tie tolerance.
The statistic receives a sequence of indices into the concatenated group array through [`ResampledIndices`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResampledIndices.html).
Group $g$ starts at $n_0+\dots+n_{g-1}$; extract its indices with `group(g)`.
`ResamplingOptions` defaults to 9999 replicates, one replicate in the workspace at a time (`batch_size`), no memory or random-call limits, and relative tie tolerance $100\varepsilon$.
`with_max_rng_calls` limits generator calls per replicate; a replicate exceeding it stops execution with `BudgetExhausted`.

The main errors from `ResamplingPlan::new` are as follows.

| Condition | Kind |
|---|---|
| No groups, a zero-sized group, or zero replicate count or `batch_size` | `InvalidInput` |
| Unequal group lengths for `Paired`, `PairedLabels`, or `Pairings` | `InvalidInput` |
| Multiple groups for a block design, or block length outside $1..=n$ | `InvalidInput` |
| Block design for BCa or jackknife, or fewer than two groups | `InvalidInput` |
| Fewer than two bootstrap replicates | `InvalidInput` |
| One group for `Independent` or `PairedLabels` permutations, or multiple groups for a Monte Carlo test | `InvalidInput` |
| Exhaustive assignment count or jackknife deletion count exceeds the replicate count | `ConvergenceFailure` |
| Exhaustive assignment count cannot be represented by `usize` | `SizeOverflow` |

Choose from four statistic function types.
[`IndexStatistic`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/type.IndexStatistic.html) returns a value from the index sequence and is supplied through `ResamplingProblem::Indexed`.
[`WorkspaceIndexStatistic`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/type.WorkspaceIndexStatistic.html) additionally receives a per-worker scratch array and is supplied through `IndexedWorkspace`.
Set the array length with `with_statistic_scratch`; its contents are undefined on each call, so write every element before reading it.
For Monte Carlo tests, [`SampleStatistic`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/type.SampleStatistic.html) and [`WorkspaceSampleStatistic`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/type.WorkspaceSampleStatistic.html) are the corresponding types.
For statistics that temporarily arrange permuted values, such as the median, scratch arrays avoid allocation in each replicate.
Functions may be called from multiple threads and must be `Sync`; side effects are not rolled back on errors.

Select random streams with [`ReplicateOptions`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ReplicateOptions.html).
`ReplicateOptions::sequential(rng, sampling)` draws replicates sequentially from one generator.
The state policy in `sampling` determines whether an error restores the generator (`SamplingPolicy::rollback`) or leaves it advanced (`advance_on_error`); the output policy determines whether the output array is written once at the end (`OutputPolicy::Atomic`) or after each replicate.
`ReplicateOptions::indexed(root, first_stream, policy, publication)` gives replicate $i$ the split stream `root.split(first_stream + i)`, allowing parallel execution with results independent of worker count.
Exhaustive enumeration, jackknife, and BCa deletions use no random numbers.
The two constructors return `ReplicateOptions<SequentialReplicates<…>>` and `ReplicateOptions<IndexedReplicates<…>>`; `evaluate` and `evaluate_into` accept only these two types.
The trait `ReplicateExecution` determines the accepted types; users are not expected to implement it.

`evaluate` allocates output and workspace, executes, and returns [`ResamplingOutput`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingOutput.html).
`distribution()` returns replicate statistics in replicate order; if execution stops early, it contains only completed replicates.
It excludes jackknife values used for BCa.
The borrowed form `evaluate_into(problem, replicates, output, workspace, scope)` writes into an output array of length `replicates()` and a [`ResamplingWorkspace`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingWorkspace.html) created with `workspace()`.
With a workspace available, replicate execution performs no allocation.
Passing an execution context (`ExecutionContext`) as `scope` makes this a root computation; passing another computation's `SolveContext` counts it as a child within that computation's limits.
`workspace_bytes()` gives the workspace size; exceeding `max_workspace_bytes` produces `ResourceLimit`.
`ResamplingWorkspace::last_attempt()` returns the record of the last execution.

The resulting [`ResamplingResult`](https://docs.rs/scioxide-stats/0.1.0/scioxide_stats/struct.ResamplingResult.html) contains the observed statistic `observed()`, requested replicate count `requested()`, completed count `completed()`, termination status `termination()`, and inference `inference()`.
Termination is one of `Completed`, `BudgetExhausted`, or `Stopped(_)` from [`StatsTermination`](oxref:stats-finalization-and-termination).
Stopping at a computation limit, cancellation, or deadline still returns `Ok`, with a status other than `Completed` and inference `None`.
Inference is one of `Bootstrap` (`BootstrapInterval`), `Jackknife` (`JackknifeEstimate`), or `Test` (`ResamplingTest`) from `ResamplingInference`.

One-shot functions construct and execute a Plan with fixed defaults.

| Function | Procedure | Fixed settings |
|---|---|---|
| `bootstrap(statistic, &x, &mut rng)` | BCa interval | `Independent`, 9999 replicates, 95%, two-sided |
| `jackknife(statistic, &x)` | Bias and standard error | `Independent`, $n$ deletions, no random numbers |
| `permutation_test(statistic, &x, &y, &mut rng)` | Independent two-group permutation test | `Randomized`, 9999 replicates, two-sided |
| `monte_carlo_test(statistic, sampler, &observed, &mut rng)` | Monte Carlo test | 9999 replicates, two-sided |

A function receiving a sample array, such as `mean`, can be passed directly as the statistic.
One-shot calls do not return incomplete estimates; incomplete replication produces an error (`BudgetExhausted` maps to a sample resource limit, `Stopped` to `ExecutionStopped`).

## Methods not provided

The following methods are absent from scioxide-stats.

- Studentized bootstrap (bootstrap-$t$) and ABC intervals are not provided.
  Construct bootstrap-$t$ by making the statistic function return $(\hat\theta^{*}-\hat\theta)/\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}^{*}$ for each replicate and assembling intervals from quantiles of `distribution()`.
- Double-bootstrap calibration, $m$-out-of-$n$ bootstrap, smoothed bootstrap, and wild bootstrap are not provided.
  Every design preserves group sizes and resamples the observations themselves with replacement.
- The stationary bootstrap, which chooses block lengths geometrically (Politis and Romano 1994), is not provided.
  `CircularBlock` is the closest alternative.
- Delete-$d$ and block jackknife are not provided.
- Freedman–Lane permutation tests that permute regression residuals are not provided.
  `Pairings` permutes the pairings of observations themselves.

## Summary

Construct bootstrap intervals by supplying `BootstrapDesign` and `BootstrapMethod` to `ResamplingPlan`; use BCa for skewed statistics and avoid the basic method for bounded quantities.
BCa's $z_0$ and $a$ are included in the result and can be checked against jackknife values and the replicate distribution.
Interval endpoints retain Monte Carlo error depending on $B$, which `mean_monte_carlo_error` does not describe; use $B\ge1000$ for quantile-based intervals.
For small samples, use `Exact` to obtain exact permutation p-values; for larger samples, use `Randomized` and check whether $B$ is sufficient through the Wilson interval from `tail_uncertainty`.
Monte Carlo tests apply to fully specified null models, with exact size regardless of $B$.
Use MGC permutation tests to detect nonlinear dependence.
