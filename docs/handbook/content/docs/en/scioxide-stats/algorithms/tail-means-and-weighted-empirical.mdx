A quantile $Q(p)$ locates the point at probability $p$, but does not describe how large the values beyond it in the tail are.
The **tail mean** averages the mass above probability $p$ (or below $p$); in finance it is called Expected Shortfall (ES) or CVaR.
This page explains weighted empirical distribution functions and quantiles, tail-mean definitions and properties, and fractional allocation when tied observations lie at the boundary.
It then shows how to read the information returned by scioxide-stats tail means and checks subadditivity of quantiles and tail means with a discrete example.

## Weighted empirical distribution function

For pairs of values $x_i$ and nonnegative weights $w_i$ (total weight $W = \sum w_i > 0$), define the weighted empirical distribution function as follows.

$$
F_w(x) = \frac{1}{W} \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\, \mathbf 1\{x_i \le x\}
$$

Its left-continuous inverse returns the first observation at which cumulative weight reaches $pW$.

$$
Q_w(p) = \inf\{x : F_w(x) \ge p\}
$$

With equal weights, this equals the type 1 (`NearestRank`) quantile.

Pass relative mass weights with `Weights::<_, Mass>`.
`EmpiricalDistribution::new(values, Weights::<_, Mass>::new(&masses))` copies and sorts values and masses, rejecting empty input, negative masses, and zero total mass with `InvalidInput`, and nonfinite values with `NonFinite`.
Zero-mass pairs are accepted but contribute neither support nor mass.
Passing `QuantileRule::inverse_ecdf()` to `quantile` returns $Q_w(p)$; at $p = 0$ it returns the smallest observation with positive mass.
For a single query, pass an array of observation–mass pairs `Weights<&mut [(S, S)], Mass>` to `quantile_in_place`.
It uses deterministic three-way introselect on the caller's array, taking worst-case $O(n)$ time without allocation.
Cumulative masses are summed internally with exact integer arithmetic, preventing rounding from changing comparisons with the target mass $pW$.

For mass weights, only this inverse quantile (corresponding to type 1) is defined.
Mass has no sample size, so the interpolation position $np + m$ is undefined.
For integer frequencies, `Weights::<_, Frequency>` supports all nine definitions ([Mathematics of empirical distributions, sample quantiles, and interpolation conventions](oxref:stats-quantile-hyndman-fan-definitions)).

## Tail-mean definition and properties

Define upper and lower tail means at probability $p \in (0, 1)$ through integrals of the quantile function as follows.

$$
\mathrm{ES}^{\mathrm{upper}}_p = \frac{1}{1-p} \int_p^1 Q(u)\,du, \qquad
\mathrm{ES}^{\mathrm{lower}}_p = \frac{1}{p} \int_0^p Q(u)\,du
$$

For continuous distributions, this equals $\mathrm{ES}^{\mathrm{upper}}_p = E[X \mid X \ge Q(p)]$.
Use the upper tail for quantities where large values are undesirable, such as losses or latency, and the lower tail where small values are undesirable, such as strength or remaining capacity.
`TailSide::Upper` and `TailSide::Lower` select these two sides.

The upper tail mean is also the minimum of the following optimization problem (Rockafellar and Uryasev, 2002).

$$
\mathrm{ES}^{\mathrm{upper}}_p = \min_{c \in \mathbb R}\Bigl\{ c + \frac{1}{1-p} E\bigl[(X - c)_+\bigr] \Bigr\}
$$

One minimizer is $c = Q(p)$.
This representation converts optimization problems whose objective includes a tail mean (such as portfolio selection) into linear programs.
scioxide-stats does not solve this minimization; it directly evaluates the integral as described next.

Artzner et al. (1999) called a risk measure $\rho$ **coherent** when it satisfies four properties.
These are subadditivity $\rho(X + Y) \le \rho(X) + \rho(Y)$, positive homogeneity $\rho(\lambda X) = \lambda \rho(X)$ ($\lambda \ge 0$), monotonicity ($X \le Y$ implies $\rho(X) \le \rho(Y)$), and translation invariance $\rho(X + c) = \rho(X) + c$.
Subadditivity means that combining two risks produces a measure no greater than the sum of their separate measures: diversification does not increase the measure.
Quantiles themselves (called VaR in finance) can violate subadditivity; the section [A counterexample to quantile subadditivity](#a-counterexample-to-quantile-subadditivity) computes an example.
The tail mean defined by integrating the quantile function satisfies all four properties (Acerbi and Tasche, 2002).

These are properties of a distribution functional, distinct from the accuracy of finite-sample estimates.
When tail mass $n(1-p)$ is small, a few observations determine the tail-mean estimate, which varies greatly across samples.

## Fractional allocation of boundary mass

In a finite sample, the upper-tail mass $n(1-p)$ need not be an integer, and multiple observations may tie at the boundary value.
Including or excluding every boundary observation makes the tail mass differ from $1-p$ and causes the tail mean to jump after a small change in $p$.
`TailMeanConvention::FractionalInverseEcdf` evaluates the integral above exactly for the inverse empirical distribution function.
In the general weighted case, let the target upper-tail mass be $W_{\mathrm{tail}} = (1-p)W$ and the boundary value $q$; proceed as follows.

1. Compute the mass $W_{>} = \sum_{x_i > q} w_i$ and weighted value sum $S_{>} = \sum_{x_i > q} w_i x_i$ of observations greater than $q$.
2. Set the mass taken from boundary value $q$ to $\Delta = W_{\mathrm{tail}} - W_{>}$.
   $q$ is chosen so that $0 < \Delta \le \sum_{x_i = q} w_i$.
3. Compute the tail mean from the following formula.

$$
\mathrm{ES}^{\mathrm{upper}}_p = \frac{S_{>} + \Delta\, q}{W_{\mathrm{tail}}}
$$

The numerator adds the exceedance sum $S_{>}$ and the contribution $\Delta q$ from the fraction of mass taken at the boundary.
Keeping the denominator equal to the target mass $W_{\mathrm{tail}}$ makes the map $p \mapsto \mathrm{ES}_p$ continuous.
The lower tail is computed analogously with mass $pW$.
Lower-tail $p = 0$ and upper-tail $p = 1$ are empty tails and give `InvalidInput`; the opposite endpoints (lower-tail $p = 1$ and upper-tail $p = 0$) return the full-sample mean.

The resulting `TailMeanResult` contains the mean `mean`, probability `probability`, tail side `side`, convention `convention`, tail mass `tail_mass`, boundary value `boundary_value`, mass taken from the boundary `boundary_mass`, and its numerator contribution `boundary_contribution`.
Mass is measured in observation counts for unweighted data and in the caller's weight units for mass-weighted data.
Auxiliary masses and contributions return `None` when unrepresentable in `S`.
If the mean is unrepresentable in `S`, the call returns `Err` rather than a result.
For mass weights, a nonempty tail mass rounding to 0 gives `NumericalInstability`.

Tail means accept `Unweighted` and `Mass` weights implementing the trait `TailWeights`.
Passing frequency counts as masses produces the same tail mean for frequency-weighted samples.
`tail_mean_in_place` sorts an owned array in place, accepting an unweighted array or an array of observation–mass pairs, and computes the result in $O(n \log n)$ time without temporary allocation.
Validation precedes reordering, so failure leaves the input array unchanged.
The presorted `EmpiricalDistribution::tail_mean` takes $O(n)$ time, making it suitable for querying multiple levels on the same sample.

## Thresholds and tail means as the level changes

For eight right-tailed observations $[1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 12, 20, 35]$, vary the level $\alpha$ from 0.50 to 0.95, computing the type 1 quantile (threshold) and upper tail mean with `quantile_in_place` and `tail_mean_in_place`.
Both functions reorder their working arrays, so each level receives a copy of the same sample.
Compare the tail mean with an independent hand calculation that adds the largest $\lfloor m \rfloor$ sorted observations and a fraction $m - \lfloor m \rfloor$ of the next observation ($m = 8(1-\alpha)$).
The table shows boundary information at the selected level.

```rust
//| id: stats-tail-mean-level
//| ref: stats-tail-mean-level
//| title: "Vary the quantile level to compare thresholds and upper tail means"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-stats]
//| inputs:
//|   quantile_level: {"type":"range","label":"Quantile level α [-]","description":"Change the empirical mass 1−α retained in the upper tail.","min":0.5,"max":0.95,"step":0.01,"value":0.75}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"half","label":"α = 0.5","description":"A tail mass of 4 observations equals exactly the largest four observations.","inputs":{"quantile_level":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"fractional","label":"α = 0.8","description":"Of the 1.6 observations of tail mass, take 0.6 from boundary value 20.","inputs":{"quantile_level":0.8}}
//|   - {"id":"thin-tail","label":"α = 0.95","description":"Tail mass is only 0.4 observations, so only the maximum determines the result.","inputs":{"quantile_level":0.95}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":12}
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":27.5,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":20}
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/5","range":{"max":1e-13}}
//|   - case: half
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":18.5,"atol":1e-13}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":7}
//|   - case: fractional
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":29.375,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/4","approx":{"value":0.6,"atol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: thin-tail
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":35.0,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"tail","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.4,"atol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_stats::{
    QuantileMethod, QuantileProbability, QuantileRule, TailMeanResult, TailSide,
    quantile_in_place, tail_mean_in_place,
};

// Right-tailed sample (sorted, dimensionless).
let sample = [1.0_f64, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0, 7.0, 12.0, 20.0, 35.0];
let evaluate = |alpha: f64| -> Result<(f64, TailMeanResult<f64>), String> {
    let p = QuantileProbability::new(alpha).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Copy the working array for each call because the function reorders it.
    let mut work = sample;
    let threshold =
        quantile_in_place(&mut work, p, QuantileRule::new(QuantileMethod::NearestRank))
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            .value();
    let mut work = sample;
    let tail = tail_mean_in_place(&mut work, p, TailSide::Upper).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok((threshold, tail))
};
// Independent check: average of the largest floor(m) observations and fraction m − floor(m) of the next (m = n(1 − α)).
let by_hand = |alpha: f64| {
    let m = (1.0 - alpha) * sample.len() as f64;
    let whole = m.floor() as usize;
    let top: f64 = sample.iter().rev().take(whole).sum();
    let edge = if whole < sample.len() {
        (m - whole as f64) * sample[sample.len() - 1 - whole]
    } else {
        0.0
    };
    (top + edge) / m
};
let (mut thresholds, mut means, mut worst) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), 0.0_f64);
for i in 50..=95 {
    let alpha = f64::from(i) / 100.0;
    let (threshold, tail) = evaluate(alpha)?;
    worst = worst.max((tail.mean() - by_hand(alpha)).abs());
    thresholds.push([alpha, threshold]);
    means.push([alpha, tail.mean()]);
}
let (threshold, tail) = evaluate(quantile_level)?;
emit_line_chart!(
    &vec![
        serde_json::json!({"name": "Type 1 quantile (threshold)", "points": thresholds, "style": {"step": "end"}}),
        serde_json::json!({"name": "Upper tail mean", "points": means}),
    ],
    options = &serde_json::json!({
        "xLabel": "Quantile level α [-]",
        "yLabel": "Sample value [-]",
        "xMin": 0.5,
        "xMax": 0.95,
        "yMin": 0.0,
        "yMax": 36.0,
        "annotations": [{"kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": quantile_level, "label": "Selected α"}]
    })
);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Threshold (type 1 quantile) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Upper tail mean [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Tail mass [observations]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Boundary value [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Mass taken from boundary [observations]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Maximum difference from hand calculation (46 levels) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
let row = (
    threshold,
    tail.mean(),
    tail.tail_mass(),
    tail.boundary_value(),
    tail.boundary_mass(),
    worst,
);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    vec![row],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "tail" })
);
```

The threshold (type 1 quantile) is a step function jumping between sample values; the tail mean is continuous and piecewise linear in the level.
At the default $\alpha = 0.75$, tail mass is $8 \times 0.25 = 2$ observations, the threshold is 12, and the tail mean is 27.5, averaging the two largest observations, 35 and 20.
The boundary value is 20, and its entire mass of one observation is taken.
The threshold 12 lies outside the tail; the tail mean averages only observations exceeding the threshold.
At $\alpha = 0.8$, tail mass is 1.6 observations, taking one observation of 35 and 0.6 of boundary value 20, giving tail mean $(35 + 0.6 \cdot 20)/1.6 = 29.375$.
At $\alpha = 0.5$, the threshold is 4 and the tail mean 18.5 (the average of the largest four); at $\alpha = 0.95$, tail mass is only 0.4 observations, so a fraction of the maximum 35 alone determines the tail mean, equal to the threshold 35.
At all 46 levels, the tail mean agrees with the hand-calculated formula within $7.1 \times 10^{-15}$.

## A counterexample to quantile subadditivity

Let two independent loans $X$ and $Y$ each incur loss 100 with probability $q$ and loss 0 with probability $1-q$.
Their total $X + Y$ takes losses 0, 100, and 200 with probabilities $(1-q)^2$, $2q(1-q)$, and $q^2$.
When $q \le 1 - \alpha < 1 - (1-q)^2$, the level-$\alpha$ quantiles of $X$ and $Y$ are both 0, but the total's quantile is 100.
Combining them gives a quantile exceeding the sum of individual quantiles, violating subadditivity.
For tail means, the sum of the individual values $100\,q/(1-\alpha)$ is at least the total's value.

The next cell passes the three distributions as value–probability pairs through `Weights::<_, Mass>` to `EmpiricalDistribution`, obtaining upper level-$\alpha$ quantiles (`QuantileRule::inverse_ecdf`) and upper tail means (`tail_mean`).
Compare the tail means with an independent calculation summing the quantile-function integral $\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\int_\alpha^1 Q(u)\,du$ over each value's probability interval.
The last row is “sum of individual values − combined value”; a negative value violates subadditivity.

```rust
//| id: stats-tail-subadditivity
//| ref: stats-tail-subadditivity
//| title: "Compare subadditivity of quantiles and tail means for losses on two loans"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-stats]
//| inputs:
//|   default_probability: {"type":"range","label":"Loss probability q [-]","description":"Probability that each loan incurs a loss of 100.","min":0.01,"max":0.1,"step":0.01,"value":0.04}
//|   tail_level: {"type":"range","label":"Level α [-]","description":"Set the probability 1−α retained in the upper tail.","min":0.9,"max":0.99,"step":0.01,"value":0.95}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"rare-loss","label":"q = 0.01","description":"Since (1 − q)² ≥ α, the total's quantile is also 0 and subadditivity is not violated.","inputs":{"default_probability":0.01}}
//|   - {"id":"high-level","label":"α = 0.99","description":"Since q ≥ 1 − α, each individual quantile is 100 and subadditivity is not violated.","inputs":{"tail_level":0.99}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":100}
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":80.0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":103.2,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/2/3","range":{"min":-1e-11,"max":1e-11}}
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":-100}
//|   - case: rare-loss
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":0}
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":0.0,"atol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: high-level
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/3/1","equals":100}
//|       - {"artifact":"risk","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":116.0,"atol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_stats::{
    EmpiricalDistribution, Mass, QuantileProbability, QuantileRule, TailSide, Weights,
};

let q = default_probability;
let alpha = QuantileProbability::new(tail_level).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let single = (vec![0.0_f64, 100.0], vec![1.0 - q, q]);
let total = (
    vec![0.0_f64, 100.0, 200.0],
    vec![(1.0 - q) * (1.0 - q), 2.0 * q * (1.0 - q), q * q],
);
// Independent check: Q(u) equals x_k on the probability interval [F(x_{k−1}), F(x_k)), so sum its overlaps with [α, 1].
let by_hand = |values: &[f64], masses: &[f64]| {
    let (mut lower, mut integral) = (0.0_f64, 0.0_f64);
    for (&x, &m) in values.iter().zip(masses) {
        let upper = lower + m;
        integral += x * (upper.min(1.0) - lower.max(tail_level)).max(0.0);
        lower = upper;
    }
    integral / (1.0 - tail_level)
};
let measure = |(values, masses): &(Vec<f64>, Vec<f64>)| -> Result<(f64, f64, f64), String> {
    let law = EmpiricalDistribution::new(values, Weights::<_, Mass>::new(masses))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let var = law
        .quantile(alpha, QuantileRule::inverse_ecdf())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .value();
    let es = law
        .tail_mean(alpha, TailSide::Upper)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .mean();
    Ok((var, es, es - by_hand(values, masses)))
};
let (single_var, single_es, single_check) = measure(&single)?;
let (total_var, total_es, total_check) = measure(&total)?;
let rows = vec![
    (
        "X (first loan)",
        single_var,
        single_es,
        Some(single_check),
    ),
    (
        "Y (second loan)",
        single_var,
        single_es,
        Some(single_check),
    ),
    ("X + Y", total_var, total_es, Some(total_check)),
    (
        "Sum of individual values − combined value",
        2.0 * single_var - total_var,
        2.0 * single_es - total_es,
        None,
    ),
];
let fixed2 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 2 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Loss", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Quantile (VaR) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Upper tail mean (ES) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed2 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Tail mean minus integral [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "risk" })
);
```

With defaults $q = 0.04$ and $\alpha = 0.95$, both individual quantiles are 0 and the combined quantile is 100; the sum of individual quantiles minus the combined quantile is $-100$.
Each individual tail mean is $100 \times 0.04/0.05 = 80$ and the combined value is 103.2; subtracting the combined value from the individual sum 160 gives the positive value 56.8.
The combined tail mean 103.2 equals $(0.0016 \cdot 200 + 0.0484 \cdot 100)/0.05$, taking loss 200 with probability 0.0016 and probability 0.0484 from loss 100, then dividing by tail probability 0.05.
The difference between `tail_mean` values and the quantile-function integral is at most $10^{-13}$, arising from rounded probability values.

At $q = 0.01$, $(1-q)^2 = 0.9801 \ge 0.95$ gives a combined quantile of 0 as well, so quantile subadditivity is not violated.
Individual tail means are then 20 and the combined value is 40; both tails fall within the same event range (loss 100), so equality holds apart from rounding.
At $\alpha = 0.99$, the individual quantiles are 100, and their sum 200 exceeds the combined quantile 100.
Quantiles violate subadditivity when rare losses have probability below $1 - \alpha$, hiding outside each individual tail but entering the combined tail.

## Summary

A tail mean averages all mass beyond the quantile; defining it through the quantile-function integral gives subadditivity and continuity in the level.
Quantiles themselves are not subadditive: combining two rare losses can exceed the sum of individual quantiles.
For discrete samples, including a fractional mass of boundary observations evaluates the integral exactly; the `FractionalInverseEcdf` convention performs this allocation.
The resulting `TailMeanResult` retains tail-mass and boundary information; check how many observations' worth of mass actually enter the tail before interpreting the value.
At levels whose tail mass is less than one observation, a few observations near the maximum determine the estimate.
