The weighted mean $\bar x_w = \sum w_i x_i / \sum w_i$ of observations $x_i$ with weights $w_i \ge 0$ uses the same formula regardless of what the weights represent.
However, the mean's standard error and the variance degrees of freedom depend on whether weights represent observation counts, sample representativeness, or measurement precision.
scioxide-stats requires weight semantics to be specified through types `Frequency`, `Reliability`, and `Analytic`, defining divisors, degrees of freedom, and interval assumptions for each meaning.
This page derives variance estimators and degrees of freedom for the three meanings and maps weight types to supported estimators.
It then explains confidence-interval construction for means and its limitations, and experimentally checks coverage when weight meanings are confused.

## Three meanings of weights

For $n$ records $(x_i, w_i)$, define total weight $W = \sum w_i$, sum of squared weights $Q = \sum w_i^2$, and weighted sum of squared deviations $S_w = \sum w_i (x_i - \bar x_w)^2$.
Zero-weight records are excluded even from record count $n$.

**Frequency weights** (`Frequency`) represent observing value $x_i$ a total of $w_i$ times.
Weights must be integers, and the replicated sample size is $N = W$.
Estimate the variance and the variance of the mean as follows to match ordinary estimators for the expanded $N$ observations.

$$
s^2 = \frac{S_w}{N-1}, \qquad \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar x_w) = \frac{s^2}{N}
$$

Multiplying all weights by $c$ increases the apparent sample size by $c$, so the standard error is multiplied by approximately $1/\sqrt c$.

**Reliability weights** (`Reliability`) represent relative importance, such as sample representativeness or inverse sampling probabilities.
Assume independent observations with common variance $\sigma^2$ and weights unrelated to observation variance.
Then $\mathrm{Var}(\bar x_w) = \sigma^2 Q/W^2$ and $E[S_w] = \sigma^2 (W - Q/W)$, giving the following estimators of the variance and the variance of the mean.

$$
s^2 = \frac{S_w}{W - Q/W}, \qquad \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar x_w) = s^2 \frac{Q}{W^2}
$$

$s^2$ is unbiased for $\sigma^2$; multiplying weights by a constant leaves the mean, variance, and standard error unchanged.

**Analytic weights** (`Analytic`) represent differing measurement precision, as in $\mathrm{Var}(x_i) = \tau^2 / w_i$.
$\tau^2$ is an unknown common scale, called the noise scale.
When weights are proportional to true precision, the weighted mean has minimum variance among linear unbiased estimators (the Gauss–Markov theorem).
In this model, $\mathrm{Var}(\bar x_w) = \tau^2/W$ and $E[S_w] = (n-1)\tau^2$, so use the following estimators.

$$
\hat\tau^2 = \frac{S_w}{n-1}, \qquad \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar x_w) = \frac{\hat\tau^2}{W}
$$

Degrees of freedom equal the positive-weight record count $n$ minus 1.
Multiplying weights by $c$ also multiplies $\hat\tau^2$ by $c$, leaving the variance of the mean unchanged.

| Weight meaning | Variance divisor | Variance of the mean | Degrees of freedom | Weights multiplied by $c$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | $N - 1$ | $s^2/N$ | $N - 1$ | Standard error multiplied by approximately $1/\sqrt c$ |
| Reliability | $W - Q/W$ | $s^2 Q/W^2$ | $W^2/Q - 1$ | Unchanged |
| Analytic | $n - 1$ ($\hat\tau^2$) | $\hat\tau^2/W$ | $n - 1$ | Unchanged |

Each estimator correctly estimates mean variance only under its own model.
Applied to data from another model, it systematically overestimates or underestimates mean variance.
The magnitude is derived and tested in [Weight meanings and coverage](#weight-meanings-and-coverage).

## Weight types and supported estimators

Weights in scioxide-stats belong to one of five semantic types (weight models).
`Unweighted` means no weighting, `Frequency` integer replication counts, `Mass` relative masses defining a discrete distribution, `Reliability` reliability weights, and `Analytic` analytic weights.
`Mass` defines only distribution summaries (weighted means, quantiles, and tail means); without a sampling model, it defines neither sample variance nor standard error.
All five types implement the trait `WeightModel`.
`WeightModel` is a sealed trait that external types cannot implement, fixing the set of weight meanings.

Functions express accepted weight types through trait bounds, rejecting undefined combinations at compile time.

| Trait | Implementing types | Main accepted APIs |
|---|---|---|
| `BatchWeights` | `Unweighted`, `Weights<&[u64], Frequency>`, `Weights<&[S], M>` (`M` is `Mass`, `Reliability`, or `Analytic`) | Weight storage for `BatchView::with_weights`, `statistic`, and `EmpiricalDistribution::new` |
| `EmpiricalWeights` | `Unweighted`, `Frequency`, `Mass` | Order-based estimators such as empirical distributions, quantiles, and empirical CDFs |
| `MomentWeights` | `Frequency`, `Reliability`, `Analytic` | `OnlineWeightedMoments`, `OnlineWeightedCovariance`, `reduce_weighted_moments` |
| `ConjugateWeights` | `Frequency`, `Analytic` | Conjugate normal-model updates (`NormalInverseGammaPrior::update_weighted`) |
| `RegressionWeights` | `Unweighted`, `Weights<&[S], Frequency>`, `Weights<&[S], Analytic>` | Row weights for linear regression, operator regression, and generalized linear models |

`ConjugateWeights` excludes `Reliability` because reliability weights do not define a likelihood.
Regression excludes reliability and mass weights for the same reason.
`BatchWeights` also determines weight storage: frequency weights use `u64` arrays; other weights use arrays of the same real type as the observations.
Weight values are validated by the function reading them.
Negative or nonfinite weights and noninteger frequency weights produce errors at that point.
More specific traits (`InterpolatedQuantiles`, `TailWeights`) express supported quantile definitions and tail means ([Mathematics of empirical distributions, sample quantiles, and interpolation conventions](../empirical-quantiles/), [Mathematics of weighted empirical distributions and tail means](../tail-means-and-weighted-empirical/)).

`WeightSemantics` is an enum reporting which model an accumulator or interval used.
Type arguments select weight semantics; no API selects them by accepting `WeightSemantics`.

Masses also serve as probability masses for entropy.
`entropy_with_options` selects mass treatment through `MassNormalization` in `EntropyOptions`.
`Normalize` accepts nonnegative masses with a positive sum; `RequireUnit(t)` requires $|\sum_i p_i - 1| \le t$.
Both normalize masses by their sum before use ([Circular statistics, information, and distribution distances](../circular-information-and-distances/)).

## Weighted accumulators

`OnlineWeightedMoments<S, M>` incrementally accumulates weighted means and central moments for model `M` (a type implementing `MomentWeights`).
`observe(x, w)` adds one observation, and `merge(&other)` combines accumulators for disjoint partitions.
Updates and merges generalize the unweighted Pébay et al. merge formulas to weights, costing $O(1)$ operations each.
`from_slices(values, Weights::<_, M>::new(&weights), order)` constructs an accumulator from complete slices, using two compensated passes when values and weights are within the ordinary f64 range.

Accumulators with different models have different types and cannot be merged at compile time.
Merging accumulators that retain different orders produces `InvalidInput`, even if one is empty.
`observe` returns `NonFinite` for nonfinite values or weights, `InvalidInput` for negative weights, and `ScalarConversion` for frequency weights that are not integers representable by `usize`.
It returns `NumericalInstability` if cancellation corrupts weight state or even central moments.
Every failure leaves state unchanged.

The `WeightedMomentsSnapshot` returned by `snapshot()` rounds the following quantities on each query.

- `observation_count()` returns positive-weight record count $n$; `frequency_count()` returns replicated frequency count $N$ (`None` for other models).
- `total_weight()` returns $W$, `effective_sample_size()` effective sample size, `degrees_of_freedom()` degrees of freedom, and `variance_divisor()` the variance divisor.
- `mean()`, `weighted_population_variance()` ($S_w/W$), `sample_variance()`, `variance_of_weighted_mean()`, and `standard_error_of_weighted_mean()` return the estimators in the table above.
  For analytic weights, `sample_variance` is always `Ok(None)`; read $\hat\tau^2$ through `noise_scale()` instead.
- `central_moment(k)`, `skewness`, and `kurtosis` return weighted central moments $m_k = \sum w_i (x_i - \bar x_w)^k / W$ and their ratios.

Corrected skewness and kurtosis (`BiasCorrection::Corrected`) are defined only for frequency weights interpretable as expanded independent observations; other weights return `NotImplemented`.
The k-statistics `k_statistic` and `k_statistic_variance` exist only on frequency-weight snapshots, while `noise_scale` exists only on analytic-weight snapshots.
Calls on other models fail to compile.

Bivariate `OnlineWeightedCovariance` and `WeightedCovarianceSnapshot` follow the same conventions.
For analytic weights, sample covariance is undefined (`validity()` returns `PopulationOnly`); the common noise covariance `noise_covariance` is returned instead.
The covariance between weighted means `covariance_of_weighted_means` multiplies the codeviation by the same factor as `variance_of_weighted_mean`.
Weighted correlation `correlation` is independent of the variance divisor.

For weighted reduction of each matrix-view batch, attach weights with `BatchView::with_weights` and pass the view to `reduce_weighted_moments` or `reduce_weighted_covariance` ([Batch matrix-view reductions](oxref:stats-online-matrix-view-reduction)).
Weighted accumulators always center on the estimated mean.
For moments $\sum_i (x_i - c)^k / n$ around a known center $c$, pass `Some(c)` as `center` to the unweighted `reduce_moment`.

## Effective sample size and degrees of freedom

Comparing the variance $s^2 Q/W^2$ of the reliability-weighted mean with the variance $s^2/n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of a mean of $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ independent observations gives the following quantity.

$$
n_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{W^2}{Q} = \frac{\bigl(\sum w_i\bigr)^2}{\sum w_i^2}
$$

This is Kish's **effective sample size** (Kish, 1965).
Cauchy–Schwarz gives $1 \le n_{\mathrm{eff}} \le n$; uniform weights give $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = n$, while concentration on one record makes it approach 1.
The variance divisor $W - Q/W = W(1 - 1/n_{\mathrm{eff}})$ can also be expressed through this quantity.

`effective_sample_size` returns replicated count $N$ for frequency weights and $W^2/Q$ for other weights.
`degrees_of_freedom` returns $N - 1$ for frequency, $n_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1$ for reliability, and $n - 1$ for analytic weights.
Inference with analytic weights uses record count rather than $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
The t distribution with reliability-weight degrees of freedom $n_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1$ is approximate, unlike the exact t distribution for independent normal samples.

## Constructing confidence intervals for means

For estimate $\hat\theta$ and standard error $\mathrm{SE}$, construct a two-sided confidence-$1 - \alpha$ interval as $\hat\theta \pm c\,\mathrm{SE}$.
Select the distribution for critical value $c$ with `MeanIntervalMethod`.

- `StudentT` (default) applies to estimated variance from independent normal samples.
  $(\bar X - \mu)/(S/\sqrt n)$ follows a t distribution with $\nu = n-1$ degrees of freedom, giving $c = t_{\nu,\,1-\alpha/2}$.
- `NormalKnownVariance` uses normal critical value $z_{1-\alpha/2}$ for a known standard error.
- `NormalAsymptotic` is a large-sample approximation applying the central limit theorem with an estimated standard error, using the same $z_{1-\alpha/2}$.
  It makes no finite-sample coverage claim.

Create confidence levels with `ConfidenceProbability::new(p)`; values outside $p \in (0, 1)$ return `InvalidInput` and nonfinite values return `NonFinite`.
The default is 0.95.
`normal_critical_value` computes normal critical value $z_{1-\alpha/2} = \sqrt 2\,\mathrm{erf}^{-1}(p)$ using $\mathrm{erf}^{-1}(p)$ when $p \le 1/2$ and $\mathrm{erfc}^{-1}(1 - p)$ otherwise.
$1 - p$ is exact in f64 for $p \ge 1/2$, preserving information about $\alpha$ even for confidence levels near 1.
For $\nu < 4096$, the t critical value `student_t_critical_value(ν)` is found by inverting the regularized incomplete beta function in $P(|T| \le t) = I_x(1/2, \nu/2)$ with $x = t^2/(\nu + t^2)$.
It inverts a central or complementary form without constructing $(1 + p)/2$, again preserving information about $\alpha$.
For $\nu \ge 4096$, it expands around the normal critical value in powers of $1/\nu$ through the $\nu^{-4}$ term (Abramowitz and Stegun 26.7.5).
Its relative truncation error is below $10^{-17}$.
The `scioxide_special::BetaOptions` passed to `student_t_critical_value_with_options` sets inverse-function iteration limits and tolerances.

`MomentsSnapshot::confidence_interval_of_mean()` and `WeightedMomentsSnapshot::confidence_interval_of_weighted_mean()` return two-sided 95% Student t intervals.
Select confidence level and method through `confidence_interval_of_mean_with_options` and `confidence_interval_of_weighted_mean_with_options`.
Since variance is estimated, passing `NormalKnownVariance` produces `InvalidInput`.
The resulting `MeanConfidenceInterval` contains endpoints `bounds`, estimate `estimate`, standard error `standard_error`, degrees of freedom `degrees_of_freedom`, method `method`, and confidence level `confidence`.
`assumptions()` returns the assumptions for nominal coverage as `MeanIntervalAssumptions`.
Unweighted t intervals record `IndependentNormal`, frequency weights `FrequencyNormal`, analytic weights `AnalyticNormal`, and reliability weights the approximation `EffectiveSampleApproximation`; normal-approximation methods record `AsymptoticNormal`.
`weight_semantics()` returns a model only for intervals constructed from weighted snapshots.
These assumptions are recorded, not tested against the data.
An undefined standard error (such as $n < 2$) returns `Ok(None)`.
For an unweighted slice, the one-shot `mean_confidence_interval(&x)` returns the same two-sided 95% t interval and rejects fewer than two points with `InvalidInput`.

If estimate and standard error are already available separately, compute the critical value once with `MeanIntervalPlan::new(confidence, method, degrees, options)`.
Pass `degrees = Some(ν)` (finite $\nu > 0$) for `StudentT` and `None` for normal methods; mismatches produce `InvalidInput`.
Then `interval(estimate, standard_error)` constructs an interval for any scalar type in $O(1)$ without recomputing the critical value.
A nonfinite estimate returns `NonFinite`, and a negative standard error returns `InvalidInput`.

Endpoints are rounded to the result scalar, not outward-rounded enclosures.
`is_degenerate()` is true when the two rounded endpoints coincide.
Confidence is the target proportion of intervals covering the true value under repeated application of the procedure, not the probability that one interval contains it.

### Extended-range standard errors

The variance of the mean $s^2/n$ scales as the square of the data scale.
Beyond data scales $2^{\pm 512}$, the variance overflows f64 or rounds to zero even when standard error $s/\sqrt n$ is representable.
`variance_of_sample_mean()` rounds variance to f64, returning `Err` (overflow) or zero in this situation.
`standard_error_of_sample_mean()` takes a square root before rounding, returning the correct value whenever the standard error is representable.

`MomentsSnapshot::scaled_variance_of_sample_mean()` returns mean variance as `scioxide_core::Scaled<S>` with a separate binary exponent.
Only the significand is rounded to `S` (error at most half an ulp); the exponent is not rounded, preventing variance overflow or rounding to zero.
It costs $O(1)$ with no allocation.
The returned value's `sqrt()` gives an extended-range standard error; `add` can also sum several estimators' variances before taking the square root.

`MeanIntervalPlan::interval_scaled(estimate, standard_error)` multiplies a `Scaled` standard error by the critical value without rounding it to `S`, rounding endpoints to `S` only once.
`interval` first rounds the standard error to `S`; if subnormal, the product of that rounded standard error and critical value is rounded again.
For standard errors smaller than the smallest `S` subnormal, `interval` constructs a zero-width interval from the zero-rounded standard error, while `interval_scaled` may return nonzero endpoints.
The interval's `standard_error()` returns the standard error rounded to `S`, but endpoint computation uses the unrounded value.

The next cell compares both paths using four deviations $[-1.5, -0.5, 0.5, 1.5]$ scaled by $2^k$ (scaling by $2^k$ is exact in f64).
At $k = 0$, the variance of the mean is $5/12$, standard error $\sqrt{5/12}$, and the 95% t-interval half-width (three degrees of freedom) $t_{3,\,0.975}\sqrt{5/12}$.
The table divides quantities by the corresponding power of $2^k$ and reports relative errors against these reference values.

```rust
//| id: stats-scaled-mean-interval
//| ref: stats-scaled-mean-interval
//| title: "Change data scale to compare mean-variance and interval computation paths"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-stats, scioxide-core]
//| inputs:
//|   scale_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Data-scale exponent k (scale 2^k)","description":"Change the power of two multiplying four deviations.","min":-1073,"max":1020,"step":1,"value":-540}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"unit","label":"k = 0","description":"Every path agrees with reference values.","inputs":{"scale_exponent":0}}
//|   - {"id":"overflow","label":"k = 600","description":"Mean variance overflows f64, but standard errors and intervals remain representable.","inputs":{"scale_exponent":600}}
//|   - {"id":"subnormal","label":"k = −1073","description":"Standard error becomes subnormal, and the interval paths give different endpoints.","inputs":{"scale_exponent":-1073}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Rounded to zero"}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/1/5","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/2/5","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/4/5","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: unit
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.4166666666666667,"rtol":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":2.054260256760521,"rtol":1e-14}}
//|   - case: overflow
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":"Err (overflow)"}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":null}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/4/5","range":{"max":1e-15}}
//|   - case: subnormal
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/3/3","approx":{"value":1.5,"atol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"routes","path":"/rows/4/3","approx":{"value":2.0,"atol":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::Scaled;
use scioxide_stats::{
    ConfidenceProbability, MeanIntervalMethod, MeanIntervalPlan, MomentOrder, OnlineMoments,
};

let k = scale_exponent as i64;
let values = [-1.5_f64, -0.5, 0.5, 1.5]
    .iter()
    .map(|&d| Scaled::from_parts(d, k).and_then(|s| s.round()))
    .collect::<Result<Vec<f64>, _>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let snapshot = OnlineMoments::from_slice(&values, MomentOrder::Second)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .snapshot();
let plan = MeanIntervalPlan::new(
    ConfidenceProbability::default(),
    MeanIntervalMethod::StudentT,
    Some(3.0),
    Default::default(),
)
.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// References (k = 0): variance 5/12, standard error √(5/12), half-width t·√(5/12). Square roots are f64 reference values.
let reference_se = (5.0_f64 / 12.0).sqrt();
let reference_half_width = plan
    .interval(0.0_f64, reference_se)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let reference_half_width = reference_half_width.bounds().upper();
// Divide v by 2^(power·k); return None for Err.
let normalize = |v: Option<f64>, power: i64| -> Result<Option<f64>, String> {
    v.map(|v| {
        Scaled::from_value(v)
            .and_then(|s| s.shifted(-power * k))
            .and_then(|s| s.round())
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
    })
    .transpose()
};
let rounded_variance = snapshot.variance_of_sample_mean().ok().flatten();
let scaled_variance = snapshot
    .scaled_variance_of_sample_mean()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .ok_or("Mean variance is undefined")?;
let rounded_se = snapshot
    .standard_error_of_sample_mean()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let rounded_interval = snapshot
    .confidence_interval_of_mean()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .ok_or("Interval is undefined")?;
let scaled_se = scaled_variance.sqrt().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let scaled_interval = plan
    .interval_scaled(0.0_f64, scaled_se)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let scaled_variance_normalized = scaled_variance
    .shifted(-2 * k)
    .and_then(|s| s.round())
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let entries = [
    (
        "variance_of_sample_mean",
        rounded_variance,
        normalize(rounded_variance, 2)?,
        5.0 / 12.0,
    ),
    (
        "scaled_variance_of_sample_mean",
        None,
        Some(scaled_variance_normalized),
        5.0 / 12.0,
    ),
    (
        "standard_error_of_sample_mean",
        rounded_se,
        normalize(rounded_se, 1)?,
        reference_se,
    ),
    (
        "confidence_interval_of_mean upper endpoint",
        Some(rounded_interval.bounds().upper()),
        normalize(Some(rounded_interval.bounds().upper()), 1)?,
        reference_half_width,
    ),
    (
        "interval_scaled upper endpoint",
        Some(scaled_interval.bounds().upper()),
        normalize(Some(scaled_interval.bounds().upper()), 1)?,
        reference_half_width,
    ),
];
// State of values returned in f64: Err (overflow), rounded to zero, subnormal, or retained as Scaled.
let state = |raw: Option<f64>, scaled: bool| match raw {
    _ if scaled => "Retained as Scaled",
    None => "Err (overflow)",
    Some(v) if v == 0.0 => "Rounded to zero",
    Some(v) if v.abs() < f64::MIN_POSITIVE => "Subnormal",
    Some(_) => "Normal",
};
let rows: Vec<_> = entries
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(i, &(name, raw, normalized, reference))| {
        let error = normalized.map(|v| (v - reference).abs() / reference);
        (name, state(raw, i == 1), raw, normalized, reference, error)
    })
    .collect();
let sci4 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 4 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Path", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "f64 result", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Value returned in f64 [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Value divided by a power of 2^k [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "4", "label": "Reference (k = 0) [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Relative error [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci2, "nullText": "—" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "routes" })
);
```

At default $k = -540$, the variance of the mean $\tfrac{5}{12} \cdot 2^{-1080}$ is smaller than the smallest f64 subnormal $2^{-1074}$, so `variance_of_sample_mean` returns zero and its relative error is 1.
`scaled_variance_of_sample_mean` retains the same quantity exactly, and the standard error and both interval upper endpoints agree with their reference values.
At $k = 600$, the variance of the mean $\tfrac{5}{12} \cdot 2^{1200}$ overflows f64 and produces `Err`; other paths agree with their references.

At $k = -1073$, standard error $\sqrt{5/12} \cdot 2^{-1073} \approx 1.29 \cdot 2^{-1074}$ shrinks near the smallest subnormal unit $2^{-1074}$.
`standard_error_of_sample_mean` rounds it to $2^{-1074}$, giving 23% relative error.
The upper endpoint of `confidence_interval_of_mean` from the rounded standard error is $3 \cdot 2^{-1074}$ (1.5 after division by $2^{-1073}$), differing from reference 2.054 by 27%.
`interval_scaled` multiplies the unrounded standard error by the critical value and rounds once, giving upper endpoint $4 \cdot 2^{-1074}$ (2.0 after division), nearest to true value $4.11 \cdot 2^{-1074}$; the discrepancy is only 2.6%.
This remaining discrepancy comes from rounding to the nearest representable f64 value and cannot be reduced.

## Confidence level and interval width

Attach weights $[1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 1]$ to six pendulum measurements of gravitational acceleration $[9.78, 9.81, 9.80, 9.83, 9.79, 9.82]$ m/s² and summarize the same values and weights under three meanings.
The weights can represent counts, representativeness, or relative precision.
The next definitions block contains the measurements, weights, and a helper constructing summaries for model `M`.
Two cells on this page use it.

```rust
//| id: stats-pendulum-weights
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Pendulum measurements, weights, and model-specific summaries"
//| crates: [scioxide-stats]
use scioxide_stats::{
    MomentOrder, MomentWeights, OnlineWeightedMoments, WeightedMomentsSnapshot, Weights,
};

/// Pendulum measurements of gravitational acceleration [m/s²].
pub(crate) fn pendulum_values() -> [f64; 6] {
    [9.78, 9.81, 9.80, 9.83, 9.79, 9.82]
}

/// Measurement weights (interpreted as counts, representativeness, or relative precision).
pub(crate) fn pendulum_weights() -> [f64; 6] {
    [1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 1.0, 2.0, 1.0]
}

/// Snapshot summarizing values and weights under weight model M.
pub(crate) fn weighted_snapshot<M: MomentWeights>(
    values: &[f64],
    weights: &[f64],
) -> Result<WeightedMomentsSnapshot<f64, M>, String> {
    let moments = OnlineWeightedMoments::<f64, M>::from_slices(
        values,
        Weights::<_, M>::new(weights),
        MomentOrder::Second,
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    Ok(moments.snapshot())
}
```

## Weight meanings and coverage

Confusing weight meanings makes the expected estimator of mean variance differ from its true variance.
For each true model, first derive ratios of the expectations of all three estimators to true variance.

Under a true reliability model (independent $x_i$ with common variance $\sigma^2$ and weights unrelated to variance), $\mathrm{Var}(\bar x_w) = \sigma^2 Q/W^2$ and $E[S_w] = \sigma^2(W - Q/W)$.
For analytic-weight estimator $S_w/((n-1)W)$, the expected-to-true variance ratio is $(n_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1)/(n - 1) \le 1$; more uneven weights cause greater underestimation.
For frequency-weight estimator $S_w/((W-1)W)$, the ratio is $(n_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1)/(W - 1)$; a larger total weight $W$ causes greater underestimation.

Under a true analytic model ($\mathrm{Var}(x_i) = \tau^2/w_i$), $\mathrm{Var}(\bar x_w) = \tau^2/W$ and $E[S_w] = (n-1)\tau^2$.
Reliability-weight estimator $S_w Q/(W^2 (W - Q/W))$ has ratio $(n-1)/(n_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1) \ge 1$, giving intervals that are too wide (conservative).
The frequency-weight estimator has ratio $(n-1)/(W-1)$, again depending on weight scale.

Variance-estimator bias appears as departures in interval coverage.
The next cell randomly regenerates pendulum measurements $R = 4000$ times from the selected true model and counts the proportion of 95% t intervals for each weight meaning containing true mean $\mu = 9.80$ m/s².
Weights are $c$ times $[1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 1]$, as in the preceding cell; standard deviation for a weight-1 measurement is $\sigma = 0.02$ m/s².
The Monte Carlo standard error of coverage is $\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/R}$.
The table's “Variance ratio” column computes the expectation ratios derived above from the weights, predicting the direction of coverage departures.

//| run: reactive

use scioxide_stats::{
    Analytic, ConfidenceProbability, Frequency, MeanIntervalMethod, MeanIntervalPlan,
    MomentWeights, Reliability,

const REPLICATES: usize = 4000;
let (mu, sigma) = (9.80_f64, 0.02_f64);
let weights = pendulum_weights().map(|w| w * weight_scale);
let analytic_truth = true_model.starts_with("Analytic");
let (n, w, q) = (
    weights.len() as f64,
    weights.iter().sum::<f64>(),
    weights.iter().map(|w| w * w).sum::<f64>(),
);

let ratios = if analytic_truth {
    [(n - 1.0) / (w - 1.0), (n - 1.0) / (n_eff - 1.0), 1.0]
} else {
    [(n_eff - 1.0) / (w - 1.0), 1.0, (n_eff - 1.0) / (n - 1.0)]
};
// Degrees of freedom depend only on weights, so compute each model's critical value once.
fn plan_for<M: MomentWeights>(weights: &[f64]) -> Result<MeanIntervalPlan, String> {
    let snapshot = weighted_snapshot::<M>(&pendulum_values(), weights)?;
    let degrees = snapshot
        .degrees_of_freedom()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .ok_or("Degrees of freedom are undefined")?;
    MeanIntervalPlan::new(
        ConfidenceProbability::default(),
        MeanIntervalMethod::StudentT,
        Some(degrees),
        Default::default(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())
}
fn covers<M: MomentWeights>(
    values: &[f64],
    weights: &[f64],
    plan: MeanIntervalPlan,
    mu: f64,
) -> Result<bool, String> {
    let snapshot = weighted_snapshot::<M>(values, weights)?;
    let mean = snapshot
        .mean()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .ok_or("Undefined mean")?;
    let se = snapshot
        .standard_error_of_weighted_mean()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
        .ok_or("Undefined standard error")?;
    let bounds = plan.interval(mean, se).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?.bounds();
    Ok(bounds.lower() <= mu && mu <= bounds.upper())
}
let plans = [
    plan_for::<Frequency>(&weights)?,
    plan_for::<Reliability>(&weights)?,
    plan_for::<Analytic>(&weights)?,
];
let standard = Normal::new(0.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(sampling_seed as u64);
let mut hits = [0_usize; 3];
let mut values = [0.0_f64; 6];
for _ in 0..REPLICATES {
    for (x, &wi) in values.iter_mut().zip(&weights) {
        let z = standard.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        // Analytic: standard deviation σ/√(w/c) (σ for weight-1 measurements). Reliability: common σ. Square roots are f64 reference values.
        let sd = if analytic_truth {
            sigma / (wi / weight_scale).sqrt()
        } else {
            sigma
        };
        *x = mu + sd * z;
    }
    hits[0] += usize::from(covers::<Frequency>(&values, &weights, plans[0], mu)?);
    hits[1] += usize::from(covers::<Reliability>(&values, &weights, plans[1], mu)?);
    hits[2] += usize::from(covers::<Analytic>(&values, &weights, plans[2], mu)?);
}
let names = ["Frequency", "Reliability", "Analytic"];
let rows: Vec<_> = (0..3)
    .map(|j| {
        let p = hits[j] as f64 / REPLICATES as f64;
        (
            names[j],
            ratios[j],
            p,
            (p * (1.0 - p) / REPLICATES as f64).sqrt(),
        )
    })
    .collect();
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Weight meaning", "type": "string" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Variance ratio (expectation) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Coverage [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Standard error of coverage [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "coverage" })
);

For the default true model (analytic) and seed 7, analytic-weight interval coverage is 0.954 (standard error 0.003), within two standard errors of nominal 0.95.
Reliability-weight coverage is 0.974, too wide and conservative as predicted by variance ratio 1.25.
Frequency-weight coverage is 0.849, too narrow as predicted by variance ratio 0.56.

With a true reliability model, reliability-weight coverage is 0.967 and analytic-weight coverage 0.939.
The analytic-weight variance ratio is 0.8; its intervals are narrower than the nominal level requires.
Reliability-weight coverage exceeds 0.95 by about six standard errors, showing that the t-distribution approximation (`EffectiveSampleApproximation`) with $n_{\mathrm{eff}} - 1 = 4$ degrees of freedom is conservative for these weights.
Frequency-weight coverage is 0.810; multiplier $c = 4$ reduces the variance ratio to at most 0.13 for either true model, pushing coverage below 0.5.
Use frequency-weight intervals only when weights truly count repeated observations of the same value.

## Summary

The weighted mean does not depend on weight meanings, but standard errors and degrees of freedom do.
Frequency weights reproduce expanded-sample estimators, and their standard errors change with weight scaling.
Reliability and analytic weights are scale invariant, using Kish effective sample size and record count respectively as the basis for degrees of freedom.
Select meanings through types `Frequency`, `Reliability`, and `Analytic`; interpret summary quantities `effective_sample_size`, `degrees_of_freedom`, and `noise_scale` alongside the interval's recorded `MeanIntervalAssumptions`.
Confusing weight meanings shifts coverage from the nominal level, as do violations of independence or normality.
Near the limits of the f64 data range, use `scaled_variance_of_sample_mean` and `interval_scaled` to carry mean variance through to intervals without rounding.
