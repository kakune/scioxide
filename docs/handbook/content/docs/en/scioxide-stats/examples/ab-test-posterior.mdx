An A/B test randomly assigns visitors to two webpage variants A and B and compares their proportions of purchases or registrations: their success rates.
A frequentist test answers the probability of a difference at least as extreme as observed if there were no difference: the p-value.
Decision-making often calls for the probability that B's success rate exceeds A's and the expected loss from adopting B. Bayesian inference places priors on success rates and computes these quantities from posteriors updated by observations.
This page uses Beta distributions, conjugate priors for success rates, to compute posteriors and decision quantities and cross-check their values in three ways.
Finally, repeated experiments measure how much repeated checking while collecting data increases the fraction of tests declaring B the winner despite no difference.

## Beta–binomial model

Let the success rates of A and B be $p_A$ and $p_B$, with $k_A$ successes among $n_A$ visitors assigned to A and $k_B$ among $n_B$ assigned to B. If visitors succeed independently with constant probability, $k_A\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n_A,p_A)$ and $k_B\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n_B,p_B)$.
With prior $p\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_0,\beta_0)$ (density $\propto p^{\alpha_0-1}(1-p)^{\beta_0-1}$), Bayes' theorem gives the posterior distribution:

$$
p\mid k,n\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_0+k,\ \beta_0+n-k)
$$


The Beta distribution is conjugate to the binomial because prior and posterior belong to the same family.
$\alpha_0$ and $\beta_0$ can be read as anticipated success and failure counts before observations: pseudo-observations.
The posterior mean $(\alpha_0+k)/(\alpha_0+\beta_0+n)$ averages the sample success proportion $k/n$ and prior mean $\alpha_0/(\alpha_0+\beta_0)$ with weights proportional to observation and pseudo-observation counts.
Posterior variance is $\alpha\beta/\bigl((\alpha+\beta)^2(\alpha+\beta+1)\bigr)$ ($\alpha$ and $\beta$ are posterior parameters), decreasing approximately inversely with $n$.

Common priors are uniform $\mathrm{Beta}(1,1)$, Jeffreys' prior $\mathrm{Beta}(1/2,1/2)$, and informative priors reflecting historical results.
$\mathrm{Beta}(0,0)$ (Haldane's prior) is improper and cannot be normalized; its posterior becomes proper after observing at least one success and one failure.
Once $n$ exceeds a few hundred, differences between priors with $\alpha_0+\beta_0$ of only a few barely affect the posterior.
If an informative prior's pseudo-observation count is comparable to $n$, the posterior is strongly pulled toward the prior mean.

## Decision quantities

The posteriors of $p_A$ and $p_B$ are independent, giving the following posterior probability that B exceeds A:

$$
P(p_B>p_A)=\int_0^1 f_B(x)\,F_A(x)\,dx
$$

Here $f_B$ is B's posterior density and $F_A$ is A's posterior distribution function.
For integer $\alpha_B$, Cook (2005) gives the closed form:

$$
P(p_B>p_A)=\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha_B-1}\frac{B(\alpha_A+i,\ \beta_A+\beta_B)}{(\beta_B+i)\,B(1+i,\ \beta_B)\,B(\alpha_A,\ \beta_A)}
$$

Here $B$ is the beta function.
The ratio of adjacent terms is $(\alpha_A+i)(\beta_B+i)/\bigl((\alpha_A+\beta_A+\beta_B+i)(1+i)\bigr)$, so computing the initial term $B(\alpha_A,\beta_A+\beta_B)/B(\alpha_A,\beta_A)$ with `scioxide_special::ln_beta` lets the remaining $\alpha_B-1$ terms be summed using multiplication alone.

The expected loss from adopting B, meaning the expected part of the success-rate difference where B is worse, is:

$$
L_B=E\bigl[\max(p_A-p_B,0)\bigr],\qquad L_A=E\bigl[\max(p_B-p_A,0)\bigr]
$$


The rule “adopt B if $L_B$ is below tolerable loss $\varepsilon$” considers the size of losses as well as the probability of winning.
Even with the same $P(p_B>p_A)$, longer tails in the difference distribution increase $L_B$.

## scioxide-stats API

`BetaBinomialPrior::new(alpha, beta)` constructs a proper prior, rejecting nonpositive parameters with `InvalidInput` and NaN/infinity with `NonFinite`.
`BetaBinomialPrior::improper(alpha, beta)` constructs an improper prior with a zero parameter, such as Haldane's prior.
`update(successes, trials)` returns $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha+k,\beta+n-k)$, or `InvalidInput` if $k>n$.
`update_frequency` updates for $f$ repetitions of the same observation; `update_batch` updates from an observation sequence; all use $O(1)$ addition.
Parameters are retained with extended exponents and rounded to `f64` when read through `parameters()`.
`posterior_law()` returns scioxide-random's `Beta`, directly providing densities, distribution functions, quantiles, means, variances, and sampling.
Calling it while the posterior remains improper returns `InvalidInput`.
`predictive(m)` returns the posterior predictive distribution `BetaBinomial` of successes among $m$ future visitors.

`credible_interval(law, probability, options)` returns an equal-tailed credible interval leaving posterior probability $(1-c)/2$ in each tail.
It solves $\ln F(x)=\ln((1-c)/2)$ at the lower endpoint and $\ln S(x)=\ln((1-c)/2)$ at the upper endpoint ($S$ is the survival function).
Solving the upper tail with the survival function rather than a $1-(1-c)/2$ quantile avoids cancellation even for small tail probabilities.
A credible interval contains the true value with posterior probability $c$, distinct from a frequentist confidence interval whose procedure has repeated-sampling coverage $c$.

`posterior_summary(chains)` takes a sample matrix with chains in rows and returns mean, variance, 5%/50%/95% quantiles, split $\hat R$, effective sample size, and Monte Carlo standard error `mean_mcse` of the mean.
Although intended for MCMC output diagnostics, it also supports independent draws, whose effective sample size is close to their count.

## Posteriors and cross-checks

The following cell constructs two posteriors from visitor counts and observed success proportions (success counts are rounded products of these inputs).
The first table shows posterior parameters, posterior means, and 95% equal-tailed credible intervals.
The second table compares three computations of $P(p_B>p_A)$; the third shows expected losses and a posterior summary of difference $p_B-p_A$.
Monte Carlo draws 10,000 posterior pairs, divides them into four chains, and summarizes difference $p_B-p_A$ with `posterior_summary`.
Numerical integration independently checks $\int f_BF_A$ by a hand implementation of Simpson's rule (400 intervals) over an interval containing $1-2\times10^{-10}$ of the mass of $f_B$; the change after halving interval count is an error indicator.
Cook's closed form is computed only for integer $\alpha_B$; otherwise its entry is blank.
The fourth table compares frequentist Pearson $\chi^2$ (without continuity correction), Fisher's exact test, and a Wald interval for the difference (90%) with the posterior difference's 90% interval (5%/95% quantiles).
Plots show the two posterior densities and a histogram of posterior difference draws $p_B-p_A$.

//| id: stats-ab-test-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "P(p_B > p_A) from Cook's closed form"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-special]
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, RealScalar};

/// P(p_B > p_A) for integer α_B (Cook 2005). Sum successive terms using their ratio. None for noninteger α_B.
pub(crate) fn exact_superiority(
    alpha_a: f64,
    beta_a: f64,
    alpha_b: f64,
    beta_b: f64,
) -> Result<Option<f64>, SciOxideError> {
    if alpha_b.fract() != 0.0 {
        return Ok(None);
    }
    let first = scioxide_special::ln_beta(alpha_a, beta_a + beta_b)?
        - scioxide_special::ln_beta(alpha_a, beta_a)?;
    let mut term = RealScalar::exp(first);
    let mut sum = term;
    for i in 0..(alpha_b as usize - 1) {
        let i = i as f64;
        term *= (alpha_a + i) * (beta_b + i) / ((alpha_a + beta_a + beta_b + i) * (1.0 + i));
        sum += term;
    }
    Ok(Some(sum))
}
```

Meanwhile, $1-P(p_B>p_A)=0.0498$ is close to the one-sided p-value (half the two-sided $X^2$ p-value, 0.0496).

## Repeated interim decisions

In A/B testing, it is tempting to inspect results as data arrive and stop once a difference becomes clear.
Consider computing $P(p_B>p_A)$ daily and declaring B the winner when it exceeds threshold $c$ (for example, 0.95).
At every time, the posterior probability is valid given the observations, but the frequency of declaring B the winner when repeatedly applying this rule under no difference (frequentist type I error) is a separate question.
For a single decision with a uniform prior, this fraction is close to one-sided test level $1-c$.
More decisions create more opportunities for random fluctuations to cross the threshold at least once, increasing the fraction beyond $1-c$.

The following cell repeats $R$ experiments under $p_A=p_B=p$, adding $m$ visitors per variant at each decision and checking up to $K$ times.
Success counts are drawn from scioxide-random's binomial distribution, and $P(p_B>p_A)$ is computed by Cook's closed form from uniform-prior posteriors.
The plot compares the fraction crossing the threshold at least once by decision $k$ (cumulative false-decision rate), with Wilson 95% intervals, against the single-decision reference $1-c$ (dashed).
The table compares false-decision rates for checking only at the end and checking every time.

//| id: stats-ab-test-peeking
//| ref: stats-ab-test-peeking
//| title: "Count false decisions under repeated checking in an A/B test with no difference"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [stats-ab-test-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-random, scioxide-stats]
//| inputs:
//|   rate: {"type":"range","label":"Common success rate p [-]","min":0.05,"max":0.3,"step":0.05,"value":0.1}
//|   batch: {"type":"range","label":"Visitors added per variant per decision m","min":50,"max":500,"step":50,"value":200}
//|   looks: {"type":"range","label":"Decision count K","min":1,"max":30,"step":1,"value":20}
//|   threshold: {"type":"range","label":"Decision threshold c [-]","min":0.9,"max":0.99,"step":0.01,"value":0.95}
//|   replicates: {"type":"range","label":"Replicate count R","min":200,"max":2000,"step":200,"value":1000}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"single-look","label":"K = 1","description":"The false-decision rate returns near 1 − c.","inputs":{"looks":1}}
//|   - {"id":"strict","label":"c = 0.99","description":"Even with a higher threshold, repeated checking exceeds 1 − c.","inputs":{"threshold":0.99}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: single-look
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peeking","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.042,"atol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"peeking","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.046,"atol":1e-9}}
//|       - {"artifact":"peeking","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":0.213,"atol":1e-9}}
use scioxide_random::{Binomial, CountDistribution, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};
use scioxide_stats::BetaBinomialPrior;

let m = batch as usize;
let k_max = looks as usize;
let r = replicates as usize;
let sampler = Binomial::new(m, rate)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .prepare_sampler()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let start = BetaBinomialPrior::new(1.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// first[k] = count first crossing at decision k + 1; last = count crossing at the final decision alone
let mut first = vec![0_usize; k_max];
let mut last = 0_usize;
for _ in 0..r {
    let (mut post_a, mut post_b) = (start, start);
    let mut stopped = false;
    for k in 0..k_max {
        post_a = post_a
            .update(sampler.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?, m)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        post_b = post_b
            .update(sampler.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?, m)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let (alpha_a, beta_a) = post_a.parameters().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let (alpha_b, beta_b) = post_b.parameters().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let superiority = exact_superiority(alpha_a, beta_a, alpha_b, beta_b)
            .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
            .ok_or("Noninteger α_B")?;
        let exceeded = superiority > threshold;
        if exceeded && !stopped {
            first[k] += 1;
            stopped = true;
        }
        if k + 1 == k_max {
            last += usize::from(exceeded);
        }
    }
}
// Wilson score interval (95%)
let wilson = |count: usize| {
    let (p, n, z) = (count as f64 / r as f64, r as f64, 1.96_f64);
    let center = (p + z * z / (2.0 * n)) / (1.0 + z * z / n);
    let radius = z * (p * (1.0 - p) / n + z * z / (4.0 * n * n)).sqrt() / (1.0 + z * z / n);
    (p, center - radius, center + radius)
};
let mut cumulative = 0;
let mut points = Vec::with_capacity(k_max);
for (k, &count) in first.iter().enumerate() {
    cumulative += count;
    let (p, low, high) = wilson(cumulative);
    points.push([(k + 1) as f64, p, low, high]);
}
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "errorbar",
    "xLabel": "Decision count k [count]",
    "yLabel": "Fraction declaring B the winner despite no difference [-]",
    "xMin": 0.5,
    "xMax": looks + 0.5,
    "yMin": 0.0,
    "tooltip": true,
    "series": [{ "name": "Cumulative false-decision rate and Wilson interval", "points": points }],
    "annotations": [{
        "kind": "line",
        "axis": "y",
        "value": 1.0 - threshold,
        "label": "Single-decision reference 1 − c",
        "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
    }]
}));
let (single, single_low, single_high) = wilson(last);
let (any, any_low, any_high) = wilson(cumulative);
let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
emit_table_with_columns!(
    serde_json::json!([
        { "key": "0", "label": "Decision rule" },
        { "key": "1", "label": "False-decision rate [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
        { "key": "2", "label": "Wilson interval lower endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
        { "key": "3", "label": "Wilson interval upper endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 }
    ]),
    vec![
        ("Check only at the end", single, single_low, single_high),
        ("Check every time and stop at first crossing", any, any_low, any_high),
    ],
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "peeking" })
);
```

For default inputs ($p=0.1$, $m=200$, $K=20$, $c=0.95$, $R=1000$, seed 3), checking only at the end gives a false-decision rate of 0.046 (Wilson interval 0.035–0.061), whose interval contains 0.05.
Checking every time increases the cumulative false-decision rate from 0.060 at the first check to 0.213 (0.189–0.239) at the twentieth, about four times the single-check rate.
At $K=1$, both rules coincide and the false-decision rate returns to 0.042 (0.031–0.056), near $1-c$.
At $c=0.99$, the final-only rate is 0.012, whereas 20 checks give 0.058, about six times 0.01.

Whenever computed, posterior probability $P(p_B>p_A)$ is valid as the posterior probability given observations.
However, the frequentist false-decision rate of the rule “stop when the threshold is crossed” increases with check count.
For interim stopping, measures include stricter thresholds, a predetermined check count, and repeated experiments assessing the expected-loss rule and prior choice.
scioxide-stats provides neither frequentist sequential tests (such as O'Brien–Fleming boundaries) nor tests permitting arbitrary stopping times.

## Summary

For a Beta prior and binomial observations, the posterior has closed form $\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_0+k,\beta_0+n-k)$ and can be used as scioxide-random's `Beta` through `BetaBinomialPrior::update` and `posterior_law`.
Cross-check $P(p_B>p_A)$ through Monte Carlo, numerical integration of $\int f_BF_A$, and Cook's closed form for integer $\alpha_B$, interpreting Monte Carlo differences against standard errors.
With a uniform prior and large samples, $1-P(p_B>p_A)$ is close to the one-sided p-value, but posterior probabilities and p-values answer different questions.
The equal-tailed credible interval from `credible_interval` solves tail probabilities through the survival function, avoiding cancellation even for small probabilities.
Repeated interim checking and stopping increases the fraction of false winners, so assess the decision rule itself through repeated experiments before using it.
