Means, variances, correlation coefficients, and regression lines do not uniquely determine the shape of a dataset.
[Anscombe's 1973 paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.1973.10478966) made this limitation memorable.
This page explores the same statistical principle with four new datasets constructed for SciOxide.
The construction below generates every coordinate; the data are not Anscombe's quartet.
See [Licenses and data attribution](../../../reference/licenses/) for the provenance and terms.

## Four constructed datasets

Each dataset contains 13 points.
Datasets I–III use $x_i=i-6$ for $i=0,\ldots,12$.
Dataset IV uses twelve copies of $-a$ and one value $12a$, where $a=\sqrt{7/6}$.
Both designs have mean zero and $S_{xx}=\sum_i x_i^2=182$.

To construct $y$, start from a seed vector $z$, remove its constant and linear components, and normalize its remaining length:

$$
u_i=z_i-\bar z-x_i\frac{\sum_j x_j(z_j-\bar z)}{182},\qquad
 e_i=u_i\sqrt{\frac{45.5}{\sum_j u_j^2}},\qquad y_i=3+0.5x_i+e_i.
$$

Here $\sum_i e_i=\sum_i x_i e_i=0$ and $\sum_i e_i^2=45.5$.
The four seeds are chosen independently for this example:

- I: $z_i=((17i+7i^2+3)\bmod23)-11$, giving irregular deviations from a line.
- II: $z_i=x_i^2$, giving a quadratic curve.
- III: $z_{10}=1$ and all other entries zero, giving twelve collinear points and one response outlier at $x=4$.
- IV: $z=(-6,-5,-4,-3,-2,-1,1,2,3,4,5,6,0)$, giving a vertical group and one leverage-one point.

All four have $\bar x=0$, $\bar y=3$, sample variances $s_x^2=182/12$ and $s_y^2=91/12$, least-squares line $y=3+0.5x$, $r=1/\sqrt2$, $R^2=1/2$, and residual sum of squares 45.5.
The slope standard error is $1/\sqrt{44}$, with 11 residual degrees of freedom.
These identities hold before rounding; the stored binary64 coordinates reproduce them to floating-point precision.
The deterministic seeds illustrate diagnostics and do not assert normally distributed observational errors.

## Matching summary statistics and scatterplots

The least-squares estimates for simple regression $y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x + \varepsilon$ are:

$$
\hat\beta_1 = \frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}},\qquad \hat\beta_0 = \bar y - \hat\beta_1 \bar x,\qquad
S_{xx} = \sum_i (x_i-\bar x)^2,\quad S_{xy} = \sum_i (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)
$$


Assuming independent errors $\varepsilon_i$ following $N(0, \sigma^2)$, the slope standard error is $\mathrm{SE}(\hat\beta_1) = s/\sqrt{S_{xx}}$ with $s^2 = \mathrm{RSS}/(n-2)$, and $t = \hat\beta_1/\mathrm{SE}$ follows a t distribution with $n-2$ degrees of freedom.
The coefficient of determination is $R^2 = r^2$ ($r$ is Pearson correlation).

`linear_regression(x, y)` computes this line in one $O(n)$ scan, accumulating sums about means with extended exponents to avoid cancellation even for data with a large constant offset.
The `ProbabilityPlotFit` returned by `line()` holds `slope`, `intercept`, and `correlation` (`None` for constant $y$).
`slope_inference(confidence, alternative)` returns a slope t test and two-sided confidence interval in `RegressionContrast`, or `None` for $n = 2$ (zero degrees of freedom).
Compute means and sample variances (divisor $n-1$) with `mean` and `sample_variance`.

## Residuals, leverage, and Cook's distance

For multivariable regression where scatterplots are insufficient, inspect residuals $e_i = y_i - \hat y_i$.
The diagonal entries (leverages) of the simple-regression hat matrix $H = X(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top$ are:

$$
h_i = \frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x_i-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}},\qquad \frac{1}{n} \le h_i \le 1,\qquad \sum_i h_i = 2
$$

Leverage increases as $x$ moves away from the mean.
Because $\operatorname{Var}(e_i) = \sigma^2(1-h_i)$, compare residuals through internally studentized residuals $r_i = e_i / (s\sqrt{1-h_i})$.
Cook's distance summarizes changes in fitted values after deleting one point; with coefficient count $p = 2$, it is:

$$
D_i = \frac{r_i^2}{p}\,\frac{h_i}{1-h_i}
$$


A point with $D_i$ of about 1 or greater is commonly taken as a sign that it substantially changes the line (Cook and Weisberg, *Residuals and Influence in Regression*, 1982).
At a point with $h_i = 1$, $e_i = 0$, making $r_i$ and $D_i$ undefined.
The point at $x=12\sqrt{7/6}$ in dataset IV has this property: removing it makes $x$ constant and leaves the slope undetermined.

`scioxide-stats` provides no functions returning leverage, studentized residuals, or Cook's distance.
The cell below computes them manually from the formulas above using coefficients from `linear_regression` and `mean`.
Leverage is used internally by HC3 covariance of `contrast` in `PreparedRegression` and by GLM HC3 covariance, but its values are not exposed.

## Fitting robust lines

`scioxide-stats` provides three fits less sensitive to outliers.

- `PreparedHuber` performs Huber M-estimation.
  For residuals scaled by $\hat\sigma = \mathrm{MAD}(r)/0.6745$ to $u$, it applies loss $u^2/2$ within $|u| \le c$ and $c|u| - c^2/2$ outside, solving with iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS).
  The initial fit is ordinary least squares; subsequent weighted least-squares problems with weights $w_i = \min(1, c\hat\sigma/|r_i|)$ use thin SVD, stopping at `Converged` when coefficient changes satisfy the step tolerance in `HuberOptions`.
  Defaults are $c = 1.345$ (95% asymptotic efficiency for normal errors) and at most 100 iterations; each iteration costs $O(np^2)$.
  Supply the design matrix including an intercept column.
  As rustdoc cautions, it resists response outliers but not high-leverage design points.
- `robust_slope(x, y, RobustSlopeMethod::TheilSen)` returns the median of slopes $(y_j-y_i)/(x_j-x_i)$ for all pairs satisfying $x_i \ne x_j$ (Theil–Sen estimation, breakdown point about 29%) and Sen's slope confidence interval from Kendall-statistic variance.
- `RobustSlopeMethod::Siegel` takes the median slope from each point to other points, then the median of these (repeated median, breakdown point 50%).
  It returns no confidence interval.

Both rank-based estimators take $O(n^2)$ time and compute intercepts with default `RobustIntercept::Joint` (median of $y_i - \hat\beta_1 x_i$).

## Error and complexity

- `linear_regression` accumulates sums about means with extended exponents, giving summary-statistic relative errors at double-precision rounding scale.
  Any tiny differences between rows arise from floating-point construction and evaluation.
- Leverage divides by $S_{xx}$; when $x$ is nearly constant, $h_i$ approaches 1 and $1 - h_i$ suffers cancellation.
  The cell treats $1 - h_i \le 10^{-12}$ as $h_i = 1$ and omits $r_i$ and $D_i$.
- Least squares costs $O(n)$; Theil–Sen retains slopes for $O(n^2)$ pairs; Siegel takes $O(n^2)$ time and $O(n)$ memory.
  Each Huber iteration costs $O(np^2)$; read the reported termination before interpreting its coefficients.
