Confidence intervals for population means struggle to retain nominal coverage with skewed data.
For right-tailed data such as income, response times, particle sizes, or insurance claims, sample means are also right-skewed and vary together with sample variance.
Bootstrap approximates a statistic's distribution through replacement resampling of the sample, constructing intervals without assuming normality.
However, finite-sample coverage depends on the interval construction.
This page constructs a t interval and three bootstrap intervals for a lognormal mean and measures the fraction covering the true mean through repeated experiments.
Coverage estimates include binomial confidence intervals, allowing differences from nominal 95% to be judged against Monte Carlo error.

## Lognormal mean

For $Z\sim N(0,1)$, the distribution of $X=e^{\sigma Z}$ is called lognormal, $\mathrm{LogNormal}(0,\sigma)$.
The target is the population mean:

$$
\theta=E[X]=e^{\sigma^2/2}
$$


Variance is $\mathrm{Var}(X)=(e^{\sigma^2}-1)e^{\sigma^2}$ and skewness is $\gamma=(e^{\sigma^2}+2)\sqrt{e^{\sigma^2}-1}$.
At $\sigma=1$, $\theta\approx1.6487$ and $\gamma\approx6.18$; at $\sigma=1.5$, it reaches $\gamma\approx33.5$.
The skewness of sample mean $\bar X$ from $n$ independent observations is $\gamma/\sqrt n$, remaining about 1.38 even for $\sigma=1$ and $n=20$.
Construct a two-sided 95% interval for $\theta$ from observations $x_1,\dots,x_n$ and check whether it contains $\theta$.

## Four intervals

Student's t interval is $\bar x\pm t_{0.975,n-1}\,s/\sqrt n$ ($s$ is sample standard deviation with divisor $n-1$).
It is exact for independent normal observations, but $\bar X$ and $s$ are positively correlated for skewed distributions.
Samples with smaller $\bar x$ also have smaller $s$, making the entire interval more likely to lie below $\theta$.

Bootstrap substitutes the empirical distribution $\hat F$ of observations for the population.
Draw $B$ resamples of size $n$ from $\hat F$ (replacement sampling of observations) and compute statistic $\hat\theta^*_1,\dots,\hat\theta^*_B$ for each.
With sample quantiles $q(p)$ (Hyndman–Fan type 7) and $\alpha=0.05$, scioxide-stats provides the following three intervals.

| Method | Interval | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| percentile | $[q(\alpha/2),\ q(1-\alpha/2)]$ | Treats the resampled-statistic distribution directly as the distribution of $\hat\theta$ |
| basic | $[2\hat\theta-q(1-\alpha/2),\ 2\hat\theta-q(\alpha/2)]$ | Approximates the distribution of $\hat\theta-\theta$ with that of $\hat\theta^*-\hat\theta$ and inverts it |
| BCa | $[q(\alpha_1),\ q(\alpha_2)]$ | Adjusts percentile probability levels for bias and acceleration |

The BCa probability levels are:

$$
\alpha_i=\Phi\!\left(z_0+\frac{z_0+z^{(i)}}{1-a\,(z_0+z^{(i)})}\right),\qquad z^{(1)}=\Phi^{-1}(\alpha/2),\ z^{(2)}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)
$$


$\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF; bias correction $z_0=\Phi^{-1}\bigl((\#\{\hat\theta^*<\hat\theta\}+\#\{\hat\theta^*=\hat\theta\}/2)/B\bigr)$ follows the fraction of resampled statistics below the observed value.
Acceleration $a=\sum_iU_i^3/\bigl(6(\sum_iU_i^2)^{3/2}\bigr)$ is computed from influence values $U_i=\bar\theta_{(\cdot)}-\hat\theta_{(i)}$ derived from leave-one-out jackknife statistics $\hat\theta_{(i)}$.
For a sample mean, $U_i=(x_i-\bar x)/(n-1)$, so $a=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^3/\bigl(6(\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2)^{3/2}\bigr)$ equals sample skewness divided by $6\sqrt n$.
When $z_0=a=0$, BCa equals percentile.

Interval accuracy is measured by departures of one-sided noncoverage probabilities from nominal $\alpha/2$.
Edgeworth-expansion analysis gives one-sided errors $O(n^{-1/2})$ for t, percentile, and basic intervals and $O(n^{-1})$ for BCa (Efron 1987; Hall 1988).
For two-sided intervals, left/right $O(n^{-1/2})$ terms cancel, giving coverage error $O(n^{-1})$ for every method.
However, strongly skewed distributions have large coefficients on $n^{-1}$ terms and incomplete cancellation, so practical differences at $n$ require repeated experiments.
$B$ determines Monte Carlo error in interval endpoints.
A guideline for stabilizing 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles is about $B\ge1000$ (Davison–Hinkley 1997, Chapter 5); BCa additionally evaluates $n$ jackknife statistics.

## API and result structure

`ResamplingPlan::new(&[n], method, options)` constructs a resampling plan.
Pass `ResamplingMethod::Bootstrap { design, method, confidence }` to `method` and select `BootstrapMethod::{Percentile, Basic, Bca}` and nominal confidence level `ConfidenceProbability`.
`design` specifies resampling: `BootstrapDesign::Independent` independently resamples each group with replacement, `Paired` resamples aligned rows, `Stratified` fixes stratum sizes, and `MovingBlock { length }`/`CircularBlock { length }` resample consecutive time-series blocks.
BCa does not support block designs; `ResamplingPlan::new` returns `InvalidInput`.
Set resample count with `ResamplingOptions::with_replicates(B)` (default 9999).

Pass a statistic function returning a value from resample indices `ResampledIndices` to `ResamplingProblem::Indexed`.
`ResamplingPlan::evaluate(problem, replicates)` allocates workspace/output, executes, and returns `ResamplingOutput`.
`ReplicateOptions::sequential(&mut rng, policy)` draws resamples sequentially from one generator; `ReplicateOptions::indexed` uses a split random stream per resample so parallel execution preserves results.
When using one plan with many datasets, construct workspace once with `ResamplingPlan::workspace` and pass it with output arrays to `evaluate_into` to avoid per-execution allocations.

The result's `ResamplingResult` contains termination `termination()` and inference `inference()`.
Inference exists only for termination `StatsTermination::Completed`; a computation-count limit or stopping gives `None`.
Bootstrap inference `ResamplingInference::Bootstrap(BootstrapInterval)` contains interval `bounds`, resample standard deviation `standard_error`, Monte Carlo error `mean_monte_carlo_error` of the resample mean ($s^*/\sqrt B$), bias estimate `bias` ($\bar\theta^*-\hat\theta$), `degenerate` indicating identical resamples, and BCa `bias_correction` ($z_0$) and `acceleration` ($a$).
`mean_monte_carlo_error` is not an error estimate for interval endpoints.
If all jackknife statistics are equal and $a$ is undefined, BCa returns `NumericalInstability`.

One-shot `bootstrap(statistic, sample, rng)` returns a two-sided 95% BCa interval with 9999 replicates for slice-accepting statistics such as `mean`.
`jackknife(statistic, sample)` returns bias and standard error from $n$ leave-one-out statistics.

## Intervals for one sample

The next cell draws one lognormal sample of size $n$ and constructs a t interval and three bootstrap intervals.
The three plans start from the same seed and use identical resamples, differing only in interval construction.
The first table shows endpoints, width, distances from $\bar x$ to each endpoint, and whether each interval contains $\theta$.
The second table compares bootstrap diagnostics with independent checks.
For a sample mean, ideal bootstrap standard error ($B\to\infty$) equals $\sqrt{(n-1)/n}\,s/\sqrt n$, while jackknife standard error equals $s/\sqrt n$ exactly.
Acceleration $a$ is computed manually from the closed form above.
The plot shows a histogram of resample means with vertical lines at $\bar x$ and $\theta$.

```rust
//| id: stats-bootstrap-coverage-api-1
//| ref: stats-bootstrap-coverage-api-1
//| title: "Construct four intervals for one lognormal sample"
//| run: reactive
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-stats]
//| inputs:
//|   samples: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size n","min":10,"max":80,"step":10,"value":20}
//|   sigma: {"type":"range","label":"Log standard deviation σ [-]","description":"Larger values give a longer right tail.","min":0.25,"max":1.5,"step":0.25,"value":1.0}
//|   resamples: {"type":"range","label":"Resample count B","min":199,"max":3999,"step":200,"value":1999}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":3}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"heavy-tail","label":"σ = 1.5","description":"Acceleration a and bias correction z₀ increase, extending BCa rightward.","inputs":{"sigma":1.5}}
//|   - {"id":"mild-skew","label":"σ = 0.25, n = 80","description":"Skewness is small and the four intervals nearly coincide.","inputs":{"sigma":0.25,"samples":80}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"intervals","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":3.39318,"atol":1e-4}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/0/2","range":{"max":0.05}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/3/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/3/state","equals":"satisfied"}
//|   - case: heavy-tail
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/3/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
//|   - case: mild-skew
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"diagnostics","path":"/rows/1/2","range":{"max":1e-12}}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_random::SplitMix64;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, LogNormal, Mean, OutputPolicy, SamplingPolicy, SeedableRng};
use scioxide_stats::{
    BootstrapDesign, BootstrapMethod, ConfidenceProbability, ReplicateOptions,
    ResampledIndices, ResamplingInference, ResamplingMethod, ResamplingOptions, ResamplingPlan,
    ResamplingProblem, jackknife, mean, mean_confidence_interval,
};

let n = samples as usize;
let law = LogNormal::new(0.0_f64, sigma).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let theta = law.mean().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let mut data = Vec::with_capacity(n);
for _ in 0..n {
    data.push(law.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
}
let xbar = mean(&data).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let t = mean_confidence_interval(&data).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let statistic = |sample: ResampledIndices<'_>| {
    let idx = sample.indices();
    Ok(idx.iter().map(|&i| data[i]).sum::<f64>() / idx.len() as f64)
};
let confidence = ConfidenceProbability::new(0.95).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = ResamplingOptions::default().with_replicates(resamples as usize);
let mut rows = vec![("Student t", t.bounds().lower(), t.bounds().upper())];
let mut bca = None;
let mut distribution = Vec::new();
for (name, method) in [
    ("percentile", BootstrapMethod::Percentile),
    ("basic", BootstrapMethod::Basic),
    ("BCa", BootstrapMethod::Bca),
] {
    let design = BootstrapDesign::Independent;
    let kind = ResamplingMethod::Bootstrap {
        design,
        method,
        confidence,
    };
    let plan = ResamplingPlan::new(&[n], kind, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // The three plans start from the same seed and use identical resamples.
    let mut generator = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64 + 1000);
    let policy = SamplingPolicy::rollback(OutputPolicy::Atomic);
    let output = plan
        .evaluate(
            ResamplingProblem::Indexed(&statistic),
            ReplicateOptions::sequential(&mut generator, policy),
        )
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let Some(ResamplingInference::Bootstrap(interval)) = output.report().inference() else {
        return Err(format!("Stopped with {:?}", output.report().termination()));
    };
    rows.push((name, interval.bounds.lower(), interval.bounds.upper()));
    if method == BootstrapMethod::Bca {
        bca = Some((interval, output.report().termination()));
        distribution = output.distribution().to_vec();
    }
}
let (bca, termination) = bca.ok_or("No BCa result")?;
let interval_rows: Vec<_> = rows
    .iter()
    .map(|&(name, low, high)| {
        (
            name,
            low,
            high,
            high - low,
            xbar - low,
            high - xbar,
            low <= theta && theta <= high,
        )
    })
    .collect();
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Interval" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Lower endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Upper endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Width [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "x̄ − lower endpoint [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Upper endpoint − x̄ [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Contains θ", "type": "boolean" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    interval_rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "intervals" })
);

// Independent check: ideal bootstrap standard error, jackknife standard error, and closed-form acceleration a for the mean
let s = t.standard_error() * RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64);
let ideal = RealScalar::sqrt((n as f64 - 1.0) / n as f64) * s / RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64);
let jack = jackknife(mean, &data).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (m2, m3) = data.iter().fold((0.0, 0.0), |(a, b), &x| {
    let d = x - xbar;
    (a + d * d, b + d * d * d)
});
let closed_a = m3 / (6.0 * RealScalar::powf(m2, 1.5));
let a = bca.acceleration.ok_or("Missing acceleration")?;
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let diagnostic_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    {
        "key": "1",
        "label": "scioxide-stats [-]",
        "type": "number",
        "format": { "significantDigits": 6 }
    },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Relative difference from check [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" }
]);
let relative = |value: f64, check: f64| ((value - check) / check).abs();
let diagnostics = vec![
    (
        "Bootstrap standard error / ideal value",
        bca.standard_error,
        Some(relative(bca.standard_error, ideal)),
    ),
    (
        "Jackknife standard error / s/√n",
        jack.standard_error,
        Some(relative(
            jack.standard_error,
            s / RealScalar::sqrt(n as f64),
        )),
    ),
    (
        "Bias correction z₀",
        bca.bias_correction.ok_or("Missing z₀")?,
        None,
    ),
    ("Acceleration a / closed form", a, Some(relative(a, closed_a))),
    ("Bias estimate θ̄* − θ̂", bca.bias, None),
    (
        "Monte Carlo error of resample mean",
        bca.mean_monte_carlo_error,
        None,
    ),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    diagnostic_columns,
    diagnostics,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "diagnostics" })
);

// Histogram of resample means (30 display bins)
let (low, high) = distribution
    .iter()
    .fold((f64::MAX, f64::MIN), |(a, b), &v| (a.min(v), b.max(v)));
let width = (high - low) / 30.0;
let mut counts = [0_usize; 30];
for &v in &distribution {
    counts[(((v - low) / width) as usize).min(29)] += 1;
}
let bins: Vec<[f64; 3]> = (0..30)
    .map(|k| {
        [
            low + k as f64 * width,
            low + (k + 1) as f64 * width,
            counts[k] as f64,
        ]
    })
    .collect();
emit_chart!(serde_json::json!({
    "kind": "histogram",
    "xLabel": "Resample mean θ̂* [-]",
    "yLabel": "Frequency [count]",
    "bins": bins,
    "tooltip": true,
    "annotations": [
        { "kind": "line", "axis": "x", "value": xbar, "label": "x̄" },
        {
            "kind": "line",
            "axis": "x",
            "value": theta,
            "label": "θ",
            "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
        },
        {
            "kind": "range",
            "axis": "x",
            "from": bca.bounds.lower(),
            "to": bca.bounds.upper(),
            "label": "BCa interval"
        }
    ]
}));

let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("All resamples evaluated")
        .explanation("Intervals are statistical estimates with no accuracy claim.")
} else {
    Outcome::partial("Resample evaluation stopped early")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{termination:?}"))
        .diagnostic("Resample count", resamples, None)
        .diagnostic("True mean θ", theta, None)
);
```

For default inputs ($n=20$, $\sigma=1$, $B=1999$, seed 3), sample mean $\bar x\approx1.663$ is near $\theta\approx1.649$ and all four intervals contain $\theta$.
Differences appear in the distances from $\bar x$ to each endpoint.
The t interval extends symmetrically by 1.143 each way; percentile extends 0.858 left and 1.213 right; BCa extends 0.682 left and 1.731 right.
Basic extends 1.213 left and 0.858 right, reversing percentile's distances.
Basic inverts the distribution of $\hat\theta^*-\hat\theta$ with its long right tail, reversing the tail direction.
Because sample means are right-skewed, percentile and BCa extend their upper endpoints in the appropriate direction to reduce the risk of true $\theta$ lying beyond the interval on the right.

In the diagnostics table, bootstrap standard error 0.5307 agrees with the ideal value within 0.3%.
This difference is Monte Carlo error from finite $B$ and shrinks as $B$ increases.
Jackknife standard error and $s/\sqrt n$, and BCa acceleration $a=0.103$ and its closed form, agree at rounding scale.
Bias correction is $z_0=0.145$; 56% of resample means lie below observed $\bar x$.
At $\sigma=1.5$, $a=0.133$ and $z_0=0.180$ increase; the BCa upper endpoint moves to 10.1, farther right than percentile's 7.26.
Then the t and basic lower endpoints become negative, making them meaningless as intervals for the mean of a positive quantity.
At $\sigma=0.25$ and $n=80$, skewness is small and endpoint differences among the four intervals are at most 0.01.

## Repeated coverage experiment

Whether one sample's interval contains $\theta$ depends on that particular sample.
Coverage is the proportion from repeating the same procedure across many samples.
The next cell draws $R$ samples at each $n=10,20,40,80$ and constructs four intervals for each sample.
It counts intervals containing $\theta$, those with $\theta$ below the lower endpoint (left noncoverage), and those with the truth above the upper endpoint (right noncoverage), and records mean interval width.
All four intervals use the same samples, reducing the effect of sample variability on method differences (common random numbers).
The three bootstrap plans are constructed once per sample size, reusing workspace and output arrays when calling `evaluate_into`.
Complexity is $O(R\,B\sum n)$ and even defaults take several seconds, so execution uses a button and reuses results for identical inputs.
To limit computation, default $B$ is 399, adding more endpoint Monte Carlo error than values around $B=1000$.

```rust
//| id: stats-bootstrap-coverage-calc
//| ref: stats-bootstrap-coverage-calc
//| title: "Count coverage and left/right noncoverage rates of four intervals by sample size"
//| run: button
//| cache: inputs
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-random, scioxide-stats]
//| exports: [sizes, theta, replicates, counts]
//| inputs:
//|   sigma: {"type":"range","label":"Log standard deviation σ [-]","description":"Larger values give a longer right tail.","min":0.25,"max":1.5,"step":0.25,"value":1.0}
//|   resamples: {"type":"range","label":"Resample count B","min":199,"max":599,"step":100,"value":399}
//|   replicates: {"type":"range","label":"Sample replicate count R","description":"Changes the Monte Carlo error in coverage.","min":100,"max":300,"step":50,"value":200}
//|   seed: {"type":"range","label":"Random seed","min":1,"max":100,"step":1,"value":5}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"mild-skew","label":"σ = 0.5","description":"Skewness is small and all four methods approach nominal coverage at n = 20.","inputs":{"sigma":0.5}}
//|   - {"id":"heavy-tail","label":"σ = 1.5","description":"All intervals remain far below nominal even at n = 80.","inputs":{"sigma":1.5}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"path":"/exports/sizes/3","equals":80}
//|       - {"path":"/exports/replicates","equals":200}
use scioxide_core::ExecutionContext;
use scioxide_random::SplitMix64;
use scioxide_random::{Distribution, LogNormal, Mean, OutputPolicy, SamplingPolicy, SeedableRng};
use scioxide_stats::{
    BootstrapDesign, BootstrapMethod, ConfidenceProbability, ReplicateOptions,
    ResampledIndices, ResamplingInference, ResamplingMethod, ResamplingOptions, ResamplingPlan,
    ResamplingProblem, mean_confidence_interval,
};

let law = LogNormal::new(0.0_f64, sigma).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let theta = law.mean().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let confidence = ConfidenceProbability::new(0.95).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = ResamplingOptions::default().with_replicates(resamples as usize);
let methods = [
    BootstrapMethod::Percentile,
    BootstrapMethod::Basic,
    BootstrapMethod::Bca,
];
let sizes = [10_usize, 20, 40, 80];
let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
let mut boot = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64 + 1000);
let policy = SamplingPolicy::rollback(OutputPolicy::Atomic);
// counts[sample size][method] = [covered, left noncoverage, right noncoverage, sum of widths] (method 0 is the t interval)
let mut counts = vec![[[0.0_f64; 4]; 4]; sizes.len()];
let mut data = Vec::new();
for (k, &n) in sizes.iter().enumerate() {
    let mut plans = Vec::with_capacity(methods.len());
    for method in methods {
        let design = BootstrapDesign::Independent;
        let kind = ResamplingMethod::Bootstrap {
            design,
            method,
            confidence,
        };
        let plan = ResamplingPlan::new(&[n], kind, options).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let workspace = plan.workspace().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        plans.push((plan, workspace, vec![0.0_f64; resamples as usize]));
    }
    for _ in 0..replicates as usize {
        data.clear();
        for _ in 0..n {
            data.push(law.sample(&mut rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?);
        }
        let t = mean_confidence_interval(&data).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        let mut bounds = vec![(t.bounds().lower(), t.bounds().upper())];
        let statistic = |sample: ResampledIndices<'_>| {
            let idx = sample.indices();
            Ok(idx.iter().map(|&i| data[i]).sum::<f64>() / idx.len() as f64)
        };
        // Copy generator state before use so all three methods use identical resamples.
        let start = boot.clone();
        for (plan, workspace, output) in plans.iter_mut() {
            let mut generator = start.clone();
            let report = plan
                .evaluate_into(
                    ResamplingProblem::Indexed(&statistic),
                    ReplicateOptions::sequential(&mut generator, policy),
                    output,
                    workspace,
                    ExecutionContext::sequential_native(),
                )
                .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            let Some(ResamplingInference::Bootstrap(interval)) = report.inference() else {
                return Err(format!("Stopped with {:?}", report.termination()));
            };
            bounds.push((interval.bounds.lower(), interval.bounds.upper()));
            boot = generator;
        }
        for (j, &(low, high)) in bounds.iter().enumerate() {
            let cell = &mut counts[k][j];
            cell[0] += f64::from(u8::from(low <= theta && theta <= high));
            cell[1] += f64::from(u8::from(theta < low));
            cell[2] += f64::from(u8::from(theta > high));
            cell[3] += high - low;
        }
    }
}
export!(sizes, sizes);
export!(theta, theta);
export!(replicates, replicates as usize);
export!(counts, counts);
```

## Interpreting coverage

The display cell computes coverage and 95% Wilson intervals from the previous cell's counts and plots them against sample size.
Points for the four methods are slightly offset horizontally to avoid overlap.
The bar chart compares left/right noncoverage rates at the chosen sample size against nominal $\alpha/2=0.025$.
The table shows coverage, Wilson intervals, left/right noncoverage rates, and mean widths.
The $R$ checks are independent Bernoulli trials, so coverage estimate $\hat c$ has standard error $\sqrt{\hat c(1-\hat c)/R}$, about 0.021 for $R=200$ and $\hat c=0.9$.
If nominal 0.95 lies outside the Wilson interval, the interval procedure's coverage can be judged different from nominal.

//|       - {"artifact":"coverage","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"Student t"}

//|       - {"artifact":"coverage","path":"/rows/2/5","approx":{"value":0.17,"atol":1e-9}}
//|   - case: small-sample
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"coverage","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.785,"atol":1e-9}}

    serde_json::from_value(run["sizes"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let counts: Vec<[[f64; 4]; 4]> =
    serde_json::from_value(run["counts"].clone()).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let r = run["replicates"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing replicate count")?;
let chosen: usize = table_size.parse().map_err(|_| "Cannot read sample size")?;
let k = sizes
    .iter()
    .position(|&n| n == chosen)
    .ok_or("Sample size is absent from the grid")?;
let names = ["Student t", "percentile", "basic", "BCa"];
// Wilson score interval (95%)
let wilson = |p: f64| {
    let z = 1.96_f64;
    let center = (p + z * z / (2.0 * r)) / (1.0 + z * z / r);
    let radius = z * (p * (1.0 - p) / r + z * z / (4.0 * r * r)).sqrt() / (1.0 + z * z / r);
    (center - radius, center + radius)
};
let mut series = Vec::new();
for (j, name) in names.iter().enumerate() {
    let points: Vec<[f64; 4]> = sizes
        .iter()
        .enumerate()
        .map(|(i, &n)| {
            let c = counts[i][j][0] / r;
            let (low, high) = wilson(c);
            [n as f64 * (0.955 + 0.03 * j as f64), c, low, high]
        })
        .collect();
    series.push(serde_json::json!({ "name": name, "points": points }));
}

}));

let rows: Vec<_> = names
    .iter()
    .enumerate()
    .map(|(j, &name)| {
        let cell = counts[k][j];
        let c = cell[0] / r;
        let (low, high) = wilson(c);
        (name, c, low, high, cell[1] / r, cell[2] / r, cell[3] / r)
    })
    .collect();

]);
emit_table_with_columns!(

let left: Vec<f64> = (0..names.len()).map(|j| counts[k][j][1] / r).collect();

Read default-input results ($\sigma=1$, $B=399$, $R=200$, seed 5) in increasing sample-size order.
At $n=10$, coverage is 0.815 for t, 0.765 for percentile, 0.755 for basic, and 0.785 for BCa; every Wilson upper endpoint (0.82–0.86) falls below nominal 0.95.
Most noncoverage occurs with $\theta$ above the upper endpoint (right); right noncoverage rates 0.17–0.24 are 7–9 times nominal 0.025.
For samples where sample mean and variance are both small, the entire interval lies left of $\theta$.
Left noncoverage rates 0–0.045 remain around nominal 0.025.
Coverage improves with $n$ but remains 0.875–0.91 even at $n=80$; Wilson intervals (upper endpoints 0.91–0.94) exclude 0.95.
The convergence order $O(n^{-1})$ is asymptotic; this shows its large coefficient for a distribution with skewness 6.

Among methods, basic is lowest at every $n$.
Basic flips the long right tail leftward and has the largest right noncoverage rate (0.12 at $n=80$).
BCa acceleration extends the upper endpoint rightward, giving the smallest right noncoverage among the four (0.07 at $n=80$), still above nominal 0.025.
The t interval exceeds all three bootstrap coverages at $n=10$ and becomes comparable to percentile/BCa at $n\ge40$.
BCa's one-sided error order $O(n^{-1})$ does not mean finite-$n$ noncoverage equals nominal.
Because all four intervals share samples, method differences are more precisely determined than differences between independent experiments, even with overlapping Wilson intervals.

At $\sigma=0.5$ (skewness 1.75), coverage rises to 0.915–0.935 for $n=20$, and the t interval at $n=40$ reaches nominal with 0.955.
Nevertheless, right noncoverage remains greater than left, preserving directional skewness effects.
At $\sigma=1.5$ (skewness 33.5), coverage remains only 0.77–0.85 even at $n=80$.
With extremely long tails, inclusion of a rare large observation determines the sample mean, which none of the intervals can compensate for.
For such distributions, consider estimating medians or quantiles instead of means, or constructing intervals on a logarithmic scale and transforming back.

If observations are dependent, as in time series, none of the intervals above satisfies its assumptions.
Resampling consecutive blocks with `BootstrapDesign::MovingBlock` or `CircularBlock` can preserve dependence, but BCa is unavailable and block-length choice affects results.
[Check confidence-interval coverage through repeated experiments](../confidence-coverage/) shows how intervals assuming independence lose coverage on autocorrelated sequences.

## Summary

For lognormal means, t and all three bootstrap intervals fall below nominal 95% with small samples; most noncoverage places the true mean above the upper endpoint.
Use `ResamplingPlan` and `BootstrapMethod` to construct percentile/basic/BCa intervals from the same resamples and inspect their distribution through `standard_error`, `bias`, `bias_correction`, and `acceleration` in `BootstrapInterval`.
When reusing one plan on many datasets, pass workspace created with `workspace` to `evaluate_into` to avoid allocation.
Compare coverage with binomial confidence intervals and separate left/right noncoverage rates to identify directional bias in intervals.
BCa's asymptotic advantage is limited for small samples from strongly skewed distributions, so assess it through repeated experiments at the relevant sample size and skewness.
