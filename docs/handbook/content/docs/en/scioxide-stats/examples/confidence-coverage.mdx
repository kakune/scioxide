The confidence interval $\bar X \pm t\,S/\sqrt n$ for a mean assumes independent observations.
This assumption fails for sequences with similar neighboring values, such as continuous sensor measurements or simulation output.
This page uses repeated scioxide-random experiments to measure how far the fraction of intervals containing the true mean (**coverage**) falls below nominal confidence when independence-based intervals are applied to autocorrelated sequences, comparing analytic predictions.
It also constructs intervals with standard errors corrected by estimated autocorrelation and checks how much coverage recovers.

## Meaning of frequentist confidence intervals

Confidence level $1-\alpha$ for interval $[L(X), U(X)]$ means:

$$
P_\mu\bigl(L(X) \le \mu \le U(X)\bigr) = 1 - \alpha
$$


The data-derived endpoints are random variables; population mean $\mu$ is an unknown constant.
Reading one observed interval as having a 95% probability of containing $\mu$ is incorrect; 95% of intervals from repeated applications of the same procedure contain $\mu$.
This fraction can be measured directly in repeated experiments.

For independent normal samples, $T = (\bar X - \mu)/(S/\sqrt n)$ follows Student t with $n-1$ degrees of freedom, making the coverage of $\bar X \pm t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-1}\,S/\sqrt n$ exactly nominal $1-\alpha$ (Casella–Berger §9.2).
`MomentsSnapshot::confidence_interval_of_mean` returns this interval at confidence 0.95; `confidence_interval_of_mean_with_options` accepts confidence level, critical-value distribution (`MeanIntervalMethod`), and inverse-incomplete-beta settings for t quantiles.
`MeanIntervalMethod::StudentT` uses t with $n-1$ degrees of freedom; `NormalAsymptotic` uses standard normal critical values.
Since intervals use sample variance, passing known-variance method `NormalKnownVariance` produces `InvalidInput`; fewer than 2 observations return `Ok(None)`.
Returned `MeanConfidenceInterval` holds endpoints `bounds`, estimate `estimate`, standard error `standard_error`, degrees of freedom `degrees_of_freedom`, and assumption `assumptions` required for nominal coverage (here `MeanIntervalAssumptions::IndependentNormal`).
Assumptions are recorded, not tested against the data.

## Variance inflation in AR(1) sequences

Consider the stationary first-order autoregressive process:

$$
X_t - \mu = \rho\,(X_{t-1} - \mu) + \sigma\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\, Z_t, \qquad Z_t \sim \mathcal N(0, 1)\ \text{independent}, \quad |\rho| < 1
$$

Its marginal variance is $\sigma^2$ and lag-$k$ autocovariance is $\gamma_k = \sigma^2 \rho^{|k|}$ (Brockwell–Davis §3.1).
Drawing initial value $X_1$ from marginal distribution $\mathcal N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ makes the sequence stationary from the start.
Sample-mean variance sums the covariance of all pairs, giving:

$$
\mathrm{Var}(\bar X) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\,g_n(\rho), \qquad
g_n(\rho) = 1 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Bigl(1 - \frac{k}{n}\Bigr)\rho^k \;\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\; \frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}
$$


$g_n$ is the **variance inflation factor** and $n/g_n$ the **effective sample size**.
At $\rho = 0.8$, $g_\infty = 9$: standard error triples relative to independence, and effective sample size is about one ninth of nominal.

| $\rho$ | $g_\infty$ | $n_{\mathrm{eff}}/n$ | Standard-error ratio $\sqrt{g_\infty}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 1.00 | 100% | 1.00 |
| 0.3 | 1.86 | 53.8% | 1.36 |
| 0.5 | 3.00 | 33.3% | 1.73 |
| 0.8 | 9.00 | 11.1% | 3.00 |
| 0.9 | 19.00 | 5.26% | 4.36 |

Expected squared-deviation sum is $E\bigl[\sum_t (X_t - \bar X)^2\bigr] = n\sigma^2 - n\,\mathrm{Var}(\bar X)$, so sample variance is also biased downward:

$$
E[S^2] = \sigma^2\,\frac{n - g_n}{n - 1}
$$


Intervals assuming independence become narrow for two reasons: standard-error formula $S/\sqrt n$ omits $g_n$, and $S^2$ itself is smaller than $\sigma^2$.
Replacing $S^2$ by its expectation and treating $\bar X$ as normal predicts coverage of independence-based intervals as:

$$
c_{\mathrm{pred}} = P\!\left(|Z| \le t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-1}\sqrt{\frac{n - g_n}{(n-1)\,g_n}}\right) = \operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{t_{1-\alpha/2,\,n-1}}{\sqrt 2}\sqrt{\frac{n - g_n}{(n-1)\,g_n}}\right)
$$


This approximation ignores variation in $S^2$; even at $\rho = 0$ it gives the normal approximation rather than nominal coverage ($n = 64$; 0.9545 at 95%).

## Correction using estimated autocorrelation

If $\rho$ is known, estimate $\sigma^2$ by $S^2 (n-1)/(n - g_n)$ and correct mean variance as:

$$
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar X) = \frac{S^2}{n}\cdot\frac{(n-1)\,g_n(\hat\rho)}{n - g_n(\hat\rho)}
$$


In practice $\rho$ is unknown, so replace it with correlation $\hat\rho$ of neighboring pairs $(X_{t-1}, X_t)$.
This page accumulates those pairs with `OnlineCovariance` and uses `correlation` for $\hat\rho$.
For small $n$, $\hat\rho$ is biased toward zero (mean about 0.75 at $\rho = 0.8$ and $n = 64$), causing undercorrection.
Use a t critical value with degrees of freedom $\nu = \lfloor \max(n/g_n(\hat\rho) - 1,\ 1) \rfloor$ determined from effective sample size.
Rounding down to an integer allows a single reusable critical value per degree of freedom and slightly increases it, widening intervals.
This degree-of-freedom choice is empirical; corrected-interval coverage is approximate.

Use `MeanIntervalPlan` to construct intervals from corrected standard errors.
`MeanIntervalPlan::new(confidence, MeanIntervalMethod::StudentT, Some(ν), options)` computes critical value $c$ once; `interval(estimate, standard_error)` returns $\bar X \pm c\,\mathrm{se}$.
`confidence_interval_of_mean_with_options` computes t quantiles through inverse incomplete beta on every call; constructing and reusing plans is faster for repeated experiments building thousands of intervals at the same degrees of freedom.
Passing $\bar X$ and `standard_error_of_sample_mean` to a plan with $n-1$ degrees of freedom gives the same interval as `StudentT` on `confidence_interval_of_mean_with_options`.
`interval_scaled` accepts standard error as `scioxide_core::Scaled` (an extended-range value with separate significand and binary exponent), without rounding to `f64` before multiplication by $c$.
`MomentsSnapshot::scaled_variance_of_sample_mean` returns $S^2/n$ in the same `Scaled`, preserving variances that underflow or overflow in `f64` until the correction factor is applied and square root taken.
Values in this example fit comfortably in `f64`, so the unrounded path leaves results unchanged while demonstrating correction of mean variance by another factor.
`variance_of_sample_mean` and `standard_error_of_sample_mean` return $S^2/n$ and $S/\sqrt n$ rounded to `f64`; both give `None` for fewer than 2 observations.
`interval_scaled` returns `NonFinite` for a non-finite estimate and `InvalidInput` for a negative standard error.

## Repeated coverage experiment

The next cell generates $R$ AR(1) sequences with mean $\mu = 50$ and marginal standard deviation $\sigma = 4$, constructing independence-based t intervals and intervals corrected with $\hat\rho$ for each.
First construct `MeanIntervalPlan` for degrees of freedom 1 through $n-1$; use a plan with $n-1$ degrees of freedom for independence-based intervals and $\nu$ for corrected intervals.
Because there are many repetitions, the cell runs by button.
Plot the fraction $\hat c$ of intervals containing $\mu$ while varying autocorrelation $\rho$ across 13 points from zero to $\rho_{\max}$.
Every $\rho$ starts from the same seed, isolating changes in $\rho$ (common random numbers).

Coverage estimate $\hat c = k/R$ is the success fraction of $R$ binomial trials with success probability $c$.
Bands are Clopper–Pearson 95% intervals returned by `binomial_confidence_limits`; excluding nominal confidence indicates coverage different from nominal.
The dashed line is variance-inflation prediction $c_{\mathrm{pred}}$; the horizontal line is nominal confidence.
The upper table shows coverage and intervals at every fourth point; the lower shows analytic $g_n$, effective sample size, mean $\hat\rho$, and mean interval widths.

use scioxide_random::{Distribution, Normal, SeedableRng, SplitMix64};
use scioxide_stats::{
    ConfidenceProbability, MeanIntervalMethod, MeanIntervalPlan, MomentOrder, OnlineCovariance,
    OnlineMoments, binomial_confidence_limits,
};

let (n, reps) = (
    coverage_sample_count as usize,
    coverage_replications as usize,
);
let count = n as f64;
let level = ConfidenceProbability::new(nominal_confidence).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let normal = Normal::new(0.0_f64, 1.0).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mu, sigma) = (50.0_f64, 4.0_f64);
// Finite-length variance inflation g_n(ρ) = 1 + 2 Σ_{k=1}^{n−1} (1 − k/n) ρᵏ.
let inflation = |rho: f64| {
    let weights = (1..n).map(|k| (1.0 - k as f64 / count) * RealScalar::powi(rho, k as i32));
    1.0 + 2.0 * weights.sum::<f64>()
};
// Compute t critical values once for degrees of freedom 1..n−1. plans[k − 1] has k degrees of freedom.

    .map(|k| {
        let degrees = Some(k as f64);
        MeanIntervalPlan::new(
            level,
            MeanIntervalMethod::StudentT,
            degrees,
            Default::default(),
        )
    })
    .collect::<Result<Vec<_>, _>>()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// One sequence: whether independence-based and ρ̂-corrected intervals contain μ, their widths, and ρ̂.
let replicate = |rho: f64, rng: &mut SplitMix64| -> Result<[f64; 5], String> {
    let mut state = normal.sample(rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mut moments = OnlineMoments::new(MomentOrder::Second);
    let mut lagged = OnlineCovariance::new(MomentOrder::Second);
    let mut previous = None;
    for i in 0..n {
        if i > 0 {
            let shock = normal.sample(rng).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
            state = rho * state + RealScalar::sqrt(1.0 - rho * rho) * shock;
        }
        let x = mu + sigma * state;
        moments.observe(x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        if let Some(last) = previous {
            lagged.observe(last, x).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
        }
        previous = Some(x);
    }
    // Independence-based interval: t interval x̄ ± t s/√n with n − 1 degrees of freedom.
    let snapshot = moments.snapshot();
    let mean = snapshot.mean().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mean = mean.ok_or("Undefined mean")?;
    let se = snapshot
        .standard_error_of_sample_mean()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let naive = plans[n - 2]
        .interval(mean, se.ok_or("Undefined standard error")?)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    // Correction: multiply s²/n by (n − 1) g / (n − g); use a t interval with ⌊n/g − 1⌋ degrees of freedom.
    let correlation = lagged.snapshot().correlation().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let rho_hat = RealScalar::clamp(correlation.unwrap_or(0.0), -0.99, 0.99);
    let g = inflation(rho_hat);
    let factor =
        Scaled::from_value((count - 1.0) * g / (count - g)).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let variance = snapshot
        .scaled_variance_of_sample_mean()
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let variance = variance.ok_or("Mean variance is undefined")?;
    let error = variance
        .mul(factor)
        .and_then(|v| v.sqrt())
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let degrees = RealScalar::floor(RealScalar::max(count / g - 1.0, 1.0)) as usize;
    let corrected = plans[degrees.min(n - 1) - 1]
        .interval_scaled(mean, error)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let covers = |bounds: scioxide_core::Interval<f64>| f64::from(u8::from(bounds.contains(mu)));
    let width = |bounds: scioxide_core::Interval<f64>| bounds.width().map_err(|e| e.to_string());
    Ok([
        covers(naive.bounds()),
        covers(corrected.bounds()),
        width(naive.bounds())?,
        width(corrected.bounds())?,
        rho_hat,
    ])
};
// Binomial Clopper–Pearson 95% interval [lower, upper].
let clopper_pearson = |hits: f64| -> Result<(f64, f64), String> {
    let limits =
        binomial_confidence_limits::<f64>(hits as usize, reps).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let (lower, upper) = limits.set().bounds().ok_or("Empty interval")?;
    Ok((lower.unwrap_or(0.0), upper.unwrap_or(1.0)))
};
let t = level
    .student_t_critical_value(count - 1.0)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let (mut naive_band, mut corrected_band, mut predicted) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
let (mut coverage_rows, mut inflation_rows) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for step in 0..=12 {
    let rho = maximum_autocorrelation * f64::from(step) / 12.0;
    let mut rng = SplitMix64::from_seed(seed as u64);
    let mut totals = [0.0_f64; 5];
    for _ in 0..reps {
        let result = replicate(rho, &mut rng)?;
        for (total, value) in totals.iter_mut().zip(result) {
            *total += value;
        }
    }
    let [
        naive_hits,
        corrected_hits,
        naive_width,
        corrected_width,
        rho_sum,
    ] = totals;
    let (naive_rate, corrected_rate) = (naive_hits / reps as f64, corrected_hits / reps as f64);
    let (naive_low, naive_high) = clopper_pearson(naive_hits)?;
    let (corrected_low, corrected_high) = clopper_pearson(corrected_hits)?;
    // Prediction: replace S² with its expectation in the normal approximation erf(t √((n − g)/((n − 1) g)) / √2).
    let g = inflation(rho);
    let scale = RealScalar::sqrt((count - g) / ((count - 1.0) * g));
    let prediction =
        scioxide_special::erf(t * scale / std::f64::consts::SQRT_2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    naive_band.push([rho, naive_rate, naive_low, naive_high]);
    corrected_band.push([rho, corrected_rate, corrected_low, corrected_high]);
    predicted.push([rho, prediction]);
    if step % 4 == 0 {
        coverage_rows.push((
            rho,
            prediction,
            naive_rate,
            naive_low,
            naive_high,
            corrected_rate,
            corrected_low,
            corrected_high,
        ));

        inflation_rows.push((rho, g, count / g, rho_sum / reps as f64, w0, w1));
    }
}

At $n = 16$, mean $\hat\rho$ for $\rho = 0.8$ is biased down to 0.568, leaving corrected coverage only 0.823.

Independence-based coverage 0.432 $[0.382, 0.484]$ excludes prediction 0.490; ignoring variation in $S^2$ makes predictions coarse for short sequences.

## Constructing intervals for time-series means

Increasing replicate count $R$ or sample size $n$ does not fix narrow intervals that ignore autocorrelation.
If the observation period is short relative to correlation length, finer sampling barely increases effective sample size.
Besides the correction above, dependence can be handled through the following approaches.

- Block bootstrap resamples blocks longer than the correlation length, approximating sampling distributions while preserving dependence.
  In scioxide-stats, set `BootstrapDesign::MovingBlock` or `CircularBlock` in `ResamplingPlan` ([Resampling and tests](../../algorithms/resampling/)).
  Block-length choice affects results.
- To obtain $g_n$ from an estimated ACF, compute sample autocorrelations with `time_series_summary`, estimate $g_n$ from an AR model or similar model, and correct standard errors.
  The correction above is the simplest form; $\hat\rho$ used for correction itself has estimation error and bias.
- scioxide-stats provides no HAC estimators such as Newey–West, which estimate long-run variance $\sum_k \gamma_k$ from weighted autocovariance sums.
  If needed, sum Bartlett-weighted autocovariances from `time_series_summary` and pass the result to `MeanIntervalPlan::interval_scaled` as in the cell above.

## Summary

For independent normal samples, Student t-interval coverage matches nominal confidence within Monte Carlo error.
Positive autocorrelation narrows intervals through omitted $g_n$ in the standard-error formula and biased sample variance, reducing coverage far below nominal as predicted by variance inflation.
Coverage itself is estimated from $R$ binomial trials; assess whether Clopper–Pearson intervals include nominal confidence.
Correcting standard errors with estimated $\hat\rho$ moves coverage toward nominal, but bias in $\hat\rho$ prevents full recovery with strong autocorrelation and short sequences.
For time-series means, pass corrected standard errors to `MeanIntervalPlan::interval_scaled` or use a dependence-preserving block bootstrap.
