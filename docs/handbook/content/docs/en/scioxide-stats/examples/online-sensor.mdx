Use online aggregation without retaining the sample when observations must be summarized in arrival order without storing measurements.
Actual measurement series combine large baselines, periodic fluctuations, and instrument drift.
This page aggregates temperature measurements with `OnlineMoments`, checking what the online mean follows, which components make up sample variance, and what is lost at large baselines against analytical values and two-pass calculations.
The second half aggregates skewness and kurtosis of the same series and compares them with formulas based on raw power sums.

## Measurement-series model

Define the measurement at time $i = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$ by

$$
x_i = B + A \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{P}\right) + D\,\frac{i}{N-1}
$$


$B = 20 + 10^p$ °C is the baseline, $A = 0.2$ °C the amplitude of fluctuations with period $P = 16$ observations, and $D$ °C the drift over the full observation period.
The baseline exponent $p$ determines how many orders of magnitude smaller the fluctuations are than the mean.
An actual thermometer does not measure $10^6$ °C, but the same relative scales commonly arise with large absolute timestamps, cumulative energy consumption, and latitude/longitude coordinates.

If $N$ is a multiple of $P$, the sine sums to 0; since $\sum_i i = N(N-1)/2$, the mean is

$$
\bar x = B + \frac{D}{2}
$$


The online mean $\bar x_n$ averages the first $n$ observations, so its drift contribution rises gradually as $D(n-1)/(2(N-1))$, reaching $D/2$ at $n = N$.
An online mean weights every past observation equally, tracking the midpoint of the drift so far rather than the current value.
Tracking the current value requires another estimator, such as an exponential moving average or state-space model.

Sample variance does not describe measurement noise alone.
Splitting the sum of squared deviations $\sum_i (x_i - \bar x)^2$ into sine, drift, and cross terms gives the following when $N$ is a multiple of $P$:

$$
(N-1)\,s^2 = \frac{N A^2}{2} - \frac{A D N}{N-1}\cot\frac{\pi}{P} + \frac{D^2 N (N+1)}{12 (N-1)}
$$


The first term comes from the sine sum of squares over complete periods, $\sum_i \sin^2(2\pi i/P) = N/2$; the third comes from the equally spaced grid's sum of squared deviations, $\sum_i (i - (N-1)/2)^2 = N(N^2-1)/12$.
The second is a cross term using $\sum_i i \sin(2\pi i/P) = -(N/2)\cot(\pi/P)$.
The sine peaks lie in the first half of each period, so its covariance with the drift is negative.
Variance computed without detrending includes this deterministic change.

The following definitions block constructs the measurement series used by both cells on this page.
`sensor_series` returns deviations $A\sin(2\pi i/P) + D\,i/(N-1)$ and measurements $x_i$ obtained by adding $B$ and rounding to `f64`.

```rust
//| id: stats-online-sensor-model
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Measurement series combining baseline, periodic variation, and drift"
//| crates: [scioxide-core]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

/// Periodic amplitude A [°C] and period P [observations].
pub(crate) const AMPLITUDE: f64 = 0.2;
pub(crate) const PERIOD: f64 = 16.0;

/// Baseline B [°C], deviations before adding the baseline [°C], and stored measurements xᵢ = B + deviation [°C].
pub(crate) struct SensorSeries {
    pub(crate) baseline: f64,
    pub(crate) deviations: Vec<f64>,
    pub(crate) values: Vec<f64>,
}

/// xᵢ = B + A sin(2πi/P) + D i/(N − 1), B = 20 + 10ᵖ.
pub(crate) fn sensor_series(drift: f64, baseline_exponent: f64, count: usize) -> SensorSeries {
    let baseline = 20.0 + RealScalar::powf(10.0_f64, baseline_exponent);
    let last = (count - 1) as f64;
    let deviations: Vec<f64> = (0..count)
        .map(|i| {
            let phase = 2.0 * std::f64::consts::PI * i as f64 / PERIOD;
            AMPLITUDE * RealScalar::sin(phase) + drift * i as f64 / last
        })
        .collect();
    let values = deviations.iter().map(|d| baseline + d).collect();
    SensorSeries {
        baseline,
        deviations,
        values,
    }
}
```

## Online aggregation experiment

The next cell varies drift $D$, baseline exponent $p$, and sample size $N$, generating measurements and aggregating them individually with `OnlineMoments::observe`.
At each step, read `snapshot().mean()` and plot the online mean's path as a deviation from the baseline.
The dashed line is the drift-only online mean $D\,i/(2(N-1))$; the horizontal line is the final theoretical mean $D/2$.

Compute variance by four paths and compare them with the analytical value above.
These are `sample_variance` from `OnlineMoments`, a two-pass calculation computing the mean of stored measurements by ordinary summation before summing deviations, the one-pass formula (difference of sums of squares), and a two-pass calculation on deviations before adding the baseline.
The last computes the model variance without baseline rounding, providing an independent check of the analytical value.
The two-pass and one-pass calculations are comparison paths written with `f64` arithmetic in the cell, bypassing SciOxide functions.
The lower table shows the final mean minus the theoretical mean, floating-point spacing near the baseline (1 ulp), and the standard error $s/\sqrt N$ of the mean assuming independence.

```rust
//| id: stats-online-sensor
//| ref: stats-online-sensor
//| title: "Aggregate a drifting measurement series with a large baseline online"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [stats-online-sensor-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-stats]
//| exports: [drift, baseline_exponent, sensor_sample_count]
//| inputs:
//|   drift: {"type":"range","label":"Full-period drift D [°C]","description":"Linear change added to the measurement series.","min":0,"max":4,"step":0.25,"value":0.5}
//|   baseline_exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Baseline exponent p (B = 20 + 10ᵖ °C)","description":"Change the orders of magnitude separating fluctuations from the mean.","min":0,"max":15,"step":1,"value":6}
//|   sensor_sample_count: {"type":"range","label":"Sample size N [observations]","description":"Observation period spanning an integer number of 16-observation cycles.","min":64,"max":256,"step":16,"value":128}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"strong-drift","label":"D = 4 °C","description":"Drift accounts for most variance, and the online mean rises linearly.","inputs":{"drift":4}}
//|   - {"id":"small-baseline","label":"p = 0","description":"With a small baseline, the one-pass formula also approaches the analytical value.","inputs":{"baseline_exponent":0}}
//|   - {"id":"quantized","label":"p = 15","description":"Floating-point spacing approaches the amplitude, quantizing stored measurements themselves.","inputs":{"baseline_exponent":15}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0374958183729549,"rtol":1e-09}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":0.000767,"rtol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":2.269e-12,"rtol":0.01}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/4/2","range":{"min":-1e-15,"max":1e-15}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0374958183706857,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":1.1641532182693481e-10,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: strong-drift
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.35323457351,"rtol":1e-09}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1.35323457350154,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: small-baseline
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/2/2","range":{"min":-1e-12,"max":1e-12}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/2/1","approx":{"value":3.552713678800501e-15,"rtol":1e-12}}
//|   - case: quantized
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0393700787401575,"rtol":1e-09}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.0393700787401575,"rtol":1e-09}}
//|       - {"artifact":"variance","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.94488,"rtol":0.001}}
//|       - {"artifact":"summary","path":"/rows/2/1","equals":0.125}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_stats::{MomentOrder, OnlineMoments};

let n = sensor_sample_count as usize;
let count = n as f64;
let SensorSeries {
    baseline,
    deviations,
    values,
} = sensor_series(drift, baseline_exponent, n);
// Main calculation: aggregate in arrival order and record the online mean at every step.
let mut moments = OnlineMoments::new(MomentOrder::Second);
let (mut signal, mut running, mut drift_mean) = (Vec::new(), Vec::new(), Vec::new());
for (i, &value) in values.iter().enumerate() {
    moments.observe(value).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mean = moments.snapshot().mean().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let mean = mean.ok_or("Undefined mean")?;
    signal.push([i as f64, value - baseline]);
    running.push([i as f64, mean - baseline]);
    drift_mean.push([i as f64, drift * i as f64 / (2.0 * (count - 1.0))]);
}
let snapshot = moments.snapshot();
let online = snapshot.sample_variance().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let online = online.ok_or("Sample variance is undefined")?;
// Comparison: two-pass and one-pass formulas using f64 arithmetic (bypassing SciOxide).
let two_pass = |xs: &[f64]| {
    let m = xs.iter().sum::<f64>() / count;
    xs.iter().map(|x| (x - m) * (x - m)).sum::<f64>() / (count - 1.0)
};
let sum: f64 = values.iter().sum();
let squares: f64 = values.iter().map(|v| v * v).sum();
let one_pass = (squares - sum * sum / count) / (count - 1.0);
// Check: analytical value for N a multiple of P (formula in the text).
let cot = 1.0 / RealScalar::tan(std::f64::consts::PI / PERIOD);
let analytic = (count * AMPLITUDE * AMPLITUDE / 2.0
    - AMPLITUDE * drift * count * cot / (count - 1.0)
    + drift * drift * count * (count + 1.0) / (12.0 * (count - 1.0)))
    / (count - 1.0);
emit_chart!(
    serde_json::json!({
        "kind": "line",
        "dataZoom": true,
        "tooltip": true,
        "series": [
            { "name": "Measurement xᵢ − B", "points": signal, "style": { "symbol": "circle" } },
            { "name": "Online mean x̄ₙ − B", "points": running },
            {
                "name": "Drift-only online mean",
                "points": drift_mean,
                "style": { "lineType": "dashed" }
            }
        ],
        "xLabel": "Observation index i [-]",
        "yLabel": "Deviation from baseline B [°C]",
        "annotations": [
            { "kind": "line", "axis": "y", "value": drift / 2.0, "label": "Theoretical mean D/2" }
        ]
    }),
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "running" })
);
let full = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 10 });
let sci2 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 2 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Variance calculation" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Sample variance [°C²]", "type": "number", "format": full },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Difference from analytical value [°C²]", "type": "number", "format": sci2 }
]);
// Stored measurements minus B (this subtraction does not round). Reference variance of the rounded measurements themselves.
let stored: Vec<f64> = values.iter().map(|v| v - baseline).collect();
let paths = [
    ("OnlineMoments", online),
    ("Two-pass on measurements", two_pass(&values)),
    ("One-pass formula", one_pass),
    ("Two-pass on stored measurements − B", two_pass(&stored)),
    ("Two-pass on deviations (model before rounding)", two_pass(&deviations)),
];
let rows: Vec<(&str, f64, f64)> = paths
    .iter()
    .map(|&(name, v)| (name, v, v - analytic))
    .collect();
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "variance" })
);
let final_mean = snapshot.mean().map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let final_mean = final_mean.ok_or("Undefined mean")?;
let standard_error = snapshot
    .standard_error_of_sample_mean()
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?
    .ok_or("Undefined standard error")?;
let ulp = RealScalar::next_after(baseline, f64::INFINITY) - baseline;
let summary = vec![
    ("Analytical sample variance [°C²]", analytic),
    (
        "Final mean − theoretical mean B + D/2 [°C]",
        final_mean - (baseline + drift / 2.0),
    ),
    ("Floating-point spacing near baseline [°C]", ulp),
    ("Mean standard error s/√N assuming independence [°C]", standard_error),
];
let value_format = serde_json::json!({ "significantDigits": 5 });
let summary_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Value", "type": "number", "format": value_format }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    summary_columns,
    summary,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "summary" })
);
export!(drift, drift);
export!(baseline_exponent, baseline_exponent);
export!(sensor_sample_count, sensor_sample_count);
```

For defaults $D = 0.5$ °C, $p = 6$, and $N = 128$, the analytical sample variance is $3.7496 \times 10^{-2}$ °C².
Its components are the sine term $NA^2/(2(N-1)) = 0.02016$ °C², drift term 0.02133 °C², and negative cross term $-0.00399$ °C².
The two-pass calculation on deviations before adding the baseline agrees with the analytical value to about $10^{-17}$ °C², confirming consistency between the analytical formula and series construction.

`OnlineMoments` and the two-pass calculation on measurements differ from the analytical value by $2.3 \times 10^{-12}$ °C²; a two-pass calculation after subtracting $B$ from stored measurements differs by the same amount.
Floating-point spacing near baseline $10^6$ is $1.16 \times 10^{-10}$ °C, and stored measurements are rounded to this grid.
The difference is between the variance of rounded measurements and the unrounded model, not an aggregation-algorithm error.
The one-pass formula deviates by $7.7 \times 10^{-4}$ °C² (2% relative error).
It extracts a sum of squared deviations of 4.76 °C² from the difference of two sums of squares of size $\sum_i x_i^2 \approx 1.28 \times 10^{14}$ °C²; their rounding, with 1 ulp = 0.016 °C², remains as an error of 0.097 °C² in the sum of squared deviations.

The online mean rises while oscillating around the drift-only mean (dashed) by the periodic component, finally reaching theoretical mean $D/2$.
The final mean minus theoretical mean $B + D/2$ (computed in `f64`) is 0 for every tested input.
`OnlineMoments` stores the mean as a compensated sum with high and low parts, returning the stored-measurement mean with error roughly equal to one final rounding to `f64`, so it matches $B + D/2$ rounded to `f64`.
The table's final-row value $s/\sqrt N = 0.0171$ °C would be the mean's standard error for independent observations.
This series consists of a deterministic sine and drift, so this value does not describe uncertainty in its mean.

Setting drift to $D = 4$ °C increases variance to 1.3532 °C², including a drift contribution of 1.3650 °C².
At $p = 0$, even the one-pass formula stays within $3.3 \times 10^{-13}$ °C² of the analytical value.

At $p = 15$, spacing near the baseline is 0.125 °C, the same order as amplitude 0.2 °C.
Stored measurements are quantized to a 0.125 °C grid; the `OnlineMoments` variance 0.03937 °C² agrees with the two-pass calculation after subtracting $B$ from stored measurements.
The difference $1.9 \times 10^{-3}$ °C² from the model variance is due to quantization; no aggregation algorithm can recover information lost during representation.
Meanwhile, the two-pass calculation using an ordinary sum for the mean gives 1.94 °C² and the one-pass formula $1.6 \times 10^{15}$ °C², failing even to recover the variance of the rounded measurements.

## Online skewness and kurtosis

Define the shape summaries skewness and kurtosis from central moments $m_k = \frac1N \sum_i (x_i - \bar x)^k$ by

$$
g_1 = \frac{m_3}{m_2^{3/2}}, \qquad g_2 = \frac{m_4}{m_2^2} - 3
$$


$g_1$ is Fisher–Pearson skewness, and $g_2$ is excess kurtosis, equal to 0 for a normal distribution.
For a sine over complete periods alone, $m_2 = A^2/2$, $m_3 = 0$, and $m_4 = 3A^4/8$ give $g_1 = 0$ and $g_2 = -1.5$.
Drift adds approximately uniformly distributed values, moving $g_2$ toward the uniform-distribution value $-1.2$; cross terms with the sine move $g_1$ slightly away from 0.

`OnlineMoments::new(MomentOrder::Fourth)` also updates third and fourth central moments online.
Updates and merges use Pébay's formulas, accumulating only powers of deviations from the mean and never forming raw power sums $\sum_i x_i^k$.
`MomentsSnapshot::skewness` and `kurtosis` return $g_1$ and $g_2$ under `BiasCorrection::None`, and independent-sample bias corrections $G_1$ and $G_2$ under `BiasCorrection::Corrected`.
`KurtosisConvention::Fisher` returns excess kurtosis; `Pearson` returns $m_4/m_2^2$.
`central_moment(k)` returns $m_k$; requesting an order the accumulator does not retain gives `NotImplemented`.
For constant samples, skewness and kurtosis are `None`; with `Corrected`, fewer than three observations for skewness or four for kurtosis also gives `None`.

The comparison formula based on raw power sums constructs central moments from $S_k = \sum_i x_i^k$ and $\bar x = S_1/N$, for example:

$$
m_4 = \frac{S_4}{N} - 4\bar x \frac{S_3}{N} + 6\bar x^2 \frac{S_2}{N} - 3\bar x^4
$$


At $p = 6$, each term is of size $B^4 \approx 10^{24}$ °C⁴, subtracting terms 27 orders of magnitude larger than $m_4 \approx 3 \times 10^{-3}$ °C⁴.

The next cell receives $D$, $p$, and $N$ from the preceding cell, generates the same series, and compares $g_1$, $g_2$, and $m_4$ computed by online accumulation through order four against two-pass deviation calculations (reference) and raw-power-sum formulas.
Changing the preceding cell's inputs recomputes this cell with the same series.
If the raw-power-sum formula gives $m_2$ at most 0, skewness and kurtosis are displayed as undefined.

```rust
//| id: stats-online-sensor-api-2
//| ref: stats-online-sensor-api-2
//| title: "Aggregate skewness and kurtosis online and compare with raw-power-sum formulas"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [stats-online-sensor-model]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-stats]
//| dependsOn:
//|   sensor: stats-online-sensor
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"shape","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.0132019073,"rtol":1e-06}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shape","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":-0.66765323,"rtol":1e-07}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shape","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":-0.66765323,"rtol":1e-07}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shape","path":"/rows/3/1","approx":{"value":0.0132019076,"rtol":1e-06}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shape","path":"/rows/3/2","approx":{"value":-0.6676532303,"rtol":1e-09}}
//|       - {"artifact":"shape","path":"/rows/1/3","equals":null}
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;
use scioxide_stats::{BiasCorrection, KurtosisConvention, MomentOrder, OnlineMoments};

// Receive the preceding cell's inputs and generate the same measurement series.
let drift = sensor["drift"].as_f64().ok_or("Missing drift")?;
let exponent = sensor["baseline_exponent"]
    .as_f64()
    .ok_or("Missing baseline exponent")?;
let n = sensor["sensor_sample_count"]
    .as_f64()
    .ok_or("Missing sample count")? as usize;
let SensorSeries {
    deviations, values, ..
} = sensor_series(drift, exponent, n);
let count = n as f64;
// Main calculation: aggregate central moments through fourth order in arrival order.
let mut moments = OnlineMoments::new(MomentOrder::Fourth);
for &value in &values {
    moments.observe(value).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
}
let s = moments.snapshot();
let shape = |correction| -> Result<(Option<f64>, Option<f64>), String> {
    let skew = s.skewness(correction).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let kurt = s.kurtosis(correction, KurtosisConvention::Fisher);
    Ok((skew, kurt.map_err(|e| e.to_string())?))
};
let (g1, g2) = shape(BiasCorrection::None)?;
let (big_g1, big_g2) = shape(BiasCorrection::Corrected)?;
let m4 = s.central_moment(4).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
// Construct (g₁, g₂) from central moments (m₂, m₃, m₄). Undefined if m₂ ≤ 0.
let coefficients = |m2: f64, m3: f64, m4: f64| {
    (m2 > 0.0).then(|| (m3 / RealScalar::powf(m2, 1.5), m4 / (m2 * m2) - 3.0))
};
// Reference: two-pass central moments (f64 arithmetic). Subtract x₀ from stored measurements first.
let central = |xs: &[f64]| {
    let center = xs.iter().sum::<f64>() / count;
    let moment = |k: i32| {
        xs.iter()
            .map(|x| RealScalar::powi(x - center, k))
            .sum::<f64>()
            / count
    };
    (moment(2), moment(3), moment(4))
};
let shifted: Vec<f64> = values.iter().map(|v| v - values[0]).collect();
let (s2, s3, s4) = central(&shifted);
let (r2, r3, r4) = central(&deviations);
let stored = coefficients(s2, s3, s4);
let reference = coefficients(r2, r3, r4);
// Comparison: construct central moments from raw power sums S_k (f64 arithmetic).
let raw = |k: i32| values.iter().map(|v| RealScalar::powi(*v, k)).sum::<f64>() / count;
let (a1, a2, a3, a4) = (raw(1), raw(2), raw(3), raw(4));
let n2 = a2 - a1 * a1;
let n3 = a3 - 3.0 * a1 * a2 + 2.0 * a1 * a1 * a1;
let n4 = a4 - 4.0 * a1 * a3 + 6.0 * a1 * a1 * a2 - 3.0 * a1 * a1 * a1 * a1;
let naive = coefficients(n2, n3, n4);
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Path" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "Skewness [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Excess kurtosis [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4, "nullText": "—" },
    { "key": "3", "label": "m₄ [°C⁴]", "type": "number", "format": sci3, "nullText": "—" }
]);
let rows = vec![
    ("OnlineMoments (g₁, g₂)", g1, g2, m4),
    ("OnlineMoments (corrected G₁, G₂)", big_g1, big_g2, None),
    (
        "Two-pass on stored measurements − x₀",
        stored.map(|r| r.0),
        stored.map(|r| r.1),
        Some(s4),
    ),
    (
        "Two-pass on deviations (model before rounding)",
        reference.map(|r| r.0),
        reference.map(|r| r.1),
        Some(r4),
    ),
    (
        "Raw-power-sum formula",
        naive.map(|r| r.0),
        naive.map(|r| r.1),
        Some(n4),
    ),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "shape" })
);
```

For default inputs, $g_1 = 0.0132$, $g_2 = -0.6677$, and $m_4 = 3.228 \times 10^{-3}$ °C⁴ from `OnlineMoments` agree with the two-pass stored-measurement calculation to the displayed digits.
Differences from the unrounded model are about $10^{-10}$ for $g_2$ and $m_4$, and relative $2 \times 10^{-8}$ even for $g_1$ with strong cancellation.
The raw-power-sum formula gives $g_1$ equal to $-1.7 \times 10^5$ and $g_2$ equal to $1.9 \times 10^{12}$, retaining no shape information.
The corrections $G_1 = 0.0134$ and $G_2 = -0.6461$ address independent-sample bias; for this deterministic series, their interpretation is the same as $g_1$ and $g_2$.

Setting the preceding cell to $D = 0$ gives $g_1$ of order $10^{-16}$ and $g_2$ agreeing with $-1.5$ within $3 \times 10^{-10}$, reproducing the analytical sine values.
At $D = 4$ °C, drift dominates, and $g_2 = -1.2208$ approaches the uniform-distribution value $-1.2$.
At $p = 15$, stored measurements have $g_1$ near 0 and $g_2$ equal to $-0.680$, differing from model values $0.0132$ and $-0.6677$.
This is `OnlineMoments` correctly aggregating the shape of quantized measurements; again the information was lost at the representation stage.

## Summary

The online mean weights all past observations equally, so in a drifting series it approaches the drift's midpoint rather than the current value.
Sample variance includes deterministic periodic and drift changes and their negative cross term, as well as noise; detrend before interpreting it as measurement uncertainty.
For the same reason, $s/\sqrt N$ returned by `standard_error_of_sample_mean` assumes independent observations and does not describe uncertainty in this series' mean.
`OnlineMoments` keeps stored-measurement variances and higher central moments near analytical values even with large baselines; at $p = 15$, where ordinary-sum two-pass and raw-power-sum formulas fail, it still returns statistics of the rounded measurements themselves.
However, as floating-point spacing near the baseline approaches the fluctuations, stored measurements become quantized and lose information that no aggregation algorithm can recover.
