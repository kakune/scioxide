When comparing success rates of two treatments, treatment A can perform better in every patient-background stratum while treatment B appears better after the strata are pooled.
This reversal is called Simpson's paradox (E. H. Simpson, *J. R. Stat. Soc. B* 13 (1951), 238–241).
It occurs when a third variable affecting the outcome (a confounder) is also associated with treatment allocation.
This page reproduces the reversal with real kidney stone treatment data and examines which questions are answered by stratum-specific odds ratios, a common odds ratio across strata, and a hypothetical experiment varying allocation.

## Kidney stone treatment outcomes

C. R. Charig, D. R. Webb, S. R. Payne and J. E. A. Wickham, "Comparison of treatment of renal calculi by open surgery, percutaneous nephrolithotomy, and extracorporeal shockwave lithotripsy", *BMJ* 292 (1986), 879–882, reported kidney stone treatment outcomes stratified at a stone diameter of 2 cm.
S. A. Julious and M. A. Mullee, *BMJ* 309 (1994), 1480–1481, presented this table as an example of Simpson's paradox.
Below, open surgery is treatment A and percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL) is treatment B.

| Stratum | Treatment A (open surgery) | Treatment B (PCNL) |
|---|---|---|
| Small stones (under 2 cm) | 81/87 (93%) | 234/270 (87%) |
| Large stones (2 cm or larger) | 192/263 (73%) | 55/80 (69%) |
| Pooled | 273/350 (78%) | 289/350 (83%) |

A has the higher success rate in both strata, while B has the higher pooled rate.
A was used more often for difficult large stones (263 of 350 patients), and B more often for easier small stones (270 of 350 patients).
Stone size is a confounder because it is associated with both success rate and allocation.

This was an observational study with physician-selected treatments, rather than a randomized trial.
Other confounders, such as stone location and number or patient condition, are absent from the table, so stratification need not identify the causal treatment effect.
The adjusted values below describe association when comparing at the same stone size.

The definitions block stores the tables as row-major 2 × 2 counts (rows: treatments A and B; columns: success and failure).
Since `scioxide-stats` does not provide the Mantel–Haenszel common odds ratio or Woolf standard error, helper functions compute their closed forms.

```rust
//| id: stats-simpsons-paradox-data
//| kind: definitions
//| title: "Kidney stone tables from Charig et al. (1986) and closed-form helpers"
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-stats]
use scioxide_core::RealScalar;

/// Small-stone stratum: [A success, A failure, B success, B failure].
pub(crate) const SMALL: [usize; 4] = [81, 6, 234, 36];
/// Large-stone stratum.
pub(crate) const LARGE: [usize; 4] = [192, 71, 55, 25];

/// Table obtained by summing stratum counts.
pub(crate) fn pooled(strata: &[[usize; 4]]) -> [usize; 4] {
    let mut total = [0; 4];
    for table in strata {
        for (sum, &count) in total.iter_mut().zip(table) {
            *sum += count;
        }
    }
    total
}

/// Woolf standard error of the log odds ratio √(1/a + 1/b + 1/c + 1/d).
pub(crate) fn woolf_se(table: &[usize; 4]) -> f64 {
    let sum: f64 = table.iter().map(|&count| 1.0 / count as f64).sum();
    RealScalar::sqrt(sum)
}

/// Mantel–Haenszel common odds ratio Σ aᵢdᵢ/nᵢ ÷ Σ bᵢcᵢ/nᵢ.
pub(crate) fn mantel_haenszel(strata: &[[usize; 4]]) -> f64 {
    let (mut numerator, mut denominator) = (0.0, 0.0);
    for &[a, b, c, d] in strata {
        let n = (a + b + c + d) as f64;
        numerator += (a * d) as f64 / n;
        denominator += (b * c) as f64 / n;
    }
    numerator / denominator
}
```

## Stratified and pooled comparisons

For a 2 × 2 table $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ (rows: treatments A and B; columns: success and failure), the success rates are $p_A = a/(a+b)$ and $p_B = c/(c+d)$.
Use three measures of effect.

- Success-rate difference $p_A - p_B$.
- Success-rate ratio (relative risk) $\mathrm{RR} = p_A / p_B$.
  Returned by `relative_risk`.
- Odds ratio $\mathrm{OR} = ad/(bc)$.
  Returned by `odds_ratio`; it is 0 if $ad = 0$, $+\infty$ if $bc = 0$, and `InvalidInput` if both are 0.

Woolf's formula $\sqrt{1/a + 1/b + 1/c + 1/d}$ approximates the standard error of the log odds ratio, giving a 95% interval $\exp(\ln\mathrm{OR} \pm 1.96\,\mathrm{SE})$.
`scioxide-stats` does not return odds-ratio intervals, so a helper computes Woolf intervals for comparison with logistic regression Wald intervals in the next section.

Use two tests of independence.
`power_divergence_contingency_test(table, CressieReadParameter::PEARSON, ContinuityCorrection::None)` computes Pearson's $\chi^2 = n(ad-bc)^2 / \{(a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d)\}$ and uses the upper-tail probability of a $\chi^2$ distribution with 1 degree of freedom as the asymptotic p-value.
The one-shot `chi2_contingency_test` applies Yates's continuity correction (`ContinuityCorrection::HalfUnit`) to the same statistic; this page uses the uncorrected form for comparison with the closed form.
`fisher_exact_test(table)` returns a two-sided exact p-value by summing probabilities of tables no more probable than the observed table under a hypergeometric distribution with fixed row and column totals.

The cell below tabulates success rates, the three effect measures, Woolf intervals, and both tests for the two strata and pooled table, and displays success rates side by side in a bar chart.

The small-stone odds ratio is $81 \cdot 36 / (6 \cdot 234) = 2.077$ and the large-stone odds ratio is $192 \cdot 25 / (71 \cdot 55) = 1.229$; both exceed 1 (favoring A).
The pooled odds ratio is $273 \cdot 61 / (77 \cdot 289) = 0.748$, below 1 (favoring B).
The table agrees with these manual calculations to six decimal places.

Every Woolf 95% interval contains 1 (small stones: 0.84–5.11; large stones: 0.71–2.12; pooled: 0.51–1.09).
Pearson's $\chi^2$ values are 2.63, 0.55, and 2.31 (p-values 0.105, 0.458, 0.128); Fisher exact p-values are 0.127, 0.479, and 0.154.
Thus neither the stratum-specific nor pooled differences alone can be distinguished from chance variation with this table.
Simpson's paradox concerns which comparison is being asked about, rather than significance.
The pooled comparison describes the outcome difference between patients who received A and those who received B; the stratified comparison describes the difference between treatments at the same stone size.
The latter comparison is relevant to treatment selection.

## Odds ratios adjusted for stone size

There are two ways to estimate a common odds ratio across strata.

One is the Mantel–Haenszel estimate $\widehat{\mathrm{OR}}_{\mathrm{MH}} = \sum_k a_k d_k / n_k \big/ \sum_k b_k c_k / n_k$, obtained in closed form solely from the table counts in stratum $k$.
For this table, $(8.168 + 13.994) / (3.933 + 11.385) = 1.447$.
The Robins–Breslow–Greenland variance gives a 95% interval of 0.92–2.29; the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel statistic is 2.43 (1 degree of freedom, p = 0.12) (Agresti, *Categorical Data Analysis*, 3rd ed., 2013, §6.4).
`scioxide-stats` does not provide Mantel–Haenszel estimation or testing, or the Breslow–Day homogeneity test.

The other method is logistic regression with stratum as an explanatory variable.
With treatment-A indicator $z_A$ and large-stone indicator $z_L$, fit

$$
\operatorname{logit} P(\text{success}) = \beta_0 + \beta_A z_A + \beta_L z_L
$$

Then $\exp(\beta_A)$ is the maximum-likelihood estimate of the odds ratio at fixed stone size (a common odds ratio assumed equal across strata).
Specify `GlmFamily::Binomial` in `PreparedGlm` and pass one row per (stratum, treatment) combination, using success proportions as responses and subject counts as `trials`.
The GLM is solved by iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS); on convergence, `GlmReport::termination` is `Converged`.
`coefficient_inference` uses the inverse Fisher information to return Wald standard errors, two-sided p-values $2\{1-\Phi(|\hat\beta|/\mathrm{SE})\}$, and intervals $\hat\beta \pm z\,\mathrm{SE}$ (asymptotic).

The model's residual deviance is a likelihood-ratio statistic against the saturated model with different stratum-specific odds ratios (four coefficients for four rows).
Comparing it with $\chi^2$ with $4 - 3 = 1$ degrees of freedom tests whether the odds ratios are equal across strata.
`GlmFitView::degrees_of_freedom` returns effective sample size minus coefficient count; for binomial rows, effective sample size is the total number of trials (700 here).
This is the degrees of freedom used to estimate Pearson dispersion $X^2/(n_{\mathrm{eff}} - p)$, distinct from the degrees of freedom for comparing aggregated-row deviances (row count minus coefficient count), so the cell computes the latter directly.
The model excluding $z_L$ (treatment only) has the same sufficient statistics as the pooled table, so $\exp(\hat\beta_A)$ equals its odds ratio 0.748, and its standard error equals the pooled Woolf value.
The cell below fits these two models and compares them with closed-form values.

```rust
//| id: stats-simpsons-paradox-api-2
//| ref: stats-simpsons-paradox-api-2
//| title: "Adjust for stone size with logistic regression"
//| run: reactive
//| uses: [stats-simpsons-paradox-data]
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-linalg, scioxide-random, scioxide-stats]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"models","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":0.748349,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"models","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":0.514605,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"models","path":"/rows/1/1","range":{"min":1.3,"max":1.6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"models","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.429363,"atol":1e-5}}
//|       - {"artifact":"models","path":"/rows/0/6","range":{"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"models","path":"/rows/1/6","approx":{"value":0.3153,"atol":1e-3}}
//|       - {"artifact":"models","path":"/rows/1/7","equals":"Converged"}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/0/3","range":{"max":1e-8}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/1/3","range":{"max":1e-6}}
//|       - {"artifact":"checks","path":"/rows/2/2","approx":{"value":1.446847,"atol":1e-6}}
//|       - {"path":"/outputs/2/state","equals":"satisfied"}
use scioxide_linalg::Matrix;
use scioxide_random::{ChiSquared, Survival};
use scioxide_stats::{
    ConfidenceProbability, ContingencyView, GlmCovariance, GlmFamily, GlmInputs, GlmOptions,
    GlmStorageOptions, PreparedGlm, Unweighted, odds_ratio,
};

// Rows: (small, A), (small, B), (large, A), (large, B). Columns: intercept, z_A, z_L
let layout = [
    (SMALL, 0, 1.0, 0.0),
    (SMALL, 2, 0.0, 0.0),
    (LARGE, 0, 1.0, 1.0),
    (LARGE, 2, 0.0, 1.0),
];
let trials: Vec<u64> = layout
    .iter()
    .map(|&(t, row, _, _)| (t[row] + t[row + 1]) as u64)
    .collect();
let response: Vec<f64> = layout
    .iter()
    .map(|&(t, row, _, _)| t[row] as f64 / (t[row] + t[row + 1]) as f64)
    .collect();
let confidence = ConfidenceProbability::new(0.95).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let options = GlmOptions::default();
let mut rows = Vec::new();
let mut estimates = Vec::new();
let mut adjusted_report = None;
for (name, columns) in [("Treatment only", 2), ("Treatment + stone size", 3)] {
    let mut values = Vec::new();
    for &(_, _, z_a, z_l) in &layout {
        values.extend_from_slice(&[1.0, z_a, z_l][..columns]);
    }
    let design = Matrix::from_vec(4, columns, values).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let plan = PreparedGlm::new(
        design.view(),
        GlmFamily::Binomial,
        Unweighted,
        Some(trials.as_slice()),
        None,
        None,
        GlmStorageOptions::new(),
    )
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let result = plan
        .fit(GlmInputs::new(&response, options))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let report = *result.report();
    let fit = result.fit().ok_or("Missing fitted values")?;
    let inference = plan
        .coefficient_inference(&response, &fit, GlmCovariance::Model, confidence)
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    let treatment = inference[1];
    // Deviance degrees of freedom = row count − coefficient count. degrees_of_freedom() returns Σ trials − coefficient count
    let df = (4 - columns) as f64;
    let deviance_p = ChiSquared::<f64>::new(df)
        .and_then(|law| law.sf(fit.deviance()))
        .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
    rows.push((
        name,
        scioxide_core::RealScalar::exp(treatment.estimate()),
        scioxide_core::RealScalar::exp(treatment.interval().lower()),
        scioxide_core::RealScalar::exp(treatment.interval().upper()),
        treatment.p_value(),
        fit.deviance(),
        deviance_p,
        format!("{:?}", report.termination),
    ));
    estimates.push((treatment.estimate(), treatment.standard_error()));
    adjusted_report = Some(report);
}

// Independent check: pooled ln OR and Woolf SE, and Mantel–Haenszel common odds ratio (closed form)
let total = pooled(&[SMALL, LARGE]);
let pooled_or = odds_ratio(ContingencyView::new(&total, 2, 2).map_err(|e| e.to_string())?)
    .map_err(|e| e.to_string())?;
let mh = mantel_haenszel(&[SMALL, LARGE]);
let adjusted_or = scioxide_core::RealScalar::exp(estimates[1].0);
let checks = vec![
    (
        "Treatment-only β̂_A and ln(pooled OR)",
        estimates[0].0,
        scioxide_core::RealScalar::ln(pooled_or),
    ),
    (
        "Treatment-only SE and pooled Woolf SE",
        estimates[0].1,
        woolf_se(&total),
    ),
    (
        "Adjusted OR (maximum likelihood) and Mantel–Haenszel OR",
        adjusted_or,
        mh,
    ),
];
let checks: Vec<_> = checks
    .into_iter()
    .map(|(name, glm, closed)| (name, glm, closed, (glm - closed).abs()))
    .collect();

let fixed3 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 3 });
let fixed4 = serde_json::json!({ "fractionDigits": 4 });
let sci3 = serde_json::json!({ "notation": "scientific", "significantDigits": 3 });
let model_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Model" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "A odds ratio exp(β̂_A) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Lower Wald 95% interval limit [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Upper Wald 95% interval limit [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "4", "label": "p-value for β_A = 0 [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "5", "label": "Residual deviance [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed3 },
    { "key": "6", "label": "Deviance p-value (degrees of freedom = 4 − coefficient count) [-]", "type": "number", "format": fixed4 },
    { "key": "7", "label": "Termination" }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    model_columns,
    rows,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "models" })
);
let check_columns = serde_json::json!([
    { "key": "0", "label": "Quantity compared" },
    { "key": "1", "label": "GLM [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 8 } },
    { "key": "2", "label": "Closed form [-]", "type": "number", "format": { "significantDigits": 8 } },
    { "key": "3", "label": "Absolute difference [-]", "type": "number", "format": sci3 }
]);
emit_table_with_columns!(
    check_columns,
    checks,
    metadata = serde_json::json!({ "id": "checks" })
);

let report = adjusted_report.ok_or("No fit")?;
let termination = report.termination;
let outcome = if termination.is_success() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Adjusted-model IRLS converged")
} else {
    Outcome::failed("Adjusted-model IRLS did not converge")
};
emit_outcome!(
    outcome
        .status(format!("{termination:?}"))
        .diagnostic("IRLS iterations", report.accepted_iterations as f64, None)
        .diagnostic("Adjusted odds ratio", adjusted_or, None)
);
```

The treatment-only model's odds ratio equals the pooled table's 0.748, and its standard error 0.1911 agrees with Woolf's formula within rounding error (difference about $10^{-15}$).
This model has residual deviance 30.8 (2 degrees of freedom), so ignoring stone size fails to explain the success rates in the four rows.
Adding stone size moves A's odds ratio above 1, to 1.429 (95% interval: 0.91–2.24; p = 0.119).
It differs from the Mantel–Haenszel estimate 1.447 by 0.017.
The methods estimate the same common odds ratio differently (maximum likelihood versus a ratio of weighted stratum quantities), so their values do not coincide.
The adjusted interval also contains 1, so this table alone does not establish that A is better when stone size is held fixed.

The adjusted model's residual deviance 1.01 (1 degree of freedom, p = 0.32) tests the difference between the stratum-specific odds ratios 2.08 and 1.23.
A large p-value only indicates compatibility with the common-odds-ratio assumption; it does not prove that the odds ratios are equal.

## How changing allocation affects pooling

A hypothetical experiment changing allocation alone checks that allocation imbalance causes the reversal.
The cell below fixes B's allocation (270 small-stone patients, 80 large-stone patients) and all stratum-specific treatment success rates at their table values, changing only the number $m$ of small-stone patients among A's 350 subjects.
Success counts in each stratum are rounded from the number of patients times the table's success rate.

Changing $m$ leaves stratum-specific success rates unchanged, but changes A's pooled success rate as $p_A(m) = \{m \cdot 81/87 + (350-m) \cdot 192/263\}/350$.
At the observed $m = 87$, the pooled odds ratio is below 1.
At $m = 270$, A and B have the same stone-size composition, eliminating confounding; the pooled success-rate difference becomes a weighted average of the stratum-specific differences.
However, a pooled odds ratio is not an average of stratum-specific odds ratios (noncollapsibility; Greenland, Robins and Pearl, *Statistical Science* 14 (1999), 29–46).
Even without confounding, the pooled odds ratio can be closer to 1 than the stratum-specific odds ratios.

//|   - {"id":"balanced","label":"m = 270 (same composition as B)","description":"Confounding disappears, and A also performs better after pooling.","inputs":{"small_a":270}}

//|   - {"id":"extreme","label":"m = 10","description":"A consists almost entirely of large-stone patients, strengthening the reversal.","inputs":{"small_a":10}}

//|   - case: default

At the default $m = 87$, the pooled odds ratio is 0.748, matching the table.
Increasing $m$ makes the pooled odds ratio rise almost monotonically; at $m = 170$ it equals 1 (both pooled success rates are 0.826), and for larger $m$ it exceeds 1.
At $m = 270$, the pooled odds ratio is 1.59 and the pooled success-rate difference is 0.057, so the reversal disappears.
The pooled odds ratio then lies between the stratum-specific values 2.03 and 1.20.
At $m = 10$, the pooled odds ratio falls to 0.58.
Rounding makes stratum-specific odds ratios vary with $m$; at $m = 10$, A has only 10 small-stone patients and one failure, giving a small-stone odds ratio of 1.38.

## Summary

In Charig et al.'s kidney stone table, the open-surgery odds ratio exceeds 1 in both stone-size strata (2.08 and 1.23), but reverses to 0.748 after pooling.
The reversal comes from allocation imbalance; matching A's composition to B's in the hypothetical experiment removes it.
Compare treatments within strata or use the stone-size-adjusted common odds ratio (Mantel–Haenszel: 1.447; logistic maximum likelihood: 1.429).
`scioxide-stats` does not provide Mantel–Haenszel estimation or testing; compute the closed form or include stratum as an explanatory variable in `PreparedGlm`.
Every interval in this table contains 1, and the observational study leaves unmeasured confounders.
