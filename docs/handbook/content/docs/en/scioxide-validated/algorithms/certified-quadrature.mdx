Ordinary adaptive quadrature often estimates error from differences between rules of different orders.
Verified quadrature requires an upper bound on the remainder that encloses the true integral.
This page derives an enclosure for one interval, then connects it to piece assumptions, adaptive bisection, and certificate reverification.

## Conditions for the proof

Let finite panel $P=[a,b]$ have width $h=b-a\ge0$ and integral $I_P=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
If interval extension $F(P)$ contains the values over the entire panel and $f$ is bounded and integrable, its integral mean also lies in that interval.
The most basic enclosure is therefore $I_P\in hF(P)$.
This method requires no derivatives, but panels with wide ranges produce wide enclosures.

### Trapezoidal-rule remainder

If $f$ is twice continuously differentiable, integrating the linear interpolant through its endpoints gives trapezoidal rule $T_P=h(f(a)+f(b))/2$.
Integrating the interpolation error gives

$$
I_P=T_P-\frac12\int_a^b(x-a)(b-x)f''(x)\,dx
$$


Weight $(x-a)(b-x)$ is nonnegative and integrates to $h^3/6$, so a second-derivative enclosure $F_2(P)$ gives

$$
I_P\in \frac h2\bigl(F(\{a\})+F(\{b\})\bigr)-\frac{h^3}{12}F_2(P)
$$


For example, with $f(x)=x^2$ and $P=[0,1]$, we have $T_P=1/2$ and $f''=2$, giving correction $-1/6$ and result $1/3$.
This enclosure assumes derivative inclusion; it is not an error estimate from the difference between two quadrature values.

### Four-point Gauss–Legendre rule

For an eight-times continuously differentiable function, one can use the four-point Gauss–Legendre rule $G_4$, which is exact for polynomials up to degree 7.
With eighth-derivative enclosure $F_8(P)$,

$$
I_P\in G_4(f)+\frac{h^9}{1778112000}F_8(P)
$$


Since nodes and weights generally cannot themselves be stored as exact real numbers, SciOxide requests function values on narrow intervals enclosing the nodes.
An oracle that assumes point inputs and ignores these interval widths violates the guarantee's assumptions.
Higher-order remainders may reduce the required panel count, but offer less benefit when high-order derivatives cannot be enclosed tightly.

| `QuadratureAssumption` | Required enclosure | Requests per panel | Subdivision |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `IntegrableRange` | Range over the entire panel | 1 | Yes |
| `TwiceContinuouslyDifferentiable` | Endpoint values and second derivative over the panel | 3 | Yes |
| `EightTimesContinuouslyDifferentiable` | Values at 4 nodes and eighth derivative | 5 | Yes |
| `IntegrableSingularity` | Integral over an entire finite piece | 1 | No |
| `ConvergentTail` | Integral over an entire piece with an infinite endpoint | 1 | No |

## API and result interpretation

`QuadraturePiece` stores a piece and its assumptions.
When arranging multiple pieces in order, leave neither gaps nor interior overlaps; the verifier treats their convex hull as the full domain.
`QuadratureAssumption` is the user's mathematical assertion; the verifier does not automatically prove smoothness.

`QuadratureOracle` reads `piece`, `domain`, and `query` of `QuadratureRequest`.
Write range, second-derivative, eighth-derivative, or integral enclosures to the output array according to `query`.
Return `Established` if enclosures for all components are available; otherwise return `Unavailable`.
The output for `Unavailable` is unused, so do not fill unknown values with zeros.
For real components, set the imaginary part of `IntervalBox` to exact zero.

### Adaptive bisection and termination

Generation covers each regular piece with panels and sums their enclosures outward.
It then bisects the panel with the largest ratio of radius to tolerance.
Regular panels with unbounded enclosures are subdivided first, but no valid proposal can be returned until all panels and the total are finite.
For singular and tail pieces, the oracle encloses the entire integral, so `generate` does not subdivide their interiors.

`ErrorTolerance` specifies absolute and relative tolerances and the norm.
For a single real component, the target is a total-interval radius $r$ no greater than $\tau_{\rm abs}+\tau_{\rm rel}|m|$.
$m$ is the midpoint of the total interval; do not confuse width with radius.
For multiple components, match the tolerance component count to the output dimension.

`has_enclosure()` of `QuadratureGenerationReport` indicates whether a finite proposal over the full domain is available.
`ToleranceReached` indicates that the requested accuracy was reached, `Limit(Capacity)` insufficient panel capacity, and `Limit(Resolution)` an unrepresentable subdivision point.
`FixedBounds` indicates that bounds on pieces that cannot be subdivided, or similar factors, limit accuracy improvement.
If termination leaves an enclosure, its value can be read, but the requested accuracy cannot be claimed.

### Certificate reverification

`QuadratureCertificate` owns the subdivision, panel enclosures, and total of `QuadraturePanel`.
Passing `QuadratureCertificateView` of `view()` to `verify` checks panel coverage and recomputes each bound through the oracle.
The result is `Verified` when recomputed bounds lie inside the recorded bounds and their outward sum lies inside the recorded total.
`QuadratureVerificationStatus` tests this inclusion; it does not recheck the tolerance used during generation.
Thus, even a wide proposal stopped by insufficient capacity may yield `Verified` on reverification.

`QuadratureRejection` gives a reason why proposal coverage or bounds cannot be accepted; `QuadratureInconclusive` and `QuadratureProofFailure` give reasons why required bounds cannot be established.
Errors from the callback itself propagate as `Err`.
Displaying generation termination and reverification status in separate columns distinguishes unmet accuracy from proof failure.

## Experiment on derivative information and computational work

Integrating $f(x)=e^x$ on $[0,1]$ gives analytic value $e-1$, and derivatives of every order are also $e^x$.
The same interval extension can serve range, second-derivative, and eighth-derivative requests, isolating the effect of remainder information.
The cell varies absolute tolerance and panel capacity and compares enclosures, radii, panel counts, and both termination statuses for three assumptions.
All quantities are dimensionless; the analytic-value column is an f64 reference.
In wasm, only reference values and settings are displayed.

```rust
//| id: validated-quadrature-assumptions
//| ref: validated-quadrature-assumptions
//| title: "Comparing remainder information and panel capacity"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-validated]
//| inputs:
//|   exponent: {"type":"range","label":"Exponent q of absolute tolerance 10^q","min":-10,"max":-3,"step":1,"value":-6}
//|   capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Panel capacity [count]","min":1,"max":129,"step":16,"value":65}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"one-panel","label":"Capacity 1","inputs":{"capacity":1}}
//|   - {"id":"tight","label":"Tolerance 10⁻¹⁰","inputs":{"exponent":-10}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/0","approx":{"value":1.718281828459045,"atol":1e-14}}
//|   - case: one-panel
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":1}
//|   - case: tight
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":1e-10,"atol":1e-20}}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SciOxideErrorView, InvalidInputReason};
use serde_json::json;
let unsupported = |error: &SciOxideError| {
    matches!(error.view(),
SciOxideErrorView::InvalidInput { argument, reason: InvalidInputReason::UnsupportedConfiguration, .. }
if argument == scioxide_core::argument!("rounding_environment"))
};
use scioxide_core::{Complex, ErrorTolerance, NormSelection};
use scioxide_validated::{
    DecoratedInterval as D, IntervalBox, QuadratureAssumption as A, QuadratureObligation,
    QuadraturePiece, QuadratureQuery, QuadratureRequest, QuadratureTermination,
    QuadratureVerificationStatus, QuadratureVerifier, ValidatedOptions,
};
let tolerance = 10.0_f64.powf(exponent);
let cap = capacity.round() as usize;
let reference = std::f64::consts::E - 1.0;
emit_table_with_columns!(
    [
        "Reference e − 1 (f64, uncertified)",
        "Absolute tolerance",
        "Panel capacity"
    ],
    vec![(reference, tolerance, cap)],
    metadata = json!({"id":"reference"})
);
let proof = (|| -> Result<_, SciOxideError> {
    let oracle = |request: QuadratureRequest<f64>, out: &mut [IntervalBox<f64>]| {
        if request.query == QuadratureQuery::Integral {
            return Ok(QuadratureObligation::Unavailable);
        }
        let value = D::from(request.domain).exp()?.hull();
        out[0] = Complex::new(value.set(), D::point(0.0)?.set());
        Ok(QuadratureObligation::Established)
    };
    let mut rows = Vec::new();
    let mut complete = true;
    for (label, assumption) in [
        ("Range", A::IntegrableRange),
        ("Trapezoidal rule", A::TwiceContinuouslyDifferentiable),
        ("4-point Gauss", A::EightTimesContinuouslyDifferentiable),
    ] {
        let pieces = [QuadraturePiece {
            domain: D::new(0.0, 1.0)?.set(),
            assumption,
        }];
        let verifier =
            QuadratureVerifier::<f64>::new(&pieces, 1, &oracle, ValidatedOptions::new())?;
        let tol = ErrorTolerance::scalar(tolerance, 0.0, NormSelection::Infinity)?;
        let (report, certificate) = verifier.generate(&tol, cap)?;
        let (bounds, verified) = if let Some(c) = certificate {
            let status = verifier.verify(c.view())?;
            (c.view().total[0].re.bounds(), Some(status))
        } else {
            (None, None)
        };
        let lo = bounds.and_then(|b| b.0);
        let hi = bounds.and_then(|b| b.1);
        let radius = lo.zip(hi).map(|(lo, hi)| (hi - lo) / 2.0);
        complete &= report.termination() == QuadratureTermination::ToleranceReached
            && verified == Some(QuadratureVerificationStatus::Verified);
        rows.push((
            label,
            report.panels(),
            lo,
            hi,
            radius,
            format!("{:?}", report.termination()),
            verified.map(|s| format!("{s:?}")),
        ));
    }
    Ok((rows, complete))
})();
let outcome = match proof {
    Ok((rows, complete)) => {
        let fmt = json!({"notation":"scientific","significantDigits":8});
        emit_table_with_columns!(
            json!([
                {"key":"0","label":"Assumption and quadrature rule"},
                {"key":"1","label":"Panel count","type":"integer"},
                {"key":"2","label":"Lower endpoint","type":"number","format":fmt,"nullText":"No enclosure"},
                {"key":"3","label":"Upper endpoint","type":"number","format":fmt,"nullText":"No enclosure"},
                {"key":"4","label":"Radius","type":"number","format":fmt,"nullText":"No enclosure"},
                {"key":"5","label":"Generation termination"},
                {"key":"6","label":"Reverification","nullText":"Not executed"}
            ]),
            rows,
            metadata = json!({"id":"rules"})
        );
        if complete {
            Outcome::satisfied("All rules achieved accuracy and completed reverification")
        } else {
            Outcome::partial("Some rules have unmet accuracy or incomplete proofs; consult each row's status")
        }
    }
    Err(e) if unsupported(&e) => Outcome::unavailable("Proof computation not executed in this environment"),
    Err(e) => return Err(e.to_string()),
};
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```


At the same capacity, a range-only rule tends to give wider results than trapezoidal or Gauss rules because it integrates the full variation range.
With defaults, the range rule reaches capacity at 65 panels, while the trapezoidal rule meets tolerance with 48 panels and the Gauss rule with 1 panel.
All three have reverification status `Verified`, so the capacity-limited row retains a valid wide enclosure.
The local trapezoidal remainder is $O(h^3)$ and the four-point Gauss remainder is $O(h^9)$, giving global truncation errors $O(h^2)$ and $O(h^8)$ under uniform subdivision.
However, the tabulated radius also includes interval-extension overestimation and rounding, so this order does not persist indefinitely.
The capacity-1 case demonstrates that successful reverification of a wide enclosure does not mean generation met its accuracy target.

## Singularities, infinite intervals, and storage

For example, integrating $x^{-1/2}$ on $[0,\delta]$ gives $2\sqrt\delta$: the integral is finite even though the function is unbounded.
Use `IntegrableSingularity` for this piece and return an outward evaluation of this closed form for a `Integral` request.
If $|f(x)|\le Ce^{-\alpha x}$ and $\alpha>0$ can be proved on an infinite tail, the absolute tail integral of $[A,\infty)$ is at most $Ce^{-\alpha A}/\alpha$.
The user must also guarantee these inequalities and enclosures of their constants.
Do not infer a tail upper bound merely from small samples.

`workspace(ProofRequest::refinement(capacity))` reserves `QuadratureWorkspace` according to panel capacity.
`generate_into` writes to the caller's certificate, which `verify_scoped` can reverify using the same storage.
Managing $N$ panels takes $O(N\log N)$ work and, with a fixed component count, $O(N)$ storage, plus oracle costs.
The one-shot `integrate` targets real functions that are twice continuously differentiable on a finite interval, using default accuracy and at most 4096 panels.

## Summary

A guaranteed integral requires enclosures of the range or derivatives used in the remainder.
Check generation's accuracy achievement separately from reverification of certificate inclusion.
For singularities and tails, the oracle needs justification for enclosing the entire integral.
The [error-function example](../../examples/erf-certified-quadrature/) examines guarantees including the normalization constant, while the [API list](../../api/quadrature/) explains ownership and borrowing of each input and output.

For Gauss-quadrature remainders, see [NIST DLMF §3.5(v)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/3.5.v); for interval enclosures of integrals, see Chapter 6 of Moore, Kearfott, and Cloud, *Introduction to Interval Analysis* (SIAM, 2009).
