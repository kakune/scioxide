Enclosing $f(X)=\{f(x):x\in X\}$ for every real number in interval $X$ differs from evaluating the function at both endpoints.
For example, the sine on $X=[0,\pi]$ attains its maximum at the interior point $\pi/2$.
Also, $X=[1,2]$ crosses the tangent pole $\pi/2$.
The elementary functions of `DecoratedInterval` and `ElementaryEnclosure` express these distinctions.

## Monotonicity, critical points, and undefined inputs

The exponential is monotonically increasing, so outward-rounded exponential values at both endpoints enclose its range.
Sine and cosine also require examination of periodicity and interior extrema.
Logarithms and inverse trigonometric functions require intersection with their real domains, distinguishing definition over the entire original input interval from definition over only part of it.

The following elementary functions of `DecoratedInterval` all return `ElementaryEnclosure`.

| Category | Methods | Construction of the enclosure |
|---|---|---|
| Monotone univariate functions | `exp`, `exp2`, `exp10`, `exp_m1`, `ln`, `log2`, `log10`, `ln_1p`, `asin`, `acos`, `atan`, `sinh`, `tanh`, `asinh`, `acosh`, `atanh`, `cbrt` | Evaluate both endpoints of the intersection with the domain. The domains are $x>0$ for `ln`, $x>-1$ for `ln_1p`, $[-1,1]$ for `asin` and `acos`, $[1,\infty)$ for `acosh`, and $(-1,1)$ for `atanh` |
| Functions with extrema | `cosh`, `sin`, `cos` | `cosh` is monotone in $\lvert x \rvert$. `sin` and `cos` locate extrema within the interval and return $[-1,1]$ if its width is at least 6 or it is unbounded |
| Functions with poles | `tan` | Preserve two pieces when crossing one pole. Return all real numbers for an interval containing two poles, of width at least 4, or unbounded |
| Bivariate functions and powers | `hypot`, `atan2`, `powi`, `powf`, `log` | `hypot` scales operands by powers of 2, so its result neither overflows nor underflows if the true value does not. `atan2` excludes the origin and has values in $(-\pi, \pi]$; a box crossing the negative real axis returns two pieces near $-\pi$ and $+\pi$. A negative odd power in `powi` with both signs present, and a base crossing 1 in `log`, also preserve two pieces. Negative bases with many integer exponents in `powf` return pieces grouped by result sign and set `gaps_filled` |

Each endpoint is computed by a fixed-degree polynomial approximation after argument reduction (Payne–Hanek reduction for trigonometric functions), adding bounds for approximation and rounding errors before outward rounding.
For point arguments, enclosure widths are at most two floating-point spacings; this has been checked for 21 univariate functions against correctly rounded reference values in `f32` and `f64`.
Every method takes constant time and performs no allocation.
A failed outward-rounding environment check produces an error of `InvalidInput`; breakdown of the internal enclosure procedure produces an error of `NumericalInstability`.

`EnclosureStatus` classifies the domain into five cases: `EmptyInput` (empty input), `Defined` (defined throughout the input), `PartiallyDefined` (defined only on part), `Undefined` (every point outside the domain), and `Inconclusive` (a correct enclosure, but domain or branch classification undecidable at fixed precision).
Outside `Defined`, interval pieces have decoration `Trv`.
Obtaining a numerical interval does not establish a continuous function over the entire input.
An empty range and a computational failure are also distinct.
Read both the returned status and the interval pieces.

## Do not hide tangent poles with a convex hull

Since $1<\pi/2<2$, the tangent image splits into two infinite intervals.
Taking their convex hull over all real numbers also includes zero, which is absent from the image.
`ElementaryEnclosure::pieces` retains enclosures of at most two closed branches, and `contains` tests membership in their union.

The following experiment tabulates exponential and tangent enclosures on $X=[1,2]$ as at most two interval pieces per function.
The exponential range $[e, e^2]$ contains $3$; the tangent image $(-\infty, \tan 2] \cup [\tan 1, +\infty)$ splits into two pieces and excludes zero.
The f(1) and f(2) columns are uncertified endpoint reference values computed with f64's inherent methods ($e \approx 2.718$, $e^2 \approx 7.389$, $\tan 1 \approx 1.557$, and $\tan 2 \approx -2.185$).
The reference columns appear in every environment.
Environments where the rounding assumptions cannot be checked, including wasm32, display “Not executed” in the enclosure columns, without replacing them with reference values.

```rust
//| id: validated-elementary-branches
//| ref: validated-elementary-branches
//| title: "Exponential enclosure and two tangent branches"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-validated]
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"table-0","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":"exp"}
//|       - {"artifact":"table-0","path":"/rows/0/1","approx":{"value":2.718281828459045,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"table-0","path":"/rows/0/2","approx":{"value":7.38905609893065,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"table-0","path":"/rows/1/0","equals":"tan"}
//|       - {"artifact":"table-0","path":"/rows/1/1","approx":{"value":1.5574077246549023,"atol":1e-14}}
//|       - {"artifact":"table-0","path":"/rows/1/2","approx":{"value":-2.185039863261519,"atol":1e-14}}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SciOxideErrorView, InvalidInputReason};
use scioxide_validated::DecoratedInterval;

// 1. Enclose the exponential and tangent on X = [1, 2]. Propagate rounding-environment errors unchanged.
let calculation = || -> Result<_, SciOxideError> {
    let input = DecoratedInterval::new(1.0_f64, 2.0)?;
    Ok([input.exp()?, input.tan()?])
};
let unsupported = |error: &SciOxideError| {
    matches!(
        error.view(),
        SciOxideErrorView::InvalidInput {
            argument,
            reason: InvalidInputReason::UnsupportedConfiguration,
            ..
        } if argument == scioxide_core::argument!("rounding_environment")
    )
};
let enclosures = match calculation() {
    Ok(results) => Some(results),
    Err(error) if unsupported(&error) => None,
    Err(error) => return Err(error.to_string()),
};
use serde_json::json;
let references = [
    ("exp", 1.0_f64.exp(), 2.0_f64.exp()),
    ("tan", 1.0_f64.tan(), 2.0_f64.tan()),
];
emit_table_with_columns!(
    json!([
        {"key":"0","label":"Function"},
        {"key":"1","label":"f(1) (uncertified)","type":"number"},
        {"key":"2","label":"f(2) (uncertified)","type":"number"}
    ]),
    references,
    metadata = json!({"id":"table-0"})
);
let mut rows = Vec::new();
if let Some(results) = &enclosures {
    for (i, result) in results.iter().enumerate() {
        for (j, piece) in result.pieces().iter().enumerate() {
            let (lo, hi) = piece.set().bounds().unwrap();
            rows.push((
                references[i].0,
                j + 1,
                lo,
                hi,
                format!("{:?}", result.status()),
                result.contains(0.0),
            ));
        }
    }
}
let fmt = json!({"significantDigits":8});
emit_table_with_columns!(
    json!([
        {"key":"0","label":"Function"},
        {"key":"1","label":"Interval piece","type":"integer"},
        {"key":"2","label":"Lower endpoint","type":"number","format":fmt,"nullText":"−∞"},
        {"key":"3","label":"Upper endpoint","type":"number","format":fmt,"nullText":"+∞"},
        {"key":"4","label":"Domain classification"},
        {"key":"5","label":"Union contains zero"}
    ]),
    rows,
    metadata = json!({"id":"pieces"})
);
emit_outcome!(if enclosures.is_some() {
    Outcome::satisfied("Computed branch enclosures")
} else {
    Outcome::unavailable("Proof computation not executed in this environment")
});
```

In a native environment, the exponential has one interval piece with decoration `Com` and classification `Defined`, containing endpoint reference values $e$ and $e^2$.
The tangent has two unbounded interval pieces whose finite endpoints enclose $\tan 2$ and $\tan 1$, respectively, through outward rounding.
The displayed table values are rounded for readability; do not use the displayed digits as new certified endpoints.
`contains(3.0)` is true for the exponential, `contains(0.0)` is false for the tangent, and `contains(0.0)` for the tangent's convex hull is true.
Both tangent pieces have decoration `Trv` and domain classification `PartiallyDefined` because pole $\pi/2$ is excluded from the domain.
Taking the convex hull with `hull()` gives all real numbers, including zero.
`gaps_filled()` conservatively indicates whether forming the branch convex hull may have filled a gap in the image.

## Numerical error and model uncertainty

Interval enclosures retain overestimation due to dependency.
For example, treating the two occurrences of the same $x\in[0,1]$ as independent intervals in subtraction widens the enclosure of $x-x$.
Correct outward rounding does not eliminate this overestimation.
Consider subdividing the input interval, mathematical reformulation, using monotonicity, or affine arithmetic with shared noise symbols (`AffineForm`).

Do not confuse elementary-function enclosures with ordinary solver error estimates.
Even when interval functions are correct, existence and uniqueness proofs for an entire iterative solution require additional conditions, handled by verification in `roots`, `ScalarRootVerifier`, and `SystemRootVerifier`.

## Summary and further reading

Elementary-function enclosures examine monotonicity, interior extrema, and pole positions and return at most two interval pieces.
`contains` tests membership in the union of the pieces, avoiding erroneous inclusion of zero absent from the image even when an interval crosses a tangent pole.
Inputs defined only in part are distinguished by `EnclosureStatus` and decoration `Trv`.
Enclosures are available only in native environments that pass the rounding-environment check.
