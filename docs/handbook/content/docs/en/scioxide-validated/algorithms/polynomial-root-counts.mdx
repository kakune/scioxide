When approximate polynomial roots cluster, proving the count of the whole cluster is more appropriate than isolating each root.
Counts here include multiplicity: a double root counts as 2.
We distinguish proving the count inside a disk from proving a set of disks covers all roots.

## Conditions for the proof

The target is $p(z)=a_0+a_1z+\cdots+a_dz^d$, with real or complex coefficients.
Let $D(c,r)=\{z:|z-c|\le r\}$ denote the closed disk with center $c$ and radius $r>0$.
Rouché's theorem states that if one term strictly dominates the remainder on the boundary, removing the remainder does not change the number of zeros inside.
SciOxide checks this strict dominance using outward rounding.
Success also establishes the absence of boundary roots, so the open and closed disks have equal counts.

### A test for simple roots

With $z=c+w$, the Taylor expansion at the center is

$$
p(c+w)=p(c)+p'(c)w+\mathcal R_2(w),\qquad
|\mathcal R_2(w)|\le M_2|w|^2
$$


$M_2$ bounds $|p''(z)|/2$ over the disk.
If $|p(c)|+r^2M_2<r|p'(c)|$ on boundary $|w|=r$, the linear term $p'(c)w$ dominates the sum of the other terms in magnitude.
The linear term has exactly one root in the disk, so the original polynomial has the same count.
This test often succeeds when the center approximates a simple root well, but is unsuitable for multiple roots with small $p'(c)$.

Horner's method evaluates $p(c)$ and $p'(c)$ simultaneously with $O(d)$ operations for degree $d$.
The implementation uses complex center-radius enclosures to limit overestimation from repeatedly widening the real and imaginary parts as independent intervals.
This radius encloses rounding in polynomial evaluation; distinguish it from radius $r$ of a disk enclosing roots.

### Taylor shifts and Pellet's test

If the simple-root test is inconclusive, transform the disk into the unit disk and compute

$$
p(c+rz)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_jz^j,\qquad
b_j=r^j\sum_{\ell=j}^{d}{\ell\choose j}a_\ell c^{\ell-j}
$$

using interval arithmetic.
This transformation is the Taylor shift; obtaining all coefficients takes $O(d^2)$ operations.
Since $|b_kz^k|=|b_k|$ on $|z|=1$ and the absolute value of the remaining sum is at most $\sum_{j\ne k}|b_j|$,

$$
|b_k|>\sum_{j\ne k}|b_j|
$$

if this inequality holds, the disk contains exactly $k$ roots.
$k=0$ establishes absence of roots; $k>1$ also handles multiple roots or clusters.
Use a lower bound for the absolute value on the left and upper bounds on the right; equality is not success.
Failure of the inequality does not determine the count and yields `Inconclusive` as a limitation of the sufficient condition.

## API and result interpretation

`PolynomialRootPlan::new(coefficients, options)` takes coefficients in ascending order from the constant term, removing exact trailing zeros to determine the degree.
The zero polynomial has no defined count of isolated roots and produces `InvalidInput`.
The stored floating-point coefficients themselves are the exact inputs; errors incurred when forming them from an original expression are not automatically covered.
Preparation stores coefficients scaled by a power of 2, which leaves the zeros unchanged.

`PolynomialRootCertificate` pairs `RootDisk` with a claimed `count`; construction alone provides no proof.
In `PolynomialVerificationReport` returned by `verify`, `status()` is the decision on the claim and `count()` is the count actually proved.
Proving a different count gives `Refuted`; establishing no count gives `Inconclusive`.
Increasing the disk radius changes the dominant term, so even though the count increases monotonically, ease of deciding it need not.

### Experiment with a double root and boundary roots

Expanding $p(z)=(z-1)^2(z+2)=2-3z+z^3$ around center $c=1$ gives $p(1+w)=3w^2+w^3$.
Thus $b_2=3r^2$ and $b_3=r^3$, and when $0<r<3$ the quadratic term dominates, proving a count of 2.
For $r>3$, the cubic term dominates and the count is 3; at $r=3$, root $-2$ lies on the boundary.
At equality, even if the closed-disk count is known analytically, the test requiring no boundary roots cannot be used.

The cell varies the disk radius and claimed count independently, comparing the analytic count with verification status.
Coordinates and radii are dimensionless; reference roots $1$ and $-2$ are both exactly representable.
In wasm, only analytic values are displayed, and the count proof is not executed.

```rust
//| id: validated-pellet-multiplicity
//| ref: validated-pellet-multiplicity
//| title: "Disk radius and count including multiplicity"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-validated]
//| inputs:
//|   radius: {"type":"range","label":"Radius r of disk centered at 1 [-]","min":0.125,"max":4,"step":0.125,"value":0.5}
//|   count: {"type":"range","label":"Claimed root count","min":0,"max":3,"step":1,"value":2}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"boundary","label":"Boundary root","inputs":{"radius":3}}
//|   - {"id":"refuted","label":"Incorrect simple-root claim","inputs":{"count":1}}
//|   - {"id":"all-roots","label":"Disk containing three roots","inputs":{"radius":4,"count":3}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":2}
//|   - case: boundary
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/2","equals":true}
//|   - case: all-roots
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":3}
//|   - case: refuted
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/3","equals":1}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SciOxideErrorView, InvalidInputReason};
use serde_json::json;
let unsupported = |error: &SciOxideError| {
    matches!(error.view(),
SciOxideErrorView::InvalidInput { argument, reason: InvalidInputReason::UnsupportedConfiguration, .. }
if argument == scioxide_core::argument!("rounding_environment"))
};
use scioxide_core::Complex;
use scioxide_validated::{
    PolynomialRootCertificate, PolynomialRootPlan, RootDisk, RootVerificationStatus,
    ValidatedOptions,
};
let exact = if radius < 3.0 { 2 } else { 3 };
let claimed = count.round() as usize;
emit_table_with_columns!(
    [
        "Radius",
        "Known count in closed disk",
        "Known root on boundary",
        "Claimed count"
    ],
    vec![(radius, exact, radius == 3.0, claimed)],
    metadata = json!({"id":"reference"})
);
let proof = (|| -> Result<_, SciOxideError> {
    let plan = PolynomialRootPlan::new(&[2.0_f64, -3.0, 0.0, 1.0], ValidatedOptions::new())?;
    plan.verify(PolynomialRootCertificate {
        disk: RootDisk {
            center: Complex::new(1.0, 0.0),
            radius,
        },
        count: claimed,
    })
})();
let outcome = match proof {
    Ok(report) => {
        emit_table_with_columns!(
            json!([
                {"key":"0","label":"Proved count","type":"integer","nullText":"Undetermined"},
                {"key":"1","label":"Status"}
            ]),
            vec![(report.count(), format!("{:?}", report.status()))],
            metadata = json!({"id":"proof"})
        );
        match report.status() {
            RootVerificationStatus::Proved => Outcome::satisfied("Proved the root count in the disk"),
            RootVerificationStatus::Refuted => Outcome::failed("Proved a count different from the claim"),
            _ => Outcome::partial("Root count undetermined in this disk"),
        }
        .status(format!("{:?}", report.status()))
    }
    Err(e) if unsupported(&e) => Outcome::unavailable("Proof computation not executed in this environment"),
    Err(e) => return Err(e.to_string()),
};
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```


Selecting the simple-root claim at radius $1/2$ makes the verifier prove a count of 2 and refute the claim.
The undetermined result at radius 3 corresponds to an exact boundary condition rather than a numerical accident.
For a general polynomial, however, an undetermined result need not imply a boundary root.
Wide coefficient enclosures or multiple terms of similar magnitude can also make the test inconclusive.

## Covering all roots and reusing a Plan

Counting roots in individual disks alone may leave roots outside them.
Besides individual disk claims, `verify_cover` checks that disks are pairwise disjoint and their counts sum to the degree.
If the whole covering is proved, the fundamental theorem of algebra excludes any remaining roots.
Even many disks with zero roots do not establish coverage of all roots.

`isolate` starts with disks around approximate roots, expands radii when inconclusive, and merges overlapping disks.
`PolynomialRootCover` owns the resulting certificates, and the termination status of `PolynomialIsolationReport` indicates completeness.
Nearby roots may be counted together in one disk, so one root per disk is not guaranteed.
The one-shot `polynomial_roots` starts this process from default radii and returns only complete coverings.

Preparation requires $O(d)$ operations and storage, while a general test for one disk takes $O(d^2)$ operations.
Reserving `RootWorkspace` using `ProofRequest::proofs(k)` or requirements for disk capacity allows workspace reuse across verifications.
The same Plan represents the same coefficients; rebuild it when changing coefficient perturbations.
The [Wilkinson polynomial](../../examples/wilkinson-root-counts/) illustrates the distinction between input-coefficient rounding and count decisions.

## Summary

Counts inside disks are proved by strict dominance of a term on the boundary.
Choose between the simple-root test and the test that counts multiple roots after a Taylor shift.
Read `count()` separately from `status()` on the claim; do not treat an undetermined count as zero.
To conclude that all roots have been examined, verify the whole covering in addition to individual disks.

For Rouché's theorem and root inclusion, see Chapter 6 of Henrici, *Applied and Computational Complex Analysis*, Vol. 1 (Wiley, 1974).
