A root-finding iteration that stops with a small residual need not establish a root in the search domain.
For example, $f(x)=x^2+10^{-20}$ has a small residual at the origin but no real root.
Interval verification establishes existence, uniqueness, and absence separately from inclusion of function and derivative values.
Distinguish verification of a claim about one region from root isolation over the entire specified domain.

## Conditions for the proof

Assume $f$ is continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of bounded closed interval $X=[a,b]$.
Write the value interval extension as $F(X)\supseteq\{f(x):x\in X\}$ and the derivative interval extension as $D(X)\supseteq\{f'(x):x\in X\}$.
The two closures passed to SciOxide must represent the same function and its derivative without changing their meaning during the proof.
`IntervalScalarFunction` is the trait expressing this calling convention; closures implement it directly.

### Deriving the interval Newton map

Choose $c\in X$.
If $x^*\in X$ is a root, the mean value theorem gives, for some $\xi$,

$$
0=f(x^*)=f(c)+f'(\xi)(x^*-c)
$$


When $0\notin D(X)$, division is possible, and all roots lie in

$$
x^*\in N(X)=c-\frac{F(\{c\})}{D(X)}
$$


Here, $F(\{c\})$ is an enclosure including rounding error, rather than an ordinary point evaluation.
Thus, $N(X)\cap X=\emptyset$ implies that $X$ has no root.
Conversely, $N(X)\subset\operatorname{int}(X)$ establishes a unique root by the interval Newton inclusion theorem.
A narrow $N(X)$ or nonempty $N(X)\cap X$ alone is not sufficient for existence.

The intermediate value theorem gives another existence proof.
Strictly opposite-signed endpoint enclosures, or an exact $[0,0]$ enclosure at an endpoint or the center, establishes a root.
If also $0\notin D(X)$, the derivative retains one sign and the function is strictly monotone, so there is at most one root.
A derivative excluding zero alone does not establish a root; combine it with an existence condition.

| Claim | Sufficient condition used in verification |
|---|---|
| `Empty` | $0\notin F(X)$, or $0\notin D(X)$ and $N(X)\cap X=\emptyset$ |
| `Exists` | Exact zero at the center or an endpoint, or a strict endpoint sign change; a proof of `Unique` also implies this |
| `Unique` | Existence and $0\notin D(X)$, or strict interior inclusion of the interval Newton image |

Root proofs are inconclusive when an interval is empty or unbounded, or its decoration is below `Dac` and continuity is unknown.
Do not arbitrarily discard out-of-domain values and reassign `Com`.
The verifier cannot detect an incorrectly narrow interval extension.

## API and result interpretation

`ScalarRootCertificate` stores `region`, a finite `center` inside it, and `RootClaim`.
`ScalarRootVerifier::new(&value, &derivative, options)` borrows the two extensions.
`RootVerificationReport::status()` of `verify(certificate)` is the sole authoritative decision.

| Decision | Interpretation | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| `Proved` | The specified claim was established | Use the result within the claim's scope |
| `Refuted` | A fact contradicting the claim was proved | Read `evidence()` and revise the claim |
| `Inconclusive` | Neither the claim nor its opposite was established | Reconsider the region, extension, or work limits |
| `NotRun` | This batch-verification entry has not run yet | Do not use it as a successful result |

For example, claiming `Empty` in a region proved to contain a unique root gives `Refuted`.
Failure to prove `Unique` in a wide interval does not immediately imply `Refuted`.
In `RootInconclusive`, `Reason(RootProofFailure::Inequality)` indicates an undecided method inequality, and `Domain` indicates insufficient extension continuity or related information.

### Examining the whole domain with a binary tree

`isolate(domain, tolerance, max_nodes)` creates a `RootIsolation` whose root is the entire domain.
It verifies absence or uniqueness in each region and bisects regions where neither is established.
Proved leaves are retained, and only undecided leaves are subdivided.
Each bisection needs two children, so insufficient capacity stops the process with `Limit(Capacity)`.
An unrepresentable subdivision point gives `Limit(Resolution)`; an unresolved subdivision threshold or similar issue gives an undecided report.

`RootIsolationNode` represents a subdivision axis and position or a leaf claim.
`RootIsolationView` borrows a domain and tree; passing it to `verify_isolation` reverifies leaves from the original function.
No unexamined region remains only when `RootIsolationReport::is_complete()` is true.
Even after finding a unique-root leaf, another undecided leaf may contain additional roots.

In one dimension, two leaves can share an endpoint, and both may count a root at that endpoint.
Thus, `isolated()` need not count distinct roots.
The one-shot `roots` merges these duplicates and returns ascending, pairwise nonoverlapping intervals.
Each interval is the first leaf proving uniqueness, rather than an interval converged to a specified width.

The following cell executes four claims for $f(x)=x^2-2$ and isolation over all of $[-2,2]$.
The analytic roots are $\pm\sqrt2$; their count of 2 provides an independent check.
The only input is node capacity; values and domains are dimensionless.
Capacity 1 cannot bisect the root region; increasing capacity produces leaves containing the two roots.

```rust
//| id: validated-root-claims-and-tree
//| ref: validated-root-claims-and-tree
//| title: "Individual claims and isolation of all roots"
//| run: button
//| crates: [scioxide-core, scioxide-validated]
//| inputs:
//|   capacity: {"type":"range","label":"Binary-tree node capacity [count]","min":1,"max":31,"step":2,"value":15}
//| cases:
//|   - {"id":"one-node","label":"Capacity 1","inputs":{"capacity":1}}
//| tests:
//|   - case: default
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/0","equals":2}
//|   - case: one-node
//|     expect:
//|       - {"artifact":"reference","path":"/rows/0/1","equals":1}
use scioxide_core::{SciOxideError, SciOxideErrorView, InvalidInputReason};
use serde_json::json;
let unsupported = |error: &SciOxideError| {
    matches!(error.view(),
SciOxideErrorView::InvalidInput { argument, reason: InvalidInputReason::UnsupportedConfiguration, .. }
if argument == scioxide_core::argument!("rounding_environment"))
};
use scioxide_core::StepTolerance;
use scioxide_validated::{
    DecoratedInterval as D, RootClaim, ScalarRootCertificate, ScalarRootVerifier,
    ValidatedOptions,
};
let n = capacity.round() as usize;
emit_table_with_columns!(
    ["Known root count", "Node capacity"],
    vec![(2, n)],
    metadata = json!({"id":"reference"})
);
let proof = (|| -> Result<_, SciOxideError> {
    let two = D::point(2.0)?;
    let f = |x: D<f64>| x.square()?.sub(&two);
    let df = |x: D<f64>| x.mul(&two);
    let verifier = ScalarRootVerifier::new(&f, &df, ValidatedOptions::new());
    let mut claims = Vec::new();
    for (lo, hi, claim) in [
        (1.0, 2.0, RootClaim::Unique),
        (1.0, 2.0, RootClaim::Empty),
        (2.0, 3.0, RootClaim::Empty),
        (-2.0, 2.0, RootClaim::Unique),
    ] {
        let proposal = ScalarRootCertificate {
            region: D::new(lo, hi)?.set(),
            center: (lo + hi) / 2.0,
            claim,
        };
        let report = verifier.verify(proposal)?;
        claims.push((
            lo,
            hi,
            format!("{claim:?}"),
            format!("{:?}", report.status()),
            format!("{:?}", report.evidence()),
        ));
    }
    let domain = D::new(-2.0, 2.0)?.set();
    let (report, tree) = verifier.isolate(domain, StepTolerance::default(), n)?;
    let regions: Vec<_> = tree
        .isolated_regions()
        .map(|r| r[0].re.bounds().unwrap())
        .collect();
    let replay = verifier.verify_isolation(domain, tree.view())?;
    Ok((claims, report, regions, replay))
})();
let outcome = match proof {
    Ok((claims, report, regions, replay)) => {
        emit_table_with_columns!(
            ["Lower endpoint", "Upper endpoint", "Claim", "Decision", "Established fact"],
            claims,
            metadata = json!({"id":"claims"})
        );
        emit_table_with_columns!(
            ["Lower endpoint with proved uniqueness", "Upper endpoint"],
            regions,
            metadata = json!({"id":"regions"})
        );
        emit_table_with_columns!(
            [
                "Node count",
                "Unique-root leaves",
                "Excluded leaves",
                "Undecided leaves",
                "Reverification termination"
            ],
            vec![(
                report.nodes(),
                report.isolated(),
                report.excluded(),
                report.unresolved(),
                format!("{:?}", replay.termination())
            )],
            metadata = json!({"id":"tree"})
        );
        let result = if report.is_complete() && replay.is_complete() {
            Outcome::satisfied("Isolation of all roots and reverification completed")
        } else {
            Outcome::partial("Undecided regions remain in the tree")
        };
        result.status(format!("{:?}", report.termination()))
    }
    Err(e) if unsupported(&e) => Outcome::unavailable("Proof computation not executed in this environment"),
    Err(e) => return Err(e.to_string()),
};
emit_outcome!(outcome);
```


`Unique` for `[1,2]` and `Empty` for `[2,3]` can be proved, while `Empty` for `[1,2]` is refuted.
For `[-2,2]`, the derivative enclosure contains zero, so one verification cannot decide uniqueness.
This is consistent with the analytic fact that there are two roots.
To distinguish all roots, the isolation process verifies negative and positive regions separately.
Conclude that all roots have been examined only after confirming `Complete` and successful reverification.

## Krawczyk map for systems of equations

For $F:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$, search box $X$, and center $c\in X$, let $R$ approximate $J(c)^{-1}$.
A fixed point of map $T(x)=x-RF(x)$ is a root of $F$ if $R$ is nonsingular.
Using $J(X)$ enclosing Jacobians along line segments within $X$, enclose $T(X)$ by

$$
K(X)=c-RF(c)+(I-RJ(X))(X-c)
$$


$K(X)\subseteq X$ ensures mapping into the box, and $\|I-RJ(X)\|<1$ ensures contraction.
When both hold, the fixed-point theorem establishes existence and uniqueness, and also nonsingularity of $R$.
SciOxide's `SystemRootVerifier` checks both conditions.
If $K(X)$ fails to intersect $X$ in even one component direction, absence is established.

Supply values and Jacobian actions through `IntervalResidualOperator` and `IntervalJacobianOperator`.
There is no need to store a Jacobian; implement an action enclosing $J(x)v$ for every $x\in X$ and direction $v\in V$.
The preconditioner `IntervalLinearOperator` must represent one fixed matrix.
Complex problems require a holomorphic function; rewrite nonholomorphic maps involving conjugates or absolute values in twice the real dimension.
The [system implementation example](../../examples/nonlinear-system-proof/) implements ordinary and interval actions.

## Accuracy and complexity

One scalar verification uses 1 iteration and at most 5 function and derivative evaluations combined.
For $n$ unknowns, one system verification takes $2n+3$ operator evaluations and $n$ iterations, plus the work inside operators.
Workspace is $O(n)$, excluding operators' temporary storage.
Reserve `RootWorkspace` from `ProofRequest` for the verification count and node capacity, and reuse it with `verify_scoped` and `isolate_into`.

At multiple roots, the derivative vanishes, so even fine subdivision may fail uniqueness conditions intended for simple roots.
Even very near an approximate root, rounding widths may prevent determination of endpoint signs.
[Kepler's equation](../../examples/kepler-uniqueness/) illustrates how an overly narrow proposal around the same root can make proof harder.
For multiple polynomial roots, use the next page's [disk root-count proofs](../polynomial-root-counts/).

## Summary

Scalar roots are verified by combining value signs, nonzero derivatives, and inclusion of the Newton image.
Read individual claim decisions separately from the termination status of isolation over the full domain.
For systems, check both box inclusion and contraction, and distinguish ordinary Jacobian actions from interval actions used for proofs.
For inconclusiveness unresolved by higher work limits, examine multiple roots, domains, and rounding widths.

For inclusion theorems, see Chapter 8 of Moore, Kearfott, and Cloud, *Introduction to Interval Analysis* (SIAM, 2009); for the system method, see Krawczyk, *Newton-Algorithmen zur Bestimmung von Nullstellen mit Fehlerschranken*, Computing 4 (1969), 187–201.
